# 1. Introduction

Recently, methylammonium lead trihalide (MAPbX $ _3 $ , X = Cl, Br, and I) perovskite materials have attracted enormous interest as light-harvesting materials owing to their intense broadband adsorption, high charge carrier mobility, ambipolar semi-conductivity, low-cost precursor materials and facile process (Liu et al., 2013; Zhou et al., 2016). Benefiting from these advantageous properties, the organometallic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been widely researched as a result of the significant rise in their solar-to-electric power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) in recent years (Xing et al., 2013). In 2009, Mihasaka first employed the perovskite semiconductors in photovoltaic devices, and achieved a PCE of  $ \sim $ 4% (Kojima et al., 2009). Since then, tremendous progress has been made in film preparation methodology, device architecture, and compositional engineering of perovskite materials, leading to the rapid improvement in PCE of PSCs (Pham et al., 2019; Wei et al., 2019; Aibin et al., 2017; Kai et al., 2019). Despite the great advance in power conversion efficiency (PCE), the poor stability of perovskite solar cells in ambient condition becomes a tremendous obstruction for real application. Solution-processed all-metal-oxide charge transport layers exhibit remarkable improvement in stability in ambient air according to the previous report. However, the solution process is confined to the limited effective area and morphology of the substrate. Here we report the magnetron sputtered charge transport layers, of which Cu doped  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $   $ (\mathrm{Cu:NiO_x}) $  acts as the hole transport layer and  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  as the electron transport layer, respectively. We precisely control their properties on unlimited substrates without post-annealing for balanced charge transport efficiency in both interfaces, which demonstrates better power conversion efficiency. The all-metal-oxide based devices also demonstrate excellent stability in ambient condition when compared to the control devices based on conventional organic transport layers.

Typically, the planar heterojunction PSC is constituted of a layer of perovskite material sandwiched by charge transport materials and their corresponding electrodes (Chen et al., 2015). Generally; organic small-molecule or polymeric charge transport layers was incorporated in high-performance perovskite solar cells. p-type poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrene sulphonate (PEDOT:PSS), 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N, N(4-methoxyphenyl)amino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD), poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA) and poly(3-hexylthiophene-2,5-diyl) (P3HT), have been developed as hole transport layers, and n-type (Wei et al., 2019a, 2019b)phenyl C  $  _{61}  $  butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM), C  $  _{60}  $  and their derivatives have been served as electron transport layers (Chen et al., 2015; Huang et al., 2017). However, all of them are liable to degrade at ambient conditions because of their low energy of formation. Thus, the all-metal-oxide charge transport layers with higher chemical inertness were obviously helpful to improve the stability of the devices. Many approaches have been explored to tackle this drawback by applying inorganic materials, such as CuSCN, CuI and  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  as hole transport layers (HTLs) (Sajid et al., 2018; You et al., 2016) and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  (Sawanta and Chang, 2017); ZnO as electron transport layers (ETLs) (Mo et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2014). Y. Yang has reported PSCs which have metal-oxide charge transport layers prepared by solution-process method. Comparing to the above-mentioned organic materials; the metal oxides exhibit much higher carrier mobility and superior stability. ZnO and Cu doped  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  have demonstrated good electron and hole transport capability (Bai et al., 2018). We also select the all-metal-oxide charge transport layers, but they were realized by magnetron sputtering. Compared with solution processes (Huang et al., 2018), the magnetron sputtered films show more flat and compact morphology on an unspecified substrate, which is not limited to the size and elasticity. We could precisely control the thickness of the film at the sub-nanometer scale to obtain the optimized parameters and make considerable reproducibility possible. Also, post-annealing is unnecessary for our sputtered films, while it is inevitable to evaporate the residual solvent for the solution processed films. Furthermore, the DC power used in this work is nonradiative and economic in contrast with the previous reported RF (radio frequency) operating mode.

Flat  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer is widely considered to be an effective approach to upgrade charge carrier balance in conventional PSCs owing to the large interface and fast separation of photo-induced charge carriers from the self-connected perovskite network to the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer and electrode (Lei et al., 2019; Khan et al., 2019). However, it was hard to balance the electron and hole flux transport through each charge transport layer in planar PSCs by interface engineering (Bai et al., 2019). In addition, the too high sputtering power of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  would damage the active layer even with a layer sacrificial layer (Mohammad et al., 2018; Xin et al., 2017). Thus far, the proposed methods to realize the balance property mainly included the following: the interface contact optimization, gradient energy band level design, post-annealing and heterogeneous atoms doping. On the contrary of the previous work solely focused on ETL or HTL, we aim to optimize them simultaneously and demonstrate their correlation.

In addition, the sustainable ion bombing during magnetron sputtering process would inevitably cause damage on the surface  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  , which was significantly related to carrier separation efficiency in the interfaces. A layer of PTFE buffer was introduced by gentle vacuum evaporation to prevent the surface of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  from injuring, and the layer would evaporate gradually during the deposition process on account of absorbing heat from bombardment. As a consequence, the inserted PTFE would not influence the structure and performance of our all-metal-oxide perovskite solar cells (Sun et al., 2016).

In this work, we employed   $  NiO_{x}  $   and ZnO as charge hole transport layer and electron transport layer, respectively, in inverted structured PSC like FTO (F doped   $  SnO_{2}  $  )/  $  NiO_{x}  $   (HTL)/perovskite absorber material/ZnO (ETL)/Ag (p-i-n). We have ever reported that Cu doping in   $  NiO_{x}  $   layer (Cu:  $  NiO_{x}  $  ) by facile co-sputtering would lead to improved hole transport capacity. A PTFE buffer layer was introduced to reduce the damage caused by ion bombardment during ZnO deposition. Through carefully adjusting the properties of   $  NiO_{x}  $   and ZnO layers for balanced charge transport efficiency, a significantly enhanced PCE was achieved. On account of ETL and HTL engineering, carrier accumulation and recombination were suppressed by accelerated and balanced transportation and separation efficiency. Furthermore, the device with interface engineering showed greatly improved stability under ambient condition.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Substrates preparation and HTL deposition

FTO glasses used as transparent conductive substrates were cleaned by deionized water, acetone and ethanol respectively for 30 min and then blow-dried by pressurized   $  N_{2}  $  . Cu doped   $  NiO_{x}  $   were directly magnetron sputtered on FTO glasses with Copper and Nickel targets (99.99% purity) at room temperature.   $  Cu:NiO_{x}  $   films of different thickness was prepared and named as   $  Cu:NiO_{x}-1  $   (15 nm),   $  Cu:NiO_{x}-2  $   (25 nm) and   $  Cu:NiO_{x}-3  $   (40 nm) according to their depositing times (5 min, 10 min and 15 min). In this work, the intersection angle between the target and the substrates was fixed at 37°. During the sputtering process, the initial pressure of the working chamber was below   $  4 \times 10^{-4}  $   Pa and the working pressure was 1.5 Pa. The power on nickel target was 300 W for 2 min, while the doping power exerted on copper target was 15 W. The mixed gas flow atmosphere was composed of 35 sccm Ar and 5 sccm   $  O_{2}  $  .

# 2.2. Fabrication of perovskite solar cells

$  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  films were prepared with a precursor solution containing 1.5 M  $  PbI_{2}  $  (Sigma-Aldrich, 99%), 0.477 g  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  (TCI, 99%), 0.213 mL diethyl sulphoxide (DMSO, Sigma-Aldrich, 99%) and 1.905 mL anhydrous N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF, Sigma-Aldrich, 99%). 30  $  \mu  $  L perovskite solution was casted on  $  NiO_{x}  $  film at 3000 rpm, during which 0.5 mL ethyl ether was continuously dripped. Dripping process started at 7th s after the substrate rotation and remaining about 2 s. The preparation of perovskite films in this work was fulfilled at dry room at 17 °C, and the relative humidity was less than 1%. As the temperature increases, the dripping process should be started advanced and the remaining time should be reduced. While the pressure rises, the opposite is true. The detailed parameters require specific experiments to determine.

# 2.3. ZnO ETL deposition

A PTFE layer was thermally evaporated on the perovskite. A tungsten basket was used as a heating source and a quartz crucible (diameter: 8 mm) was used as the container for the PTFE powder (1 mm, Sigma Aldrich). The pressure was maintained at  $ \sim 10^{-3}\mathrm{Pa} $  during the deposition. The operation current was gradually increased to  $ 56\mathrm{A} $  with a ramp rate of  $ 5\% $ . The shutter was not open until the stabilization of PTFE evaporation. The evaporation lasted for  $ 5\mathrm{min} $  yielding a mesoporous PTFE layer (Shao et al., 2014). Then ZnO was deposited by magnetron sputtering with Zinc target. The work power and time was  $ 100\mathrm{W} $  and 5 min with a mixed Ar and  $ \mathrm{O}_2 $  flow atmosphere (35:5). The other conditions were the same as that of preparing HTL. Based on different sputtering time, ZnO films of different thickness were prepared as ZnO-1 (10 min,  $ 20\mathrm{nm} $ ), ZnO-2 (15 min,  $ 30\mathrm{nm} $ ), ZnO-3 (20 min,  $ 50\mathrm{nm} $ ) and ZnO-4 (25 min,  $ 70\mathrm{nm} $ ). Thereafter, a 80-nm-thick silver act as the counter electrode was thermally evaporated on the top of ZnO through a metal shadow mask, with an active area of  $ 0.2\mathrm{cm}^2 $  in keeping with Seok's work (Lee et al., 2019).

# 2.4. Device characterization

Field emission scanning electron microscope (FEI, Magellan 400) to investigate the surface and cross-sectional morphology of ETLs and HTLs. Atomic force microscopy (AFM, SII Nano Technology Ltd., Nanonavi) was used to measure the surface topography of each film in noncontact mode. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was employed to analyze the elementary composition of the ZnO and Cu: NiO  $  _{x}  $  films with monochromated Al K  $  \alpha  $  radiation at a pass energy of 29.4 eV. The UV–vis transmittance spectra (HITACHI U-3010) were used to measure the optical properties and steady photoluminescence (PL, Horiba-Ltd. FluoroMax-4) were applied at room temperature with an

# 3. Results and discussion

The AFM images (Fig. 1) of the magnetron sputtered  $ \mathrm{Cu:NiO_x} $  film and ZnO film on FTO substrates presented smooth and continuous surface with root-mean-square (RMS) roughness of  $ 2.912\mathrm{nm} $  and 2.783 nm, respectively. The low roughness resulted from the high energy ions bombardment during sputtering process. The  $ \mathrm{Cu:NiO_x} $  and ZnO films were served as hole transport layer and electron transport layer, respectively, and both of them described high-quality metal oxide films grown by magnetron sputtering. It was convinced that the smooth surface of charge transport layers would result in a good electric contact with the perovskite absorber material. Conductive atomic force microscopy was also utilized to evaluate the carrier transport loss of  $ \mathrm{Cu:NiO_x} $  film and ZnO film. As presented in Fig. 2, both the  $ \mathrm{Cu:NiO_x} $  film and ZnO film on FTO with bias from  $ -1 $  to  $ 1\mathrm{V} $  shown good charge transport capacity.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, long-term stable PSCs based on sputtered all-metal-oxide charge transport layers have been successfully developed. Such layers could effectively block all pathways of water and oxygen, endowing the devices with considerably moisture stability under ambient conditions. According to the evidence from the PL, TRPL and performance tests, the balanced charge transport efficiency between ETL and HTL could effectively suppress the recombination in the interface and improve the charge transport efficiency. As a result, the device with optimized thickness of the charge transport layers could achieve a PCE of 16.51% with negligible hysteresis and retain 90% of its original PCE after being exposed in ambient conditions for half a year. Hence, our research proposed an efficient and facile strategy to prepare air-stable PSCs with relative good performance.
