Abstract: Stability, as an important factor limiting the practical application of perovskite solar cells, has attracted many scientists. Herein, we introduce a hydrophobic molecule of thiosalicylic acid as a passivator to enhance the device stability. The strategy not only improves the thermal stability and anti-moisture of the perovskite film by double anchoring of functional groups to uncoordinated lead ions and the hydrophobicity of the aromatic ring, but also enhances the electronic properties by removing deep trap states of the perovskite film. Further research on the perovskite solar cells with thiosalicylic acid passivation shows that the efficiency of the device can reach  $ 18.07\% $  and good stability. When stored in the air with

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic metal halide perovskites have become the star materials in the new generation of thin film photovoltaic devices due to their advantages of high absorption coefficient, long carrier life, and adjustable band gap [1-4]. Based on the star materials, the photoelectric conversion efficiency (PCE) of the perovskite solar cell (PSC) has experienced an unprecedented rise from initial  $ 3.8\% $  to a certified value of  $ 25.2\% $  [5]. Surprisingly, some works have been reported on the commercial application of PSCs [6,7]. However, the long-term stability of the device seriously restricts its market orientation [8]. The structural defects of the polycrystalline perovskite film (such as the lack of ion coordination) are the important factors on the long-term stability of the device [9], which cause the degradation of perovskite and lead to the loss of non-radiation charge recombination [10,11]. Currently, some literatures reported on that the lack of ion coordination was mainly caused by the ions migration, iodide oxidation or methylammonium iodide evaporation [12,13]. Therefore, it is necessary to find a simple and efficient method to compensate for the lack of ion coordination and other structural defects.

It is considered to be one of the most effective methods to neutralize the surface charge defects by using passivators in PSCs. At present, various passivating agents (such as lewis

2

acid, lewis base, quantum dots, and metal oxide) are used to passivate the perovskite surface [14-20]. For instance, Li et al. used a functional group of -COOH to coordinate with the uncoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2+}} $  ions, so as to improve the performance of devices [15]. Because the carboxyl and amino groups could passivate the defects and the aromatic structure through electrostatic action could reduce the related defeats of iodine, Yang et al. used D-4-tert-butylphenylalanine as the passivator to improve PCE and stability of devices [16]. However, the weak interaction between the most passivating molecules and the uncoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2+}} $  ions is easily destroyed. Zhang et al. reported on that the efficiency and stability of PSCs could be greatly enhanced by the bidentate anchoring effect of 2-mercaptopyridine for the  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2+}} $  ions [18], which provided a new idea for selecting more efficient passivating molecules.

In this paper, we firstly used a thiosalicylic acid (TSA) as the passivator to prepare the perovskite films by a two-step method. By utilizing the double anchoring effects between the mercapto and carboxyl groups of TSA molecule and the  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2+}} $  ions, the thermal stability of the perovskite film could be significantly improved. In addition, the aromatic ring as an hydrophobic functional group in TSA could improve the moisture resistance of the film. Furthermore, TSA passivated films could remove deep trap states and then improve the electronic properties. These experiments fully proved that TSA passivated films could significantly improve the stability and PCE of perovskite devices.

# 2. Experimental

Materials: Ethanol, isopropyl alcohol (IPA), thiosalicylic acid (TSA), N,N-dimethyl

formamide (DMF), N,N-dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and acetonitrile were got from Aladdin. 4-tert-butyl-pyridine (TBP) and lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulphonyl) imide (Li-TFSI) were bought from Aldrich. Chlorobenzene and lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2) $  were purchased from the Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. Spiro-OMeTAD was purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. The above mentioned agents were used directly without any treatment. The  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  compact layer solution,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  paste, methylammonium acetate (MAAc), and methylamine iodide (MAI) were prepared according to our previous results [21-23].

Preparation of the hole transporting layer solution: First, a lithium solution was received by dissolving 11.24 mg of Li-TFSI in 33.50 μL TBP and 21.50 μL acetonitrile. Then, 46.00 μL lithium solution and 73.00 mg Spiro-OMeTAD were added into 1.00 mL of chlorobenzene to form the final hole transporting layer solution.

Device fabrication: Etched FTO glasses were ultrasonic washed with soap water, deionized water, and ethanol, successively. The   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer solution was spin-coated (500 rpm, 9 s; 2500 rpm, 30 s) to prepare   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer on a cleaned FTO glass and followed annealing at 450 °C for 30 min [21]. Then, the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was obtained by spin-coating (500 rpm, 9 s; 2500 rpm, 30 s) the   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste and annealed at 450 °C for 30 min [22]. For the perovskite layer, firstly, 1.00 mol   $  L^{-1}  $     $  PbI_{2}  $   (  $  V_{DMF}: V_{DMSO} = 7:3  $  ) with 9% MAAc (molar ratio) precursor solution was spin-coated (500 rpm, 6 s; 2000 rpm, 30 s) onto the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer and heated on a hotplate at 100 °C for 10 min. After cooling to room temperature, 10 mg   $  mL^{-1}  $   MAI in IPA was infiltrated onto the   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer for 1 min and spin-coated (2000 rpm, 10 s), followed by a 10 min annealing at 100 °C. For the surface passivation layer, the fresh 0.00, 2.50, 5.00 and 7.50 mmol   $  L^{-1}  $   TSA in IPA was used by

spin-coating (500 rpm, 6 s; 2500 rpm, 20 s) and then annealed at 100 °C for 5 min. The hole transport layer was achieved by spin-coating (500 rpm, 6 s; 2000 rpm, 20 s) the hole transport layer solution on the surface of passivation layer. After completing all the air spin-coating steps, the 200 nm silver (Ag) electrode was deposited by a vacuum evaporation.

Characterizations and measurements: Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra were obtained by a spectrometer of BRUKER TENSOR 27. A field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM, JEOL-JSM-7900F) was used to investigate the morphology of perovskite films. Contact angles were measured on a contact angle meter CA-100C (Shanghai Innuo Precision Instruments CO., LTD).

Fig. 3 (A) shows the molecular structure of TSA and the FTIR spectra of the interaction between TSA and perovskite film. In the FTIR spectra, the giant peak at  $ 3400~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $  of the perovskite film passivated by TSA can be attributed to the stretching vibration of N-H in the initial perovskite [25]. We observe that the characteristic absorption peak of S-H in pure TSA locates at  $ 2517~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ , but it disappears in the passivated perovskite film. The disappearance of the S-H group characteristic absorption peak indicates that the TSA molecule interacts with the perovskite surface by breaking the S-H bond. Moreover, the characteristic absorption peak of  $ \mathrm{C = O} $  in TSA moves from  $ 1678~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $  to  $ 1618~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ . The reason of the  $ \mathrm{C = O} $  peak shifting to a lower wavenumber is that the lone electron pair of O on  $ \mathrm{C = O} $  can coordinate

According to the above analysis, there are two main modes of TSA's interaction with the perovskite: (i) The S atom of S-H coordinates with the uncoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2+}} $  ion, which leads to the disappearance of the characteristic absorption peak of S-H [26]; (ii) The coordination between the O atoms of C=O and the uncoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2+}} $  ions can lead to the characteristic absorption peak of C=O moving towards lower wavenumber [15,21]. With the increase of TSA concentration, the short-circuit current density ( $ J_{\text{SC}} $ ),  $ V_{\text{OC}} $ , filling factor (FF), and PCE increased from 23.13 to 23.65 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , 1.05 to 1.09 V, 66.88% to 70.10%, and 16.24% to 18.07%, respectively. Further increasing the TSA concentration would decrease the photovoltaic parameters, which might be due to that the too thick passivation layer could hinder the transmission of the carrier from the perovskite to the hole transport layer [24].

Fig. 8 (D) records the PCE values of the PSCs without sealing for 15 days to assess the device stability. The devices were stored in the air with a relative humidity of  $ 30\% $ . After 15 days, the PCE of the PSC with TSA passivation can reach  $ 16.26\% $  (89.98% of its initial value), while the PCE of the reference device without TSA passivation remains only  $ 12.67\% $  (78.02% of the initial value). The result demonstrates that TSA molecules with the S and O atoms and the hydrophobicity of aromatic ring could significantly enhance the stability of PSC devices.

Through the above analyses, the mechanism of TSA passivation for the perovskite films might be following: On the one hand, the S and O atoms of TSA could simultaneously coordinate with the uncoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  ions, removing the deep trap states and making the anchoring effect on  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  ions more stable; On the other hand, the hydrophobic group of the aromatic ring from the TSA could prevent the perovskite from decomposing by water molecules [21,32]. In summary, the excellent photovoltaic performance and stability is due to the effective passivation process.

# 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, the introduction of TSA passivation layer into PSCs could effectively

Journal Pre-proof

enhance the device performances. The   $  V_{OC}  $   of the device passivated by TSA is significantly increased from 1.05 to 1.09 V, resulting in a PCE of 18.07%. Moreover, the initial efficiency of the passivated device could still maintain at 89.98% after being stored for 15 days in the air with a relative humidity of 30%, while that of the untreated device only maintain at 78.02% of its initial value. The performance improvement of the device with TSA passivation could be attributed to the S and O atoms of TSA could simultaneously coordinate with the uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions, which can eliminate the deep trap states and make the anchoring effect on   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions more stable. Besides, the aromatic ring of TSA as the hydrophobic group could prevent the perovskite from decomposing by water molecules. Based on above, our method provides a new direction for the further design of organic molecules with multiple effects to improve the stability and efficiency of organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite. By this simple, easy to control, and effective passivation method, we could improve the stability of commercially available PSCs easily, which would give a novel feasible solution to improve the photovoltaic properties of PSCs.
