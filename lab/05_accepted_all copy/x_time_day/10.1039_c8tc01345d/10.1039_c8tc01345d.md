# Efficient and Stable Mixed Perovskite Solar Cells using P3HT as Hole Transporting Layer

Peng Zhou,   $  ^{ia}  $   Tongle Bu,   $  ^{ia}  $   Shengwei Shi,   $  ^{a}  $   Lingfeng Li,   $  ^{a}  $   Yulong Zhang,   $  ^{a}  $   Zhiliang Ku,   $  ^{a}  $   Yong Peng,   $  ^{a}  $   Jie Zhong,   $  ^{*a}  $   Yi-Bing Cheng,   $  ^{a,b}  $   and Fuzhi Huang   $  ^{*a}  $

Inorganic-organic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have drawn great attention in the past several years. As the stability of the PSCs has been the major obstacle for the commercialization, a lot of work have been focused on improving the long-term stability. Here we reported a meso-structured mixed PSC employing poly(3-hexylthiophene-2,5-diyl) (P3HT) as hole transport layer (HTL) showing a stable performance even after one-year exposure to air. The impact of different HTL thickness on the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the PSCs has been investigated. The performance of the PSCs was further improved by doping Li-salt/4-tert-butylpyridine to the P3HT HTL. A maximal PCE of  $ 17.55\% $  was achieved, superior to  $ 14.30\% $  of the pristine P3HT-based devices. The effect of the dopants on the stability of the devices has also been studied.

# Introduction

In the past few years, metal-halide hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have drawn great attention because of their rapid increase in power conversion efficiency (PCE). Perovskites have a common formula of ABX $ _3 $ , where A, B and X represent organic cations (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3^+ $ , MA or NH=CHNH $ _3^+ $ , FA) or Cs $ ^+ $ , metal cations (Sn $ ^{2+} $ , Pb $ ^{2+} $ ) and halide anions (I $ ^- $ , Br $ ^- $ , Cl $ ^- $ ), respectively. The efficiency of PSCs has been improved from 3.8% $  ^1  $  to 22.7% $  ^2  $  since 2009, as perovskites have excellent mobility of charge carriers (>10 cm $  ^2  $  V $  ^{-1}  $  s $  ^{-1}  $ ), long electron-hole diffusion length (>1 μm) $  ^3  $  and low exciton binding energy (<10 meV) $  ^4  $ . PSCs have shown promising prospect of commercialization, but there are still several problems to be addressed. For example, to scale up from the lab-scale devices is one of the challenges for the community. $  ^{5-7}  $  Another key problem for the commercialization is the stability. It has reported that the degradation of the perovskite was influenced by four key factors: UV light, solution processing, thermal effects and the moisture in the air. $  ^8  $  The moisture effect could be eliminated by proper sealing method, $  ^9  $  while to address the other factors, it needs to optimize the materials, such as perovskite materials, electron transport materials, and hole transport materials (HTMs). Jeon and co-workers $  ^{10}  $  utilized CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ Pb(I $ _{1-x} $ Br $ _x $ ) as perovskite material and showed a great resistance in

atmosphere environment. The Br  $  ^{-}  $  additive is considered to improve the stability. Similarly, Noh and co-workers  $  ^{11}  $  demonstrated that  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.4}Br_{0.6}  $  and  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.13}Br_{0.87}  $  presented a great stability at the humidity of 55% RH. Later, mixed  $  Cs^{+}  $ ,  $  Rb^{+}  $ ,  $  Br^{-}  $  and  $  FA^{+}  $  in  $  MAPbI_{3}  $  have been proved enhancing the stability and efficiency of the PSCs.  $  ^{12-15}  $  Besides the perovskite materials, the HTMs also affect the stability of the devices. Spiro-OMeTAD is one of the most common HTMs in PSCs. Unfortunately, the pristine Spiro-OMeTAD possesses an inherent low hole mobility (  $  \approx 10^{-4}  $  cm  $  ^{2}  $  V  $  ^{-1}  $  s  $  ^{-1}  $  ) and poor conductivity due to its triangular pyramid configuration and large intermolecular distance.  $  ^{16}  $  Therefore, normally some dopants are added into Spiro-OMeTAD to improve the hole mobility,  $  ^{17-18}  $  such as lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI)  $  ^{19}  $  and cobaltic complexes  $  ^{20}  $ . However, the ionic salts added are usually hygroscopic and thus would decompose the perovskite film which is sensitive to moisture.  $  ^{21}  $  4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP), another dopant commonly used in Spiro-OMeTAD, can also react with perovskite and decompose it into  $  PbI_{2}  $ .  $  ^{22}  $  There is a tendency to develop a dopant-free HTM in consideration of the stability of devices.  $  ^{23-25}  $

P3HT is a commonly used HTM in organic solar cells  $  ^{26}  $  with relatively high hole mobility and great stability.  $  ^{27-28}  $  This polymer is also a dopant-free HTM and thus could be a suitable choice for stable PSCs. The PCE of P3HT based PSCs first reported by Cahen and co-workers  $  ^{29}  $  was only 0.52% combined with MAPbBr  $  _{3}  $ . Giacomo and co-workers  $  ^{30}  $  demonstrated a P3HT based PSC with CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $  as light absorber and reached a PCE of 9.3%. Abbas et al.  $  ^{31}  $  reported a planar heterojunction PSC with a PCE of 13.7% using vacuum deposited perovskite film. Xiao and co-workers  $  ^{32}  $  used a π-conjugated carbon material, graphdiyne (GD), as the dopant to

Thus by combining the mixed perovskite with P3HT, it may open a way to address the stability issue of the PSCs. Here we prepared a meso-structured device with pristine P3HT and FAPbI $ _3 $ /MAPbBr $ _3 $  mixed perovskite that showed a good stability in ambient atmosphere even for one year. Instead of toxic chlorobenzene, ethyl acetate (EA) was used as an anti-solvent to form a more compact perovskite film with large crystals. $ ^7 $  The electron transport layer (ETL) consisted of a compact TiO $ _2 $  (c-TiO $ _2 $ ) layer and a meso-porous TiO $ _2 $  (m-TiO $ _2 $ ) layer. The aperture and abundant interface between m-TiO $ _2 $  and perovskite can commendably protect perovskite films. A PCE of 14.30% was achieved in the pristine P3HT devices and then a little mount of Li-TFSI was added into the HTM to improve the carrier density of the HTM $ ^{35} $  and achieved a relatively high PCE of 17.55%. The devices were stored in a dark environment under 25 °C, 25% RH to evaluate the long-term stability.

To evaluate the stability of the devices, typical devices were stored in the dark environment under 25 °C, 25% RH for one year. As is shown in Fig. 5, the device with the pristine P3HT obviously shows a better stability. The PCE of the device using the pristine P3HT decreased to about 80% of the best value after 700 hours and then remained relatively stable within 1 year. Due to the hygroscopicity of Li-TFSI, the efficiencies of the H2 device decreased apparently in the long-term storage.

Nevertheless, as is mentioned above, the  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{MAPbBr}_3 $  mixed perovskite possesses resistance to moisture and thereby reducing the influence of Li-TFSI. The final PCE of H2 decreased to nearly 0 after 160 days. A compared device fabricated with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and Li-doped P3HT degraded completely after only 3 days. The hydrophobic P3HT on the perovskite film can well protect it and thus keep a long-term stability. To further improve the performance of devices and preserve the excellent stability, a harmless dopant without hygroscopicity and corrosivity should be employed in future.

# Conclusions

The P3HT has been optimized to be used as an effective HTL for the mixed PSCs. According to the 1-year observation of these devices, we proved the outstanding stability of the structure: dense  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / m - TiO_2 / (FAPbI_3)_{0.85}(MAPbBr_3)_{0.15} / P3HT} $ . After a slow decay process in the first dozens of days, the performance of the devices with pristine P3HT maintained a relatively stable level and could keep more than 1 year. Li-TFSI can improve the performance of devices but impair the stability. Thus, it is necessary to develop proper dopants for P3HT in order to improve both the stability and the PCE of the PSCs.
