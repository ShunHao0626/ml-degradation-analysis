# 1. Introduction

In order to reduce the dependence on traditional fossil energy, photovoltaic devices which can generate electricity directly from nature sunlight have been extensively studied during the past few decades. Among them, perovskite based solar cell (PSC) has attracted great attention due to its high power conversion efficiency (with certified 22.1%)[1]. The PSC also takes a lot of other advantages such as low cost [2] and easy to be used as flexible devices [3,4]. In general, there are three main device architectures of PSC including mesoscopic structure [5], planar heterojunction structure [6] and meso-superstructure [7]. No matter which kind of cell structure, electron transport layer (ETL) always plays quite important roles such as charge separation and transport in PSCs. The morphology and the physical/chemical characteristics of the ETL have a great impact on the quality of deposited perovstike film and the charge transport at the ETL/perovskite interface, which are always crucial to the PSC performance [8,9]. So far, the perovskite solar cell based on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  has reached a power conversion efficiency (PCE) exceeding  $ 20\% $  [1].

Besides customary   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanostructures [10], other n-type semiconductors such as ZnO [11,12],   $  WO_{x}[13]  $   can also be used as ETL in PSCs. As a typical oxide semiconductor, ZnO has several advantages including suitable bandgap (  $  \sim 3.4 eV  $  )[11], high transparency [11],

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

Lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ , 99%) and Methylammonium iodide (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I, 99%) were purchased from Xi'an p-OLED technology Crop. (China). Co-doped 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiroOMeTAD) solution was purchased from Shanghai Materwin New Materials Co., Ltd. Zinc acetate dihydrate (Zn (CH $ _3 $ COO) $ _2\cdot $ 2H $ _2 $ O; > 95%), Dimethyformamide (DMF; > 99%), isopropanol (> 99%). Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass substrates (2.2 mm, 14  $ \Omega $ /sq sheet resistivity, transmittance: 90%, Nippon Sheet Glass, Japan) were obtained from Wuhan Jinge-Solar Energy Technology Co.,Ltd. All other solvents and chemicals were purchased from commercial suppliers.

# 2.2. Synthesis of ZnO nanorod arrays (ZNAs)

The ZNAs were synthesized directly on the fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass substrates (14 Ω/sq sheet resistivity) using a PVP soft template method as reported by Zhong [22] previously. Briefly, FTO substrates were first cleaned ultrasonically in deionized water, ethyl alcohol and acetone respectively, and then dried with nitrogen. ZnO seed layer was deposited on the FTO substrates by spin-coating precursor solution(0.1317 g zinc acetate dihydrate   $  (\mathrm{Zn}(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{COO})_{2}\cdot2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O})  $   dissolved in 20 ml ethanol) and subsequently annealed at 150 °C for 15 min. This process was repeated three times and finally the substrates were annealed at 350 °C for another 30 min.

Then, two different kinds of solutions were prepared. Solution A was prepared by dissolving zinc acetate dihydrate (Zn   $  (\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{COO})_{2}\cdot2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}  $  ) in 54.7 ml deionized water and solution B was prepared by dissolving Polyrinylpyrrolidone (PVP) in 100 ml deionized water. After PVP was dissolved completely, the two solution were mixed at 60 °C under constant stirring. Meanwhile ammonia   $  (\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O})  $   was added to adjust the pH value of the mixture solution. Then, the film substrates were immersed upside down in the growth solution at 99 °C for different times. Finally, these films were annealed in the tube furnace in oxygen atmosphere under 420 °C for 30 min.

# 2.3. Synthesis of  $ \mathrm{ZnO / TiO_2} $  heterostructured nanorod arrays

$ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  shells were grown on the surface of  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  nanorods by a sol-gel method. Firstly, the FTO/ZNAs samples were placed upside down in a mixed solution of Butyl titanate and isopropanol. A hydrolysis reaction was initiated by adding  $ 500\mu \mathrm{l} $  water. # 2.4. Perovskite film and cell fabrication

The whole fabrication process was carried out in ambient atmosphere with relative humidity of 55%. Firstly, 1.386 g PbI  $  _{2}  $  was dissolved in N,N-dimethylformamide under stirring at 70 °C. The solution was kept at 70 °C during the whole fabrication procedure. Then the PbI  $  _{2}  $  solution was infiltrated into the ZnO-TiO  $  _{2}  $  core-shell nanorod arrays by spin-coating PbI  $  _{2}  $  solution at 4000 rpm for 90 s and dried at 70 °C for 30 min. Then the films were dipped in a solution of CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  I in isopropanol (10 mg/ml) to covert PbI  $  _{2}  $  to CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $ . After that, the films were dried at 70 °C for 30 min. The hole transport layer (HTL) was prepared by spin-coating 50  $  \mu  $  l co-doped spiroMeOTAD solution on the perovskite film. Finally, an 80 nm thick gold layer was thermally evaporated on the HTL to form the back contact.

# 2.5. Characterization

The morphologies of the film samples with silicon as substrate were characterized by using SU8010 field emission scanning electron microscope operating at accelerating voltage of 10.0 kV. The crystal structures and phase identifications of the samples were performed with a D2 PHASER X-ray diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda = 1.54056 \AA  $  ). The UV–visible absorption spectra of the samples were collected by using a UV-3600 spectrophotometer. Fluorescence investigations of the perovskite films deposited on quartz glasses and ZnO-TiO  $  _{2}  $   nanorod arrays were represented by using a FL3-211-P fluorophotometer. Current density-voltage (J-V) characteristics and the stability of the PSC were measured under AM 1.5 G one sun (100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) illumination by using a solar simulator. The spectra of the device incident photoelectron conversion efficiency (IPCE) were measured by a solar cell quantum efficiency measurement system (Zahner-cimps, photo-electrochemical workstation).

# 3.4. PL spectra analysis

The band to band direct transition was also investigated by photoluminescence measurement. Fig. 7 shows the PL spectra of the films of ZnO nanorod arrays with different length. All of these ZnO films were excited at the wavelength of 325 nm at room temperature. The observed strong emission peak located at   $  \sim400  $   nm is attributed to the near band-edge (NBE) transition of ZnO materials [20]. Another weak red emission peak located in the range of 760–780 nm is also observed for all of these ZnO films and eventually leads to the second-order excitonic emission [23]. The broad band varied from 500 nm to 700 nm is related to the deep level and trap-state emission caused by the defects in ZnO crystals [22]. The PL intensity of the intrinsic emission peak at 380 nm increased when the length of ZnO nanorod arrays increased from 350 nm to 600 nm and then decreased with a further increasing of the length of ZnO nanorods from 600 nm to 2000 nm. On the contrary, the PL intensity of emission peak at 570 nm firstly decreased while the length of nanorod increased from 350 nm to 600 nm and then increased again with the further increasing of the length of nanorod from 600 nm to 2000 nm. The ZnO nanorod arrays with the nanorod length of 600 nm shows the strong ultraviolet band emission and relatively weak visible band emission, indicating a relatively low concentration of deep level defects in ZnO nanorods and ensuring a high electron transfer and low formation of carrier recombination center [22]. Furthermore, the PL spectra of the films of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   deposited on quartz glasses and   $  ZnO-TiO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays with different length exited at the wavelength of 525 nm was shown in Fig. 8. A high PL intensity was observed for perovskite/quartz sample because of its high crystal quality. After the deposition of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   into   $  ZnO-TiO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays, the PL intensity decreased obviously, revealing the efficient carrier transport at the interface between   $  ZnO-TiO_{2}  $   nanorods and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $ . Moreover, the PL intensity of   $  ZnO-TiO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays/  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film decreased in a continual manner as the length of   $  ZnO-TiO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays increased from 350 nm to 600 nm. With a further increase of the length of   $  ZnO-TiO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays from 600 nm to 2000 nm, the PL intensity increased. The decrease of the PL intensity indicates a higher carrier transport efficiency, longer carrier lifetime and a lower charge recombination rate at the   $  ZnO-TiO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays/  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   interface. The carrier transport efficiency reaches the highest when the length of   $  ZnO-TiO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays is 600 nm. The variation tendency of the PL intensity of the films is consistent to the variation trend of short-circuit current density as shown in Fig. 10.

# 3.5. Device structure and performance

With this special ETL, the structure of the PSC is shown in Fig. 9(a). The ZnO layer is expected to play a role in preventing direct contact between FTO and Spiro-MeOTAD. The energy level diagram is exhibited in Fig. 9(b). Free electrons can be generated from the perovskite absorber layer and extracted by ZnO-TiO2 nanorod arrays under illumination condition. Fig. 10 shows the  $ J-V $  curse of the PSC based on  $ \mathrm{ZnO - TiO_2} $  nanorod arrays with different length. Specific photovoltaic performance average parameters are summarized in Table 1. The short-circuit current density  $ (J_{sc}) $  first increases from  $ 17.29\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $  to  $ 19.97\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $  when the length of  $ \mathrm{ZnO - TiO_2} $  nanorod arrays increases from  $ 350\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 600\mathrm{nm} $  and then decreases from  $ 19.97\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $  to  $ 16.11\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $  with a further increasing length of  $ \mathrm{ZnO - TiO_2} $  nanorod arrays from  $ 600\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 2000\mathrm{nm} $ . Analogously, the open-circuit voltage  $ (V_{oc}) $  of the PSC first increases from  $ 0.84\mathrm{V} $  to  $ 0.91\mathrm{V} $  when the length of  $ \mathrm{ZnO - TiO_2} $  nanorods increasing from  $ 350\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 600\mathrm{nm} $  and then decreases to  $ 0.83\mathrm{V} $  when the length of  $ \mathrm{ZnO - TiO_2} $  nanorods reaches  $ 2000\mathrm{nm} $ . Thus, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the PSC increases from  $ 7.24\% $  to  $ 10.24\% $  with the increase of the length of  $ \mathrm{ZnO - } $   $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanorods from  $ 350\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 600\mathrm{nm} $ . After that, the PCE decreases to  $ 7.01\% $  with a further increasing of the length of  $ \mathrm{ZnO - TiO_2} $  nanorods to  $ 2000\mathrm{nm} $ . When the length of  $ \mathrm{ZnO - TiO_2} $  nanorods reaches  $ 600\mathrm{nm} $ , the PCE reaches the highest  $ (10.24\%) $ . Compared with  $ V_{oc}, J_{sc} $  is much strongly dependent on the length of  $ \mathrm{ZnO - TiO_2} $  nanorods, which can be attributed to the higher light harvesting efficiency owing to that a larger amount of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  were loaded into the  $ \mathrm{ZnO - TiO_2} $  nanorod arrays with a larger surface area. In general, the longer the length of nanorods, the more the loading of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite, and the more the harvesting of sun light, which is beneficial for the cell performance. However, after  $ J_{sc} $  reaches a maximum of  $ 19.97\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $ , it decreases slightly to  $ 16.11\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $  with the further increase of the length of  $ \mathrm{ZnO - TiO_2} $  nanorods from  $ 600\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 2000\mathrm{nm} $ , which is caused by the increased charge recombination as discussed in morphology analysis and PL spectra analysis. Furthermore, the  $ V_{oc} $  decreased from  $ 0.91\mathrm{V} $  to  $ 0.83\mathrm{V} $  as the length of  $ \mathrm{ZnO - TiO_2} $  nanorods increased from  $ 850\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 2000\mathrm{nm} $ . We also compared the device performance with those reported previously in other literatures which use ZnO as electron transport

The PCE obtained in this article finally reaches a best of 10.24%, which is higher than the mean level of those of perovskite solar cell which uses ZnO nanostructure system as ETL in previous report [11,12,15,16,24–27], as can be seen in Table 2. However, the PCE value is also relatively low. We believe there are two main reasons. First, the carrier lifetime of the PSC based on ZnO nanorods (  $  10^{-5}-10^{-3}  $  s) is much shorter than that of the PSC based on mesoporous  $  TiO_{2}  $  (  $  10^{-2}-10^{\circ}s  $  ) [12]. Such a short carrier lifetime can cause serious charge recombination, which is definitely bad for the cell performance. Another reason is that the whole device fabrication process was carried out in ambient atmosphere. It is well known that both perovskite  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  and Co-doped Spiro-OMeTAD materials are very sensitive to water and oxygen. During the ambient preparation process, the moisture and oxygen can directly react with  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  film and drive it to negative direction.  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  would be decomposed to  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  and  $  PbI_{2}  $  and the  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  film presents bad surface morphology. Moreover, pinholes and defect states in the  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  film can also cause serious charge recombination, which is definitely harmful to the device performance.

Except efficiency, the stability of the PSCs is still a serious issue that has to be resolved. In this paper, two groups of unsealed PSCs based on  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  nanorod arrays without and with  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  coating were prepared under relative humidity of  $ 40\% $ , respectively. The stabilities of these cells which were stored in ambient environment were investigated. The performances of the cells were collected under one sun AM 1.5 G illumination, as shown in Fig. 12. The PCE of  $ \mathrm{ZnO - TiO_2} $  based PSC retained  $ 80\% $  of its initial PCE after 7 days. The stability of  $ \mathrm{ZnO - TiO_2} $  based PSC is much better than that of  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  based PSC. The above evidence strongly suggests that  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  coating can greatly prevent perovskite film from reacting with  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  and significantly improve the stability of PSCs.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated effective control of the length of   $  ZnO-TiO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays by changing the hydrothermal growth time of ZnO nanorod arrays and depositing   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanocrystals on the surface of ZnO nanorods via a sol-gel method. A series of FTO/ZnO/  $  ZnO-TiO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays/  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au  $   PSCs based on   $  ZnO-TiO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays with different length were fabricated and characterized in this paper. The   $  TiO_{2}  $   shell structure can improve the contact surface area between ETL and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite. ZnO nanorod can offer a fast transport pathway for electrons and decrease the charge recombination. Moreover,   $  ZnO-TiO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays with a suitable length can not only improve the infiltration of perovskite and the quality of the deposited perovskite film, but also transport the electron and suppress the charge recombination efficiently, which eventually enhances the cell performance. For the best performance device, the power conversion efficiency reaches 10.24% in ambient atmosphere (RH~40%). The stabilities of the PSCs based on ZnO and   $  ZnO-TiO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays were also tested. The PCE of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   modified cell maintains nearly 80% of its initial level even after 7 days under ambient
