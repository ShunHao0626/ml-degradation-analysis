# Hydrophilic Fullerene Derivative Doping in Active Layer and Electron Transport Layer for Enhancing Oxygen Stability of Perovskite Solar Cells

Guiying Xu, Shuhui Wang, Pengqing Bi, Haiyang Chen, Moyao Zhang, Rongming Xue, Xiaotao Hao, Zhaokui Wang, Yaowen Li,* and Yongfang Li

# 1. Introduction

Poor stability is one of the main limiting factors for the commercialization of perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs). The degradation of perovskite films is usually triggered by the reaction of the perovskite active layer with the superoxide when exposed in ambient atmosphere, which is not prevented by simple encapsulation. Herein, an oxygen-stabilizing effect is found by utilizing a hydrophilic   $  [6,6]  $  -phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid-(3,4,5-tris(2-(2-(2-methoxyethoxy)ethoxy)ethoxy)phenyl)methanol ester (PCBB-OEG) as a dopant of the perovskite film and electron-transporting layer (ETL). PCBB-OEG accelerates photoelectron transport in perovskite films and enhances the electron-extracting ability of ETL. This process is demonstrated to efficiently prevent the reaction between electrons and oxygen to form a superoxide. Hence, the presence of PCBB-OEG in the perovskite film improves its stability against oxygen. The stability and efficiency of pero-SCs are further improved by doping PCBB-OEG in   $  [6,6]  $  -phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) ETL. As a result, the p-i-n pero-SCs with PCBB-OEG as an additive in both the perovskite active layer and ETL show the best power conversion efficiency of 20.49%. Importantly, the related device retains 98% of this initial efficiency after 60 days of storage in ambient atmosphere without encapsulation.

10 date, a great deal of encapsulation technologies have been developed to protect pero-SCs from moisture,  $  ^{[20-22]}  $  UV,  $  ^{[23]}  $  and high temperature.  $  ^{[19]}  $  However, the oxygen-induced degradation, which occurs very rapidly, is hardly to be prevented by an encapsulation strategy. Few studies have directly addressed the degradation of pero-SCs induced by oxygen. Wang and coworkers reported that the perovskite active layer such as  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  has a suitable band gap and matched band edges with the redox potential of oxygen/superoxide (  $  O_{2}/O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $  ).  $  ^{[16,24]}  $  Therefore, the photogenerated electrons can quickly transfer from the conduction band of  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  to  $  O_{2}  $  under illumination, then forming a superoxide (  $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $  ), which is then likely to react with the  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}  $  moiety of perovskite crystals and trigger degradation.  $  ^{[25-27]}  $  A possible process for  $  O_{2}  $ -induced degradation was proposed by Haque and coworkers (Figure S1, Supporting Information),  $  ^{[25]}  $  including the following four steps: 1) oxygen molecules in the environment diffuse and incorporate into the perovskite lattice; 2) the perovskite layer (e.g.,  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $ ) creates electrons and holes under illumination; 3) the oxygen molecules react with the electrons to form  $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $  in the perovskite layer; and 4)  $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $  reacts with  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  crystals which then decompose into  $  PbI_{2}  $ ,  $  H_{2}O  $ ,  $  I_{2}  $ , and  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}  $ .  $  ^{[27,28]}  $  During the abovementioned process,

Perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) present cost-effective and environmentally benign avenues to realize sustainable green energy.  $  ^{[1-5]}  $   The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of pero-SCs surged from 3.8%  $  ^{[6]}  $   to 24.2%  $  ^{[7]}  $   in the past 10 years owing to excellent features such as broad and strong absorption,  $  ^{[8-10]}  $   direct band gap,  $  ^{[11]}  $   long exciton diffusion lengths,  $  ^{[12]}  $   high charge-carrier mobilities, and unique defect tolerance.  $  ^{[13,14]}  $   Such high efficiency is remarkable for a young photovoltaic technology as it is comparable with those of the commercialized multicrystalline silicon-based photovoltaics. However, it is still very challenging to address the poor stability of perovskite films and devices in harsh conditions, such as moisture,  $  ^{[15]}  $   oxygen,  $  ^{[16]}  $   ultraviolet (UV) light,  $  ^{[17,18]}  $   and high temperature,  $  ^{[19,20]}  $   when considering their future practical applications.

$  O_{2}^{\cdot-}  $  plays a critical role in the stability of the perovskite layer, where  $  O_{2}^{\cdot-}  $  can rapidly form then easily triggered perovskite degradation even under a tungsten halogen lamp with low power  $  ^{[29]}  $  or current–voltage measurement.  $  ^{[30]}  $  Therefore, it is imperative to improve the stability of pero-SCs by reducing or hindering the generation of the  $  O_{2}^{\cdot-}  $  superoxide.

Iodide vacancies in perovskite films are demonstrated to be the preferred sites for the reaction between oxygen and electrons to accelerate the formation of   $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $  . Strategies such as controlling crystal growth  $  ^{[28]}  $   or passivating the existed defects  $  ^{[31]}  $   were proposed to improve the quality of the perovskite film, so that the generation probability of   $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $   could be reduced. In addition, Haque and coworkers found that replacing the mesoporous   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   with high-electron-mobility   $  TiO_{2}  $   as ETL could increase electron extraction ability, which undoubtedly led to a less amount of electrons existing in the perovskite film. Therefore, the generation of   $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $   could be restrained to a certain extent due to the reduced reaction probability between oxygen and electrons, leading to a dramatically improved device stability against oxygen.  $  ^{[25]}  $

In our previous work, we used a hydrophilic fullerene derivative [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid-(3,4,5-tris(2-(2-(2-methoxyethoxy)ethoxy)ethoxy)phenyl)methanol ester (PCBB-OEG) as an additive in the perovskite film, which was demonstrated to enhance the resistance to moisture. The pero-SCs based on the perovskite film with 0.1 wt% PCBB-OEG retained 98.4% of the initial PCEs after 300 h of exposure in ambient atmosphere with 70–80% relative humidity (RH), while the PCE of the pristine-perovskite-based devices degraded to 88.1% of its initial PCE. The dramatically improved stability was mainly attributed to the encapsulation effect because the PCBB-OEG could not only gradient distribute in the perovskite film but also form an ultrathin film on the surface of the perovskite film. More importantly, the end group (-OEG) of PCBB-OEG can anchor on the surface of perovskite grains by hydrogen bonding, and the C $  _{60}  $  moieties in PCBB-OEG are left outside on the surface of the perovskite film to protect the CH $  _{3}  $ NH $  _{3}  $ PbI $  _{3}  $  layer. $ ^{[32]} $  However, through this encapsulation theory, the degradation caused by oxygen is hardly to be explained.

Here, we investigate the high stability of pero-SCs doped with PCBB-OEG against oxygen in depth and propose the oxygen-stabilizing effect and mechanism for this behavior. We demonstrate that the top-down distribution of PCBB-OEG in the perovskite active layer can facilitate photogenerated electron transportation from perovskite before reacting with oxygen to form   $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $  , and the reduced generation of   $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $   can be used to explain the enhanced stability against oxygen. Based on this theory, we further used PCBB-OEG to dope [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) ETL, and the resultant PCBM:PCBB-OEG ETL-based pero-SC showed a better electron extraction ability than bare PCBM ETL. As a result, the pero-SCs based on the PCBB-OEG-doped   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   active layer and the PCBB-OEG-doped PCBM ETL showed a promising PCE of 20.49% and robust stability in ambient atmosphere without encapsulation, where a 98% of initial PCE retained after 60 days (  $  \approx  $  1500 h). In comparison, the control device degraded to 40% of its initial PCE under the same condition. Our results demonstrate a new way to improve the pero-SCs' stability against oxygen.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Photovoltaic Performance and Stability of the p-i-n Pero-SCs

Planar p-i-n pero-SCs with a configuration of indium tin oxide (ITO)/polytriarylamine (PTAA)/perovskite/C  $  _{60}  $  /bathocuproine (BCP)/copper (Cu) were employed, where the perovskite active layers were fabricated by a two-step sequential deposition method and the methylammonium iodide (MAI) precursor solution doped with or without 0.1 wt% PCBB-OEG. As a result, a gradient-distributed PCBB-OEG was incorporated in the perovskite film, and an ultrathin PCBB-OEG layer formed on top of it.  $  ^{[32]}  $   We define the perovskite film without PCBB-OEG as pero-pristine and with 0.1 wt% PCBB-OEG as pero-0.1. Figure 1a shows the current density–voltage (J-V) characteristics of pero-SCs, measured under the illumination of AM1.5G 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  . The corresponding device parameters are listed in Table 1. The pero-SCs doped with 0.1 wt% PCBB-OEG showed significantly enhanced PCE compared with that of the peropristine-based device. Moreover, pero-SCs also showed almost coincident reverse and forward scan J-V curves (Figure S2, Supporting Information), which confirmed that the p-i-n pero-SCs with or without PCBB-OEG both had weak hysteresis behavior. We then use the stabilized photocurrent density at maximum power point for 200 s to accurately evaluate their photovoltaic performance (Figure 1b). The stabilized PCEs of   $  \approx  $  19.10% for the pero-0.1-based device and 17.2% for the pero-pristine-based device confirmed the enhanced efficiency by incorporating PCBB-OEG in the perovskite film. In addition, the integrated   $  J_{sc}  $   from the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of pero-SCs (Figure S3, Supporting Information) agrees well with those obtained from J-V curves. The enhanced performance of pero-0.1-based device is attributed to the high-quality perovskite film, the improved electronic coupling and band alignment at the interface, and the reduced grain boundary trap states, as reported in our previous work.  $  ^{[32]}  $   To reveal the influence of PCBB-OEG on device stability, the unencapsulated devices were kept in ambient atmosphere with an RH of   $  \approx  $  50%. As shown in Figure 1c, the pero-0.1-based device retained 85% of its initial efficiency after 60 days (1500 h), while the control device (pero pristine) degraded to 40% of its initial PCE under the same condition.

To clarify the stabilizing effects of PCBB-OEG, the morphology evolution of pero-pristine and pero-0.1 films stored in ambient atmosphere with an RH of   $  \approx50\%  $   was measured. The top-view scanning electron microscope (SEM) images of aged perovskite films with different exposure times are displayed in Figure 2a–f. For the fresh samples, both pero-pristine (Figure 2a) and pero-0.1 (Figure 2d) films showed uniform and compact morphologies. After 30 days, some gaps and cracks were observed in the pero-pristine film (Figure 2b), while pero-0.1 films well maintained their initial morphology features (Figure 2e). With further prolonging the exposure time to 60 days, bright grains appeared in the pero-pristine film accompanied with serious gaps and cracks (Figure 2c), which means that some perovskite crystals had already decomposed. In contrast, the pero-0.1 films showed a consistent morphology even after 60 days (Figure 2f), indicating that PCBB-OEG could prevent the decomposition of perovskite crystals. # 2.2. Stabilizing Effect and the Mechanism of PCBB-OEG

As mentioned in our previous report, the improved device stability is attributed to the resistance of the perovskite film against water due to the enhanced hydrophobic perovskite surface and the formed protecting layer after incorporating PCBB-OEG.  $  ^{[36]}  $   However, the degradation caused by oxygen cannot be explained by this encapsulation strategy. As aforementioned, the decomposition of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   under oxygen may result from a reaction between the superoxide and the organic (i.e.,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}  $  ) component of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  . The degradation can be significantly reduced or even prevented by transferring the photogenerated electrons from   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   before reacting with oxygen to form a superoxide. Therefore, the significantly improved stability could be attributed to the critical role of PCBB-OEG in transferring photogenerated electrons. Hence, in the following section, we investigate the influence of photogenerated electrons' transfer ability on device stability.

# 2.2.2. Stabilizing Mechanism of PCBB-OEG

To evaluate the influence of the charge-transfer process of photogenerated electrons on the formation of the superoxide   $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $  , we employed the hydroethidine (HE) molecular fluorescent probe method to monitor the amount of   $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $  , where HE can be oxidized by the generated   $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $   producing a fluorescence peak at 610 nm when excited at 520 nm.  $  ^{[28,39]}  $   The evolution of the   $  I_{F}(t)/I_{F}(t_{0})  $   ratio along with the immersion time is shown in Figure 5a, where   $  I_{F}(t)  $   is the fluorescence intensity at time t, and   $  I_{F}(t_{0})  $   is at 0 min. The obtained   $  I_{F}(t)/I_{F}(t_{0})  $   ratio can be used to evaluate the amount of   $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $   in perovskite films. It can be found that pero-0.1 shows a much lower   $  I_{F}(t)/I_{F}(t_{0})  $   ratio than that of pero-pristine, suggesting that the PCBB-OEG molecule can significantly reduce the amount of   $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $   in perovskite films. This result may be attributed to a more efficient charge transfer of the photogenerated electrons between the perovskite and PCBB-OEG at the grain boundary and on the surface of the perovskite film. Based on above results, as shown in Figure 5b, we propose that PCBB-OEG with gradient distribution in the perovskite film, including at grain boundaries and perovskite film surfaces, could effectively reduce   $  O_{2}^{\bullet-}  $   generation due to the rapidly transferring photogenerated electrons, which can well explain the high stability of the device against oxygen in ambient atmosphere. # 2.3. Interface Engineering Using PCBB-OEG for High-Performance and High-Stability Pero-SCs

Considering the critical role of PCBB-OEG in the PCE and stability of pero-SCs, we then doped PCBM ETL using PCBB-OEG (PCBM:PCBB-OEG) to fine tune the electron-transporting properties of ETL. The lifetime distribution mapping was first used to evaluate the electron-extracting ability of PCBM:PCBB-OEG to the pero-0.1 film. As shown in Figure 6a,b, the PCBM:PCBB-OEG ETL-coated pero-0.1 film showed fluorescence decay lifetime in the range of 1226–1534 ps, whereas a 1341–1712 ps lifetime range was observed for the PCBM ETL. The decreased decay lifetime for the PCBM:PCBB-OEG-ETL-based sample indicates an improved electron-extracting ability from the pero-0.1 film to PCBM:PCBB-OEG ETL. A similar effect of the PCBB-OEG-doped ETL was also observed by TRPL spectra characterization (Figure 6c), and the corresponding fitting parameters via the biexponential decay function are summarized in Table S3, Supporting Information. As expected, the PCBM:PCBB-OEG ETL-coated pero-0.1 film showed a smaller   $  \tau_{average}  $   (1338.35 ps) than that of PCBM ETL (1542.65 ps). The abovementioned results demonstrated that the presence of PCBB-OEG in ETL such as PCBM plays an important role in improving electronic coupling at the interface between pero-0.1 and PCBM, which can facilitate the electron-extracting ability. We then fabricated the planar p-i-n pero-SCs with a configuration of ITO/PTAA/pero-0.1/PCBM:PCBB-OEG/C  $  _{60}  $  /BCP/Cu to evaluate the effect of PCBB-OEG-doped ETL on device performance. As shown in Figure 6d, the PCBM:PCBB-OEG-ETL-based pero-SCs delivered a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.08 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 23.70 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and an FF of 80.08%, giving a PCE of 20.49%. The   $  J_{sc}  $   agreed well with the value calculated from the integration of the EQE spectrum

(Figure S5, Supporting Information) within 5% deviation. The improved PCE and FF compared with those of undoped PCBM-ETL-based devices (PCE = 19.74%, FF = 77.36) are probably attributed to the enhanced electron-extracting ability of PCBM:PCBB-OEG. Meanwhile, the PCEs under forward and reverse scanning were also measured (Figure S6, Supporting Information); the coincident J-V curves means that the pero-0.1/PCBM:PCBB-OEG-based device had negligible hysteresis. In Figure S7, Supporting Information, we also measured steady-state output photocurrent to further accurately evaluate the efficiency and hysteresis behavior. The device shows a fast rising photocurrent to maximum value, and delivered a photocurrent of 22.25 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , corresponding to a PCE of 20.03% which is very close to the values from both reverse and forward bias scans.

When storing the pero-SCs in ambient atmosphere with an RH of   $  \approx50\%  $  , the PCBM:PCBB-OEG-based device also showed robust stability. Figure 6e shows the normalized PCE of pero-SCs plotted against the aging time. It can be found that a 98% of initial PCE for the device based on PCBM:PCBB-OEG ETL and pero-0.1 active layer can be retained after 60 days, which is superior than those of pero-SCs with undoped ETL. We also measured the unencapsulated device stability under continuous white-light LED illumination, which could accelerate the generation of superoxides and the oxygen-induced degradation of perovskites. The pero-pristine-based devices in this aging test lost 50% of its initial efficiency after 120 h of exposure, whereas the stability of the pero-0.1-based device was improved. Meanwhile the pero-0.1/PCBM:PCBB-OEG devices presented the best stability; its efficiency can be maintained at 70% after continuous illumination for 120 h. Such behavior can be explained in that both the PCBB-OEG-doped perovskite active layer and the PCBB-OEG-doped PCBM ETL facilitates photogenerated electron extraction from the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   crystal to the attached PCBB-OEG molecules, then to the PCBM:PCBB-OEG ETL. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, we investigated the mechanism of the oxygen-induced degradation reaction and explored the oxygen-stabilizing

effect of PCBB-OEG doping in the perovskite active layer and in PCBM ETL for pero-SCs. Time-resolved fluorescence mapping, time-resolved PL spectra, and TA measurements revealed that the doped PCBB-OEG is beneficial to the electron transfer from  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  crystals to the attached PCBB-OEG molecules. This process was demonstrated to be able to reduce the reaction between photoelectrons and oxygen to form  $ \mathrm{O}_2^{\bullet -} $  that could accelerate  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  decomposition.
