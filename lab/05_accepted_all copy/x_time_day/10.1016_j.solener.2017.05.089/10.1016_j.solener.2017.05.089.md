# 1. Introduction

During the past few years, the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted great attention in the fields of photovoltaic research. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) has increased from 4% to 22.1% (Kojima et al., 2009; Yang et al., 2015; Li et al., 2016a,b; Zhang et al., 2017, 2016a–g). Unfortunately, the widely used hole transporting materials (HTM) 2,2',7,7'-Tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) suffers from many drawbacks such as high manufacturing cost, complicated purification and insufficient long-term stabilities that represents a major bottleneck for the large-scale applications for PSCs (Docampo et al., 2014).

# ABSTRACT

Two novel asymmetric metallophthalocyanines with different metal core (2,9,16-triphenoxy-23-nitrophthalocyaninatozinc,   $  ZnPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   and 2,9,16-triphenoxy-23-nitrophthalocyaninatocopper,   $  CuPcNO_{2}-OPh  $  ) are synthesized and employed as hole transporting materials (HTMs) for perovskite   $  ((FAPbI_{3})_{0.85}(MAPbBr_{3})_{0.15})  $   solar cells (PSCs), reaching high power conversion efficiency (PCE). Both of the two metallophthalocyanines show particular solubility, absorption, thermal stability and suitable energy levels. Results confirm that the photovoltaic performance is strongly influenced by the core metals. The optimized devices based on   $  ZnPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   exhibit a maximum power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 14.35% with a high open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.071 V under standard global 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $   AM 1.5G illumination, while devices based on   $  CuPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   show a lower PCE of 12.72% with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.064 V. This newly developed perovskite solar cells demonstrated dramatically enhanced durability under ambient atmosphere. This study provides a promising prospect for an alternative approach based on the highly stable asymmetric metallophthalocyanine complexes to enhance the stability of perovskite solar cells.

The optical and electrical properties of these compounds can be easily tuned by varying the peripheral substituents and the metal center (Torre et al., 2013; Bottari et al., 2010). In this paper, we firstly report two novel asymmetric metallophthalocyanine complexes based on different metal cores (2,9,16-triphenoxy-23-nitrophthalocyaninatozinc,   $  ZnPcNO_{2}-OPh  $  ; 2,9,16-triphenoxy-23-nitrophthalocyaninatocopper,   $  CuPcNO_{2}-OPh  $  ). The introduction of the flexible phenoxy groups allows good molecular packing and extended   $  \pi-\pi  $   interaction thus increasing conductivity as well as improving the solubility and optical absorption of metallophthalocyanines (Guo et al., 2012; Zhang, Wang, Li and Xiao et al., 2016c). The D-  $  \pi  $  -A structure of metallophthalocyanines with three phenoxy and nitro groups acting as “push” and “pull” groups are favorite for the transformation of holes (Guo et al., 2016). The UV–vis absorbance and thermal properties were investigated. Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) decay spectra showed these two new HTMs can extract the holes from the perovskite efficiently. PSCs based on   $  ZnPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   with closed shell nature zinc exhibited the highest PCE of 14.35%, while PSCs based on   $  CuPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   with the open-shell copper exhibited the highest PCE of 12.72%. Our findings suggest that proper core metal could further boost the commercialization of phthalocyanines perovskite solar cells, giving a chance to find their place in the photovoltaic market.

# 2.3. Synthesis

2.3.1. The synthesis of 2,9,16-triphenoxy-23-nitrophthalocyaninatoznic  $ (\mathrm{ZnPcNO}_2\text{-OPh}) $

The mixture was filtered immediately and the filtrate was cooled to room temperature. The blue-black precipitate was obtained by dropping the mixture into petroleum ether afterwards. And then it was washed with deionized water to remove unreacted   $  \mathrm{Zn(OAc)_{2}\cdot2H_{2}O}  $  . The precipitate was extracted by ethanol, ethyl acetate and petroleum respectively till the solvent turned to colorless. 2.3.2. The synthesis of 2,9,16-triphenoxy-23-nitrophthalocyaninatocopper (CuPcNO  $  _{2}  $  -OPh)

# 2.4. Device fabrication

The layer of blocking   $  TiO_{2}  $   was deposited by immersing the FTO substrate into 0.05 M   $  TiCl_{4}  $   solution and kept in an oven at 70 °C, following by annealing at 500 °C in air. After diluted in ethanol (1:3, w/w), the paste of   $  TiO_{2}  $   mesoporous (18NR-T) was deposited by spin coating (4500 rpm), following by heating at 500 °C. The   $  (\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3})_{0.85}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})_{0.15}  $   (FA = formamidinium, MA = methylammonium) perovskite was fabricated by anti-solvent as reported in reference (Bi et al., 2016a,b). Then the solution of HTMs was spin-coated at 4000 rpm for 20 s.   $  ZnPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   or   $  CuPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   was dissolved in chlorobenzene with 20 mg/mL, while Spiro-MeOTAD was dissolved in chlorobenzene solvent at the concentrations of 72.3 mg/mL (additive: 28.8 μL 4-tert-butylpyridine, 17.5 μL of 520 mg/mL lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulphonSyl) imide acetonitrile solution). The gold counter electrode with 80 nm was deposited by thermal evaporation method.

# 2.5. Photovoltaic characterization procedures

For  $ J-V $  characteristics, the cells were illuminated under  $ 100\mathrm{mW/cm^2} $  (AM 1.5) by an Oriel solar simulator 91192, and the data were recorded on Keithley 2400 Source meter. # 3.2. UV–vis absorption and fluorescence spectra

Both of the asymmetric nitrophthalocyanines show typical Soret band at 300–400 nm (B-band) and intense Q band with an unconspicuous shoulder in the NIR-infrared region at 600–750 nm (Fig. 1a and b) in DMF solution. The absorption maximum of   $  ZnPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   (680 nm) is 13 nm bathochromic-shift than   $  CuPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   (667 nm) which can be seen in Table 1. For the strong conjugative electron donating effect of substituent groups,   $  ZnPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   and   $  CuPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   show much more intense absorbance and bathochromic-shift than unsubstituted phthalocyanines. Due to the close-shell nature of zinc (Guo et al., 2015),   $  ZnPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   shows excellent light absorption abilities with the molar extinction coefficient (  $  \varepsilon  $  ) of B and Q band being as high as   $  6.46 \times 10^{4}  $   and   $  1.22 \times 10^{5} dm^{3} mol^{-1} cm^{-1}  $   respectively, higher than that of   $  CuPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   with the open-shell core metal.

$ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2 - OPh} $  and  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2 - OPh} $  showed typical phthalocyanines fluorescence emission peak at 688 and  $ 691~\mathrm{nm} $  in DMF respectively. The extremely intense fluorescence of  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2 - OPh} $  is due to the close-shell nature of the metal zinc which reduces the d-  $ \pi $  interactions hence promoting the singlet state of the compounds. The reduced spin-orbit coupling and the bigger the energy gap between S1 and T1 states might also be responsible for the increasing of the fluorescence intensity (Chen et al., 2009). While  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2 - OPh} $  shows extremely weak fluorescence intensity for the open-shell nature of the metal copper that promotes d-  $ \pi $  interactions thereby quenching the singlet state of the complexes.

# 3.4. Theoretical calculations

In order to get further insight into the molecular structure and electron distribution of  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OPh and  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OPh, the geometry and energy were optimized by density functional theory (DFT) calculations via Gaussian 09 program package. The B3LYP/6-31G (d) level has been employed to predict the optoelectronic properties of the two macromolecules (Qi et al., 2017). As shown in Fig. 2a and b, the optimized ground-state geometry indicates that phthalocyanine ring is coplanar, but the three phenoxy groups are twisted, which could reduce the association of the molecules. While the nitric group is at the outside of phthalocyanine ring, the coplanar structures facilitate the transportation of electrons or holes.

The calculated frontier molecular orbitals are shown in Fig. 2c. The HOMO energy levels of the two complexes are located on the carbon moiety of phthalocyanine ring, while the LUMO levels are mainly located on phthalocyanine ring and nitro group. The HOMO levels of  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OPh and  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OPh are estimated to be  $ -5.13 $  and  $ -5.14\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. The main difference between the two HTMs lies in their central metal, zinc for  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OPh

# 3.5.2. Long-Term stability of PSCs with   $  ZnPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   and   $  CuPcNO_{2}-OPh  $  -based HTM

For the advancement of practical PSCs, the cell durability is a critical issue. In this research, the stability of the perovskites solar cell with   $  ZnPcNO_{2}-OPh  $  ,   $  CuPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   and a typical solar cell with Spiro-OMeTAD as HTM deployed as reference for comparison were tested. All of the solar cells were stored under 30% air condition relative humidity without encapsulation and measured every three days for 33 days. The corresponding normalized   $  V_{OC}  $  ,   $  J_{SC}  $  , FF and PCEs changes are plotted in Fig. 5. The results show that   $  ZnPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   based device showed a little improvement in   $  V_{OC}  $  , FF and PCE during the first three days' stability tests, and then a slightly decrement in the following days, which is attributed to the gradually optimizing of the device and slightly decomposition of   $  (FAPbI_{3})_{0.85}(MAPbBr_{3})_{0.15}  $   next. To be specific, during the whole tests, the PCE only reduced 4% during 33 d durability tests. While   $  CuPcNO_{2}-OPh  $   based device showed a larger decrease in all index, with the dropped PCE of 18%. Under the same perovskite fabrication and storage conditions, the Spiro-OMeTAD based device showed a dramatically decrease in   $  V_{OC}  $  ,   $  J_{SC}  $   and FF, that lead PCE dropped to 68%.

It is obvious that the PCEs of  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OPh and  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OPh based PSC device demonstrated much better stability in ambient atmosphere than Spiro-OMeTAD based device. The better hydrophobicity of  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OPh and  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OPh layer than that of Spiro-OMeTAD can protect the perovskite film damaged by the humidity present in atmosphere which can seen from the contact angles in Fig. S6. The fewer microvoids of phthalocyanines layer than that of Spiro-OMeTAD and the absence of doping additives will also contribute to the stability of PSCs. Compared to the PSCs with Spiro-OMeTAD as HTMs, these results showed the potentially practical application of asymmetric metallophthalocyanines based devices for low cost, highly efficient and stable perovskite solar cells.

# 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we report the synthesis and characterization of two novel asymmetric metallophthalocyanine,  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OPh and  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OPh respectively. They were applied as dopant-free hole transporting materials in perovskite solar cells. Both of them show good solubility, high optical absorption property and thermal stability. The DFT method of  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OPh and  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OPh shows appropriate HOMO and LUMO energies for the HTMs materials. The effective device of  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OPh demonstrated an impressive  $ PCE $  of  $ 14.35\% $  with  $ V_{OC} $  of  $ 1.071\mathrm{V} $  and  $ J_{SC} $  of  $ 20.832\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  under AM 1.5G standard conditions.  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OPh shows relatively lower performance of solar parameter than  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OPh for the central metal. Moreover, this newly developed solar cell demonstrated dramatically enhanced durability under ambient atmosphere. This work provides a promising prospect for an alternative approach based on the asymmetric metallophthalocyanine complexes to obtain high performance and stable perovskite solar cells.
