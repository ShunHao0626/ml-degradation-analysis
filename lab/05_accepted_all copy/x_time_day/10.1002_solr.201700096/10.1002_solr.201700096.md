# Hole-Transport Materials Containing Triphenylamine Donors with a Spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene] Core for Efficient and Stable Large Area Perovskite Solar Cells

Guohua Wu, Yaohong Zhang, Ryuji Kaneko, Yoshiyuki Kojima, Kosuke Sugawa, Towhid H. Chowdhury, Ashraful Islam,* Qing Shen,* Md. Akhtaruzzaman, Takeshi Noda, and Joe Otsuki*

# 1. Introduction

Three low-cost triphenylamine-based hole-transport materials (HTMs) with spiro[fluorene-9,9-xanthene] (SFX) skeleton are synthesized through a facile three-step procedure. The effect of the chemical structure of the HTMs on the optical property, energy levels,   $  H_{2}O/HTM  $   interface, as well as the perovskite solar cell performance are studied. With the increasing bulkiness of attached groups on the SFX skeleton, the charge recombination resistance at the   $  TiO_{2}/perovskite/HTMs  $   interfaces is gradually increased, leading to increased open-circuit voltages. The device based on the bulkiest HTM (named BTPA-6) exhibits a better solar cell performance (11.57%, area:   $  1.02 cm^{2}  $  ) than those with BTPA-4 and BTPA-5 in the forward scan. BTPA-6 also exhibits a power conversion efficiency of 14.4% (area:   $  0.375 cm^{2}  $  ) which nearly matches spiro-OMeTAD (15.0%) in the reverse scan. Furthermore, all the three cost-effective (  $  \sim1/3  $   of that of spiro-OMeTAD) HTMs exhibit better long-term stabilities than spiro-OMeTAD.

# 2.4. Water Contact Angle and Thermal Properties

It has been known that perovskite materials are easily decomposed by humidity due to the hygroscopic ammonium salts.  $  ^{[38,39]}  $   HTMs also play an important role of preventing water penetration into the surface of perovskite material and thus it is valuable to investigate water contact angles of HTMs. As shown in Figure 4, the water contact angles of the HTM-coated films were 76° for BTPA-4, 75° for BTPA-5, and 73° for BTPA-6. In comparison with reported 70° for spiro-OMeTAD,  $  ^{[39]}  $   these three HTMs show gently higher hydrophobic properties, which may enhance their PSC stabilities. # 2.7. Durability of PSC Devices

The long-term stability is also a vital issue for PSCs. We investigated the stability of the PSC devices based on BTPA-4, BTPA-5, BTPA-6, and the standard spiro-OMeTAD, which were kept in the dark with the humidity of 25%. After one day, the solar cell efficiencies for these four HTMs were significantly increased and after that day, the device performances were declined as the aging time increased, which may be ascribed to the slow infiltration of water from air.  $  ^{[45]}  $   The highest PCEs in the reverse scan at the second day were 13.2% for BTPA-4, 14.1% for BTPA-5, 14.4% for BTPA-6, and 15.0% for spiro-OMeTAD, respectively. The respective PCEs became 10.7, 12.4, 10.3, and 10.5%. It is remarkable that the PSC with BTPA-5 exhibited a better PCE than the cells with the other HTMs from day 4 on, even exceeding a cell with spiro-OMeTAD by a margin as large as 2% after 10 days. Furthermore, the cells with BTPA-4 and BTPA-6 showed comparable efficiencies with a cell with spiro-OMeTAD after 10 days. Here a conclusion can be made that BTPA-4, BTPA-5, and BTPA-6-based devices showed better long-term stability than spiro-OMeTAD-based one, which is in good agreement with the result of water contact angle test at the   $  H_{2}O/HTMs  $   interface.

# 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, we have developed two novel small molecular HTMs, BTPA-5 and BTPA-6, as well as an analogous BTPA-4 by employing the SFX core as the central bridge for large-area PSCs (  $  >1\ cm^{2}  $  ). The effect of the molecular structural modification of these three HTMs on their optical properties, electrochemical properties, water contact angles, interfacial charge resistance as well as device performances have been investigated in detail. Among them, the BTPA-6-based solar cell device yielded a PCE of 11.57% in the forward scan with the best   $  J_{sc}  $   and   $  V_{oc}  $   values due to the highest IPCE value in the visible region, the highest hole extraction ability, and the largest recombination resistance at the   $  TiO_{2}/perovskite/HTM  $   interface, which was comparable to the cell fabricated with the standard spiro-OMeTAD. Notably, all the three SFX derivatives-based devices showed superior long-term stability than spiro-OMeTAD-based one. Particularly, the present results highlight BTPA-5 and BTPA-6 with high efficiencies combined with its low cost and facile synthesis as highly competitive HTMs for future large scale application in PSCs.

Characterization:   $  {}^{1}  $  H and   $  {}^{13}  $  C NMR spectra were recorded on a JOEL JNM-ECX 400 spectrometer. Mass spectrometry was performed on an Agilent LC/MSD TOF 1100 spectrometer operating in the atmospheric chemical ionization (APCI) mode. The NMR and MS spectra for novel compounds are shown in Figures S6–S17. Elemental analysis was carried out by the Center for Creative Materials Research, the College of Science and Technology, Nihon University. The UV-vis absorption spectra of the three HTMs in CH  $  _{2}  $  Cl  $  _{2}  $   solution were measured with a JASCO V-770 spectrophotometer and their florescence spectra were measured on a FP-8600 spectrofluorometer. The thermogravimetric analysis was performed using a Rigaku Thermo Plus differential thermal balance at a heating rate of 20 °C min  $  ^{-1}  $   in a range from room temperature to 1000 °C in air. Cyclic voltammetry was performed on a Hokuto Denko HZ-3000 electrochemical workstation with a conventional three-electrode system which is composed of a Pt disk working electrode, a Pt wire counter electrode, and an Ag wire pseudo-reference electrode. The oxidation potentials of these three materials were measured in CH  $  _{2}  $  Cl  $  _{2}  $   solution with 0.1 M TBAPF  $  _{6}  $   as the supporting electrolyte at a scan rate of 100 mV s  $  ^{-1}  $  . The HOMO potentials of these three HTMs were calculated by adding the internal reference ferricinium/ferrocene (Fc  $  ^{+}  $  /Fc) couple. Conductivities were obtained from current–voltage curves recorded on the electrochemical workstation for Au/HTM/Au devices. The current–voltage characteristics of PSCs were measured using a solar simulator with standard air mass 1.5 sunlight (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , WXS-155S-10: Wacom Denso) under ambient conditions. The current density–voltage (J-V) curves were measured by forward (-0.2 to 1.2 V forward bias) or reverse (1.2 to -0.2 V) scans. The step voltage was fixed at 5–10 mV and the delay time was set at 100 ms. J-V curves for all devices were measured by masking the cells with a metal mask 1.022 cm  $  ^{2}  $   in area. Monochromatic incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra were measured with a monochromatic incident light of 1 × 10  $  ^{16}  $   photons cm  $  ^{-2}  $   in director current mode (CEP-2000BX, Bunko-Keiki). The light intensity of the solar simulator was calibrated by a standard silicon solar cell provided by PV Measurements.

Device Fabrication: The solar cell devices were prepared in the National Institute of Materials Science (NIMS) (data for Figures 5 and S3 and Tables 2 and S3) as well as in the University of Electro-Communications (UEC) (data for Figures 6, 7, and S5) in very similar procedures. The following description follows the NIMS procedure but different conditions used in the UEC are also given with an asterisk. First, a cleaned FTO-substrate  $ (14\Omega sq^{-1}) $  was coated with titanium diisopropoxide bis-(acetylacetonate) (Sigma-Aldrich) in ethanol solution by spray pyrolysis at  $ 470^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  to form a compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer  $ (\mathrm{cl - TiO_2}) $  with a thickness of  $ 60~\mathrm{nm} $ . Then a mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film was deposited on the  $ \mathrm{cl - TiO_2} $  coated substrate by spin coating (3000 rpm, 30s) from an ethanol solution of diluted  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  paste (18 nm particle Dyesol-18NRD, Dyesol) with a mass

ratio of 1:4 followed by drying at 70°C for 10 min and sintered for 500°C for 1.5 h in air. After annealing, the mesoporous TiO₂ (mp-TiO₂) substrates were cooled down to room temperature and transferred to a N₂ glovebox. Subsequently, a 1.45 M (1.2 M⁺) CH₃NH₃PbI₃ perovskite precursor dimethyl sulfoxide solution, which was stirred at 50°C overnight, was deposited on the 60°C heat-treated mp-TiO₂ substrate by two consecutive spin coating steps at 1000 rpm (800 rpm⁺) for 12 s (10 s⁺) followed by 4000 rpm (4500 rpm⁺) for 30 s. After starting the second spin-coating stage, 800 μL of toluene was dropped onto the spinning substrate for 10 s. These perovskite deposited substrates were then heat-treated at 100°C (105°C⁺) for 10 min on a hotplate. The HTM solutions were then deposited on the individual perovskite films by spin-coating and these solutions were prepared by the following details. HTMs were each dissolved in 1 mL chlorobenzene (20 mM for BTPA-4, BTPA-5, and BTPA-6) or spiro-OMeTAD (60 mM) with 4-tert-butylpyridine (24 μL) and a certain amount of bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide lithium salt (Li-TFSI). For BTPA-4, BTPA-5, and BTPA-6, 0.032 mM Li-TFSI acetonitrile solution (17.5 μL) was used, while for spiro-OMeTAD, 0.043 mM Li-TFSI acetonitrile solution (72 μL) was used. The spin-coating process was carried out at 3000 rpm (4000 rpm⁺) for 30 s for BTPA-4, BTPA-5, and spiro-OMeTAD solutions while 2500 rpm (4000 rpm⁺) for 30 s for BTPA-6 solution. All the HTMs were spin-coated on CH₃NH₃PbI₃/mp-TiO₂/cl-TiO₂/FTO. Finally, 50 nm thick gold and 100 nm thick silver were deposited by thermal evaporation under vacuum to complete the fabrication of the solar cells. The active area of all the cells was 1.02 cm² (0.375 cm²⁺).
