# ABSTRACT

As the promising substitution for toxic lead-based perovskite, tin (Sn)-based halide perovskite has drawn much attention in the photovoltaic application. However, unsatisfied open circuit voltage ( $ V_{\text{OC}} $ ) and fill factor (FF) of the available Sn-based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) remain a lingering cloud. In this work, we report a bilateral interfacial engineering strategy to fabricate 2D-3D bulk heterojunction Sn-based perovskite solar cells. Specifically, large cation PEAI and bifunctional LiF are evaporated at the bilateral interfaces of FASnI $ _3 $  film. The presence of PEAI improves the  $ V_{\text{OC}} $  and FF of the PSCs via improved surface coverage and formed 2D-3D bulk heterojunction structure, while the bifunctional LiF (i) lowers the work function of PEDOT: PSS and (ii) facilitates the hole extraction at ITO/PEDOT: PSS interface. This strategy enabled power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 6.98% with  $ V_{\text{OC}} $  of 0.47 V and FF of 0.74. Our findings will add critical building bricks towards efficient Sn-based PSCs.

As a rising star in the field of advanced energy materials, organic-inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskite semiconductors have been extensively studied as “core brain” in many optoelectronic and electronic applications.  $  ^{1}  $   In particular, solar cell device based on methylammonium lead triiodide (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) perovskite material has proceeded rapid development, and the latest certificated power conversion efficiency (PCE) of such perovskite solar cell (PSCs) has unprecedentedly achieved 22.1% in small cells and 19.7% in 1-square-centimeter cells.  $  ^{2}  $   Nevertheless, it remains two concerns that hamper the large-scale commercial manufacturing of the Pb-based PSCs: (i) majority usage of toxic element Pb that is fatal to the environment as well as human beings;  $  ^{3}  $   (ii) poor long-term stability of Pb-based perovskite material.  $  ^{4}  $   With this in mind, great efforts have been made to develop alternative eco-friendly Pb-free perovskite light absorber materials for solar cell application.

To seek for eligible Pb substitution, the intrinsic features of the substitution should be considered in the first place. Many of the excellent properties in Pb-based perovskite are considered to originate from the outer   $  6s^{2}  $   electronic configuration of Pb, which is a configuration of   $  ns^{2}  $   that is possessed by many post-transition metal elements.  $  ^{5}  $   The   $  ns^{2}  $   cations in a halide are believed to lead to more delocalized valence and conduction band states by enhancing cation-anion hybridization, from where many superior properties derive.  $  ^{6}  $   Theoretically, for photovoltaic application, in addition to the basic optoelectronic properties (suitable band gap, high optical absorption, low carrier effective mass, low exciton binding energy and high charge mobility), the qualified candidates of efficient and defect-tolerance light absorber material should also possess the properties of large dielectric constant, small effective masses, valence band maximum composed of anti-bonding states, and high levels of band dispersion.  $  ^{7}  $   In this scenario, post-transition metals with an   $  ns^{2}  $   electronic configuration beyond

What's more, Sn-based perovskite materials shows an absorption edge locating in the near-infrared region (900-1000 nm) and is expected to produce higher short-circuit current densities towards higher PCE performance. $ ^{10, 11} $  Over the past 3 years, many groups have successfully fabricated Sn-based Pb-free PSCs using both regular and inverted device structures, and considerable progress has been made. $ ^{12-25} $  Among those works,  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $ -based PSCs have drawn particular attention due to its suitable band gap (1.41 eV) and the single stable phase over a broad temperature range up to  $ 200^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ . $ ^{15} $  The reported highest PCE of  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $ -based PSCs has reached over  $ 9\% $ . $ ^{25} $  However, the performance (PCE and stability) of the  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $ -based PSCs is still low due to the following reasons: (i)  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  can be easily oxidized into  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{4+} $  owing to the lack of inert pair effect as in  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $ , which causes obvious device degradation even under trace amounts of water and oxygen in glove box; $ ^{13} $  (ii) The low formation energy of Sn vacancy ( $ \mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{Sn}} $ ) introduces deep carrier recombination centre, leading to the low FF in PSCs; $ ^{15} $  (iii) The rapid reaction between  $ \mathrm{SnI}_2 $  and FAI salts makes it difficult to control the film morphology fabricating via one-step spin-coating method; (iv) The mismatch of energy levels between  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  and charge transport layers could set energy barriers at the interfaces and lead to unfavorable charge recombination.

We first improve the surface coverage and stability of the   $  FASnI_{3}  $   film by the introduction of PEAI. As demonstrated in Figure 1a, the original film fabrication process we developed previously involves the spin-coating of FAI/PEDOT: PSS aqueous solution (60 mg/mL), evaporation of   $  SnI_{2}  $  , and post-annealing processes to form the   $  FASnI_{3}  $   film (route with red arrows). The FAI film showed morphology of well-monodispersed discrete FAI particles (Figure 1b, Figure S1a), which is due to the nucleation of FAI molecule induced by the active groups on the PEDOT: PSS polymer chains.  $  ^{28}  $   Although the volume of FAI molecule would be expanded during the interdiffusion reaction between FAI and   $  SnI_{2}  $  , the resultant   $  FASnI_{3}  $   film after   $  SnI_{2}  $   evaporation still showed noticeable holes on the film (Figure 1(d)), it is due to the relatively large spaces between the FAI particles Figure 1(f). In this case, decreasing the spaces between FAI particles can rationally eliminate the holes in   $  FASnI_{3}  $   film. One reasonable strategy is to increase the concentration of FAI/PEDOT: PSS aqueous solution. However, we found that the concentration of FAI in PEDOT: PSS aqueous solution was unable to further increase, because higher concentration (>60 mg/mL) would lead to the sedimentation of the FAI/PEDOT: PSS solvent in aqueous solution, which result in non-uniform FAI film after spin-coating and the undulate   $  FASnI_{3}  $   film surface (Figure S2). It has been well-established that the maximum   $  V_{OC}  $   of a specific light absorber material in solar cell device is determined by the differentials between the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of the HTL and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of electron transport layer (ETL).  $  ^{41, 42}  $   Thus, strategies that lowering the HOMO of HTL material  $  ^{43-45}  $   and elevating LUMO of ETL  $  ^{46, 47}  $   to match the band edges of the light absorber material are proved to be effective for   $  V_{OC}  $   improvement.
