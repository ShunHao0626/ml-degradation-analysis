# Symmetrical Conjugated Molecular Additive for Defect Passivation and Charge Transfer Bridge in Perovskite Solar Cells

Longfei Li,  $  ^{§}  $   Xiaoli Xu,  $  ^{§}  $   Lingbo Xiao, Wen Jiang, Jie Zhao,  $  ^{*}  $   Xiangqiang Kong,  $  ^{*}  $   and Guifu Zou  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Defect passivation of functional additives is an effective approach to suppress the nonradiative recombination centers in perovskite solar cells. Here, a symmetrical conjugated molecular additive (4,4'-bipyridyl, BPY[4,4]) with bilateral nitrogen atoms is employed for effective defect passivation by bonding with uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   cations within perovskite. Meanwhile,   $  \pi  $  -conjugated BPY[4,4] affords large bridging interaction for better charge carrier transfer pathway between perovskite grains than control, as witnessed by increased electron and hole mobility. Consequently, the device with optimal BPY[4,4] device displays a power conversion efficiency of 20.82% higher than control device of 18.38% and exhibits better long-term thermal stability. It is worth noting that BPY[4,4] has symmetrical and conjugated molecular structures, which benefit from   $  \pi-\pi  $   stacking and face-on orientation structure, so it has more advantages than asymmetric and non-conjugated molecular additives, such as charge transfer, defect passivation, and device performance. Our work demonstrates that symmetrical conjugated molecule

lditives can construct bridged charge transfer channels between perovskite grains as well as effective defect passivation.

KEYWORDS: perovskite solar cells, symmetrical conjugated molecular additive, bipyridine, defect passivation, charge transfer bridge

# INTRODUCTION

During the past decade, photovoltaic fields witnessed the skyrocketing progress of organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs).  $  ^{1-6}  $   One major factor depends on the excellent optical and electronic characters of perovskite materials including bandgap adjustment (1.0–3.0 eV), high absorption coefficient (  $  10^{5}  $   cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ), low exciton binding energy (40 meV), long carrier diffusion length (1   $  \mu  $  m), large photoluminescence lifetime, and carrier mobility.  $  ^{7-10}  $   These advantages enable state-of-the-art PSCs exceeding 25% of the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs),  $  ^{11}  $   delivering them to be one of the most promising leaders in next-generation thin film photovoltaic technology. Another major factor is that perovskite active layers can be obtained by very feasible solution-processed and annealing approach.  $  ^{12-14}  $   The fabrication not only simplifies the process and saves the energy but also reduces the manufacturing cost for future large-scale commercialized output. Notably, high quality perovskite films are very critical to high PCEs and long-term stability for PSCs.  $  ^{15-17}  $   However, solution-processed method will form polycrystalline perovskite with structural disorder. Compared with single crystal perovskite, they possess much more shallow-level traps (e.g., point vacancies) and extended deep-level traps (e.g., grain boundaries, surface defects, and crystallographic voids).  $  ^{18-20}  $   In addition, due to low thermal stability of hybrid perovskite themselves, the annealing procedure will produce irreversible volatilization and deterioration of methylammonium

This means that the relatively insulating alkyl chain will affect the charge transfer.  $  ^{40}  $   P-conjugation-induced extension of electronic states of A-site cations affects perovskite frontier orbitals. The p-conjugated pyrene-containing A-site cations electronically contribute to the surface band edges and influence the carrier dynamics, with a properly tailored intercalation distance between layers of the inorganic framework, and the alkyl chain length affected the electronic interaction between the two moieties.  $  ^{41}  $   Therefore, those nonconjugated and relatively insulating compounds/additives with long alkyl chains may largely hinder the charge transfer between perovskite grain boundaries.

Inspired by previous molecule diversity of chemical structure design, sustainable exploration of defect passivation will strengthen further understanding on passivation mechanism and performance enhancement of PSCs. In this work, we present a symmetrical conjugated molecular additive for effective defect passivation toward high performance PSCs. A small amount of 4,4'-bipyridyl additive BPY[4,4] can provide

functional bilateral nitrogen groups to passivate with uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   in perovskite, which plays a critical role in improving perovskite film quality and device performance. For ideal perovskite octahedral crystal structures with layer distance of about 0.63 nm,  $  ^{42}  $   symmetrical BPY[4,4] with molecular length of about 0.7 nm is also compatible with their framework. The structure symmetry may endow BPY[4,4] ordered assembly for better charge transporting effect in perovskite films.  $  ^{43}  $   Moreover, the   $  \pi  $  -conjugated molecular structure of BPY[4,4] can afford enhanced bridge interaction for better charge carrier transfer between perovskite grains. A series of characterizations are carried out to investigate the influence of BPY[4,4] additive on intermolecular coordination interaction, passivation effectiveness, charge carrier transfer, film quality improvement, efficiency, and stability enhancement.

To investigate the electron trap-state density and charge mobility in the perovskite films before and after BPY[4,4] addition, as depicted in Figure 4d–g, dark  $ I-V $  response is presented by space-charge-limited current (SCLC) methods $ ^{62} $  for electron-only device (FTO/TiO $ _2 $ /perovskite/PCBM/Ag) and hole-only device (ITO/PEDOT:PSS/perovskite/SpiroOMeTAD/Au). Generally, at the low bias voltage, the linear relation means an ohmic response, while the current obviously increases nonlinearly at larger bias voltage exceeding the kink point, which is indicative of completely filled trap states with onset voltage ( $ V_{\text{TFL}} $ ). The defect density of perovskite films can be examined by the following equation: $ ^{56} $

$$
N _ {\text { defects }} = \frac {2 \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0} V _ {\mathrm{TFL}}}{e L ^ {2}}
$$

(1)

The carrier diffusion length is calculated by the formula   $  L_{\mathrm{D}} = (K_{\mathrm{B}} T \mu \tau_{\mathrm{r}} / e)^{1/2}  $  , where   $  K_{B}  $   is Boltzmann's constant, T is temperature,   $  \mu  $   is carrier mobility,   $  \tau_{r}  $   is carrier lifetime, and e is elementary charge.  $  ^{66}  $   Here, the calculated carrier diffusion length of the control device is 3.0 nm, while the value of the device with BPY[4,4] is 10.5 nm. In contrast, the carrier diffusion length has a significant promote.

The energy level distribution and charge-transfer processes are also shown in Figure S15 to confirm the BPY[4,4] effect on perovskite Fermi levels by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurement. It can be found that the addition of BPY[4,4] will lead to a slight upshift of Femi levels, conduction band, and valence band. Therefore, it is thought that the addition of BPY[4,4] cannot prohibit effective charge separation and transport.

Long-term stability is of importance for practical perovskite solar cells. Figure 5a shows the PCE variation of BPY[4,4] added PSCs under dark conditions for aging 12 days in argon at ambient temperature. The device can maintain a PCE of about 18.7% from the initial 19.8%. For comparison, the control device exhibits an obviously decreased PCE of 10.67% from the initial 18.32%. As shown in Figure 5b, after aging 12 days, the normalized PCEs for BPY[4,4] added and control PSCs are 89.5% and 60.3%, respectively. In addition, thermal stability is carried out at 60 °C in argon, and the PCE and normalized PCE change are presented in Figure 5c and Figure 5d, respectively. The device with BPY[4,4] can still display about 82.3% of its initial PCE after aging, while the control just preserves about 50.6% of its initial PCE value. The demonstrated stability measurements can be attributed to the hydrophobic property of BPY[4,4] and better Pb $  ^{2+}  $  defect passivation of perovskite films through BPY[4,4] additive. Besides, the champion device represents a stabilized photocurrent of ~24 mA cm $  ^{-2}  $  under continuous illumination time for 0.15 mol % BPY[4,4] based PSCs (Figure S16), which also supports the device stability.

# CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we employed a unique bipyridine BPY[4,4] as a functional additive toward high performance PSCs. The BPY[4,4] bearing bilateral nitrogen moiety could effectively passivate uncoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2+}} $  within perovskite active layers to reduce charge trap-state density. It is worth noting that its symmetrical conjugated molecular structure constructed better charge transfer bridge by large  $ \pi $ -stacking interaction than nonconjugated or asymmetric additives between perovskite grains, as witnessed by increased electron and hole mobility. As a result, the PSC containing optimal BPY[4,4] addition exhibited a PCE of  $ 20.82\% $  higher than control device of  $ 18.38\% $  and displayed better long-term thermal stability. Our work can develop functional additives of perovskite via designing of symmetrical conjugated molecules for charge transfer bridge as well as effective defect passivation.
