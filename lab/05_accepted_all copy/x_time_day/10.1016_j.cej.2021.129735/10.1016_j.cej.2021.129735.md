# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been attracting more and more attention due to its rapid development and a superior power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.5% over the conventional multicrystalline silica solar cells [1]. The device configuration of PSCs has three types, regular mesoporous, regular planar and inverted planar ones, where the last one now becomes prevailing owing to its easy and time-saving fabrication procedure without the annealing treatment at a high temperature of over 450 °C, effective hole collection brought by the shorter distance between the sites of hole generation and collection in view of the relative lower charge mobility for holes, and a much lowered hysteresis bearing a reduced difference on the PCE values when tested at different scanning directions and/or speeds [2], however, its PCE values still fall

# ABSTRACT

Owing to its high wettability and the resulting compatibility with the future industrial device fabrication process, the conjugated polyelectrolyte (CPE) has becoming a promising hole-transporting material (HTM) in the inverted perovskite solar cell (iPSC); however, only a few highly efficient CPEs have been reported probably due to the limited pool of molecular designing strategies. Here we construct a CPE named DTB(Na) with the same main-chain of a polymer DTB(EH) that was previously employed as a dopant-free HTM in a highly efficient regular type PSC (rPSC) and with simple water/alcohol soluble side-chains. Compared with its analog bearing the only variation of less thiophene units in the main-chain, DTB(Na) shows stronger capacities of hole-extraction and defect-passivation, which should be ascribed to the more intensive exposure of thiophene functional unit to the perovskite layer. As a result, the DTB(Na) iPSC device presents enhanced values on all the photovoltaic parameters and long-term stability, and a power conversion efficiency of  $ 19.92\% $  is realized. Our results demonstrate the feasibility for efficient polymeric HTMs in rPSCs and iPSCs to share the same main-chains, thus enriching the molecular designing strategies and probably expanding the library of efficient HTMs for iPSCs.

Originally, the inverted planar PSC (iPSC) held poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) as the HTM; nevertheless, the weak acidic nature of PEDOT:PSS may damage the underneath conductive oxides, consequently endowing the PSC device with a poor long-term stability. Among various organic alternatives, conjugated polyelectrolytes (CPEs), which bear ionic pairs and similar wettability to the upper polar perovskite materials compared with PEDOT:PSS, may be of high prospect to serve as a talented HTM in iPSCs if considering the convenience of industrial device-fabrication process that requires the perovskite solution to spread out easily atop the HTM layer. So far, many examples of CPE HTMs have been displayed since its first report in 2012 [8,9,11,13,14], and PCE values have gradually increased from  $ 12.51\% $  to the present  $ 19.76\% $ ;[26] nonetheless, their designing principle and strategy are still very limited. In view of the extensive exploration on other kinds of HTMs, especially those non-ionic ones used in regular type PSCs (rPSCs) [27-31], experience and lessons from their molecular design could be beneficial to CPE materials. As a significant point in the design of HTMs for rPSCs and in the device engineering, defect-passivation is now frequently employed [32-35]. Therefore, we previously decorated a defect-passivation group of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3^+ $  cation onto the side-chain of polymer, and the resulting CPE HTM was revealed to efficiently passivate the defects on the surface and in the bulk of perovskite film, and exhibited the highest PCE of  $ 19.76\% $  for CPE based iPSCs [26]. Apart from the referenced strategies used in the designing of HTMs in rPSCs, the straightforward utilization of their backbones to construct CPE in iPSCs would greatly broaden the pool of HTMs and deepen the understanding of the interaction between HTM and perovskite; however, this has seldom been reported. Although one may think about the prospect of designing a non-ionic polymeric HTM for iPSCs with the same backbone of rPSC-targeted HTMs, the high hydrophobicity of the resulting polymer renders it less compatible than the CPE one with the future industrial fabrication procedures. Previously, CPEs based on the main-chains of polythiophene and the ionic side-chains of COONa [11] and  $ \mathrm{COOCH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3 $  [12] were scrutinized, and a PCE of  $ 19.6\% $  was realized. Nevertheless, HTMs based on non-ionic polythiophene in rPSCs could only obtain a low PCE of  $ 15.8\% $  [36,37] once device optimization on the perovskite layer and the interface of HTM/perovskite was not carried out [37]. If a strategy comes up to be able to directly utilize the backbones of non-ionic HTMs to build efficient ionic HTMs for iPSCs, the limitation on the library of efficient HTMs for iPSCs may be relieved, and a deeper exploration on the advantages of iPSCs would be then expected, consequently rendering this strategy of great significance to the development of iPSCs.

In this work, we design and synthesize a CPE using the same main-chain of a high-performance dopant-free polymeric HTM in rPSC based on our previous work. The original polymer noted as DTB(EH) (Scheme S1) yielded a PCE of 19.68% for rPSC device lying in its strong ability of hole-extraction from and defect-passivation to the perovskite layer [38]. The CPE here noted as DTB(Na) is alternatingly composed of dithiophene units and benzene units with   $  SO_{3}Na  $   end-capped side-chains (Fig. 1a). Due to the huge difference between the rPSC and iPSC devices as well as the resulting complexity in the comparison of DTB(EH) and DTB(Na), their analog TB(Na) whose main-chain is very similar to that of DTB(Na) but constructed with lower number ratio of thiophene/benzene is synthesized for comparison to elucidate the features of DTB (Na). Relative to TB(Na), DTB(Na) exhibited a superior capacity of hole-extraction from and defect-passivation to the perovskite film, which is similar to the observed phenomena for DTB(EH), yielding an enhancement to all the photovoltaic parameters and long-term stability. A much increased PCE value of 19.92% was attained, which is higher than the previous record efficiency for CPE based iPSCs [26]. Our work shows that there is an outstanding feasibility of mutual learning in the molecular design between the polymeric HTMs of rPSCs and iPSCs, and a big probability to expand the pool of high-performance HTMs for iPSCs can be expected.

To uncover the reason for the enhanced photovoltaic performance of DTB(Na), several crucial mechanism exploration experiments were conducted. Fig. 4a, 4b and 4c present the photoluminescence (PL) spectra assessed on ITO/CPE/  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.85}\mathrm{MA}_{0.15}\mathrm{Pb(Br}_{0.15}\mathrm{I}_{0.85})_3 $  films, where the former two figures are the steady-state PL (SSPL) and the latter one is the time-resolved PL (TRPL). The PL intensity is believed to be mainly determined by three factors, one is the recombination of free charge carriers, another one is the carrier trapping by the defects, and the last one is the charge extraction by the charge-transporting layers. The first one is a kind of radiative recombination, while the latter two belong to the nonradiative recombination [40]. As seen in Fig. 4a and b, the PL intensity gradually increases as the scanning time rises, which should be attributed to the trap-filling and the subsequently suppressed carrier trapping over the illumination time. The intensity is increased by around  $ 120\% $  when comparing the 1st and 4th round of measurement for TB (Na) sample, while this is lifted by only  $ 40\% $  for DTB(Na) sample. This smaller increasing amplitude should be due to the lower defect concentration in the perovskite layer atop DTB(Na), which results in less

traps that could be filled over illumination time. Comparing the absolute intensity of the two SSPL spectra, the PL quenching is much severer for the case of DTB(Na), indicative of the stronger hole-extraction ability of DTB(Na) and/or the poorer quality of perovskite film atop DTB(Na) layer. The SSPL spectra of the samples on quartz substrates were also recorded (Fig. S14), and the drastic decrease of the PL intensity after inserting CPE layers indicates the effective hole-extraction by the CPE films. The TRPL spectrum is fitted with a bi-component exponential decay model, and the time constants are summarized in Table S1, where the fast decay owns a time constant  $ (\tau_{1}) $  of 7.29 ns and 5.86 ns for the TB (Na) and DTB(Na) samples, respectively, and the slow decay has a corresponding time constant  $ (\tau_{2}) $  of 54.63 ns and 14.60 ns, respectively. As widely accepted that the fast decay arises from the charge-extraction by the charge-transporting layer and the slow one is from the non-radiative recombination in the perovskite layer [12], the smaller time constants indicate a stronger hole-extraction ability of DTB(Na) and a poorer quality of perovskite film for the DTB(Na) sample, consistent with the results from SSPL evaluation. Here it should be noted that the enhanced hole-extraction capability of DTB(Na) may stem from extensive exposure of thiophene unit to the perovskite layer, which is triggered by the increased concentration of thiophene units and the enlarged space between adjacent side-chains to get closer of the thiophene unit and perovskite material. To clarify how the defects affect the performance of the perovskite film, we carried out the thermal admittance spectroscopy (TAS) examination based on the same device configuration of PSC, in order to quantitatively determine the distribution of defect in the device. As illustrated in Fig. 4d, where  $ E(\omega) $  stands for the depth of trap-states stretching into the bandgap of perovskite and DOS is the density of state for traps [41,42], the DOS value of TB(Na) device overwhelms that of DTB(Na) device in the testing range of  $ E(\omega) < 0.375 $  eV, while this situation reverses when  $ E(\omega) > 0.375 $  eV. The integrated concentrations of defects are then summarized in Table S2. The concentrations of defects in the shallow trap-state, which locate in the bulk of the perovskite layer [43,44], are  $ 3.89 \times 10^{14} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3} $  and  $ 4.89 \times 10^{14} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3} $  for the TB (Na) and DTB(Na) devices, respectively, while the values of deep trap-state defects that stay at the interface of CPE/perovskite are  $ 6.17 \times 10^{15} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3} $  and  $ 2.16 \times 10^{15} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3} $  for the TB(Na) and DTB(Na) devices, respectively. As a critical judging point, the long-term stability of the iPSCs was also examined. As shown in Fig. 3d, the DTB(Na) device could maintain  $ 85\% $  of its initial PCE after the storage for 30 days in air at room temperature and a relative humidity of around  $ 60\% $ , which is slightly better than the TB(Na) device which retains  $ 80\% $ . Generally, the defects in the PSC device could act as active reaction centers to trigger the degradation of perovskite [44], and as a consequence, the reduced defect concentration (Table S2) could reasonably bring a better long-term stability for DTB(Na) based device.

# 3. Conclusion

To sum up, we constructed a CPE HTM named DTB(Na) based on the same main-chain of a dopant-free polymeric HTM that we previously applied in a highly efficient rPSC device. To disclose the feature of DTB (Na), its CPE analog TB(Na) composed of less thiophene units was used as a control. DTB(Na) was demonstrated to own a stronger ability of hole-extraction from and defect-passivation to the perovskite film, probably attributed to the more extensive exposure of thiophene units, which is the same as the observation in DTB(EH) for rPSCs. The DTB(Na) iPSC device attained improvement on all the photovoltaic parameters and long-term stability relative to the TB(Na) one. A much enhanced PCE value of  $ 19.92\% $  was achieved, suggesting that high-performance

polymeric HTMs in rPSCs and iPSCs can share the same main-chains. With the designing concept of this work, new efficient HTMs for iPSCs may be invented based on the efficient HTMs for rPSCs, and even those efficient HTMs for rPSCs previously reported that can only be soluble in nonpolar solvents can now be redesigned to be soluble in polar solvents, thus remarkably expanding the library of efficient HTMs for iPSCs and relieving the present limitation for efficient iPSCs. Moreover, those organic electron-transporting materials (ETMs) in iPSCs can also be referred to design new ionic ETMs used in rPSCs, which may be beneficial to their future integration in flexible electronic devices owing to their intrinsic high mechanical flexibility compared with inorganic ones.
