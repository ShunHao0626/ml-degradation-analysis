Abstract: Two dimensional (2D) perovskite solar cell (PSC) can achieve high stability by alternating interface cations. However, its main transmissive charge is limited owing to the 2D structure. Therefore, compared with a 3D device, the 2D PSC has a poor power conversion efficiency (PCE). Further enhanced performance will require an increase in the transmission dimension of 2D PSC. Here, a novel Tetraethylenepent(TEPA)-MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  analogous 2D unsymmetrical perovskite film was developed to improve the stability and PCE of the corresponding device. Based on the interaction of the active amino linear short chain of TEPA and halogen ion, the symmetry of the mechanical structure of ions is disrupted and the TEPA ion is embedded in the perovskite structure to form a perovskite structure with a dimension between 3D and 2D. Noticeably, the TEPA-MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  devices deliver high PCEs up to  $ 19.73\% $  which stands as the highest for MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  based PSC. The environmental, thermal and illumination stability also showed improvements ranging between  $ 10\% \sim 30\% $ . # 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells exhibit merits such as high-performance and cost-effectiveness and have attracted enormous attention over the years. The PCE has surged to over 24%  $  ^{1}  $  within a relatively few years of development. This is owing to the excellent properties of perovskite materials, such as: wide range spectral absorption, balanced hole and electron mobility and so on. MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $  perovskite is a derivative of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskite with a chemical composition more beneficial for film-formation and exciton diffusion  $  ^{2-6}  $ . As is known, exciton transmission and film quality are very crucial in PSCs  $  ^{7-11}  $  and they make MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $  a potentially better absorber layer. The MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $  based PSC attained a highest PCE of 19.03% since 2015  $  ^{7}  $ . Despite the rapid development of the PCE of PSCs, there remains a problem of long term stability under different conditions  $  ^{12-15}  $ .

Incidentally, 2D PSCs have an advantage of high stability by alternating interface cations between amino and halogen ions  $  ^{18}  $  . However, compared with the 3D devices, the 2D PSCs exhibit poor PCEs  $  ^{16,17}  $   due to limitations on the main transmissive charge as a result of the 2D structure  $  ^{19}  $  . Therefore, increasing the transmission channel is a feasible method to realize enhanced performance in 2D PSCs. Meanwhile, recent reports have shown that unsymmetrical perovskite structures such as:  $  \beta  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  ,   $  \gamma  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   have a more stable feature  $  ^{20}  $  . Since the migration channel of halogen ions is most compatible with symmetrical structure  $  ^{21}  $  , the unsymmetrical structure can interrupt the migration channel, fixed ions and the stability of perovskite structure.

TEPA can be dissolve in the DMF (N, N-dimethylformamide) solvent. It can interact with halogen ion by its active amino group and its linear short chain can be embedded in the perovskite structure. In this work, the tetraethylenepent(TEPA)-MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  analogous 2D perovskite structure is developed. Utilizing the linear short chain of TEPA, the TEPA molecule was

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1 Solar cell fabrication

The fabricated structure of PSC is FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /TEPA (ymol%)-MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $  /Spiro-OMeTAD/Au, the y is representative of TEPA ratio. Specific details are given as follows:

FTO-coated glass substrates were cleaned ultrasonically with deionized water, acetone and ethanol. The substrates were also exposed to oxygen plasma (50w, 5min). The c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  was deposited via chemical bath deposition at 70 °C for 1 hour. The substrates were annealed at 180 °C for 30 mins. Next, the prepared substrates were transferred into a glove box with inert atmosphere. The perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated at 3500 rpm for 40 s on the  $  TiO_{2}  $  films. The resulting perovskite films were annealed at 100 °C for 1.5 hours. The substrates were left to cool to room temperature and Spiro-OMETAD hole transport material (HTM) was spin-coated at 4000 rpm for 40 s on the perovskite films. The films were placed in a drying cupboard to oxidize for 8 hours. Finally, gold cathode was deposited via thermal evaporation. A shadow mask defined an active area of 0.1 cm  $  ^{2}  $ .

TEPA-MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  perovskite precursor solution preparation: Since a very small TEPA ratio is needed, a TEPA solution of slightly high percentage was prepared and was attenuated to the needed ratio. A varied ratio of TEPA solution (0~1.25mol%) was prepared in N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 1mL) and a 3:1 ratio of PbCl $ _2 $  and MAI was added to make a 40wt% solution in each case. The prepared solution was heated at 60 °C for 12 hours. Spiro-OMETAD precursor solution preparation and TiO $ _2 $ -sol synthesis followed previously reported methods $ ^{22} $ . # 2.2 Measurements and characterization

The  $ J-V $  curves of PSCs were measured with a Keithley 2400 source and AM1.5G solar simulator  $ (100\mathrm{mW}\cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-2}) $ . SEM (scanning electron microscopy) was performed on an FEI Quanta 250. XRD (X-ray diffraction) patterns were obtained with a D/MAX 2400 diffractometer equipped with Cu Ka radiation (Rigaku). PL spectra were recorded with a FLS980 spectrometer (Edinburgh Instruments Ltd) and TPRL spectra were recorded with a Pico Quant Fluo Quant 300. The impedance spectroscopy was achieved with an electrochemical workstation (CHI660D, Chenhua, Shanghai). The EQE spectra of devices were characterized with a QTest Station 2000ADI system (Crowntech Inc.).

# 3. Results and discussion

As is known, the performance of PSCs depends on the quality and morphology of the perovskite film. Incomplete surface coverage could lead to contact between the hole transport layer (HTL) and the electron transport layer (ETL), increasing the probability of charge recombination over transmission. Rough surface morphology also can influence the formation of the overlaying films which may further facilitate charge recombination by the formation of excessive trap states  $  ^{22}  $  . Figure 1 shows the top-view SEM images of MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $   perovskite films with various TEPA ratios. The morphology and compactness of the MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $   perovskite films are enhanced with increasing TEPA ratio. At high ratio, excess TEPA begin to appear on the MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $   perovskite surface as shown in Figure 1(f). The active amino ion of TEPA can interact with halogen ion like the amino ion of MAI, forming a composite perovskite structure. Also, due to its linear short chain, TEPA is able to embed in the perovskite structure like MAI, as shown in Figure 1(g). The structure not only exhibits a 2D PSC feature by alternating interface cations between amino and halogen ions but also have a transmission channel like a 3D PSC. Meanwhile, the migration channel is based on the symmetry of the mechanical structure of the perovskite film  $  ^{21}  $  . If a

The specific parameters are shown in Table 1 with increasing the TEPA ratio from  $ 0.00\mathrm{mol}\% $  to  $ 0.50\mathrm{mol}\% $ , an improvement PCE from  $ 17.32\% $  to 19.73 was significantly observed with a decreased series resistance and increased shunt resistance. Compared with the pristine  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  based devices, the PCE of the optimized TEPA- $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  based devices increased from  $ 17.32\% $  to  $ 19.73\% $ . The excess TEPA covers the perovskite film surface and prevents transmission between  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  and HTL. These characteristics are also evident the in SEM, XRD and  $ J-V $  results. The amino group of TEPA modifies the interlayer space and ensures compactness and improved surface morphology in  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  perovskite films. The optimal ratio of TEPA reduces grain boundaries (Figure 1(a)-(f)), which cause charge recombination[29] thereby improving the performance of the PSCs. Figure 3(b) shows that the optimized PSC has no obvious hysteresis. The hysteresis index (HI, 0.019) is less[25] than that of the pristine  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  device  $ (0.083)^{24} $ . The less HI suggests less ion migration and better stability in  $ 0.50\mathrm{mol}\% $  TEPA based  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  perovskite films[26-28].

# 4. Conclusion

In this work, TEPA-MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  composite PSCs were successfully fabricated by altering the concentration of TEPA dopant. The composite perovskite film exhibited a smoother compact surface, less grain boundary and stronger crystallinity with an optimal 0.5mol% TEPA dopant. The Bragg peaks also revealed the unsymmetrical nature of the perovskite structure. The TEPA(0.5mol%)-MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  based PSC achieved higher PCE than the pristine MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  based PSC with an enhancement from 17.32% to 19.73%. The lower series resistance and higher shunt resistance implied better charge transport and separation and the lower traps density led to improvements in the PSC performance, PL intensity and fluorescence lifetime. Stability properties also showed obvious enhancements in the TEPA(0.5mol%)-MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  PSC under environmental conditions, thermal treatment and illumination. The improvements are attributed to less traps density and higher quality of the perovskite film by TEPA doping and the formation of an analogous 2D unsymmetrical perovskite structure. The fabricated PSC not only exhibited a high 3D-like PSC structure but also showed high stability like a 2D PSC.
