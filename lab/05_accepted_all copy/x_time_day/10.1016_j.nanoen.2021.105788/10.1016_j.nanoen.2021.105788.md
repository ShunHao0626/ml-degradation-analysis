# 1. Introduction

In the past decade, the sunlight-to-electricity power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has rapidly increased from 3.8% to a certified efficiency of 25.5% [1–6] through intensive efforts in controlling the perovskite film quality [7,8], engineering of perovskite composition [9,10], and optimization of the functional layers [5,11,12]. High performances have been obtained for PSCs with a mesoporous TiO₂ (mp-TiO₂) layer [1–4,13–15]. This layer is made from anatase TiO₂ nanoparticles (NPs) having a size of ~30 nm as an active scaffold to enable efficient photogenerated electron extraction and transportation. On top of the TiO₂ layer, CH₃NH₃PbI₃ (methylammonium lead halide, briefly referred to as MAPbI₃) layer is deposited with thin disordered interface between the TiO₂ NPs and MAPbI₃ layer. This TiO₂/MAPbI₃ heterointerface region generally comprises of strained lattice structure marked by the coexistence of tetragonal and cubic perovskite phases [16,17] due to the misfit strain between the anatase structured TiO₂ and the tetragonal MAPbI₃ with preferential [110] facets at room temperature (RT), as shown in Tables S1–S3. In mesoporous organo-halide perovskite solar cells, the coexistence of tetragonal phase and cubic phase in the perovskite layer is found to result in strained crystal structure at the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   (mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $  )/perovskite heterointerface. This results in structural defects pinholes, grain boundaries, and interfacial porosity which is detrimental towards photovoltaic performance and device stability. The strain at the interface originates from the lattice mismatch between the mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticles (NPs) and perovskite. Here, a transformative approach is demonstrated to realize relaxed and high purity tetragonal phase   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   (RP) near the mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   interface region. This approach involves inserting a   $  \sim2  $   nm lattice matched buffer layer of cubic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   between mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ , which serves as a template for epitaxial growth for top   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ . The solar cell with relaxed   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   shows a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 22.12% with significantly enhanced environmental, light, and thermal stability.

Here, we provide insight into relaxing this strain at the heterointerface and in doing so provide methodology to achieve  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer with significantly reduced defects.

Strain engineering has been demonstrated in literature for stabilizing the photoactive black phase in unstable perovskites, such as black phases of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $ , by neutralizing the strain generated from the phase transition to undesirable photoinactive yellow phase. Chen et al. reported the strained epitaxial growth of RT stable  $ \alpha $ - $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  single-crystal thin films on lattice mismatched  $ \mathrm{MAPbCl}_2\mathrm{Br}_{3 - x} $  substrates which exhibited lower bandgap and higher hole mobility [19]. Steele et al. demonstrated that RT stable black phase  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  could be maintained with the aid of the in- and out-of-plane biaxial strain, making it possible for the fabrication of light-emitting diode. The biaxial strain of the perovskite was generated by the substrate clamping force between the bottom indium tin oxide (ITO) and top glass [20]. Zhao et al. have discussed the effect of residual strain in a   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   polycrystalline film deposited on ITO substrate with regard to the device performance and stability. This is driven by the presence of a  $ \sim $ 2 nm lattice matched cubic CsPbBr $ _3 $  layer (Table S4) that acts as an epitaxial growth template for the RP, as illustrated in Schematic 1. These results demonstrate that engineering the crystal strain at the perovskite/transporter heterointerfaces is a facile pathway towards achieving high-performance perovskite optoelectronics.

# 2.2. Preparation of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   solution

The  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  precursor solution was prepared by dissolving different molar concentrations (25 mM, 50 mM, 100 mM and 200 mM) of CsBr and  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  (1:1) in 1 mL mixed solvent with 1:4 (volume ratio) of anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO).

# 2.3. Preparation of MAPbI$_{3}$ solution

1 M solution containing   $  PbI_{2}  $   and MAI (1:1) was prepared in DMF. The solution was filtered by using 0.2   $  \mu  $  m PTFE filter. Then, 5 mL of the filtrate was placed in a vial and kept in an oil bath undisturbed at 120 °C for crystal formation. To increase the solvent evaporation, a vacuum pump was used to keep the pressure in the inner vial at 0.7 atm. 120 mg of obtained   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   crystals were placed into a vial and exposed to   $  CH_{3}NH_{2}  $   gas to form a light-yellow liquid intermedia. Then, 100   $  \mu  $  L anhydrous ACN and 90   $  \mu  $  L methylamine solution (33 wt% in absolute ethanol (EtOH)) were added into the liquid intermedia to form the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   precursor solution. The obtained   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   precursor solution is nonionic and rapid crystallization of the perovskite can be achieved. The non-ionic   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   ink method is different from traditional perovskite precursor solution in terms of the solvent. Briefly, the nonionic ink disperses the perovskite in a mixed solvent of ACN & EtOH with the presence of monomethylamine (MMA) gas (MMA-gas); while the typical precursor solution dissolves the perovskite in DMF [or DMSO or g-butyrolactone (GBL)]. Different from the solvation of perovskite into free ions such as   $  MA^{+}  $ ,   $  Pb^{2+}  $   and   $  I^{-}  $  in the DMF (or DMSO or GBL), the non-ionic ink contains an MMA-gas infiltrated intermediate (MA (MMA)  $  _{n13}  $  ) dispersed in the mixed solvent of ACN & EtOH. MA(MMA)  $  _{n13}  $   is an intermediate, soluble in low boiling point solvent such as ACN and EtOH. The structure characteristics of this MA(MMA)  $  _{n13}  $   intermediate in the nonionic ink has been studied in detail in our prior investigations [38].

# 2.5. Fabrication of electron-only and hole-only devices

Electron-only devices with FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $ /mp-TiO $ _2 $ /CsPbBr $ _3 $  (with 50 mM or without)/MAPbI $ _3 $ /PC $ _{61} $ BM/Ag structure were constructed. The c-TiO $ _2 $ , mp-TiO $ _2 $ , CsPbBr $ _3 $  and MAPbI $ _3 $  layers were spin-coated onto FTO glass with the same method and condition for device fabrication. The 20 mg/mL of PC $ _{60} $ BM/chlorobenzene solution was spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, 100 nm of Ag electrode was deposited on the substrate to complete the device fabrication. Hole-only devices were fabricated with a structure FTO/PEDOT:PSS/50 mM CsPbBr $ _3 $  (with or without)/MAPbI $ _3 $ /Spiro-OMeTAD/Au. A PEDOT:PSS layer was deposited on top of the FTO substrate by spin-coating the precursor solution at 4000 rpm for 30 s. Then, it was heated at 140 °C for 20 min in a N $ _2 $  filled glovebox. The CsPbBr $ _3 $ , MAPbI $ _3 $  and spiro-OMeTAD layers were spin-coated onto the substrate with the same method and condition for device fabrication. Finally, 100 nm of Au electrode was deposited on the substrate to complete the device fabrication. The J-V responses of the electron-only and hole-only devices were measured by a Keithley 2400 source meter at RT in the dark.

# 2.6. Solar cell characterization

Photovoltaic performance of solar cell device was evaluated by measuring the  $ J-V $  characteristics of unencapsulated device in the air (RT) under simulated AM 1.5 G light irradiation generated by a 450 W xenon lamp (Oriel Sol 2A ABA), which was calibrated by a standard reference silicon cell covered with KG5 filter glass (Newport). The  $ J-V $  curves were recorded by a Keithley 2400 source meter. The sweeping conditions were: reverse scan (1.2 V to  $ -0.02\mathrm{V} $ , with different scan rates, and no delay time) and forward scan (-0.02 to 1.2 V, with different scan rates, and no delay time). The measured device size area was  $ 0.096~\mathrm{cm}^2 $  as defined with a metal mask. IPCE was measured by a Newport QuantX-300 quantum efficiency measurement system at ambient condition without encapsulation. Steady-state output of the solar cell device was measured under simulated AM 1.5G light irradiation using a 660E Electrochemical workstation (CHI Instruments Inc.). EIS of the solar cell device was measured in the dark at  $ 0.9\mathrm{V} $  bias using the same Electrochemical workstation in the frequency range of  $ 10^{-2} - 10^{6}\mathrm{Hz} $ .

The high quality MAPbI $ _3 $  layers were deposited on top of the FTO-TiO $ _2 $  and FTO-TiO $ _2 $ -CsPbBr $ _3 $  substrates by one-step spin-coating of a nonionic precursor solution, as shown in Experimental section. The conventional ionic precursor ink containing highly disordered mobile ions (such as MA $ ^{+} $ , Pb $ ^{2+} $  and I $ ^{-} $ ) in high-boiling-point solvents [dimethylacetamide (DMA,  $ \sim $ 165  $ ^{\circ} $ C), DMSO ( $ \sim $ 189  $ ^{\circ} $ C), GBL ( $ \sim $ 204  $ ^{\circ} $ C), or DMF ( $ \sim $ 153  $ ^{\circ} $ C)] requires additional energy exchange with its surroundings to reorganize disordered precursor ions and assemble them into the ordered crystal lattice. In comparison, the nonionic MAPbI $ _3 $  precursor solution with less-disordered mobile ions overcomes this problem. Briefly, the nonionic precursor solution consists of a less disorder MA(MMA)nI $ _3 $  intermediate dispersed in the solution, the transition from this intermediate state towards the final black-phase crystal is more thermodynamically favourable than the transition from disordered ionic state to the black-phase perovskite [36–38].

A highly ordered and uniform  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  thin film with less defects can be obtained using nonionic precursor solution (the deposition processes of the perovskite films can be found in Supporting Video 1 and 2) [18, 38]. # 4. Conclusion

Results reported here demonstrate that relaxed and high purity tetragonal  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  in the mesoporous solar cell architecture can be obtained by inclusion of a  $ \sim 2\mathrm{nm} $  thick lattice matched cubic  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  layer. In conventional mesoporous device, the coexistence of tetragonal phase and cubic phase and strained crystal structure in the  $ \mathrm{mp - TiO_2} $  region, which results in structural and electronic defects that have detrimental effect on photovoltaic performance and device stability. After the insertion of the compact and uniform cubic  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  layer, relaxed and high purity tetragonal  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  with decreased charge carriers trap density is obtained in the  $ \mathrm{mp - TiO_2} $  region. The modified relaxed  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based device exhibited a PCE of  $ 22.12\% $  with remarkably enhanced environmental (maintained  $ 94.2\% $  of initial PCE after 45 days), thermal (retained  $ 76.3\% $  of its original PCE after  $ 21\mathrm{h} $  of  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  annealing), and light (kept  $ 50.0\% $  of its original PCE after  $ 240\mathrm{h} $  of 1 Sun illumination) stability as compared to its strained counterpart under similar test conditions (PCE reduced to  $ 70.2\% $ ,  $ 49.4\% $ , and  $ 20.0\% $  of its original PCE for the environmental, thermal, and light measurements, respectively). These results demonstrate that strain engineering provides a robust solution for realization of high-performance perovskite-based optoelectronics.
