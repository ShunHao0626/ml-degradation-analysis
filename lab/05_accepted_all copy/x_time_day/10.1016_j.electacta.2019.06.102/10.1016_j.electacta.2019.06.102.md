# 1. Introduction

Hybrid organic-inorganic perovskite materials developed rapidly in the past decade [1,2]. The power conversion efficient (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has rapidly been increased to 23.7% from 3.8% within less ten years due to their excellent photoelectric properties [3–6]. The planar structure PSCs have attracted much attention due to low temperature fabrication process and high performance with PCE over 21% [7–9]. Most high performance planar n-i-p configuration PSCs use stannic oxide (SnO $ _2 $ ) and Spiro-oMeTAD as electron transport layer (ETL) and hole transport layer (HTL) materials. The SnO $ _2 $  ETL could be prepared at 150°C and exhibit excellent electron transport ability. However, the doped Spiro-oMeTAD shows complicated synthesis process and instability, which may impede their commercialization. The inverted p-i-n configuration PSCs usually using nickel oxide (NiO $ _x $ ) and [6,6]-phenyl-C $ _{61} $ -butyric acid methyl (PCBM) as HTL and ETL become

# ABSTRACT

Poly(9-vinylcarbazole) (PVK) was introduced to modify the interface of perovskite/PCBM. The modified PVK material can induce the formation of the quasi-continuous sheet-shaped perovskite film at the surface of 3D perovskite layer, which may reduce the exposed grain boundaries. Besides, the PVK modified perovskite layer can reduce carrier recombination and accumulation at the interface of perovskite/PCBM and improve the crystallinity of perovskite layer and the interface contact properties. The PVK modification can greatly improve the PCEs and thermal and moisture stability and suppress the J-V hysteresis of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). PSCs with PVK modification achieved champion PCE of  $ 19.03\% $  (average PCE of  $ 18.40\% $ ), maintained  $ 80\% $  of the original PCE value aging for 50 days under ambient conduction without encapsulation. Furthermore, PSCs with PVK modification can maintain  $ 87\% $  of the origin PCEs aging for  $ 3\mathrm{h} $  under  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  thermal stress, which are greatly higher than that  $ (26\%) $  of PSCs without PVK modification.

However, the relative low conductivity and surface defects of the pristine   $  NiO_{x}  $   usually limits the performance of p-i-n planar PSCs. It's proven that Ag and Au nanoparticles can improve carrier transport and extraction abilities of solar cells [13–15]. Thus, doping Ag nanoparticles is expected to improve the conductivity of   $  NiO_{x}  $   layer without further annealing process at high temperature. Therefore, the Ag nanoparticles doped   $  NiO_{x}  $   (Ag@  $  NiO_{x}  $  ) would be an ideal hole transport layer in p-i-n planar PSCs.

PCBM is the most common ETL material used in p-i-n planar PSCs due to its excellent conductivity and electron extraction capability. However, limited by the low solubility and viscosity, it's difficult to form uniform and defect free PCBM films on the rough surface of perovskite   $  [16]  $  . Moreover, the aggregation of PCBM results in the incomplete coverage on the perovskite layer, which induces charge recombination and accumulation at perovskite/PCBM interface, as well as the degradation of perovskite   $  [17–19]  $  . It is worth note that PCBM ETL is not an ideal blocking layer in prevention humidity and oxygen in external environment or of ion migration from perovskite layer to Ag electrode   $  [20]  $  . Previously report proved that Poly(9-vinylcarbazole) (PVK) can be used as multifunctional layer (around 10 nm) between perovskite and hole transport layer, which facilitated the hole transportation and improved the moisture stability of n-i-p configuration PSCs [27]. In this work, an ultra-thin PVK interlayer was combined with n-type organic material PCBM to form uniform and compact electron transport layer. PVK dissolved into anti-solvent (chlorobenzene) improved the surface and crystallization of perovskite layer during replacing DMSO solvent due to the interaction between Pb $  ^{2+}  $  and N atom in PVK [28]. Therefore, the PVK interfacial modification greatly improve PCEs and stability of PSCs, including moisture and thermal stabilities.

Fig. 5a shows the dependence of  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  on the light intensity. For p-i-n planar PSCs,  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  is function of cell temperature (T) and short-circuit photo current ( $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $ ), described as,  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}} = (nk_{\mathrm{B}}Tq^{-1})ln(J_{\mathrm{sc}}/J_0 + 1)  $ , where n is ideality factor,  $  k_{\mathrm{B}}  $  is the Boltzmann constant, T is absolute temperature, and q is elementary charge,  $  J_0  $  is saturated current density (in the dark). Because  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}} >> J_0  $  and  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}} \propto  $  light intensity,  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  dependence on the natural logarithm of light intensity thus gives a slope of  $  nk_{\mathrm{B}}Tq^{-1}  $ . # 4. Conclusion

In summary, p-type Poly(9-vinylcarbazole) (PVK) was introduced as interfacial layer between perovskite and PCBM to reduce carrier recombination and accumulation. At the same time, PVK modification layer can increase VBM energy band, and obviously improve the contact property and the crystallization of perovskite layer, which greatly reduce the trap state density and carrier recombination. The formation of the quasi-continuous sheet-shaped perovskite film at the surface of 3D perovskite film leads to better thermal and moisture stability due to reducing the exposed grain boundaries of 3D perovskite film. The effect of PVK modification greatly boost PCE and stability of PSCs, including moisture and thermal stabilities, got  $ 19.03\% $  of PCE (average PCE of  $ 18.40\% $ ) and maintained  $ 80\% $  of the original PCE value aging for 50 days under ambient air. Therefore, developing p-type PVK as modification layer at the interface of perovskite/PCBM is an effective strategy to improve PCEs and stability of p-i-n planar PSCs.
