# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) have already achieved the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 25.5\% $  [1]. The ambipolar semiconductor optical properties of perovskite make it possible to design different device architectures [2]. Among them, the planar inverted device attracts extensive attention and presents the potential to become the commercialized photovoltaic device, which are attributed to the low-temperature solution processing and simpler device architecture [3]. Nickel oxide  $ (\mathrm{NiO_x}) $  is the most suitable hole transport layer (HTL) for planar inverted device due to its low cost, large bandgap with high transmittance in visible range and deep valence band edge which can well match the energy level of different perovskite materials [4,5]. However, the PCE of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ -based device is lower in comparison with that of regular devices. Particularly, PVSCs based on  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  HTL typically present inferior open-circuit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{oc}}) $  compared with the organic HTLs, such as poly(4-butylphenyl

In   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based perovskite solar cells (PVSCs), the interfacial redox reaction between   $  Ni^{3+}  $   (on the surface of   $  NiO_{x}  $  ) and A-site cation salt (MAI in perovskite precursor solution) is invariably ignored. This adverse reaction will generate   $  PbI_{2}  $  -rich hole extraction barriers at the   $  NiO_{x}  $  -perovskite interface, which limits hole transmission and increases charge recombination, thus resulting in open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) loss. Furthermore, it will accelerate perovskite degradation by deprotonating the precursor amine and oxidizing iodide to interstitial iodine, which induces the severe instability of devices. Herein, a physical separation strategy by introducing a modifier layer to obstruct the detrimental reaction is explored. The results demonstrate that the trimethylolpropane tris(2-methyl-1-aziridinepropionate) (SaC-100) depositing onto   $  NiO_{x}  $   can suppress the reaction between   $  Ni^{3+}  $   and MAI to endow the improvement of conductivity and reduction of interfacial defects, thus reducing   $  V_{oc}  $   loss and enhancing device stability. Moreover, the interfacial energy level alignment and the morphology of perovskite are also optimized. As a result, the PVSCs device based on   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $   presents the best power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 20.21% with a superior   $  V_{oc}  $   value of 1.12 V. Furthermore, the device shows better light and thermal stability because of the hindering effect and defect passivation effect of SaC-100.

In addition to the above-mentioned optimization strategies, doping with metal elements can also increase the conductivity of   $  NiO_{x}  $  , which have already been proved by several effective dopants including Li, Cu, Cs, Ag, Co, and Mg [24–28]. Such surface modification and doping strategies have resulted in high-performance   $  NiO_{x}  $   and induced high-quality perovskite films, leading to high PCE value with minor   $  V_{oc}  $   loss. Although various approaches have been proved to be facile and effective in reducing the   $  V_{oc}  $   loss and improving the efficiency of   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based device, there is still a lack of consensus on detailed origin of the intrinsic limitations in   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based devices. Considering the complexity and multiformity of the origin for   $  V_{oc}  $   loss, Michael et al. has provided a comprehensive discussion and put forward a new insight into the   $  V_{oc}  $   loss based on the recent development [29]. Unlike the results described in the previous researches, which have ascribed   $  V_{oc}  $   loss diminution to the work function change or interfacial defects passivation of   $  NiO_{x}  $   [30–32], Michael et al. has certified that the primary cause for lower   $  V_{oc}  $   is the interfacial redox reaction between metal cation sites (  $  Ni^{3+}  $   on the surface of the   $  NiO_{x}  $  ) and A-site cation salts (MAI or FAI in perovskite precursors) [29]. Unfortunately, this detrimental reaction will reduce the ratio of   $  Ni^{3+}  $   in   $  NiO_{x}  $   and generate PbI  $  _{2}  $  -rich hole extraction barriers at the   $  NiO_{x}  $  -perovskite interface, which hampers its intrinsic conductivity and increases charge recombination, resulting in serious   $  V_{oc}  $   loss. Furthermore, it will also accelerate perovskite degradation by deprotonating the precursor amine and oxidizing iodide to interstitial iodine, which induces the severe instability of devices. Thus, they have demonstrated that manipulating the perovskite precursor can efficiently

compensate the undesired effects ascribed from the interfacial redox reaction, which has yielded an increase in  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  values for PVSCs. Although the use of excess A-site salts in perovskite precursor solution can make up for the losses of precursor amine,  $  \mathrm{Ni}^{3+}  $  has still been consumed due to the unavoidable reaction. Thus, it is of great significance to find efficient strategy to simultaneously maintain the amount of  $  \mathrm{Ni}^{3+}  $  and obstruct the undesirable reaction between  $  \mathrm{NiO_x}  $  and perovskite.

In this work, a physical separation strategy by introducing a modifier layer to impede the detrimental reaction is explored. The trimethylol-propane tris(2-methyl-1-aziridinepropionate) (SaC-100), which can work as blocked layer and surface modifier simultaneously, is successfully employed to the interface between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   in the planar inverted PVSCs. The molecular structure of SaC-100 is shown in Fig. S1. SaC-100 is a trifunctional pyridine crosslinking agent, which can dissolve in polar solvents such as isopropanol and DMF. The SaC-100 molecule with N and O atoms can easily produce Lewis adducts with uncoordinated Ni and Pb ions in   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   [13,20], respectively, which is favorable for passivating uncoordinated Ni ion-induced defects at the top surface of   $  NiO_{x}  $   and buried interfacial defects of perovskite, ensuring better interfacial contact and efficient hole transmission. Interestingly, the content of   $  Ni^{3+}  $   in   $  NiO_{x}  $   can slightly be improved and the interfacial redox reaction can efficiently be hindered after SaC-100 modification, indicating the retention of conductivity and the reduction of interfacial defects. Meanwhile, the interfacial energy level alignment is also adjusted due to the existence of dipole moment.

Moreover, the deposition of SaC-100 onto   $  NiO_{x}  $   can induce outstanding improvement in the morphology and crystallinity of perovskite owing to the better interfacial contact. Last but not least, SaC-100 blanketing on   $  NiO_{x}  $   inhibits the accelerated decomposition of perovskite by interfacial redox reaction, which is conductive to improve the long-term stability of inverted devices. Hence,   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $  -based device with high conductivity, favorable energy level alignment, less interfacial defects, and high-quality perovskite film have contributed the remarkable photovoltaic performance in inverted PVSCs. The planar inverted devices (ITO/  $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100/MAPbI_{3}/PCBM/BCP/Ag  $  ) obtain a significant enhancement PCE of 20.21%, with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.12 V, a current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 22.37 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and a fill factor (FF) of 81.26%. Meanwhile, the SaC-100-treated devices maintain >80% and >85% of their initial PCE values after 30 days of thermal stability testing at 85 °C and light stability testing at AM 1.5 G in nitrogen, respectively. This work provides a unique insight into the degradation of   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based perovskite and explores a physical separation strategy to obstruct the interfacial redox reaction between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and perovskite.

# 2. Results and discussion

In order to identify whether SaC-100 layer between  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  can obstruct interfacial redox reaction, a series of film characterizations based on  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  and  $ \mathrm{NiO_x / SaC - 100} $  are carried out. UV-ozone (UVO) surface treatment is performed to increase the amount of  $ \mathrm{Ni}^{3 + } $  on

the surface   $  NiO_{x}  $   (Fig. S2 and Fig. 1a), which can more clearly identify the redox reaction between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and perovskite ingredients. Photographs qualitative assessments of   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $   without any post-treatment (labeled as “as-dep”), treated with UVO and various perovskite precursor ingredients (DMF,   $  PbI_{2}  $   and MAI) are presented. Moreover, the changes in absorbance of UVO-treated   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $   after infiltration with   $  PbI_{2}  $ , and MAI solutions (0.1 M in DMF) for 1 min, respectively, have also been presented. As shown from the photographs (Fig. S3a) and UV–Vis absorption spectra (Fig. S3c), it is obvious that   $  NiO_{x}  $   (after UVO treatment) film has been bleached with infiltration by MAI solution, suggesting that MAI has clearly reacted with   $  NiO_{x}  $ . This result is corresponding with the previous study, which has proved that   $  Ni^{3+}  $   in   $  NiO_{x}  $   can act both as Brønsted proton acceptors and Lewis electron acceptors [29], deprotonating cationic amines and oxidizing iodide species (Schematic diagram of the interfacial redox reactions between MAI and   $  Ni^{3+}  $   is represented in Fig. S4). Excitingly, with the SaC-100 treatment, perovskite precursor ingredients have not reacted with   $  NiO_{x}  $   to an extent that is discernible from the photographs and UV–Vis absorption spectra (Fig. S3b, d). We speculate that the SaC-100 layer between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   can obstruct the redox reaction and reduce the interfacial defects.

To verify this speculation, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) has been carried out on  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  and  $ \mathrm{NiO_x / SaC - 100} $  to provide further mechanistic insight of redox reaction between  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI_3} $ . Keeping this in mind, it is of great interest to further investigate the effect of SaC-100 layer on perovskite film. XRD patterns of perovskite are taken to evaluate the effect of SaC-100 on perovskite crystallinity. As shown in Fig. 2a, two strong XRD diffraction peaks at  $ 14.5^{\circ} $  (110) and  $ 28.4^{\circ} $  (220) are observed for both  $ \mathrm{NiO_x / MAPbI_3} $  and  $ \mathrm{NiO_x / SaC - 100 / } $   $ \mathrm{MAPbI_3} $ . It is recognized that the absolute intensities of both (110) and (220) peaks for the perovskite on  $ \mathrm{NiO_x / SaC - 100} $  are obviously higher than those based on pristine  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ , implying that the SaC-100 modification has improved the perovskite crystallinity [33]. Moreover, when the SaC-100 modifier layer is introduced, the signal peak of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  disappear and the peak position of (110) shifts to lower degree (Fig. 2b), indicating that the interfacial defects have been restrained and the lattice strain of perovskite has been relaxed, which is similar with previously reported results [34]. According to the above results, we speculate that the reduction of interfacial defects is expected to provide better photocarrier extraction characteristics. A series of methods for analysis and characterization of PVSCs with different concentration of SaC-100 (  $  C_{SaC-100}  $  ) demonstrate that the optimum   $  C_{SaC-100}  $   is 10 mg/mL, which is adopted in the following discussion. As shown in Fig. 2f, the lower   $  n_{t}  $   and higher   $  \mu_{h}  $   of   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $ -based device indicate that the SaC-100 can efficiently reduce the interfacial defects and facilitate charges extraction.

The electronic property is clearly an important index for modified   $  NiO_{x}  $   films in PVSCs. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurement has been performed to determine the valence band (VB) of   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $   (Fig. 3a). As shown in Fig. 3b, the VB (-5.3 eV) of   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $   is more well-aligned with   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   (-5.4 eV) compared to   $  NiO_{x}  $   (-5.2 eV), which is beneficial to the holes extraction. Further, atomic force microscopy (AFM) and contact angel measurements are performed to investigate the surface properties of modified   $  NiO_{x}  $ . As shown in Fig. 3c, d, the morphology of   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $   is more uniform and smoother than that of the pristine   $  NiO_{x}  $ , with a decreased root mean square (RMS) values from 16.5 nm to 13.5 nm, which will be conducive to increasing the interfacial contact and reducing the interfacial defects. The contact angels of water on   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $   are   $  \sim58^{\circ}  $   and   $  \sim69^{\circ}  $   (Fig. 3e), respectively. Thus, the   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $   serve as substrate for perovskite growth may reduce the nucleation site and slow down the crystallization rate of perovskite due to the higher surface energy. Considering that SaC-100 can be dissolved in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), the contact angel measurement of different layers after being washed with DMF are carried out. As shown in Fig. 3f, the contact angel on   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $   after DMF treatment is measured as   $  \sim69^{\circ}  $ , which is clearly larger than that on   $  NiO_{x}  $   (  $  \sim58^{\circ}  $ ), suggesting SaC-100 cannot be completely removed during spin-coating of the perovskite solution. The effect of SaC-100 on the perovskite morphology is investigated via scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (Figs. 3g, h, and S8). It can be seen that most of the perovskite grains on   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $   are notably larger than those on   $  NiO_{x}  $ , suggesting that SaC-100 can serve as a template to control nucleation and crystal growth of perovskite, due to the complexations of SaC-100 with the uncoordinated Ni in   $  NiO_{x}  $   and uncoordinated Pb in   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ , thus improving the quality of perovskite film.

The device with NiO $ _x $ /SaC-100 achieves a PCE of 20.21% with a V $ _{oc} $  of 1.12 V, a J $ _{sc} $  of 22.37 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and a FF of 81.26% (Fig. 4b), which is superior to the reference device with a PCE of 17.54%, a V $ _{oc} $  of 1.05 V, a J $ _{sc} $  of 21.29 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and an FF of 78.09%. It shows the same trend as other p-i-n PSCs based on NiO $ _x $  modified with different molecules (Table S3). Furthermore, the generally higher PCE with narrow distribution can be observed from the histograms of PCE for 50 devices with SaC-100 treatment (Fig. 4c). The outstanding reproducibility is attributed to the better interfacial contact between NiO $ _x $  and perovskite. Fig. 4d presents the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of the corresponding devices (based on NiO $ _x $  and NiO $ _x $ /SaC-100), showing the integrated J $ _{sc} $  values of 20.35 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  and 21.38 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , respectively, which are in agreement with the values acquired from the  $ J $ - $ V $  curves. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements are carried out to verify the charge-carrier recombination in devices. The Nyquist plots for the devices with and without SaC-100 treatment are shown in Fig. S9b. The larger semicircle corresponding to

the device with SaC-100 treatment demonstrates a larger recombination resistance, which indicates the lower carriers recombination rate due to the reduction of interfacial defects. To further investigate the charge-carrier dynamics of devices, the dependences of  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  and  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  versus light intensity for devices based on  $  \mathrm{NiO_x}  $  and  $  \mathrm{NiO_x / SaC - 100}  $  are measured, respectively. As shown in Fig. S9c, a slop of  $  1.37~\mathrm{kT / q}  $  is obtained from  $  \mathrm{NiO_x / SaC - 100}  $ -based device, which is lower than that of  $  \mathrm{NiO_x}  $ -based device (1.53 kT/q), indicating that SaC-100 can significantly reduce trap-assisted recombination in the device [36]. Moreover, Fig. S9d shows the good linear relationship between  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  and light intensity at short circuit condition. The slope for  $  \mathrm{NiO_x / SaC - 100}  $ -based device is closer to 1, implying that the bimolecular recombination in the device is almost negligible. The dark-current measurements demonstrate that the leakage current is reduced and the charge-carrier recombination is suppressed for  $  \mathrm{NiO_x / SaC - 100}  $ -based device (Fig. 4e), indicating that more charge carriers can pass through the device instead of direct shunting. The improvement in photovoltaics performance can also be reflected by their corresponding steady-state power output (SPO) under a constant voltage bias near the maximum power point.  $  \mathrm{NiO_x}  $ - and  $  \mathrm{NiO_x / SaC - 100}  $ -based devices exhibit the SPOs of  $  17.6\%  $  and  $  20.3\%  $ , respectively (Fig. 4f), which are in agreement with the  $  J - V  $  measurement. To further verify the lower   $  V_{oc}  $   is ascribed from the interfacial redox reaction between   $  Ni^{3+}  $   and perovskite. The   $  V_{oc}  $   and PCE distribution for a batch of 10 devices based on   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $   (with UVO treatment for different times) are presented in Fig. 5a, b. UVO surface treatment is performed to accelerate potential reaction kinetics for the redox reaction between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and perovskite. It can be observed that the descent rate of   $  V_{oc}  $   for   $  NiO_{x}  $ -based devices is more obvious with increasing UVO-treatment times. While for   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $ -based devices, there is small decreasing rate comparing to the initial   $  V_{oc}  $   values. These results demonstrate that SaC-100 endows the significant barrier effect of interfacial reaction. In addition, Fig. S11a, b show that the descent rate of   $  J_{sc}  $   for   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $ -based devices is more obvious than the   $  NiO_{x}  $ -based devices with the increase of UVO-treatment time owing to the less   $  Ni^{3+}  $   involving in the redox reaction and the more   $  Ni^{3+}  $   retaining in the   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $   film. This would result in the lower light transmittance of   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $   film, thus lead to the   $  J_{sc}  $   and PCE of the devices show the same decay trend (upon prolonged UVO treatment time). This phenomenon demonstrates that the SaC-100 effectively suppress the interfacial reaction of   $  Ni^{3+}  $   and perovskite. Consider that   $  NiO_{x}  $   will accelerate the degradation of perovskite via deprotonating the precursor amine and oxidizing iodide to interstitial iodine (be induced from interfacial redox reaction), it is of great significance to examine the long-term stability of perovskite films and unencapsulated devices to determine if the perovskite degradation will be inhibited by introducing SaC-100 layer. The XRD patterns of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ ,   $  NiO_{x}/MAPbI_{3}  $   and   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100/MAPbI_{3}  $   and normalized averaged PCE of corresponding devices have been measured under 85 °C or continuous illumination conditions in a nitrogen glovebox for 30 days. As shown in Fig. 5c, d, the intensities of PbI  $  _{2}  $   peaks for   $  NiO_{x}/MAPbI_{3}  $   films upon 85 °C and continuous illumination conditions, are significantly higher than those of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films, indicating   $  NiO_{x}  $   can accelerate perovskite degradation. Most importantly, the   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100/MAPbI_{3}  $   films display negligible diffraction peak of PbI  $  _{2}  $ . The enhancement thermal and light stability can benefit from the physical separation effect of SaC-100, which can effectively suppress perovskite degradation caused by interfacial redox reaction. Moreover, the normalized PCEs of corresponding devices aging at 85 °C or continuous illumination over time have been conducted (Fig. 5e, f). The SaC-100-treated devices maintain >80% and >85% of their initial PCE values after 30 days of thermal stability testing at 85 °C and light stability testing at AM 1.5 G in nitrogen, respectively. Similarly, after thermal stability testing in the air at 85 °C for 7 days with RH of 40–50%, the pristine and SaC-100-modified devices remain 31% and 85% of the initial PCE values, respectively (Fig. S12).

# 3. Conclusions

The detrimental interfacial redox reaction between   $  Ni^{3+}  $   in   $  NiO_{x}  $   and MAI (deprotonating the precursor amine and oxidizing iodide to interstitial iodine) will result in serious   $  V_{oc}  $   loss for devices and accelerate the degradation of perovskite. Thus, a physical separation strategy by introducing a modifier layer to impede the detrimental reaction is explored. The used of SaC-100 can suppress the reaction between   $  Ni^{3+}  $   and MAI to endow the improvement of conductivity and reduction of interfacial defects. Moreover, the interfacial energy level alignment and the morphology of perovskite are also optimized. The   $  NiO_{x}/SaC-100  $  -based device present a PCE of 20.21%, with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.12 V, a current density   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.37 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and a FF of 81.26%. Meanwhile, the SaC-100-treated devices maintain >80% and >85% of their initial PCE values after 30 days of thermal stability testing at 85 °C and light stability testing at AM 1.5 G in nitrogen, respectively.
