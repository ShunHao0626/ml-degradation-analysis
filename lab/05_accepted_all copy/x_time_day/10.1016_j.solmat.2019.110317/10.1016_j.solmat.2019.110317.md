# 1. Introduction

Since perovskite materials were firstly introduced to photovoltaics in 2009, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted tremendous attentions because of outstanding device properties [1-3]. Regardless of the rapid development in cell efficiency, the instability issue of hybrid halide perovskites remains to be solved [4,5]. Replacing organic components with inorganic ions (such as  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $ ) to prepare inorganic perovskites is an effective way to improve the stability of perovskite films [6]. Among various inorganic perovskites,  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  is an outstanding wide band gap ( $ \sim 2.3\mathrm{eV} $ ) semiconductor with high visible light transmittance and desirable stability towards both heat and humidity, which proves its promising potentials in building integrated photovoltaics and tandem solar cells [7-10]. In the past few years,  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  PSCs have experienced a rapid development [11-13] and a power conversion efficiency (PCE) record of  $ 10.6\% $  has been reported by Tang's group recently [14]. # ABSTRACT

Inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite has emerged as a promising material for fabricating highly stable perovskite solar cell devices. However, during the formation of thermally evaporated   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film, an all-solid reaction between CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   leads to non-uniform interfaces and high defect density in the perovskite film. In this work, we utilize a two-step sintering (TSS) method which successfully modifies the morphologies of thermally evaporated   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films and the power conversion efficiency of a champion   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite solar cell achieves 9.35%. Steady-state photoluminescence, time-resolved photoluminescence and space-charge-limited-current results prove that the TSS process can effectively reduce defect densities also. Besides these improvements, the TSS device maintains 94% of initial efficiency after being heated at 100 °C without encapsulation for over 40 days under ambient lab condition. In comparison, the untreated device reduces about 15%. Transmission electron microscope results further prove the reduction of line defects in   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   crystals after TSS treatment.

However, few high performance   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   devices are fabricated via industrial technologies like thermal evaporation. Up to date, PCE of devices made of thermally evaporated   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite films is still less than 8% [17,18], which is much lower than solution-processed devices [14,19]. Generally, in a typical thermal evaporation process, the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film formed after a solid-solid reaction between CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $  . However, defects (such as dislocations) would generate and multiply unavoidably while materials pass through an all-solid-state reacting stage to form dense films or bulks [20,21], thus giving rise to the unsatisfactory PCE of thermally evaporated   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs. Hence, controlling and reducing defect density in thermally evaporated   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film is essential.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Device fabrication

All-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSC devices were fabricated with an architecture of FTO/compact   $  TiO_{2}/CsPbBr_{3}/carbon  $   electrode (see Fig. 1a and b). FTO glass (purchased from OPV Technology Corp.) was firstly etched by using a femtosecond laser machine (Universal VLS2.30, USA). After then, etched FTO glass was cleaned with ultra pure water and ethanol under an ultrasonic condition for 15 min successively. The washed glass was dried by a dried airflow, followed by 15 min ultraviolet ozone treating. The compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was prepared by following steps: 1. Immersing the as-cleaned FTO glass in a 0.2 mol   $  L^{-1}TiCl_{4}  $   aqueous solution at 70 °C for 40 min. 2. Rinsing the as-treated FTO substrate with pure water and drying it with a dried airflow. This process was repeated twice in order to obtain desirable   $  TiO_{2}  $   thickness, and then the FTO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate was annealed at 450 °C for 30 min with a heating rate of 15 °C   $  min^{-1}  $  . After that, as described in Fig. 1c, CsBr (  $  \geq99.999\%  $  , purchased from Alfa Aesar) and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   (  $  \geq99.99\%  $  , purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.) precursors were thermally deposited onto the FTO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate sequentially in a vacuum chamber. The FTO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate was attached to a sample holder which kept rotating during the whole evaporating process. The evaporation rates of CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   were both set to 5 Ås  $  ^{-1}  $  , monitored by respective quartz crystal microbalance sensor (sensor 1 for CsBr and sensor 2 for   $  PbBr_{2}  $  ). Then, the scheduled sintering processes were carried out in a muffle furnace under ambient circumstances. The sintering temperature of as prepared CsBr/  $  PbBr_{2}  $   films was programmed at 320 °C and the heating rate was 15 °C   $  min^{-1}  $  . The first-sintering stage was held for 20 min, and then decreased to 200 °C, 250 °C and 300 °C (TSS at 200 °C, 250 °C and 300 °C) respectively for the second-sintering stage for another 40 min (Fig. 1d). After   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films were cooled down naturally to room temperature, carbon pastes (purchased from JUJO Printing Supplies & Technology Corp.) were directly printed on the top of perovskite films by using a doctor-blading technique. All-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite devices were ready for test after the as-printed carbon pastes were dried at 100 °C for 10 min.

# 2.2. Characterizations

The properties and morphologies of perovskite films were investigated by using an X-ray diffractometer (PANalytical-Empyrean, Cu Kα), an ultraviolet spectrometer (Lambda 750S, PerkinElmer), a field-emission scanning electron microscope (Hitachi SU8020), an atomic force microscope (Bruker Multimode 8) and a transmission electron microscope (Talos F200S, Thermo Fisher). The stated-state PL was conducted with a PL microscopic spectrometer (SHIMADZU RF6000, Japan) and the TRPL decay spectra was tested by a DS081403 (Newport, USA). These PL tests were conducted by exciting the perovskite films which were deposited on the bare glass substrate. The EIS measurement was carried out by an EC-lab (SP300). The photovoltaic performances including J-V curves and steady-state outputs of the devices were measured by using a solar simulator (Oriel 94023A) and a computer-controlled Keithley 2400 source meter. All devices were tested under simulated air mass 1.5 global (AM 1.5G) sunlight using a black mental mask with an aperture area of 0.1475 cm². The light intensity (100 mW cm⁻²) was calibrated by a standard silicon solar cell (Oriel, VLSI standards). The IPCE measurement was performed by using a QEX10 solar cell instrument (PV Measurements, Inc., USA). The monochromatic light intensity for IPCE measurements was calibrated with a reference silicon photodiode provided by Newport.

# 3. Results and discussion

# 3.1. Characterizations of CsPbBr$_{3}$ films

The TSS strategy which was carried out in a muffle furnace consisted of two temperature steps: 1. Forming   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   materials at 320 °C [17]. 2.   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   crystals grew and relocated to form dense films at a relatively lower temperature (as illustrated in Fig. S1, Supporting information). Likewise, defects reformed at the second-sintering stage. We firstly studied the phase composition of one-step sintering (OSS) and different TSS   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films via X-ray diffraction (XRD). It's worth noting that we applied nonstoichiometric CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   with a molar ratio of 1:1.1 because redundant   $  PbBr_{2}  $   could generate a small quantity of   $  CsPb_{2}Br_{5}  $   which is functional as an interface passivation substance [26]. The formation of   $  CsPb_{2}Br_{5}  $   can be described by following reactions:

$$
C s B r + P b B r _ {2} \rightarrow C s P b B r _ {3}, C s P b B r _ {3} + P b B r _ {2} \rightarrow C s P b _ {2} B r _ {5}
$$

As expected, the cubic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   phase (Pm3m) in XRD patterns (Fig. 2a) well matched with corresponding crystal planes [18], and the   $  PbBr_{2}  $  -rich tetragonal   $  CsPb_{2}Br_{5}  $   phase was assigned to intensity peaks located at   $  2\theta = 11.6^{\circ}  $   and   $  29.4^{\circ}  $   [26]. A lower temperature in the second-sintering stage ensured that the sintering process proceeded in a 'frozen' microstructure, and grain-boundary diffusion suppressed grain-boundary migration because grain-boundary migration has a higher activation energy than grain-boundary diffusion itself, as originally proposed by Chen and Wang [27]. Fig. S4 shows that  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  crystals start disappearing if second-sintering stage was carried out at the same temperature as first-sintering stage. Therefore, no higher TSS temperature was tried.

Normally, the losses of   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF are mainly induced by surface and grain boundary defects in perovskite films [30]. Herein, we investigated the photo-excited carrier behaviors and defect densities in   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films by conducting steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements. The steady-state PL peak centers at a wavelength of 531 nm, while an enhanced emission intensity of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films treated with a higher second-sintering temperature can be observed (Fig. 5a). A similar result derived from the TRPL spectra reveals that PL lifetimes were prolonged as the increase of second-sintering temperature (Fig. 5b). The lifetime plots were fitted by a decay function:   $  I = Ae^{-(t-t_{0})/\tau}  $   [31], where I represents the time-dependent PL intensity, A is a constant and   $  \tau  $   refers to characteristic PL decay lifetime. The PL results reveal reduced nonradiative defect densities in TSS perovskite films. To quantitatively assess trap densities in OSS and TSS perovskite films, we measured the dark J-V characteristics of electron-only devices with an architecture of FTO/  $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite/phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM)/carbon (Fig. 5c), and trap densities can be estimated based on the space-charge-limited-current technique [32]. The trap density levels were filled by increasing the voltage until reaching trap-filling limit voltage (  $  V_{TEL}  $  ) so that the trap state density (  $  n_{trap}  $  ) can be approximately calculated according to the equation   $  V_{TEL} = qn_{trap}L^{2}/2\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}  $  , where q is elementary charge, L is the thickness of perovskite film (around 600 nm, Fig. 1b),   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative dielectric constant of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite (considered the value as 16.46 according to previous report [33]) and   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is vacuum permittivity. The electron trap density in OSS perovskite was calculated to be   $  5.41 \times 10^{15} cm^{-3}  $  , which is much higher than the TSS at 200 °C (  $  4.68 \times 10^{15} cm^{-3}  $  ), TSS at 250 °C (  $  4.53 \times 10^{15} cm^{-3}  $  ) and TSS at 300 °C (  $  3.94 \times 10^{15} cm^{-3}  $  ) perovskites. The estimated trap density values coincide with PL tests. To further confirm the effect of defect densities on recombination reactions within these devices, we studied the relationship between   $  V_{oc}  $   and various imported light intensities (  $  I_{light}  $  ) of OSS and TSS devices. The dependence of   $  V_{oc}  $   on light intensity plot gives a relationship   $  V_{oc} \propto KTI\ln(I_{light})/q  $  , where K, T, and q are Boltzmann constant, temperature in Kelvin, and elementary charge respectively [34]. Normally, a higher slope of   $  V_{oc}  $   versus   $  \ln(I_{light})  $   than KT/q verifies the presence of trap-assisted recombination in this relation [35,36]. The OSS device exhibited a slope of 2.08 KT/q, while it continuously decreased to 1.83 KT/q, 1.66 KT/q and 1.44 KT/q for TSS at 200 °C, 250 °C and 300 °C devices respectively (Fig. 5d). This result suggests that the TSS process suppressed trap-assisted recombination significantly.

Transmission electron microscope (TEM) (Fig. S7) and high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) provide further insights into the microstructural details of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films. Fig. 6a is the HRTEM image of OSS film. Fig. 6d is the HRTEM image of TSS at 300 °C film. Fig. 6b and e are corresponding fast Fourier transform (FFT) patterns of the red region in Fig. 6a and d, which can be indexed to the reflection of a cubic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   crystal recorded from   $  [12\overline{1}]  $   and   $  [\overline{1}01]  $   zone axis respectively. Further analyses based on inverse FFT patterns [37] alone the [111] direction are shown in Fig. 6c and f. We found many edge dislocations in Fig. 6c [38], while no edge dislocation was observed in a similar red region in the TSS at 300 °C film as shown in Fig. 6f. The TEM observations provide direct evidences that TSS processes improved crystallinity and eliminated edge dislocations for   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   crystals, and eliminated edge dislocations confirms that lower defect densities in TSS films were mainly ascribed to the reduction of line defects, thus boosting the efficiency of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   devices.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, we introduced a TSS method, which has been proved to successfully modify morphologies and defect densities of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films. With the TSS process, the average   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   grain size increased to 2   $  \mu  $  m. Moreover, TSS process related line defects reduction inside   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   crystals suppressed recombination actions in devices, which contributed to the prolonged charge lifetimes. Arising from these improvements, the thermally evaporated   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs with a device architecture of FTO/  $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite/carbon achieve a maximized PCE of 9.35% under 1 sun illumination. Moreover, the unencapsulated TSS device exhibited excellent thermal stability: keeping nearly 94% of initial efficiency after being stored at 100 °C for over 40 days. In comparison, the OSS device dropped 15% under the same conditions.
