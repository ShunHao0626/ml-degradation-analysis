# Interface Dipole Induced Field-Effect Passivation for Achieving 21.7% Efficiency and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Fengyou Wang, Yuhong Zhang, Meifang Yang, Donglai Han, Lili Yang,* Lin Fan, Yingrui Sui, Yunfei Sun, Xiaoyan Liu, Xiangwei Meng, and Jinghai Yang*

# 1. Introduction

Organolead halide hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have become a shining star in the renewable devices field due to the sharp growth of power conversion efficiency; however, interfacial recombination and carrier-extraction losses at heterointerfaces between the perovskite active layer and the carrier transport layers remain the two main obstacles to further improve the power conversion efficiency. Here, novel field-effect passivation has been successfully induced to effectively suppress the interfacial recombination and improve interfacial charge transfer by incorporating interfacial polarization via inserting a high work function interlayer between perovskite and holes transport layer. The charge dynamics within the device and the mechanism of the field-effect passivation are elucidated in detail. The unique interfacial dipoles reinforce the built-in field and prevent the photogenerated charges from recombining, resulting in power conversion efficiency up to 21.7% with negligible hysteresis. Furthermore, the hydrophobic interlayer also suppresses the perovskite decomposition by preventing the moisture penetration, thereby improving the humidity stability of the PSCs (>91% of the initial power conversion efficiency (PCE) after 30 d in 65 ± 5% humidity). Finally, several promising research perspectives based on field-effect passivation are also suggested for further conversion efficiency improvements and photovoltaic applications.

On the other hand, at the perovskite films surface or the grain boundaries, there still have several magnitude higher density of traps than in the bulk grains, implying higher electron-hole recombination may occur at the heterointerface. All these cases demonstrate that mitigating interfacial recombination by terminating the surface trap states is an effective approach to promote the photovoltaic performance. These surface passivation techniques can be categorized as chemical passivation, which means to chemically passivate electrically active surface trap states by forming unique chemical bonds. Except that, inspired by the success of applying in Si solar cells, there is another passivation strategy named field-effect passivation (FEP).  $  ^{[24-28]}  $   The FEP is to eliminate the interfacial recombination by creating a robust interfacial dipole via inserting a dielectric film which selectively separates photogenerated carriers from the silicon surface. The term “passivation” involves a virtual imbalance between the populations of positive and negative charges near the surface, which prevents minority carrier recombination at the photoabsorber surface and yields a low contact resistance for majority carrier.  $  ^{[29,30]}  $   Moreover, by modulating charge polarization orientation, FEP can also improve the photogenerated charge transfer efficiency at the heterointerface, which provides a more comprehensive performance improvement than chemical passivation. However, up till now, how to introduce the FEP in PSCs by incorporating appropriate materials has not yet been proposed, regardless of the implementation methods, the eventual device performance, and the involved working mechanisms. Therefore, it is imperative as

well as meaningful to investigate the implementation of FEP and hence identify schemes for subsequent optimization.

Traditional FEP materials, including   $  SiO_{2}  $  ,   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $  , etc. is unfavorable for PSCs application because the fabrication process contains moisture and high-temperature condition, which could decompose the perovskite layer. As a transition metal oxide, nonstoichiometric molybdenum oxide (  $  MoO_{x}  $  ) with low preparation-temperature and nontoxicity is traditionally utilized as a modification layer between metal electrodes and carrier transport layer.  $  ^{[31-33]}  $   Recently, we also used room-temperature evaporated   $  MoO_{x}  $   as carrier-selective layer in Si solar cells to achieve low-cost and high efficiency.  $  ^{[7]}  $   The   $  MoO_{x}  $   has a much higher work function (>5.5 eV) than Si (  $  \approx4.61  $   eV), which can create a dipole layer in   $  Si/MoO_{x}  $   heterointerface as the FEP layer to enhance the separation of charges and reduce the interface recombination. This case inspired us that depositing   $  MoO_{x}  $   on perovskite surface may also induce interfacial dipole layer to suppress the interface recombination in PSCs owing to the work function of perovskite (  $  MAPbI_{3}, \approx4.7  $   eV) is also much lower than   $  MoO_{x}  $  . Moreover, since the ambient stable   $  MoO_{x}  $   has the extra merit of protecting the PSCs from decaying in air, it is natural as well as valuable to explore their potential as FEP interlayers for PSCs application.

Accordingly, in this work, we successful implement FEP on PSCs by inserting an ultrathin   $  MoO_{x}  $   between the perovskite and the HTL. The large work function difference between perovskite and   $  MoO_{x}  $   causes surface charge transfer and leads to a dipole layer at perovskite/  $  MoO_{x}  $   heterointerface, which forms an extra field to enhance the photoexcited charge separation and thereby mitigate the interfacial charge recombination. Furthermore, the dense and hydrophobic   $  MoO_{x}  $   on perovskite films can also prevent moisture penetration, which improves the stability of the PSCs. The   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   solar cells exhibit an increased PCE from 19.1% to 21.7% by applying the facile FEP. The cells without any encapsulation degrade less than 10% after 30 d of exposure in 65±5% humidity at room temperature, compare to more than 15% degradation for the control cells.

Empirically, when a layer is inserted into the device, the alteration of charge behavior at the heterointerface may be another possible routine to affects the photovoltaic performance.

As illustrated above, the as-prepared   $  MoO_{x}  $   thin films have a much higher work function than the perovskite thin films. Therefore, when the   $  MoO_{x}  $   was deposited on the perovskite films, the differences in work functions drive electrons to migrate from perovskite to   $  MoO_{x}  $   to line up Fermi levels, leaving the holes at the perovskite surface (Figure 4a). Recently, we have observed this phenomenon in Si solar cells.  $  ^{[35,39]}  $   This charge transfer creates interfacial polarization, which induces the FEP by efficient separating photogenerated carriers and reducing the probability of Shockley–Read–Hall (SRH) recombination at the heterointerface.  $  ^{[40]}  $   Thus, we argue that the FEP caused by the   $  MoO_{x}  $   interlayer may domain the photovoltaic improvement. To confirm this speculation, we proposed a systematic investigation involves numerical simulations and experimental measurements. We firstly used a one-dimensional device simulation software wx-AMPS to examine the effect of interfacial dipole on the bandgap alignment, the built-in field, and the carrier recombination of the PSCs. This software solves the continuity and Poisson equations at each mesh point, and includes effects of tunneling currents based on two different tunneling models (trap-assisted tunneling and intraband tunneling).  $  ^{[41]}  $   The detailed simulation parameters are presented in Table S3 in the Supporting Information. Figure 4b shows the simulated bandgap diagram of the PSCs with- and without-interfacial dipole under one-sun illumination. It shows that the interfacial dipole causes the band

bending upward from the bulk of perovskite to the rear surface. Notably, the electron/hole quasi-Fermi level   $  (E_{\mathrm{fp}}/E_{\mathrm{fn}})  $   splitting becomes larger after introducing the interfacial dipole, which indicates the built-in field is increased. We conducted XPS to further study the energy level alignment at the perovskite/MoO  $  _{x}  $   interface (Figure S9, Supporting Information). After depositing MoO  $  _{x}  $  , lead and iodine core energy levels of the perovskite layer shifted to the low binding energy position. The results not only show that the   $  E_{v}  $   (and core energy level) of the perovskite has moved to   $  E_{F}  $  , but also indicate that the bend of bulk perovskite is bent upward to the rear surface, which is entirely due to the interface dipole caused by the high work function of MoO  $  _{x}  $   layer. This is also consistent with the scanning Kelvin probe microscopy (SKPM) and simulation results. Furthermore, 1D distribution of the electric field and recombination in the PSCs were examined to verify this argument. Figure 4c shows the electric field of the PSCs with interfacial dipole. We can see the electric field is increased at the bulk and the rear interface of the PSCs. Especially, at the rear interface, the electrical field is increased by two orders of magnitude. The stronger built-in field is much favorable for efficiently separating of photogenerated electron and hole before they are recombination, thereby the probability of electron–hole recombination within the perovskite is reduced. This can be certified by the recombination rate in Figure 4d. After forming the interfacial dipole, the recombination rate is obviously reduced at the rear interface, which is consistent with our previous speculation. To further experimentally verify the enhanced built-in field, capacitance–voltage response (C–V) and Mott–Schottky analysis were performed to the PSCs in this work. Figure 6a shows that introducing of the   $  MoO_{x}  $   interlayer results in decreasing capacitance of the PSCs. As the dipole direction at perovskite/  $  MoO_{x}  $   interface is aligned with the built-in potential, the reduced capacitance implies the charge extraction at heterointerface is enhanced.  $  ^{[44]}  $   Correspondingly, the Mott–Schottky calculation in electrochemical configuration is also performed using the well-known equation  $  ^{[45]}  $

$$
\frac {1}{C ^ {2}} = \frac {2 (V _ {\mathrm{bi}} - V)}{q \varepsilon_ {\mathrm{e}} \varepsilon_ {0} N _ {\mathrm{a}}}
$$

(2)

More and better FEP materials will be certainly expected to boost the development of PSCs in the further.

# 3. Conclusion and Perspectives

In summary, we have successfully presented that simultaneous improvement in the photovoltaic performance and long-term stability can be achieved in efficient PSCs by incorporating appropriate FEP interlayer, resulting in PCE and   $  V_{oc}  $   up to 21.7% and 1.18 V for MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   solar cells. The enhancement of PSCs performance is demonstrated to be threefold: an improved built-in field across the cell owing to the existence of interfacial dipole, a reduced interface recombination due to the efficiently separate the photogenerated electron and holes at heterointerface, and an enhanced humidity stability caused by the sealing-effect of the hydrophobic MoO  $  _{x}  $   interlayer. Further optimization is conceivable because the chemical passivation for perovskite surface and grain boundaries has not been introduced in this work. We argue that two factors should be stressed to further improve the PCE and stability of the PSCs: First, continue exploring advanced hydrophobic FEP materials that may fit chemical passivation and FEP simultaneously. The interface trap states should be fully saturated by forming chemical interface passivation. And the FEP could further enhance the separation of photogenerated charges to decrease the chance of charges trapping. The second factor is to induce the FEP at the opposite electron transport layer/perovskite interface by incorporating low work function interlayer, which could bifacially reduce the recombination in PSCs. Finally, we believe our exploration on the FEP of the perovskite thin films can be a useful guideline toward high efficiency PSCs and trigger facile passivating methods for versatile applications.
