# High-efficiency perovskite solar cell prepared by a sandwich structure MAI-PbI $ _2 $ -MAI precursor film

Xuhui Zhang  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Jiajiu Ye  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Liangzheng Zhu  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Haiying Zheng  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Guozhen Liu  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Xuepeng Liu  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Bin Duan  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Xu Pan  $  ^{*a}  $   and Songyuan Dai  $  ^{*c}  $

$  ^{a}  $   Key Laboratory of Novel Thin-Film Solar Cells, Institute of Applied Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Hefei 230031

$  ^{b}  $  . University of Science and Technology of China, Hefei 230026, China

$  ^{c}  $   State Key Laboratory of Alternate Electrical Power System with Renewable Energy Sources, North China Electric Power University, Beijing 102206, China

# Abstract:

Two-step deposition has been widely used in the perovskite layers preparation for perovskite solar cells due to its attractive morphology controllability. However, the limited diffusivity of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  (MAI) might remain some  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  in the perovskite film. The residual  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  in the perovskite film would lead to inferior performance of devices, such as, low power conversion efficiency (PCE), poor reproducibility and weak air stability. In this work, we developed a sandwich structure MAI- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -MAI precursor film to prepare  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -free  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite film. In comparison to the two-step approach, the MAI- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -MAI precursor film with a typical sandwich structure formed a uniform and pinhole-free perovskite film without any  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  residue, which could significantly improve the performance of the devices. Moreover, the bottom MAI layer of the MAI- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -MAI precursor film could improve the interfacial contact of porous  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  layer, leading to promoting the charge transfer and reducing the recombination rate. Therefore, the devices fabricated by the sandwich structure MAI- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -MAI precursor films showed dramatic improvements of open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $ ), short-circuit current density ( $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $ ), fill factor (FF) and PCE. As a result, a promising PCE of  $ 17.8\% $  with a good long-term air stability were achieved for the MAI- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -MAI precursor film based PSC, which are better than that prepared by two-step approach.

# 1. Introduction

Due to their long diffusion length, high carrier mobility, suitable optical bandgaps, and strong absorption of light, organometal halide perovskites have recently been attracting attention for use in perovskite solar cells (PSCs).  $  ^{1-4}  $   Miyasaka and his colleagues first employed   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbX_{3}  $   (X = Br, I) as sensitizer in dye-sensitized solar cells to replace organic dyes and achieved a PCE of 3.8% in 2009,  $  ^{5}  $   then Nam-Gyu Park and his colleagues used spiro-MeOTAD as a hole-transporting layer for the all-solid-state perovskite solar cell and achieved a strikingly PCE of 9.7% in 2012,  $  ^{6}  $   the efficiency of perovskite solar cells has increased to 22.1% within 4 years,  $  ^{7-12}  $   which is the fastest growing solar cell technology today.  $  ^{13}  $

The most classical approach for perovskite deposition reported in the literature is solution chemistry, mainly including two preparation routes. The first route is one-step solution process via spin coating of the perovskite precursor solution onto the substrate, followed by thermal annealing to form the perovskite film. $ ^{14-21} $  The second commonly used route is two-step deposition. $ ^{22-25} $  Two-step deposition of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites was initially adapted by Michael Grätzel and his colleagues to fabricate mesoscopic PSCs. $ ^{22} $  The advantages of the two-step method are its great morphology controllability of perovskite films and good pore-filling in mesoscopic PSCs, leading to a compact and uniform perovskite film and high performances of PSCs. $ ^{26,27} $  However, the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film prepared by the two-step deposition method generally remains a certain amount of residual  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . $ ^{28} $  Most of the residual  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  is the unreacted  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  left in the conversion of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  to  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  which is due to the insufficiency of MAI solution diffusion into  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  films. $ ^{29,30} $  Many groups have demonstrated that the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  residue in perovskite film has negative effects on the performance of PSCs. $ ^{26,31,32} $  In order to obtain a full conversion of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  to  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ , Mitzi et al. have soaked the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film in MAI isopropanol solution for hours, $ ^{33} $  this method could peel off or dissolve the perovskite. Yixin Zhao and Kai Zhu have used a three-step sequential solution deposition method to obtain  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -free  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite films. $ ^{28} $  A thermally unstable stoichiometric  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2\cdot \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{Cl} $  precursor film was first deposited on the

Herein, we demonstrated a sandwich structure MAI-  $  PbI_{2}  $  -MAI precursor film to prepare   $  PbI_{2}  $  -free   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite film. Compared to the conventional two-step method, the devices fabricated from the sandwich structure MAI-  $  PbI_{2}  $  -MAI precursor films showed dramatic improvements for all the performance parameters. A promising efficiency of 17.8% was achieved with a uniform, pinhole-free of   $  PbI_{2}  $  -free perovskite layer prepared by the optimized sandwich structure MAI-  $  PbI_{2}  $  -MAI precursor film. Moreover, the devices based on the sandwich structure MAI-  $  PbI_{2}  $  -MAI precursor film showed a better long-term stability.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1 Materials

F-doped  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  glass (FTO,  $ 15\Omega $  per square) was obtained from Pilkington TEC.  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  was purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Titanium isopropoxide (98%), lithium bis (trifluoromethylsulphonyl) imide (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP) were bought from Aldrich. Bis (acetylacetonate) (99%) was purchased from SCRC. Isopropanol (IPA, 99.7%) and N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.9%) were purchased from Alfa.  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  (particle size: about 30 nm, crystalline phase: anatase) was got from Dysol. Lead(II) iodide ( $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , 99%) was purchased from TCI. 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobifluorene (spiroOMeTAD) was purchased from Merck KGaA. All chemicals were used as received without further purification.

# 2.2 Device fabrication

A 35-60-nm-thick  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  compact layer (c- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ ) was deposited on the clean and etched FTO substrate by aerosol spray pyrolysis at  $ 450^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , using a precursor solution of 0.4

3

Page 4 of 20

mL of bis (acetylacetonate) and 0.6 mL of titanium diisopropoxide in 70 mL of isopropanol. A meso-micro porous TiO  $  _{2}  $  layer (100-150nm in thickness) was then deposited on the c-TiO  $  _{2}  $ /FTO substrate by spin coating at 5000 rpm for 30 s. After drying at 100 °C, the meso-micro porous TiO  $  _{2}  $  films were annealed at 510 °C for 20 min to remove the organic residue.

Two-step deposition of perovskite: a   $  PbI_{2}  $   DMF solution (1 M) was first spin coated onto the porous   $  TiO_{2}  $   at 3,000 rpm for 30 s, then a 50 mg/mL of MAI IPA solution was spin coated on the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film at 3,000 rpm for 30 s immediately. The deposited film was then annealed at 105 °C for 60 min to form the perovskite layer.

MAI-  $  PbI_{2}  $  -MAI precursor film preparation for perovskite: a high concentration (30 mg/mL, 40 mg/mL or 50 mg/mL) of MAI IPA solution was first spin coated onto the porous   $  TiO_{2}  $   film at 3,000 rpm for 30 s, and then a   $  PbI_{2}  $   DMF solution (1M) was dripped onto the MAI film during spinning. After that, a 50 mg/mL of MAI IPA solution was spin coated on the film at 3,000 rpm for 30 s again. Then the MAI-  $  PbI_{2}  $  -MAI film was annealed at 105 °C for 60 min to form the perovskite layer.

The HTM solution (25   $  \mu  $  L) was dripped onto the perovskite layer during spinning at 3,000 rpm for 20 s. The solution was made by mixing spiro-OMeTAD (73.5 mg), TBP (29   $  \mu  $  L), LiTFSI solution (17.5   $  \mu  $  L, 520 mg of LiTFSI in 1 mL of acetonitrile) and FK209 (tris(2-(1H-pyrazol-1-yl)-4-tert-butylpyridine)cobalt(III) bis(trifluoro-methylsulphonyl)imide solution (6   $  \mu  $  L, 300 mg of FK209 in 1 mL of acetonitrile). Finally, an Au counterelectrode (60-80-nm-thick) was made by thermally evaporating on the top of the device.

# 2.3 Characterization

The morphologies of all samples were characterized by a field-emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM, sirion200, FEI Corp., Holland). The X-ray diffraction spectra of   $  PbI_{2}  $   films and perovskite films were recorded on an X'pert PRO X-ray Diffractometer. The data were collected at room temperature in the 2θ range 10-60°. Ultraviolet-visible (UV-VIS) absorption spectra were measured by an UV-VIS spectrophotometer (U-3900H, HITACHI, Japan) at room temperature. PL spectra

The incident monochromatic photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) was measured using an IPCE measuring system (Newport Corporation, CA) equipped with a Xe lamp as the light source.

The cell architecture (Fig. 1a) includes a conductive substrate, a compact layer, a porous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer, a perovskite layer, a hole transporting layer, and a metal electrode. For this architecture, the formation of a   $  PbI_{2}  $  -free, uniform, density and complete filling perovskite layer is extremely important, and we developed a sandwich structure MAI-  $  PbI_{2}  $  -MAI precursor film as an effective precursor film to generate such a layer. The process involves six stages, as shown in Fig. 1c. First, a high concentration (30 mg/mL, 40 mg/mL or 50 mg/mL) of MAI solution was spread over the entire surface of the porous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer. XRD analysis was conducted to verify the crystallization of the   $  PbI_{2}-x  $   films. The XRD patterns of   $  PbI_{2}-x  $   films were shown in Fig. 4. When the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film deposited on the MAI-free porous   $  TiO_{2}  $   film (  $  PbI_{2}-0  $  ), it displayed a strong characteristic XRD peak at about 12.5°. Fig. 6 showed the surface morphology of MAPI-x films. It was obvious that the MAIPbI  $  _{3}  $  film of MAPI-0 achieved by two step method had a lot of pinholes. This would lead to the ohmic contacts of HTM and electron transport layer, leading to recombination of charge carriers and affects the device performances. In contrast, compact and hole-free MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  layers were achieved for MAPI-30, MAPI-40 and MAPI-50 by the sandwich structure MAI-PbI  $  _{2}  $ -MAI precursor films. Although the MAPI-30 film was compact and hole-free, it was ununiform and the grains was too small, which usually produces more defects.  $  ^{24}  $  Meanwhile, we can see a compact layer with a low degree of crystallinity covered on the surface of MAPI-50. This may because of an excess of MAI assembled on its surface. Among the four films, the MAPI-40 film was the most uniform and compact, and the grain size of the film was 200-500 nm. Thus, to achieve a high uniform and compact MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  film, the concentration of MAI for the bottom MAI layer should be kept around 40 mg/mL.

Moreover, among the three kinds of perovskite prepared by sandwich structure MAI-PbI  $  _{2}  $  -MAI precursor films, the MAPI-40 had showed the strongest peak intensity, followed by MAPI-50 and MAPI-30 perovskite films, indicating that MAPI-40 had harvested the highest quality perovskite film with least trap sites and defects, which was ascribed to the film had the best morphology, high crystallinity and without any PbI  $  _{2}  $   residue.

To further understand the quality of MAPI-x films, we used transient absorption (TA) response to detect the internal recombination in the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layers. If the recombination of electrons and holes occurred difficultly, the longer of recombination time will get.[39,40] In other words, the perovskite layer with a longer recombination time represents a better film which has appropriate morphology and few defect states, which means more effective charges will be outputted. The TA response and dynamics were shown in Fig. 8, we observed an increase trend of the recombination time when the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films prepared by sandwich structure MAI- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -MAI precursor films. The recombination time of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer prepared by two step method only sustained for 35 ns, while perovskite layers prepared by sandwich structure MAI- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -MAI precursor films, the recombination times significantly increased to 45-173 ns. The best one exceeded 173 ns was based on the MAPI-40. Obviously, the high quality perovskite film without any traceable residue  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  was conducive to the electron and hole diffusion.

Finally, we studied the stability of devices of PSC-0 and PSC-40. All cells were tested in ambient air, which the temperature and humidity were 25 °C and 40%-70%, respectively. As shown in Fig. 10, the PSC-40 exhibited a better stability than the PSC-0. After two months, the PCE of PSC-40 still remained over 65%, but the PSC-0 remained 40% only.
