# 1. Introduction

Based on the superior optic-electrical advantage of organic-in-organic hybrid lead halide materials with a perovskite structure, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have obtained extensive research interest extremely. As a burgeoning photovoltaic technology, the power energy conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has been raised from the beginning of 3.8% to the current of 25.5% outstanding [1–5]. But through all this, owing to the non-radiative recombination during the process of carrier transportation, especially in interfaces, the achieved PCE is always lower than the theoretical limit value defined by Shockley-Queisser theory [6–10].

Extensive studies have shown that the optical, electrical, and structural characteristics of the electron transport layer (ETL) are critical factors influencing the PCE of PSCs [11-15]. The ETL material used in PSCs is titanium oxide  $ (\mathrm{TiO}_2) $  commonly. # ABSTRACT

The electron transport layer (ETL) is one of the most important factors to determine the photovoltage (PV) performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs), and defects and non-radiative recombination are the negative factors limiting the efficiency improvement of PSCs. Herein, using tin oxide  $ (\mathrm{SnO}_2) $  as the electron transport layer and multiple functional groups organic molecule of 3-(formamidinothio)-1-propanesulfonic acid (FTPS) as additive of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ , we fabricated a high-effective  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ -based planar PSC. The introduction of FTPS reduces the oxygen vacancy defects of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ETLs and accommodates perovskite crystal growth on it. The modification of FTPS improves the conductivity and the electron extraction ability of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ , mitigates the trap-state density and carrier non-radiative recombination at the interfaces of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2/ $ perovskite. As a result, the PSC based on pristine  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ETL obtains a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 20.49\% $ , while the champion device based on  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 + \mathrm{FTPS} $  ( $ 0.3\mathrm{mg/mL} $ ) ETL achieves an efficiency of  $ 22.52\% $  with insignificant hysteresis effect and excellent stability. This effective defect passivation strategy work provides a promising low-cost technology for the preparation of planar PSCs with high efficiency and stability.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

Formamidinium iodide (FAI), methylammonium iodide (MAI), methylamine hydrochloride (MACl) was purchased from Xi'an Bright Optoelectronics Technology Co., Ltd. 3-(formamidinothio)-1-propanesulfonic acid (FTPS) was obtained from Innochem. The  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  aqueous colloidal precursor (tin (IV) oxide,  $ 15\% $  in  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  colloidal dispersion) was bought from Alfa Aesar. Lead iodide (PbI2,  $ 99.99\% $  ) was ordered from Advanced Election Technology Co., Ltd. All other chemicals were bought as in the previous studies [25]. All chemicals were applied as got that we don't use further procedures to process.

# 2.2. Preparation of devices

The indium tin oxide (ITO) substrate was prepared with the same procedure in previous reports [26]. Then, FTPS (0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, and  $ 0.5\mathrm{mg / mL} $ ) was dissolved in deionized water and stirred for  $ 10\mathrm{min} $ , then  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  solution was added to obtain  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 + } $  FTPS precursor solution (volume ratio 4:1) under ultrasonic vibration for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $ . First,  $ 80~\mu \mathrm{L} $  of  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  precursor solutions were spin-coated onto the treated ITO substrate at a speed of  $ 3500~\mathrm{rpm} $ , and then placed on a hot plate heated to  $ 180^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $  to anneal.  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  film and  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 + } $  FTPS film were obtained thus. The above samples were moved to the glove box after being treated with UV- $ \mathrm{O}_3 $  for  $ 20\mathrm{min} $ .  $ 1.5\mathrm{M} $  of  $ \mathrm{FA}_{1 - x}\mathrm{MA}_x\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  -based perovskite precursor solution was prepared by a two-step process [27]. PbI2 was dissolved in the solvent mixing with dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) to deposit a yellow  $ \mathrm{PbI_2} $  film, then a mixed ammonium salt (MAI, MACI, and FAI) isopropanol solution was added to produce a perovskite film, the color changes from yellow to dark. Next, the hole transport layer (Spiro-OMeTAD) was deposited on perovskite film by the same method as reported previously [26]. Lastly, an  $ 80~\mathrm{nm} $  Au electrode was formed on the upper layer of the hole transport layer by thermal evaporation.

# 2.3. Film and device characterization

Morphologies of samples were observed by a field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM, JSM-7610F Plus, Japan). The roughness of the perovskite films was measured by atomic force microscopy (AFM, Multimode-8, America). X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern of samples were measured by a X-ray diffractometer (SmartLa, Netherlands). X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS) and ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) were conducted by the Thermo Fisher Scientific K-alpha+. Ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) absorption spectra were measured on a UV-vis spectrometer (PerkinElmer Lambda 950). Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy measurements were carried out in an electrochemical workstation (IM6ex, Zahner, Germany) under ambient condition  $ (30\pm 5\%) $  humidity), the data was calculated and analyzed by ZView

program. [26,28] External quantum efficiency (EQE) was recorded on a QE-R-3010 system (Enlitech, Taiwan). The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra (excitation at 534 nm) were measured using a Thermo Scientific Luminescence Fluorescence Spectrometer (LF-1303003). time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra (emission at 800 nm) were measured with a time correlated single photon counting method (Zolix) on Omin-  $  \lambda  $  Monochromator/Spectrograph. [27] The photocurrent density-voltage (J-V) curves were measured (2400 Source Meter) under standard 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  (AM 1.5G) illumination which was provided by Oriel Sol AAA solar simulator (Newport), while the dark J-V curves were tested in a box without illumination. The incident light intensity was calibrated using NREL-calibrated silicon solar cells equipped with a KG-2 filter. All devices were investigated and characterized under unencapsulated state.

# 3. Results and discussion

To figure out the influence of additive FTPS on the morphology of   $  SnO_{2}  $   crystal growth, the scanning electron microscope (SEM) and X-ray diffraction (XRD) of   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2} + FTPS  $   films were measured. It can be seen from Fig. S2 that the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film after adding FTPS is more uniformly dispersed. In Fig. S3, the peak positions of   $  SnO_{2}  $   without and with FTPS hardly change [24]. Therefore, after the introduction of FTPS, the dispersibility of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film is improved, but the crystal structure of   $  SnO_{2}  $   remains unchanged. Additionally, compared to the SEM images of the perovskite light-absorbing layer formed on SnO2 and   $  SnO_{2} + FTPS  $   films (Fig. 1a and b and Fig. S4), the introduction of FTPS improves the crystal quality of   $  SnO_{2}  $ , induces the growth of perovskite crystals, and the cavities of perovskite grown on it are reduced obviously. The atomic force microscopy (AFM) images (Fig. 1c and d) also identified the FTPS. After 0.3 mg/mL FTPS-treatment, the roughness of the perovskite light-absorbing layer films declines, and the root-means-square (RMS) of the films reduced from 41.7 nm to 36.4 nm.

Fig. S5 exhibits the cross-view SEM images of the structure of ITO/various   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD. The   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite device has more chaotic grain boundaries, while   $  SnO_{2}  $   + FTPS (0.3 mg/mL)/perovskite has a good grain morphology and larger grain size owing to the reduced surface energy of modified ETL and the optimization of flatness [28]. To search for the reason for the improvement of perovskite quality, the contact angle of the films of   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}  $   + FTPS (0.3 mg/mL) with the perovskite precursor solution was measured (Fig. S6). The smaller contact angle for the   $  SnO_{2}  $   + FTPS (0.3 mg/mL) film than the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film signifies that the perovskite can grow better on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   + FTPS film than that of the pristine film. FTPS contains ternary perovskite (  $  FA_{1-x}MA_{x}PbI_{3}  $  ) compatible groups, such as -FA and -S=O [19,25,26], especially formamidine (FA) as the same group the perovskite film. The XRD of the perovskite film based on SnO2 without and with FTPS were measured and shown in Fig. S7. All perovskite films belong to the cubic structure (JCPDS card: No.010-0737). There is almost no change in the peak position after doping with FTPS, which proves that FTPS did not enter the lattice of the perovskite lattice. On the other hand, the peak intensity of   $  PbI_{2}  $   is weakened while the peak intensity of perovskite is strengthened, indicating that the perovskite crystals have better crystallinity after introducing FTPS into   $  SnO_{2}  $   [29], in accordance with the SEM images. Therefore, perovskite crystals can grow easily on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film containing FTPS, the FTPS reduces the surface energy of   $  SnO_{2}  $ , improves the wettability of the interface perovskite/  $  SnO_{2}  $ , and is conducive to the even growth of perovskite, thus promoting the quality of perovskite film.

$$

The transient photovoltage (TPV) curves of the devices with ETL being   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}  $   + FTPS were tested to understand the charge recombination kinetics in PSCs. We can see from Fig. 3e, the carrier lifetime increases from 26.56   $  \mu  $  s of the pristine equipment to 72.00   $  \mu  $  s of the PSC device modified with FTPS (0.3 mg/mL), indicating that non-radiative recombination was suppressed significantly [40]. The reduction of non-radiative recombination is mainly because of the improvement of interface selective contact, the improvement of the quality of   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL and perovskite layer [41]. Moreover, we measured the I-V characteristics under varying light intensities from 5 to 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $   (Fig. 3f). According to the

fitted data, in contrast with the control samples  $ (\varepsilon = 1.772\mathrm{KT} / e) $ , we can see that the ideal factor (n) of the device fabricated with  $ 0.3\mathrm{mg}/ $  mL FTPS-modified  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $   $ (\varepsilon = 1.636\mathrm{KT} / e) $  was lower, demonstrating that FTPS modified  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  can inhibit non-radiative recombination successfully [42]. This is almost consistent with the TPV results completely.

S10 and S11 The valence band edges of the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 + FTPS} $  are determined to be 8.13 and  $ 8.19\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively, and the conduction band edges are determined to be 4.15 and  $ 4.21\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively [26,43,44]. According to the investigation of PL, TRPL, Dark  $ I - V $  curve, UPS, and so on, the above inferences have been confirmed. Hence, we can conclude that the introduction of FTPS into  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ETL decreases trapstate density in the ETL and passivates defects at the ETL/perovskite interface effectively, thus alleviate the non-radiative recombination, promote the carrier transportation.

# 4. Conclusion

In short, we develop an effective additive engineering strategy. That is, multiple functional groups of organic molecules of 3-(formamidinothio)-1-propanesulfonic acid (FTPS) were used as an additive to modify   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETLs. The morphology observation, XPS characterization, and photoelectrochemical measurement demonstrate that lead FTPS into   $  SnO_{2}  $   not just diminishes the oxygen vacancy defects in   $  SnO_{2}  $   and the density of trap states at the   $  SnO_{2}/perovskite  $   interface, but also improves the conductivity of   $  SnO_{2}  $   and the contact resistance at the interface of   $  SnO_{2}/perovskite  $   interface, which results in the reduction of carrier non-radiative recombination and the improvement of photovoltaic property of the PSCs. Subsequently, the optimal PCE of 22.52% was found for the planar PSC based on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL modified with 0.3 mg/mL FTPS while the original device at the efficiency of 20.49%. Besides, after introducing FTPS into the device, it suppressed effectively the hysteresis and made the stability better. This simple modification method and crucial performance improvement depict the multifunctional additive with good development prospects.
