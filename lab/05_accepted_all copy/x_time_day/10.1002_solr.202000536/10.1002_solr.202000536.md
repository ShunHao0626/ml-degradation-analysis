Keywords: small molecule, hole transporting materials, inverted perovskite solar cells, hole mobility

Hole transport material (HTM) is a significant constituent in perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, HTM generally is not utilized in its pristine form but with dopants (such as lithium-salt, tert-butyl pyridine,  $ \mathrm{F_4} $ -TCNQ), which would accelerate device degradation and lead to poor stability. Therefore, dopant-free HTM is highly desirable to fabricate stable devices. Herein, a fused furan organic small-molecular ( $ \mathrm{C_8} $ -DPNDF) is introduced as a dopant-free HTM in inverted PSCs. As a potential HTM candidate,  $ \mathrm{C_8} $ -DPNDF shows excellent properties, such as high hole mobility, matched energy level with perovskite and resistant to perovskite precursor solution. As a result, the device based on  $ \mathrm{C_8} $ -DPNDF as HTM shows power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 17.5\% $ , compared to  $ 17.1\% $  of the control device based on classic poly(bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine) (PTAA) as HTM. # 1. Introduction

Owing to the excellent intrinsic properties, including high absorption coefficient, direct band-gap and long carrier diffusion length, solution-processible metal-halide perovskites hold huge potential to serve as the light-harvesting layers for the photovoltaic application.  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   After extensive development over the past decade, the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have made great achievements, as indicated by the PCEs from the initial 3.8% to a certificated 25.5%.  $  ^{[4, 5]}  $   Generally, the PSCs could be divided into two categories depending on the charge transport direction, which are the traditional n-i-p structure and the inverted p-i-n structure.  $  ^{[6,7]}  $   Although the underlying mechanism is still unclear, the inverted structure usually shows lower voltage-current hysterisis and higher long-term stability, which are definitely important for the commercialization in the future.  $  ^{[8]}  $

For inverted device, the HTM was first deposited on the transparent conducting oxide, and the perovskite was commonly sandwiched between HTM and fullerene (PCBM and/or   $  C_{60}  $  ). The most efficient HTMs in inverted PSCs are   $  NiO_{x}  $   and PTAA.  $  ^{[9, 10]}  $   The inorganic   $  NiO_{x}  $   needs high temperature sintering (  $  \sim 500\;^{\circ}C  $  ), which increases the fabrication complexity. Though the PTAA polymer is low-temperature processible and shows excellent film-forming property, it still suffers from batch-to-batch variation. In this context, organic small-molecules are good candidate because of low-temperature processible, no batch-to-batch variation and ease of tailoring.  $  ^{[11-16]}  $   The excellent small molecular HTM candidate in inverted devices should meet but not limit to the following properties: matched energy level with perovskite, high hole mobility and poor solubility in DMF and/or DMSO. Fused furan derivative of 2,7-Bis(4-octylphenyl)naphtho [2,1-b:6,5-b']difuran (  $  C_{8}  $  -DPNDF, Figure 1a) shows hole mobility up to 3.6 cm  $  ^{2}  $  V  $  ^{-1}  $  s  $  ^{-1}  $ , which is much higher than the HTMs utilized in PSCs.  $  ^{[17, 18]}  $   In addition, the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of   $  C_{8}  $  -DPNDF (-5.0 eV, calculated from density functional theory (DFT)) matches well with the valent band of

In this work, we introduce   $  C_{8}  $  -DPNDF as HTM in inverted PSCs, and the widely-used PTAA is chosen as reference. The   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  -based device with   $  C_{8}  $  -DPNDF as HTM shows a decent champion PCE of 17.5% compared to 17.1% from the control device with PTAA as HTM. Besides, the devices based on   $  C_{8}  $  -DPNDF also show high storage stability, keeping 92% of its original PCE in air with relative humidity (RH) of ~40% after 30 days. These comparable results are attributed to high conductivity, hole mobility, appropriate energy level and hydrophobic property of   $  C_{8}  $  -DPNDF, originating probably from the favorable molecular packing.

# 2. Results and Discussions

Figure 1a shows the synthetic strategy of C $ _8 $ -DPNDF following the reported route. $ ^{[17]} $  It can be readily achieved through sonogashira coupling of commercially available 1,5-dibromo-2,6-bis(methoxymethoxy)naphthalene followed by hydroxylation and cyclization with high productivity. This compound is thermally stable characterized by thermogravimetry-differential thermal analysis (TG -DTA). $ ^{[17]} $  Additionally, it is extremely stable even under ambient air without any metamorphosis after more than 5 years storage confirmed by  $ ^1 $ H NMR. Figure 1b shows the calculated HOMO and LUMO energy levels with the electron wave of its HOMO delocalizing on the central conjugated backbone, contributing to the small reorganization energy, which is good for hole transportation. $ ^{[19]} $  In consideration of its high carrier mobility, appropriate energy level and resistance to the erosion of DMF, inverted devices were assembled with C $ _8 $ -DPNDF as HTM (Figure 2a). The optimal concentration of C $ _8 $ -DPNDF solution for device performance was optimized as shown in Figure S1 and Table S1. Figure 2b shows the  $ J $ - $ V $  curves of the optimal device with negligible hysteresis. The optimal device with C $ _8 $ -DPNDF as HTM ( $ \sim $  20 nm) exhibited a short-circuit current density ( $ J_{SC} $ ) of 20.48 mA/cm $ ^2 $ , an open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{OC} $ ) of 1.065V, a fill factor ( $ FF $ ) of 0.803, and the decent PCE of 17.51% under reverse-scan direction. The resistance of both films in vertical direction was obtained by measuring the dark current of devices with HTM sandwiched between two electrodes. The surface morphology and microstructure of the perovskite films atop of C $ _8 $ -DPNDF and PTAA were investigated via the performing of SEM as shown in Figure 4a and b. Besides, Figure S6 showed the cross-section of devices based on C $ _8 $ -DPNDF and PTAA. The thickness of both perovskite films was almost the same. To investigate the influence of C $ _8 $ -DPNDF and PTAA on the crystallinity of perovskite films, X-ray diffraction (XRD) diffraction analysis was performed. Besides, Figure 4d shows similar UV–vis absorption, implying the close thickness of perovskite films based on C $ _8 $ -DPNDF and PTAA. To study the hole extraction efficiencies of these two HTMs, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) decay were measured, respectively. All perovskite films showed the primary PL peaks at around 770 nm (Figure 4e), which agrees with the UV-Vis absorption of these perovskite films as shown in Figure 4d. The long-term stability of device is another critical factor for potential applications. The devices without encapsulation exposed to ambient atmosphere with a RH of around 40%. # 3 Conclusion

In conclusion, we have successfully exploited readily synthesizable  $ \mathrm{C_8} $ -DPNDF as dopant-free HTM in inverted PSCs. The uniform and full coverage of perovskite films can be fabricated atop the  $ \mathrm{C_8} $ -DPNDF, which exhibits high carrier mobility and appropriate energy level, facilitating carrier transport and extraction. As a result, the device based on  $ \mathrm{C_8} $ -DPNDF as HTM without dopant exhibits higher PCE than that of the device based on classic PTAA as HTM. Furthermore, the  $ \mathrm{C_8} $ -DPNDF-based device shows high storage stability keeping  $ 92\% $  of its initial PCE after 30 days in ambient air with RH of  $ \sim 40\% $ . The  $ \mathrm{C_8} $ -DPNDF, as an alternative to PTAA and  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ , is a promising HTM for stable and efficient inverted PSC that even can be utilized on flexible substrates.

Solar cell fabrication. The indium-doped tin oxide (ITO) glass substrates with an optical transmission of   $  >80\%  $   in the visible range and a sheet resistance of   $  8-10\ \Omega^{-2}  $   were purchased from Techno Print Co., Ltd., Japan. The   $  C_{8}  $  -DPNDF solutions were obtained by dissolving various amounts of   $  C_{8}  $  -DPNDF in 1,1,2,2-tetrachloroethane. The PTAA solution is 2 mg/mL in chlorobenzene. Then the-obtained film layer was annealed at 110 °C for 10 min. After forming perovskite film, electron transporting layer of   $  C_{60}  $   (30 nm) and hole blocking layer of BCP (10 nm) were evaporated successively. A 100 nm thick of Ag cathode was thermally evaporated under a reduced pressure of   $  2\times10^{-5}  $   Torr to achieve a complete device via a metal shadow mask.

# short summary

Bearing high hole mobility and appropriate energy level, an organic small-molecule 2,7-Bis(4-octylphenyl)naphtho[2,1-b:6,5-b']difuran (C8-DPNDF) is introduced as a dopant-free hole transporting material in inverted perovskite solar cells. The device with C8-DPNDF as HTM shows decent power conversion efficiency of  $ 17.5\% $ , compared to  $ 17.1\% $  of control device with classic PTAA as HTM. Additionally, C8-DPNDF also exhibit non-deliquescence property, which significantly contribute to the storage stability, keeping  $ 92\% $  of its initial value after 30 days in ambient air.
