# Stabilization of Inorganic   $  CsPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{2}Br  $   Perovskite Compounds by Antioxidant Tea Polyphenol

Huaxia Ban, Qiang Sun, Tao Zhang, Hao Li, Yan Shen, and Mingkui Wang*

Implementation of inorganic perovskite compounds and reduction toxicity of lead are important for developing sustainable and renewable photovoltaic power generation. The inorganic Pb/Sn binary metal halide perovskites offer a perfect opportunity for tuning optical bandgap and thus hold significant potential in emerging technologies such as solar cells. However, an easy oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   to   $  Sn^{4+}  $   has become one of the main issues for achieving efficient and stable Sn-based perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, an effective method for stabilizing   $  CsPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{2}Br  $   is proposed to realize high-efficiency PSCs via antioxidant tea polyphenol (TP). It is found that TP can not only slow down the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   but also regulate perovskite film crystallization during the formation of perovskite films via coordination interaction, leading to a reduced density of defects and an enlarged open-circuit voltage. The resultant perovskite solar cell using   $  CsPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{2}Br  $   (TP) with an all-inorganic mesoscopic framework of FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/NiO  $  /carbon achieves an impressive power conversion efficiency of 8.10% with high stability.

# 1. Introduction

Over the past years, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of thin film solar cells based on organic-inorganic hybrid halide perovskite compounds with a general formula of  $ \mathrm{ABX}_3 $  has increased significantly from  $ 3.8\% $  in 2009 to  $ 25.2\% $  in 2019, where A is an organic cation such as  $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $ ,  $ \mathrm{FA}^+ $ , or the mixture of them, B is  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $ , and X is  $ \mathrm{Br}^- $ ,  $ \mathrm{I}^- $ , or their mixture.[1-4] Nevertheless, several crucial issues such as the instability of volatile organic components and toxicity of lead (Pb) contaminants for these organic-inorganic hybrid halide compounds need to be solved before the successful commercialization of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). A feasible method to solve the instability of organic composition is to replace the organic cations at A site completely by inorganic cations like cesium cation ( $ \mathrm{Cs}^+ $ ).  $ \mathrm{Cs}^+ $  has the most appropriate ion radius ( $ \approx 167~\mathrm{pm} $ ) among the inorganic ions that have been reported so far.[5-8] A complete substitution by  $ \mathrm{Cs}^+ $  improves the geometric symmetry between the cations at A site and the inorganic [PbX6] $ ^{4-} $  framework because of the slightly

So the Pb/Sn binary perovskite films show a stabilization toward oxidation compared with pure Sn perovskites.  $  ^{[20,21]}  $   Recently, several studies have been reported where partially substituting Pb with Sn to form the Pb/Sn binary metal halide perovskites can significantly enhance devices' efficiency and stability.  $  ^{[22-24]}  $   These results suggest that partly displacing Pb by Sn in perovskite composition might become an effective method to improve the performance of Sn-containing PSCs. In addition, by adjusting the corresponding ratio between Pb and Sn, the optical bandgap (  $  E_{g}  $  ) of all-inorganic   $  CsPb_{x}Sn_{1-x}X_{3}  $   can be easily tuned to promote the material absorbing visible light in the long wavelength region, which provides a perfect opportunity to develop all-perovskite tandem solar cells.  $  ^{[25]}  $

Some reducing additives have been proposed to retard the   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation of Sn-based PSCs such as   $  SnF_{2}  $ , ammonium hypophosphite (AHP), the potassium salt of hydroquinone sulfonic acid (KHQSA), and so on.  $  ^{[26-31]}  $   A commonly used solvent additive   $  SnF_{2}  $   has been reported to alleviate   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation by increasing the chemical potential of Sn and the Sn vacancies' formation of energy to a certain extent.  $  ^{[26]}  $   However, recent studies have clearly indicated that   $  SnF_{2}  $   leads to phase separation in the fabricated films because of its inhomogeneous dispersion during the film evolution process, which brings the difficulty in preparing high-quality Sn-based perovskite film on substrates with full coverage.  $  ^{[32]}  $   It is important for an additive to keep the bifunction of not only retarding

Tea polyphenol (TP) is known as an excellent antioxidant in biology, medicine, and so on to inhibit the oxidation of oxidizable substances.  $  ^{[33,34]}  $   In this study, we for the first time introduce TP as an antioxidant to inorganic perovskite precursor solution, aiming to obtain high-quality and stable   $  CsPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{2}Br  $   films. First, TP can modulate the process of crystallization due to the formation of   $  SnI_{2}-TP-PbI_{2}  $   composites which results in the uniform perovskite film. Second, the high antioxidant activity correlated with the reducing hydroxybenzene groups of the TP molecules can effectively slow down the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   in binary perovskite precursor solution. Therefore, an all-inorganic PSC based on 0.5% TP-incorporated   $  CsPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{2}Br  $   within a printable mesoscopic metal oxide framework of   $  TiO_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/NiO/carbon  $   architecture exhibited an impressive efficiency of 8.10%. Moreover, the optimized PSC with the addition of TP exhibits excellent stability which can maintain 94% of its initial PCE after being stored in a   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glove box for 60 days. This work gives a new way to fabricate Sn/Pb binary PSCs with high efficiency and stability.

We further performed steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) characterization to verify the influence of TP on the trap states and carrier lifetime. Under 532 nm excitation, the PL intensity of the   $  CsPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{2}Br  $   film with TP addition is increased by about 530% relative to that of the controlled film as a result of the improved suppression of trap-assisted nonradiative recombination of charge carriers (Figure 2e). The narrower full width at half maximum (FWHM) after the addition of TP further demonstrates the reduction of deep-level defect states within the bulk perovskite layer and improvement of perovskite crystallinity. The fitting TRPL curves with a double-exponential decay equation delivered two carrier lifetimes for the perovskite films (Figure 2f), in which the shorter   $  \tau_{1}  $   stands for the fast decay component assigned to the defect-induced nonradiative recombination and the longer   $  \tau_{2}  $   is attributed to the recombination of free carriers in the radiative channel.  $  ^{[40-42]}  $   The   $  CsPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{2}Br  $   film with TP addition displays a PL lifetime of   $  \approx1.19  $   ns and a long carrier lifetime   $  \approx9.67  $   ns, whereas the controlled film exhibits PL lifetimes of   $  \approx0.987  $   and   $  \approx5.47  $   ns, respectively (Table S2, Supporting Information). As a solvent additive, TP with antioxidation feature can not only retard the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   effectively but also form coordination complexes by coordination interaction between TP and   $  CsPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{2}Br  $   to optimize the process of crystallization, so as to achieve high-quality films. Figure 3a shows the corresponding top-view scanning electron microscope (SEM) images of   $  CsPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{2}Br  $   films with different TP proportions. Because of the higher Lewis acidity of Sn than that of Pb, the crystallization of Sn-based perovskite is much faster than Pb-based perovskite during the formation of perovskite film, making it difficult to obtain compact and uniform Pb/Sn binary perovskite films.  $  ^{[43]}  $   Therefore, the film with a rough surface and many pinholes is constructed by cobblestone-like crystalline grains with apparent gaps between the adjacent grains without TP addition. This could be one of the reasons for difficulties of fabrication of planar-structured Sn/Pb binary PSCs. However, adding TP has drastically changed the morphology of the perovskite and resulted in the appearance of compact large grains without pinholes. We can observe the full coverage of grains clearly with the 0.5% TP addition from the image. A higher ratio of TP up to 1.0% results in lamellar cross-linking at grain boundaries (GBs) because excessive TP has accumulated on the GBs.

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, we proposed a simple but effective solvent additive TP for efficient and stable binary PSCs based on   $  CsPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{2}Br  $  . # 4. Experimental Section

Materials: CsBr (99.99%, Sigma Aldrich), PbI  $  _{2}  $  (99.999%, Alfa Aesar), TP (99%, Macklin), SnI  $  _{2}  $  (99.99%, Sigma Aldrich), N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, anhydrous 99.8%, Sigma Aldrich), and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, anhydrous  $  \geq  $  99.5%, Sigma Aldrich) were used as received without further purification.

PSC Devices Fabrication: CsBr (170 mg) and   $  PbI_{2}  $   (369 mg) were dissolved in a mixed solvent of DMF (800 μL) and DMSO (200 μL) to obtain 0.8 mL  $  ^{-1}  $     $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solution, whereas CsBr (170 mg) and   $  SnI_{2}  $   (328 mg) were dissolved in a mixed solvent of DMF (500 μL) and DMSO (500 μL) to obtain 0.8 mL  $  ^{-1}  $     $  CsSnI_{2}Br  $   solution. TP (45 mg) was dissolved in DMF (200 μL) to obtain 0.8 mL  $  ^{-1}  $   TP solution. Then the binary perovskite precursor solution was prepared by mixing   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   (200 μL) and   $  CsSnI_{2}Br  $   (200 μL) precursor solution. The additive (TP) was added with 0, 0.2%, 0.5%, and 1% mole ratio in the binary precursor solutions. About 2.4 μL solution was dropped on the prepared all-inorganic FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /m-  $  TiO_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/NiO  $  /carbon mesoscopic framework. Finally, the devices were annealed at 80°C for 10 min to finish the fabrication. All the fabrication steps were conducted in a   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glove box.

$  CsPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{2}Br  $  Films Fabrication: The  $  CsPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{2}Br  $  films were fabricated on glasses in a  $  N_{2}  $ -filled glove box. The substrates were sequentially ultrasonically cleaned with detergent, water, deionized water, and ethanol. Then, the substrates were treated by ozone for 25 min. The prepared mixture precursor solution was deposited on the substrate through the one-step spin coating process (40 s at 2000 rpm). Finally, the  $  CsPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{2}Br  $  films were annealed at 80 °C for 5 min.

The fabrication details of the all-inorganic mesoscopic framework and characterizations are shown in Supporting Information.
