# Abstract:

Since solvent engineering method was applied in treating perovskite film, the performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have shown rapid growth and achieved remarkable achievement. Here we report highly reproducible method for controlling perovskite crystal growth by spraying anti-solvent process, which is quite different from conventional method by dripping. Results showed that the change of anti-solvent using tactic has a significant impact on the morphology and formation process of perovskite. It has a high probability to prepare a mirror-like surface without wave circle or ring defects, and get a longer carrier lifetime than those of crystals by dripping. # 1 Introduction

Organic-inorganic perovskite has shown one of the most rapid growths ever in photovoltaic history since miyasaka and co-workers reported perovskite solar cells (PSCs) for the first time in 2009.  $  ^{1-4}  $   To achieve high-quality perovskite films, a lot of research efforts had contributed to versatile films deposition approaches based on lead organo-halide perovskite such as sequential deposition,  $  ^{5,6}  $   screen printing,  $  ^{7}  $   vapor-assisted  $  ^{8}  $  , co-evaporation of the precursors  $  ^{9,10}  $   and near space sublimation etc.  $  ^{11}  $   Meanwhile, several strategies have been used to increase perovskite film quality, besides a wide range of compositions for precursor solution,  $  ^{12}  $   additives like HX,  $  ^{13}  $   gas-assisted method,  $  ^{14}  $   microwave irradiation process,  $  ^{15}  $   laser-assisted crystallization  $  ^{16}  $   and high temperature treatment.  $  ^{17}  $

In 2014, seok and coworkers introduced solvent-engineering method by dripping anti-solvent to fabricate PSCs and reached a PCE of exceeding 16.2%, through which, the morphology of the perovskite film can be controlled easily. This technology was widely used in one-step deposition method as yet.  $  ^{18}  $   However, this method would inevitably induce perovskite films with relatively rough and pinhole-rich surface when the anti-solvent amount, dripping timing, ambient operating environment and

As a result, there will often occur a phenomenon that perovskite film surface appeared uneven, rough, circle around like wavy ups and downs, or even cracking which is particularly evident for large scale devices.  $  ^{19}  $   This can result in serious perovskite crystal disorder, excessive grain boundary and recombination between electrons and holes, leading to a large current leakage as well as low fill factor (FF).  $  ^{20,21}  $

It was probably caused by two main reasons including solvent evaporation rate and impact force of anti-solvent droplets. The precursor solvent will evaporate quickly and extruded out when the superfluous anti-solvent was adding. The film lost support solvent to keep smooth integrity surface, and thus resulting in film crack which can be seen visually. In contrast, too little anti-solvent will make it lose the ability to squeeze precursor solvent and promote crystal growth, and finally leading to rough surface.  $  ^{22}  $   On the other hand, inappropriate dripping time or improper rotating speed can also cause out-off-balance between solvent evaporation rate and crystal grow process. Another important limitation of solvent engineering method is impact force by dripping. When the anti-solvent lands on the substrate, it has a great chance to produce damaging film with circles like wave or cracks. Large area of film substrate is also impossible for the dripping owning to small region of anti-solvent drop dot.  $  ^{23}  $

Moreover, due to drops of anti-solvent operated all by manual work, the dropping rate, dropping angle, dropping height were unable to do exactly the same every time. And just this operational details will have direct impact on the perovskite film morphology. In order to reduce drop impact effect and fragile solvent evaporation

Spray technology is one of the commonly used techniques which has been used to deposit many kinds of thin films in the past decades. Recently, some literatures have reported the deposition of perovskite solar cells using this technique to fabricate blocking layer, light absorbing materials and hole transport layer.  $  ^{25,26}  $   Different from previous spraying materials for the purpose of deposition, we used this technology for atomizing anti-solvent. When the anti-solvent landed on the substrate, atomized solvent particles can be evenly dispersed. That will reduce negative effects caused by impact and centrifugal force, as illustrated in Fig. 1. An outward flux of the precursor solvents was generated, while the perovskite began to nucleate and crystallize. The crystallization process from the bottom to the top of the perovskite film has a correlation with continuous spraying conditions. By continuously supplying anti-solvent, the small early formed perovskite crystal grains can be grown into larger crystals.  $  ^{27-29}  $

The resulting film showed enhanced carrier transport properties compared to film made by conventional method. # 2.1 Materials

Lead (II) Iodide (99.99%, trace metals basis) [for Perovskite precursor] was purchased from TCI,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.,   $  TiO_{2}  $   (particle size: about 30 nm, crystalline phase: anatase)   $  TiO_{2}  $   from dysol. # 2.2 Solar cell device fabrication

A thick dense blocking layer of   $  TiO_{2}  $   (bl-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) was deposited onto F-doped   $  SnO_{2}  $   (FTO) substrate by spin coating to prevent direct contact between the FTO and the hole-conducting layer. A   $  TiO_{2}  $   blocking layer was deposited on the cleaned FTO by spray pyrolysis, using dry air as carrier gas, at 460°C from a precursor solution of 0.6 ml of titanium diisopropoxide and 0.4 ml of bis (acetylacetonate) in 7 ml of anhydrous isopropanol. A mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer (dysol, particle size: about 30 nm, crystalline phase: anatase, diluted to w/w=1/6 in ethanol) about 160-180 nm thickness film was deposited by spin coating at 4000 rpm for 20 s onto the bl-  $  TiO_{2}  $ /FTO substrate. After spin coating, the substrate was immediately dried on a hotplate at 120°C, and the substrates were then calcined at 510°C to remove organic components. The perovskite film was deposited by spin coating onto the   $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate. The prepared FAI powders and   $  PbI_{2}  $   (TCI) for   $  FA_{0.81}MA_{0.15}Cs_{0.025}PbI_{2.5}Br_{0.45}  $   solution (1.4M   $  Pb^{+}  $  ) were stirred in a mixture of DMSO and DMF (v/v=2:8) at 60°C for

The precursor solution was prepared in a glovebox of 1.4 M   $  Pb^{2+}  $   (  $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}  $  ) in a mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO, the volume ratio of DMF to DMSO was 4:1. The molar ratio for   $  PbI_{2}/PbBr_{2}  $   was fixed at 0.85:0.15,   $  PbI_{2}/FAI  $   was fixed at 1.05, FAI/CsI was fixed at 32.4, and the molar ratio for   $  MABr/PbBr_{2}  $   was fixed at 1:1. The resulting solution was coated onto the mp-  $  TiO_{2}/bl-TiO_{2}/FTO  $   substrate by a consecutive two-step spin-coating process at 1100 and 5000 r.p.m for 10s and 60 s respectively. During the second spin-coating step at 20s, the substrate was treated with anti-solvent (chlorobenzenel) by spraying method and lasted for 20s to form the perovskite crystal, and the substrate was then heated at 105°C for 1h on a hotplate under low speed flow clean dry air protection. FAI was synthesized from hydroiodic acid reacting with formamidine acetate. The HTM layer was deposited by spin-coating at 4000 rpm for 20 s, using a solution of spiro-MeOTAD, 4-tert-butylpyridine, and lithium bis (trifluoromethylsulphonyl) imide and the Co (III)-complex. Finally, Gold (80 nm) counter electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation on top of the device to form the back contact.

It is crucial for devices to obtain a flat perovskite layer whose surface is fully and homogenously covered. Herein, we adopted spraying anti-solvent technology as an effective method for preparing such a layer. Fig. 1 presents a schematic diagram of spraying process and dripping method.

Dripping anti-solvent onto the substrate generates impact force. In contrast, SAP can produce uniformly dispersed small particles, and the rate is controllable via carrier gas flow rate.

The spraying anti-solvent process (SAP) was performed in two steps. Firstly, the perovskite film was deposited via spin-coating. Secondly, anti-solvent was sprayed on the film, which can be rapidly dispersed in the whole film and greatly alleviate negative effects caused by liquid drop impact force and rotating centrifugal force. The pristine chlorobenzene and precursor solvent have good affinity, so solvent can be rapidly evaporated. We deduced that the SAP could be benefit to quickly taking the solvent away very softly, which induced perovskite films tend to form more uniform and flat high quality perovskite film. Finally, the homogeneous flat film converted into a smooth and shiny perovskite layer after annealing.

To check the reproducibility of the devices performance by anti-solvent spraying method, we repeated the device fabrication using the optimal process. Distribution of the efficiencies are compared using histograms obtained from 24 integrated perovskite devices respectively, as shown in Fig5 (a, b). As can be seen from the results, both of two type devices performed well. The average efficiency of SAP group (18.14%) was higher than that of conventional group (17.05%), and the degree of dispersion was smaller. Both of them revealed that reproducibility of SAP group is improved with respect to conventional group. In consideration of the same  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and HTM layers used for the two groups of devices, the improved reproducibility of SAP group should be attributed to the optimized perovskite layer with mean distributed crystals.

The high quality of continues perovskite films prepared by SAP method gives the devices a better photoelectric performance as well as increased stability. The device exhibited slower deterioration rate with the PCE keeping over 90% which was stored in dry air and dark conditions after 60 days. To the contrary, devices made by dripping anti-solvent showed faster deteriorating rate with PCE dropping to 80% as depicted in Fig. 5(c). The device showed significant superiority probably due to compact and orderly crystal and excellent crystallinity, and this will result perovskite itself have strong ability to resist decomposition.

By introducing SAP method, we can effectively control the grain size, surface coverage and flatness of the films. The influence on crystallization, microstructure and carrier transportation property of perovskite films were systemically investigated. After optimizing spraying operating conditions, the power conversion efficiency was boosted from 15.34% to 18.78%.
