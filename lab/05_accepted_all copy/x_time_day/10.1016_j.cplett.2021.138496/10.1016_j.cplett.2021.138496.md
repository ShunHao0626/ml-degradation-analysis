# 1. Introduction

During past few years, organic-inorganic hybrid lead trihalide   $  \left(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{PbX}_{3},\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{Cl},\mathrm{Br}\text{ or }\mathrm{I}\right)  $   based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been intensively studied and rapidly developed owing to their advantages such as light weight, low cost, simple solution processability and high power conversion efficiency (PCE) [1–4]. Since the first application of perovskite materials in solar cells in 2009 [5], the PCE of PSCs was rapidly improved from initial 3.8 to recently certified 25.2% [6], which indicates a great potential for future commercialization.

Depending on the position of charge transport layers, PSCs can be fabricated following a regular (n-i-p) or inverted (p-i-n) device structure   $  [1,5,7–9]  $  . For both kinds of PSCs, a high quality perovskite film with uniform morphology and high crystallinity is a basic requirement for fabricating high-performance PSCs   $  [10]  $  . However, the charge transport layers should also be very carefully optimized, because they are highly related charge transportation and dissociation of PSCs   $  [8,11]  $  . For example, regular structured PSCs have been developed from dye sensitized solar cells, which usually utilize titanium oxide (TiO  $  _{2}  $  ) and 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) as the electron and hole transport materials, respectively   $  [12–15]  $  . In this work, we introduced an electron-transport layer (ETL), which combined chrome oxide  $ (\mathrm{Cr_2O_3}) $  nanoparticles and compact titanium oxide  $ (\mathrm{c - TiO_2}) $ , to fabricate high-performance perovskite solar cells (PSCs). We find that, the  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  annealed  $ \mathrm{Cr_2O_3} $  shows a uniform morphology, which leads to an average PCE of  $ 15.3\% $  and champion PCE of  $ 15.7\% $  ( $ 16.0\% $  for mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  based case). However, the stability of the PSCs are improved with a suppressed PCE degradation of  $ 19\% $  ( $ 26\% $  for mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  based case after 7 days storage in ambient condition). This method provides some inspiration for the preparation of regular PSCs with higher stability.

such as poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS, for hole transport) and phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM, for electron transport) [16–20]. From previous studies, the PCE of regular PSCs is higher than that of inverted ones, although the fabrication procedure is more complicated [12–20]. For example, the electron-transport layer (ETL) of regular PSCs is composed of a compact and mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers (c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}  $  ), which usually requires two steps of spin coating operation [12,15,21]. Moreover, for sintering the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL, usually a high temperature of about 500 °C is needed, which would dramatically increase the cost of large-scale production in the future [22,23]. Furthermore, the photocatalytic effect of m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   can decompose   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite into lead iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  ) under the irradiation of ultraviolet (UV) light, which is a significant restriction to the stability of PSCs [24,25]. As a result, finding new ETLs to replace m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   becomes an important issue for improving the stability performance of PSCs. For example, planar structured PSCs have recently been developed, in which a smooth   $  TiO_{2}  $   or doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   film was used in stead of m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   as the ETLs in PSCs [26–28]. Compared with conventional mesoporous PSCs, these planar PSCs have similar or even higher PCEs with enhanced stability [24–26]. Xie et al. developed a two step method for perovskite preparation, which enabled the use of PCBM as ETL in regular PSCs [29]. Jiang et al. used commercial available tin oxide (  $  SnO_{2}  $  ) nanoparticles to replace c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}  $   as the ETL [30]. The

PSCs using this technique showed a high PCE of 19.9%, with significantly improved stability [30]. In addition, some bilayer type ETLs have been intensively studied, which usually utilized two kinds of materials as the ETLs [31–32]. Lee et al. combined the compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}  $   as the ETL, which improved the PCE of the PSCs to 19.8% with superior stability [33]. Otoufi et al. found that the combination of   $  TiO_{2}  $   and tungsten oxide (  $  WO_{3}  $  ) could also be a good candidate for replacing m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   [34]. Other than   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  WO_{3}  $ , chrome oxide (  $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  ) is another widely used electron transport material in organic solar cells and inverted PSCs, which owns a suitable conduction band (CB) [35–37]. Since   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   nanoparticles can be easily obtained (purchased or synthesized), it is very important to investigate the use of   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   nanoparticles for electron transport in regular PSCs.

In this study, we developed a combined ETL by depositing   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   nanoparticles onto c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   layer for fabricating high-performance PSCs. We find the 500 °C annealed c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  -based ETL showed a uniform morphology, which could more efficiently extract electrons from the perovskite absorber. As a result, the PSCs using the 500 °C annealed c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   show an average PCE of 15.3%, with a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 20.4 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.03 V and a fill factor (FF) of 72.8%. This performance is comparable to the   $  TiO_{2}  $   based mesoporous PSCs, indicating the excellent electron transport property of   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   nanoparticles. Moreover, the stability of c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  -based PSCs becomes better than that of   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based mesoporous PSCs, with a reduced PCE degradation from 26% to 19% after 7 days storage in ambient condition. The best PSC using 500 °C annealed c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   showed a high PCE of 15.7% with a stable power output and negligible hysteresis. We provide a good candidate for replacing m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   as the ETL for fabricating regular PSCs with high-performance.

# 2.2. Characterization

The UV–visible (UV–vis) absorption spectrum were characterized by using a Perkin Elmer Lambda 750 (USA). The X-ray diffraction (XRD) and photoluminescence (PL) spectra were measured using an X-ray diffractometer (Panalytical, Netherlands) and a spectrometer (FLS920, Edinburgh Instruments, UK), respectively. The cross-sectional image of the PSC and the top view images of the perovskite surfaces were measured using an SU8020 scanning electron microscope (SEM, Hitachi, Japan), operated at an acceleration voltage of 8 kV. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) spectra of the PSCs were tested using an electrochemical workstation (CH Instruments, USA). The current density–voltage (J–V) characteristics of the PSCs were measured for an irradiation intensity of 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  (AM1.5). The incident photon-to-current efficiency (IPCE) was measured using a Solar Cell IPCE measurement system (Solar Cell Scan 100, Zolix, China).

# 3. Results and discussion

The schematic in Fig. 1(a) indicates a layer-by-layer structure of the PSCs, in which the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite absorber was sandwiched by the electron and hole transport layers of spiro-OMeTAD and c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  , respectively. The SEM image in Fig. 1(b) shows the   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   nanoparticles on a silicon wafer, which indicates a diameter of 20–30 nm for the nanoparticles. Fig. 1(c) shows the cross-sectional SEM image of a c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  -based PSC, which is in good agreement with Fig. 1(a). The thicknesses of perovskite and spiro-OMeTAD layers are about 450 and 250 nm, which are typical for   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   based PSCs with high-performance [12,14]. From the SEM image, the c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  -based ETL can be clearly observed between the FTO electrode and perovskite absorber with a thickness of about 100 nm. The energy alignment in Fig. 1(d) exhibits the working mechanism of the PSCs under sunlight irradiation. When charges are generated from the perovskite absorber, holes and electrons will transport to spiroOMeTAD and c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  -based ETL, respectively. Compared with   $  TiO_{2}  $  , the CB level of   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   is -3.9 eV [35–37], which coincides with that of perovskite absorber. As a result, the generated electrons can be easily extracted from perovskite to   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  , which is beneficial for charge dissociation. Moreover, the deep valance band (-7.4 eV) of   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   can efficiently block holes, which is a suppression to charge recombination [35]. This energy alignment indicate that   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   is a promising electron transport material for regular structured PSCs. From the transmission spectrum in Fig. S1, other than a small valley at about 530 nm, the   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  -based ETL shows a high transparency at wavelength range of 400–800 nm.

Fig. 2 shows the morphology of the c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  -based ETLs with   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   nanoparticles thermally annealed at different temperatures. When annealed at a low temperature (400 °C), the   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   nanoparticles (with a diameter of 20–30 nm) and aggregations (with a diameter of 30–50 nm) can be observed (the bright dots in Fig. 2(a)), which induce a rough surface morphology. The quality of the prepared   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite films were characterized by SEM, UV–vis absorption and XRD measurements. As shown in Fig. 3(a), the perovskite film on c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   shows a uniform morphology with a full surface coverage. The size of perovskite grains is between 100 and 250 nm, which is similar to those of Pb(Ac)  $  _{2}  $  -based perovskite appearing in previous studies [16–19]. As shown in Fig. 2(b), the UV–vis absorption spectrum shows a sufficient light absorption at the wavelength range of 400–800 nm, which is a typical shape for absorption of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite [16–18]. The crystallinity of the perovskite film can be explored from the XRD measurement. As shown in Fig. 2(c). The two significant peaks at 14.1° and 28.4° are indexed by the (110) and (220) characteristic lattice planes of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite, which indicates a high crystallinity of the prepared perovskite film [11–13]. The SEM, UV–vis absorption and XRD results demonstrate the high quality of the prepared perovskite film on c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  -based ETL, which is a fundamental aspect for the high performance of PSCs [10]. The SEM images and UV–vis absorption spectra for perovskite on c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}  $   and c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   (annealed at 400, 500 or 600 °C) are shown in Figs. S3 and S4, which indicate a similar quality of perovskite films. Fig. 2(d) shows the steady state PL spectra of perovskite films on bare glass, c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   and c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}  $   based ETLs. The perovskite film on bare glass shows a strong PL intensity at a specific wavelength of 778 nm, which is consistent with previous studies using   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   as absorber [16–18]. The PL intensity of perovskite on c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}  $   was significantly reduced, which indicates a strong quenching effect of photo-generated excitons [8,14]. When using c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  -based ETL, the quenched PL intensity is similar with that of perovskite on c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}  $ , indicating the excellent electron extraction property of   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   nanoparticles. The PL intensity of perovskite on c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   is slightly higher than that of perovskite on c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}  $ , which is possibly caused by the enlarged contact area between perovskite and m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   [8,14]. The comparison of PL spectra for perovskite films on c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   (annealed at 400, 500 or 600 °C) are shown in Fig. S5. The perovskite film on 500 °C annealed c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   exhibited the lowest PL intensity among the three cases. From Fig. 2, this was possibly caused by the improved contact between perovskite and   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $ , which reduced the barrier for charge transport from perovskite to ETL.

We also compared the long-term stability performance of the PSCs using c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   and c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}  $   based ETLs. Fig. 5(b) shows the PCE degradation (normalized) of the PSCs based on different ETLs, which have been stored in ambient condition (under humidity of about 30%, at 25 °C) without encapsulation. After 7 days, the c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   based PSCs maintains 81% of their initial PCE (15.3%). Whereas the c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}  $   based PSCs only have a PCE of 11.8%, which is 74% of the initial PCE (16.0%). The degradation (19%) of c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   based PSCs is 36.8% lower than that (26%) of c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}  $   based PSCs. Previous studies already demonstrated the photocatalytic effect of m-  $  TiO_{2}  $ , which could decompose   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite into   $  PbI_{2}  $   under the irradiation of UV light [24,25]. This mechanism can well explain the reason for the improvement of stability in our work: due to the addition of   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $ , the contact between   $  TiO_{2}  $   and perovskite layer is partially blocked, thus passivating the above photocatalytic effect, which further lead to an improved stability of the PSCs.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we investigated the use of c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  as the ETL for fabricating  $  Pb(Ac)_{2}  $ -based PSCs. The SEM, UV–vis absorption and XRD results indicate the high quality of the prepared perovskite film on c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  based ETL. The characterizations of PL spectra and EIS spectra demonstrates the excellent electron transport property of c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $ , which is consistent with the energy alignment. As a result, the PSCs using 500 °C annealed c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  showed a high PCE of 15.3%, with a  $  J_{sc}  $  of 20.4 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , a  $  V_{oc}  $  of 1.03 V and a FF of 72.8%. The above data show that the addition of  $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  slightly affect the photoelectric properties of the device. However, the long-term stability of c-  $  TiO_{2}/Cr_{2}O_{3}  $ -based PSCs was improved, with a reduced PCE degradation from 26% to 19% after 7 days storage in ambient condition. The best PSC from the 500 °C annealed group showed a champion PCE of 15.7% with a stable power output and negligible hysteresis. In this research, we demonstrate the film, which combined c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  and  $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  nanoparticles, is an effective alternative ETL for replacing m-  $  TiO_{2}  $  in the fabrication of regular PSCs with superior performance.
