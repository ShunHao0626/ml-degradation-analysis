# 1. Introduction

A perovskite solar cell is a type of solar cell which includes an organic-inorganic lead halide compound as the light-harvesting active layer. Methylammonium lead halide perovskites  $ \mathrm{(CH_3NH_3PbX_3} $ ,  $ X = Cl $ , Br, I) have recently emerged as promising materials for optoelectronics due to their unique advantages, such as ease of fabrication, low cost, and high light-harvesting potential (Kojima et al., 2009; Im et al., 2011; Lee et al., 2012; Burschka et al., 2013; Liu et al., 2013). The efficiency of perovskite solar cells has increased from  $ 3.8\% $  (Kojima et al., 2009) in 2009 to a certified  $ 20.1\% $  (Yang et al., 2015) in 2014, making this the fastest-advancing solar technology to date. In the structure of conventional-type perovskite solar cells, titanium oxide is used as the electron transport layer (ETL), but high-temperature annealing is required, limiting its use for flexible applications.

Inverted-type perovskite solar cells have developed (Jeng et al., 2013; Chiang et al., 2014) as an alternative structure to conventional-type perovskite solar cells due to their low-temperature solution process and low hysteresis. In this structure, poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly-styrene sulfonate (PEDOT: PSS) is commonly used as the hole transport layer (HTL). However,

# ABSTRACT

Inverted-type perovskite solar cells offer the potential of fabrication by low-temperature solution processing as an alternative to conventional-type perovskite solar cells. PEDOT:PSS is commonly used as the hole transport layer (HTL), but its acidic nature may damage the reliability and stability of the photovoltaic device. In this study, we demonstrate the use of   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   HTLs as an alternative to PEDOT:PSS for planar   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells. Furthermore, the   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   HTLs are treated using a UV-ozone treatment to improve the surface coverage of the perovskite layer. The resultant conversion efficiency (PCE) for the best cells at AM1.5G illumination with a shadow mask was 13.1%.

However, thermal annealing treatment is needed to obtain crystalline NiO films to ensure hole transporting capability and device efficiency. Inverted-type perovskite solar cells based on inorganic single  $ \mathrm{MoO_x} $  layers for use as HTLs has also been reported, but they exhibit poor device performance. Recently, perovskite solar cells fabricated using  $ \mathrm{MoO_x / PEDOT:PSS} $  (Hou et al., 2015) and  $ \mathrm{MoO_x / NPB} $  (B.-S. Kim et al., 2015) dual-layers as HTLs in perovskite solar cells have shown good device performance and stability, but this dual layer structure requires a more extensive fabrication process and extra costs.

In this study, we report on perovskite solar cells fabricated using single-layer type thermal evaporated  $ \mathrm{MoO_x} $  layers as the HTLs. A UV-ozone treatment was used to improve the surface coverage of the perovskite layers because of the excellent wetting of the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  precursor solution on the  $ \mathrm{MoO_x} $  layer. In addition,  $ \mathrm{WO_x} $ , which is widely used as a HTL in organic electro-optical devices, was also thermally evaporated as the HTL for perovskite solar cells. These  $ \mathrm{MoO_x} $  and  $ \mathrm{WO_x} $  photovoltaic devices achieved power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of 13.1 and  $ 9.8\% $  with enhanced perovskite coverage, respectively. # 2. Methodology

Both   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   with thicknesses of 10 nm were deposited using thermal evaporation on patterned ITO-covered glass substrates (obtained from Ruilong Inc.). Then the as-deposited films were treated with UV-ozone for 10 min. For depositing the perovskite layer, first, several drops of a   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution (1.0 M in DMF) was spin-coated on the top of UV-ozone treated   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   at a spin rate of 3000 rpm. After the   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer was dried, a   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   solution (52 mg/mL isopropanol) was spin-coated on top of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film at spin rates of 3000 rpm for 15 s to form the perovskite structures. After spin coating, the film was annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. More detailed information about the two-step spin-coating method can be found in the literature (Tseng et al., 2015). After perovskite deposition, 2 wt% of PCBM in a chlorobenzene solution was spin-coated onto the perovskite layer at 1250 rpm. Finally, a silver (Ag) electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation with a thickness of 100 nm.

# 3. Material and device measurement

A field-emission scanning electron microscope (GeminiSEM, ZEISS) was used to observe the surface morphology of the cells. Photoluminescence (PL) and absorption spectra were measured on a fluorescence spectrophotometer (Hitachi F-7000) and UV/VIS/NIR spectrophotometer (Hitachi U-4100 spectrometers), respectively. Moreover, the current density-voltage (J-V) characteristics were measured using a Keithley 4200 programmable source meter under irradiation by a  $ 1000\mathrm{W} $  xenon lamp. The forward scan was set from  $ -0.05 $  to  $ 1.05\mathrm{V} $  and the reverse scan was set from 1.05 to  $ -0.05\mathrm{V} $ . The time (including the data extraction and waiting time) and the voltage interval used for getting one current data point was  $ 200~\mathrm{ms} $  and  $ 0.01\mathrm{V} $  for both scans. The irradiation power density on the surface of the sample was calibrated to be  $ 1000\mathrm{W / m^2} $ . The external quantum efficiency (EQE) or incident photo-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) was measured in air with a QE-R3011 measurement system (Enlitech Inc., Taiwan).

# 4. Results and discussion

It was found that it was difficult to deposit the   $  PbI_{2}  $   precursor directly on the surface of the as-deposited   $  MoO_{x}  $   (or   $  WO_{x}  $  ) thin films because of the serious hydrophobicity at the interface between the DMF and the as-deposited thin films. After the   $  MoO_{x}  $   (or   $  WO_{x}  $  ) thin films were modified via a UV-ozone treatment, contact between the   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   films and DMF was excellent and therefore both films provided good wettability with DMF, as

shown in Fig. 1. These results indicate that the UV-ozone modification of  $ \mathrm{MoO_x} $  and  $ \mathrm{WO_x} $  thin films is necessary for device fabrication and relevant for the perovskite solution to enable the formation of high coverage uniform perovskite films.

The surface coverage and the morphology of perovskite films are important for determining device performance. To observe the morphology of perovskite films on the   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   thin films, we utilized scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Fig. 2(a) and (b) presents top-view SEM images of the perovskite films deposited on the   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   thin films, respectively. Spin-coating on both the   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   thin films leads to uniform perovskite films with the grain sizes of about 100–300 nm and high surface coverages without voids or pinholes between the grain boundaries. The cross-sectional SEM images (Fig. 2(c) and (d)) are consistent with the top-view SEM results. The perovskite films on both the   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   layers showed smooth surfaces with high surface coverage and dense crystal packing. Under the same growth conditions, the thickness of the perovskite films on the   $  WO_{x}  $   layer were slightly thicker (450–470 nm) than the films deposited on the   $  MoO_{x}  $   layer (430–450 nm).

We prepared samples with configurations of glass/ITO/MoO $ _{x} $  or WO $ _{x} $ /MAPbI $ _{3} $  perovskite to investigate the light absorbance of the perovskite films and hole transport from the perovskite films to the ITO anode. The absorption spectra of the perovskite films are exhibited in Fig. 3(a). The slightly larger absorbance of the perovskite film on the WO $ _{x} $  film was due to the slightly greater thickness as shown in the cross-sectional SEM. The absorbance spectra of the perovskite films on the MoO $ _{x} $  and WO $ _{x} $  films have strong absorption coefficients of  $ 6.1 \times 10^{4} $  and  $ 6.0 \times 10^{4} $  cm $ ^{-1} $  respectively, at the wavelength of 550 nm. This confirms that highly dense perovskite films were obtained, (Tseng et al., 2015) which is consistent with the SEM result. Fig. 3(b) shows the photoluminescence (PL) of the perovskite films on both oxides. MoO $ _{x} $  led to perovskite with more efficient PL quenching than WO $ _{x} $ , implying that holes separated from photo-generated excitons within the perovskite layer are more efficiently extracted from perovskite to MoO $ _{x} $ .

To verify the merits of the   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   films in actual devices, we fabricated perovskite solar cells using the simple architecture of ITO/  $  MoO_{x}  $   or   $  WO_{x}/MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite/PCBM/Ag (see inset to Fig. 4(a)). PCBM was used as the electron transporting layer due to the well-known efficient electron transporting/hole-blocking capability from perovskite to the Ag cathode. Fig. 4(a) presents the current density–voltage (J–V) curves of the best perovskite solar cells fabricated using the   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   films as the HTLs. Devices with   $  WO_{x}  $   exhibited a PCE of 9.8% with a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 16.6 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 0.93 V and fill factor (FF) of 0.64. Replacing   $  WO_{x}  $   with   $  MoO_{x}  $   led to a significant enhancement in device efficiency. The device with   $  MoO_{x}  $   yielded a PCE of 13.1% with a   $  J_{SC}  $   of 18.8 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{OC}  $   of 0.99 V and FF of 0.71. (2014) suggested that the hysteresis is a property of the perovskite absorber (ferroelectric behavior) and the interface between the perovskite and the charge collection layers. Because no difference was found in the perovskite absorbers deposited on   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $ , it is suggested that the interface contact between perovskite and   $  MoO_{x}  $   is better than that between perovskite and   $  WO_{x}  $ . Therefore, the perovskite/HTM interface contacts may be related to the energy level alignment.

The stability of the devices with   $  MoO_{x}  $   and PEDOT:PSS was also observed, as shown in Fig. 7. The devices were stored in air with a relative humidity of 30–40%. The initial performance of the PEDOT:PSS device exhibited a PCE of 15.1% with a   $  J_{SC}  $   of 20.8 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{OC}  $   of 0.98 V and FF of 0.74. Although the PEDOT:PSS device showed good performance due to the good electric conductivity, the stability of the   $  MoO_{x}  $   device was much better than that of the PEDOT:PSS device. # 5. Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated the characteristics of inverted type perovskite solar cells using UV-ozone treated   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   as the hole transporting layers. The optimum device fabricated using the UV-ozone treated   $  MoO_{x}  $   exhibited outstanding performance, with   $  J_{SC} = 18.8 \, mA cm^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{OC} = 0.99 \, V  $  , FF = 0.71, and PCE = 13.1%. Both   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   thin films needed the UV-ozone treatment to increase wettability with DMF for a two-step perovskite formation process. After UV-ozone treatment, the perovskite films grown on both the   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   thin films showed high density and good coverage. The difference in device efficiency between   $  MoO_{x}  $   and   $  WO_{x}  $   HTLs is due to the band matching at the HTL/perovskite interface. This study provides some evidence for the use of metal oxides as HTLs in perovskite solar cells.
