# Poly(vinylpyrrolidone)-doped  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  as an electron transport layer for perovskite solar cells with improved performance

Di Wang,  $  ^{a,b}  $   Shan-Ci Chen,  $  ^{a}  $   and Qingdong Zheng  $  ^{a,*}  $

Thin film tin oxide (  $  SnO_{2}  $  ) is a good electron transport layer (ETL) for perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) with its excellent optical and electrical properties as well as facile accessibility. However, for high-performance PVSCs, the electron mobility of   $  SnO_{2}  $   should be further improved by optimizing its film quality. Herein, we report a poly(vinylpyrrolidone) (PVP)-doped   $  SnO_{2}  $   (P-  $  SnO_{2}  $  ) as an ETL for PVSCs with increased efficiencies and improved stability. The P-  $  SnO_{2}  $   film exhibits improved electron transport than the control   $  SnO_{2}  $ . At the same time, the perovskite grown on P-  $  SnO_{2}  $   shows more uniform crystalline grain, higher electron mobility and lower defect density than the control sample grown on the pure   $  SnO_{2}  $ . Moreover, P-  $  SnO_{2}  $   can efficiently extract electrons from the perovskite layer with its optimal conduction band. Therefore, the optimized device based on P-  $  SnO_{2}  $   shows an improved power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 19.42% compared with the control PVSC based on   $  SnO_{2}  $   which exhibits a PCE of 18.05%. Furthermore, the P-  $  SnO_{2}  $ -based devices show reduced hysteresis and better stability without any encapsulation. Our results provide a facile strategy to fabricate PVSCs with improved performance by incorporating a small amount of PVP into the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL.

# 1. Introduction

With their unique properties of intense and adjustable optical absorption, bipolar carrier transportation, excellent carrier mobility and long carrier lifetime, perovskite materials have been used in diversiform applications such as light-emitting devices, photodetectors, photovoltaic devices, optically pumped lasers, etc.  $  ^{1-6}  $   Among these applications, perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) in particular, have received increasing attention as evidenced by the fact that the best power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PVSCs has exceeded 23% within 10 years.  $  ^{7-10}  $   Besides the top and bottom electrodes, PVSCs are made from three functional layers, i.e. electron transport layer (ETL), perovskite layer, and hole transport layer (HTL).  $  ^{11, 12}  $   Among the three functional layers, the perovskite layer plays a key role in determining the performance of PVSCs. Thus, various modifications on the perovskite layers have been carried out to improve the performance of PVSCs.  $  ^{4, 13, 14}  $   For example, Saliba et al. reported stable PVSCs with a best PCE of 21.1% by using cesium-containing triple cation perovskite.  $  ^{4}  $   In addition to the perovskite layer, ETL of the conventional planar-type PVSCs is also of vital importance for high-

performance devices.  $  ^{15}  $   Originated from the solid-state dye-sensitized solar cells, mesoporous titanium oxide (  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) coated on compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   was first used as the electron transport materials for PVSCs.  $  ^{5}  $   However, the electron-mobility of   $  TiO_{2}  $   is an order of magnitude less than those of the most commonly used hole transport materials, such as 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spiro-bifluorene.  $  ^{16}  $   At the same time,   $  TiO_{2}  $   usually needs a high-temperature annealing at 500 °C which is a disadvantage for future mass production of PVSCs.  $  ^{17-19}  $   Therefore, low-temperature-processed electron transport materials such as tin oxide (  $  SnO_{2}  $  ), zinc oxide (ZnO), and magnesium oxide (MgO), are gradually explored for PVSCs considering their high electron mobilities and low-temperature processability.  $  ^{20-22}  $

Among the many ETLs for PVSCs,   $  SnO_{2}  $   stands out as an excellent electron transport material with the following salient advantages: (i) wide bandgap, deep conduction band and high transparency, (ii) high electron mobility and few pinhole, (iii) simple solution processability.  $  ^{[22-26]}  $   So far, compact and pinhole-free   $  SnO_{2}  $   films have been successfully prepared by diverse methods which include spin-coating, atomic layer deposition, slot-die printing, and so on.  $  ^{[22, 23, 27, 28]}  $   To avoid charge accumulation at the ETL/perovskite interface, researchers have developed several strategies to further increase the electron mobility of   $  SnO_{2}  $  . For example, with its strong chelation function, ethylenediamine-N,N,N',N'-tetraacetic acid (EDTA) was successfully added into the   $  SnO_{2}  $   to tune the energy level and improve charge transport ability of   $  SnO_{2}  $   thereby leading to PVSCs with improved efficiency and negligible hysteresis.  $  ^{[11]}  $   In addition, a water-soluble polymer of polyethylene glycol (PEG) has also been used to reduce the

As a common water-soluble polymer, polyvinyl pyrrolidone (PVP) can adhere to metal surface with the bonding between lone pair electrons of nitrogen (or oxygen) in PVP and metal ions. At the same time, the long vinyl polymeric chain in the PVP backbone would cause a steric hindrance to prevent the agglomeration of nanoparticles. Thus, PVP was incorporated into ZnO ETLs for organic photovoltaics previously.  $  ^{30}  $   However, the modification of   $  SnO_{2}  $   with PVP has never been reported for PVSCs, to the best of our knowledge. Here, we report on the use of PVP-doped   $  SnO_{2}  $   (  $  P-SnO_{2}  $  ) as an ETL for PVSCs with increased efficiency, reduced hysteresis and improved stability. # 2.1 Characterization of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  films with PVP and without PVP

The thicknesses of both   $  SnO_{2}  $   films are about 30 nm. It is found that the transmittance values of   $  ITO/SnO_{2}  $   and   $  ITO/P-SnO_{2}  $   films in the wavelength range from 300 to 400 nm are higher than those of the bare ITO. The electron mobilities of   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  P-SnO_{2}  $   (5 wt% PVP in   $  SnO_{2}  $  ) films were investigated by

# 3. Conclusions

In conclusion, PVP was incorporated into   $  SnO_{2}  $   to form an excellent ETL for perovskite solar cells with improved performance. The interaction between PVP and Sn ions would lead to a dense ETL with an increased electron mobility. Furthermore, the PVP incorporation leads to the perovskite layer with more uniform domain size, increased electron transportation and reduced defects. Thus, the best-performance PVSC based on   $  P-SnO_{2}  $   exhibited an improved PCE of 19.42% in comparison with the control PVSC based on   $  SnO_{2}  $   which showed a lower PCE of 18.05%. Without any encapsulation, the   $  P-SnO_{2}  $  -based devices showed reduced hysteresis and better shelf-stability compared to the   $  SnO_{2}  $  -based counterpart. In combination with the improved reproducibility of   $  P-SnO_{2}  $  -based devices, PVP-doped   $  SnO_{2}  $   is a promising electron transport material for high-performance and stable PVSCs.
