# Regulating the Film Growth and Reducing the Defects for Efficient   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   Solar Cells

Zhu Chen, Qian Wang, Yinyan Xu, Ru Zhou, Lun Zhang, Yang Huang, Linhua Hu, Mei Lyu,  $  ^{*}  $   and Jun Zhu  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Inorganic lead halide perovskite   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   possesses good stability with a suitable band gap for tandem solar cells. Decreasing the defect concentration and improving the film quality is crucial to further increase the power conversion efficiency of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   solar cells. Here, the crystallization dynamics of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   films is regulated by introducing the volatile organic salt, formamidinium acetate (FAAc) into the precursor solution. It is found that FAAc slows the crystallization process of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   films and pinhole-free films with large grains and smooth surfaces are obtained. The defect concentration of the films is decreased and the nonradiative recombination is significantly inhibited. By improving the film quality, the FAAc remarkably enhances the efficiency of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   solar cells. The champion device delivers a power conversion efficiency of 9.44% and exhibits higher stability than the reference device. This finding provides an effective strategy for reducing defects, suppressing the recombination, and improving the performance of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   solar cells.

KEYWORDS: perovskite solar cells, formamidinium acetate, film quality, stability

# INTRODUCTION

The organic–inorganic lead halide perovskite material possesses excellent optoelectronic properties, such as tunable band gap, large absorption coefficient, high carrier mobility, long carrier diffusion length, etc., and has received extensive attention in recent years.  $  ^{1-5}  $   At present, the best power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells has reached 25.5%.  $  ^{6}  $   However, the organic component in the organic–inorganic lead halide perovskite is very sensitive to moisture and heat, which will cause rapid degradation of the device performance.  $  ^{7-9}  $   Compared with the organic–inorganic perovskite, the all-inorganic perovskite   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   (X=Cl, Br, I) exhibits excellent stability against heat and moisture, and the band gap is more suitable for use in tandem solar cells. Therefore, all-inorganic perovskite materials have been developing rapidly.  $  ^{10-13}  $

Among numerous all-inorganic perovskite materials,   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   has a relatively narrow band gap (  $  \sim1.73~eV  $  ),  $  ^{14}  $   quite suitable for serving as a light absorber of single-junction perovskite solar cells. However, the photoactive   $  \alpha  $  -phase (black phase) is prone to a non-photoactive   $  \delta  $  -phase (yellow phase) at room temperature.  $  ^{15}  $   By contrast,   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   involves superior room-temperature stability, whereas its band gap (  $  \sim2.3~eV  $  ) is too wide to efficiently harvest the visible-infrared region of sunlight.  $  ^{16}  $   Fortunately,   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   has a reasonable band gap of   $  \sim2.05~eV  $   coupled with satisfactory stability.  $  ^{17-19}  $   For example, the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite films show excellent stability

Moreover, the Lewis base poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG) has also been employed as the additive to improve the film morphology and coverage of CsPbIBr  $  _{2}  $  .  $  ^{22,23}  $   The optimization of the molecular weight and dosage of PEG successfully decreases the defect states and improves the PCE and stability of devices. Although improved efficiency and stability have been reached for CsPbIBr  $  _{2}  $   solar cells, these methods either introduced additional doping ions or additives into the perovskite layer or added an extra fabrication procedure, making the preparation process more complicated.

Here, we report a facile strategy of introducing a volatile organic salt, formamidinium acetate (FAAc) into the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   precursor solution to improve the film quality. FAAc can effectively control the crystallization and growth process of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   films, thereby forming pinhole-free films with large grains and a low defect density. FAAc is totally sublimated during the annealing process of perovskite films. After the regulation using FAAc, the short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of the solar cells is increased from 8.99 to 11.65 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) is increased from 1.05 to 1.12 V, and the PCE is increased from 6.86 to 9.44%, among those high efficiencies reported for   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   solar cells. The results provide a facile strategy for reducing defects, suppressing the recombination, and improving the performance of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   solar cells.

# EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS

Materials. All materials and reagents are used as they are without any purification, including titanium tetrachloride (TiCl $ _{4} $ , 98%) from (Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd.), cesium iodide (CsI, 99.99%), lead bromide (PbBr $ _{2} $ , 99.99%), [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM, 99%), poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)-poly(styrene sulfonic acid ester) (PEDOT:PSS, 1.3–1.7 wt % solution in water) from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp., formamidinium acetate (FAAc, 99%) and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.7%) from Shanghai Bailingwei Chemical Technology Co., Ltd., and 2,2',7,7'-tetra(N,N-bis-p-methoxyaniline)-9,9'-spiro difluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD, 99.8%) from Ningbo Borun New Material Co., Ltd.

Fabrication of Solar Cells. The cleaned fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass was treated with ozone–UV for 10 min, placed in a 0.04 M TiCl  $  _{4}  $  aqueous solution, and kept in an oven at 70 °C for 30 min. The FTO conductive glass was rinsed with deionized water and dried under a nitrogen flow, and annealed on a heating stage at 200 °C for 60 min. PbBr  $  _{2}  $  (376 mg), 260 mg of CsI, x mg of FAAc (x = 0, 20, 30,

40, 50), and 1 mL of DMSO were mixed and stirred until the precursor solution was clear and transparent. The precursor solution was filtered with a 0.22   $  \mu  $  m filter head for use. The CsPbIBr  $  _{2}  $   precursor solution containing different concentrations of FAAc was spin-coated on the FTO/TiO  $  _{2}  $   substrate by a stepwise spin-coating method. The speed in the first step was set as 1000 rpm for 10 s; the speed in the second step was set as 3000 rpm for 50 s. Then, the film was put on the heating stage, and the temperature was kept at 30 °C for 10 min and then at 280 °C for 10 min. Spiro-OMeTAD (72.3 mg), 17.5   $  \mu  $  L of lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulfone)imide (Li-TFSI) in acetonitrile (520 mg/mL), and 28.8   $  \mu  $  L of 4-TBP were added to 1 mL of chlorobenzene and stirred, filtered with a 0.22   $  \mu  $  m filter, and spin-coated on the perovskite layer (4000 rpm, 30 s). The sample was then put into a vacuum chamber, and Au (60 nm) was deposited at a vacuum of about 1 × 10  $  ^{-4}  $   Pa as the back electrode of the solar cells. The active area of the device was 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  .

Characterization. Surface morphology of the perovskite film was obtained using a scanning electron microscope (ZEISS Gemini 500). The surface roughness of the perovskite film was acquired with an atomic force microscope (Bruker Dimension Icon). The crystal structure of the perovskite film was studied via an X-ray diffractometer (Malvern PANalytical X-Pert PRO MPD) with Cu Kα radiation. UV–visible spectra were obtained with a UV–vis spectrophotometer (Shimadzu UV2550). The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra were obtained with a photoluminescence spectrometer (Horiba PTI QM400). The time-resolved fluorescence spectra were obtained with a photoluminescence spectrometer (Edinburgh Instruments FLS1000). The chemical state of the perovskite film was acquired with an X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (Thermo Scientific ESCALAB250Xi). The J-V curves of the solar cells were tested under AM 1.5G illumination at 100 mW/cm² with a solar simulator (Newport Oriel Sol3A), which was calibrated with a standard silicon solar cell. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) measurements were obtained with an EQE measurement kit (Newport). The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were conducted on an electrochemical workstation (CHI660D, Shanghai Chenhua Instrument Co., Ltd.) under dark conditions. The frequency range was between 0.1 Hz and 1 MHz with an amplitude of 5 mV and a fixed bias of 0.8 V.

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

FAAc is a volatile organic salt.  $  ^{24}  $   It has been demonstrated to promote a robust   $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite interfacial contact through hydrogen bonding in   $  CsPb(I_{0.75}Br_{0.25})_{3}  $   solar cells.  $  ^{25}  $   Here, it is confirmed that the addition of FAAc to the precursor solution exhibits a significant effect on the crystallization kinetics of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   films (Figures 1 and S1). It can be seen from Figure 1 that the sample Cs-0 begins to change color in 2 min under 30 °C annealing and the color change is negligible after 50 min, indicating that the crystallization process is almost completed. The decrease of the surface roughness of the perovskite layer will improve the interface contact with the subsequent organic HTL, thereby promoting hole transfer and improving the device performance.

The optical properties of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   films were characterized by UV–vis absorption spectra and steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra. It can be seen from Figure 4a that the absorbance of the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   film is slightly enhanced after adding FAAc into the precursor solution and there is no distinct change in the absorption edge, indicating that the introduction of FAAc does not alter the band gap of perovskite materials. If the FA cation or Ac anion enters the crystal lattice, the lattice structure will change because their sizes are different from original   $  Cs^{+}  $  or halogen anions (  $  I^{-}  $  and   $  Br^{-}  $  ),  $  ^{25,26}  $   which in turn leads to the variation of the electronic structure and band gap. The space-charge-limited current (SCLC) model of a single charge carrier device was used to measure the trap state density of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   films without and with FAAc regulation.  $  ^{30}  $   We prepared single-electron and single-hole devices based on Cs-0 and Cs-40 films, respectively. As shown in Figure 7, in the low-voltage range, the current increases linearly with the voltage, the response of which is assigned to the ohmic contact. As the bias voltage increases, the current exhibits a nonlinear rapid increase, which is a sign of an excessive trap filling limit state;  $  ^{14,17}  $   in this state, all trap states will be filled with injected charges. The point connecting these two regions is denoted as the trap fill limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ). The defect density of

CsPbIBr  $  _{2}  $  films can be calculated according to the following formula

$$
n _ {\mathrm{trap}} = \frac {2 V _ {\mathrm{TFL} \varepsilon \varepsilon 0}}{e L ^ {2}}
$$

(1)

where   $  n_{trap}  $   is the defect state density,   $  V_{TFL}  $   is the limit voltage of defect filling,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative permittivity of the perovskite,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity, e is the element charge, and L is the thickness of the film. It can be known from the formula that the smaller the   $  V_{TFL}  $  , the smaller the defect state density. As shown, the   $  V_{TFL}  $   of the single-carrier device based on the Cs-40 film is lower than that of the Cs-0 film, indicating that there are fewer defects in perovskite films with FAAc regulation.

# CONCLUSIONS

By introducing the volatile organic salt FAAc into the precursor solution of inorganic perovskite  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $ , the crystallization dynamics is effectively regulated. The retarded film growth results in large-grain, pinhole-free, and smooth films. FAAc is completely removed during the subsequent annealing process and therefore the chemical nature of the inorganic perovskite film remains unchanged. By FAAc regulation, the defect density of the film is significantly reduced and the nonirradiation recombination is inhibited.

Moreover, the charge transfer from the perovskite layer to the charge transport layer is remarkably enhanced. Therefore, the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   solar cells exhibit a higher efficiency of 9.44 than 6.86% of the unregulated solar cells. This work proves that FAAc is an efficient additive to improve the film quality and device performance in   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   solar cells.
