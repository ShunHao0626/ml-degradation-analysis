# $  C_{60}  $   Additive-Assisted Crystallization in   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Pb_{0.75}Sn_{0.25}I_{3}  $   Perovskite Solar Cells with High Stability and Efficiency

Chong Liu  $  ^{a,\#}  $  , Wenzhe Li  $  ^{a,\#}  $  , Hongliang Li  $  ^{b}  $  , Cuiling Zhang  $  ^{b}  $  , Jiandong Fan  $  ^{*,a}  $  , Yaohua Mai  $  ^{*,a,b}  $

The development of hybrid tin (Sn)-lead (Pb) perovskite solar cells likely tackle the toxic problem with the power conversion efficiency (PCE) exceeding  $ 15.0\% $ . However, the stability problems, e.g. hysteresis effect, degeneration and oxidation, appear to be the bottleneck that limit its further development. Here, we innovatively introduced the  $ C_{60} $  at the grain boundaries throughout the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{Pb}_{0.75}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.25}\mathrm{I}_3 $  ( $ \mathrm{MAPb}_{0.75}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.25}\mathrm{I}_3 $ ) thin film, which played a role not only in in-situ passivating the interfaces and reducing the pinholes of perovskite thin films, but also in preventing the penetration of moisture and oxygen from ambient atmosphere. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) illustrated that the recombination life time of both the bulk and surface of  $ \mathrm{MAPb}_{0.75}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.25}\mathrm{I}_3 $  thin films were increased by  $ C_{60} $  additive incorporation. Dark I-V results for the electron/hole-only devices displayed that the charge trap-states density decreased with  $ C_{60} $  additive involved inside the hybrid Sn-Pb perovskite thin films. Importantly, the modified hybrid Sn-Pb perovskite solar cells by  $ C_{60} $  additive were demonstrated to own superior stability and efficiency when exposed to the ambient environment without encapsulation.

# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSC) have achieved significant progress as their appealing advantages of intense broad-band absorption, higher charge carrier mobility and longer charge diffusion length.  $  ^{1}  $   Their power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) have increased rapidly from 3.8 % to over 22 % within seven years.  $  ^{2-6}  $   Unfortunately, almost all of the perovskite solar cells with high efficiency were based on the traditional pure lead halide perovskite materials so far.  $  ^{7}  $   To speed up their commercialization, the intrinsic toxicity and stability should be paid more attention aside from the efficiency.  $  ^{8,9}  $   Recently, the researchers have focused on the   $  (\mathrm{MA}/\mathrm{FA})\mathrm{Pb}_{(1-x)}\mathrm{Sn}_{x}\mathrm{l}_{3}  $   (x≤1) PSCs with significantly improved PCEs. For x<0.5, Jen et al. reported a combined compositional process, and interfacial engineering to develop highly efficient and stable   $  MA_{0.5}FA_{0.5}Pb_{0.75}Sn_{0.25}\mathrm{l}_{3}  $   PSCs to realize a PCE up to 14.19%.  $  ^{10}  $   Choy et al. demonstrated high-quality preferential orientation   $  MASn_{0.25}Pb_{0.75}\mathrm{l}_{3}  $   thin films and gained the PCE of 15.2% via using   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (3:1 by weight) as the electron transport layer.  $  ^{11}  $   For x=0.5, Huang et al. reported a Pb-assisted two-step spin coating method to construct   $  MASn_{0.5}Pb_{0.5}\mathrm{l}_{3}  $   PSCs and showed a PCE up to 13.6%.  $  ^{12}  $   Snaith et al. demonstrated cesium-contained

$  FA_{0.75}Cs_{0.25}Sn_{0.5}Pb_{0.5}I_{3}  $  thin film, and delivered 14.8% efficiency. $ ^{13} $  For x>0.5, Sadhanala et al. demonstrated a novel elevated temperature processing method to prepare uniform Sn–Pb based low-bandgap perovskite thin films, and achieved over 10% with  $  MAPb_{0.4}Sn_{0.6}I_{3}  $  PSCs. $ ^{14} $  Yan et al. developed a new precursor combining FASnI3 with MAPbI3 and the as-prepared  $  (FASnI_{3})_{0.6}(MAPbI_{3})_{0.4}  $  allowed to yield the PCE of 15.08%. $ ^{15} $  Soon afterward, they optimized the conditions and finally achieved the highest PCE of 17.6%. $ ^{16} $  For x=1, Yan et al. demonstrated the highest PCE over 6.22 % for Pb-free planar  $  FASnI_{3}  $  PSC with a simple inverted device architecture. $ ^{17} $

Considering the rapid crystallization of Sn-based perovskite films, in our previous report, we have introduced a solvent engineering, which allowed forming the   $  PbI_{2}/SnI_{2}(DMSO)_{x}  $   complexes precursor, followed by molecular exchange to obtain high quality   $  MAPb_{1-x}Sn_{x}I_{3}  $   perovskite thin-films. The optimized   $  MAPb_{0.75}Sn_{0.25}I_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells with inverted structure were consequently realized with maximum power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 14.12%.  $  ^{18}  $   However, there still have some problems to be solved: (i) The average PCE of   $  MAPb_{0.75}Sn_{0.25}I_{3}  $   perovskite solar cell is about 12.6%, which is quite lower in comparison to the champion PCE of 14.12%. (ii) The hybrid Sn-Pb perovskite solar cells still have larger hysteresis effect even with inverted structure. (iii) Inferior stability when the hybrid Sn-Pb devices are exposed to ambient environment.

Similarly, C  $  _{60}  $  was demonstrated to have the advantages of passivating the perovskite thin film surfaces and facilitating electron transport with reducing leakage loss due to its wide band gap, high mobility, and low charge transport resistance.  $  ^{28}  $  Thus, it is widely used as the electron transport layer in organic solar cells and perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{28-30}  $  Likewise, C  $  _{60}  $  has much higher electron mobility (1.6 cm V s  $  ^{-1}  $ ) and conductivity (2.3 × 10  $  ^{-3}  $  S cm  $  ^{-1}  $ ) in comparison to PCBM with the values of electron mobility (6.1 × 10  $  ^{-2}  $  cm V s  $  ^{-1}  $ ) and conductivity (3.2 × 10  $  ^{-4}  $  S cm  $  ^{-1}  $ ), respectively.  $  ^{31}  $  In this scenario, it may have more theoretical advantages to facilitate intermolecular charge transport if C  $  _{60}  $  is distributed at perovskite grain boundaries throughout the film.

Here, we introduced the  $ C_{60} $  additive to hybrid Sn-Pb perovskite films with an aim of in-situ passivating the grain boundary and prevent the oxygen and moisture from ambient atmosphere, which was favorable to enhance the stability and alleviate the hysteresis of  $ \mathrm{MAPb}_{0.75}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.25}\mathrm{I}_3 $  perovskite solar cells. In this work,  $ C_{60} $  is used as additive to the hybrid Sn-Pb precursor solution to guarantee the uniform doping in molecular dimensions and distribution at the perovskite grain boundary after thin film fabricated. By optimizing the doping concentration of  $ C_{60} $  in the precursor solution, the PCE as high as  $ 13.9\% $  of  $ \mathrm{MAPb}_{0.75}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.25}\mathrm{I}_3 $  perovskite solar with highly alleviated hysteresis effects cells can be obtained and only  $ 20\% $  PCE decay was observed when the corresponding solar cell without encapsulation exposed in air for 7 days.

# Experimental

# Materials and methods

The FTO-coated glass (8 Ω cm $  ^{-2}  $ , Beneq TFS 200) was sequentially cleaned with deionized water, acetone and alcohol in an ultrasonic cleaning machine. After drying in a N $  _{2}  $  stream, the substrates were further treated under oxygen plasma for 10 min. PEDOT:PSS (Heraeus-Clevios P 4083, Xi'an p-OLED) was spun on the as-treated substrate at a speed of 3,500 rounds per minute (r.p.m.) after diluted with water at volume ratio 1:1. The substrates were then annealed at 140°C for 10 min. 170 mg of PbI $  _{2}  $  (Alfa Aesar, 99.9985%) and 46 mg SnI $  _{2}  $  (Aldrich, 99.99%) were dissolved in a mixed solvent containing 256 μL of DMF (Aldrich, 99.9%) and 64 μL of DMSO (Aldrich, 99.9%). After completely dissolved, 1.5 mg C $  _{60}  $  was added into the precursor solution, and then filtered. The mixed precursor solution was spun on the PEDOT:PSS layer at 5000 (r.p.m.) for 30 s. Then the MAI solution with the concentration of 60 mg ml $  ^{-1}  $  was spun on the substrate at 5000 (r.p.m.) for 30 s. Afterward, the obtained thin films were annealed at 110°C for 20 min. The 6,6-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) dissolved into dichlorobenzene with the concentration of 20 mg ml $  ^{-1}  $  was spun on the top of the as-prepared perovskite layers at 2500 (r.p.m.) for 30 s. Finally, 6 nm 2,9-dimethyl-4,7-diphenyl-1,10-Phenanthroline (BCP) and 120 nm Ag electrode were sequentially deposited by thermal evaporation. Note that all of the film fabrications were processed in nitrogen-filled glovebox.

# Characterization

The crystal structure was characterized by Bruker D8 Advance X-ray diffractometer (XRD) with CuKα radiation at 40 kV and 40 mA. Field-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was used to characterize the morphology of the obtained thin film. Both top-down and cross-sectional views were obtained using a FEI NovaNanoSEM450. A double beam spectrophotometer (Lambda 950, PerkinElmer) equipped with an integrated sphere was used for the UV-vis absorption measurements in the range from 300 to 1100 nm. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was measured with a PHI 5300 ESCA Perkin-Elmer spectrometer. All spectra were shifted to account for sample charging using inorganic carbon at 284.80 eV as a reference. UPS were analyzed on Thermo Scientific ESCALab 250Xi. Gas discharge lamp was used for UPS, with helium gas admitted and the Hel (21.22eV) emission line employed. The data were acquired with -10V bias. The electrochemical impedance spectra were measured by electrochemical workstation (ZAHNER CIMPS, Germany). The fluorescence lifetime imaging microscopy was measured by a FV1200 laser scanning confocal microscopy. A 514 nm pulsed diode laser was used for excitation with repetition rates at 40 MHz.

Current-voltage (J-V) characteristics of perovskite solar cells were measured using a semiconductor device analyzer (Keithley 2601B) and a SAN-EI solar simulator (XES-100S1) with an AM 1.5 G spectrum. The illumination power on the sample was adjusted to  $ 1000\mathrm{Wm}^{-2} $  using a certified reference solar cell (RS-ID-4). # Results and discussion

Due to the unique structure of spherical symmetric nonpolar molecule,   $  C_{60}  $   is able to combine with some solvent molecules to form a charge transfer complex. According to the like-dissolves-like rule, the formation of charge transfer complex could enable the   $  C_{60}  $   to disperse in some organic solvents at a low solubility.  $  ^{32}  $   The left photo of Fig. S1a exhibits the transparent DMSO:DMF mixed solvent with the volume ratio of 1:5. The right one is the saturated solution of   $  C_{60}  $   in DMSO:DMF mixed solvent with slightly brown color. To prepare the saturated solution, excess   $  C_{60}  $   (5 mg) was added into 1 mL DMSO:DMF mixed solvent, after shacked at 25°C for 10 min, the supernatant was extracted. Similarly, Fig. S1b exhibits the pristine precursor (left) and   $  C_{60}  $   introduced precursor (right) with deeper color. We used the spectrophotometer to determine the concentration of   $  C_{60}  $   saturated solution in the precursor solution, with the known concentration of   $  C_{60}  $   dissolved in 1,2-dichlorobenzene (5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) as the standard solution. Fig. S2 shows the corresponding absorption spectrum of these two solutions. The absorbance value of the standard solution and the   $  C_{60}  $   saturated solution are 3.5 and 0.07 at the wavelength of 600 nm, respectively. According to the formula A=εlc, we calculated that the concentration of   $  C_{60}  $   saturated solution was 0.1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , a relatively smaller concentration compared to the PbI  $  _{2}  $  /SnI  $  _{2}  $   precursor.

In our previous reports, it was demonstrated that the   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  SnI_{2}  $   complexed with the DMSO molecules, which allowed to form the   $  PbI_{2}/SnI_{2}(DMSO)_{x}  $   complex  $  ^{18,33}  $  . Similarly, in the present study, the   $  C_{60}  $   was dissolved into the precursor solution, in which the   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  SnI_{2}  $   complexed with the DMSO molecules. As shown in the schematic 1, due to the certain order of magnitude of binding energy (839 kJ m  $  ^{-3}  $  ) between the   $  C_{60}  $   and DMSO molecules, the   $  C_{60}  $   was wrapped around by the DMSO molecules and formed a solvent shell  $  ^{34}  $  , as well as mixing together with the   $  PbI_{2}/SnI_{2}(DMSO)_{x}  $   complex. Afterward, the molecule exchange process between DMSO and MAI was followed while the MAI solution was spin coated. Finally, the   $  MAPb_{1-x}Sn_{x}I_{3}  $   perovskite thin films were consequently obtained by annealing process, with the evaporation of solvent molecules and the   $  C_{60}  $   at the grain boundary of perovskites. It's worth noting that the presence of binding energy between   $  C_{60}  $   and DMSO molecules are expected to tune the crystallization process of perovskites, which gives rise to the formation of perovskite thin film with decreased pinholes and uniformed distribution of   $  C_{60}  $   at the gain boundary. The hybrid Sn-Pb perovskite solar cells tent to be of importance to overcome the toxic issue due to its competitive efficiency in comparison to the pure lead perovskite solar cells. However, the worse chemistry stability is still a restricted factor with respect to the moisture and oxygen, which are thought to be the main decompose factors in the hybrid Sn-Pb perovskite solar cells when exposed to the ambient atmosphere. Particularly, the reasons are included as follows: (i) the inherent bibulous and diffluent characters for the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite absorb layer. (ii) the hole transport layer (HTL) PEDOT:PSS show a strong acid after absorbed water.  $  ^{45}  $   (iii) the existence form of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   is easy oxidized to   $  Sn^{4+}  $   when contact with oxygen.  $  ^{19}  $   Thus, it is necessary to prevent the damage of moisture and oxygen toward a high efficiency and stability hybrid Sn-Pb perovskite solar cells. Normalized cell parameters to the values obtained after device preparation (0 day). Values represented are average values of 6 devices and error bars indicates the standard deviation. Other parameters (  $  J_{sc}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $  , FF) evolution are shown in Fig. 5b, 5c and 5d, respectively. The results indicated that the main cause of the stability enhancement of PCE using   $  C_{60}  $   additive were attributed to the steady of   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF, which closely related to the charge extraction and transmission through the bulk and interface of perovskite thin films.

In order to elucidate the mechanism in improvement of chemical stability after adding  $  C_{60}  $ , we introduce a scheme of inverted planar hybrid Sn-Pb perovskite solar cell, as shown in Fig. 6a. The  $  C_{60}  $  is homogeneously distributed at grain boundaries throughout the perovskite layer with a few

molecules thick. It is well accepted that the   $  C_{60}  $   has a low solubility in water (  $  <10^{-9} mg L^{-1}  $  )  $  ^{46}  $  , and almost no oxidation at room temperature.  $  ^{47}  $   Thus, the   $  C_{60}  $   distributed at grain boundaries throughout the perovskite layer as an isolating layer that is effectively against moisture and oxygen in air. Fig. 6b shows the water contact angle increase from   $  94.8^{\circ}  $   to   $  103.5^{\circ}  $   after the introduction of   $  C_{60}  $  . As we all know, the organic-inorganic perovskite materials are hydroscopic, insoluble in water. In this regard, the introduction of   $  C_{60}  $   distributed at the grain boundaries has the function as a raincoat to prevent it from moisture permeation. The degradation of perovskite caused by moisture has been widely studied that the main final products of the decomposition are   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  I_{2}  $  , which would cause the peak of   $  PbI_{2}  $   gradually increased.  $  ^{24}  $   Fig. 6c shows the X-ray diffraction patterns of these two films after exposed to ambient atmosphere for five days. The X-ray diffraction patterns of these two films after prepared are shown in Fig. S6. Obviously, the   $  PbI_{2}  $   peak have increased for the control one, and remained unchanged for the   $  C_{60}  $   additive one after exposed to air. That could be explained that   $  C_{60}  $   additive could separate perovskite grain from moisture. Besides, the XPS spectrums of these two films after exposed to air for five days were shown in Fig. 6d. The peak assigned to Sn element was fitted into two main peaks, the corresponding peaks of 486.3 eV and 487.3 eV were associated with the   $  Sn^{2+}  $   and   $  Sn^{4+}  $  , respectively. The ratio of   $  Sn^{4+}  $   to   $  Sn^{2+}  $   was reduced from 1.33 to 1.25 after   $  C_{60}  $   additive, indicating that   $  C_{60}  $   additive could also prevent perovskite grain from oxidation. Although the encapsulation technology can work to protect the solar cell device from ambient environment, the in-situ modification by   $  C_{60}  $   additive can not only protect perovskite (especially the hybrid Sn-Pb perovskite) from degradation, but also improve the bulk and interface recombination life time which are favorable to effectively enhance the efficiency and reduce the hysteresis effect.

In this work, we employed a slight  $  C_{60}  $  doping technique to prepare MAPb $ _{0.75} $ Sn $ _{0.25} $  $  ^{13}  $  hybrid Sn-Pb perovskite films. The presence of binding energy between  $  C_{60}  $  and DMSO molecules are expected to tune the crystallization process of perovskites, which gives rise to the formation of perovskite thin film with decreased pinholes and uniformed distribution of  $  C_{60}  $  at the gain boundary. The perovskite- $  C_{60}  $  hybrid solar cells demonstrated higher efficiency, less hysteresis effects and higher long-term stability. The excellent performances are mainly associated with the  $  C_{60}  $  interfacial passivation effects which increased the bulk and surface recombination life time and decreased the charge trap-state density. Besides, the improved stability of the hybrid Sn-Pb perovskite was also ascribed to the prevention of moisture and oxygen from ambient atmosphere by  $  C_{60}  $  additive. This work provides a facile approach to prepare high efficiency, stable hybrid Sn-Pb perovskite solar cells toward commercial applications.
