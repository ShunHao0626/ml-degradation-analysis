# Polyethylene Glycol Dodecyl Ether as Interfacial Modifier for Improving Efficiency of Perovskite Solar Cells

Xia Chen, Juanmei Liu, Jihuai Wu,* Guodong Li, Qi Chen, Fangfang Cai, Sijia Zhu, Jingxu Tian, Weihai Sun, and Zhang Lan

The rapid development of the perovskite solar cell (PSC) makes its best research efficiency comparable with that of silicon solar cells. However, the large-scale application of PSCs still faces the dual challenges of improving efficiency and stability. Herein, an interfacial modifier of polyethylene glycol dodecyl ether (Brij) with ether group (C–O–C) and long alkyl chain is introduced into the interface of the perovskite layer and hole transport layer. It is demonstrated that the interaction of the ether group on Brij with uncoordinated lead ions on the perovskite passivates the point defects and reduces carrier recombination. The introduction of Brij tunes the energy gradient among electron transport layer/perovskite layer/hole transport layer and promotes the carrier extraction and transportation. The long alkyl chain of Brij covers the surface of perovskite and effectively improves the moisture resistance of perovskite films and the environmental stability of devices. Under an optimal Brij concentration, the modified perovskite solar cell achieves the best power conversion efficiency of 22.57%, along with improved environmental stability, while the pristine device obtains an efficiency of 20.66%.

The molecular structure of Brij is shown in Figure S1, Supporting Information. On Brij film and Brij-modified perovskite film (Figure 1a), there is a characteristic peak of stretching vibration of O–H bond at   $  3614 \, cm^{-1}  $  , and a stretching vibration peak of   $  -CH_{2}-  $   at   $  2921 \, cm^{-1}  $  , which can prove the existence of Brij on the perovskite surface. For Brij films, there is a stretching vibration peak of C–O at   $  1117 \, cm^{-1}  $  , while in Brij-modified perovskite films, this peak moves to   $  1113 \, cm^{-1}  $   (Figure 1b), which indicates that there is an interaction between Brij C–O bond and perovskite, which may be due to the coordination of Brij with Pb in perovskite or the formation of hydrogen bond between Brij and perovskite.  $  ^{[18]}  $

In addition, X-ray photo-electron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra were characterized to determine the existence of Brij on perovskite and the interaction between Brij and perovskite. Figure 1c shows the XPS spectrum of Pb 4f. Pb 4f peak of the perovskite film modified by Brij moves toward lower binding energy, which is due to the increase of electron cloud density of Pb as a result of the coordination between lone-pair electrons on O in Brij and Pb in perovskite. Besides, the binding energy of I 3d decreased (Figure 1d), because the binding of Brij to Pb weakens the interaction between Pb and I.  $  ^{[20]}  $   Figure 1e shows the XPS spectrum of C 1s. The peak of C–O can be seen in the modified perovskite film.  $  ^{[21]}  $   which also proves the existence of Brij in the perovskite film.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we demonstrate a novel strategy for improving the performance and stability of perovskite solar cells using multifunctional interface modifiers of polyethylene glycol dodecyl ether (Brij) with ether group (C–O–C) and long alkyl chain. Through Lewis acid–base coordination between C–O–C group on Brij and   $  Pb^{2+}  $   on perovskite, the surface and interfacial defects of perovskite are effectively passivated, and the trap-assisted carrier nonradiative recombination is inhibited. On the other hand, the modification of Brij tunes the energy gradient among electron transport layer/perovskite layer/HTL and promotes the carrier extraction and transportation. In addition, the hydrophobicity of the Brij greatly increases the water contact angle of the modified perovskite film and improves the environmental stability of PSCs. Consequently, under the optimal Brij concentration of   $  0.5 mg mL^{-1}  $  , the modified perovskite solar cell achieves a power conversion efficiency of 22.57% with reduced hysteresis effect, while the pristine device obtains an efficiency of 20.66%. Under 40–50% RH condition, the Brij-modified device retained 84.2% of its initial efficiency after 32 days, while the pristine device dropped to 67.6%.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials:   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (MAI, 99.5%, Xi'an p-OLED), N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.9%, Sigma-Aldrich), Li-TFSI (99%, Sigma-Aldrich),   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Br  $   (MABr, 99.5%, Xi'an p-OLED), 2-propanol (IPA, 99.5%, Sigma-Aldrich), 4-tert-butyl pyridine (TBP, 98%, Sigma-Aldrich), chlorobenzene (CB, 99.8%, Sigma-Aldrich), acetonitrile (ACE, 99.9%, Sigma-Aldrich), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.9%, Sigma-Aldrich),   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Cl  $   (MACl, 99.5%, Xi'an p-OLED),   $  PbI_{2}  $   (99.99%, Advanced Election Technology Co., Ltd.),   $  HC(NH_{2})_{2}^{+1-}  $   (FAI, 99.9%, Advanced Election Technology Co., Ltd.), and spiro-OMeTAD (99.99%, Shenzhen Feiming Science and Technology Co., Ltd.) were used without further operation. Indium tin oxide (ITO, 14 Ω sq  $  ^{-1}  $  ) was purchased from Advanced Election Technology Co., Ltd. Polyethylene glycol dodecyl ether (Brij) was obtained from Aladdin.

Device Fabrication: In air,   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer was deposited onto the cleaned ITO glasses at 3500 rpm for 25 s and heated at 180°C for 30 min. Perovskite films with compositions of   $  FA_{0.92}MA_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $   were prepared by a two-step method. The   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution was processed by dissolving 1.50 M   $  PbI_{2}  $   in a mixed solvent with a volume ratio of DMF and

DMSO, 9:1. The 20 mL PbI  $  _{2}  $  solution was deposited onto ITO/SnO  $  _{2}  $  substrates at 1500 rpm for 30 s, and postannealing treatment was done at 70°C for 60 s. Organic amine salt prepared by dissolving 0.50 M FAI, 0.05 M MAI, and 0.15 M MACI in IPA was dropped on the PbI  $  _{2}  $  layer at 2000 rpm for 30 s and then heated at 150°C for 20 min. The CB solution of Brij was poured on the surface of the perovskite layer at 4000 rpm for 20 s and subsequently heated at 70°C for 10 min. The solution of HTL was produced with spiro-OMeTAD, TBP, and Li-TFSI based on the reported method.  $  ^{[40]}  $  The HTL was deposited at 3500 rpm for 30 s, subsequently being oxidated in the dryer for 24 h. Under high vacuum, Au cathodes (  $  \approx  $  80 nm) were deposited on the HTL via thermal evaporation. Thus, perovskite solar cells with active area of 0.060 cm  $  ^{2}  $  were fabricated.

Characterizations: The morphologies of perovskite films and the lateral structure of PSCs were obtained from field emission scanning electron microscopy (Hitachi SU8010). The XPS/UPS spectrum was collected by a Thermo Fisher Scientific K-Alpha+ scientific system with an energy of 1486.68 eV. The UV–vis absorption spectra of films were measured by a PerkinElmer Lambda 950UV/VIS/NIR spectrometer. Steady-state PL spectroscopy was collected by LF-1 303 003 (Thermo scientific). The roughness of the films surface was provided by AFM (Multimode-8J). The spectra of stable photocurrent output were examined by CHI660E electrochemical workstation (Chenhua Co. Ltd). Contact angles were conducted on Data physics OCA-20. Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) spectra were obtained from the Nicolet 6700 (USA). Under 1 sun illumination (AM 1.5G, 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ), the J-V curves were obtained from a Keithley 2420 Source Meter of an Oriel Sol 3A solar simulator. TRPL spectra were recorded on Leica TCS SP8. EIS,M–S, and capacitance voltage (C–V) were conducted in the electrochemical workstation. All characterizations were conducted without encapsulation in air condition.
