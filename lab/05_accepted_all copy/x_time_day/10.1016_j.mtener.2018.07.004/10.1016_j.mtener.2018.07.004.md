# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted attention because of their proper bandgap   $  [1,2]  $  , high charge carrier mobility   $  [3,4]  $   and low cost of the solution processing   $  [5,6]  $  . The performance has achieved a meteoric rise, up to 22.1% in traditional structure based on mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $     $  [7]  $  . Comparatively, the inverted structures usually present a slightly low PCE, in which the absence of mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer is time-saving and low-temperature solution method could be applied to prepare perovskite devices. It would not only decrease the complexity of the preparation process but also widen the range of potential substrates such as ITO and flexible electrode   $  [8,9]  $  . As for the inverted planar architecture, although poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):polystyrenesulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) is widely used as hole transport material (HTM)   $  [10–13]  $  , PEDOT:PSS is not regarded as a perfect hole transport material due to its inefficient electron-blocking capability and poor chemical stability.

# ABSTRACT

In perovskite solar cells, inorganic hole transport materials have attracted intense attention because of the properties of high chemical stability, hole mobility and low cost. However, most of the inorganic hole conductors exhibit low performance compared with organic hole transport materials. In this study, a facile solution method was developed to prepare a  $ \mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{O}_5 $  film combined with P3CT-K thus forming a bilayer structure as hole transport layers in inverted  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  solar cells. It was found that the hole transport bilayer not only increased the rate of extraction charge but also reduced charge recombination compared with the P3CT-K only based device. As a result, the power conversion efficiency of inverted  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  solar cells based on the bilayer was boosted up to the optimal value of  $ 19.7\% $  (average value:  $ 19.0\% $ ). Furthermore, the device maintained an efficiency of more than  $ 86\% $  through one-month follow-up, in which the dense and hydrophobic  $ \mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{O}_5 $  film accounted for the improved device stability.

As early as in 2015, Fang group applied a type of water soluble material of poly[3-(4-carboxylbutyl)thiophene-Na (P3CT-Na) as HTMs in inverted PSC with a PCE of  $ 16.6\% $ , which was more than  $ 20\% $  improvement compared with those based on the PEDOT:PSS [15]. $$
- d V / d J = \mathrm{Ak} _ {\mathrm{B}} \mathrm{T} / \mathrm{e} (J _ {\mathrm{sc}} - J) ^ {- 1} + R _ {s},
$$

(2)

To further understand the influence of the  $ \mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{O}_5 $  film on the devices, electronic impedance spectra (EIS) measurements for the devices with or without  $ \mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{O}_5 $  film were performed. In the Nyquist plots, the charge transfer resistance  $ (R_{\mathrm{tr}}) $  is equal to the value of the high-frequency. Fig. 4c shows that the photovoltaic devices based

on   $  V_{2}O_{5}  $   film has much lower   $  R_{tr}  $  . In addition, recombination resistance (  $  R_{r}  $  ) values are ascribed to the low-frequency one in EIS. Larger   $  R_{r}  $   means a lower leakage current and electron recombination occurred at the surface. As can be seen, the   $  R_{r}  $   values of the devices based on bilayer structured HTL significantly increased compared with the devices based on pure P3CT-K HTL, which means that bilayer structured HTL lead to the reduction of current leakage and electron recombination occurred at the interface. Therefore,   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF were enhanced tremendously after using bilayer structured HTL. The variation trend is consistent with previous conclusion mentioned in J-V characteristics. The electron lifetime for recombination (  $  \tau_{r}  $  ) in the photoanode was calculated by the equation of   $  \tau_{r} = 1/(2\pi f_{p})  $  , in which   $  f_{p}  $   represents the frequency of the intermediate-frequency semicircle that can be obtained from bode plot (Fig. 4d). The   $  \tau_{r}  $   value for the device based on bilayer structured HTL (  $  5.4 \times 10^{-3}  $   s) is larger than that of the devices based on pure P3CT-K HTL (  $  3.9 \times 10^{-3}  $   s). The larger   $  \tau_{r}  $   value could decrease the electron recombination, resulting in an improved devices performance.

Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) was applied to examine the charge transfer between the absorber layer and the HTLs. A strong PL quenching provides evidence for the significant charge transfer from the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  layer to the HTLs. The quenching of the PL emission by the P3CT-K layers can be observed in Fig. 4e. Particularly, the PL quenching can be further enhanced after using  $ \mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{O}_5 $  to form  $ \mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{O}_5 / \mathrm{P3CT - K} $  HTLs. In order to further investigate the effect of  $ \mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{O}_5 $  film on charge transfer dynamic, time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements were employed to explore the lifetime of the carriers in  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ . As shown in Fig. 4f, the curves were fitted by a biexponential function according to the computational formula:  $ f(t) = A_{1}\exp (-t / \tau_{1}) + A_{2}\exp (-t / \tau_{2}) $  [39,40].  $ A_{1} $  and  $ A_{2} $  represent the decay amplitude,  $ t $  is the time,  $ \tau_{1} $  is the decay time caused by the intrinsic excitation relaxation and  $ \tau_{2} $  is the decay time caused by the non-geminate/free carrier recombination [41]. Meanwhile, the average decay time can be calculated by the computational formula:  $ \tau_{\mathrm{ave}} = (A_1\tau_1^2 +A_2\tau_2^2) / (A_1\tau_1 + A_2\tau_2) $  and various parameters were listed in Table S2. As a consequence, we can obtain that the lifetime values of ITO/P3CT-K/CH₃NH₃PbI₃, and ITO/V₂O₅/P3CT-K/CH₃NH₃PbI₃ film are 39.58 and 16.67 ns, respectively, whereas that of the ITO/CH₃NH₃PbI₃ film is 91.66 ns, indicating a positive effect on charge carrier extraction from CH₃NH₃PbI₃ active layer to HTLs by  $ \mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{O}_5 $  film.

We also tested the stability in the glove with complete devices without encapsulation. # 3. Conclusion

In this work, a facile solution method was used to prepare  $ \mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{O}_5 $  film with high quality combined with P3CT-K to form a bilayer structured HTL in the inverted  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  solar cell. Compared with the P3CT-K based devices, the device based on bilayer HTL exhibited a higher PCE of  $ 19.7\% $ . AFM and EIS measurements indicated that the device possessed increased smoothness and recombination resistance than the P3CT-K based device, leading to an increment in the output  $ J_{sc} $  and FF. Steady-state PL and TRPL measurements showed that  $ \mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{O}_5 $  film can effectively improve the

charge transfer from the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   active layer to the interfacial layer. Meanwhile, the stability of the devices was improved significantly and an efficiency of more than 86% was maintained even after one month, which cannot be readily achieved with P3CT-K interfacial materials. The inorganic material   $  V_{2}O_{5}  $   currently used, which can be processed at low temperatures, exhibits a great potential in the fabrication of other related semiconductors and optoelectronics for improving the device performances.

# 4.2. Device fabrication

ITO glass (CSG Holding Co., Ltd., 24 U·sq  $  ^{-1}  $  ) substrates were cleaned in an ultrasonic bath with detergent, deionized water, acetone, and isopropyl alcohol for 20 min followed by drying with N  $  _{2}  $  . After treating the substrates with plasma for 10 min, the V  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $   film was deposited by spin-coating the precursor solution at 4000 rpm for 60 s. The layers thus obtained were subsequently annealed at 200 °C for 60 min P3CT-K solution was synthesized through the reaction of poly[3-(4-carboxylbutyl)thiophene (P3CT, Mw: 30–40 k, Rieke Metals) with sodium hydroxide (KOH, molar ratio 1:1) in H  $  _{2}  $  O at 60 °C under stirring. After treating the substrates with plasma for 5 min, a 30 nm thick layer of P3CT-K was spin coated on it. And then the resulting layers were subsequently annealed at 140 °C for 10 min and transferred to a glove box. The CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $   precursor solution was prepared by mixing CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  I with PbI  $  _{2}  $   at a molar ratio of 1:1 in a mixing solution of N-dimethylformamide (DMF) and DMSO (v:v = 4:1) (43 wt% solution) at 60 °C, stirring for 12 h inside a nitrogen-filled glove box with

oxygen and moisture levels <0.1 ppm. Perovskite films were deposited by spin-coating the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   precursor at 5000 rpm for 30 s on a HTL-coated ITO glass substrate. Chlorobenzene (CB) solution (400 iL) was added to   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   with a delay time of ~15 s during the spin-coating process. The obtained films were then dried at 80 °C for 10 min. Later, 20 mg/mL   $  PC_{61}BM  $   was spin coated on the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film. And then 12 mg/mL ZnO nanoparticles solution was spin coated on the   $  PC_{61}BM  $   film. Finally Al (~100 nm) was thermally deposited on the substrates inside the vacuum chamber. The active area of the device was measured to be 0.060 cm².
