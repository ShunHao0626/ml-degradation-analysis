Cesium-based inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs), especially for the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based solar cells, have attained extensive attention due to its excellent thermal and phase stability. Solution-prepared   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films usually have some defects which demand to be passivated for PSCs and also their degradations often happen at the grain boundaries (GBs). Herein, by using polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) solution, we optimize the perovskite film growth and further investigate the effect of PMMA treatment on the properties of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films. Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) displays that the PMMA coating can improve the photoluminescence lifetime of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films. Therefore, it is proved that PMMA coating film can effectively passivate the defects of the pure   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films, suppress the charge recombination with increased electron extraction efficiency, which improves the performance of solar cells. Consequently, the devices based on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films with PMMA solution treatment exhibit average power conversion efficiency (PCE) over 11.18%, which is much higher than the efficiency of 9.69% for pure   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based solar cells.

# 1. Introduction

Hybrid organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attained more extensive attention in the past few years, and their efficiencies have skyrocketed from 3.8% to 25.2% within a relatively development time [1–5]. However, the device instability is major problem for further commercialization of PSCs [6–10]. At present, the best way to improve their stability is to substitute the volatile organic component (MA $  ^{+}  $  and FA $  ^{+}  $ ) with an inorganic component such as Cs [11–14]. Among the inorganic perovskite CsPbX $  _{3}  $  (X = Cl, Br, I), CsPbI $  _{3}  $  owes a suitable energy band gap (E $  _{g}  $ ) of 1.73 eV, which is also beneficial to configure a tandem device. Unfortunately, black CsPbI $  _{3}  $  with a poor stability and easily converts to its yellow δ-phase in the ambient atmosphere [15–19]. Meanwhile, CsPbBr $  _{3}  $  with better stability but has a too large band gap (E $  _{g}  $  = 2.3 eV) to absorb solar light beyond 540 nm, which limits the light-harvesting ability and the current density of PSCs [20–23]. Bandgap-tunable CsPbI $  _{3-x}  $ Br $  _{x}  $  perovskite is expected to have

high phase stability and low phase transition temperature [24–28]. Among these,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite with a band gap of   $  \sim1.9~eV  $  , is suitable for solar cell applications and accordingly has been concerned by many researchers [29–34]. Moreover, the theoretical efficiency of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs is predicted to be up to 22.1% [35], and therefore the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   material is a promising candidate for solar cell applications. For example, Lau et al. prepared a   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cell by spray-assisted deposition in the atmosphere and obtained a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 6.3% [36]. Nam et al. reported the formation of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films under optimal annealing temperature, and a PCE of 10.7% was achieved [37]. Up to now,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ -based PSCs have achieved 14.8% efficiency and exhibit excellent stability in the air [38].

Up to now, most of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films are prepared by spin-coating method [39,40]. It is hard to grow a high-quality and uniform   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film due to its crystallization process is relatively slow. These films are composed of many small crystal domains and grain boundaries (GBs), where photocarriers usually recombine [41,42].

Besides that, it also reported that the decomposition of these films always begins at the GBs [43]. To further enhance the performance of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, a great deal of research have been done to optimize the film surface or interface and remove the trap states [44–46]. For instance, Park et al. reported that the   $  K^{+}  $   ions passivation can enhance the performance of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs, and the PCE can reach up to 10.0% for   $  Cs_{0.925}K_{0.075}PbI_{2}Br  $   solar cell [47]. Moreover, Zeng et al. introduced   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ -based PSCs by passivating the GBs defects with polymers, and they achieved a PCE of 12.02% [48]. Besides, Han et al. added   $  \mathrm{Pb(Ac)}_{2}  $   into the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution, which can passivate the defects of precursors and the charge recombination can be alleviated [49]. And it is widely recognized that the lower efficiency of inorganic solar cells is closely related to the high trap state density. The trap states can not only induce ion migration, current density-voltage (I-V) hysteresis and device degradation, but also cause large charge recombination and a reduced open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) and fill factor (FF) of devices. It is urgent to reduce or passivate the trap states and get efficient inorganic PSCs. It is also found that charge recombination can be suppressed by polymers [44,45]. It is urgent to reduce or passivate the trap states in order to get highly efficient inorganic PSCs. Therefore, several polymers have been adopted to restrain the trap states and promote charge extraction in organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs [50–53]. Among these polymers, polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) as an amorphous, environmentally friendly polymer, was used in many applications. Many researchers have demonstrated the passivation effect of PMMA on organic-inorganic perovskite films by reducing the surface traps or filling the cracks [54]. However, PMMA has rarely been used in the inorganic PSCs up to now.

In this article, we attempt to improve the quality of perovskite

$ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  films by spinning an additional PMMA coating. The PMMA coating was deposited onto the perovskite film by spin-coating process with different concentrations of PMMA precursors. The effects of PMMA coating on the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  perovskite films and the photophysical performance of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $ -based PSCs have been studied in detail. We found that the PMMA coating can retain the original morphology and not generate a new phase. And the PMMA coating can effectively suppress the non-radiative recombination induced by the trap states of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  film. Furthermore, the PMMA coating could enhance the light-harvesting ability and carrier transportation of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  film. As a result, PMMA-coated  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $ -based PSCs exhibited a PCE of  $ 11.18\% $ , which is higher than the control device with  $ 9.69\% $  efficiency. The result suggests that the PMMA coating film can enhance the performance of PSCs and this work also is promising method for the development of related photovoltaic devices.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

The clean Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass was placed in a 0.04 M TiCl $ _{4} $  aqueous solution for half an hour at 70 °C and then being washed by ethanol and distilled water, followed by heating at 200 °C for 30 min to get a compact TiO $ _{2} $  layer. Then 0.8 M CsPbI $ _{2} $ Br precursor solution was prepared by dissolving CsI: PbI $ _{2} $ : PbBr $ _{2} $  (2:1:1) in the mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO (V $ _{DMF} $ /V $ _{DMSO} $  = 4:1) and vigorously stirring at 70 °C for overnight. The CsPbI $ _{2} $ Br precursor solution was deposited by spin coating on TiO $ _{2} $ /FTO substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s and the film was annealed at 150 °C for 10 min. For the PMMA-coated PSCs, different concentrations of PMMA chlorobenzene solutions (0.2 mg/mL, 0.4 mg/mL and 0.6 mg/mL) were deposited onto the CsPbI $ _{2} $ Br film at the condition of 4000 rpm for 30 s and then heated at 100 °C for 10 min. For a hole transport layer (HTL), the spiro-OMeTAD solution was coated on the perovskite film at 4000 rpm for 30 s. 72.3 mg spiro-OMeTAD powders was mixed with 17.5 μL of Li-TFSI (300 mg/mL in acetonitrile) and 29 μL of TBP in 1 mL of chlorobenzene solvent to form the spiro-OMeTAD solution. Finally, 70 nm silver electrode was prepared on HTL film by thermal evaporation method to accomplish the device fabrication. The active areas of devices is 0.1 cm $ ^{2} $ .

# 2.3. Characterization

The structure of films were confirmed by X-ray diffraction (XRD, D8-Advance, Bruker) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, ESCALAB 250, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The surface and cross-sectional morphology images were measured by S4800 scanning electron microscope (SEM). The photoluminescence (PL) spectra were obtained by using photomultiplier (PMTH-S1-CR131) with lock-in amplifier (SR 830). For the measurement of time-resolved PL (TRPL) spectra, we use a  $ 450~\mathrm{nm} $  diode laser as excitation source. The UV-visible (UV-vis) optical absorption curves were measured with a Shimadzu UV-3600 spectrophotometer. The Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR studies) was obtained with the Thermo Scientific Nicolet IS5. The current-voltage (I-V) curves were recorded using AM 1.5 solar simulator (San-Ei, 3A, 150 W) with an intensity of  $ 100\mathrm{mW/cm^2} $ . The QEX10 system (PV Measurement) can be used to record the external quantum efficiency

(EQE) spectra of solar cells.

# 2.4. Calculations

All density functional theory (DFT) calculations were carried out using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) [54,55]. The generalized gradient approximation of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (GGA-PBE) was used to describe the exchange-correlation functional [56,57]. A plane-wave basis set with an energy cutoff of  $ 750\mathrm{eV} $  was used to expand the one-electron wave function, and Brillouin zone (BZ) sampling grids were improved to  $ 2\pi \times 0.03\AA^{-1} $  for structural optimization. The hybrid functional methods [58] were also used to correct the band structure. The geometry optimization was stopped when the forces on each atom were less than  $ 0.02\mathrm{eV} / \mathring{\mathrm{A}} $ .

The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL spectroscopy were adopted to evaluate the excitation and recombination processes of the photogenerated carriers. As shown in Fig. 5(a), the PL

intensity of the PMMA-coated   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pervoskite films is much higher than the films without any treatment, suggesting that the PMMA coating can passivate the GBs or surface defects. Moreover, to further confirm the electron extraction mechanism, the TRPL spectra of the pure   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and PMMA-coated   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films displayed in Fig. 5(b) and fitted by a bi-exponential decay function   $  f(t) = A_{1}\exp(-t/\tau_{1}) + A_{2}\exp(-t/\tau_{2}) + y_{0}  $  , where   $  \tau_{1}  $   reflects the fast decay lifetime of nonradiative recombination caused by defects and   $  \tau_{2}  $   represents the slow decay lifetime associate with the radiative recombination in perovskite [38,41]. The fitted TRPL decay parameters were summarized in Table S2. The average carrier lifetimes (  $  \tau_{ave}  $  ) were calculated to be 15.3 and 27.4 ns for the pure   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and PMMA-coated   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  , respectively. It's obvious that the perovskite film with PMMA coating shows a longer PL lifetime contrast with others have no any passivation treatment, indicating that PMMA solution can passivate the trap states of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   by reducing the charge non-radiative recombination process. So, above results illustrated that the PMMA coating can distinctly improve   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF of the PSCs finally. According to Eq (1), the   $  N_{trap}  $   were calculated to be   $  1.0\times10^{16}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $   and   $  0.78\times10^{16}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $   for the pure   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and PMMA-coated   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   device, respectively. The result directly confirmed that the trap states at the perovskite film can be passivated by PMMA coating, which is consistent with PL and TRPL results. The decreased defect density in the PMMA-  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film is favorable for preventing the charge recombination, which can further improve the   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF of the PMMA-coated   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cells. This result can also explain the reason for the improved device performance of PMMA-coated   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film based solar cells.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, we have indicated that PMMA coating can availably passivate the surface defects of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, thus decreasing the possibility of non-radiative recombination with improving the efficiency of electron extraction. Moreover,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films with PMMA coating display extended photoluminescence life-time and reduce trap states, which improve the PCE of the solar cells. The best-performance of device based on PMMA-coated   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film displays a efficiency of 11.18%, which is higher than those of the PSCs without PMMA coating (9.69%). In addition, the PSC with PMMA coating shows an excellent long-term stability. Therefore, this research offers a simple and efficient method for enhancing the photovoltaic performance and long-term stability of solar cells.
