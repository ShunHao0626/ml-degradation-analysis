# Functionalized CNTs as Effective Additives to Improve the Efficiency of Perovskite Solar Cells

Xiaotong Ma, Teng Zhang,  $  ^{*}  $   Baohua Zhao, Chengben Liu, Xinmei Li, Heyuan Liu, Guangwu Yang,  $  ^{*}  $   Yanli Chen, Lilin Jiang, and Xiyou Li  $  ^{*}  $

Cite This: https://dx.doi.org/10.1021/acsaem.0c01675

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: High-quality and uniform perovskite films are the first step toward high-performance perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Adding additives to regulating the film formation process is proved to be an efficient way to acquire high-quality perovskite films. In this study, we use functionalized carbon nanotubes (CNTs) as effective additives to improve the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs. Three kinds of CNTs, namely, pristine CNTs, amino-group-functionalized CNTs (NH₂-CNTs), and carboxyl-group-functionalized CNTs (COOH-CNTs) are selected. The PCE of PSCs doped with pristine CNTs (13.91%) drops compared with that of a control device (16.56%), while the PCE increases to 17.47% for NH₂-CNT-doped PSCs and 17.75% for COOH-CNT-doped PSCs. A detailed study shows that both NH₂-CNTs and COOH-CNTs can facilitate the crystallization of the perovskite films.

Compared with a control film, the hole mobility of the perovskite films can be increased by 1 order of magnitude in a COOH-CNT-doped perovskite film. This contributes to better carrier transportation in PSCs. After optimizing the length and doping concentration of the COOH-CNTs, the PCE for PSCs doped with COOH-CNTs steadily improves to  $ 18.68\% $ . Furthermore, the stability of PSCs has also been improved with COOH-CNT-doped perovskite films. Our study provides insightful hints for using functionalized carbon-based materials to develop high-performance perovskite-based optoelectronic devices.

KEYWORDS: perovskite solar cell, carbon nanotubes, crystal nucleation and growth, carrier transfer, defect density

# INTRODUCTION

Very recently, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has reached 25.2%, which is comparable with most commercialized solar cells, such as multicrystalline silicon (23.3%) and cadmium telluride (22.1%).  $  ^{1}  $   The excellent performance of PSCs require high-quality perovskite films with good carrier transportation. At the beginning of PSC research, the prepared perovskite films suffered from incomplete surface coverage and poor crystallinity.  $  ^{2,3}  $   Then, the antisolvent dripping method successfully solved the morphological problem and boosted the performance by over 16%.  $  ^{4}  $   Afterward, solvent engineering,  $  ^{4-7}  $   formula engineering,  $  ^{8,9}  $   additive engineering,  $  ^{10-14}  $   and hot casting  $  ^{15,16}  $   were developed to regulate the nucleation and growth of the perovskite films.

Additive engineering is a facile and practical method to prepare high-performance PSCs.  $  ^{10-14,17,18}  $   Using methylammonium acetate (MAAc) and thiosemicarbazide (TSC) as additives, Wu achieved a large-scale   $  (1\ cm^{2})  $   PSC with an efficiency of   $  \sim20\%  $  .  $  ^{11}  $   Bi and co-workers introduced a polymer template-assisted nucleation strategy and achieved an efficiency exceeding 21%.  $  ^{17}  $   The morphology and quality of these perovskite films can be effectively regulated through additive engineering. Additives such as acetate and thiocyanate also contribute to the formation of microsized perovskite grains.  $  ^{19-22}  $   The reason for the additive-enhanced PCE can be

Due to the superb electric properties, carbon-based materials have been widely used in perovskite photovoltaics.  $  ^{2,5,41-44}  $   In this project, we use functionalized carbon nanotubes (CNTs) as additives to improve the efficiency and stability of PSCs. Our results suggest that both amino-group-functionalized CNTs (NH  $  _{2}  $  -CNTs) and carboxyl-group-functionalized CNTs (COOH-CNTs) can facilitate the crystallization of the perovskite crystals, which significantly improves the performance of PSCs. Compared with the control devices (16.56%), the efficiency of CNT-doped PSCs has been improved to 17.47% (NH  $  _{2}  $  -CNT-doped PSCs) and 17.75% (COOH-CNT-doped PSCs). It has been further optimized to 18.68% (COOH-CNT (2   $  \mu  $  m) doped PSCs) by changing the doping concentration. Furthermore, the COOH-CNT-doped PSCs maintain almost 90% of their initial efficiency while being kept in ambient conditions (45% relative humidity) for half a month. The above results successfully demonstrate that functionalized CNTs are effective additives for PSCs.

The (110) peak intensity has been increased by about 1.6 times on the XRD pattern of   $  NH_{2}  $  -CNT-doped perovskite film and 2.3 times for COOH-CNT-doped perovskite films, whereas it has been slightly dropped for pristine CNT-doped perovskite films (Figure S2). It seems that instead of facilitating the crystal growth, pristine CNTs impede the growth of the perovskite crystals. The poor crystallinity of the pristine CNT-doped perovskite films has also been confirmed by the absorption spectrum (Figure S3).  $  ^{46,47}  $   As shown in Figure 2d, relatively smaller full width at half-maximum (FWHM) values are observed on the XRD patterns of   $  NH_{2}  $  -CNT- and COOH-CNT-doped perovskite films. Since a smaller FWHM value suggests a larger grain size,  $  ^{48}  $   the FWHM results are consistent with scanning electron microscopy (SEM) observations.

We next discuss the performance characterization of the devices. As shown in Figure 3a, the best PCE of the control device is 16.56%, while it drops to 13.91% in PSCs with pristine CNTs but increases to 17.47 and 17.75% for PSCs with   $  NH_{2}  $  -CNTs and COOH-CNTs, respectively. Here, we also fabricated PSCs with the antisolvent spin-coating method. Compared with the PSCs formed using the vapor healing method, the PCEs of spin-coated PSCs are relatively lower (Figure S4), and therefore, the vapor healing method has been selected in the current study. The reduced performance of pristine CNT-doped PSCs can be attributed to the poor crystallinity of the perovskite films as supported by XRD experiments shown in Figure 2c,d. The PL experiments (Figure 3b) reveal enhanced fluorescence intensity for the functionalized CNT-doped perovskite films in comparison with the control film. This suggests that the carrier recombination has been reduced due to the reduced number of defects and better crystallinity of these functionalized CNT-doped films. The functionalized groups on CNTs should be responsible for the difference between pristine CNT- and functionalized CNT-doped perovskite films. We use Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) to characterize the interaction between the COOH-CNT additive and perovskite matrices. The full FTIR spectrum is shown in Figure S5, while the enlarged FTIR spectrum shows that the C=O stretching mode peak at   $  1717\ cm^{-1}  $   has been negatively shifted by about   $  10\ cm^{-1}  $   in COOH-CNT-doped perovskite films (Figure 3c). This suggests a strong interaction between carboxyl groups and the perovskite films. According to density functional theory (DFT), the distance (  $  \sim2.5\ \AA  $  ) between   $  Pb^{2+}  $   of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   and   $  O^{2-}  $   of   $  COO^{-}  $  is regarded to be close enough to form a stable Pb–O bond (3.05 Å).  $  ^{41}  $   So, we speculate that functionalized groups such as carboxyl groups serve as effective nucleation sites during the growth of perovskite crystals.

We select COOH-CNT-doped PSCs for further optimization. After optimizing the length (Figure 4a) and concentration (Figure 4b) of the COOH-CNT dopant, a maximum PCE of 18.86% (small-area) has been obtained, with a 14% improvement compared with the control devices (Figure 4b). The PCE drops with the increased length of CNTs, and “overdoping” (>0.02 mg/mL) also results in reduced performance. This can be attributed to the aggregation tendency of the CNT dopant (Figure S6). A large-scale (1 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) device has also been fabricated, and we have an optimized PCE of 13.75% for COOH-CNT-doped PSCs (the data are recorded in Figure S7). The integrated   $  J_{sc}  $   calculated from the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectrum is consistent with the   $  J_{sc}  $   from the J-V curves (Figure 4c). The electron mobility has been reduced from   $  9.068 \, cm^{2}/(V \, s)  $   (control film) to   $  4.879 \, cm^{2}/(V \, s)  $   for COOH-CNT-doped perovskite films (Figure 5b). The calculated carrier mobility values of the perovskite film are consistent with the result reported by other groups.  $  ^{53,54}  $   Since the hole mobility (  $  0.3787 \, cm^{2}/(V \, s)  $  ) is much smaller than that of the electron mobility (  $  9.068 \, cm^{2}/(V \, s)  $  ) in our control films, the hole mobility can be regarded as the dominant factor limiting the carrier transportation of our PSCs. The greatly improved hole mobility of COOH-CNT-doped perovskite films leads to better carrier transportation and an improved fill factor (FF) (Figure 3a).

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) analysis is conducted under dark conditions. Previous works suggest that the semicircle for PSCs under dark conditions represents the recombination resistance   $  (R_{\mathrm{rec}})  $  . The larger the   $  R_{rec}  $  , the lesser the possibility of carrier recombination.  $  ^{[28,55,56]}  $   Figure 6a suggests that   $  R_{rec}  $   has been increased from 1082   $  \Omega  $   for control devices to 1558   $  \Omega  $   for COOH-CNT (2   $  \mu  $  m) doped PSCs, reducing the possibility of carrier recombination. Improved stability has also been observed in COOH-CNT (2   $  \mu  $  m) doped PSCs, as shown in Figure 6b, possibly due to the better crystallinity and grain boundary passivation effect of the COOH-CNT additive.

# CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, the functionalized CNTs are effective additives to improve the efficiency and stability of PSCs. The functional groups on CNTs play an important role in regulating the nucleation and growth of the perovskite films. By carefully tuning the functional groups on CNTs, high-quality and stable perovskite films with reduced defect density are prepared. Besides, functionalized CNTs also act as effective carrier transportation channels in perovskite films and thus promote carrier mobility. The current study reveals that functional CNTs should be an efficient additive to improve the carrier transporting property and promote the efficiency of PSCs. This conclusion also indicates that other carbon materials with abundant functional groups and high conductivity might be good additives for improving the performance of perovskite optoelectronic devices.
