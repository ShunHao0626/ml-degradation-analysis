# Modification Engineering in   $  SnO_{2}  $   Electron Transport Layer toward Perovskite Solar Cells: Efficiency and Stability

Kaimo Deng, Qinghua Chen, and Liang Li*

The electron transport layer plays a key role in affecting the charge dynamics and photovoltaic parameters in perovskite solar cells. Compared to other counterparts,  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  has unique advantages such as low temperature fabrication and high electron extraction ability, and it receives extra attentions from the research community since the first report. Planar-type perovskite solar cells based on  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  exhibit a simple architecture and state of art device can achieve a power conversion efficiency of over  $ 23\% $ , which can compete with traditional devices using mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $ . The modification engineering of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  has contributed significantly to the enhanced device performance during the past years. There is still great potential for further improvement in the efficiency and long-term stability. Herein recent advances toward modifying the optoelectronic properties of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  from the perspective of the optimization strategies are summarized and the remaining challenges as well as opportunities for future research are discussed. The continuous efforts dedicated to this exciting field may pave the way for developing commercial perovskite solar cells.

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have undergone an extremely fast development in the past few years[1-16] and the continuously increased power conversion efficiency (PCE) enables it to compete with silicon solar cells, which currently dominate the photovoltaic market share. As one of the most promising photovoltaic devices, PSCs have unique advantages over other counterparts, such as easy fabrication, low cost, and compatibility to flexible electronics. The performance of PSCs is largely affected by device structure, active perovskite materials, charge transport layers, and involved interfaces, which are among the most widely studied fields. Currently, PSCs with sandwiching charge transport layers can be divided into two categories from the perspective of device structure, namely, the regular type  $ (n - i - p) $  and the inverted type  $ (p - i - n) $ , in both of which active material layer

The charge transport layer plays a significant role in determining the device performance by directly affecting the photocurrent density ( $ J_{\text{SC}} $ ), open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{\text{OC}} $ ), and fill factor (FF). $ ^{[31-33]} $  In fact, the PSCs can still function without the use of charge transport layers. However, the ETL-free or HTL-free PSCs typically show a poor performance in PCE and stability. $ ^{[34]} $  Even for the high-quality perovskite films, a high PCE for the corresponding PSCs can only be achieved when using well-designed charge transport layers.

In the regular-type PSCs, metal oxide with an n-type semiconductor property is generally chosen as the ETL.  $  ^{[35-37]}  $   The state-of-art device employs a composite ETL that includes a compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer and a mesoporous one.  $  ^{[38,39]}  $   However, it needs high-temperature annealing and cannot be compatible to flexible devices. This severely limits the wide use of   $  TiO_{2}  $   based ETL. To simplify the fabrication and expand the application, a low-temperature processed compact ETL is highly desired.   $  SnO_{2}  $   stands out for the application in the planar regular-type PSCs due to unique advantages compared to other candidates.  $  ^{[40-44]}  $   First, it has an excellent optoelectronic properties such as a large charge mobility, a suitable energy band structure, and an excellent chemical stability. # 2. Fabrication Methods of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  Film

To simultaneously serve as the charge selective and charge transport functions, the ETL must be compact and pinhole free, so that it can avoid the direct contact between the transparent conductive substrate and the perovskite and minimize the charge recombination. Furthermore, the thickness of the ETL should be as thin as possible to reduce the series resistance of the final device. To date, various methods have been developed to fabricate   $  SnO_{2}  $   films and the resulted morphology and optoelectronic properties may be different. Figure 2 summarizes the frequently used methods for   $  SnO_{2}  $   film fabrication in PSCs. Each method will be briefly introduced in this section.

# 2.2. Sol–Gel Method

Metal oxide thin film can be prepared by spin-coating sol–gel precursor followed by heat treatment, which is easy and cheap. SnO  $  _{2}  $  films can be grown on conductive substrates by this way.  $  ^{[62,63]}  $  In a typical application, organic solvent solution that contains precursors such as SnCl  $  _{2}  $  ·2H  $  _{2}  $  O or SnCl  $  _{4}  $  ·5H  $  _{2}  $  O is spin-coated on the precleaned substrate. Then it is heated in an air condition to induce the formation of compact SnO  $  _{2}  $ . Ke et al.  $  ^{[52]}  $  first reported the use of solution processed SnO  $  _{2}  $  film as ETL in PSCs. In their study, the SnO  $  _{2}  $  ETLs were deposited on FTO substrates by spin-coating SnCl  $  _{2}  $  ·2H  $  _{2}  $  O ethanol precursor at a room temperature followed by thermal annealing in air at 180 °C for 1 h. It is noted that the concentration of precursor solution, the spin-coating velocity, and the annealing temperature are the key parameters that affect the quality of the SnO  $  _{2}  $  film. However, the PCE under forward scan reduced to 14.82% compared to over 17% for reverse scan. The hysteresis phenomenon is mainly due to the existence of surface traps on the as-prepared SnO  $  _{2}  $  and poor electron transfer at the SnO  $  _{2}  $ /perovskite interface.

# 2.3.  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  Colloid

In addition to above mentioned solution processing, the growth of   $  SnO_{2}  $   film can be started with the use of colloid dispersion, which contains   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloids with a size down to several nanometers. Spin-coating the colloid dispersion on the conductive substrate can lead to the formation of a compact   $  SnO_{2}  $   film. Jiang et al.  $  ^{[64]}  $   first reported the use of commercial   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloid solution to fabricate the ETL for PSCs. The colloid based   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film is not only smooth and compact but also efficient in extracting electrons from the perovskite. Thanks to the high quality of the colloid based   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film, an impressive PCE close to 20% for planar PSCs can be achieved and a careful control of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   content in the perovskite active layer further pushed the value to 21.6%.  $  ^{[65]}  $   In a recent report, the same group passivated the surface of perovskite film with PEAI and a PCE of over 23% was realized for colloid   $  SnO_{2}  $   based planar PSCs.  $  ^{[55]}  $

# 2.4. Atomic Layer Deposition

$ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  thin film can be also prepared by atomic layer deposition, which is a powerful tool to exactly control the growth of thin film layer by layer.[66-68] In brief, Sn-containing metal organic molecules are pumped into the reaction chamber by a carrying gas and the conductive substrates are located at the center of the chamber with the conductive side facing up. The precursor molecules are adsorbed on the top of the substrate and a degassing

process removes the excess molecules, only leaving a single layer on the surface of the substrate. Then water molecules that act as the second reactant are introduced into the chamber and react with the predeposited molecules. The reaction can complete in a short period since the chamber is preheated to promote the reaction. This process represents a reaction cycle for the growth of a single layer of   $  SnO_{2}  $  . Repeated depositions with more reaction cycles can deposit   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film with a desired thickness.   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film processed by ALD technique is compact and evenly distributed in thickness. The thickness of the deposited   $  SnO_{2}  $   compact layer is a very important parameter. Insufficient reaction cycles can lead to an ultrathin   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer, which may have a limited ability in blocking holes. If the thickness of   $  SnO_{2}  $   is beyond the optimal value, the resistance for charge transport will increase. Therefore, a balance should be reached between charge transport and charge recombination. Baena et al. reported planar PSCs with a PCE of 18.4% based on the ALD processed   $  SnO_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[48]}  $   It is worth noting that the as-synthesized   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film is usually poor in crystallization and needs further heat treatment.  $  ^{[69]}  $   According to the report by Wang et al. and Lee et al.,  $  ^{[70,71]}  $   the performance of PSCs has a direct connection with the annealing temperature of the ALD   $  SnO_{2}  $   films. The thermal annealing can increase the electron mobility and conductivity of the ALD   $  SnO_{2}  $   film, which alleviates the imbalance of charge transport at the ETL/perovskite and perovskite/HTL interface, and thus reduces the J-V hysteresis.

# 3. Controlling Bulky Electronic Properties

The bulky electronic properties of ETL can great affect the charge dynamics within the PSCs and the device performance. The open-circuit voltage, one of the key parameters that directly determines the PCE of PSCs, depends on several factors including the bandgap of perovskite, the quasi-Fermi level difference between ETL and HTL, relative band energy levels, interfacial recombination, and nonradiative recombination.[72] Among these factors, the energy band offset between the ETL/perovskite and HTL/perovskite interfaces provides the driving force for charge extraction and helps lower interfacial and nonradiative recombination. To achieve an ideal  $  V_{\mathrm{OC}}  $ , it is crucial to build up a heterojunction with well-matched energy bands and superior conductivity in order to avoid excessive band offset while maintaining efficient carrier extraction and transport. To reduce  $  V_{\mathrm{OC}}  $  loss, it must be guaranteed that at open circuit condition the quasi-Fermi level of electrons in the perovskite film under illumination is equal or higher than that in the absence of the ETL.[73] This requires that the interfacial recombination rate for electrons injected into the ETL with the holes in the perovskite must be lower than the intrinsic carrier recombination rate in the perovskite. In addition, the charge mobility of the charge transport layer has a huge influence on the charge transport behavior. A charge transport layer with a high charge mobility can effectively accelerate the charge extraction and alleviate the charge accumulation at the interface. A balanced charge transport can make sure that the charge recombination will be greatly suppressed.[74] Therefore it is necessary to develop ETLs with a reduced interfacial carrier recombination velocity through tailoring the Fermi level or charge mobility,

# 3.2. Organic Molecules–SnO  $  _{2}  $  Complexes

In contrast with element doping where the introduced dopant enters the crystal lattice, organic molecules can instead form a hybrid organic-inorganic complexes with the inorganic host materials. When organic molecules are adsorbed on the surface of inorganic nanocrystals, charge transfer may occur from

the organic molecules to the inorganic semiconductor, without inducing defects within the inorganic lattice matrix. This strategy has been proved to be effective when applying on optoelectronics and energy devices.  $  ^{[82,83]}  $

Tu et al.  $  ^{[84]}  $  successfully demonstrated an n-type doping of  $  SnO_{2}  $  with an air-robust and cheap electron donor triphenylphosphine oxide (TPPO). It has three benzene rings at the three vertices in a plane and an oxygen atom at the other vertex. Figure 4a illustrates the possible molecule configuration due to the linkage between the oxygen atom of TPPO and the tin atom on the surface of  $  SnO_{2}  $  crystal. The characterization of electrostatic force microscopy and the blue shift of Sn signal in X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy confirm the n-type doping. They also conducted density functional theory calculations and found that there is a strong interaction between TPPO and  $  SnO_{2}  $  through chemical bonding. The molecule doping can enhance the conductivity and reduce the work function of  $  SnO_{2}  $  with a shift from -5.13 to -4.91 eV after modification with TPPO. The energy band alignment at the interface shown in Figure 4b is also reconstructed by the doping and an energy band bending is formed with a decreased energy barrier from 0.55 to 0.39 eV, which lead to an enlarged built-in field from 0.01 to 0.07 eV. This enables an increase in the PCE of PSCs from 19.01% to 20.69%, mainly attributed to the increased  $  V_{OC}  $  and FF, and a decrease in the hysteresis index from 9.99% to 2.17%.

Yang et al.  $  ^{[85]}  $  introduced ethylene diamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA) into  $  SnO_{2}  $  colloid dispersion and realized an EDTA-complexed  $  SnO_{2}  $  ETLs. EDTA has been well studied in the modification of charge transport layer in organic solar cells due to its strong chelation ability. Compared to the pristine  $  SnO_{2}  $ , the modified ETL shows more superior electronic properties. On one hand, the work function of EDTA-complexed  $  SnO_{2}  $  matches well with the conduction band minimum of perovskite (Figure 4c), which can lead to a high  $  V_{OC}  $ . On the other hand, the electron mobility of ETL by complexing EDTA with  $  SnO_{2}  $  is three times larger than that of  $  SnO_{2}  $  (Figure 4d). The increased electron mobility may yield a reduced hysteresis effect for PSCs due to the improved charge extraction from perovskite to ETL and the suppressed charge accumulation at the perovskite/ETL interface. The PCE of planar-type PSCs based on EDTA-complexed  $  SnO_{2}  $  can increase to 21.6% with negligible hysteresis as shown in Figure 4e. Owing to the low temperature processing, the EDTA-modified  $  SnO_{2}  $  can also be applied on a flexible device, achieving a PCE of 18.28%.

Wei et al.  $  ^{[86]}  $  reported a novel composite ETL that contains  $  SnO_{2}  $  and polyethylene glycol (PEG). # 3.3. Introducing Inorganic Materials

Carbon families such as carbon nanotubes, fullerene, and graphene, have been widely investigated as additives in PSCs.  $  ^{[87-91]}  $   They mainly serve to improve the crystallinity and morphology of perovskite films and offer an efficient charge-collection path. In recent years, much attentions have been devoted to the modification of   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL by using carbon family members. Xie et al.  $  ^{[92]}  $   reported a method to improve the electronic properties of   $  SnO_{2}  $   as ETL through adding a small amount of graphene quantum dots (GQDs) into the   $  SnCl_{2}\cdot2H_{2}O  $   ethanol solution. They found that the conduction band minimum of GQDs is higher than that of   $  SnO_{2}  $ , which enables an efficient electron transfer from GQDs to   $  SnO_{2}  $   without any energy barrier. Under the illumination of photons with energy above the bandgap, the photogenerated electrons in GQDs can be transferred to the conduction band of   $  SnO_{2}  $ . This charge transfer will effectively fill the traps and increase the electron density in the conduction band, thus reducing the work function and enhancing the conductivity of   $  SnO_{2}  $ . Figure 5a,b schematically presents the involved mechanism. The change in electronic properties is beneficial to the electron extraction and the charge recombination suppression at the ETLs/perovskite interface. The device based on   $  SnO_{2}  $ -GQDs composite could reach an average PCE

of   $  19.2 \pm 1.0\%  $   and a steady-state PCE of 20.23% with ignored hysteresis (Figure 5c).

Hui et al.  $  ^{[93]}  $  used carboxylic acid- and hydroxyl-rich red-carbon quantum dots to fabricate a doped-  $  SnO_{2}  $  ETL for PSCs. # 4. Engineering Surface Properties

Organic molecules such as self-assembled monolayer (SAM) and conjugated polyelectrolytes have been commonly used to modify the metal oxide surface and achieve a tunable work function via the formation of permanent dipole moments.  $  ^{[97]}  $   When the dipole is directed toward the   $  SnO_{2}  $  , the band edge potential shifts away from the local vacuum level. Therefore, the effective gap between the conduction band minimum of metal oxide and the valence band of perovskite narrows compared to the unmodified   $  SnO_{2}  $  . If the dipoles point away from   $  SnO_{2}  $  , the band edge shifts closer to the vacuum level and broadens the effective gap. The presence of a dipole layer that changes the effective gap consequently affects the maximum attainable   $  V_{OC}  $  . Furthermore, this method may enhance electron extraction ability and reduce energy loss at the interface in PSCs.  $  ^{[98]}  $

Choi et al.  $  ^{[99]}  $  reported a zwitterion-modified  $  SnO_{2}  $  ETL for achieving highly efficient PSCs based on hybrid perovskite.

A zwitterionic compound, 3-(1-pyridinio)-1-propanesulfonate, is chosen to modify the surface properties of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ . As is known, a zwitterion contains immobilized cationic and anionic charges on the same molecule and has a permanent dipole. When the zwitterionic molecules are introduced onto the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  surface, the hydroxyl groups on the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  surface can chemically react with the zwitterionic molecules and form a chemical bond between the zwitterion and  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ . Figure 6a provides a schematic drawing of the device structure and the interface. The energy band diagram in Figure 6b shows that the zwitterionic molecules shift the work function of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  from 4.34 to  $ 4.23\mathrm{eV} $ . The change in energy band confirm the effect of the interfacial dipoles. The upshifted work function is advantageous to the increase of built-in electric field, while the interfacial dipoles can suppress back recombination by preventing the electron back transfer from the ETL to the perovskite. It is noted that the modification of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  with zwitterionic molecules can also enhance the conductivity of ETL and suppress the formation of deep traps in the perovskite, which can facilitate the charge transport. Figure 6c-e gives representative photovoltaic performance of the corresponding devices. The device with zwitterion modified  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  had a PCE of  $ 20.91\% $  in the reverse scan. The performance is much better than that of the control device with untreated  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ( $ 19.63\% $ ). The statistics of photovoltaic parameters indicate that the improved PCE is mainly attributed to the increased  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  that benefits from the shifted work function. Moreover, the optimized ETL induces a weaker hysteresis behavior than the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ -based device due to the improved conductivity and well-matched charge balance. # 6. Boosting ETL-Related Stability

The above-mentioned strategies to passivate the interface between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite can not only improve the PCE of PSCs, but also yield more stable devices to some extent. The improvement in stability after interface passivation can be accounted for the suppression of the defect assisted-ion diffusion and chemical reaction with moisture and oxygen. Of course, the optimization only toward the interface is not enough. The instability of PSCs mainly arises from the vulnerability of perovskite to the outdoor conditions including moisture, oxygen, UV light as well as thermal effect.  $  ^{[121]}  $   At present, most efforts are focused on understanding electronic properties and modulating thin film quality of the perovskite active layer. Other factors that can affect the stability of PSCs are overlooked and less investigated. In fact, the charge transport layers that are intimately connected with perovskites can also impact on the stability of perovskite. Dopants especially deliquescent Li-containing salts in the HTL can facilitate the decomposition of perovskite and degrade the performance when exposing in humid conditions.  $  ^{[122]}  $   The stability related issues of PSCs should be totally solved before commercial applications. To further improve the device performance and speed up the large-scale application, it is of great significance to learn more about these aspects.

# 6.2. Residual Stress

Residual stresses in perovskite films stem from the large difference in thermal expansion coefficient between perovskite and the substrate.  $  ^{[128]}  $   When the annealed perovskite films cool down to room temperature, the substrate that has a lower thermal expansion coefficient constrains the perovskite from contracting, which produces tensile stresses in the perovskite films. The thermally induced tensile stress can decrease the formation energy of defects and lower the activation energy for ion migration, which promotes the degradation in performance and instability during aging. To minimize the negative impact of tensile stress, increasing efforts have been made, including the annealing process optimization, the stress compensation effect, and the composition engineering of perovskite.  $  ^{[129-132]}  $

Wu et al.  $  ^{[133]}  $  introduced polystyrene (PS) as a buffer layer between the  $  SnO_{2}  $  and perovskite to slow down the stress effect at the heterojunction. The spin-coated PS layer on the  $  SnO_{2}  $  significantly decreases the surface roughness from 5.12 to 1.55 nm.

# 7. Conclusion and Outlook

Over the past few years, great achievements in exploring the   $  SnO_{2}  $   based ETL for PSCs have been made. In this progress report, we summarize recent advances toward the modification of   $  SnO_{2}  $   for a better performance in PSCs. The continuous efforts devoted to this exciting field will constantly promote the development of PSCs to a higher level. In our opinion, there is still huge potential for more progress in the photovoltaic performance. First of all, advanced methods may be available to rationally tune the electronic properties of   $  SnO_{2}  $   in order to obtain an increased conductivity and a desirable work function. The balance charge transport between the ETL and HTL can help reduce charge accumulation and relieve the hysteresis effect. Besides, the interface between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite should be further optimized by defect passivation to reduce the charge recombination at the interface and increase the   $  V_{OC}  $   of the PSCs. Therefore, a   $  V_{OC}  $   loss as low as possible may be expected, even close to the theoretical limit. In addition,   $  SnO_{2}  $   can be also applied in the inverted type PSCs. The excellent electronic properties of   $  SnO_{2}  $   make it possible to replace or combine with organic molecules and serve as an efficient ETL for high performance inverted type PSCs. Finally, the   $  SnO_{2}/perovskite  $   heterojunction with a mismatch in lattice constant and thermal expansion coefficient can induce the formation of defects and lattice stress, which limit the long-term stability of PSCs. A deep insight in terms of precise control at the nanoscale may contribute to solve the problem. In short, we are optimistic about the future research on   $  SnO_{2}  $ . The upcoming findings may further boost the PCE and stability and accelerate the wide application of PSCs.
