# GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT

# ABSTRACT

Fabrication of highly efficient perovskite solar cells (PSCs) under ambient environment is critical to its large-scale preparation and commercialization. However, the perovskite undergoes rapid degradation in moist environment, resulting in a significant decline of device performance. Herein, a novel sodium citrate modified poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (abbreviate as SC-PEDOT:PSS) “conductive adhesive”, is introduced into the perovskite to passivate defects, improve crystallization and boost the charge carrier transport properties of perovskite. Therefore, the high-quality perovskite film with large grain sizes and minor blemishes is obtained. As a result, the photovoltaic performance of PSC is greatly improved. The optimized device can achieve an impressive 19.1% power conversion efficiency (PCE) and negligible hysteresis of the photocurrent. Moreover, when the devices are placed under 85% relative humidity (RH) for 14 days in the dark, the normalized PCE of device with SC-PEDOT:PSS can still maintain 82.7%, which is much better than that of the PSC without SC-PEDOT:PSS (8.6%). The present strategy can facilitate development of high efficiency and well stability PSCs under ambient environment for practical applications.

# 1. Introduction

In recent years, organometallic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have generated crucial attentions due to their excellent photovoltaic performance, low-cost fabrication method and grateful potential for commercialization   $  [1-4]  $  . Within decade of the first report, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has improved remarkably from 3.8% to over 25%   $  [5-8]  $  .

In spite of many significant improvements have been achieved, a critical bottleneck in the advanced PSCs is the ubiquitous practice of PSC fabrication under inert nitrogen environment achieved using a glove box [9]. However, in industrial manufacturing, operating large equipment under inert gas environment is costly as well as impractical. Fabrication of highly efficient and stable devices in air environment without glove box is a key to large-scale preparation of PSCs. So far, a small amount of literatures reported the fabrication of PSCs without glove box. For instance, terephthalic acid (TPA) and water were added into the perovskite precursor solution to fabricate efficient and stable PSCs [10, 11]. In our previous work [12], the PEDOT:PSS film fabricated via cryo-controlled quasi-congealing spin-coating was proposed and the PSCs were obtained under air condition due to the promising PEDOT:PSS film. However, the efficiencies of these PSCs are relatively low compared to the devices fabricated in glove box.

Recently, various polymer additives, such as Polyurethane (PU) [13], Polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) [14], Poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA) [15] have been proved to be effective additives for stable and highly efficient PSCs due to their low volatility and ability to localize at grain boundaries (GBs) enabled by their flexible long chains. However, these conventional polymers are insulated and may impede the improvement of perovskite performance. In this regard, conductive polymers possess outstanding electrical conductivity and abundant functional groups that can react with perovskite. Up to now, Polypyrrole (PPy) [16] and polyaniline (PANI) [17] served as additives to improve photovoltaic properties of PSCs. Notably, Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) is a classic hole transport material owing to high transparency in the visible region and excellent hole transport performance. Moreover, it also has rich sulfonate functional groups and remarkable electrochemical activity [18–20]. These unique advantages make it a promising additive for perovskite. However, the strong acidity of PEDOT:PSS solution may damage the structure of perovskite. And the mismatched work function (WF) between the perovskite and PEDOT:PSS may limit the improvements of device performance [21]. Therefore, modification of PEDOT:PSS is imperative when it is used to be additive in perovskite.

In this study, the PEDOT:PSS is modified by a weak alkali, sodium citrate (SC), to lower its acidity. Notably, the WF of sodium citrate modified PEDOT:PSS aligns better with the perovskite   $  [22]  $  . After that, the sodium citrate modified poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (abbreviate as SC-PEDOT:PSS) serves as a “conductive adhesive” into perovskite to enhance the connection between the uncoordinated lead positive ions (  $  Pb^{2+}  $  ) and the electron-rich oxygen anion (  $  O^{2-}  $  ), simultaneously passivate   $  I^{-}  $  vacancy and reduce defect density. In addition, the passivated defects could well modify the morphology of perovskite film and greatly improve the moisture resistance and photo stability of perovskite layer with keeping high optical properties   $  [23–25]  $  . The optimized SC-PEDOT:PSS-modified PSC advances an impressive 19.1% PCE and negligible hysteresis of the photocurrent. After the unencapsulated devices are placed under 85% relative humidity (RH) for 14 days in the dark, the normalized PCE of device with SC-PEDOT:PSS can still maintain 82.7%. This strategy of SC-PEDOT:PSS-modification can facilitate development of high efficiency and well stability PSCs under air condition for practical applications.

# 2. Experimental methods

# 2.1.Materials

Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass substrates were purchased from Advanced Electronic Technology Co., Ltd. Titanium diisopropoxide bis (acetylacetonate) (75 wt% in isopropanol) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. PbI₂ and CH₃NH₃I (MAI) were purchased from Kunshan Sunlaite New Energy Technology Co., Ltd. Sodium citrate (SC) was purchased from Hangzhou gaojing fine chemical industry Co., Ltd. 2,2',7,7'-Tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxy-phenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (SpiroOMeTAD), Tris(2-(1H-pyrazol-1-yl)-4-tert-butylpyridine)-cobalt(III)Tris(bis-(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide)) (FK 209 Co(III) TFSI salt), Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulphonyl)imide (LiTFSI), 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) and Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corporation. Notably, the type of PEDOT:PSS we used is 4083 and its concentration is 1.3–1.7 wt% dispersion in H₂O. Any other materials were supplied by Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd.

# 2.2. Solution preparation

Firstly, the SC was directly added into PEDOT:PSS solution in concentration of 0.12 mol/L to obtain the solution of SC-PEDOT:PSS standby. Then, the powder of Tris(2-(1H-pyrazol-1-yl)-4-tert-butylpyridine)cobalt(III)Tris(bis-(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide)) (FK 209 Co(III) TFSI salt) and Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulphonyl)imide (LiTFSI) are dissolve separately in acetonitrile with concentration of 170 mg/ml and 150 mg/ml, as the additive of Spiro-OMeTAD solution later. Secondly, the solution of Titanium diisopropoxide bis (acetylacetonate) (75 wt% in isopropanol) was diluted to 150 mg/ml by absolute ethanol. Thirdly, the perovskite precursor solution (dissolving 1 M equimolar ratio of PbI $ _2 $  and MAI in 0.6 mL N,N-Dimethylformamide (DMF) solvent) was compound with pristine, SC-PEDOT:PSS $ _{0.5\%} $ , SC-PEDOT:PSS $ _{1\%} $  and SC-PEDOT:PSS $ _{1.5\%} $ . All kinds of the perovskite precursor solution were heated in 60 °C and stirred in 300 rpm for 1 h before using. Finally, the 65 mg Spiro-OMeTAD powder were dispersed in 1 ml chlorobenzene with 20 μl tBP, 50 μl FK 209 Co(III) TFSI salt solution and 70 μl LiTFSI solution as additives.

# 2.3. Device fabrication

FTO-etched glasses were cleaned by ultrasonic treatment with acetone, ethyl alcohol and isopropyl alcohol for 15 min, respectively. After drying at 110 °C, the substrates were treated with ultraviolet (UV) ozone for 15 min to eliminate organic compounds on the surface of FTO. A compact TiO₂ layer, acted as the electron-transport layer (ETL), was spin-coated on the dry and clean FTO substrate. After gradually increasing the temperature to 450 °C (5 °C/min), the substrate was continuously annealed for 30 min. Then the perovskite precursor solution with or without SC-PEDOT:PSS was spin-coated on the TiO₂ layer at 2000 rpm (revolutions per minute) for 10 s and 4000 rpm for 25 s in turn. Then the thermal treatment with 100 °C for 30 min was carried out to assist the perovskite crystal growths. Notably, Ethyl acetate was used as anti-solvent in the production process and the detailed description was clearly presented in Fig. 1a and b. Afterwards, the solution of hole-transport layer (HTL), Spiro-OMeTAD, was spin-coated on the perovskite layer at 4000 rpm for 30 s. To oxidize Spiro-OMeTAD post deposition, the HTL-coated substrates were kept overnight in a desiccator before depositing the counter electrode. Finally, a thickness of approximately 80 nm and effective area of 0.06 cm² of Ag was deposited via thermal evaporation on the top as an electrode. The complete structure of device was exhibited in Fig. S1a. Specifically, the whole process of fabricating well-performance PSCs was in air environment (RH ≈ 50%) without glove box and therefore greatly reduced the manufacturing costs.

# 3. Results and discussion

To significantly reduce the cracks and improve the surface coverage of the perovskite film, herein SC-PEDOT:PSS is introduced into the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   precursor solution as a “conductive adhesive” and morphology-modified agent. Fig. 1a and b demonstrate the process of fabricating   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   film without and with SC-PEDOT:PSS. Generally, when the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film is fabricated under air condition (the moisture), small perovskite grains and numerous blemishes in surface of the film can be observed as illustrated in Fig. 1a. On the contrary, a considerably compact and uniform perovskite film with much larger crystal grain sizes (less separation between grains) could be obtained by addition of SC-PEDOT:PSS into perovskite as shown in Fig. 1b. This is because that, as demonstrated in Fig. 1c, the strong ionic bonds between   $  Pb^{2+}  $   and   $  O^{2-}  $   could work as an “adhesive” to connect perovskite grains, simultaneously passivate   $  I^{-}  $  vacancy and reduce defect density [26,27]. And thus, the interconnection between the SC-PEDOT:PSS and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   results in the dense perovskite grains. In addition, a small amount of SC-PEDOT:PSS aggregated near the octahedral   $  [PbI_{6}]^{4-}  $  , decreasing the process of perovskite crystallization, forming large crystalline and uniform perovskite films.

As expected, the perovskite film with SC-PEDOT:PSS demonstrates considerably dense, flat and pinhole-free morphology with larger sized grains (the maximum is 1.1   $  \mu  $  m), as displayed in Fig. 1e and Fig. S1b. This indicates greater morphology modulation for SC-PEDOT:PSS-modified perovskite film. In contrast, the pristine perovskite film has lots of blemishes and small grains (the maximum is only 600 nm), as shown in Fig. 1d, which seriously impede the efficiency and stability of the PSCs. For comparison, the effects of pure sodium citrate (SC) and pure PEDOT:PSS on the morphology of perovskite film were also investigated, and the related SEM images are exhibited in Fig. S2. As demonstrated in Fig. S2a, there is no significant change on surface of the perovskite film after adding pure sodium citrate. Interestingly, the perovskite film with larger sized grains is also obtained with introduction of PEDOT:PSS, as shown in Fig. S2b. However, the blemishes and leakages at surface are still clear due to the strong acidity, weak “adhesion” and mismatched work function of pure PEDOT:PSS, which would seriously corrode the GBs, cause charge recombination issues and hinder the stabilities of the perovskite to the moisture [28,29].

To overcome this issue, sodium citrate (SC) is introduced into PEDOT:PSS to neutralize its acidity. Figs. S3a and b display the chemical structure of PEDOT:PSS and SC. And their neutralization reaction is demonstrated in Fig. S3c. After the neutralization reaction between SC and PEDOT:PSS, the sodium cations replace some hydrogen ions in the sulfonate. Furthermore, the dissociation constant (pKa) of sodium sulfonate is more than sulfonic acid in aqueous solution and the electronegativity of sodium cation (0.93) is lower than hydrogen ions (2.1), and hence there is fewer oxygen anions combined with sodium ions than with hydrogen ions. As a result, more oxygen anions could easily ionization and the alkalinity of SC-PEDOT:PSS solution is slightly increased. The reduction of the SC-PEDOT:PSS solution acidity by sodium citrate incorporation is beneficial to perovskite morphology and stabilities of the PSCs [30,31]. The pH values of PEDOT:PSS and SC-PEDOT:PSS aqueous solution is exhibited in Fig. S3d. It is clear that the pH of the solution can be transferred from strong acidic  $ (\mathrm{pH} \approx 1) $  to nearly neutral  $ (\mathrm{pH} \approx 6) $  after the incorporation of sodium citrate  $ (0.12\mathrm{mol} / \mathrm{L}) $  into PEDOT:PSS solution. The electrical conductivities of PEDOT:PSS and SC-PEDOT:PSS thin films are demonstrated in Fig. S4a. As is known, the PEDOT:PSS originally has a certain degree of conductivity mainly due to the conductivity of PEDOT. After the SC modified, the conductivity of SC-PEDOT:PSS could keep its original values but has been not obviously improved because the number and the status of PEDOT without any conversion [32]. Fig. 5a demonstrates the PL spectral profile of every sample. Compared to the pristine one, the PL peak intensities of both PEDOT:PSS and SC-PEDOT:PSS-modified perovskite films are sharply reduced. To further investigate the charge collection efficiency, the time-resolved PL (TRPL) decay of all types of the perovskite films is tested. As displayed in Fig. 5b, SC-PEDOT:PSS-modified  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film results in a shorter charge carrier lifetime, suggesting that the photo-generated charges can transfer more barrier-freely lead to higher transport capability during device operation. Fitted parameters of TRPL spectra are exhibited in Table 1. All experimental data can be well-fitted to biexponential decay function  $ I(t) = I_0 + A_1exp^{(-t - t_0 / \tau_1)} + A_2exp^{(-t - t_0 / \tau_2)} $ . The average decay lifetime can obtain from the following equation  $ \tau_{avg} = (\sum_{i}A_{i}\tau_{i}^{2}) / (\sum_{i}A_{i}\tau_{i}) $ . The TRPL results indicate that SC-PEDOT:PSS-modified perovskite film could cause an reduction in non-radiative interface recombination, thereby suppressed from 92.86 ns to 26.84 ns. This due to the lower traps and defects of the SC-PEDOT:PSS-modified perovskite film.

As is known, iodine vacancy defects are the most deleterious defects for organometallic halide perovskites. They act as electron traps—inducing the non-radiative recombination of charge carriers—and are responsible for the ionic conductivity of perovskites that causes operational instability [42]. Thence, from the strong chemical coordination (proved by FTIR) and the suppressed non-radiative recombination of charge carriers (certified by TRPL) of SC-PEDOT: PSS-modified perovskite film, we could reasonable speculate the I  $  ^{-}  $  vacancy defects (in pristine perovskite) have been simultaneously passivated as claimed above. To further explore the charge transfer performance in device, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) of the PSCs is carried out and exhibited in Fig. 5c. The right inset shows the equivalent circuit composed of the series resistance (Rs) and the charge transfer resistance (Rct). Table 1 lists the equivalent circuit of all fitted parameters. The main arcs in the low-frequency region represent the electron–hole recombination resistance, which is significantly enhanced in the PSC with the SC-PEDOT:PSS-modification. This further confirms that the optimized morphology of the perovskite film could effectively block the electron–hole recombination.

The detail information of J-V hysteresis for 30 sets of devices is plotted in Fig. 6b. The hysteresis index is defined by the equation  $  H = \frac{PCE_{reverse} - PCE_{forward}}{PCE_{reverse}}  $  and can be optimized to be 0.03 for SC-PEDOT:PSS modified device. The small deviation indicates better reproducibility of SC-PEDOT:PSS modified PSCs [43,44]. The main reason for the reduced hysteresis is the enhanced perovskite film quality with the passivated defects could decrease the trap states and the channels for ion migration, simultaneously strengthen the charge transportation of the device [45]. It is confirmed that the SC-PEDOT:PSS derived PSC exhibits the champion PCE of 19.1%, while the pristine device only presents a 15.2% PCE. The  $  J_{sc}  $  (short circuit current),  $  V_{oc}  $  (open circuit voltage) and FF (fill factor) are all remarkably improved after introducing SC-PEDOT:PSS as “conductive adhesive” in perovskite. The significantly increased  $  V_{oc}  $  owing to the high crystallinity of perovskite grains (measured by XRD) and uniform perovskite film (verified by SEM and AFM), leads to the suppressed non-radiative recombination (confirmed by PL and TRPL). Synergistically result in the increased impressive  $  V_{oc}  $  (1.10V) of SC-PEDOT:PSS modified PSCs.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, a novel “conductive adhesive”, SC-PEDOT:PSS, is introduced into perovskite to fabricate PSCs with excellent photovoltaic performance and long-term stability. These improvements in photovoltaic properties and stability of devices are mainly owing to the following reasons: Firstly, the passivated perovskite defects via SC-PEDOT:PSS can reduce the inherent defect intensity, which obtains an impressive large grain-sized (the maximum is 1.1   $  \mu  $  m) morphology and remarkable crystallization of perovskite. Secondly, the neutral SC-PEDOT:PSS could greatly connect the uncoordinated lead ions (in perovskite) and oxygen ions (in long chain of polymer) without damaging the original structure of perovskite and strengthen stability of device. Thirdly, the work function of SC-PEDOT:PSS can closely match with that of the perovskite, leads to reducing the energy loss and facilitating the charge carrier transportation. As a result, a dramatic PCE (19.1%) of the SC-PEDOT:PSS modified PSC can be achieved, which much better than that of the pristine device (15.2%). Moreover, the moisture resistance and photostability of the device are both significantly improved with addition of SC-PEDOT:PSS. Notably, the entire PSCs fabrication is operated under air environment without glove box, which greatly reduces the production costs. Overall, this research provides a viable method for advancing commercialization of PSCs arising from the simplified fabrication condition, low-cost and high stability.
