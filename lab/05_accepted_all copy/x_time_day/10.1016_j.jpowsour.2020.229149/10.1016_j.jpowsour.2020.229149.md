# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved the highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.2% within the last few years, which is unparalleled in photovoltaic research field [1,2]. Apart from high efficiency, the device stability is another important issue needed to be solved for the future commercialization. The 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis (N,N-p-dimethoxy-phenylamino)-9,9'-spirobi fluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) is the most commonly used hole-transporter for preparing high efficient PSCs, yet the indispensable dopants of corrosive 4-tert-butyl pyridine (TBP) and hygroscopic lithium-bis (trifluoromethane) sulfonimide

Lithium bis (trifluoromethane sulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butyl pyridine (TBP) doped 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-p-dimethoxy-phenylamino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) is the most commonly used hole-transporter in perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Although the introduction of both Li-TFSI and TBP can significantly enhance the conductivity of spiro-OMeTAD and thereby improve the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of PSCs, the dopants unfortunately render hysteresis and reduce stabilities of PSCs. In this paper, we have synthesized hydrophobic p-type PbS nanocrystals and prepared a binary hole-transporter by mixing PbS and Spiro-OMeTAD (PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD), which therefore avoids the use of traditional hygroscopic Li-TFSI and volatile TBP dual additives. The PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD exhibits excellent conductivity and well-matched valence band with that of perovskite. PSCs incorporating PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD as the hole transporting layer (HTL) can obtain a highest PCE of  $ 20.11\% $  and show excellent long-term stability, which is much better than the controlled device with Li-TFSI and TBP doped Spiro-OMeTAD as the HTL.

Inorganic compounds, organic molecules, polymeric compounds, hybrid materials and other hole-transporters, have been used to substitute Spiro-OMeTAD   $  [8–16]  $  . Nevertheless, very few devices with these dopant-free substitutes show better photovoltaic performance than the ones with Spiro-OMeTAD hole-transporter. Inorganic hole-transporters with relatively low inherent conductivity and numerous surface defects will limit the photoelectric property of the devices. Organic polymer materials may have problems with processability difficulties and variations between batch-to-batch product   $  [17]  $  . Therefore, Binary hole-transporters may become one of the effective ways to solve the problem about hole-transporters of PSCs.

Here, we propose a novel route of preparing undoped Spiro-OMeTAD and PbS binary hole-transporter (PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD) to fabricate efficient and stable planar PSCs. PbS, as a traditional p-type direct bandgap semiconductor with a wide bandgap tuning range controlled by varying the particle size due to the large excitation Bohr radius (  $  \sim  $  18 nm), has narrow bandgap, high absorption coefficient, excellent chemical stability, facile solution-processing and other advantages, has been used in infrared photodetectors, infrared memory device, quantum dot solar cells, and even perovskite solar cells [18–26]. Tang and his co-workers applied PbS colloidal quantum dots (CQDs) as an effective hole-transporter for inverted planar PSCs and engineered the energy alignment through tuning the size of PbS CQDs, which can extend the absorption of perovskite to 1000 nm and improve the photoelectric performance of the PSCs [24]. Chen and his co-workers added precursor-capped PbS which were covered with   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   through the ligand-exchange and could be active nucleation sites to modulate the nucleation and growth of perovskite crystals [25]. Fang et al. deposited a (200) orientated PbS thin film between spiro-OMeTAD and Au electrode as buffer layer, which can improve the performance and enhance the stability of PSCs [26]. The PCE of optimized PSCs can increased to 19.85% from 18.79%, and it remains almost 100% after being stored for 1000 h in the air. Inspired by these works, we prepare PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD for fabricating PSCs with both high efficiency and good long-term stability. PSCs with PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD can yield 20.11% PCE with slight hysteresis, in addition, they show excellent long-term stability, which can maintain   $  \sim  $  83% of the initial PCE after 40 d without encapsulation.

# 2. Experimental section

Materials: Unless specifically noted, all chemical reagents were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used directly without further operation. Formamidinium iodide (FAI) and methylammonium bromide (MABr) salts were obtained from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp, China. Spiro-OMeTAD was supplied by Luminescence Technology Corp, Taiwan, China. Fluorine doped tin oxide conductive glasses (FTOs) with sheet resistance of  $ 15\Omega \mathrm{sq}^{-1} $  used as transparent conductive substrates were supplied by Liaoning Youxuan New Energy Technology Co., Ltd, China.

Synthesis of PbS nanoparticles: PbS nanoparticles (PbS NPs) were synthesized with a hydrothermal method   $  [27,28]  $  . In details, Pb(Ac)  $  _{2}  $  ·3H  $  _{2}  $  O aqueous solution (0.1 M), thioacetamide aqueous solution (1 M), HNO  $  _{3}  $   aqueous solution (0.1 M) were prepared in advance. 0.1 g cetyl trimethyl ammonium bromide (CTAB) and 7.8 mL of deionized water was added in a 20 mL glass bottle, then the mixture was placed in an ultrasonic disperser to be absolutely dissolved. Afterward, 0.5 mL HNO  $  _{3}  $   solution, 0.2 mL Pb(Ac)  $  _{2}  $  ·3H  $  _{2}  $  O solution, and 1.5 mL thioacetamide solution was added into the glass bottle in sequence. The bottle should be shaken after each additional reagent. Whereafter, the mixture was put into a muffle furnace and heated at 90 °C for 3 h. After being cooled down to room temperature, it was disposed by a centrifuging at 8500 rpm for 10 min, and washed with ethanol twice. The as-prepared PbS NPs were dissolved in chlorobenzene for further preparing PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD.

Device fabrication: FTOs were sequentially cleaned by isopropanol,

acetone, distilled water, ethanol, and finally UV-ozone irradiation for 30 min. The   $  SnO_{2}  $   electron transport layers and   $  Cs_{0.05}  $   (  $  MA_{0.15}FA_{0.85})_{0.95}Pb(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_{3}  $   perovskite absorption layers were prepared on the laser-patterned FTOs with the same methods as our formerly reported [29,30]. The pure Spiro-OMeTAD chlorobenzene solution [72.3 mg Spiro-OMeTAD dissolved in 1 mL chlorobenzene], traditional Spiro-OMeTAD solution [72.3 mg Spiro-OMeTAD, 28.8 μL TBP, 17.5 μL acetonitrile solution of Li-TFSI (520 mg Li-TSFI dissolved in 1 mL acetonitrile) and 1 mL chlorobenzene] and the PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD solution with different molar ratio of PbS NPs and pure Spiro-OMeTAD were spin-coated on the perovskite films at 4000 rpm for 30 s to prepare the pure Spiro-OMeTAD, traditional Spiro-OMeTAD and PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD HTLs based planar PSCs, respectively. Last, 100 nm Au electrodes were thermally evaporated on the hole-transporters under high vacuum through a shadow mask.

Characterization: The morphologies were observed by a scanning electron microscopy (SEM, SU8000) and a transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JEM-2100). The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were detected by a Bruker D8 Advance X-ray diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda = 1.5418 \AA  $  ). The ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) spectra were measured with a photoelectron spectrometer (ESCALAB 250 Xi, Thermo Fisher Scientific).

The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra were measured with a fluorescence spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and an  $ \mathrm{Omin - }\lambda $  Monochromator/Spectrograph (Zolix) with the time-correlated single-photon counting method (Pico harp 300), and the samples were excited by a wavelength of  $ 460~\mathrm{nm} $  from a Xenon lamp and by a wavelength of  $ 507~\mathrm{nm} $  from  $ 120~\mathrm{nJ} $  per  $ \mathrm{cm}^2 $  per pulse of a pulsed laser diode, respectively. The test results of PL and TRPL are matched with the above conductivity test, and the more excellent charge transport performance also makes PSCs-(PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD) obtaining more outstanding photoelectric performance.

# 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, we have synthesized cubic phase PbS with excellent crystallinity and prepared PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD binary hole-transporter. We demonstrated that PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD HTL has excellent conductivity, and the energy level is more matched with perovskite, which can reduce carrier loss and improve carrier transmission performance. In addition, the hole defect density and the charge accumulation in the interface between perovskite and HTL are significantly reduced. PSCs with PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD HTL can yield 20.11% of PCE with slight hysteresis. In addition, because PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD does not contain mobile ions like   $  Li^{+}  $  it has excellent hydrophobic property, PSCs based PbS/Spiro-OMeTAD HTL have excellent long-term stability, which can maintain   $  \sim83\%  $   of the initial PCE after 40 days without encapsulation. The use of inorganic hole transport materials and organic Spiro-OMeTAD to form a binary hole-transporter also provides new ideas for the development of hole transport materials.
