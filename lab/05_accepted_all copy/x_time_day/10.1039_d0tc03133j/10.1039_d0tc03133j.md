# Abstract

Ruddlesden-Popper type two-dimensional (2D) perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been intensively studied because of the high power-conversion-efficiency (PCE) and long-term stability. In this work, we improved the performance of 2D PSCs by adding anti-solvent (chlorobenzene) into the perovskite precursors. The use of chlorobenzene could promote the crystallization of the 2D perovskite by accelerating the formation of perovskite nuclei. Consequently, the quality of the 2D perovskite film was improved with enhanced crystallinity, enlarged grains and reduced pinholes. By adding  $ 20\% $  chlorobenzene into the precursor, average PCE of the 2D PSCs was significantly improved from 9.83 to  $ 12.91\% $ , with simultaneous enhancements in open-circuit voltage, short-circuit current density and fill factor. Additionally, the PCE degradation of the 2D PSCs was also reduced from 21 to  $ 13\% $  after a 15 days duration, indicating the improved long-term stability. The best device processed from chlorobenzene added precursor exhibited a highest PCE of  $ 13.35\% $  with a stable power output and negligible hysteresis. Our research provides a simple and effective way for improving the performance of 2D PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

During the past few years, organic–inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been intensively investigated and rapidly developed due to their superior performance of high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and convenient fabrication process.  $  ^{1-3}  $   Since the first use of perovskite material into solar cells by Miyasaka et al. in 2009, PCE of the PSCs was significantly improved from initial 3.8% to recently certified 25.2%,  $  ^{4,5}  $   which shows a great potential for commercial applications.

Besides their high performance in power conversion, the environmental stability of the PSCs has to be further improved to satisfy the requirement of commercialization.  $  ^{6-11}  $   However, the widely investigated 3-dimensional (3D) perovskite   $  \left(\mathrm{MAPbX}_{3}, \mathrm{MA} = \mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}, \mathrm{X} = \mathrm{Cl}, \mathrm{Br}, \text{or I}\right)  $   materials usually suffer the problem of hydration and decomposition under ambient conditions.  $  ^{8,10}  $   To improve the stability of the PSCs, finding more stable perovskite materials become an important aspect. For example,   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   (X = Br or I) based all inorganic PSCs have been developed, which shows a higher stability than conventional   $  MAPbX_{3}  $   based PSCs.  $  ^{12,13}  $   However, preparing all inorganic perovskites usually require a high temperature (about 250 °C), which could increase the cost of mass production.  $  ^{7,12}  $   Moreover, the larger band-gap (over 1.8 eV) of all inorganic perovskites would limit the light absorption of the PSCs, which is a restriction for achieving high PCEs.  $  ^{8,13-15}  $   Recently, two-dimensional (2D) or quasi-2D perovskite

12

materials (Ruddlesden-Popper perovskites) with good environmental stability and tunable band gaps have been synthesized for solar applications.  $  ^{16-20}  $   For example,   $  (\mathrm{BA})_{2}(\mathrm{MA})_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{3n+1}  $   perovskites, in which n is an integer and BA stands for   $  \mathrm{CH}_{3}(\mathrm{CH}_{2})_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}  $  , can be simply prepared using one-step solution method at a lower temperature (100 °C).  $  ^{16,17}  $   Up to now, PCE (< 15%) of the 2D PSCs is still lower than that (> 20%) of conventional 3D PSCs, which needs further improvement.  $  ^{16-20}  $

Improving the quality of perovskite films plays critical roles in fabricating high-performance PSCs.  $  ^{2,3,6}  $   For one-step solution processed perovskites, usually rapid nuclei formation and retarded crystal growth are beneficial for preparing high quality perovskite films, which should be carefully controlled.  $  ^{21-24}  $   To accelerate the nuclei formation, dropping some amount of anti-solvents onto the samples during the spin-coating process is widely used for the preparation of 2D and 3D perovskites.  $  ^{24-27}  $   The anti-solvents can be miscible with the host solvent of perovskite precursor, but insoluble for the solutes (such as PbI  $  _{2}  $   and MAI).  $  ^{24,25}  $   As a result, the anti-solvents could quickly remove residual solvent from the samples, which function as the driving force for realizing the supersaturated state during the progress of crystallization.  $  ^{21}  $   For example, by using chlorobenzene (CB) or diethyl ether as the anti-solvent, the quality of perovskite films was improved with reduced pinholes and enhanced crystallinity, which further largely improved the PCE of the PSCs from 1.5% to 19.4%.  $  ^{24,25}  $   However, experimentally dropping anti-solvent onto the samples during the spin-coating process is not easy to operate, because the dropping time and target position should be carefully and exactly controlled.  $  ^{24}  $   Moreover, to fabricate a batch of PSCs, a large amount of anti-solvent would be consumed (e. g. CB, about 200   $  \mu  $  L for each sample),  $  ^{24,27}  $   which is quite harmful to the environment. Considering these disadvantages, directly mixing some amount of anti-solvent into perovskite precursor may be more convenient, which has not been investigated for 2D PSCs. Therefore, it is very important to study the effect of adding anti-solvent into precursor on the quality of 2D perovskite film and the performance of 2D PSCs.

In this study, we introduce the investigation of adding anti-solvent (CB) into 2D perovskite precursor for improving the performance of 2D PSCs. We find that, upon using CB, the quality of the 2D perovskite film was improved with enlarged grains, reduced pinholes and enhanced crystallinity. The anti-solvent of CB could promote the crystallization of the 2D perovskite by accelerating the formation of perovskite nuclei during the spin-coating process. Consequently, average PCE of the 2D PSCs was significantly improved from 9.83 to  $ 12.91\% $  by adding  $ 20\% $  CB into the precursor. This is mainly caused by the simultaneous enhancements in short-circuit current density  $ (J_{\mathrm{sc}} $ , from 16.7 to  $ 18.5\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ ) and fill factor (FF, from 58.3 to  $ 67.1\% $ ). Additionally, the PCE degradation of the 2D PSCs was also reduced from 21 to  $ 13\% $  after a 15 days duration, indicating the improved long-term stability. The best device processed from CB added precursor exhibited a highest PCE of  $ 13.35\% $  with a stable power output and negligible hysteresis. Our results indicate that directly adding a small amount of anti-solvent into the precursor can significantly improve the performance of 2D PSCs.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1 Device fabrication

[6,6]-phenyl C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) and Methylammonium iodide (MAI) were purchased from Nano-C Inc. (USA) and Ying Kou You Xuan Trade Co., Ltd (China), respectively. Butylammonium iodide (BAI),   $  PbI_{2}  $  , N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), 2-propanol, and CB were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (USA). Poly(bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine) (PTAA) and bathocuproine (BCP) were purchased from EM index (Korea) and Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. (China), respectively. As shown in Fig. 1(a), the 2D PSCs were fabricated on the patterned indium-tin oxide (ITO) coated glass substrates with the structure of glass/ITO/PTAA/perovskite/PCBM/BCP/Ag. First, the PTAA solution (in CB, 10 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) was spin coated onto the pre-cleaned ITO glasses at 3500 RPM. Then the samples were

thermally annealed at 80 °C to fully remove the residual solvent. The 2D perovskite precursor solutions were prepared by dissolving BAI, MAI, and PbI $ _2 $  (molar ratio = 2:3:4) in pure DMF and different mix-solvents (DMF:CB = 90:10, DMF:CB = 80:20, and DMF:CB = 70:30 in volume) at a total concentration of 40 wt%. The perovskite precursor was spin coated onto the PTAA layers at 4000 RPM in a N $ _2 $  filled glove-box. Then the 2D (BA) $ _2 $ (MA) $ _3 $ Pb $ _4 $ I $ _{13} $  perovskite films were formed after thermally annealed at 100 °C. Then BCP solution (in 2-propanol at 0.5 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ ) was spin coated onto the perovskite layers at 4500 RPM. The fabrication of the 2D PSCs was finished by thermally evaporating 100 nm Ag onto the samples. The effective working area of the 2D PSCs was 0.1 cm $ ^2 $ , which was determined by the ITO and Ag electrodes. The electron-only space charge limited current density (SCLC) devices were fabricated using a structure of glass/Ag/perovskite/PCBM/Ag.

# 2.2 Characterization

Ultraviolet–visible (UV–vis) absorption spectra were measured using Perkin Elmer Lambda 750 (USA). X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were obtained using an X-ray diffractometer (Panalytical, Netherlands). The cross-section image of the PSC and top view image of perovskite surfaces were measured using an SU8020 scanning electron microscope (SEM, Hitachi, Japan), operated at an acceleration voltage of 8 kV. Photoluminescence (PL) behaviors were characterized by a spectrometer (FLS920, Edinburgh Instruments, UK). The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) spectra of the electron-only devices were performed in a dark environment using an electrochemical workstation (CH Instruments, USA). The current density–voltage (J–V) characteristics of the devices were measured for the irradiation intensity of 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  (AM1.5). The incident photon-to-current efficiency (IPCE) was measured using a Solar Cell IPCE measurement system (Solar Cell Scan 100, Zolix, China).

To further analyze the crystallization of the perovskite films, we measured the UV–vis absorption, XRD patterns and PL spectra of the perovskite films, which were prepared from precursor without and with 20% CB. As shown in Fig. 4(a), the optical absorption peak at about 606 nm was enhanced, which indicates the enhanced crystal quality of the perovskite film from another aspect.  $  ^{22}  $   The enhanced crystallinity can be proved by the XRD measurement. Fig. 5 shows the progress of spin-coating and thermal annealing, which are very important procedures for influencing the quality of the perovskite films. Generally, perovskite crystallization can be consisted of two processes, which are nuclei formation and perovskite growth.  $  ^{[21,24]}  $   The nuclei formation step occurs at the initial stage of spin-coating process (usually several seconds). Then the perovskite planes grow up based on these nuclei during the later spin coating and thermal annealing processes.  $  ^{[21]}  $   The existence of CB in precursor can accelerate the formation of a supersaturated state during the initial spin-coating process, which further promotes the nuclei formation of perovskite. Then the perovskite grows into larger crystal planes during the later spin-coating and thermal annealing processes.  $  ^{[24]}  $   The mechanism is similar with that of dropping anti-solvent, which is beneficial for preparing high quality perovskite films.  $  ^{[21,24]}  $   Therefore, we attribute the improved morphology and crystallinity of the perovskite film to the accelerated nuclei formation upon adding CB into the perovskite precursor.

We also tested the environmental stability of the PSCs based on perovskite films processed from precursor without and with 20% CB. As shown in Fig. 6(d), after 15 days storage (without encapsulation, at 25 °C and 30–40% humidity in dark ambient condition), the control device retain a PCE of 7.76%, corresponding to a 21% degradation. The environmental stability improvement can be also explained by the improved quality of the perovskite film upon using CB. # 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we improved the performance of inverted 2D PSCs by adding some amount of anti-solvent (CB) into the precursor solutions. The existence of CB could promote the crystallization of the 2D perovskite by accelerating the formation of perovskite nuclei during the spin-coating process. As a result, average PCE of the PSCs was significantly improved from 9.83 to  $ 12.91\% $ , which mainly induced by the simultaneously improved  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  (from 16.7 to  $ 18.5\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ ) and FF (from 58.3 to  $ 67.1\% $ ). Maximum PCE was improved from 10.21 to  $ 13.35\% $  with a stable power output, which indicates the superior effect of adding CB.
