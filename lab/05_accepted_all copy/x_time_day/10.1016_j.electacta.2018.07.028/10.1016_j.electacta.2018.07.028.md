# 1. Introduction

Solar photovoltaic (PV) technology has the potential to make a considerable contribution to the solution of fossil energy depletion and the related environmental problems, and to be a truly sustainable candidate for future green energy sources. The remarkable latent capacity of organometal trihalide perovskite photoactive materials has recently been demonstrated in the production of efficient solar cells. Since Miyasaka et al. introduced the organic-inorganic hybrid   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   into the dye sensitized solar cells (DSSCs) for the first time in 2009, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been fleetly promoted from 3.8% to over 22%, being comparable to silicon (Si), cadmium telluride (CdTe), and copper indium gallium diselenide (CIGS) solar cells, in a little over seven years [1–4]. # ABSTRACT

We report a simple and low-cost approach to enhance the longstanding stability of the perovskite solar cell (PSC) through the application of low-temperature grown   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod (NR) arrays.   $  SnO_{2}  $   NR arrays were fabricated by an aqueous synthesis method in the presence of hydrochloric acid and using thin compact   $  SnO_{2}  $   as seeds. The influence of HCl concentration on the photovoltaic (PV) performance of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   NR array based PSC device was investigated. The formed crystalline   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods with square transversal-sections were oriented on the FTO facets in a well-defined and perpendicular fashion at an optimal HCl concentration. The optimized width and length for   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods were 30 nm and 150 nm, respectively. Consequently, efficient and stable   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod based perovskite devices were obtained with an average power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 16.57%. The   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod array based PSCs demonstrated considerably better ultraviolet (UV) and ambient air stability than the planar   $  SnO_{2}  $   and notably mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   solar cells. This paper provides a prospective scheme to grow high-quality three-dimensional   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays for highly stable and efficient PSCs.

However, perovskite solar cells suffer from challenging instabilities and degradation modes that limit their performance lifetimes. The ultraviolet (UV), ambient air and thermal stability of perovskite solar cells has to be tremendously promoted to become a commercial PV technology   $  [6,9,10]  $  .

Generally, PSCs consist of a transparent conducting oxide substrate (TCO), a n-type compact electron transporting layer (ETL) with or without mesoporous scaffold layer (MSL), an organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite layer, a p-type hole-transporting layer (HTL) and a metal back electrode. This finding demonstrates that ETL and HTL components are not to be neglected according to the photovoltaic behavior of the PSC device. Although highly efficient PSCs were completed at an easy rate for both TiO $ _2 $  and ZnO, both types of metal oxides still have some weak points that hinder the realization of stable and reliable PSCs. One of the main weaknesses for perovskite solar cells based on TiO $ _2 $  is a quick drop in PV parameters upon UV light-exposure [16–18]. This degradation is mainly caused by two phenomena. One is oxygen being desorbed from TiO $ _2 $ , which uncovers deep trap-states and aggravates the electronic features of TiO $ _2 $ , and the other is the photocatalytic characteristics of titanium dioxide, which exasperates the organic components in the PSC. UV-light exposure also modified the TiO $ _2 $  contact angle [19], which renders the manufacture procedures of perovskite photovoltaic absorber layers more difficult and less reproducible because the solution wetting and perovskite crystallization behaviors are closely associated with the surface energetics. Moreover, in the typical mesoscopic configuration with TiO $ _2 $  and spiro-MeOTAD as the electron transporting and hole transporting materials, respectively, PSCs have displayed a severe current-voltage hysteresis due to charge buildup at the interfaces between electron and hole selective contacts [20–22].

Recently, high-efficiency PSCs with a PCE of  $ 15.9\% $  have also been realized using sputtered  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  as the ETL [23].  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  seems to possess a promising prospect as a competitor. However,  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $ -based PSCs were reported to exhibit apparent thermal instability. The primary drawback when using  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  as an ETL is its chemical instability, which is a result of its basic nature and the resultant deprotonation of the perovskite surface [24]. Several works on PSCs with  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  as an ETL material have reported the acute degradation of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  ( $ \mathrm{MA} = $  methylammonium) into  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  upon annealing at even low temperatures (LT) [25,26], which raises concerns about the suitability of  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  in real world applications. It was found that  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  was thermally less stable on  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  than  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  [27].

Among various kinds of metal oxides, tin oxide (  $  SnO_{2}  $  ) appears to be a promising, stable and effective ETL candidate in constructing high performance and stability PSCs because of its high electron mobility (100–200   $  cm^{2}V^{-1}s^{-1}  $  ), wide band gap (3.8 eV), excellent chemical stability, high transparency, low conduction band edge (CBE) and high photostability under UV illumination [15,28,29].   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETLs can be processed at low temperatures in both mesoporous and compact morphology, with performance on par with the popular high-temperature-processed   $  TiO_{2}  $  . It has been reported that   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   grown on pure   $  SnO_{2}  $   exhibited higher thermal stability than the case on   $  TiO_{2}  $   and ZnO [30,31]. In a pioneering effort, Miyasaka et al. reported planar PSCs fabricated with spin-coated   $  SnO_{2}  $   as the ETL, which demonstrated a PCE of 13% and improved stability compared with   $  TiO_{2}  $ -based devices [30]. Baena et al. also investigated planar PSCs using   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETLs deposited by a low temperature atomic layer deposition process [32]. Recently, Anaraki et al. reported a low-temperature method to deposit   $  SnO_{2}  $   layers by combination of spin-coating and a chemical bath post-treatment (SC-CBD) for Cs/methylammonium (MA)/formamidinium (FA) mixed-cation lead mixed-halide perovskite solar cells. The obtained planar   $  SnO_{2}  $   Cs/MA/FA mixed-cation lead mixed-halide PSCs yielded a PCE of 20.7% [33]. The performance of the planar SC-CBD   $  SnO_{2}  $   PSCs has come close to mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   based PSCs. However, the speedy and badly commanded perovskite crystallization makes it burdensome to fabricate planar PSCs on a large scale for industrial application. A porous ETL can overcome this problem by changing the surface wettability to achieve uniform perovskite coverage over large areas. Thus, utilization of a mesoporous ETL may further increase performance by increasing electron extraction [34]. It is worth especial mention that planar   $  SnO_{2}  $   based PSCs have been reported to be less stable than mesoporous   $  SnO_{2}  $   (m-  $  SnO_{2}  $  ).

PSCs [35,36]. This fact underlines the significance of powerful  $ m $ - $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  PSCs for highly stable PSCs and industrial application.

In 2015, Li et al. reported mesoporous   $  SnO_{2}  $   based PSCs with a PCE of 6.5%. Their m-  $  SnO_{2}  $   ETLs were prepared by high-temperature sintering of   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanoparticles [29]. Roose et al. also reported a high-temperature processed m-  $  SnO_{2}  $   electrodes through the use of a structure directing block-copolymer and achieved a PCE of 7.3% [36]. Then, higher PCEs (16.17% and 16.25%) have been reached employing low-temperature processed pure or Y-doped   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanosheets [37,38]. Moreover, one-dimensional (1D)   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods are valued for their speedy electron transport and high surface area, and promising in constructing highly efficient and stable PSCs. So far, highly ordered   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays have been widely reported for sensor, field emission and energy storage applications [39,40]. Fabrication of   $  SnO_{2}  $   oriented nanorod arrays usually involves high-temperature process and expensive vapor deposition techniques such as rapid oxidation, chemical vapor deposition, thermal evaporation, and plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition [40–42]. Hitherto, only one aqueous synthesis approach has been reported for the formation of well-defined   $  SnO_{2}  $   NR arrays without using template techniques [43]. In PSC applications, there is only one paper that reported   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanotube array ETL based PSCs [44], in which   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanotubes arrays were synthesized by in-situ template self-etching strategy with ZnO nanorods as sacrifice templates.

In this work, a simple and productive strategy is presented to heighten the longstanding steadiness of the PSC by using low-temperature grown   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays. Large three-dimensional (3D)   $  SnO_{2}  $   arrays of ordered nanorods and crystalline quality were fabricated from a controlled aqueous process without using template or surfactant. The microstructure and morphology of   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods were controlled by changing the HCl concentration during the aqueous growth. The influence of HCl concentration on the PV performance of   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod based PSC devices is investigated. The growth mechanisms of 3D   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays are discussed. The optimized width and length for   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods are about 30 nm and 150 nm, respectively. Consequently, we were able to accomplish efficient and stabilized   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod based PSCs with an average PCE of 16.57%, competing with the performance of m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   PSCs fabricated in our lab. We demonstrate here that   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod array based PSCs are significantly more unfluctuating than the planar   $  SnO_{2}  $   PSC and notably m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   based devices after ambient air exposure for 5 days and intense UV-irradiation.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Chemicals

All the chemicals were bought from Aldrich or Aladdin Industrial Corporation and used without any further purification.

# 2.2. Fabrication of solar cells

Patterned Fluorine doped tin oxide (FTO) coated glass slides were first cleaned via a multi-step solvent cleaning procedure under sonication, which started from detergent and followed by deionized (DI) water, acetone and ethanol. The cleaned FTO substrates were positioned in a home-made UV-  $  O_{3}  $   instrument for 25 min before use. The   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod array electrodes were prepared in two steps by changing the procedures reported in Refs.38, 45. Firstly, to form compact (c-  $  SnO_{2}  $  ) layers,   $  SnO_{2}  $   precursor solution (0.05 M   $  SnCl_{2}\cdot2H_{2}O  $   in ethanol) was spin-coated on the clean FTO substrates at 4000 rpm for 30 s and further calcined at 180 °C for 1 h. Secondly, the resulted sample covered with   $  SnO_{2}  $   crystal seeds was immersed in a 100 mL aqueous solution of   $  SnCl_{4}\cdot5H_{2}O  $  .

The perovskite precursor mixture was prepared by mixing MAI (0.1975 g) powders and lead iodide  $  PbI_{2}  $  (0.5785 g) in GBL (700 μL) and DMSO (300 μL) at 60 °C for 12 h. The precursor mixture was dropped on the formed compact  $  SnO_{2}  $  ETL and NRs samples, and afterwards, whirled at 4000 rpm for 40 s. After 10 s of circumvolving, 500 μL of anhydrous diethyl ether was directly dripped onto the spinning precursor film. The MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  film was grown after annealing at 100 °C for 20 min in the glove box. Finally, 80 nm thick AgAl alloy electrode was thermally evaporated onto the HTL and the active area of the PSC cell is 0.10 cm  $  ^{2}  $  [47].

# 2.3. Characterization

The morphology and structure of the samples were examined by a high-resolution field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM, Hitachi S4800) and X-ray diffraction (XRD, Holland Panalytical PRO PW3040/60) with Cu Kα radiation (V = 30 kV, I = 25 mA). The chemical composition of SnO₂ nanorods was studied by XPS spectrometer (VG Scientific, ESCALAB 250). Optical spectra of the perovskite films on compact and mesoporous SnO₂ ETLs were characterized using an ultraviolet–visible light (UV–Vis) spectrometer (Hitachi, U-3010). Photoluminescence (PL) spectra were recorded at room temperature using a HORIBA Jobin Yvon fluoromax-4 fluorescence spectrophotometer with an excitation wavelength of 507 nm. The time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra were collected by using a picosecond time-correlated single-photon counting (TCSPC) apparatus. Concisely, PL was excited using a super-continuum pulsed fiber laser (Fianium, SC400-pp-4) with a pulse duration of about 10 ps at a repetition rate of 20 MHz. A single photon counting photomultiplier detector (Picoquant, PMA-165) with a stand-alone TCSPC electronic system (Picoquant, PicoHarp 300) was employed to collect the signals. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements of the PSCs were carried out using a galvanostat (PG30.FRA2, Autolab, EcoChemie B. V Utrecht, Netherlands). Photocurrent density–voltage (J-V) measurements were completed by an AM 1.5 solar simulator equipped with a 1000 W xenon lamp (Model no. 91192, Oriel, USA). The solar simulator was calibrated by using a standard silicon cell (Newport, USA). The light intensity was 100 mW cm⁻² on the surface of the test cell. During the characterization of device photovoltaic performance, a metal aperture mask with an opening of about 0.09 cm² was used. External quantum efficiency (EQE) measurements (74125, Oriel, USA) were also performed for these cells.

# 3. Results and discussion

To investigate the growth process of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  nanorod arrays on the FTO substrate using the proposed method, HCI concentration-dependent experiments were carried out, in which samples were synthesized at different HCI amounts while keeping other

experimental conditions the same. A series of interesting morphological evolutions of products were obtained. Fig. 1 (a) – (h) show the top surface and cross-sectional SEM images of   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod films grown on FTO substrates with different amounts of HCl in a 100 mL mixed aqueous solution over a growth time of 12 h. As shown in Fig. 1 (a), at a low HCl amount of 3 mL, dark-grey sparse precipitates were formed on the FTO substrate and the texture of FTO facets can be seen clearly. The dimension of the formed nano   $  SnO_{2}  $   was very small and the coverage of the nano   $  SnO_{2}  $   on the FTO substrate was very low. With an increased HCl volume of 4 mL, the nuclei rapidly developed into many regimental petals. With a further increased HCl amount of 5 mL, uniform and oriented nanorods with square sections were grown on the FTO substrate, which can be observed more clearly in the inset of Fig. 1(c). The width and the length of the formed   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods are about 30 nm and 150 nm, respectively, as shown in Fig. 1(c) and (f). However, when the HCl amount was increased further from 5 mL, the nanorods became aggregated, and the nanorods became smaller and shorter as shown in Fig. 1 (d, g) and (e, h). As we observed, the   $  SnO_{2}  $   NR film prepared at 6 mL HCl is thickest (  $  \sim  $  350 nm in thickness). The microstructural and morphological properties of the synthesized   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays have a key part to play in the photovoltaic performance of PSC devices as its   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanorod counterpart does due to the pronounced susceptibility of perovskite crystallization behavior to the interfacial structure and the carrier separation and recombining at the   $  MAPbI_{3}/metal  $   oxide NR interface [45,48–51]. The uniform and aligned nanorod arrays prepared with a HCL amount of 5 mL will facilitate the stuffing of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   semiconductor into the   $  SnO_{2}  $   pores or interspace between   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods and enhance physical and electrical contact with perovskite at the   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod ETL/perovskite interface to form high-quality absorbers.

Highly crystalline   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods were fabricated straight on the FTO slides using the proposed aqueous method at an appropriate HCl concentration as shown in Fig. 1. The formed crystalline   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods with square sections were oriented on the FTO facets in a well-defined and perpendicular fashion. The formation of crystalline   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays conformed to the theoretical expectations from thermodynamics and symmetry principles and arguments of crystals [43]. We used a quartz glass to grow the   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods with 5 mL HCl in order to get   $  SnO_{2}  $   signals only from the nanorods, rather than from the FTO substrate. The formed   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods only exhibited a rutile crystallographic phase, and no impurity peaks were observed, as shown in Fig. 1 (i) [52]. All of the XRD peaks are reasonably well matched with the standard of the rutile type tetragonal structure of   $  SnO_{2}  $   (JCPDS 41-1445). The diffraction peaks at positions 26.66°, 34.04°, 37.88°, 39.10°, 51.72°, 54.66°, 58.08°, 61.56°, 65.02°, 65.74°, 71.38° and 78.38° correspond to   $  SnO_{2}  $   crystallographic planes of (110), (101), (200), (111), (211), (220), (002), (221), (112), (301), (202) and (321), respectively, as indicated by Fig. 1 (i). No impurity peaks were detected, clearly stating that the grown   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays were highly pure and crystalline. The XRD pattern corroborates that the   $  SnO_{2}  $   NR is a single-crystalline rutile with a preferred growth direction along the [101] direction.

To further examine and analyze the composition, XPS spectra were measured from the   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod films prepared on Si substrates. Fig. 1 (j) and (k) represent XPS survey scans for the   $  SnO_{2}  $   naonrod sample with 5 mL HCl. The high-resolution XPS spectrum of O1s is shown in Fig. 1 (k). From Fig. 1 (k), the peaks corresponding to binding energies for Sn   $  3d_{3/2}  $   and Sn   $  3d_{5/2}  $   appeared at 494.9 eV and 486.7 eV, respectively, leading to a spin-orbit coupling of 8.45 eV. The values correspond to the binding energies of the   $  Sn^{4+}  $   ion [53], suggesting the formation of   $  SnO_{2}  $  .

The stability of perovskite solar cells is a leading issue restricting their telluric applications. We measured and tested the stability of un-encapsulated PSC devices based on the compact and the nanorod array   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETLs. Thus, the stability properties of planar and mesoporous scaffold   $  SnO_{2}  $   based PSCs were compared. First, we studied the influence of ambient conditions, especially the humidity in the air, on the PV performance of the PSCs. During this long-term stability measurements of PSC devices, the formed solar cells were stored in a simple opaque storage box, not a drying cupboard, under 55% - 80% relative humidity at 25 °C and their PV performance was kept under surveillance over the course of 5 days. The ambient stability of unsealed PSCs based on the compact   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer, the optimal   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod array MSL and the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   (m-   $  TiO_{2}  $  ) ETL was given in Fig. 6 (a). It can be seen that the   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod array MSL solar cell exhibited a significantly improved stability. The PCE of the optimal   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod array based PSC remained about 70% of the beginning value even after 120 h of storage in ambient surroundings, indicating that the device has an outstanding long-term stability. However, both the planner   $  SnO_{2}  $   and m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   solar cells displayed a rapid degradation over the same period in ambient surroundings. Both devices cannot endure the ambient air test and showed a PCE of almost zero after 5 days. As we observed, nanorod array m-  $  SnO_{2}  $   based PSCs had superior stability than the commonly used planner   $  SnO_{2}  $   and m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   PSCs. Our results are consistent with those reported in Refs. [35,36].

# 4. Conclusions

We have reported a simple and low-cost strategy to enhance the longstanding stableness of the perovskite solar cell by using low-temperature grown   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays.   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods have prepared by an aqueous synthesis method in the presence of HCl and using thin c-  $  SnO_{2}  $   as seeds. It is found that the HCl concentration in the aqueous Tin (IV) salt solution greatly impacts the formation of   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods, and hence significantly affects the   $  V_{OC}  $ ,   $  J_{SC}  $   and FF of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays based PSC device. Crystalline   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods with square transversal-sections have been fabricated directly on the FTO facets in a well-defined and perpendicular fashion at an optimal HCl concentration. The optimized width and length for   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorods were 30 nm and 150 nm, respectively. As a result, highly stabilized and efficient   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod based PSCs were obtained with an average PCE of 16.57%, competing the performance of m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   based PSCs. The   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod array based PSCs attained much better UV and ambient air stability than the planar   $  SnO_{2}  $   and m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   solar cells after being subjected to the influence of ambient air and strong UV-irradiation. A promising approach has been demonstrated to grow high-quality 3D   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays for highly stable and efficient PSCs.
