# Highlights:

1. Two simple piperazine derivates HTMs with   $  p-\pi  $   conjugated structure were synthesized.

2. Two HTMs exhibit good thermal stabilities, suitable HOMO levels and wide band gaps.

3. The solution-processed PSC based on dopant-free Me-QTPA achieved a PCE of 9.07%.

4. The PSCs with dopant-free Me-QTPA remain almost constant after 600 hours with a contact angle of  $ 101.6^{\circ} $  to water.

Abstract: Two simple hole-transporting materials, Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA, which consist of  $  p - \pi  $  conjugated structure, have been synthesized and studied in solid-state perovskite solar cells. Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA show outstanding thermal stabilities and appropriate HOMO levels; in addition, these two materials show wide band gaps, thus they can block the electron transport and hence suppress the carrier recombination. The solution-processed  $  \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3  $ -based device using dopant-free Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA can achieve a power conversion efficiency of  $  9.07\%  $  and  $  8.16\%  $ , respectively. The perovskite solar cells with dopant-free Me-QTPA show better performance than the cells with dopant-free spiro-OMeTAD, especially in long-term stability. The power conversion efficiency for the perovskite solar cells with dopant-free Me-QTPA remains almost constant after 600 hours. The dopant-free Me-QTPA layer shows strong hydrophobicity with a contact angle of  $  101.6^{\circ}  $  to water, which indicates that Me-QTPA has a promising long-term stability at room temperature.

Keywords: Perovskite solar cell; Hole-transporting material;   $  p-\pi  $   conjugated; Piperazine derivatives; Dopant-free.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cell (PSC) has attracted tremendous interest due to the change in high power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) which increase from less than  $ 3.8\% $  to over  $ 20\% $  within last six years [1-4]. Hole-transporting material (HTM) plays an important role in perovskite solar cells because the cells with HTM can achieve excellent performance by reducing charge recombination processes. [5,6]. To date, the most widely used and effective HTM is the wide band gap 2,2',7,7'-tetra-kis(N,N-di- $ p $ -methoxyphenylamino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD). However, the synthesis and purification steps of spiro-OMeTAD are very complicated, which results in high cost of the material and restricts potential for large scale production. In this aspect, several new types of efficient HTMs have been explored as alternative HTMs for perovskite solar cells, which have shown excellent PCEs above  $ 10\% $  [7,8]. Organic and inorganic HTMs [9,10] have attracted more attention and developed rapidly for the application in PSCs. The organic HTMs can mainly include polymers [11,12] and small molecular HTMs [13-15]. In comparison, small molecular HTMs have advantages, which include easy synthesis, purification, low production cost and good performance in the solar cell. Some of the small molecular HTMs have shown comparable PCEs when spiro-OMeTAD is used as reference material. However, most of them still need some additives or dopants to achieve high PCEs just like the traditional spiro-OMeTAD. Dopants have disadvantages in stability for the PSCs since they are hydrophilic, which can cause moisture-induced degradation [16-20]. The development of dopant-free HTMs is very important for stable perovskite solar cells.

In this work, we present two simple molecules with  $  p - \pi  $  conjugated structure that are built up by electron-rich piperazine derivates  $  N  $ -bridge connecting triarylamine donors, named Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA, which can be obtained by an easy C-N coupling reaction. The synthetic routes and chemical structures of the HTMs are shown in Scheme 1. N-bridge can reduce the conjugation system of molecules, thereby widening the band gap of materials appropriately. On the other hand, the electron-rich piperazine derivatives are introduced for adjusting their HOMO levels to well match with  $  \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3  $  and Au. Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA play a key role in charge separation and the collection process. In solution-processed  $  \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3  $ -based perovskite solar cells, devices using dopant-free Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA afford power conversion efficiencies of  $  9.07\%  $  and  $  8.16\%  $ , respectively. While under the same conditions, a device fabricated with doped spiro-OMeTAD displays a power conversion efficiency of  $  13.65\%  $ . The devices with dopant-free Me-QTPA show more stability than the devices with doped spiro-OMeTAD after 600 hours. For the device with doped spiro-OMeTAD, the PCEs decrease significantly to  $  72\%  $  due to the decrease of short-circuit current density ( $  J_{sc}  $ ), open-circuit voltage ( $  V_{oc}  $ ) and fill factor (FF) which is  $  12\%  $ ,  $  14\%  $  and  $  14\%  $ , respectively. At room temperature, values of  $  J_{sc}  $ ,  $  V_{oc}  $  and FF can be remained almost constant in the device with dopant-free Me-QTPA. The result indicates Me-QTPA has a

promising long-term stability, which may be a new method to improve stability of PSCs.

# 2.3. Solar cell fabrication

FTO-coated glass substrates were washed with detergent. After rinsing with distilled water, the substrates were further cleaned with deionized water and then with ethanol. Finally, the substrates were dried in air. Then, a dense blocking layer of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and mesoporous layer of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  were subsequently deposited by spin-coating method according to reported literature [22]. The deposition process of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite film on the mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer was obtained by two-step methods as reported [23]. For the hole-transporting layer, the Me-QTPA or Me-BPZTPA was dissolved in chlorobenzene at  $ 10\mathrm{mg / mL} $  and then spin-coated on the mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $  film at 2500 rpm for 30 seconds. At the same time, spiro-OMeTAD solutions were prepared by dissolving spiro-OMeTAD in  $ 1\mathrm{mL} $  chlorobenzene at concentration of  $ 80~\mathrm{mg / mL} $  with the addition of  $ 30~\mu \mathrm{L} $  4-tert-butylpyridine,  $ 18~\mu \mathrm{L} $  of  $ 520~\mathrm{mg~mL^{-1}} $  lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulphonyl)imide acetonitrile solution. [24]. Finally,  $ 80~\mathrm{nm} $  of gold electrode was deposited on the top of the device to form the back contact through thermal evaporation under high vacuum.

# 3. Results and discussions

# 3.1. Measurement of the HTMs

Fig. 1(a) shows the UV-Vis absorption and fluorescence spectra of Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA measured in THF solution. A summary of the resulting data is given in Table 1. The absorption peaks (  $  \lambda_{abs}  $  ) of Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA are observed at 313 and 292 nm, respectively. Consequently, the   $  \lambda_{abs}  $   of Me-QTPA is shown to be red shifted by about 21 nm as contrasted with that of Me-BPZTPA. This bathochromic shift can be attributed to the benzopiperazine core being partially involved in the n-  $  \pi  $   conjugation in Me-QTPA. Besides, two more   $  \lambda_{abs}  $   of Me-BPZTPA at 400 and 420 nm can be due to the enhanced   $  \pi-\pi  $   intermolecular interaction by the phenonaphthazine core. The fluorescence spectrum of Me-BPZTPA shows a maximum emission at 436 nm and a

larger stoke's shift (144 nm) compared with that of Me-QTPA (93 nm), reflecting the big structure difference between ground and excited states of Me-BPZTPA, which indicates that the molecule may be flexible [25].

The thermal stability of Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA is analyzed through differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) (Fig. S7). The results show excellent thermal stability of two HTMs, with high melting points ( $ T_{m} $ , 140.9 °C for Me-QTPA and 250.0 °C for Me-BPZTPA) and glass transition temperatures ( $ T_{g} $ , 92.2 °C for Me-QTPA and 146.7 °C for Me-BPZTPA). The thermal stability of Me-BPZTPA can be improved by the introduction of the planar phenonaphthazine core, which is in favor of the practical application in device.

Density functional theory (DFT) calculation confirmed by Gaussian program has been employed to predict optoelectronic properties of HTMs. The results give insight into the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of Me-QTPA, Me-BPZTPA and spiro-OMeTAD, as shown in Fig. 1(b). The calculated values have been summarized in Table 1, which makes it clear that the energy of the HOMO levels of all HTMs are very similar. It's known that spiro-OMeTAD used as HTM exhibits appropriate HOMO level for perovskite solar cells, indicating that the HOMO levels of two HTMs can also match well with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and Au.

Furthermore, photoemission yield spectroscopy (PYS) (Fig. S8) applied to each of the HTMs is recorded in order to determine their experimental energy levels. The HOMO levels are determined to be -5.25 and -5.20 eV for Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA, respectively, which indicates that the two HTMs have energetics favorable for hole transfer. The optical band gap   $  E_{g}  $   is calculated to be 3.25 and 2.84 eV by the absorption onset of the corresponding absorption spectrum for Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA, respectively. Thus, the value of LUMO levels of Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA is determined to be -2.00 and -2.36 eV, respectively. The values are higher than that of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (-3.92 eV), indicating that the electron transportation from perovskite to the Au electrode can be effectively blocked. In the end, the time-of-flight (TOF) (Fig. S9) transient hole-current measurement is applied to measure the hole mobilities of HTMs. At room temperature, the hole mobilities of Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA are   $  5.18\times10^{-4}  $   and   $  2.39\times10^{-4}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $   at applied electric fields of   $  2.0\times10^{5}  $   V cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , respectively.

# 3.2. Measurement of the HTMs for perovskite solar cells

# 3.2.1. Measurement of photovoltaic performance

Perovskite solar cells are fabricated with the structure of  $ \mathrm{FTO / TiO_2 / CH_3NH_3PbI_3 / HTM / Au} $  with dopant-free Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA as the hole-transporting layer. Fig. 2(a) and (b) display the perovskite solar cell device architecture and its SEM image of a cross section of a solar cell. The thickness of the mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer infiltrated with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  capping layer is  $ 400~\mathrm{nm} $  and  $ 300~\mathrm{nm} $ , respectively. The thickness of the HTMs for the Me-QTPA, Me-BPZTPA and spiro-OMeTAD is  $ 100~\mathrm{nm} $ . In the end, about  $ 80~\mathrm{nm} $ -thickness Au electrode is on the top of the devices.

Fig. 2(c) displays the current density-voltage  $ (J - V) $  curves of PSCs utilizing Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA as HTMs and the photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table 2. The reproducibility of the cells' performance for the dopant-free Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA is evaluated by testing photovoltaic parameters of ten cells. Related data is presented in Table S1. As can be seen, the PSCs with Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA show a relatively similar photovoltaic performance. By using dopant-free Me-QTPA as the HTM, the PSC can present a PCE of  $ 9.07\% $  with a short-circuit photocurrent density  $ (J_{sc}) $  of  $ 14.58\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , an open-circuit photovoltage  $ (V_{oc}) $  of  $ 917.1\mathrm{mV} $  and a fill factor (FF) of 0.678. The device with dopant-free Me-BPZTPA exhibits a  $ J_{sc} $  of  $ 16.26\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , a  $ V_{oc} $  of 927.6 mV and a poor FF of 0.540, yielding a PCE of  $ 8.16\% $ . For comparison, the reference device without HTM shows a  $ J_{sc} $  of  $ 12.47\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , a  $ V_{oc} $  of  $ 843.9\mathrm{mV} $ , a FF of 0.673 with a PCE of  $ 7.09\% $ . The introduction of HTM improves the performance of the devices in terms of the values of  $ J_{sc}, V_{oc} $  and PCE. Obviously, the presence of HTMs is advantageous in inhibiting electron transfer from  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  to the Au electrode and facilitating the hole

transport to Au electrode. The devices with dopant-free Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA exhibit better photovoltaic performance than the devices with dopant-free spiro-OMeTAD, due to the fact that spiro-OMeTAD exhibits a low hole mobility in its pristine form. [26]. In the absence of dopants, spiro-OMeTAD-based devices generate a PCE of merely  $ 5.5\% $ . However, the PCE of the devices with the two doped HTMs is lower than that of the devices with common doped spiro-OMeTAD, which can achieve the PCE of  $ 13.65\% $ . The dopant has positive effect on spiro-OMeTAD in generating free carriers, but it might not show similar influence on Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA, which might disrupt the molecular packing. [27, 28]. Since the device with Me-QTPA has shown better performance than the device with Me-BPZTPA, the SEM of the Me-QTPA thin film with and without dopants has been exhibited to confirm the influence of the dopants on the film coverage (Fig. S10). The thin film has been seriously damaged by the dopants. Maybe it is one of the reasons for worse performance of the device based on doped Me-QTPA than the device based on dopant-free Me-QTPA. Besides, some dopants are hydrophilic, which compromises solar cell stability considering that perovskite active layers are susceptible to moisture-induced degradation [16-20]. Without dopant, all the above-mentioned detrimental effects can be avoided.

Fig. 2(d) shows the incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra of the solar cells employing dopant-free Me-QTPA, Me-BPZTPA and doped spiro-OMeTAD as HTMs. Comparing with the device based on doped spiro-OMeTAD, the devices with dopant-free Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA show lower IPCE response between 500 and  $ 800\mathrm{nm} $ , which might be caused by the more serious charge recombination at the interface between pervoskite layer and the hole-transporting layer in the PSCs based on Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA [29]. The integrated  $ J_{sc} $  calculated from the IPCE is well matched with the  $ J_{sc} $  from  $ J - V $  curves. Obviously, relatively lower FF brings unsatisfactory performance of the device with Me-BPZTPA, and the lower FF can be explained by its higher series resistances [5]. Besides, there is no obvious variation in the value of  $ V_{oc} $  for the devices based on Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA because there is no obvious difference in the value of HOMO level of the two HTMs.

3.2.2. Measurement of time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) spectra

As we know, HTMs play a key role in charge separation and collection process. In order to examine the ability of the charge separation and collection for dopant-free Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA, the time-resolved PL spectra has been measured at the excitation of  $ 457~\mathrm{nm} $  and the emission of  $ 775~\mathrm{nm} $ . As shown in Fig. 3, for the ITO/  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film without HTM, the PL decay lifetime ( $ \tau_{\mathrm{PL}} $ ) is 17.18 ns. When spin-coating a thin HTM layer on the surface of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ , the  $ \tau_{\mathrm{PL}} $  significantly decreases to 3.61 ns for the ITO/ $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3/\mathrm{Me}-\mathrm{QTPA} $ , 4.44 ns for ITO/ $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3/ $  Me-BPZTPA and 2.47 ns for ITO/ $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3/ $  spiro-OMeTAD. The  $ \tau_{\mathrm{PL}} $  is obviously reduced for the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film with HTM, which indicates Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA can efficiently improve the ability of charge separation and collection at the interface [30]. Compared to spiro-OMeTAD, Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA still have comparable ability in hole extraction.

# 3.2.3 Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurement

The relationship between the  $  J_{sc}  $  of the devices based on the two dopant-free HTMs and the light intensities is confirmed. As shown in Fig. 4(a), the value of  $  J_{sc}  $  is almost linearly proportional to the value of light intensities, which indicates there is no relationship between the light intensities and the charge collection efficiency. To understand the interfacial charge transfer process and the recombination mechanism of the perovskite solar cell with the two HTMs, Nyquist plots of the device based on dopant-free Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA as the HTMs are carried out in the dark, in which the bias potential is applied from 400 to  $  950\mathrm{mV}  $ . The equivalent model is

applied to fit the tested data, as shown in Fig. 4(b) and (c). In general, the main arc located at the higher frequency is attributed to the hole transport in the HTMs, while the arc at the lower frequency is identified with the recombination loss process, modeled by a recombination resistance  $ (R_{rec}) $  [5]. The connection between the  $ R_{rec} $  and the applied bias potential is exhibited in the Fig. 4(d). The decreased  $ R_{rec} $  can be observed as the bias potential increased, mainly because the Fermi level in the mesoscopic  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  is elevated under the forward bias, which is beneficial to electron flow across the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $  interface [31,32]. Under the same condition, the similar variation tendency is also found in the device based on doped spiro-OMeTAD. In addition, at the same bias potential, the values of  $ R_{rec} $  for the PSC based on dopant-free Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA are similar but lower than that for the PSC based on doped spiro-OMeTAD, which indicates that more charge recombination happens at the interfaces of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / CH_3NH_3PbI_3 / Me - QTPA} $  (Me-BPZTPA) based PSCs than those of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / CH_3NH_3PbI_3 / spiro - OMeTAD} $  based PSCs. This can further explain why the performance of the PSC with Me-QTPA and Me-BPZTPA still lags behind the corresponding device with spiro-OMeTAD.

# 3.2.4 Measurement of stability test of perovskite solar cells

Long-term stability is a very important parameter for perovskite solar cells. Since the device with the Me-QTPA has shown better performance than the device with the Me-BPZTPA, the stability test of the device with dopant-free Me-QTPA and doped spiro-OMeTAD has been carried out by storing the unsealed devices in desiccator at room temperature for 600 hours and testing them once every 24 hours. As shown in Fig. 5, the devices with dopant-free Me-QTPA show more stability than the devices with doped spiro-OMeTAD after 600 hours. For the device with doped spiro-OMeTAD, the PCEs decrease significantly to  $ 72\% $  due to the decrease of  $ J_{sc} $ ,  $ V_{oc} $  and FF which is  $ 12\% $ ,  $ 14\% $  and  $ 14\% $ , respectively. For the device with dopant-free Me-QTPA, values of  $ J_{sc} $ ,  $ V_{oc} $  and FF remain almost constant, indicating that Me-QTPA has a promising long-term stability at room temperature.

The small improvement in the PCEs of the devices with dopant-free Me-QTPA can be obtained, which might be due to the better conductivity of the HTM after the oxidation reaction between Me-QTPA and the oxygen [33]. The stability of the cells based on dopant-free Me-QTPA is better than that of the cells based on doped spiro-OMeTAD due to the outstanding hydrophobicity and compactness of dopant-free Me-QTPA layer. As mentioned above, some dopants are hydrophilic, which compromises solar cell stability considering that perovskite active layers are susceptible to moisture-induced degradation [16-20]. As shown in Fig. 6, the contact angle between water and dopant-free Me-QTPA is  $ 101.6^{\circ} $ , while the contact angle between water and doped spiro-OMeTAD is  $ 70.77^{\circ} $ . With dopants, the contact angle decreases significantly. This might provide a disadvantage in terms of stability for the devices of the dopant-free HTMs.

# 4. Conclusions

Two piperazine derivates have been synthesized by facile synthesis and used as dopant-free HTMs in perovskite solar cells. The PSC based on dopant-free Me-QTPA shows a PCE of  $ 9.07\% $ , which is slightly lower than that of commonly doped spiro-OMeTAD under the same fabrication method. However, the materials show appropriate frontier molecular orbital energies and wide band gap due to the reduced conjugation system of molecules by N-bridge connecting, which paves the way for designing new low-cost hole-transporting materials and final commercialization of perovskite solar cells. The perovskite solar cells with dopant-free Me-QTPA exhibit better performance than the cells with dopant-free spiro-OMeTAD, specifically on long-term stability, with the PCE remaining almost constant after 600 hours. The dopant-free Me-QTPA layer shows strong hydrophobicity with the contact angle of  $ 101.6^{\circ} $  to water, which indicates that Me-QTPA has a promising long-term stability at room temperature.
