# Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells Achieved by Using Bifunctional Interfacial Materials to Modify   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   Simultaneously

Xusheng Zhao, Jun Dong, Daofu Wu, Jiaer Zhou, Julin Feng, Yanqing Yao, Cun Yun Xu, Xiude Yang, Xiaosheng Tang,  $  ^{*}  $   and Qunliang Song  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: As an effective electron transport layer, tin oxide   $  \left(\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\right)  $   has attracted much attention owing to its charge mobility and chemical stability, but it still suffers from high trap at the   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface. Herein, a simple interface treatment strategy of diethylenetriamine pentakis (methylphosphonic acid) (DETAPMP) to effectively passivate the   $  SnO_{2}/MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   interface is reported. Under the optimal DETAPMP concentration, the average power conversion efficiency (PCE) of   $  MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   solar cells is improved from 17.27 to 19.41% and the champion device shows a PCE of 20.02%. In the   $  \left(\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3}\right)_{0.95}\left(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3}\right)_{0.05}  $   system, the average PCE can be improved from 20.08 to 20.95% and the champion device shows a PCE of 21.65%. Such an enhancement is mainly attributed to two factors: (1) the phosphate group in DETAPMP reacts with SnO to passivate   $  SnO_{2}  $   defects and (2) the ammonium group in DETAPMP is expected to balance the   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  charge and passivate   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  defects, thus achieving lower charge recombination and better carrier transport. In addition, due to the hydrophobicity of DETAPMP and the reduced grain

boundaries in the perovskite film, the long-term stability of the perovskite with DETAPMP-modified   $  SnO_{2}  $   is improved under the ambient conditions with 20–30% humidity.

KEYWORDS: interface treatment, effective passivation, long-term stability, high efficiency, perovskite solar cells

# INTRODUCTION

Organic–inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted wide attention in the world due to the development of power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 3.8 to 25.5% in the past several years.  $  ^{1-7}  $   As far as we know, most of the effective PSCs over 20% are based on n-i-p devices, usually using mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   scaffolds to increase the contact area between perovskite materials and the electron transport layer (ETL).  $  ^{8-12}  $   However, the high annealing temperature in the sintering process hinders its further application in large-scale manufacturing and flexible devices. Therefore, researchers are developing ETL materials replacing   $  TiO_{2}  $   at low temperature. Recently, tin oxide (  $  SnO_{2}  $  ) has been proved as an alternative ETL to replace   $  TiO_{2}  $   due to its unique optoelectronic properties, such as excellent transmittance, high electron mobility, good light and thermal stability, and suitable energy level alignment.  $  ^{13-17}  $   However, there are some unavoidable defects such as lattice mismatch between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite, which can lead to electron accumulation, carrier recombination, current–voltage hysteresis, and poor stability.  $  ^{18-21}  $

One possible strategy to solve the lattice mismatch problems at the   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface is to add a buffer layer, which

In this work, we use another zwitterion, diethylenetriamine pentakis (methylphosphonic acid) (DETAPMP), to passivate   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   defects with experimental verification for highly efficient and stable PSCs. High-resolution X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) shows that there are two N 1s peaks in DETAPMP, indicating that

DETAPMP is a zwitterion. Phosphate in DETAPMP can easily react with SnO to form an ester bond, and the ammonium group in DETAPMP can balance the   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  charge and modify   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  according to XPS analysis of Sn, O, N, and Pb elements. After the DETAPMP modification, PCE is improved from 17.27 to 19.41%, indicating that electrons can be effectively extracted and carrier recombination is greatly weakened, which are verified by electrical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), space charge limited current (SCLC), light intensity-dependent   $  V_{oc}  $  , transient photovoltage (TPV), and transient photocurrent (TPC). In addition, the long-term stability of the perovskite layer with DETAPMP modification is improved owing to the hydrophobicity of DETAPMP and the reduced grain boundaries, evidenced by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), atomic force microscopy (AFM), and the contact angle.

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The device configuration is ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $ /spiro-MeOTAD/Ag. To reduce interfacial defects, DETAPMP is used to modify the SnO $ _2 $ /MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  interface. The device configuration and the molecular structure of DETAPMP are depicted in Figure 1a. Figure 1b shows that the phosphate in DETAPMP can form an ester bond with SnO and the ammonium group in DETAPMP can interact with PbI $ _3^- $ . Theoretically, one H on the hydroxyl group of phosphoric acid will completely ionize because phosphoric acid belongs to medium strong acid, and ionized H $ ^+ $  can easily react with the N of DETAPMP to form an ammonium group due to the electronegativity of N increased by the three connected alkyl groups. This deduction can be confirmed by N 1s spectra in DETAPMP, as shown in Figure 1c. There are two obvious N 1s peaks. The one at 401.4 eV belongs to the ammonium group and the other at 398.8 eV belongs to the amino group. $ ^{31} $  To understand the interaction between DETAPMP and SnO $ _2 $ , XPS measurements of SnO $ _2 $  and SnO $ _2 $ /DETAPMP (0.2 mg/mL) films were performed, where the peak of C 1s is used to clarify the spectra (Figure S1). Figure 1d shows that Sn 3d $ _{5/2} $

and Sn 3d $  _{3/2}  $  are shifted from 485.8 and 494 to 486 and 494.2 eV for SnO $  _{2}  $  films with DETAPMP modification, indicating the interaction between SnO $  _{2}  $  and DETAPMP. $  ^{24,32}  $  In order to have a deeper understanding, we carried on the peak fitting. Figure 1e shows that the magenta peak is related to Sn $  ^{4+}  $ , $  ^{33}  $  the green peak is associated with Sn $  ^{2+}  $ , $  ^{34}  $  and the blue peak is assigned to atomic Sn. $  ^{35}  $  The Sn $  ^{2+}  $  peak decreases from 43 to 29% by calculating the area ratio of Sn $  ^{2+}  $  to Sn $  ^{4+}  $  after DETAPMP modification, indicating that Sn $  ^{4+}  $  ascribed to Sn–O–P is formed by the reaction between Sn $  ^{2+}  $  and DETAPMP. In order to further confirm the abovementioned statement, the XPS of O 1s was measured. Figure 1f shows that the magenta peak is related to O from SnO $  _{2}  $ , $  ^{36}  $  the blue one is associated with O from SnO, $  ^{37}  $  and the green one belongs to the absorbed O species. $  ^{38}  $  Compared with the unmodified SnO $  _{2}  $ , we found that a new purple peak appears after DETAPMP modification, which is associated with O from Sn–O–P. $  ^{39}  $  To investigate the interaction between the ammonium group and MAPbI $  _{3-x}  $ Cl $  _{x}  $ , XPS of MAPbI $  _{3-x}  $ Cl $  _{x}  $  and MAPbI $  _{3-x}  $ Cl $  _{x}  $ /DETAPMP (15 mg/mL) was carried out, where the peak of the C 1s state is also used to clarify the spectra (Figure S2). The long-term stability is the main factor affecting the commercialization of PSCs, so the stability of perovskite devices and films was studied here. Figure 5a–d shows the stability of the unencapsulated devices (ITO/SnO  $  _{2}  $  /DETAPMP (0 and 1 mg/mL)/MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $  /spiro-MeOTAD/Ag) stored under the ambient conditions with 20–30% humidity. Compared with the device prepared with SnO  $  _{2}  $  for 38% of its initial PCE, the device with 1 mg/mL DETAPMP-modified SnO  $  _{2}  $  maintains 71% after 40 days. The reduction in PCE is attributed to J  $  _{sc}  $  and FF, which are very sensitive to defects. In addition, the stability of perovskite films on different-concentration DETAPMP-modified substrates, stored under the ambient conditions with 45–60% humidity in the dark at room temperature, was also tested. Figure 5e shows that the color of the films is less and less obvious with the increase in DETAPMP concentration after 40 days compared with the freshly prepared films, indicating that DETAPMP modification can stabilize the perovskite films. To find the reason why the stability of the modified film can be improved, the contact angle, AFM, and SEM were measured. Figure 5f shows that the contact angle of SnO  $  _{2}  $  modified with different DETAPMP concentrations is larger than that of the bare SnO  $  _{2}  $ , indicating that DETAPMP has better hydrophobicity. Because the hydrophobicity of DETAPMP can cause aggregation effects, which makes the roughness of the films increase with the increase in the DETAPMP concentrations, the contact angle decreases with the increase in the DETAPMP concentrations. The crystal size is another factor affecting the stability of perovskite materials. Figure 5g shows that the grain size of perovskite materials becomes larger after DETAPMP modification. Large grain size means less grain boundaries, indicating that the channel of water molecules into the perovskite film decreases. Therefore, we believe that the stronger hydrophobicity and less grain boundaries are the reasons for the enhancement of perovskite film and device stability.

# CONCLUSIONS

We develop a simple and effective treatment at the   $  SnO_{2}/  $   perovskite interface to obtain efficient and stable PSCs. DETAPMP, a zwitterionic compound, connects with   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite by chemical bonds due to its phosphate and ammonium group, which reduces the defects of the   $  SnO_{2}/  $   perovskite interface, thus realizing effective charge extraction and lower charge recombination. DETAPMP can also facilitate perovskite grain growth and reduce charge recombination at the grain boundaries. Hence, the champion device achieves a PCE of 20.02% with negligible hysteresis at the optimal DETAPMP concentration. In addition, the strong hydrophobicity of DETAPMP and large grain can stabilize perovskite materials under the ambient conditions. As a result, the unencapsulated PSCs with modified   $  SnO_{2}  $   retain 71% of their initial efficiency under the ambient conditions with 20–30% humidity after 40 days.
