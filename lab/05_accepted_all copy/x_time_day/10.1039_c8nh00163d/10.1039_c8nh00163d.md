# Conceptual Insights

In recent years, perovskite solar cells have reached certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 22.7% with great degradation in humid conditions. Developing new perovskite materials, such as 2D perovskite, is an efficient approach to tackling this drawback. However, as the length or bulk of alkyl carbon chains increase, the PCE of devices declines directly due to the steric hindrance. In this paper, sulfur-oleylamine precursor prepared in low temperature is introduced to modulate the chemical activity of perovskite film for the first time. Under the controllable pre-etching effect of   $  H_{2}S  $  , smoother films can be obtained. Afterwards, the volume-dependent dipping treatment reaches an equilibrium between etching and assembling, leading to an enhancement of moisture stability for devices. More importantly, devices based on such materials have shown almost no loss in energy conversion efficiency. In addition, the reaction properties of sulfur and other alkylamines remains to be investigated. The regulation of size and shape on perovskite single crystals may be realized by this precursor. This method may also be used for the surface ligand exchange of perovskite quantum dots, which may bring exceptional results. Overall, the treatment based on this method provides new insights for perovskite materials in optical field.

# Enhanced moisture stability of metal halide perovskite solar cells based on sulfur-oleylamine surface modification

Yu Hou, $  ^{a}  $  Zi Ren Zhou, $  ^{a}  $  Tian Yu Wen, $  ^{a}  $  Hong Wei Qiao, $  ^{a}  $  Ze Qing Lin, $  ^{a}  $  Bing Ge $  ^{a}  $  and Hua Gui Yang $  ^{*a}  $

As one of the most promising light-harvesting materials, perovskite has drawn tremendous attention for its unique advantages such as high efficiency, low cost and facile fabrication compared with other photovoltaic materials. Nevertheless, the poor tolerance of perovskites to moisture greatly hampers the operation of devices and hinders its industrialization. Here, we demonstrate a facile dipping treatment by using sulfur-oleylamine for the surface atomic modulation of perovskite film. The oleylammonium polysulfides (OPs) would be self-assembled on the post-etching perovskite film as ultrathin outlayer, which could passivate the surface chemical activity of perovskite layers. Moreover, the hydrophobic OPs significantly enhance the moisture stability of devices. As a result, the obtained device without encapsulation retains more than 70% of its initial power conversion efficiency (PCE) after 14 days' exposure in a relative humidity condition of   $  40 \pm 10\%  $  .

# Introduction

The organometal halide perovskite solar cells (OHPSCs) with a ABX $ _3 $  (A=alkylammonium, B=metal, X=halide) structure has developed quickly since its first appearance in 2009s, the power conversion efficiency of which has already reached a certified record of 22.7%. $ ^{1,2} $  However, the sensitive nature of perovskite materials to the moisture greatly hampers its further development. $ ^{3-8} $  The hydration of perovskite in air is thermodynamically preferred, that the MAPbI $ _3 $  derives usually degraded within 3 hours, under the relative humidity between 70-80%. $ ^{3,9} $  Previous research have revealed that the moisture instability of perovskite can be attributed to the molecularly absorption of water on surface Pb site, the (100) surface of tetragonal perovskite with a relatively low absorption energy was prone to interacting with water molecules, thereafter

causing the degradation of perovskite.  $  ^{10}  $

To improve the moisture stability of perovskites, Ruddlesden-Popper type perovskite materials with layered structure have been developed, impeding ion migration and keep the lattice from collapse inside.  $  ^{11}  $   Innovatively, Li et al. added Gallium acetylacetonate to the perovskite precursor solution.  $  ^{12}  $   The isomer of Gallium acetylacetonate aggregates in the grain boundary and surrounds the perovskite grain, which formed a core/shell structure to protect perovskite from moisture. Despite above-mentioned structural adjustment can enhance the humidity stability of perovskite effectively, the accompanying incrementally disordered structure will impede the carrier transport and impair the performance of OHPSCs.  $  ^{11}  $   Meanwhile, numerous studies have been focused on the emerging metal oxide (SnO  $  _{2}  $  ,  $  ^{13}  $   Al  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $    $  ^{14}  $  ) and binary chalcogenide (In  $  _{2}  $  S  $  _{3}  $  ,  $  ^{15}  $   ZnSe  $  ^{16}  $  ) as the replacement of traditional electron/hole transport materials, the devices based on these materials exhibit benign operation stability.

Beyond above mentioned methods, surface modification is employed as another novel strategy for its interfacial optimization while having tiny impact on bulk perovskite. The perovskite surface is passivated by introducing functionalized molecules or polymers, preventing the perovskite from erosion by water molecules as well as facilitate the electron/hole extraction between perovskite and electron/hole transport layer (ETL/HTL). After surface modification, defects can be significantly reduced, which results in lower charge recombination and enhanced device performance.  $  ^{17,18}  $   It is demonstrated that long alkyl chain or ring such as alkylphosphonic acid ammonium,  $  ^{19}  $   polystyrene  $  ^{17}  $   and polytetrafluoroethylene  $  ^{20}  $   can interact with perovskite by crosslinking, suppressing the lattice plane interaction brought by water molecules, therefore enhancing the moisture tolerance of devices. By forming the Pb-S bond, thiourea can passivate the perovskite film and achieve excellent stability after 60 days operation.  $  ^{21}  $   The hydrophobic alkylammonium cations were assembled onto the perovskite surface via dipping method, the obtained devices exhibited exceptional moisture stability under a high relative humidity over 90%

In this work, we demonstrate a novel sulfur-alkylamine precursor with facile treatment on perovskite films by post-dipping method, which is produced by dissolving powder sulfur in oleylamine with thermal and dynamic driving forces. The prepared sulfur-oleylamine solution including hydrogen sulfide  $ (\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{S}) $  and OPs, were pre-etching and post-assembling molecules, respectively. A smoother film was obtained through etching process and the subsequent assembling of OPs generated a hydrophobic capping layer on the perovskite film surface. Surprisingly, the device assembled with long-chain OPs exhibited a slightly higher performance. After dipping treatment, the energy loss of unsealed device is below  $ 30\% $  after 14 days' test in a humidity of  $ 40 \pm 10\% $ . In contrast, the device without dipping treatment almost lose all efficiency.

# Results and discussion

As shown in reported article, the sulfur exists as   $  S_{8}  $   rings at the standard state.  $  ^{25,26}  $   In a relatively low temperature (  $  <50^{\circ}C  $  ) heating condition, the   $  S_{8}  $   rings are liable to open under attack by nitrogen from alkylamine and form N-S bond (reaction (1), R represents alkyl group). Secondary amine will react with sulfur and form two N-S bonds, resulting in the liberation of   $  H_{2}S  $  .  $  ^{27,28}  $   As for the S-Oam solution in this study, the polysulfides anions react with remaining oleylammoniums and liberate   $  H_{2}S  $  , causing the decreased stoichiometry of S in polysulfides (reaction (2)). A certain volume as-prepared S-Oam solution (reaction (4)) is added into octadecene and the mixed solution is preheated to 40 °C waiting for use. Then annealed perovskite film is transferred to S-Oam/octadecene solution and dip for 3 minutes. During the dipping process, perovskite surface is etched by S-Oam in solution condition while the octadecene with long carbon chains serves as stabilizer in surface reaction.  $  ^{30}  $   Under the etching effect of   $  H_{2}S  $  , methylamine cations (  $  MA^{+}  $  ) and iodide anions (  $  I^{-}  $  ) is prone to dissolving in solution, leading to the degradation of perovskite (reaction (5)). The OPs molecules were anchored on perovskite film by interatomic interaction, nitrogen atoms in   $  S_{x}  $   rings are apt to form H-bond with   $  MA^{+}  $  while the sulfur atoms tend to form Pb-S bond with   $  Pb^{2+}  $  . Simultaneously, we measured the pH value of S-Oam (pH=11.7) and S-25 (pH=7.9) solution at 40 °C, the alkaline condition in favor of the proceeding of reactions. It can be inferred that the S-25 treated film was an outcome of dynamic equilibrium between etching and assembling, thereafter leading to a beneficial effect on device. As Scheme 1 depicting, the hydrophobic OPs layer protects perovskite from being attacked by water molecules as well as prevents ion migration between perovskite/HTL interface, resulting a stabilized perovskite film. Finally, the residual oleylamine, octadecene and other impurities can be successfully removed by rinsing with cyclohexane while leaving OPs on the perovskite film. # Conclusions

In summary, we utilized a facile and easy-controlled sulfurization dipping treatment on perovskite film with alkylammonium polysulfides and  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{S} $  in S-Oam precursor. The assembled OPs acts both as passivated and hydrophobic layer at the perovskite/HTL interface, which was formed by post-dipping method with dual-functional S-Oam in solution condition. The device after surface modification exhibited a significantly enhanced moisture stability compared to the

untreated one. This facile and low-cost solution processed craft provides new insights for surface modification of perovskite solar cells to fabricate highly stable, efficient and reproducible devices.
