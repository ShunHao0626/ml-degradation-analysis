# Enhancing the Hot Carrier Injection of Perovskite Solar Cells by Incorporating a Molecular Dipole Interlayer

Tai Wu, Rongjun Zhao, Junming Qiu, Shihuai Wang,* Xiaoliang Zhang,* and Yong Hua*

Surface passivation engineering of perovskite films via organic functional small molecules has emerged as an effective strategy for improving the efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, a systematic understanding of underlying mechanisms behind these improvements is still missing. In this work, two new naphthalimide-based organic small molecules (PX, X = F, I) are designed and employed to efficiently passivate the surface defects of perovskite films in PSCs. Consequently, superior photovoltaic properties for PI-treated PSCs are achieved with a power conversion efficiency of 23.06%, which is significantly higher than that of the reference device without passivators (20.45%). Theoretical calculations reveal that PX can give rise to interfacial electrical dipole. It is found that incorporating a dipole interlayer between perovskite layer and hole transport layer can enhance ultrafast charge-carrier injection and suppress the charge-carrier recombination in device, which is illustrated by transient absorption spectroscopy. These present results can provide valuable information on the understanding interfacial charge-carrier dynamics in PSCs to further improve the device performance.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells have attracted extensive attention during the last decade due to their remarkable optoelectronic properties such as large optical extinction coefficient, long-range charge-carrier diffusion length, low formation energy, and high charge carrier mobility.  $  ^{[1-6]}  $   The certified record power conversion efficiency (PCE) has been dramatically increased to 25.7% through

face morphology and lead to inferior interface contact between the perovskite layer and HTL, thus in turn affecting charge-carriers transport in device.  $  ^{[21,22]}  $   Additionally, the surface defects are responsible for hysteresis and long-term operational instability of PSCs.  $  ^{[23,24]}  $   Thus, effective surface passivation of the defects at interfaces is very crucial for simultaneously boosting the efficiency and long-term stability of PSCs with eliminated hysteresis.

Passivation engineering by organic small molecules has been evolved to be very simple and effective for improving the efficiency and stability of PSCs by reducing these defects at interfaces. For example, Snaith and co-workers employed iodopentafluorobenzene to passivate these trap states on the surface of perovskite films via supramolecular halogen bonding. This surface engineering strategy efficiently enhanced the PSCs photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{[25]}  $   Similarly, Grätzel et al. used crown ether-based interfacial layers between the perovskite/HTL, respectively, which reduced the surface electronic defects with low nonradiative recombination loss and enhanced the device PCE from 21.5% to 23.7%, accompanied by improved operational long-term stability.  $  ^{[26]}  $   Recently, Liu et al. used an ionic liquid as an effective passivator to cap the grain surface for improved stability and reduced trap density by anchoring the uncoordinated lead ions and the iodide ions, leading to significantly reduced nonradiative recombination and increased carrier lifetimes. As a result, the PSCs achieved a PCE of 24.33% with a high open-circuit voltage of 1.192 V. More importantly, the device showed

device configuration optimization, judicious composition engineering, and defect passivation engineering,  $  ^{[7-12]}  $  which exhibits the great promise for the future PSCs practical commercialized application. However, the quick crystal formation process of polycrystalline perovskite thin films inevitably induces the existence of large unintentional defect states at grain boundaries and interfaces (electron transport layer (ETL)/perovskite and perovskite/hole transport layer (HTL)). Previous studies demonstrated that the presence of these defects at interfaces are more likely to lead to the severe nonradiative recombination by trapping photogenerated charge-carriers (hole or electron),  $  ^{[13-20]}  $  which would induce the energy losses and consequently decrease the photovoltaic performance of PSCs device. With this aforementioned inspiration, we have designed and synthesized two homologous naphthalimide-based passivators differing only in different halogen substituents, referred to as PX (X = F and I), as shown in Figure 1a. PX can anchor on the perovskite surface by the formation of the O–Pb bonding between the C=O and the uncoordinated lead ion in perovskite film, thus effectively reducing the density of trap states. A combination of theoretical calculations and experimental studies were carried out to fully understand the defect passivation of perovskite films as well as their influence on the charge-carriers transport dynamics in device.

Finally, the   $  \tau_{3}  $   for the control sample is 0.34 ps, while these passivated perovskite films are significantly enhanced to 2.94 ps and 3.15 ps for PF and PI, respectively. The longer   $  \tau_{3}  $   indicates that the charge recombination process can be effectively suppressed in the presence of PF and PI. By contrast, the PI-treated perovskite films show smaller   $  \tau_{1}  $   and   $  \tau_{2}  $   values and larger   $  \tau_{3}  $   than that of PF, demonstrating that PI is more beneficial for the carrier transport in PSCs devices. Besides, we further elucidate hole transfer process at the interfaces of perovskite/PX/SpiroOMeTAD by fs-TA spectroscopy. Figure S14 (Supporting Information) shows the kinetic decay profile of these samples, which were also fitted with the above-mentioned three-exponential function. The decay time constants are displayed in Table S4 (Supporting Information). The control sample shows the   $  \tau_{2}  $   value of 2.00 ps, which is reduced to 0.31 ps and 0.16 ps for PF- and PI-passivated samples, respectively. These results verified the accelerated hole transfer from the perovskite layer to spiroOMeTAD layer after PF and PI passivation.

In this work, the ns-TA measurements were applied to further investigate the charge-carrier recombination process at the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  perovskite/PX/Spiro-OMeTAD interface. Figure S15 (Supporting Information) presents the ns-TA spectra dynamics

of perovskite films with or without passivators. The carrier lifetime constant   $  \tau_{TAS}  $   of glass/  $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD sample is 3.44   $  \mu  $  s, which is significantly enhanced to 5.49   $  \mu  $  s after introduction of PF as an interfacial passivator. In contrast, the PI-treated sample shows the longest decay time with   $  \tau_{TAS}  $   of 6.36   $  \mu  $  s. These results suggest that the charge-carrier recombination in device can be significantly suppressed with the treatment of PF and PI. The proposed charge-carrier transfer and recombination dynamics from above ultrafast studies is schematically illustrated in Figure 5a. Compared with the pristine sample, PI with interfacial dipole deposited on the surface of perovskite films is more favorable for the charge-carrier transfer from perovskite into CTLs, thus leading to significantly reduce the charge-carrier recombination rate and improve photovoltaic efficiency of PSCs.

Additionally, to further validate the effect of the interfacial dipole inside these molecules on the device performance, the contact potential differences (CPD) of perovskite films with and without passivation were characterized by Kelvin probe force microscope (KPFM), as presented in Figure S16 (Supporting Information). For the reference perovskite film (Figure 5b), the surface CPD value is 255 mV, which enhances to 556 and 623 mV after passivating with PF and PI, respectively. This apparent enhancement in CPD values indicates the formation of effective interfacial dipole on perovskite surface. In general, the interfacial dipole will trigger an extra electric field in device, resulting in the change of the work function (WF) of perovskite materials to some extent.  $  ^{[48,49]}  $   To experimentally evaluate the WK variation arising from the interfacial electric dipole formation induced by PF and PI layers, ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) was performed (Figure 5c). As shown in Table S5 (Supporting Information), the WF value of the bare perovskite decreases from 4.63 to 4.41 and 4.27 eV after the surface passivation of PF and PI, respectively, which well-matched with KPFM results. Clearly, the Fermi level (  $  E_{F}  $  ) of the bare perovskite shifts upward from -4.63 to -4.41 and -4.27 eV after the surface passivation of PF and PI, respectively, which is favorable for the charge-carrier transfer from the perovskite layer to the electron/hole transport layers.  $  ^{[27]}  $   Simultaneously, the valance band (VB,   $  E_{V}  $  ) value of pristine perovskite film is calculated to be -5.64 eV, which up-shifts to -5.57 and -5.53 eV by PF and PI passivation, respectively. The results indicate that the VB values of PF- and PI-passivated films match better with the Spiro-OMeTAD-based HTL than the pristine one, which could be beneficial for enhancing hole extraction and transfer at perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD interfaces, consequently contributing to the improved PSCs performance.  $  ^{[50]}  $   Moreover, the passivated perovskite films possess higher conduction band (  $  E_{C}  $  ) values (-4.04 eV for PF, -4.01 eV for PI) than that of pristine perovskite film (-4.12 eV), resulting in a higher driving force for electron transfer from the perovskite layer to the electron-transport layer (  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) in devices.  $  ^{[51]}  $   Based on these derived results, the energy levels alignment of the related layers in device is constructed in Figure 5d.
