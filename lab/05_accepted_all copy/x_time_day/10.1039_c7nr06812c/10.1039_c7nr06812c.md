# Adsorbed Carbon Nanomaterials for Surface and Interface-Engineered Stable Rubidium Multi-Cation Perovskite Solar Cells

Md Arafat Mahmud  $  ^{*1}  $  , Naveen Kumar Elumalai  $  ^{*1}  $  , Mushfika Baishakhi Upama  $  ^{1}  $  , Dian Wang  $  ^{1}  $  , Leila Zarei  $  ^{2}  $  , Vinicius R. $ ^{1} $ School of Photovoltaic and Renewable Energy Engineering, University of New South Wales, Sydney, NSW 2052, Australia

$  ^{2}  $  School of Chemistry, University of New South Wales, Sydney, NSW 2052, Australia

Corresponding Authors: MAM (mdarafat.mahmud@unsw.edu.au) and NKE (n.elumalai@unsw.edu.au)

# Abstract:

The current work reports the simultaneous enhancement in efficiency and stability of low-temperature, solution-processed triple cation based   $  MA_{0.57}FA_{0.38}Rb_{0.05}PbI_{3}  $   (MA: methyl ammonium, FA: formamidinium, Rb: Rubidium) perovskite solar cells (PSCs) by means of adsorbed carbon nanomaterials at the perovskite/electron transporting layer interface. The quantity and quality of the adsorbents is precisely controlled to electronically modify the ETL surface as well as lower the energy barrier across the interface. Carbon derivatives namely fullerene (  $  C_{60}  $  ) and   $  PC_{71}BM  $   (6,6]-phenyl C71 butyric acid methyl ester) is employed as adsorbents in conjunction with ZnO together serving as a bilayer electron transporting layer (ETL). The adsorbed fullerene (  $  C_{60}-ZnO  $  , abbreviated as C-ZnO) passivates the interstitial trap-sites of ZnO with interstitial intercalation of oxygen atoms in ZnO lattice structure. C-ZnO ETL based PSCs demonstrate about 19% higher average PCE compared to conventional ZnO ETL based devices and nearly 9% higher average PCE than   $  PC_{71}BM  $   adsorbed-ZnO (P-ZnO) ETL based PSCs. In addition, the interstitial trap-state passivation with C-ZnO film upshifts the Fermi-level position of C-ZnO ETL by 130 meV, with reference to ZnO ETL, which contributes to enhanced n-type conductivity. The photocurrent hysteresis phenomenon in C-ZnO PSC is also substantially reduced due to mitigated charge trapping phenomena and concomitant reduction in electrode polarization process. Another major highlight of this work is that, C-ZnO PSC demonstrates superior device stability retaining about 94% of its initial PCE in the course of a month-long, systematic degradation study conducted in our work. The enhanced device stability with C-ZnO PSC is attributed to its high resistance to aging-induced recombination phenomena and water-induced perovskite degradation process, due to lower content of oxygen-related chemisorbed species on C-ZnO ETL. The intricate mechanisms behind the efficiency and stability enhancements are investigated in detail and explained in the context of enhanced surface and interfacial electronic properties.

# 1. Introduction:

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have demonstrated unprecedented potential in the photovoltaic industry, with the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) soaring up to as high as 22.10%  $  ^{1}  $  of late. In particular, low-temperature processed PSCs  $  ^{2-10}  $  have paved the way for mass commercial production of perovskite devices in roll-to-roll process with flexible substrates. In this regard, PSCs, incorporating solution processed ZnO electron transport layer (ETL) offer a facile, cost-effective device fabrication pathway.  $  ^{3,8-13}  $  However, due to relatively open, hexagonal close-packed lattice structure of ZnO, its (ZnO's) unoccupied octahedral sites act as defect states (as deep as 0.2 eV below the conduction band  $  ^{14}  $ ) within its bandgap, which act as recombination centers for the photogenerated electrons from perovskite conduction band and thus affects the device performance adversely.  $  ^{15}  $  To compensate this issue with low temperature processed ZnO ETL, trap-state passivation of ZnO film has been reported by metal doping  $  ^{16-20}  $  or by the insertion of a secondary organic ETL layer [PCBM ([6,6]-phenyl C61 butyric acid methyl ester)]  $  ^{21}  $  between ZnO and perovskite layer as a mean of interfacial engineering. However, the metal-oxide doped ZnO ETL based PSCs demonstrate significantly high photo-current hysteresis phenomena,  $  ^{16-18}  $  which obstruct the reliable comprehension of their device performance under actual operating conditions.  $  ^{22}  $  In this regard, the reported dual-layer PCBM-ZnO ETL  $  ^{21}  $  based PSC is an interesting approach, since fullerene derivative like PCBM has been reported to minimize hysteresis phenomena by virtue of trap state passivation at perovskite/ETL interface.  $  ^{23-25}  $  Nevertheless, the reported bilayer PCBM-ZnO based PSC  $  ^{21}  $  demonstrates relatively low PCE (highest PCE: 12.20%). Regarding this, dual-layer fullerene (C $  _{60}  $ )-ZnO ETL bids fair to be a promising prospect, since fullerene ETL demonstrates an order of magnitude higher electron mobility (PCBM: 6.1x10 $  ^{-2}  $  cm $  ^{2}  $  V $  ^{-1}  $  s $  ^{-1}  $ , C $  _{60}  $ : 1.60 cm $  ^{2}  $  V $  ^{-1}  $  s $  ^{-1}  $ ) and nearly 7 times higher conductivity (PCBM: 3.2x10 $  ^{-4}  $  S cm $  ^{-1}  $ , C $  _{60}  $ : 2.3x10 $  ^{-3}  $  S cm $  ^{-1}  $ ) compared to PCBM ETL.  $  ^{23}  $  Moreover, the more densely-packed fullerene structure is anticipated to promote more efficient intermolecular charge transport  $  ^{26}  $  in conjunction with ZnO ETL in a PSC, which (ZnO) also exhibits substantially high electron mobility (200-300 cm $  ^{2}  $  V $  ^{-1}  $  s $  ^{-1}  $ ).  $  ^{27}  $

However, to date, most of the perovskite studies incorporating   $  C_{60}  $   as ETL have deposited the fullerene layer either via thermal evaporation   $  {}^{26,28-34}  $   or by using the sputtering deposition technique.  $  {}^{35}  $   Both these techniques necessitate sophisticated machinery, uplift the concomitant fabrication cost and most importantly are not favorable with roll-to-roll process for mass commercial production. Thus, a solution-processed route to   $  C_{60}  $   ETL deposition can offer a facile and lucrative device fabrication pathway for PSCs. So far, only two perovskite studies have reported solution-processed   $  C_{60}  $   as ETL in PSC; one with p-i-n (inverted) perovskite device structure   $  {}^{36}  $   and the other with n-i-p (normal) layer structure.  $  {}^{37}  $   Nonetheless, the p-i-n PSCs are more prone to inferior device stability, compared to their

Simultaneously, ZnO can also serve as a base layer for the overlying  $  C_{60}  $  film, denying its direct contact with ITO/glass substrate for more favorable perovskite grain growth on top of fullerene layer. It is also worth mentioning that, although fullerene-ZnO dual ETL layer remains uninvestigated in case of PSCs, hybrid fullerene-ZnO buffer layers  $  ^{40, 41}  $  have been reported to enhance the photovoltaic performance of organic solar cells (OSC), as compared to pristine ZnO based OSCs. Therefore, it is an intriguing prospect to delve into the photovoltaic performance, charge transport mechanism,  $  ^{42, 43}  $  inherent electrode polarization process and concomitant photo-current hysteresis  $  ^{44}  $  and device aging phenomena  $  ^{45}  $  of low temperature processed devices, incorporating solution processed fullerene-metal oxide dual layer as ETL.

In this work, we have reported low-temperature, solution-processed fullerene-ZnO dual layer ETL for fabricating highly efficient [highest PCE: 16.03%], triple cation based   $  MA_{0.57}FA_{0.38}Rb_{0.05}PbI_{3}  $   (MA: methyl ammonium, FA: formamidinium, Rb: Rubidium) PSC, having full temperature and process compatibility with flexible substrates for roll-to-roll process. The photovoltaic performance, hysteresis and aging phenomena of the dual layer ETL based PSCs have been compared with conventional dual-layer fullerene derivative (  $  PC_{71}BM  $  )-ZnO ETL and pristine ZnO ETL devices. The reported dual layer fullerene-ZnO ETL based PSCs outperform their counterparts in terms of both photovoltaic performance and device stability. Adding to the merits, fullerene-ZnO bilayer ETL also contributes to minimize the photo-current hysteresis, prevalent in low-temperature processed metal oxide ETL based PSCs. In our work, enhanced device performance and suppressed hysteresis phenomena with fullerene-ZnO bilayer ETL have been investigated with elemental analysis from X-Ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), material workfunction determination with ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS), microstrain and dislocation density analysis from X-Ray diffraction (XRD) spectral fitting, perovskite surface morphology and topology analysis, as well as charge transport and trap-assisted recombination characterization by electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) and Mott-Schottky analysis. # 2. Experimental Details

# 2.1. Device Fabrication

Patterned ITO/glass substrates were washed with soapy (Hellmanex III) water, DI water, acetone and 2-propanol in a cycle of 10 minutes for each step. Low temperature (140 °C) processed sol-gel ZnO film was deposited on ITO/glass substrate according to our previous works.  $  ^{[15,46]}  $   For bilayer fullerene-ZnO ETL, 10 mg/ml fullerene (  $  C_{60}  $  ) in 1,2-dichlorobenzene was spin-coated over the ZnO layer at different spin-coating speeds and the films were dried at 80 °C for five minutes. For bilayer PCBM-ZnO reference device, 10 mg/ml [6,6]-phenyl C71 butyric acid methyl ester (  $  PC_{71}BM  $  ) in chlorobenzene was spin-coated at 3000 rpm for 30s and the films were dried in the same manner as for bilayer fullerene-ZnO film. For all the devices, a precursor solution for   $  MA_{0.57}FA_{0.38}Rb_{0.05}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite was prepared using appropriate amount of   $  PbI_{2}  $ , MAI, FAI and RbI powder in anhydrous DMF (N, N-dimethylformamide) solvent and the precursor solution was stirred on hotplate at 70 °C for 24 hours. The perovskite precursor solution was filtered with 0.45 μm polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) filter and deposited on the ETL-coated substrates in nucleation-aided gas-assisted method.  $  ^{[47]}  $   Then, the substrates were annealed at 100 °C for 10 minutes on hotplate in restricted volume solvent annealing (RVSA) method.  $  ^{[48]}  $   A precursor solution for hole transporting layer (HTL) was prepared using 73.3 mg/ml 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spirobifluorene (SpiroOMeTAD) in chlorobenzene and the solution was doped with 17.5 μL bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium (Li-TFSI) (520 mg/ml in Acetonitrile) and 28.8 μL 4-tert-butylpyridine (4-TBP) for enhanced p-type conductivity. The HTL layer was spin-coated on the perovskite film with 3000 rpm for 30s. Afterwards, a 100 nm Ag layer was deposited on the HTL coated substrate by thermal evaporation (vacuum level:   $  1\times10^{-6}  $   mbar, evaporation rate of 2 Å /s). A metal shadow mask was used during the thermal evaporation to fix the device area to be 4.5 mm².

# 2.2. Device Characterization

The photovoltaic performance of the PSCs was measured with a Keithley 2400 source meter under a simulated sunlight of  $ 100\mathrm{mW / cm^2} $  (AM 1.5G), which (the illumination) was calibrated with a national renewable energy laboratory (NREL) certified reference solar cell. A black, non-reflective aperture  $ (4.5\mathrm{mm}^2) $  mask was used over the cells to ensure there is no overestimation in the device short current current density, pertaining to the device active area. XPS characterization was performed using ESCALAB250Xi machine (Thermo Scientific, UK) with background pressure of  $ 2\times 10^{-9} $  mbar using a mono-chromated AlKα ( $ \mathrm{h\nu} = 1486.68\mathrm{eV} $ ) anode (120 W, 13.8kV, 8.7 mA). # 3. Results and Discussion:

# 3.1 Device Performance:

# 3.2 Material and Optical Characterization of ETL Films

Deposited ZnO, C-ZnO and P-ZnO ETL films have been investigated with XPS elemental analysis to investigate the surface modification of pristine ZnO film due to the deposition of a secondary fullerene or PC $ _{71} $ BM ETL film on top of it (ZnO). Fig. 2(A), 2(B) and 2(C) depict the de-convoluted Gaussian sub-peaks and envelop curve of high resolution O $ _{1s} $  XPS spectra of ZnO, C-ZnO and P-ZnO ETL films, respectively. Corresponding peak binding energies, peak width and elemental atomic percentage for the three ETL films have been tabulated in Table S6, Table S7 and Table S8, respectively. From Fig. 2(A)-2(C) and Table S6-S8, it can be comprehended that the core-level peak positions of O $ _{1s} $  spectra shift to higher binding energy for C-ZnO and P-ZnO ETL film, compared to ZnO film. Similar shift in core-level peak positions of Zn $ _{2p} $  XPS spectra are also observed for C-ZnO and P-ZnO ETL films, with reference to ZnO film (Fig. S4). The shift of O $ _{1s} $  and Zn $ _{2p} $  peak positions to higher binding energy in C-ZnO and P-ZnO films indicate that more oxygen atoms occupy the interstitial ZnO sites  $ ^{53} $  in these two ETL films compared to pristine ZnO film, which (interstitial) sites can otherwise act as trap-centres  $ ^{14} $  for photo-generated charge carriers from perovskite.  $ ^{15} $  The shift in O $ _{1s} $  and Zn $ _{2p} $  peak positions for C-ZnO and P-ZnO ETL films also hints towards possible variation in their Fermi-level positions,  $ ^{54} $  compared to ZnO film. To investigate the material work-function of the ETL films, we have conducted UPS measurement of ZnO, C-ZnO and P-ZnO films on ITO/glass substrate. Fig. 2(D) shows the evolution of secondary electron edges for ZnO, C-ZnO and P-ZnO ETL films on ITO/glass substrate from UPS characterization. As observed from Fig. 2(D), the work-functions of ZnO, C-ZnO and P-ZnO on ITO/glass substrate are 3.80 eV, 3.67 eV and 3.71 eV, respectively. The up-shift in Fermi-level positions for both C-ZnO and P-ZnO ETL films indicate better n-type conductivity  $ ^{55} $  of these two ETL films compared to ZnO film. In general, Fermi-level is a combined property of charge carriers and it is strongly modified by energetic disorder in the material.  $ ^{56} $  Despite being an inorganic semiconductor, low-temperature processed ZnO does not have a well-defined Quasi Fermi-level because of the gap tail states induced by intrinsic trap states.  $ ^{57, 58} $  Thus, the up-shift in Fermi-level position for C-ZnO and P-ZnO ETL films indicates that the intrinsic trap states of ZnO ETL films are passivated in bilayer ETL films, which can be attributed to the increase in oxygen atoms in interstitial ZnO sites, as described earlier. However, it is worth mentioning that C-ZnO ETL film demonstrates a 40 meV upshift in work-function with reference to P-ZnO film, which hints towards higher n-type conductivity of C-ZnO film than P-ZnO film.

To investigate the enhanced n-type conductivity of C-ZnO film, we can refer back to the deconvoluted Gaussian sub-peaks of   $  O_{1s}  $   XPS spectra of the ETL films (Fig. 2(A)-2(C)). The chemisorbed surface species are likely to reduce the electron mobility  $  ^{59}  $   in P-ZnO ETL, which can be attributed to the lower n-type conductivity of P-ZnO ETL, compared to C-ZnO ETL, as demonstrated with UPS measurement (Fig. 2(D)).

To further investigate the higher n-type conductivity of C-ZnO ETL, with reference to P-ZnO ETL, we have also investigated the relative amount of carbon-based functional groups for each of those films. Fig. 2(E) and 2(F) depict the de-convoluted Gaussian sub-peaks and envelop curve of high resolution  $ \mathrm{C_{1s}} $  XPS spectra. (7)

As observed from Fig. 7(F), both C-ZnO and P-ZnO PSCs demonstrate smaller low-frequency (0.1-1 Hz) capacitance compared to ZnO devices. The suppressed electrode polarization process in C-ZnO and P-ZnO devices can be attributed to the intrinsic trap-state passivation at perovskite/ETL interfaces in them, compared to ZnO PSCs; since fullerene reacts with the mobile ions in perovskite to form a fullerene halide radical  $  ^{86}  $   that reduces the photo-current hysteresis by suppressing the electric-field induced ion migration.  $  ^{87}  $   Nevertheless, the presence of some J-V hysteresis even in fullerene based PSCs can be attributed to the possible partial dissolution of PCBM or   $  C_{60}  $   ETL in DMF, as reported in previous perovskite literature.  $  ^{37}  $   Partial solubility of PCBM or   $  C_{60}  $   in DMF solvent has been reported to cause an intermixing problem between perovskite layer and underlying   $  PCBM^{88}  $   or   $  C_{60}^{37}  $   film. However, since, the film thickness of PCBM or   $  C_{60}  $   ETL film is about 40 nm; in accordance with the finding of a recent perovskite study by Ha et al.,  $  ^{34}  $   it is expected that the molecular characteristic of fullerene or its derivative remains intact, even if about   $  \sim  $  10 nm of the ETL film is affected by its (ETL film's) partial dissolution with DMF.  $  ^{34}  $   Besides, PCBM or   $  C_{60}  $   ETL based PSCs demonstrated reasonable photovoltaic performance and reduced photo-current hysteresis in our case, which denotes that the partial dissolution of PCBM or   $  C_{60}  $   ETL with DMF did not substantially affect the PSC device performance. This observation was also consistent with some recent perovskite studies.  $  ^{34, 35}  $

# 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, we have reported solution-processed bilayer fullerene-ZnO ETL (C-ZnO ETL) for fabricating highly efficient (highest PCE: 16.03%) triple cation based   $  MA_{0.57}FA_{0.38}Rb_{0.05}PbI_{3}  $   PSCs, having full temperature compatibility with flexible substrates. Secondary fullerene ETL film passivates the interstitial trap-sites of ZnO film with interstitial intercalation of oxygen atoms in ZnO lattice structure. The interstitial trap-state passivation with C-ZnO film also upshifts the Fermi-level position of C-ZnO ETL by 130 meV, compared to ZnO ETL, which contributes to its (C-ZnO ETL's) enhanced n-type conductivity. In contrast, conventional PCBM-ZnO bilayer ETL (P-ZnO ETL) has been found to exhibit lower n-type conductivity, compared to C-ZnO ETL, owing to the increased chemisorbed species and functional groups on the surface of P-ZnO ETL. The charge transport characterization conducted in our work shows that P-ZnO PSC demonstrates the lowest contact resistance, the highest recombination resistance, recombination lifetime and flat-band potential of the three devices, which ensure the charge extraction property is more enhanced and the trap-assisted recombination phenomenon is more suppressed in P-ZnO PSC. As a result, C-ZnO PSCs exhibit about 19% higher average PCE compared to ZnO device and nearly 9% higher average PCE than P-ZnO PSCs. C-ZnO PSC also demonstrate suppressed photo-current hysteresis phenomenon in it, which has been found to be correlated with reduced electrode polarization process due to the trap-state passivation at perovskite/ETL surface., C-ZnO PSC also retains about 94% of its initial PCE during the course of degradation study, while the corresponding values for P-ZnO and ZnO PSCs are 88% and 81% of the commencing efficiencies. The enhanced device stability with C-ZnO PSC has been found to be correlated with its mitigated aging-induced recombination phenomena and favourably sluggish water-induced perovskite degradation process, due to lower content of oxygen-related chemisorbed species on C-ZnO ETL. Thus, the reported low temperature processed C-ZnO ETL bids fair to be a promising electron selective contact for the mass production of highly efficient, stable and flexible substrate based perovskite solar cells compatible with roll-to-roll device processing and device scalability.

Scheme-1: Fig. (A) shows wurtzite ZnO lattice structure for pristine ZnO ETL, depicting the interstitial defect states, which can act as charge trapping sites during the operation of perovskite solar cell. The corresponding work-function value is 3.80 eV. Fig. (B) demonstrates the ZnO lattice structure for C-ZnO ETL. The secondary fullerene ETL film passivates the interstitial trap-sites of ZnO film with interstitial intercalation of oxygen atoms. Due to this trap state passivation, the n-type conductivity of C-ZnO ETL film is higher and it demonstrates a 130 meV upshift in Fermi-level position, relative to ZnO ETL. Fig. (C) illustrates the ZnO lattice structure in case of P-ZnO ETL. Like fullerene, the secondary PC  $  _{71}  $  BM film passivates the interstitial trap states; however, the content of chemisorbed species on P-ZnO ETL surface is much higher, compared to C-ZnO ETL. Due to these chemisorbed species, the electron mobility and n-type conductivity of P-ZnO film are relatively low than C-ZnO ETL, with its (P-ZnO ETL's) Fermi-level position located at 3.71 eV. Scheme-2: A schematic presentation of the spatial variation of the Quasi Fermi-level position of the ETL films and concomitant effect on flat-band potential and   $  V_{OC}  $   of PSCs from energy band diagram. For ZnO PSC [Fig. (A)], the energetic offset between the Fermi-level position of ZnO ETL and perovskite is 0.92V, while the corresponding Fermi-level offset between ZnO ETL and Spiro-OMeTAD HTL is 0.96V.
