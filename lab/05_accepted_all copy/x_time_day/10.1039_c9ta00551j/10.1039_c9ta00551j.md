# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have recently gained immense research interest owing to exceptional optoelectronic properties of organo-lead halide perovskite materials including high charge carrier mobility, high absorption coefficient and extended carrier recombination time.  $  ^{1}  $   These excellent features have led to power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 22.1~23.3% for perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{1-3}  $   However, the insurmountable challenge for researchers is the compromised stability of PSCs under illumination and in moist conditions. Scientists have achieved considerable success in addressing the issue of long-term stability in moist conditions, however, progress regarding improvement in the light stability of PSCs is still of great importance and also in high demand.  $  ^{4,5}  $   The light instability of PSC devices is mainly related to, on one hand, decomposition of the perovskite layer due to the light illumination induced organic   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}  $  ions,  $  ^{6,7}  $   and on the other hand, UV-light induced oxygen defects and vacancies which ultimately result in low performance.  $  ^{7-9}  $   Therefore, there is a high potential to improve the light stability of PSCs by decreasing the UV proportion of the incident light on the

# A promising europium-based down conversion material: organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells with high photovoltaic performance and UV-light stability

Naveed Ur Rahman, $ ^{\dagger} $  Wasim Ullah Khan, $ ^{\dagger} $  Shaukat Khan, Xiaojie Chen, Javid Khan, Juan Zhao, $ ^{*} $  Zhiyong Yang, Mingmei Wu, $ ^{*} $  and Zhenguo Chi $ ^{*} $

Abstract: Metal halide perovskites solar cells (PSCs) have been studied over the past years and achieved high power conversion efficiency (PCE). However, the adverse instability remains a key factor restricting wider applications of PSCs. Herein, we report, for the first time, a viable strategy to incorporate down conversion nanophosphors   $  Sr_{2}CeO_{4}:Eu  $   (SCOE) into PSCs, and more importantly the SCOE enables the PSCs with improvement in photovoltaic performance and UV-light stability. The SCOE-coated PSCs achieve a high current density of   $  23.70\ mA\ cm^{-2}  $   and a high PCE of 18.95%, which are increased by 9% and 14.15% respectively when compared to that of control devices (without SCOE layer). Moreover, the PCEs of the SCOE-based PSCs retain 80% of its initial value after 70 hours under UV-light irradiation and remain 78% of its initial value after 75 days under ambient environment with 20–25% relative humidity. These results suggest that the incorporated SCOE down conversion material is a functional component of PSCs to broaden the solar spectral response for improving photovoltaic performance and reduce UV-light-induced photodegradation by converting UV light to visible light for prolonging the device stability.

In the present study, a newly synthesized Eu-based DC material (SCOE) was introduced in PSCs for increasing the light harvesting and preventing UV-light degradation. Significantly, the SCOE-based devices showed excellent long-term stability under both UV-light irradiation and open environment conditions, demonstrating the multifunction of the down conversion SCOE material.

# Experimental

# Materials

All solvents and reagents were commercially available pure and used without further purification. Cerium oxide (CeO $ _2 $ ), europium oxide (Eu $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ ), and strontium carbonate (SrCO $ _3 $ ) were purchased from Aladdin. Lead (II) iodide, dimethyl sulfoxide, lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulphonyl) imide (Li-TFSI), 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP) and titanium isopropoxide were purchased from Aldrich. Methylammonium lodide and Spiro-OMeTAD were obtained from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Co. Ltd., acetylacetonate, N, N-dimethylformamide, ethanol and chlorobenzene were from Sinopharm. Details for device fabrication and characterizations are provided in Supporting Information.

# Synthesis and characterization of SCOE nanophosphors

The europium-based down conversion   $  Sr_{2}CeO_{4}:Eu^{3+}  $   (SCOE) nanophosphor material was synthesized via a solid-state reaction method. The reactants were as follows,   $  SrCO_{3}  $   (A.R. grade),   $  Na_{2}CO_{3}  $   (A.R. grade),   $  Eu_{2}O_{3}  $   (99.99%) and   $  Ce_{2}O_{3}  $   (99.99%). Materials in designed stoichiometric composition were thoroughly mixed and ground by using a pestle in an agate mortar. Subsequently, the well-ground mixture was transferred to an alumina crucible and calcined at 1150 °C for 3 h in air atmosphere. Finally, the obtained SCOE nanophosphors were ground again and forwarded for further measurements. The morphology and structure of the SCOE, PSCs (surface and cross sectional) and the energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) spectrum of the SCOE were investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, FEI,

Quanta450 FEG), X-ray diffraction (XRD, Bruker  $  _{V}  $  , D8  $  _{A}  $   advance with Cu K  $  \alpha  $   radiation), high resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) and selective area electron diffraction (SAED) were investigated by FEI Tecnai G2 Spirit, UV-vis absorption spectra were detected using a UV-vis spectrometer (Hitachi U-3900). The photoluminescence (PL) spectra of the SCOE nanophosphors were examined using a fluorescence spectrophotometer (RF-5301PC). The roughness of the films was characterized by atomic force microscopy (AFM, Bruker Multimode 8 SPM system). X-ray photoelectron spectroscope (XPS) measurements were performed by using an ESCALAB 250Xi (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Diffused reflectance spectrum (DRS) was obtained from optical diffuse reflectance measurements on a UV-visible-NIR spectrometer (Carry-5000). Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements were carried out on a electrochemical workstation VMP300 (Bio-Logic), by using tetrabutylammoniumhexafluorophosphate (TBAPF  $  _{6}  $  , 0.1 M) in dichloromethane as electrolyte, a platinum as counter electrode, and a Ag/AgCl as reference electrode (versus ferrocene F  $  _{c}  $  /F  $  _{c}  $    $  ^{-1}  $  ).

# Characterizations of SCOE down conversion material

The HRTEM (Fig. 1a) and SAED (Fig. 1b) analyses demonstrate high crystalline nature of the SCOE material, while the XRD pattern (Fig. 1c) shows orthorhombic crystalline nature of the SCOE material with crystal diffraction peaks at 17°, 29.5°, 39.5°, 42.3°, and 53.1° and 60°, corresponding to (110) (111), (211), (221), (151) and (132) crystal planes respectively, indicating high degree of crystallinity.  $  ^{23}  $   The energy dispersive spectrum (EDS) mapping confirms the presence of host materials and the dopant Eu (Fig. S1). The speciation of near-surface atoms of Sr₂CeO₄:Eu³⁺ was characterized by XPS measurement (Fig. 1d), showing the surface elemental composition of O, Ce, Sr and Eu (Fig. S2 for details). As depicted in Fig. 1e, the UV-vis absorption spectrum of SCOE shows that the absorption of the SCOE thin film is in the range of UV region with an absorption peak at ~ 280 nm, which is in a good agreement with transmission spectrum of the SCOE thin film (Fig. S3). The down-conversion PL spectrum of the SCOE film is shown in Fig. 1f, where the characteristic peaks for Eu³⁺ are clearly observed at 584, 615, 654, and 704 nm, indicating electronic transitions from the excited level ⁵D₀→⁷F₁, ⁵D₀→⁷F₂, ⁵D₀→⁷F₃, and ⁵D₀→⁷F₄ respectively.  $  ^{23}  $   The strongest emission intensity observed was from the ⁵D₀→⁷F₂ transition, indicating the absence of inversion symmetry at the Eu³⁺ ion site in the crystal lattice; otherwise, in the presence of inversion symmetry the dominant transition is the magnetic dipole transition ⁵D₀→⁷F₁.  $  ^{24}  $   Besides, the PL emission of the SCOE film is not only stemmed from the lowest ⁵D₀ excited levels but also from higher energy levels of Eu³⁺ (⁵D₁ and ⁵D₂) with weaker intensity.  $  ^{24}  $   CeO₄ groups persist low energy of vibration which leads to the emission from the higher excited states of Eu³⁺. # Photovoltaic performance of the SCOE-based PSCs

As a proof of concept, PSCs were fabricated through introduction of the SCOE material. The schematic configuration of the PSCs is shown in Fig. 2a, which consists of fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) as anode, compact TiO $ _2 $  (c-TiO $ _2 $ ) as electron transport layer (ETL), a thin layer of SCOE down conversion material, the (CsFAMA)Pb(BrI) $ _3 $  perovskite layer, Spiro-OMeTAD as a hole transport layer (HTL) and Ag cathode with the thickness of 560 nm, 30 nm, 150 nm, 440 nm, 80 nm and 130 nm, respectively. The energy band gap ( $ E_g $ ) of the SCOE nanophosphors was estimated to be 2.96 eV from the DRS measurement represented by the Kubelka-Munk function plot (Fig. S4a), $ ^{25,26} $  while the valence band and conduction band were determined to be 7.1 and 4.2 eV respectively from the CV characteristic (Fig. S4b). $ ^{27} $  At the same time, the energy bands of FTO, TiO $ _2 $ , perovskite, Spiro-OMeTAD and Au (5.1 eV) are taken from previous report. $ ^{28} $  The PSCs configuration can also be confirmed from the cross-sectional SEM image (Fig. S5). When sunlight is irradiated from the FTO side, the visible portion of the solar spectrum passes through FTO and SCOE layers and then the visible photons are absorbed by the perovskite layer; meanwhile, the UV photons can be absorbed by the SCOE layer and then re-emitted as low-energy visible photons which in turn are absorbed by the perovskite layer. $ ^{10} $  For comparison, control PSCs without the SCOE layer were also fabricated. Fig. 2b shows the  $ J-V $  curves of the devices with and without the SCOE layer, and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters are listed in Table 1. As shown from the  $ J-V $  curves, the best-performing control device shows a  $ J_{SC} $  of 21.80 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{OC} $ ) of 1.04 V, FF of 73.40% in reverse scan, leading to a PCE of 16.60%. In contrast, the SCOE-based device achieves a PCE of 18.95% in reverse scan, along with a  $ J_{SC} $  of 23.70 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ,  $ V_{OC} $  of 1.06 V and FF of

75.50%. Meanwhile, as learned from the statistical histogram of 78 SCOE cells and 78 control cells (Fig. 2c), the SCOE devices present an enhancement of 11.8% in average PCE. Moreover, the SCOE-based PSCs reveal much improved PCE compared to our previous PSCs incorporated with samarium (Sm)-based DC material, indicating great effectiveness of the SCOE DC material, which is due to   $  Eu^{3+}  $   ions exhibit higher absorption efficiency and higher conversion efficiency of UV light, thus the Eu-based DC material is expected to absorb UV light and convert it into visible light as much as possible.  $  ^{29}  $   At the same time, it is clear that the SCOE device hardly exhibits hysteresis while the control device shows obviously hysteresis. # The role of SCOE thickness on PSCs performance

Moreover, the effect of the SCOE thickness on device performance of the PSCs is investigated, as shown in Fig. 4 and summarized in Table S1. It is observed that from Fig. 4a, the PCE is enhanced when the thickness of the SCOE layer increases from 0 to  $ 150\mathrm{nm} $ , due to the charge recombination chances are decreased to a certain limit with the film optimization. With further increase in the SCOE thickness, the PCE starts to decrease. The PCE drops to half of its initial value at a SCOE thickness of  $ 235\mathrm{nm} $  and then gradually declines, which could be attributed to high dependence of light harvesting and series resistance on the SCOE thickness in the PSCs. On one hand, the light harvesting can be enhanced by increasing the SCOE thickness to efficiently down convert the UV light into visible light. On the other hand, the light harvesting is also affected by the transmission of incident light. As the SCOE thickness is increased, the transmission of light to the (CsFAMA)Pb(Brl) $ _3 $  layer is decreased, as a result light harvesting is decreased (Fig. S10). The transmission spectra are in consistent with their corresponding UV-vis absorption spectra with a peak at  $ 280\mathrm{nm} $  as plotted in Fig. S11, wherein gradually enhanced absorption is observed as the SCOE thickness is increased. The thickness of the SCOE layer also affects the series resistance because it can block electron transfer from (CsFAMA)Pb(Brl) $ _3 $  to c-TiO $ _2 $  layer. As the SCOE thickness increases, the series resistance is gradually increased (Fig. 4a) and leads to decreased PCE. For more details, the  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $ ,  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  and FF decrease as the SCOE thickness arises from 150 to  $ 500\mathrm{nm} $  (Fig. 4b), ascribing to decreased transmission and increased series resistance. Thereby, the optimized SCOE thickness is  $ 150\mathrm{nm} $  for effective light harvesting, lower series resistance and better transmission of the devices.

To date, device stability is still a great issue for PSCs and high stability is critical for its commercial applications. As for unencapsulated control and SCOE devices, the moisture stability is investigated under open environment with relative humidity of 25–30% at a temperature of 20–25 °C. As described in Fig. 5a, the SCOE-based PSC retains over 72% of its initial PCE after 75 days, while the control device is degraded by more than 55% in the same interval. As seen from optical images of the perovskite films with and without SCOE layer when fresh made and then exposed under highly relative humidity of 85–90% at about 25 °C, the perovskite film without the SCOE layer (control sample) almost decomposes after 20 days (Fig. 5b), which is related to the unstable nature and rapid degradation in ambient environment of the perovskite material, leading to considerably decreased absorption for the perovskite film (Fig. 5c). On the other hand, the SCOE-coated perovskite film (SCOE sample) demonstrates relatively stable film morphology even after 20 days exposure (Fig. 5b), and presents obvious absorption to some extent (Fig. 5d). In comparison to the control PSCs, the better device stability of the SCOE-based PSCs can also be evidenced from the PCE performance measured at room temperature in glovebox filled with N₂ (Fig. S12). The SCOE-based device retains more than 78% of its initial PCE after 75 days while the control device maintains 69% of its initial PCE. These results suggest the reduced traps in the SCOE-based devices and subsequently suppressed trap-assisted nonradiative recombination, contributing to improved long-term stability when compared to that of the control devices.  $  ^{42,43}  $

# UV-light stability of the PSCs

Previous study has reported that, the absorption coefficient of the perovskite material in the UV region is   $  10^{61}  $   cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , which is comparatively larger than   $  10^{4}  $   cm  $  ^{-1}  $   in the visible region.  $  ^{44}  $   However, the absorption of perovskite film decreases rapidly when kept under UV light irradiation but keeps relatively stable under visible light, due to perovskite decomposition caused by UV-light irradiation.  $  ^{45}  $   Therefore, it is of great importance to render PSCs with high UV-light stability. Here, to investigate the UV-light stability of the PSCs, the control and the SCOE-based PSCs were kept under 365-nm UV-light irradiation for continuous 70 h.  $  ^{46}  $   As shown in Fig. 6a, for the case of the control PSC, the PCE maintains only 40% of its initial value after 70 h of UV-light irradiation. Surprisingly, the SCOE-based PSC retains 80% of its initial PCE under the same experimental conditions, presenting excellent UV-light stability. As learned from Fig. 6b, c, d, both the   $  V_{OC}  $   and   $  J_{SC}  $   of the control PSC are quite stable against aging time, whereas its degradation rate in FF is relatively more pronounced than that of the SCOE-based PSC. The SCOE-based PSC exhibits much more stable FF because the SCOE layer can protect the perovskite layer from UV-light-caused degradation and subsequent pinholes-induced recombination.  $  ^{47}  $   These results are further evidenced from UV-light stability measurement for 10 control PSC cells and 10 SCOE-based PSC cells, which were all kept under 365-nm UV-light irradiation for 70 h, and the detailed results are displayed in Fig. S13 and Table S2. Therefore, the introduction of the SCOE layer, which down converts UV light to visible light and thus suppresses photodegradation of the perovskite layer, plays a critical role on the significant improvement in the photostability of the SCOE-based PSCs.
