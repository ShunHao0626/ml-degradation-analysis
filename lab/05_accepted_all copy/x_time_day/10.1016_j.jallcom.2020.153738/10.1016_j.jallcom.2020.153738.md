# Abstract:

Full utilization of the incident light is an effective strategy to enhance photovoltaic performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, we used lanthanide-containing polyoxometalate  $ \mathrm{Na}_{9}[\mathrm{EuW}_{10}\mathrm{O}_{36}] $  ( $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $ ) as a luminescent down-conversion material incorporated in mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  layer for boosting the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the printable hole transport material-free PSCs. The  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $  can convert the high energy photons into the low energy visible photons matched well with the absorption of perovskite, so that photogenerated current carriers increase substantially. This research represents the first example of using lanthanide-containing polyoxometalate as down-conversion materials to effectively improve PCE in solar cells. Compared with pristine  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  based PSCs, the  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10}-\mathrm{TiO}_2 $  based PSCs showed an enhanced power conversion efficiency (PCE) from  $ 11.42\% $  to  $ 14.36\% $  and obtained a remarkable photocurrent density of  $ 23.46\mathrm{mA/cm}^2 $ . Besides, the  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10}-\mathrm{TiO}_2 $  based device exhibited a better long-term stability, which could maintain  $ 98\% $  initial efficiency after storage for 14 days. Since the efficiency of the printable perovskite solar cells has been a key factor to achieve commercial implementations, we consider that the results from this work demonstrate a new kind of down-conversion materials to boost PCE and stability.

Keywords: printable perovskite solar cells, lanthanide, polyoxometalate, down-conversion, power conversion efficiency

# 1. Introduction

Organic metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs), as a new frontier research topic in solar cells, have a landmark significance in photovoltaic cells [1-4]. With rapid progress, PSCs could compete with other thin-film solar cells that have been developed for decades [5-7]. In PSCs, the core material of perovskite acting as light absorption layer has the advantages of high absorption coefficient, long-range balanced ambipolar carrier diffusion lengths and high defect tolerance [8-10]. The rapid progress in PSCs is not only due to the superior properties of perovskite materials, but also the engineering and modification of various components of PSCs devices, resulting in higher power conversion efficiencies [11-13]. Although the organic lead-halide perovskite has proper direct band gap (1.55 eV) in visible light absorption, the light absorption layer of perovskite can not make full use of the incident light in the whole solar spectrum, especially wasting the ultraviolet light, near-ultraviolet light and infrared light [14]; this loss becomes a significant negative factor for boosting the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs. Therefore, the full utilization of incident sunlight may be an effective method to improve the efficiency of PSCs [15]. As for realizing practical application of PSCs, the high efficiency should be most necessary, although the low-cost and the stability are also considered.

Recently, the luminescent down-conversion materials were employed to convert the ultraviolet light into visible light for improving the efficiency of solar cells, in which the luminescent down-conversion material on the front side of light absorption layer could convert a part of the incident UV photons of high energy into the visible

photons of low energy that can well be absorbed by the photosensitive layer [16-19]. Among down-conversion materials, lanthanide oxide have been used successfully in dye sensitized solar cells for improving the light scattering and absorption [20,21]. Jiang et al. used a transparent luminescent down-conversion layer of Eu-complex to increase the light utilization efficiency of PSCs [22]. Jin et al. incorporated the  $ \mathrm{YVO}^4:\mathrm{Eu}^{3+},\mathrm{Bi}^{3+} $  into the mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  to improve both the utilization of UV light and the device stability in PSCs [23]. However, in order to achieve the commercial use of down-conversion materials, there are three factors to be taken into account as follows: (i) the availability and low-cost; (ii) the facile deposition methods suitable for a large area; (iii) the stability of materials.

Polyoxometalates (POMs), one kind of molecular metal-oxo cluster compounds, are mainly made from Mo, W, and V elements and oxygen atoms [24]. POMs can form lanthanide-containing complexes by strong coordination bonds, and the lanthanide-containing POMs with luminescent properties have the advantages of low-cost, water-soluble (facile deposition process) and high stability [25]. Therefore, lanthanide-containing POMs may be a proper candidate for down-conversion materials. However, the development of POMs-based down-conversion materials has been unexplored to date.

The printable perovskite solar cell without hole transport material (HTM) is a kind of promising model for commercialization of PSCs, benefitting from a good balance of production cost and performance [26-28]. Its device structure consists of a carbon electrode made from graphite powder rather than a precious metal electrode. At

present, the relatively low PCE of the printable PSCs has impeded its further development [29], and it is urgent to seek a new path to improve the efficiency of the printable PSCs. To the best of our knowledge, the utilization of the down-conversion materials in the printable PSCs has not been reported yet. Inspired by the above-mentioned clues, we consider that the use of lanthanide-containing POMs as down-conversion materials is innovative and realizable for improving PCE of the printable PSCs.

In this work, we synthesized Weakley-type polyoxometalate   $  Na_{9}[EuW_{10}O_{36}]\cdot32H_{2}O(EuW_{10})  $   and doped   $  EuW_{10}  $   into   $  TiO_{2}  $   mesoporous layer of the fully printable HTM-free PSCs. The   $  EuW_{10}  $   as down-conversion material was placed in front of perovskite layer, and   $  EuW_{10}  $   could absorb high energy photons and emit lower energy photons that well match the absorption of perovskite layer, so as to generate more photo-generated carriers. Therefore, the PSC device with   $  EuW_{10}  $  -doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   mesoporous layer exhibits a significant enhancement in the efficiency, as compared to the pristine   $  TiO_{2}  $   based device. For the   $  EuW_{10}-TiO_{2}  $   based device, the PCE could be boosted from 11.42% to 14.36%, together with a better stability at ambient condition. This work represents the first example of using lanthanide-containing POMs as down-conversion materials to improve PCE in solar cells.

# 2. Experimental Section

# 2.1 Synthesis of  $  EuW_{10}  $  material

$ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $  was synthesized according to the reported method [30]. First, a certain amount of  $ \mathrm{Na_2WO_4\cdot 2H_2O} $  (25.2 mmol) was dissolved in  $ 20~\mathrm{mL} $  of deionized water and the solution was adjusted to pH 7.3 with acetic acid. Then, an aqueous solution (2 mL) containing  $ \mathrm{EuCl}_3\cdot 6\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  (2.47 mmol) was added dropwise to the  $ \mathrm{Na_2WO_4} $  solution with stirring at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ . The mixed solution was cooled to room temperature and  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $  crystals were isolated. Finally, the product was collected and dried in air.

# 2.2 Fabrication of PSC device

The schematic structure of HTM-free printable PSC is shown in Fig. 1a. The fabrication procedures of the device are similar to our previous work, except for the mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer [29]. Briefly, fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass was patterned by chemical etching (Zn powder and  $ 2\mathrm{M} $  HCl) and cleaned with surfactant. A compact layer of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  was deposited by spin-coating (2000 rpm) from a precursor solution. The mesoscopic  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer was deposited by screen-printing of a  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  slurry (P25 Degussa) and then sintered at  $ 450^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $ . The  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  pastes (P25 Degussa) with a certain amount of  $ \mathrm{EuW_{10}} $  (mass ratio of  $ \mathrm{EuW_{10} / P25} $  were  $ 0,2\% $ ,  $ 5\% $ ,  $ 10\% $ , respectively) were diluted with terpineol, followed ball milling for 12 hours. Then, a  $ \mathrm{ZrO_2} $  space layer and a carbon counter electrode were printed on the top of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer and consequently heated at  $ 400^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $ . After that, the resulting films were infiltrated with the  $ (5\text{-AVA})_{\mathrm{x}}(\mathrm{MA})_{1 - \mathrm{x}}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  precursor solution ( $ 0.548\mathrm{gMAI} $ ,  $ 1.616\mathrm{gPbI}_2 $  and  $ 0.0299\mathrm{g}5\text{-AVAI} $  were dissolved in  $ 3.6\mathrm{ml}\gamma $ -butyrolactone and then stirred at  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 20\mathrm{min} $ ) by dropping on the top of the carbon counter electrode. The resulting solar cells containing perovskite were obtained after drying at  $ 50^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for

# 2.3 Characterization

Fourier transformed infrared spectra (FTIR) were measured with a Nicolet Magna 560 FT-IR Spectrometer. X-ray diffraction (XRD) analyses were recorded with a Rigaku D/max-3c X-ray diffractometer, using Cu Kα radiation (1 = 1.5405 Å). Field emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (SU-8010, Hitachi) equipped with an energy dispersive spectrum (EDS) was used to investigate the surface and cross-section morphology. The photocurrent density voltage (J-V) characteristics of the PSCs with an active area of 0.1 cm² were measured by a Keithley 2400 Source Meter under illumination of a simulated sunlight (AM1.5G, 100 mW/cm²). Electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) was recorded on an Ivium workstation (Ivium Stat.h, Ivium Holland, Inc.). The fluorescence spectra were taken by the FL900/FS920 steady-state fluorescence spectrometer. The UV-Vis diffuse reflectance spectroscopy was recorded with an UV-Vis-NIR Spectrophotometer (Varian).

# 3. Results and Discussion

To identify the as-synthesized  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $ , the FTIR spectrum of  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $  was measured as shown in Fig. 1b. The FTIR spectrum of  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $  exhibits vibrations characteristic at 936, 831, 761 and  $ 670~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $  corresponding to  $ \nu_{\mathrm{as}}(\mathrm{W} = \mathrm{O_d}) $ ,  $ \nu (\mathrm{W - O_c - W}) $ ,  $ \nu_{\mathrm{as}}(\mathrm{W - O_b - W}) $  and  $ \nu (\mathrm{W - O_b - O}) $ , respectively, indicating the characteristic structure of anionic  $ [\mathrm{EuW}_{10}\mathrm{O}_{36}]^{9-} $  [31]. Fig. 1c presents the photoluminescence excitation and emission spectrum of  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $  powder, in which

the excitation band of  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $  ranges from 240 to  $ 400\mathrm{nm} $  is ascribed to F-F transition of  $ \mathrm{Eu}^{3+} $  from the fundamental  $ {}^{7}\mathrm{F}_{0} $  state to different excited states. The emission spectra consist of sharp peaks ranging from 550 to  $ 720\mathrm{nm} $ , which are associated with the transitions from excited  $ {}^{5}\mathrm{D}_{0} $  level to  $ {}^{7}\mathrm{F} $  levels ( $ {}^{5}\mathrm{D}_{0} \rightarrow {}^{7}\mathrm{F}_{0}, {}^{5}\mathrm{D}_{0} \rightarrow {}^{7}\mathrm{F}_{2}, {}^{5}\mathrm{D}_{0} \rightarrow {}^{7}\mathrm{F}_{3} $  and  $ {}^{5}\mathrm{D}_{0} \rightarrow {}^{7}\mathrm{F}_{4} $ ) of  $ \mathrm{Eu}^{3+} $  [15,32]. The photoluminescence spectrum demonstrated that  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $  can convert UV photons into the visible photons, and the emitted light can be absorbed by the perovskite layer with narrow bandgap and thus the light harvesting efficiency is improved obviously. Therefore, we consider that the addition of  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $  will enhance the utilization of UV light in PSCs and then enhance PCE of devices.

Both mesoscopic layers exhibit uniform distribution with no obvious difference on FTO substrates, indicating that the content of  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $  is not enough to visibly change the morphology of the  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  mesoscopic layer. Meanwhile, it can be seen that  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $  is uniformly distributed throughout the  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  layer.

Based on the above-mentioned data, the corresponding fully printable HTM-free PSCs with  $ \mathrm{EuW_{10} - TiO_2} $  as mesoporous layer were fabricated and investigated systematically. Fig. 1a shows the schematic illustration of the as-prepared PSC device, which is made up of FTO, a compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer and a mesoscopic structure consisting of a double layer of mesoporous  $ \mathrm{EuW_{10} - TiO_2} $  (m- $ \mathrm{EuW_{10} - TiO_2} $ ) and  $ \mathrm{ZrO_2} $  covered by a porous carbon film for filling perovskite. The matching energy levels of the device structure are capable of leading to both the photoexcited electron extraction from perovskite to the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  conduction band and the hole collection from the perovskite valence band to the carbon. As shown in Fig. 4, when incident sunlight enters into the PSC at the FTO side, a majority of the visible photons go through directly and are absorbed by the layer of perovskite. Part of high energy photons are transferred to low energy photons by using  $ \mathrm{EuW_{10}} $ , and then are absorbed by perovskite. This process can improve the light harvesting efficiency of PSC, so that more photo-generated carriers are generated. Meanwhile, it is expectable that both the PCE and the UV stability of PSCs can be improved.

To investigate the charge-transport and interfacial charge-transfer processes of PSCs, we performed the electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) of the devices based on  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{m - EuW_{10} - TiO_2} $  in the frequency range from  $ 0.1\mathrm{Hz} $  to  $ 10\mathrm{kHz} $  in the dark with a bias of  $ 0.1\mathrm{V} $  (Fig. 6). The data are fitted by a simple equivalent circuit consisting of a parallel RC and R-CPE element connected in series, along with a series resistance  $ (R_s) $ . According to our previous report [29], the small arc at high frequency region is ascribed to contact resistance  $ (R_{ct}) $  at either the electron transport layer/perovskite or perovskite/counter electrode (CE), while the large semicircle at low frequency region is associated with the recombination resistance

$  (R_{rec})  $   and chemical capacitance of the system. After comparing the data, we found that the   $  R_{ct}  $   and   $  R_{rec}  $   of the two kind of PSCs devices were almost similar. After optimizing the content of doping  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $ , we tested the reproducibility of the corresponding PSCs. The statistical PCE histograms of 20 PSC devices based on  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{m - EuW_{10} - TiO_2} $  layer prepared by the same conditions are shown in Fig. 7. The average  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $ ,  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $ , FF, and PCE for the 20 PSCs based on  $ \mathrm{m - EuW_{10} - TiO_2} $  are  $ 0.88 \pm 0.02 \mathrm{~V} $ ,  $ 23.4 \pm 0.22 \mathrm{~mA/cm^2} $ ,  $ 0.70 \pm 0.03 $ , and  $ 14.0 \pm 1.23 \% $ , respectively. However, the average  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $ ,  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $ , FF, and PCE for the 20 PSCs using  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  are  $ 0.88 \pm 0.02 \mathrm{~V} $ ,  $ 20.1 \pm 0.35 \mathrm{~mA/cm^2} $ ,  $ 0.66 \pm 0.03 $ , and  $ 11.5 \pm 1.07\% $ , respectively. It is obvious that the use of  $ \mathrm{m - EuW_{10} - TiO_2} $  layer could greatly improve  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  and PCE, and the PSC devices exhibited a good reproducibility. Therefore, the printable PSC device provided a good balance of production cost and long-term stability [27]. Also, we repeatedly measured the PCE to investigate whether the inherent device stability could further be improved after  $ \mathrm{EuW_{10}} $  doping. After each  $ J-V $  measurement, the PSC device was stored in the atmosphere without encapsulation. As shown in Fig. 8, the  $ \mathrm{m - EuW_{10} - TiO_2} $ -based device retained  $ 98\% $  of its initial PCE after storage for 14 days and the  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $ -based device maintained  $ 96\% $  of its initial PCE value during the same aging period.

According to the previous studies, the UV-induced photocatalytic reaction of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  could cause the decomposition of perovskite film [33]. In our work, a spectral down-conversion process occurred by means of doping  $ \mathrm{EuW_{10}} $ , and the incoming UV light is converted into visible light. Such a result decreases the influence of ultraviolet light on perovskite thin films, improving the stability of the device.

# 4. Conclusions

In this work, we firstly utilize lanthanide-containing polyoxometalate   $  EuW_{10}  $   as down-conversion material into the   $  TiO_{2}  $   mesoporous layer, and thus   $  EuW_{10}  $   could absorb high energy photons and emit lower energy photons that well match the absorption of perovskite layer; this leads to a remarkable enhancement in PCE of the printable PSCs owing to bringing more photo-generated carriers. The PSC device with   $  EuW_{10}-TiO_{2}  $   mesoporous layer exhibited a significant enhancement in PEC compared to the pristine   $  TiO_{2}  $   based device, and the device showed a PCE of 14.36%, which reached a 25.7% PCE enhancement compared to that of the pristine   $  TiO_{2}  $   based PSC. Besides, the   $  EuW_{10}-TiO_{2}  $   based device also manifested a better stability at ambient environment. Our results demonstrated that the lanthanide-containing polyoxometalate   $  EuW_{10}  $   could act well as down-conversion material for efficiently improving PCE of the printable HTM-free PSCs, bringing a new insight into developing POMs-based down-conversion materials used in solar cells.

# Highlights

> EuW  $  _{10}  $  is used as down-conversion material in the printable perovskite solar cells for the first time.

> The PCE of  $ \mathrm{EuW_{10} - TiO_2} $  based device is boosted from  $ 11.42\% $  to  $ 14.36\% $  as compared to that of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -based device.

>  $ \mathrm{EuW}_{10} $  could convert UV light into visible light so as to increase photogenerated current carriers.

> An extremely high photocurrent density of 23.46 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  is obtained.
