# Abstract

Environmental instability of Spiro-OMeTAD-based hole transport layer (HTL) caused due to rapid aggregation and hydration of its additive, Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI), gives rise to an accelerated degradation of the resulting perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, we show that replacing the Li-TFSI with the more hydrophobic alkaline-earth bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide additives, namely  $ \mathrm{Mg - TFSI_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{Ca - TFSI_2} $ , can effectively stabilize the coordination complexes between the TFSI-salts and 4-tert-Butylpyridine, which in turn results in retarded additive aggregation and hydration, enabling enhanced moisture-resistance of the subsequent HTLs. Moreover, by manipulating this substitution method, we achieved high-quality HTLs with increased hole mobility, better-formed interface with the adjacent perovskite, allowing improved hole extraction process. Incorporating these HTLs into photovoltaic devices, we obtained a substantial performance improvement, with the champion PSC yielded a power conversion efficiency of over  $ 20\% $ . In addition, un-encapsulated devices stabilized by the alkaline-earth bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide additive maintained  $ 83\% $  its initial efficiency for 193 days after aging in ambient air ( $ \mathrm{RH}\% = 55 - 70\% $ ).

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic metal halide perovskites have attracted tremendous attention due to the combination of excellent optoelectronic properties and cost-effective solution process [1-5]. Adopting the structure of liquid-based dye-sensitized solar cells, the first perovskite solar cell (PSC) demonstrated poor performance and stability due to the fast dissolution of the perovskite material in the electrolyte system [6]. To address this obstacle, solid state hole transport material 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-4-methoxyphenylamino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene, known as Spiro-OMeTAD, was employed as an alternative to iodide liquid electrolyte [7, 8]. Owing to the suitable energy level, high solubility, amorphous character, and high glass transition temperature, the Spiro-OMeTAD remains the benchmark material for achieving high-performance PSCs [9-11].

The pristine Spiro-OMeTAD, however, often shows poor conductivity, so additives are required to remedy this deficiency [12]. So far, Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI), in combination with 4-tert-Butylpyridine (4-tBP) are among the most widely-used additives for the Spiro-OMeTAD, through which the record power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 23.3\% $  has been achieved [13]. Nevertheless, evidence reports show that, upon exposure to ambient air, Li-TFSI aggregates across the Spio-OMeTAD-based hole transport layer (HTL), which is then hydrated to create void structures in the HTL that eventually causes severe degradation within the device [14, 15]. Recently, it has been reported that 4-tBP and Li-TFSI can form complexes in their mixture, and careful fine-tuning of their molar ratio in the HTL precursor solution, greatly enhances device stability against moisture, although the optimal device performance is not benefited [16]. feature of   $  Li^{+}  $  ion, especially under elevated temperature and/or voltage bias, has made it a potential threat for long-term stability of the device in a real operational condition [17, 18]. Therefore, development of effective additives alternative to Li-TFSI, which has strong chemical interaction with 4-tBP, excellent hydrophobic properties and retarded ion migration behaviour is of vital importance [19].

To date, there are only few reports of using alternative additives to Li-TFSI in preparation of the Spiro-OMeTAD-based HTL as a mean of enhancing the stability of PSCs. Such additives include copper salts [20], dicationic salts of Spiro-OMeTAD [21-23], non-ionic dopant [24] and even transition metal salts of bis(trifluoromethanesulfonimide) [25, 26]. In all cases, the substitution of Li-TFSI with these salts remarkably increases the robustness of the resulting HTL against environmental degradation, and thereby device stability. Nonetheless, the chemistry of this additive engineering which is a crucial aspect to understand the fundamental origins for the performance and stability enhancement, is still unclear.

Herein, we present a method to improve optoelectronic properties and environmental stability of Spiro-OMeTAD-based HTL, and the subsequent PSCs through the substitution of the common HTL additive, Li-TFSI, with alkaline-earth bis(trifluoromethanesulfonimide) salts including  $ \mathrm{Mg - TFSI_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{Ca - TFSI_2} $  (denoted as  $ \mathrm{X - TFSI_2} $ ) in preparing the HTL. Both experimental and theoretical analysis have indicated that by substituting Li-TFSI with X-TFSI $ _2 $ , the charge transfer process at the resulting interface between the HTL and perovskite is significantly enhanced, allowing the champion device to yield a PCE of over  $ 20\% $ . Critically, we have found that evaporation of 4-tBP and thereby aggregation of  $ \mathrm{X - TFSI_n} $  ( $ \mathrm{X} = \mathrm{Li} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Mg} $  or  $ \mathrm{Ca} $ ;  $ \mathrm{n} = 1, 2 $ ) across the HTL is dramatically reduced when using the  $ \mathrm{X - TFSI_2} $  instead of the Li-TFSI. This combines with the less hygroscopicity of the  $ \mathrm{X - TFSI_2} $  dopants opposed to the Li-TFSI greatly improves the durability of the HTL against moisture, which when incorporated into complete PSCs substantially extend their environmental stability,

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Materials preparation

All materials were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used as received unless otherwise stated. Methylammonium bromide (MABr), formamidinium iodide (FAI) and 30 NR-D transparent titania paste were purchased from Greatcell Solar. Lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ , 99.9%) was purchased from Youxuan Tech, China. 2, 2', 7, 7'-Tetrakis- (N, N-di-4-methoxyphenylamino)-9, 9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-MeOTAD) was supplied by Borun New Material, China.

# Perovskite precursor solution

Double cation (FA, MA)Pb  $ \mathrm{I_xBr_{3 - x}} $  precursor solution was prepared by dissolving 1.1 mmol formamidinium iodide (FAI, Greatcell Solar), 1.2 mmol  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , 0.2 mmol MABr and 0.2 mmol lead(II) bromide  $ (\mathrm{PbBr}_2) $  in 1 mL mixture of N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF)/dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (4:1, v/v). Subsequently, 50 mLμL caesium iodide (CsI) stock precursor (1.5 M, in DMSO) was added to the pre-dissolved double cation and then magnetically stirred for one hour to form triple cation perovskite (FA, MA, Cs)  $ \mathrm{PbI_xBr_{3 - x}} $  precursor. The potassium-passivated triple-cation precursor solution was prepared by adding 100 μL potassium iodide (1.5 M, dissolved in a mixture of DMF/DMSO (4:1, v/v)) to the as-prepared triple-cation perovskite precursor solution and magnetically stirred for one more hour [27]. Methylammonium lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{MAPbI}_3) $  precursor solution was prepared by dissolving 1 mmol methylammonium iodide (MAI, Greatcell Solar), 1 mmol  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  in mixture of 740 μL DMF and 80 μL DMSO.

Hole transport material precursor solution

1.81 M Bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium (Li-TFSI) was predissolved in acetonitrile (ACN) as a stock solution. For other TFSI-based stock solution in ACN that are Magnesium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonimide)  $ (\mathrm{Mg - TFSI_2}) $ , Zinc di[bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide]  $ (\mathrm{Zn - TFSI_2} $ ,  $ 95\% $  ) and Calcium(II) Bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide  $ (\mathrm{Ca - TFSI_2} $ ,  $ 99.5\% $ ), the concentrations of the TFSI-based divalent metal salts were set to half of Li-TFSI. To prepare hole transport material solution, various amount of the TFSI-based stock solutions were added into a pre-mixed solution containing  $ 72.3\mathrm{mg} $  (0.059 mmol) Spiro-OMeTAD,  $ 29~\mu \mathrm{L} $  (0.198 mmol) 4-tert-butylpyridine in  $ 1\mathrm{mL} $  of chlorobenzene. # 2.2. Device fabrication

A compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer (~40 nm) was spin-coated on pre-patterned, pre-UV-ozone-treated FTO-coated glass (Nippon Electric Glass, 15 Ω/♦) using a precursor of titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) (0.15 M, in 1-butanol) at 2000 rpm for 20 s. The compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   film was then dried at 120°C and 500°C for 10 mins and 30 mins, respectively, before being treated with UV-ozone for 15 mins. After that a mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer (~200 nm) was prepared by spinning a precursor of 30 NR-D   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste dispersed in ethanol (1:10, w/w), at 2000 rpm for 20 s on top of the compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   film. It was then dried at 120°C and 500°C for 10 mins and 60 mins, respectively, before being cleaned with UV–ozone for 15 mins and transferred to an   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glove box. The as-prepared triple-cation perovskite precursor solution was spin coated on the substrate in two-step programs at 1000 rpm for 10 s and 6000 rpm for 20 s, respectively. # 2.4. Theoretical computation

We performed the first-principle calculations in this study based on DFT (density functional theory) by using Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP)[30] with generalized gradient approximation (GGA) using the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange correlation functional being adopted [31]. The energy and force differences are converged within  $ 10^{-4}\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ 10^{-2}\mathrm{eV} / \mathring{\mathrm{A}} $ , and the lattice vectors and atomic positions were fully relaxed for all the considered structures. A real space mesh cutoff of  $ 400\mathrm{eV} $  and k-mesh of  $ 1\times 1\times 1 $  have been used throughout the entire calculations.

To focus on the interaction of X-TFSI $ _n $  (X = Li, Mg or Ca; n = 1, 2), Spiro-OMeTAD and the surface of perovskite, two dimensional structures of perovskite are adopted, and a vacuum layer of more than 15 Å was constructed in the perpendicular direction to erase the interaction between layers. Molecular structure of Li-TFSI, Mg-TFSI $ _2 $  and Ca-TFSI $ _2 $  were drawn by using VESTA [32].

We separately incorporated Li-TFSI,  $ \mathrm{Mg - TFSI_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{Ca - TFSI_2} $  salts (molecular structure demonstrated in Figure S1-a) into a complete mesoporous-structured perovskite solar cell

The synthesized TC film has uniformly packed grains with sizes approximately 100-500 nm (Figure S1-b). As presented in Figure S1-(c-e), the thickness of c-TiO $ _2 $ , mp-TiO $ _2 $  and perovskite layer is ~40 nm, 200 nm and 500 nm, respectively. By careful tuning the dopant concentration, we obtain the optimum power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of 17.09 ± 0.58, 18.60 ± 0.21 and 18.32 ± 0.27% for the devices using Li-TFSI (32 mM), Mg-TFSI $ _2 $  (16 mM) and Ca-TFSI $ _2 $  (24 mM), respectively (Table S1 and Figure S2). Analysing the obtained PV parameters, we found that the performance enhancement associated with this processing route is mainly attributed to the increased values of open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{oc} $ ) and fill-factor ( $ FF $ ), while the short-circuit current density ( $ J_{sc} $ ) of the PSCs remained largely unchanged. Previous report has demonstrated the positive impacts of substituting Li-TFSI with Zn-TFSI $ _2 $  on PSC efficiencies [26]. Indeed, we have confirmed that the devices based on Zn-TFSI $ _2 $  could deliver higher PCEs (17.88 ± 0.69%, as shown in Figure S3) than those using Li-TFSI (17.09 ± 0.58%), but their optimal efficiencies are still fall short of the PCEs gained from Mg-TFSI $ _2 $  and Ca-TFSI $ _2 $  made under the same condition in our work (Table S1 and Figure S2). On the basis of the above results, one could conclude that Mg-TFSI $ _2 $  and Ca-TFSI $ _2 $  (denoted as X-TFSI $ _2 $ ) are more effective HTL additives in delivering efficient triple-cation based PSCs.

Having demonstrated here that the substitution Li-TFSI with alkaline-earth bis(trifluoromethanesulfonimide) salts significantly improves   $  V_{oc}  $  , FF and thereby PCE of the resulting PSCs, we proceed to investigate the possible origins behind this advancement by performing a series of characterizations with the main focus on the TC perovskite composition. In the first examination, we perform Mott-Schottky plots of PSCs based on TFSI-based additives (Figure 1-d). Fitting the “depletion zone” of capacitance we have found that, by substitution of Li-TFSI with X-TFSI  $  _{2}  $  , the   $  V_{bi}  $   of the resultant devices are greatly improved by ~80-100 mV (Figure 1-d). The large   $  V_{bi}  $   is crucial for thin-film solar cells to extract charge carriers efficiently. From the fitting results, we also obtain the slope of -1.2×10  $  ^{15}  $  , 3.8×10  $  ^{15}  $   and 4.7×10  $  ^{15}  $   F  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   cm  $  ^{-4}  $   for Mott-Schottky plots of PSCs based on Li-TFSI, Mg-TFSI  $  _{2}  $   and Ca-TFSI  $  _{2}  $  , respectively. This result indicates that this additive manipulation method has a great effect in reducing interfacial charge density and thus improving charge extraction at the electrode interfaces [33, 34].

Furthermore, through analysis of impedance spectra (IS) (Figure 1-e and Figure S7), we have found that non-radiative losses are significantly reduced in the devices using X-TFSI $ _2 $  in respect of the one using Li-TFSI. Specifically, at the same applied bias, the total recombination resistance ( $ R_{rec} $ ) as a sum of low and high-frequency resistance ( $ R_1 $  and  $ R_3 $ , respectively) of the devices using the X-TFSI $ _2 $  is always higher than that of device using Li-TFSI. Take the applied bias at 0.92 V, for example, the  $ R_{rec} $  of the Li-TFSI-based device is only  $ \sim $ 50  $ \Omega $ .cm $ ^2 $ , which is about half of that of the devices using X-TFSI $ _2 $  (Figure 1-e). This is further confirmed by analysis of the light intensity dependence of the open-circuit voltage. As shown in Figure 1-f, the ideality factor of the PSC device decreases when Li-TFSI is replaced by X-TFSI $ _2 $ , indicating reduction of non-radiative recombination taking place in the complete devices [35]. It has been previously proposed that the evaporation rate of 4-tBP from the HTL is closely associated with the evaporation behaviour of coordination complexes of Li-TFSI and 4-tBP [16]. In order to probe this, we mixed X-TFSI $ _n $  and 4-tBP with the same molar ratio used to fabricate the highest performance PSCs and found that the pyridine stretching mode peak at  $ \sim $ 1595 cm $ ^{-1} $  of the 4-tBP in the FTIR spectrum was blue-shifted upon addition of the X-TFSI $ _n $  salt and the degree of blue-shift is more significant for Mg-TFSI $ _2 $  followed by Ca-TFSI $ _2 $  (Figure S19-(a-d)). According to Yurdakul et al., could be associated with the interaction of X $ ^{n+} $  and lone electron pair of nitrogen (on 4-tBP) with metal cations and the extent of the blue-shift is proportional to the coordination ability of the metal cation [54]. On the basis of the above results, we propose the order of the coordination ability of the cation utilized in the present is: Li $ ^+ $  < Ca $ ^{2+} $  < Mg $ ^{2+} $ . In Figure 4-d, we show the normalized weight change of the above-synthesized mixture over the course of 350 minutes. Considering the thermal aging and weighing were conducted in an inert environment (N $ _2 $ -filled glovebox, H $ _2 $ O<0.1 ppm, O $ _2 $  <10 ppm), the subsequent weight change, therefore, mainly reflects the evaporation of the 4-tBP. We find that unlike the ultra-fast evaporation of the neat 4-tBP (Figure S20), the presence of the X-TFSI $ _n $  salt significantly decreases the evaporation rate and this reduction is more obvious for the mixture containing Mg-TFSI $ _2 $  compared to those consisting of Li-TFSI or Ca-TFSI $ _2 $  (Figure 4-d). Opposed to Li-TFSI, the  $ \mathrm{Mg - TFSI_2} $  appears to dramatically reduce the evaporation rate of 4-tBP, which leads to weakened additive segregation in the HTL (Figure 4-f, bottom row). We compare thermal stability of the devices by heating them at 60°C in dark under inert environment (  $  N_{2}  $  -filled glovebox) and measuring their PV performances periodically. Over the course of 93 hours, the cells using Mg-  $  TFSI_{2}  $   and Ca-  $  TFSI_{2}  $   retained 68.8 and 57.2%, respectively, which are significantly higher than that using Li-TFSI (41.9%) (Figure S23-a), indicating the enhanced thermal stability of the formers compared to the latter. We note that the reduction of FF and   $  J_{sc}  $   are major factors causing these performance decreases (Figure S23-(b-c)). Additionally, device efficiencies partially were recovered when the cells were left in the dark (Figure S23-a). As shown in Figure S24, micro-sized voids emerged in the aged Li-TFSI device, while no sign of degradation was observed in the aged X-  $  TFSI_{2}  $   devices. This correlates the improved thermal stability of the devices to the enhanced HTL morphology. The appearance of void structures in the aged Spiro-OMeTAD-based HTL was previously attributed to the evaporation of 4-tBP [56]. Moreover, the results on capacitance-frequency in dark (Figure S14) has implied that cation ion migration is more serious in the device using Li-TFSI compared to those using X-  $  TFSI_{2}  $  . At an elevated temperature, this ion migration is accelerated resulting in a dramatic performance loss, which is severer in the Li-TFSI device compared to the X-  $  TFSI_{2}  $   devices, because the   $  Li^{+}  $  ion is more mobile than the   $  Mg^{2+}  $   and   $  Ca^{2+}  $   ion [17, 26]. Besides, the glass transition temperature (  $  T_{g}  $  ) of the organic HTL is known to affect thermal stability of the subsequent devices [57]. Upon replacement of Li-TFSI with X-

# 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we have demonstrated a novel method to improve performance and stability of triple-cation based PSCs through replacing the conventional Spiro-OMeTAD-based HTL additive, Li-TFSI, with TFSI-salts based upon alkaline-earth metal, namely  $ \mathrm{Mg - TFSI_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{Ca - TFSI_2} $  (X-TFSI $ _2 $ ) used in the HTL. We have confirmed that substitution of Li-TFSI with X-TFSI $ _2 $  allows the resulting HTL to possess a higher hole mobility, more favourable band energy alignment for hole injection from perovskite adjacent. Accordingly, the subsequent PSCs exhibit lower non-radiative losses, higher built-in potential and subsequently improved photovoltaic performance while  $ J - V $  hysteresis is weakened. In addition, we have found that this HTL additive manipulation procedure greatly enhanced environmental durability of the HTL, which ultimately increases device stability.
