# 1. Introduction

As one of the most popular research topics in the field of photovoltaics, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted significant attention due to their high device performance, low cost, and ease of processability in recent years   $  [1-5]  $  . Miyasaka and co-workers reported the pioneered work on PSCs with initial power conversion efficiency (PCE) 3.8% for the first time in 2009   $  [6]  $  . Since then, the PCE of PSCs increased at an unprecedented rate and reached a certified PCE of 22.1% in 2016   $  [7]  $  . Such high efficiency was achieved by a high-temperature process (  $  \sim500\ ^{\circ}C  $  ), limiting the application of PSCs on flexible substrates   $  [8,9]  $  . One of the strategies to solve the issue above is to fabricate low-temperature processed inverted p-i-n PSCs by using p-type and n-type materials as bottom and top charge transport layers, respectively.

Inverted PSCs have advantages of low-temperature processing, high fill factor, and negligible J-V hysteresis effects with ITO glass substrates   $  [10]  $  , achieving a certified PCE of more than 18% in 2016   $  [11,12]  $  . In the pursuit of highly efficient inverted PSCs, the selection of stable, high conductivity, and energy level suitable hole transport layers (HTLs) is essentially critical   $  [13,14]  $  . # ABSTRACT

Flexible perovskite solar cells (PSCs) such as inverted PSCs are gaining increasing attention due to their wide range of potential applications, while the selection of stable, high conductivity and energy level suitable hole transport layers (HTLs) is still challenging. Here we introduce dopant-free copper phthalocyanine (CuPc) as HTL in inverted planar PSCs, and demonstrate improved photovoltaic performance and increased stability with high reproducibility and uniformity compared to the PEDOT:PSS based devices. The champion efficiency of PSCs is achieved by 15.4% on rigid substrates and 12.8% on flexible substrates, respectively. The flexible devices also exhibit excellent bending stability, retaining ~85% of the original power conversion efficiency (PCE) after 3000 bending cycles at r = 5 mm without encapsulation. Our study provides a new strategy for fabricating cost-effective, scalable and flexible PSCs with high efficiency, little hysteresis, and excellent stability.

In addition, high-temperature annealing process (  $  \sim500\;^{\circ}C  $  ) restricts their applications. In this regard, developing highly-stable, low-temperature processed and cost-effective HTLs is of great significance for inverted PSCs.

These advantages include high hole mobility, suitable highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy level (matching perovskites well), long exciton diffusion length, excellent thermal and chemical stability, low cost, and ease of synthesis, etc. The hole mobility of CuPc ( $ 10^{-3} $  to  $ 10^{-2} $  cm $ ^{2} $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ , as compared with  $ 4 \times 10^{-5} $  cm $ ^{2} $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $  for spiro-OMeTAD) is sufficient for efficient hole extraction using as HTLs in PSCs ( $ >10^{-3} $  cm $ ^{2} $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ ), indicating a promising potential in PSCs [14]. The champion efficiency (15.4%) of the PSCs is achieved on rigid substrates by using cesium-containing triple cation perovskite material as photo-sensitizer. Remarkably, the PSCs with CuPc as HTL exhibit better stability than PEDOT:PSS based devices when being exposed to ambient air (temperature: 20–25 °C, humidity: 40–50%) without encapsulation. Furthermore, the viability of CuPc based devices on flexible substrates is demonstrated with a champion efficiency of 12.8% and excellent bending stability retaining ~85% of the original PCE after 3000 bending cycles at r = 5 mm.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1.Materials

All materials were obtained without further purification. Anhydrous dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%), anhydrous dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO, 99.9%), anhydrous 2-methoxyethanol (99.8%), polyethyleneimine (PEI, Mn = 10000), copper(II) phthalocyanine (CuPc, 99%) and isopropyl alcohol (IPA, >99.5%) were purchased from Aladdin. Anhydrous chlorobenzene (99.8%) was purchased from Alfa Aesar. In addition, [6,6]-phenyl- $  C_{61}  $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) (>99%), PEDOT:PSS (Heraeus-Clevios P VP AI 4083), Bathocuproine (99.99%), PbI $ _{2} $  (99.99%), PbBr $ _{2} $  (99.99%), CsI (99.99%), HC(NH $ _{2} $ ) $ _{2} $ I (FAI, 99.5%), CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ Br (MABr, 99.5%), and CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ I (MAI, 99.5%) were purchased from Xi'an p-OLED Corp.

# 2.2. Solar cell fabrication

The ITO/glass and ITO/PENs (2.0 cm × 2.0 cm) were firstly etched by diluted hydrochloric acid (2 mol L  $  ^{-1}  $  ) and zinc powder. After ultrasonically cleaned with acetone, ethanol, and deionized water, the ITO substrates were treated with UV-ozone for 15 min. Then CuPc films were deposited on the ITO substrates under room temperature by vacuum evaporation (  $  <10^{-4}  $   Pa,   $  <0.5 \AA s^{-1}  $  ), where the thickness and deposition rate were monitored by a quartz crystal monitor. The real thickness of the CuPc films was obtained from high-resolution scanning electron microscopy (HR-SEM). PEDOT:PSS films deposition was conducted for comparison. Aqueous based PEDOT:PSS was filtered through polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) filters (0.45 μm), spin-coated at 4000 rpm for 30 s, and then thermally annealed at 130 °C for 15 min under ambient atmosphere. Next, MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   precursor solution was obtained by dissolving PbI  $  _{2}  $   (599.3 mg) and CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  I (206.7 mg) in 1 mL of 1:4 (v/v) dimethylsulfoxide/dimethylformamide. The solution was stirred at 60 °C for 2 h in an argon glovebox. The precursor solution

was dropped onto the CuPc film, and the film was spin-coated at 5000 rpm for 30 s in the glovebox. Chlorobenzene (150 μL) was rapidly dropped onto the perovskite-coated HTL film to induce a fast crystallization after ~6 s of spin-coating. Optionally, interfacial modification solution (PEI 0.4 wt % in 2-methoxyethanol) was spin-coated on the top of the HTL film at 5000 rpm for 30 s to enhance the interfacial contact if the film wettability is poor, followed by drying for 30 min under room temperature in the argon glovebox. PEI in 2-methoxyethanol has a positive effect on the interface contact, which can be reflected by contact angles (Fig. S2, Supporting Information). Inserting PEI between the HTLs and perovskite layers can produce high-energy hydrophilic surfaces to facilitate the formation of high-quality perovskite layers by the solution method [43], which was also verified by spin-coated perovskite films (Fig. S3, Supporting Information). The perovskite precursor-coated substrate was heated on a hotplate at 100 °C for 10 min. As for PSCs fabricated with Cs $  _{0.05}  $ FA $  _{0.81}  $ MA $  _{0.14}  $ PbI $  _{2.55}  $ Br $  _{0.45}  $  (MA: CH $  _{3}  $ NH $  _{3}^{+}  $ , FA: NH=CHNH $  _{3}^{+}  $ ) as mixed-perovskite absorption layer, the precursor (1.2 M) solution was obtained in a mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO with a volume ratio of 4:1. The molar ratios of PbI $  _{2}  $ /PbBr $  _{2}  $  and FAI/MABr were both fixed at 0.85:0.15, the molar ratio of CsI/(FAI + MABr) was fixed at 0.05:0.95, and the molar ratio of (FAI + MABr + CsI)/(PbI $  _{2}  $ +PbBr $  _{2}  $ ) was fixed at 1:1. The solution was stirred at 60 °C for 1 h in the argon glovebox. The perovskite films were deposited onto the HTL with a two-step spin-coating procedure. The first step was 2000 rpm for 10 s with a ramp-up of 200 rpm s $  ^{-1}  $ . The second step was 4000 rpm for 20 s with a ramp-up of 1000 rpm s $  ^{-1}  $ . Chlorobenzene (150 μL) was dropped on the spinning substrate during the second spin-coating step at 10 s before the end of the procedure. The perovskite precursor-coated substrate was heated on the hotplate at 100 °C for 10 min. Subsequently, the PC $  _{61}  $ BM (20 mg mL $  ^{-1}  $  in anhydrous chlorobenzene) and BCP (0.5 mg mL $  ^{-1}  $  in isopropyl alcohol) were sequentially deposited via spin-coating at 2000 rpm for 30 s and 4500 rpm for 60 s, respectively. The devices were completed by vacuum evaporating silver contact electrodes (100 nm, < 10 $  ^{-4}  $  Pa), where a shadow mask was used to define the active device area as 9 mm $  ^{2}  $ .

# 2.3. Characterization

The current density-voltage (J-V) curves were measured by a Keithley 2400 system (Tektronix SourceMeter SMU Instruments, USA) at a scan rate of  $ 0.01\mathrm{Vs}^{-1} $  under simulated AM 1.5 solar irradiation at  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $  irradiance generated by a solar simulator (Oriel 94043A, Newport Corporation, Irvine, CA, USA). All these were conducted in ambient atmosphere on an active area of  $ 0.09\mathrm{cm}^{-2} $ . The atomic force microscopy (AFM) images and root mean square roughness of the HTLs and perovskite films were obtained with an Innova SPM 9700 (Shimadzu, Japan) in tapping mode. The perovskite surface and solar cells cross-sectional topography were observed by the field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM, JSM-7600F, JEOL). The formation of the perovskite absorber layer was further confirmed by X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis (PANalytical PW3040/60) with Cu-Kα radiation ( $ \lambda = 1.5406\AA $ ) from  $ 10^{\circ} $  to  $ 60^{\circ} $ . UV-visible spectrophotometer (UV 2600, Shimadzu) was utilized to obtain absorption spectra of the perovskite layer. The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and Raman spectroscopy measurements were taken using a spectrometer (LabRAM HR800, Horiba JobinYvon) under an excitation laser of  $ 532\mathrm{nm} $ . Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) was measured with a self-built instrument. A  $ 440\mathrm{nm} $  laser beam doubled from a Ti:Sapphire laser (Mira 900, Coherent;  $ 76\mathrm{MHz} $ , 130 fs) was used as an excitation source. The time-resolved spectra were taken by a time-correlated single photon counting system (PicoHarp 300, PicoQuant GmbH).

# 3. Results and discussion

The schematic of the CuPc based inverted PSC structure is shown in Fig. 1a. The cell consists of layers of ITO/CuPc/Perovskite/PCBM/BCP/Ag. As depicted in Fig. 1b, the energy level is gradient distribution while the perovskite  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  has a low exciton binding energy ( $ \sim 13\mathrm{meV} $ , lower than  $ k_{\mathrm{B}}T $ ) [44], leading to an easy generation of electron-hole pairs under illumination. The HOMO level of CuPc is ascribed to  $ -5.2\mathrm{eV} $ . The lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level is ascribed to  $ -3.5\mathrm{eV} $ , which is  $ 0.4\mathrm{eV} $  higher than the conduction edge of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film and suppresses the carrier recombination [45]. The alignment of the energy levels for CuPc, perovskite, and PCBM can be expected to facilitate efficient carrier extraction and transport. The cross-sectional SEM image of the device (Fig. 1c) demonstrates that perovskite grains grow through the upper and lower, indicating a well-defined layer-by-layer structure with sharp interfaces. The Raman spectrum of the CuPc film on glass deposited by thermal evaporation process confirms that the chemical composition of CuPc does not change during the deposition process (Fig. S4, Supporting Information). Fig. 1d depicts the UV-vis transmittance spectra measurements of the CuPc and PEDOT:PSS films, both showing excellent transmission performance in the visible wavelength range which will facilitate a high light harvesting efficiency in solar cells. The UV-vis absorption, transmission and reflection spectra of ITO glass, ITO/PEDOT:PSS and ITO/CuPc are provided (Fig. S5, Supporting Information). The PL quenching and time-resolved PL decay measurements both confirm the strong capabilities of CuPc to extract and transport holes.

This can be attributed to the steady  $ J_{sc} $  and FF, which are closely related to the charge extraction and transportation through the interface of perovskite films. Furthermore, the more steady  $ J_{sc} $  of the CuPc based device comparing to the PEDOT:PSS based device can be ascribed to the difference of humidity stability for CuPc and PEDOT:PSS in ambient air. The low-temperature fabrication process enables applications for flexible optoelectronics, and the viability of CuPc based PSCs on flexible substrates is demonstrated. According to previous research, the triple cation perovskite compositions are thermally more stable, more efficient with the addition of inorganic cesium, and contain fewer phase impurities [59]. To achieve better stability and repeatable high efficiency, mixed-perovskite   $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.81}-MA_{0.14}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $   is applied to fabricate CuPc based devices. Fig. 5a displays the reverse-scan response of the PSC fabricated on ITO/PENs flexible substrates, exhibiting a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 18.5 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 0.99 V, and a FF of 70%, with a maximum PCE of 12.8% on an active area of 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  . As the control device, the PSC fabricated on ITO/glass exhibits a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 19.4 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.03 V, and a FF of 77%, with a maximum PCE of 15.4% under the reverse-scan on the same active area. A little drop in PCE can be observed on the flexible devices when compared with the rigid ones, which is related to the higher series resistance of the flexible device [34]. Fig. 5b depicts the efficiencies distribution of the CuPc based flexible PSCs, exhibiting good uniformity and concentration with an average efficiency of 11.52% (max. 12.8%). Bending test is performed up to 3000 cycles to evaluate the mechanical durability of the flexible device as shown in Fig. 5c. After 500, 1500, and 3000 cycles of bending at r = 5 mm, the PCE values are 12.26%, 11.60% and 10.91%

with a retention of 95.7%, 90.6% and 85.2% of the initial value, respectively, demonstrating a high mechanical stability.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, the application of dopant-free CuPc as hole transport layers in inverted planar PSCs is studied. The smooth energy-level transition of different layers can be expected to facilitate charge extraction and transportation within the devices and suppress the carrier recombination. The CuPc film exhibits a uniform and dense morphology, providing the efficient pathway for the hole extraction and transportation. The perovskite film on the smooth CuPc layer exhibits larger grain sizes with fewer grain boundaries, which is beneficial to light absorption and carrier extraction. In addition, the PSCs with CuPc as HTLs exhibit better stability with high reproducibility and uniformity than PEDOT:PSS based devices when exposed to ambient air without encapsulation. The champion efficiency of PSCs is achieved by 15.4% on the rigid substrate. Further, the viability of devices on flexible substrates is also demonstrated with a champion efficiency of 12.8%. The flexible PSCs exhibit excellent mechanical stability after the bending test of 3000 cycles at r = 5 mm. Our work paves the way for realizing low-temperature processed, highly efficient and low-cost inverted PSCs, and provides a new strategy for the fabrication of low-cost, scalable and flexible PSCs with excellent stability.
