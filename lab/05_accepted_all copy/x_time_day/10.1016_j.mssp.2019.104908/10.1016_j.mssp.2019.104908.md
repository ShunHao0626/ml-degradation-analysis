# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid halide perovskite solar cells have attracted intensive attention over the last few years for possessing some promising attributes such as: high efficiency, cheap constituents and simple, low-cost fabrication techniques [1-8]. Additionally, interest in the material has been intensified by the suitability of perovskites to combine with silicon to form a high efficiency tandem solar cell [9-11]. The perovskite material is composed of an  $ \mathrm{ABX}_3 $  structure where A stands for methylammonium (MA), formamidinium (FA) or caesium (Cs); B is lead (Pb) or tin (Sn) and X is chlorine (Cl), bromine (Br) or iodine (I) [12-17]. In a typical perovskite solar cell, the intrinsic perovskite absorber layer (i), is sandwiched between the n-type electron transport layer (n) and p-type hole transport layer (p) [18]. # ABSTRACT

Hybrid halide perovskites are becoming increasingly popular due to their immense potential to be used as a low cost and easily processable solar cell technology. Interfacial engineering is believed to be one of the crucial ways to improve the device performance. In this work, we report the introduction of a small molecular interfacial layer 4,4'-(naphthalene-2,6-diyl)bis(N,N-bis(4-methoxyphenyl)aniline) (TPA-NAP-TPA) between the traditional hole transporting layer poly (3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene:polystyrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) and perovskite  $ (\mathrm{Cs}_{0.15}\mathrm{FA}_{0.85}\mathrm{PbI}_3) $  active layer. Systematic investigations indicate that the incorporation of the small molecular interfacial layer improves the inverted device efficiency by  $ 7\% $  relative. The crystallinity and morphology of the perovskite formed on top of the PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA is favourable compared to the perovskite formed on top of pristine PEDOT:PSS layer. The improvement in the PCE is attributed to increased photocurrent generation. The devices containing the interfacial layer has also depicted improved quantum yield which is in line with the maximised average  $ \mathrm{J_{SC}} $ . Furthermore, the devices with PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA interfacial layer retained  $ 60\% $  of their initial efficiency after 30 days, which is  $ 30\% $  higher than the devices with pristine PEDOT:PSS layer. Including interfacial layers such as TPA-NAP-TPA could be a viable technique in order to enhance the performance and stability of inverted structure perovskite solar cells.

The deposition of these n-type metal oxides require high temperature processing which increases the cost of production and limits their applicability for flexible substrates and large-scale productions [23,24]. On the other hand, the inverted structure perovskite solar cells follow a p-i-n architecture [25]. Neophytou et al. and Pham et al. both reported TPA-NAP-TPA as HTL with MAPbI₃ perovskites and obtained PCE of 13% [33] and 14.63% [27], respectively. From the literature, it is apparent that performance improvement of perovskite solar cells in terms of PCE and stability can be achieved by incorporation of interfacial layers in between the perovskite/HTL interface [37–39,45]. Although MAPbI₃ is the most widely studied perovskite so far but due to the relatively smaller bandgap and poor stability against moisture and temperature, the research attention is now getting shifted towards developing MA-free caesium formamidinium mixed cation perovskites [46,47]. To the best of our knowledge, no group has reported the incorporation of small molecular material TPA-NAP-TPA as an interfacial layer between the PEDOT:PSS and caesium-formamidinium (methylammonium free) mixed cation perovskite Cs₀.₁₅FA₀.₈₅PbI₃ in an inverted p-i-n structure.

In this work, we have incorporated TPA-NAP-TPA as an interfacial layer for the first time between the traditional PEDOT:PSS and   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite layer. The perovskite layers grown on top of PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA layer have shown better structural and morphological properties compared to the perovskite layers grown on top of pristine PEDOT:PSS. The device performance containing the novel interfacial layer were superior compared to the reference devices without the interfacial layer. The performance enhancement is mainly observed to appear from the improved   $  J_{sc}  $  . In addition to that, the stability of the devices containing TPA-NAP-TPA interfacial layer were better compared to the devices without the interfacial layer. After 30 days, the PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA devices retained 60% of the initial efficiency, whereas, for the PEDOT only devices the efficiency dropped to 30%.

# 2.2 Characterization

The current density-voltage (J-V) measurements of the devices were measured with a NREL calibrated Keithley 2400 source meter under 100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $  (AM 1.5G) simulated sunlight. PANalytical Empyrean thin-film XRD machine with Cu-Kα radiation was used to study the structural and crystallographic properties of the perovskite layer formed on top of PEDOT:PSS and TPA-NAP-TPA layers. The surface morphology of the films was measured using scanning electron microscopy (FEI Nova NanoSEM 450). The surface topography and roughness were determined by Bruker Dimension ICON SPM AFM (Atomic Force Microscopy) equipment. External quantum efficiency (EQE) measurements were carried out by PV Measurements QEX10 spectral response system. The impedance analysis was conducted by Autolab PGSTAT-30 equipped with a frequency analyser module in the frequency range from 1 MHz to 1 Hz.

# 3. Results and discussion

The structural and crystallographic properties of the perovskite layers were studied using X-ray diffraction (XRD). Fig. 1 depicts the XRD

spectra of the   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite layers formed on top of pristine PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA layer. The obtained diffraction peaks corresponding to the lattice planes of (101), (012), (021), (202), (211), (122), (024) and (131) for both the cases indicate the formation of black phase   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $   perovskites, which is consistent with the prior reports [14,49]. Despite the presence of several peaks from the XRD spectra, the highest peak intensities found for (101) and (202) suggest that the polycrystalline perovskites grow preferentially along these planes. Additionally, no peaks are observed at around   $  2\theta = 12.7^{\circ}  $  , which confirms the absence of unreacted   $  PbI_{2}  $   in the perovskite layers [16]. The perovskite layers grown on top of PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA has higher peak heights overall compared to the perovskite layers deposited on top of pristine PEDOT:PSS. This confirms improved crystallinity for the perovskites grown on top of the PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA layer. From the literature, it is well known that the FWHM of an XRD peak depends on the crystallinity of the film and lower FWHM values indicate higher crystallinity [52]. From Table S1 and Fig. 2 the lower FWHM values and higher relative normalized peak intensities for the dominant peaks (101) and (202) confirms the higher degree of crystallization for the perovskite layer grown on top of PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA compared to the one grown on the pristine PEDOT:PSS layer. Along with higher degree of crystallinity, these perovskites have also shown relatively increased mean crystallite sizes for most of the characteristic peaks, evident from Fig. 2.

The calculated microstrain and dislocation density values for the respective XRD characteristic peaks are plotted in Fig. 3. Microstrain, also known as lattice strain, is a quantitative measure for the local deviation of atoms originating from porosity or point defects due to different deposition and post deposition conditions [53]. The presence of strain in a crystal can result in distorted lattice planes, resulting in the broadening of the XRD peaks [54]. Higher FWHM values indicate increased strain in the lattice. Dislocation density is a measure for the randomly occurring crystal imperfection [55]. For a good crystalline quality of a perovskite layer, both microstrain and dislocation density values should be as low as possible. From Fig. 3 and Table S1, the overall microstrain values are found lower for the perovskites on top of PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA compared to the perovskites grown on PEDOT:PSS layer only. The calculated dislocation density values are also found smaller for most of the peaks, including the dominant (101) and (202) peaks, which aligns with the presented microstrain values. Since the deposition and post deposition conditions are the same for both cases, except that the underlying layers are different, the variations in the microstrain and dislocation densities are originating from the underlying surfaces of the perovskite layers. In order to study the performance of the small molecular TPA-NAP-TPA material as an interfacial layer, inverted structure perovskite solar cells were fabricated with mixed cation   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $   as a perovskite active layer. The schematic diagram of the device structures both with and without the small molecular interfacial layer are displayed in Fig. 6. Table 2 contains the average and best J-V parameters for the devices with and without the TPA-NAP-TPA layer. The average values were obtained based on 10 devices. Fig. 7 depicts the power conversion efficiency (PCE), open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ), short circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) and fill factor (FF) by means of box plots for all the corresponding devices. The J-V curves corresponding to the best devices are shown in Fig. 8. The maximum PCE obtained from the reference device without the interfacial layer was 9.56% with   $  V_{oc}  $  ,   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF being 886 mV, 19.92 mA/cm² and 54.19%, respectively. With the TPA-NAP-TPA interfacial layer, the champion device has shown maximum PCE of 10.18% with corresponding   $  V_{oc}  $  ,   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF of 873 mV, 22.13 mA/cm² and 52.68%, respectively. From Table 2, the average efficiency is found to be 9.48% for the devices containing pristine PEDOT:PSS as the hole transport layer. By including the TPA-NAP-TPA interfacial layer, the average efficiency increased to 10.06% for the PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA devices which is around 7% higher compared to the average efficiency for the reference devices. The improvement in the device performance is primarily attributed to the improved average   $  J_{sc}  $   of 21.96 mA/cm² for the devices with the TPA-NAP-TPA interfacial layer, compared to the average   $  J_{sc}  $   of 19.66 mA/cm² for the devices containing PEDOT:PSS layer only. The   $  J_{sc}  $   enhancement can be attributed to improved charge transport in the devices due to the presence of the small molecular interfacial layer. This observation can also be correlated with the previous XRD and SEM results which has demonstrated improved crystallinity and bigger grain sizes along with better morphology observed for the perovskite layers grown on top of the PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA layers. From the literature it is known that high crystallinity and improved morphology of the active perovskite layer can lead towards enhancement in the photocurrent which is evident from the obtained results [58,63]. The average   $  V_{oc}  $   of 869 mV and average FF of 52.73% are found for the devices containing the small molecular interfacial layer. The obtained parameters are slightly lower compared to the average   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF of 879 mV and 54.84%, respectively for the devices having pristine PEDOT:PSS as hole transporting layers. The slight reduction in the   $  V_{oc}  $   is possibly arising from the inclusion of the new small molecular interfacial layer between the PEDOT:PSS and active perovskite layer. The reduction in the average fill factor is possibly coming from the slightly rougher surface of the active perovskite layer formed on top of the small molecular interfacial layer. In this case a poor interfacial contact is forming between the perovskite and subsequent electron transport layer due to increased roughness which in turn is decreasing the fill factor.

Fig. 9 shows the external quantum efficiency spectra for the reference and interfacial layer containing devices. The devices containing the TPA-NAP-TPA interfacial layer have shown improved spectral response compared to the device containing the pristine PEDOT:PSS as HTL. The strongest EQE peak is observed around the wavelength of  $ 500\mathrm{nm} $ . The trend found from the EQE can be directly related with the average  $ \mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{sc}} $  improvement observed from the J-V measurements.

This can be related with the poor interfacial contact between the active perovskite and subsequent charge transport layers due to comparatively higher roughness of the perovskite layer formed on top of the PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA layer. The   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF is also reduced slightly due to this issue. However, the PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA device have shown significantly higher   $  R_{REC}  $   compared to the reference devices. According to literature, higher   $  R_{REC}  $   means suppressed charge trapping in the defects [66]. Since, under dark condition, the charge carriers are injected from the external power supply, therefore, the higher recombination resistance can be attributed to the mitigated microstrain and dislocation densities along with enhanced morphological quality of the active perovskite layer which are consistent with the findings of the previous sections. This suggests that the inclusion of the small molecular interfacial layer has aided the extraction of charge carriers more efficiently than the pristine PEDOT:PSS. As a result, the photo-generated charge carriers are more likely to be collected before recombination which explains the high   $  J_{sc}  $   observed from the devices containing the interfacial TPA-NAP-TPA layer. To investigate further on the recombination phenomenon, the recombination lifetime   $  \tau_{REC}  $   was calculated from the following equation [67]:

$$
\tau_ {\mathrm{REC}} = \mathrm{R} _ {\mathrm{REC}} \mathrm{C} _ {\mathrm{REC}}

both the devices. It is observed that the recombination lifetime for the PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA devices are almost 2.6 times higher compared to the devices with PEDOT:PSS only devices. This suggests that the recombination pathways of the photogenerated carriers are noticeably suppressed with the presence of the TPA-NAP-TPA layer as an interfacial layer between the PEDOT:PSS and active perovskite layer.

The stability of the fabricated devices was investigated. Fig. 11 reveals the normalized photovoltaic parameters of the un-encapsulated devices with and without the interfacial layer over a period of 30 days. From the trend of PCE it can be clearly observed that the devices containing the small molecular interfacial layer has an improved stability compared to the devices without the interfacial layer. After 30 days, the PCE of the TPA-NAP-TPA layer containing devices have retained around  $ 60\% $  of the commencing efficiency whereas for the PEDOT:PSS only devices, the efficiency dropped to  $ 30\% $  of its initial value. From Fig. 11, the degradation in the normalized  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  is almost negligible. The main performance drop is resulting from the reduced normalized  $ \mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{sc}} $  and FF. Hence, it is speculated that with ageing, the perovskite structure is being distorted by the trapped charges in the defect states of the perovskite/HTL interface leading to moisture penetration and degradation of the photoactive layer [68]. Due to the water contents and hydrophilic nature of the PEDOT:PSS layer the degradation rate is much severe in the case of pristine PEDOT:PSS as the HTL [34]. The existence of the small molecular TPA-NAP-TPA interfacial layer acts somewhat as a barrier to impede the degradation process through the HTL/Perovskite interface. The similar phenomenon of improved device stability by incorporation of an interfacial layer between the hole selective layer and active layer is consistent with other reports found in the

Overall the incorporation of the small molecular TPA-NAP-TPA material has worked as a surface modifier on top of the PEDOT:PSS layer. As a result, the morphology and crystallinity of the deposited perovskite layer was enhanced. The PCE improvement mainly came from the increased  $ \mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{SC}} $ . This is also consistent with the observed EQE spectra. Slightly higher surface roughness and increased contact resistance evident from AFM and EIS analysis, respectively is assumed to be responsible behind the reduced FF and  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $ . A slight reduction in the FF after employing TPA-NAP-TPA as an HTL with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite was also reported in the literature [27]. An improvement in the stability trend was also observed for the devices containing small molecular TPA-NAP-TPA material as an interfacial layer between the HTL and active layer. Considering this work as the first demonstration of MA-free perovskite ( $ \mathrm{Cs}_{0.15}\mathrm{FA}_{0.85}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ ) fabricated on top of PEDOT: PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA layer it is believed that further improvement can be carried out by optimisation of different deposition and post deposition parameters.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, we have successfully incorporated a novel small molecular material TPA-NAP-TPA as an interfacial layer in between the traditional PEDOT:PSS hole selective layer and   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite active layer in an inverted structure perovskite device configuration. The structural and morphological properties of the active perovskite layers are found to be more favourable when grown on top of the PEDOT:PSS/TPA-NAP-TPA layer, compared to the active layers grown on top of pristine PEDOT:PSS. A 7% improvement in the average PCE was observed for the devices containing the small molecular interfacial layer. The improved device performance mainly came from the enhanced   $  J_{sc}  $   after the inclusion of the small molecular interfacial layer. Additionally, the devices containing the TPA-NAP-TPA interfacial layer demonstrated improved stability compared to the reference devices. Thus, our results demonstrate that novel small molecular material TPA-NAP-TPA is a potential material to be used as an interfacial layer between the HTL and perovskite layer. Further optimizations of the deposition and post-deposition conditions can lead to further improvements in device performance and stability.
