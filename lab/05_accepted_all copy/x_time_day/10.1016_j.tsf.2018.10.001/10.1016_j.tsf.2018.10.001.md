# Abstract

Organometal halide perovskite solar cells are regarded as a remarkable progress in the field of photovoltaic technology. However, the poor stability of perovskite solar cells becomes a serious issue restricting the commercial applications. Here, we investigate the doping of cesium cations in methylammonium lead iodide perovskite solar cells and the effect on the device stability. Homogeneous doping of cesium into methylammonium lead iodide has been obtained with a concentration up to 15%. For the doped solar cells, an efficiency of 13.2~16.5% has been achieved without significant loss compared with the undoped cells. More importantly, the device stability has been dramatically improved by cesium doping. After staying in a dark atmospheric environment for 7 and 30 days, the unencapsulated devices with 20% cesium doping retained 83% and 60% of the initial efficiency, while the undoped cells remained only 59% and 25% of the initial efficiency, respectively. Density functional theory calculations suggest that the cesium dopants are more positively charged than the methylammonium cations. Meanwhile, the doping of cesium increases the averaged charge of iodines. As a result, the Coulomb interactions between the methylammonium cations and the framework of   $  [PbI_{6}]  $   octahedrons are strengthened, which is considered as an efficient factor to prohibit the diffusion of methylammonium cations and therefore stabilize the perovskite structure.

Keywords: Organometal halide perovskite solar cells; Cesium doping; Stability;

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are highly efficient for photovoltaic energy conversion and solution-processable, making them potentially cost-effective for large-scale applications. Nowadays, the best-performing PSCs have reached an efficiency of  $ 22.7\% $  [1]. However, the limited operating lifetime of PSCs is one of the major drawbacks hindering the commercial applications. The degradation mechanism of PSCs in the atmosphere has been intensively studied and the decomposition of perovskite materials induced by water, oxygen and ultraviolet light irradiation is considered the major cause for the device degradation [2-4]. To date, much effort has been made to address the stability issue, such as tuning perovskite materials with mixed halides or more stable organic cations [5, 6], optimizing fabrication techniques of perovskite films [7, 8, 9], choosing inorganic or hydrophobic charge transport layers or electrodes to mitigate the moisture impact [10-12], modifying the interface of electron transport layer and perovskite with a mesoporous scaffold layer or planar self-assembled monolayer to get a homogeneous and compact perovskite layer [13, 14, 15], and encapsulating the devices with hydrophobic and inert sealing materials, [16, 17] etc.

Among the above approaches, adopting more robust organic cations has been verified as an efficient way to increase the device stability   $  [5, 6, 18, 19]  $  . For instance, formamidinium (FA) was used to replace methylammonium (MA) cations, resulting in more thermally stable FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite   $  [5]  $  . Compared with MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  , the stability

improvement of FAPbI $ _3 $  is attributed to the increasing number of hydrogen bonds between the hydrogen atoms of the ammonium cations and iodine ions in the inorganic cage [20]. Furthermore, the introduction of inorganic alkali cations partially substituting FA $ ^+ $  or MA $ ^+ $  groups will also improve the stability [21-25]. In 2013, Boopathi and coworkers introduced lithium (Li $ ^+ $ ), sodium (Na $ ^+ $ ) and potassium (K $ ^+ $ ) as additives in the PbI $ _2 $  precursor solution and found that a small fraction of Na $ ^+ $  and K $ ^+ $  will improve the morphology and crystallinity of the MAPbI $ _3 $  films and increase the long-term stability [21]. Cesium (Cs $ ^+ $ ) and rubidium (Rb $ ^+ $ ), which exhibit more appropriate Goldschmidt tolerance factors, are considered better for the stability of lead iodide perovskites. The polymer-coated RbCsMAFA solar cell maintained 95% of their initial performance at 85 °C for 500 hours under full illumination and maximum power point tracking [25].

Here, we report on the fabrication of   $  Cs^{+}  $  -doped   $  MA_{1-x}Cs_{x}PbI_{3}  $   solar cells with increased efficiency and stability by one-step spin coating method. Homogeneous doping of cesium into methylammonium lead iodide has been obtained with a concentration up to 15%. An efficiency of 13.2~16.5% for cesium-doped cells has been achieved without significant loss compared with the undoped ones. Furthermore, the device stability has been dramatically improved by cesium doping. # Synthesis of the MA $ _{1-x} $ Cs $ _{x} $ PbI $ _{3} $  perovskite

The  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  precursor solution was prepared by dissolving  $ 461\mathrm{mgPbI}_2 $  and  $ 159\mathrm{mgCH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  (1:1 molar ratio) in  $ 400~\mu l $  DMF and  $ 300~\mu l $  DMSO solution. The  $ \mathrm{MA}_{1 - x}\mathrm{Cs}_x\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  precursor solution with  $ x = 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20 $  were obtained by dissolving  $ 461\mathrm{mgPbI}_2 $ ,  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  and CsI with corresponding molar ratio in  $ 620~\mu l $  DMF and  $ 80~\mu l $  DMSO solution. All perovskite solutions were heated at  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 30 min inside the glovebox filled with nitrogen gas prior to film deposition.

# Solar cell fabrication

We fabricated the PSCs with a device structure of FTO/TiO $ _2 $ /MA $ _{1-x} $ Cs $ _x $ PbI $ _3 $ /Spiro-OMeTAD/Au. Etched FTO-coated glass substrates (NSG, 300 nm, 7 Ω/sq) were first cleaned by ultrasonication in deionized water, acetone and isopropanol for 20 min, respectively, followed by ethanol rinsing and O $ _3 $ /ultraviolet treatment for 20 min. A blocking layer of TiO $ _2 $  (bl-TiO $ _2 $ ) about 100 nm in thickness was deposited on the FTO substrate by spin-coating at 2000 rpm for 30 s using titanium diisopropoxide bis (acetylacetonate) solution (75wt% in isopropanol) diluted in n-butyl alcohol (1:10, weight ratio). Then compact FTO/bl-TiO $ _2 $  substrate was heated at 135 °C for 10 min. The mesoporous TiO $ _2 $  (mp-TiO $ _2 $ ) was further spin-coated with TiO $ _2 $  pastes (20 nm-sized particles) diluted in ethanol (1:7 weight ratio) at 3000 rpm for 30 s and sintered at 550 °C for 30 min. The perovskite solutions

Diethyl ether was dripped on the rotating substrate as the antisolvent. The samples were annealed on a hot plate at 100 °C for 10-20 min to produce uniform MA $ _{1-x} $ Cs $ _x $ PbI $ _3 $  films. We have further investigated the effect of  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $ -doping on the device stability during a period of 30 days. The unencapsulated devices were stored in a dark atmosphere ( $ \sim 40\% $  humidity at room temperature) and tested every several days. Figure 7 shows the evolution of normalized PCE,  $ J_{sc} $ ,  $ V_{oc} $  and FF as a function of the storage time. All devices showed a performance degradation. For the undoped cells, the PCE was dramatically decreased during the first seven days and only  $ 59\% $  of the initial PCE remained. And finally only  $ 25\% $  of the initial value remained after 30 days. Compared with the undoped cells, the stability of the  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $ -doped cells was improved with the increase of  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $  ratio. The devices with a  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $  ratio of 0.20 exhibited the best stability, remaining  $ 83\% $  and  $ 60\% $  of the initial efficiency after being exposed in the atmosphere for 7 and 30 days, respectively. The performance parameters of the  $ J_{sc} $ ,  $ V_{oc} $  and FF follow the same trend as PCE. Compared with the  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $ -doped device, the faster decrease of PCE for undoped cells mainly originate from the faster decay of  $ J_{sc} $  and  $ V_{oc} $  (especially  $ J_{sc} $ ), which may be attributed to the faster decomposition of  $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $  cations in cesium-free  $ \mathrm{MAPbI_3} $ . The decomposition process weakens the light absorption and increases the density of defect states in the devices and therefore influences the  $ J_{sc} $  and  $ V_{oc} $ . Our results suggest that the stability of PSC devices is significantly enhanced by a small amount of cesium doping in  $ \mathrm{MAPbI_3} $ .

In order to gain a deep insight into the mechanism of enhanced stability by   $  Cs^{+}  $  doping in   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ , we have conducted DFT calculations to unveil the interactions of doped   $  Cs^{+}  $  cations with the   $  [PbI_{6}]  $  framework. We built the model system containing a   $  \sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2} \times 1  $  superstructure of the tetragonal phase   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ , in which one (two) of the eight MA cations (labeled by S1, S2, ..., S8, see Figure 8) are substituted with   $  Cs^{+}  $ , corresponding to a   $  Cs^{+}  $  concentration of 12.5% (25%). Vienna Ab Initio Simulation Package (VASP) with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof exchange-correlation functional was used for the calculations. The valence-core interactions are described using the Projector Augmented Wave (PAW) method. The plane-wave energy cutoff used for all calculations is 500 eV. The convergence criterion for the forces of all structure relaxations is 0.01 eV/  $  \mathring{A}  $ . Brillouin-zone integrations were performed by using Monkhorst–Pack grids of special points with   $  (2 \times 2 \times 2)  $ . The charge density difference is calculated by using Bader charge analysis method. We first optimized the tetragonal structures of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  and   $  Cs^{+}  $ -doped   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  crystal. The initial lattice constants, the orientation of   $  MA^{+}  $  cations and atomic coordinates is consistent with the result reported in the literature [39]. For 12.5% and 25% Cs-doped   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  crystals, one or two of the   $  MA^{+}  $  cations were substituted by   $  Cs^{+}  $  in the   $  \sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2} \times 1  $  unit cell. Actually, in energetically stable  $ \mathrm{APbI}_{3} $  (A: organic or inorganic cation) perovskites, the Goldschmidt tolerance factor  $ [\mathrm{t}=(\mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{A}}+\mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{I}})/\sqrt{2}(\mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{Pb}}+\mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{I}}) $ , where r is ionic radius] is between 0.8 and 1.0 [40]. Therefore,  $ \mathrm{Cs}^{+} $ -doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3} $  will sustain the perovskite tetragonal phase.

We calculated the charge density difference of all atoms in the three  $ \sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2} \times 1 $  unit cells of tetragonal perovskite phase by Bader charge analysis method. The total charges of Pb, I and MA ions in a unit cell are  $ +7.168\mathrm{e} $ ,  $ -13.128\mathrm{e} $ , and  $ +5.961\mathrm{e} $  for the undoped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ . They are  $ +7.170\mathrm{e} $ ,  $ -13.240\mathrm{e} $ ,  $ +5.216\mathrm{e} $ ,  $ +0.854\mathrm{e} $  ( $ \mathrm{Cs}^+ $ ) for  $ 12.5\% $   $ \mathrm{Cs}^+ $  concentration, and  $ +7.191\mathrm{e} $ ,  $ -13.365\mathrm{e} $ ,  $ +4.464\mathrm{e} $ ,  $ +0.854\mathrm{e}/+0.855\mathrm{e} $  (two  $ \mathrm{Cs}^+ $ ) for  $ 25\% $  cesium concentration. As shown in Table 1, the averaged charge of Pb and MA ions have no significant change with  $ \mathrm{Cs}^+ $  doping, while the charge of I anions exhibits a considerable increase, from  $ -0.547\mathrm{e} $  to  $ -0.552\mathrm{e} $  and  $ -0.557\mathrm{e} $ . In addition, we find the cesium dopants (+0.854e) are more positively charged than MA cations (maximum +0.745e). We then calculated the Coulomb energy of each  $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $  or  $ \mathrm{Cs}^+ $  (S1 to S8 in Figure 8a-c) with the surrounding iodine anions, as depicted in Figure 8d. We considered all cations and anions as point charges. The result indicates that Cs cations

possesses the maximum Coulomb energy of about -2.05 eV (attractive interaction), owing to more positive charge on Cs ions. More importantly, the Coulomb energy of MA cations displays a growing trend with the increase of cesium concentration, particularly for the MA cations at the sites of S5, S6 and S7. For example, the Coulomb energy for the MA cation at the S5 site is -1.572 eV for undoped MAPbI $ _3 $ , -1.762 eV for 12.5% Cs-doped MAPbI $ _3 $  and -1.779 eV for 25% Cs $ ^+ $ -doped MAPbI $ _3 $ . Thus, we conclude that the increased charge of iodines strengthens the Coulomb interactions between the MA cations and the framework of [PbI $ _6 $ ] octahedrons. Different decomposition pathways of MAPbI $ _3 $  have been reported in literature, implying the loss of MA organic groups and the PbI $ _2 $ -rich final products [33, 34, 36, 41, 42]. The intrinsic instability makes the perovskite easy to decompose into PbI $ _2 $  and MAI [38], or vulnerable to humidity and heat [33, 38]. Based on our calculations, the enhanced Coulomb interactions due to the Cs $ ^+ $  incorporation are an efficient factor to prohibit the MA cations from diffusing in the framework and suppressing the phase segregation of perovskite into PbI $ _2 $  and MAI, therefore improving the chemical stability of perovskite structure.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, we have investigated the doping of cesium cations in methylammonium lead iodide perovskite solar cells and the effect on the device stability. Homogeneous doping of cesium into  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  has been obtained with a concentration up to  $ 15\% $ . For

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

the doped solar cells, an efficiency of 13.2~16.5% has been achieved without significant loss compared with the undoped cells. More importantly, the device stability has been dramatically improved by cesium doping. Based on DFT calculations, we found that, the cesium dopants, which are more positively charged than the MA $  ^{+}  $  cations, increase the averaged charge of iodines. As a result, the Coulomb interactions between the MA $  ^{+}  $  cations and the framework of [PbI $  _{6}  $ ] octahedrons are strengthened, preventing the MA cations from diffusing in the [PbI $  _{6}  $ ] framework and therefore stabilize the overall perovskite structure.
