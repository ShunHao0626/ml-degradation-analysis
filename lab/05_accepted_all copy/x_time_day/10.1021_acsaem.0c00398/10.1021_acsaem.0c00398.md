Abstract: Lacking of solvent assisted crystallization, vapor and reaction process controls are essential to structural regulation in vapor deposited organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite thin film. In this work, we prepared   $  (\mathrm{PEA})_{2}(\mathrm{MA})_{n-1}\mathrm{PbnI}_{3n}  $   mixed-dimensional perovskite thin film using mixed-vapor deposition. It was found that the surface morphology and crystalline structure of perovskite thin film could be effectively modulated by changing the chemical mass ratio of PEAI   $  (\mathrm{C}_{6}\mathrm{H}_{5}\mathrm{CH}_{2}\mathrm{CH}_{2}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{I})  $   to MAI   $  (\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{I})  $   powder. The carrier behavior was illustrated by transient absorption spectroscopy and conductive atomic force microscope. Small amount of   $  PEA^{+}  $  was found to re-orientate the growth of perovskite grain to [110] direction and narrow the band edge by passivating the defect states for superior local charge transport, leading to improved power conversion efficiency (PCE) to 18.08%. Increasing the amount of   $  PEA^{+}  $  gradually caused structural transition from three-dimensional (3D) to two-dimensional (2D) perovskite, resulting in uniform charge transport but lower total conductance, however, significantly enhanced humidity stability. An unencapsulated sample with 60% PEAI ratio showed little efficiency decay during the first 12 hours in humidity of 92%, and maintained 87% of the initial efficiency for one month in humidity of 55%. This work provides a further understanding to optimize the structural properties of vapor deposited perovskite thin film for efficient photovoltaic devices.

# Introduction

In the past few years, organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite material has gained great attention in academic research and photovoltaic industry due to its attractive photoelectric properties such as tunable bandgap  $  ^{1}  $  , long carrier diffusion length  $  ^{2}  $   and high absorption coefficient in visible range  $  ^{3}  $  . Incredible improvements in power conversion efficiency (PCE) were achieved from 3.8% to 25.2% in the last decade.

Although three-dimensional (3D) perovskites have achieved impressive high efficiency, problems such as large-area manufacturing, long-term stability and toxicity should be overcome before commercialization  $  ^{4}  $  . Annoyingly, the perovskite thin film is quite sensitive to moisture and oxygen, resulting in degradation under atmosphere, which greatly affects the long-term stability of perovskite solar cells  $  ^{5-6}  $  . It has been reported that water can permeate through the surface of the film, move along the boundaries of the particles, and subsequently degrade the entire film  $  ^{7}  $  . In order to improve the air stability of the device, the methods reported in the literature include device encapsulation   $  {}^{8}  $   and surface passivation   $  {}^{9-10}  $  . However, the above method does not improve the stability of the perovskite layer itself. One of the most common methods for stabilizing halide perovskites is to use the two-dimensional (2D) perovskite layered structure (Ruddlesden-Popper layered perovskite), in which organic hydrophobic macromolecular organics are used as spacer cations to isolate 3D MAPbI  $  _{3}  $    $  ^{11-12}  $  . The 2D perovskite has a general structure formula (A)  $  _{2}  $  (MA)  $  _{n-1}  $  PbnI  $  _{3n+1}  $   (n is an integer), where A is the main aliphatic or aromatic alkyl ammonium cations, such as phenyl ethylammonium (PEA)  $  ^{13-14}  $  , n-butylamine (BA)  $  ^{15-16}  $  , cyclopropylamine

Besides, the device could still maintain relatively high efficiency after stored in a low humidity environment for two months   $  {}^{20}  $  . Hsinhan Tsai et al. proposed “Hot-Casting” technology to fix the out-of-plane perovskite crystal orientation in the (BA)  $  _{2}  $  (MA)  $  _{3}  $  Pb  $  _{4}  $  I  $  _{13}  $   perovskite solar cell. In general, Ruddlesden-Popper layered perovskites are produced by the solution method with varied n value by controlling the molar ratio of organic macro cations to MA cations and lead iodide.

Vapor deposition is one of the mature methods for preparation of large-scale uniform thin film for commercial photovoltaic application. It's also a solvent-free method that avoids the use of toxic solvent in solution methods. Since the first report of vapor deposition for lead-halide perovskite film fabrication by Snaith et al $ ^{22} $ , considerable amount of researches followed up to optimize the deposition process and improved the PCE up to  $ 20.13\%^{23} $ . As the rise of 2D halide perovskite, how to prepare 2D structure in vapor deposited perovskite thin film cause rising attentions. For example, Guo $ ^{24} $  and Jung $ ^{25} $  et al. added organic macromolecular ammonium salt to the precursor of lead iodide, and then reacted with MAI vapor to form 2D/3D hybrid Perovskite. In reported literatures, the introduction of organic macromolecule cation was generally realized by solution method, and the as-prepared 2D thin film was treated by MAI vapor. At the same time, dual source vacuum deposition was applied to prepare 2D/3D/2D structure by Maria-Grazia et al, which showed a reduced charge transport with no direct evidence of surface passivation  $ ^{27} $ . The device PCE can be further improved from  $ 17.03\% $  in pure  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  to  $ 18.08\% $  with  $ 10\% $  PEAI addition. Structural transition and charge transport behaviors were discussed.

The crystal structure of the perovskite films were confirmed by XRD shown in Figure 2a. The pure MAPbI $ _3 $  film showed prominent peaks at 14.24°, 20.10°, 23.65°, 24.58°, 28.65°, 31.99°, which corresponded to (110), (112), (211), (202), (220), (310) crystal planes of the tetragonal MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite. The strongest diffraction peak was the (310) facet. When 10% PEAI powder was blended, the crystal orientation of the perovskite film changed immediately. The (112), (211), (202), (220), (310) peaks became weaker, and (110) peak became the strongest. As the PEAI ratio increased from 10% to 85%, the (110) peak slowly shifted to the lower angle, and a small shoulder belonging to the (0k0) plane of two-dimensional perovskite appears $ ^{28} $  (see Figure 2d). This indicated that the intercalation of PEA $ ^+ $  ions expanded the lattices distance. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) in Figure S2a showed the appearance of  $ \pi-\pi $  bonding at 292 eV, confirming the existence of PEA $ ^+ $  cations $ ^{29} $ . To identify the change of film spectral properties, UV-vis and PL were further employed. From Figure 2b and Figure 2c, it was found that when the PEAI ratio was below 85%, the absorption edge and PL peak at 760 nm were nearly unchanged, and no obvious characteristic signal of 2D perovskite was observed. Besides, the film absorption intensity decreased continuously along with the increase of PEAI ratio (Figure 2e). When the PEAI doping ratio exceeded 85%, strong peaks below 10° appear, implying a quick transition to 2D perovskite with low n value. It may imply a completion between PEAI/PbI $ _2 $  and MAI/PbI $ _2 $  system whose mechanism required further investigation. It's found that absorption peaks at 614 nm

Here, we only give a phenomenological interpretation based on the change of morphology. SEM image in Figure 3a shows that the maximum grain size of pure 3D the control (MAPbI $ _3 $ ) is around 1 um. Besides grain boundaries, there are steps on large grains. In the  $ 10\% $  PEAI sample in Figure 3b, the surface morphology of perovskite films shows densely packed layer structure. Such structure was also observed in samples when increasing PEAI ratio until  $ 85\% $ , while the grains shrank in size. When the ratio reaches  $ 90\% $ , the morphology is different. A distinct large flaky two-dimensional perovskite structure appears. Such a morphology change was likely support the above Figure 2 assumption that the perovskite undergoes change induced by the boundaries and steps of perovskite as shown by Figure 3f. Intercalation of  $ \mathrm{PEA^{+}} $  cations at the boundary or steps would split large grains into several two densely packed small grains. As more  $ \mathrm{PEA^{+}} $  inserted into grains, the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI_3} $  continuously expanded, so that the crystal grains became become smaller and smaller, and the layered structure became more and more obvious. Finally, a 2D perovskite structure with small n value appears. Although it has been studied in the previous literature that large grains seem to have a positive effect on the performance of the device $ ^{31} $ , from the SEM in Figure 3, their morphology has changed significantly, so the grain size cannot be compared laterally.

# Conclusion

In summary, we have successfully fabricated regulated and stabilized perovskite photovoltaic devices using mixed-vapor deposition method. The structural dependence of the perovskite thin film on the ratio of PEAI/MAI was established and its effect on the efficiency and humidity stability was revealed. Change of carrier transport behavior was further illustrated, showing passivation of defect states by  $ \mathrm{PEA^{+}} $ . This work helps to develop fully vapor deposited perovskite thin film for industrially production of green, highly stable solar cells.
