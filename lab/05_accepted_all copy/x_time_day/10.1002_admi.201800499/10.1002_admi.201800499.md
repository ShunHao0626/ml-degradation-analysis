# $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 / \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{Cl} $  Mixed Precursor-Induced Micrometer-Scale Grain Perovskite Film and Room-Temperature Film Encapsulation toward High Efficiency and Stability of Planar Perovskite Solar Cells

Kaimo Deng, Zhongze Liu, Yu Xin, and Liang Li*

The power conversion efficiency and the stability are the most important figures of merit for perovskite solar cells which attract increasing attention in recent years. Herein, the growth of high-quality perovskite films with micrometer-scale grain size through sequential deposition is first reported using  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 / \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{Cl} $  mixed precursors during the first step of deposition. The amount of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{Cl} $  in the precursor has a great influence on the morphology of perovskite films and the device performance. Compared with traditional sequential deposition with pure  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  in the first step, this method renders the perovskite solar cells with an improved power conversion efficiency up to  $ 18.7\% $  and reduced hysteresis. In addition, the fluorinated carbon film prepared at room temperature is reported as an encapsulation layer for perovskite solar cells. The compact and hydrophobic thin film can effectively isolate the device from moisture and oxygen, leading to the largely improved stability in the air. This study not only reveals the effect of chlorine doping on perovskite film formation in the first step of sequential deposition, but also provides a new film encapsulation strategy to improve the device stability in ambient air toward future application.

# 1. Introduction

Hybrid halide perovskite solar cells have been undergoing an unprecedented development, and the record power conversion efficiency (PCE) has reached over 20%.  $  ^{[1-8]}  $   It represents one of the most promising candidates to compete or integrate with silicon-based photovoltaic devices due to its low cost and easy fabrication. Up until now, mesoporous, planar, and inverted architecture have been developed, and each kind exhibits unique features.  $  ^{[9-16]}  $   Typical perovskite solar cells consist of a

voltaic devices.  $  ^{[19,20]}  $   According to the fabrication process, the perovskite film can be obtained through a one-step method or sequential deposition.  $  ^{[9,12]}  $   As for the one-step method, stoichiometric precursors are mixed in a solvent and transformed into perovskite film after solvent evaporation and post-treatment, which stands for the simplest processing. In spite of advantages in cost and upscaling fabrication, crystal growth in the one-step method is relatively hard to control, and the crystallization process is sensitive to the atmosphere condition. Alternatively, the perovskite thin film can also be prepared through sequential deposition, a process with easily controlled nucleus formation and crystal growth.  $  ^{[21,22]}  $   However, the PCE of the device based on sequential deposition processed perovskite thin film still lags behind and needs further improvement.

The stability of perovskite solar cell is another key issue which is closely related to its practical application. The current organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells typically show poor stability in ambient environment, where the moisture is regarded as the main cause of performance deterioration.  $  ^{[23-26]}  $   Compared to the constant pursuit of PCE, less efforts have been paid on the understanding of degradation mechanism and enhancement of device stability. Herein, we report a facile sequential deposition method to fabricate iodide–chloride perovskite films for highly efficient solar cells. For the first time, the morphology of perovskite films can be easily tuned by controlling the amount of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Cl  $   (MACl) in the   $  PbI_{2}/dimethylformamide  $   (DMF) mixed precursor solution during the first step of deposition. Compared with the traditional sequential spin-coating of   $  PbI_{2}  $   and MAI solution, the perovskite film prepared with   $  PbI_{2}/MACl  $   mixed precursor demonstrates larger size crystal grains. Perovskite solar cells based on the mixed precursor exhibit a champion PCE of 18.7%, and the hysteresis is largely reduced, which is mainly ascribed to the increased crystal size and the improved interface property by chlorine incorporation. The impressive PCE of our champion device is higher than that of previous normal structured devices with chlorine incorporation in perovskite film as summarized in Table S1 in the Supporting Information. In the present method, the chlorine doping amount in the precursor can be evaluated exactly. Meanwhile, depositing a hydrophobic fluorinated carbon film (CF-film) to isolate devices through plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD), the perovskite solar cells can maintain 75% of initial PCEs after storing in ambient air for 2 weeks with a humidity of 20–40%. As far as we are concerned, this is the first report about application of CF-film as a protecting layer for durable performance of perovskite solar cells. The PECVD technique has advantages of room-temperature processing, inert atmosphere, and good compatibility with large-scale production, which meet the rigorous requirements of perovskite film treatment.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we develop a modified sequential deposition technology to prepare high-quality perovskite thin films through using   $  PbI_{2}/MACl  $   mixed precursor during the first step of deposition. The morphology of perovskite films can be tuned by the amount of MACl in precursor solutions. The grain size of perovskite films increases as the MACl content in the precursor increases. The PCE of the mixed precursor-based perovskite solar cell is higher than that of the device without MACl, and the hysteresis effect is well suppressed due to the chlorine doping. Perovskite solar cells based on the optimum precursor exhibit a champion PCE of 18.7%. The hydrophobic CF-film prepared by PECVD at room temperature acts as a protecting layer to effectively isolate moisture and oxygen, largely boosting the stability of perovskite solar cells in the air. Our results provide an alternative method to synthesize pinhole-free perovskite films with micrometer-scale grain size and a room temperature–processed protection technology to simultaneously improve the PCE and stability of perovskite solar cells.

# 4. Experiment Section

Fabrication of Perovskite Solar Cells: fluorine doped tin oxide (FTO) substrates were patterned with Zn powder and dilute HCl, and then cleaned by ultrasonication with acetone, ethanol, and deionized water each for 15 min. The UV-treated FTO substrates were spin-coated with a Ti precursor solution and heated in a muffle furnace at 500 °C. A TiCl₄ treatment (50 × 10⁻³ M) at 70 °C followed by a repeated annealing process was used to eliminate the tiny pinholes in the TiO₂ thin film for enhanced hole-blocking property. After cooling down to room temperature, the compact TiO₂/FTO substrates were transferred into a glove box under nitrogen atmosphere with moisture and oxygen level below 0.1 ppm. The PbI₂-xMACl precursor solution (462 mg PbI₂ in 1 mL DMF, 50 μL) was dropped on the compact TiO₂/FTO substrates and spin-coated at 3000 rpm for 30 s. After drying, the MAI/isopropanol solution (50 mg mL⁻¹) was spin-coated at 3000 rpm for 30 s. The resulting films were heated at 150 °C for 20 min. The hole transport layer was spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 30 s with a solution of spiro-OMeTAD (72.3 mg) in anhydrous chlorobenzene (Aldrich, 1 mL) containing 4-tert-butylpyridine (Aldrich, 28.8 μL) and 17.5 μL of lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) solution (520 mg Li-TFSI in 1 mL of acetonitrile, Aldrich). The device was left in a desiccator overnight. Finally, 90 nm thick silver was thermally evaporated as the electrode. The final structure of perovskite solar cells is FTO/compact-TiO₂/perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD/Ag with an active area of 0.078 cm².

CF-Film Deposition: CF-film films were prepared in a capacitance-coupled PECVD reactor, and the schematic representation of the apparatus has been provided in the Supporting Information. It consists of two parallel-plate circular electrodes with a diameter of 220 mm and a fixed distance of 50 mm. The upper electrode was powered by a 27.12 MHz power supply through a matching network, while the lower electrode was grounded. The as-prepared devices were placed on the lower electrode with the metal electrode facing upward. The gas-extraction system equipped with a mechanical pump and a molecular pump reduced the chamber pressure to   $  5 \times 10^{-3}  $  Pa. Then, the Ar/C  $  _{4}  $  F  $  _{8}  $  mixture gas was injected into the discharge chamber with Ar and C  $  _{4}  $  F  $  _{8}  $  gas flow rates of 100 and 20 sccm, respectively, at a working pressure of 2 Pa. The discharge power was set at 100 W with a growth rate of 4 nm min  $  ^{-1}  $  .

Characterizations: The morphology of the films was characterized by a field-emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM) (Hitachi, SU8010). The phase of samples was measured using an XRD (D/MAX-III-B-40KV, Cu Kα radiation = 0.15418 nm). The UV–visible absorption spectra were recorded with a spectrophotometer (Shimadzu, UV 3600). J–V curves were measured in air with a source meter (Keithley, 2400) under the illumination with AM 1.5G simulated solar light. The light intensity was adjusted to 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  with a calibrated silicon cell. The EQE spectra were measured by using a Newport IPCE system under monochromatic light illumination. The impedance spectroscopy was measured by an electrochemical workstation (Autolab, PGSTAT 302N) under AM 1.5G illumination at applied voltages close to open-circuit voltage with an alternative signal amplitude of 5 mV in the frequency range of 400–0.01 kHz. The thickness of the CF-film deposited on silicon was assessed by a profilometer to calculate the growth rate. FT-IR spectra for CF-films were recorded in transmission mode in a JASCO FT/IR 6200 apparatus. The CF-film surface morphology was measured with an atomic force microscope (Asylum Research, MFP-3D-BIO). For the aging test of the perovskite solar cells with or without fluorinated carbon film, the devices were placed in ambient air with a temperature of 25 °C and a humidity level of 20–40%.
