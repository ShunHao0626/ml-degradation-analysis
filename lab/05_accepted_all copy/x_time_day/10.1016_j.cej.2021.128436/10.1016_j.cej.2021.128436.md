Interfacial engineering has been proved to be an effective way to enhance the performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) by reducing interfacial charge recombination. In this work, two-dimensional (2D)   $  Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   nanoflakes with high charge mobility are synthesized by facile composited molten salt method, and used as electron transport layer (ETL) of PSCs for the first time. The PSCs based on single 2D   $  Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   nanoflakes ETL exhibit power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 9.12%, which is 110% higher than those without ETL (4.32%). To fill pinholes and defects on the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film, we also use the 2D   $  Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   nanoflakes to modify the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film, and fabricate   $  SnO_{2}/2D-Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   new hybrid ETL to effectively reduce recombination at hetero-interface. Owing to the high charge mobility of 2D   $  Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   nanoflakes and cascade band alignment between perovskite active layer and tin dioxide, more efficient electron transport in PSCs is obtained than the single   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL. Moreover, novel hybrid ETL provides a smoother and more hydrophobic surface for larger perovskite crystal formation. Compared with PSCs with single   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL, the PCE of PSCs based on   $  SnO_{2}/2D\ Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   hybrid ETL is improved to 19.06% from 16.29% with suppressed hysteresis. More interesting, the stability of PSCs with the new hybrid ETLs is also improved due to the improved crystallinity of perovskite active layer. This work shows the 2D   $  Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   material has potential for applications in PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved astonishing rapid development in the past ten years, its power conversion efficiency (PCE) has risen from 3.8% to 25.5% in the past ten years [1-5]. The impressive photovoltaic performance of PSCs benefits from superior optoelectronic properties of perovskite light absorbing material, such as low exciton binding energy, ambipolar charge transport, high charge carrier mobility, large light absorption coefficient and long carrier diffusion length [6-8]. The PCE of single junction PSCs is approaching the theoretical limit of 33.5% at an extremely fast rate, which is comparable with common state-of-art solar cells, such as CIGS, GaAs, CdTe and single-crystalline silicon-based solar cells [9-11]. Especially, PSCs can be manufactured by simple solution processing methods at low temperature, showing great potential for large scale

In general, high performance PSCs usually use organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite material as the light absorption layer sandwiched between electron transfer layer (ETL) and a hole transfer layer (HTL). Although light absorption layer is an important part of solar cells, the ETL between cathode and perovskite light absorbing layer cannot be ignored, which can effectively transport electrons, as well as prohibit the holes from recombination   $  [14–16]  $  . Among many ETL materials, inorganic metal oxides have arisen much interest due to their good transparency, low work function and high thermal stability   $  [17–19]  $  . Inspired by the widely use of   $  TiO_{2}  $   in dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs),   $  TiO_{2}  $   was first successfully used as ETL in PSCs   $  [20–22]  $  . However,   $  TiO_{2}  $   suffers from intrinsic disadvantages, including poor conductivity, low carrier mobility, high temperature processing and UV light unstable   $  [23,24]  $  . Recently,   $  SnO_{2}  $   has been emerged as an efficient alternative ETL thanks to its appealing optoelectronic properties such as low temperature fabrication, transparent conductivity and favorable band alignment with perovskite materials   $  [27–29]  $  . In 2015, several independent works have opened the gate for the application of   $  SnO_{2}  $   in PSCs, which greatly promoted the development of   $  SnO_{2}  $   based PSCs. For example, Ma et al. first applied the   $  SnO_{2}  $   compact layer as ETL through sol-gel method, which achieved a PCE of 7.43%   $  [30]  $  . Tian et al. improved   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film fabrication method by spin-coating   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanoparticles and annealed at 200 °C, the based on device achieved 13% PCE   $  [31]  $  . Fang et al. made a big step forward for the application of   $  SnO_{2}  $   in PSCs, they prepared   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film by thermal annealing   $  SnCl_{2}\cdot2H_{2}O  $   precursor solution at 180°C. As a result, they achieved 17.21% reverse scan PCE at low temperature due to fully covered   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film, which provided a guiding method for low temperature solution processed high efficient PSCs   $  [32]  $  . Nevertheless, fabricating   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film by spin-coating   $  SnO_{2}  $   precursor solution on ITO or FTO substrate can result in an uneven surface with pinholes and protrusions, which can cause serious carrier recombination because of direct contact between the perovskite absorber layer and ITO or FTO   $  [33–35]  $  . Some groups tried to fabricate fully covered and pinhole-free high quality   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film by changing   $  SnO_{2}  $   precursor solution or improving deposition method. Hagfeldt et al. adopted atomic layer deposition (ALD) method to fabricate a continuous, fully covered, virtually pinhole-free   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film, the corresponding device gave a PCE of 18% with extremely high   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.19 V   $  [15]  $  . You et al. fabricated high quality dense and pinhole-free   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film by spin-coating commercialized   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloidal precursor, the corresponding PSCs achieved 19.9% certificated efficiency, which opened a new era for   $  SnO_{2}  $   based PSCs   $  [36]  $  . Thus, a fully covered and pinhole-free   $  SnO_{2}  $   film is required for achieving highly efficient PSCs.

To fabricate dense and pinhole-free  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  thin film, some researchers have adopted a new compact layer on the surface of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  thin film to suppress hetero-interface recombination. Tian et al. used  $ \mathrm{ZnO / SnO_2} $  double layer as ETL, leading to good charge collection and large carrier recombination resistance, the corresponding device exhibited a PCE of  $ 15.2\% $  and improved stability [37]. Seulki et al. used  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / SnO_2} $  bilayer as ETL, leading to better physical contact, better energy level transition and suppressed electron-hole recombination, the corresponding PSCs achieved a PCE exceeding  $ 21\% $  and excellent stability.[34] Gratzel et al. used gradient F-doped  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  bilayer as ETL, leading to decreased band offset and more efficient charge carrier transport, the corresponding PSCs gave a PCE over  $ 20\% $  and enhanced  $ V_{oc} $  [38]. Zhang et al. adopted  $ \mathrm{WO_x / SnO_2} $  double ETL structure to prevent electrons and holes from recombination through pinholes and cracks, resulting in more efficient charge extraction and transfer [39]. Here, the recent published work based on bilayer  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ETLs to improve the performance of PSCs is summarized in Table S1.

Recently, layered Bi-containing compounds (Bi $ _2 $ O $ _2 $ S, Bi $ _4 $ O $ _4 $ S $ _3 $ , Bi $ _2 $ O $ _2 $ Se, etc.) have received widespread attention from researchers, and they have great potential application in photoelectric field, thermoelectric field and photo catalysts thanks to their ultrahigh carrier mobility, suitable bandgap [40,41]. Among them, 2D n-type semiconductor Bi $ _2 $ O $ _2 $ Se exhibits ultrahigh hall mobility over 20,000 cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $  and high carrier concentration of 10 $ ^{19} $  –10 $ ^{20} $  cm $ ^{-3} $ [42–45], which is comparable to classical 2D material: graphene, black phosphors, MoS $ _2 $  [46–48]. Additionally, ultrathin Bi $ _2 $ O $ _2 $ Se plates show excellent environmental stability even after exposure to air for months [42,45]. Our group had applied another kind of bismuth compound Bi $ _2 $ O $ _2 $ S as ternary component into polymer solar cells, leading to enhanced photon harvesting and better surface morphology of photoactive layer [49]. In addition, we also found that the modification of the ZnO nanorod array with Bi $ _2 $ O $ _2 $ S can passivate oxygen vacancies related defects and enhance the crystallinity of the active layer [50]. Overall, since Bi $ _2 $ O $ _2 $ Se possesses

such superior electrical properties, it should be a promising material in the application of solar cells.

Here, pure 2D   $  Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   nanoflakes are obtained through facile composite molten-salt growth method, and used as ETL in PSCs for the first time. PSCs based on single   $  Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   ETL show highest PCE of 9.12%, which is 110% higher than those without ETL (4.32%). We also use the   $  Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   nanoflakes to modify the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film, and fabricate   $  SnO_{2}/2D-Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   new hybrid ETL. The results show that prepared   $  SnO_{2}/2D-Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   new hybrid ETL can effectively reduce recombination at hetero-interface. Thanks to the high carrier mobility of   $  Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   nanoflakes and cascade band alignment between perovskite active layer and   $  SnO_{2}  $ , more efficient electron transport is obtained than the single   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL. Moreover, the new hybrid ETL provides a smoother and more hydrophobic surface for larger perovskite crystal formation. The PSC based on   $  SnO_{2}/2D-Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   hybrid ETL exhibits a higher average PCE of 19.06%, and improves by 17% compared with those devices based on single   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL (16.29%). More interestingly, the stability of PSCs with the hybrid ETLs is enhanced. This work indicates that 2D   $  Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   nanoflakes have great potential in the application of PSCs.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Materials

The ITO glass was purchased from China Southern Glass Holding Corp. MAI (99.5%),   $  PbI_{2}  $   (99.99%), 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N'-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene) (Spiro-OMeTAD) (99.8%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. without further purification. Chlorobenzene (99.9%), DMF (99%), Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI) (99.95%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich.   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloidal dispersion liquid, DMSO (99%) were purchased from Alfa Aesar. Aceton-itrile (99.9%) was purchased from Acros, 4-tert-butylpyridine (96%) was purchased from TCI. All reagents were of analytical grade and used without further purification.

# 2.2. Synthesis of  $ \mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm{O}_2\mathrm{Se} $

$ \mathrm{Bi}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{2}\mathrm{Se} $  power was synthesized by the composite molten-salt method using  $ \mathrm{KNO}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{LiNO}_3 $  as composite molten salts as reported [51]. Firstly,  $ 12.032\mathrm{g} $   $ \mathrm{LiNO}_3 $  and  $ 23.968\mathrm{g} $   $ \mathrm{KNO}_3 $  were put into  $ 100~\mathrm{mL} $  Teflon vessel. Then a mixture of  $ 4\mathrm{mmol} $   $ \mathrm{Bi(NO_3)_3\cdot 5H_2O} $  crystal and  $ 2\mathrm{mmol} $  Se powder was throw into Teflon vessel. After that  $ 8\mathrm{mL} $  hydrazine hydrate  $ (\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{H}_4\cdot \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}) $  ( $ >80\% $ ) and  $ 20~\mathrm{mL} $  deionized water were added into the vessel. And then  $ 20\mathrm{min} $  ultrasonic concussion was adopted to make sure that the reactants were fully mixed. The Teflon vessel was then sealed in stainless autoclave and then put into a  $ 200^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  furnace for  $ 24\mathrm{h} $ . After naturally cooled down to room temperature, the solid product was rinsed with deionized water and ethanol, and then it was collected by suction filtration.  $ 1.5\mathrm{mg} $   $ \mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm{O}_2\mathrm{Se} $  powder was put into  $ 1\mathrm{mL} $  dichlorobenzene by  $ 48\mathrm{h} $  vigorously ultrasonic concussion. Afterward, the green solution (2D  $ \mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm{O}_2\mathrm{Se} $ ) was centrifuged at  $ 8000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 15\mathrm{min} $ .

# 2.3. Preparation of precursor solution

$ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  colloidal dispersion liquid was used before mixed up with deionized water at 1:4 by volume. To prepare perovskite precursor solution,  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  (1.25 mM) was mixed with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  (1.2 mM) in 1 mL anhydrous DMF and DMSO mixed solution (9:1 by volume). The hole transporting layer (HTL) precursor solution was prepared by dissolving  $ 72.3\mathrm{mg} $  Spiro-OMeTAD into  $ 1\mathrm{mL} $  chlorobenzene, with addition of  $ 29~\mu \mathrm{l} $  of 4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP, TCI,  $ >96.0\% $  ) and  $ 18~\mu \mathrm{l} $  of lithium bis (trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI, Sigma-Aldrich,  $ 99.95\% $  ) solution. Li-TFSI solution was prepared by dissolving  $ 520\mathrm{mg} $  Li-TFSI into  $ 1\mathrm{mL} $  acetonitrile (Sigma-Aldrich,  $ 99.8\% $  ) in advance.

# 2.4. Device fabrication

ITO-coated substrates (  $  \sim  $  10   $  \Omega/square  $  ) were sequentially cleaned by sonication with detergent, deionized water, acetone and isopropanol for 15 min. Then ITO substrate was transferred to an 80°C oven for drying, followed by oxygen plasma treating for 10 min. SnO  $  _{2}  $   colloidal dispersion liquid was then casted onto ITO substrate at 3500 revolutions per minute (rpm) for 30 s. Next, the substrate was placed on a hot plate at 150 °C for 1 h. 2D Bi  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{2}  $  Se dichlorobenzene solution was spin-coated onto substrate at 2000, 4000 and 6000 rpm for 40 s. The above samples were treated with oxygen plasma to increase compatibility with perovskite. Subsequently, perovskite precursor solution was casted on the samples at 3500 rpm for 40 s. During rotation, 200   $  \mu  $  l chlorobenzene was quickly casted onto perovskite layer at 10 s. Then the samples were quickly transferred to 100°C hot plate for 20 min to form black and bright perovskite thin film. The HTL precursor solution was then spin-coated onto perovskite thin film at 4000 rpm for 30 s, and then place the sample in a desiccator overnight to ensure that Spiro-OMeTAD was adequately oxidized. Finally, 2 nm MoO  $  _{x}  $   and 80 nm Ag cathode was evaporated onto HTL at a vacuum below 3 × 10  $  ^{-4}  $   Pa.

# 2.5. Characterizations of film and device

The UV–vis spectra were measured with a HP 8453 spectrophotometer. Photoluminescence spectra (PL) were recorded by SPEX 1681 automated spectrum fluorometer. Electrochemical measurement was carried on a VMP3 sp-150 electrochemical workstation at a scan rate of   $  20 \, mV s^{-1}  $   in a solution of   $  0.1 \, mol/L  $   tetrabutylammonium hexafluorophosphate (TBAPF6)/acetonitrile (CH₃CN). The working electrodes were polished and cleaned before depositing the droplet and the electrolyte solutions were thoroughly deoxygenated. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was measured on ZEISS Merlin. Phase analysis was performed on X-ray diffraction (XRD) using Bruker Discovery 08. The current density–voltage (J-V) curves of PSCs were recorded on Keithley 2400 source unit under AM 1.5 G solar simulator.

It is clear that the perovskite thin film deposited on   $  SnO_{2}/2D\ Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   (20 nm) gives more enhanced XRD intensity compared with that deposited on   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL. Among the diffraction peaks, peak at   $  2\theta = 12.67^{\circ}  $   represents (110) planes of   $  PbI_{2}  $   (marked with star), which can reflect the degradation of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  . It is obvious that the perovskite thin film deposited on single   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL exhibits strongest   $  PbI_{2}  $   peak, suggesting most serious degradation. The perovskite thin film deposited on   $  SnO_{2}/2D\ Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   (30 nm) and   $  SnO_{2}/2D\ Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   (20 nm) maintain better stability against oxygen and   $  H_{2}O  $  . The XRD result of mild decomposed perovskite thin film deposited on   $  SnO_{2}/2D\ Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   hybrid ETL confirms the improvement of stability. The continuous output stability is also tested under AM 1.5G illumination at maximum power point, as shown in Fig. 8e. The devices with   $  SnO_{2}/2D\ Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   (20 nm) hybrid ETL hold steady   $  J_{sc}  $   for 300 s, while the devices with pure   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL decrease gradually. These stability tests prove that the devices with   $  SnO_{2}/2D\ Bi_{2}O_{2}Se  $   (20 nm) hybrid ETL show better stability than the devices with pure   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL.

# 4. Conclusion

We have synthesized pure 2D  $ \mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm{O}_2\mathrm{Se} $  nanoflakes through facile composite molten-salt growth method. We use 2D  $ \mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm{O}_2\mathrm{Se} $  nanoflakes as ETL, and PSC based on single 2D  $ \mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm{O}_2\mathrm{Se} $  ETL shows a PCE of  $ 9.12\% $ , which suggests that 2D  $ \mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm{O}_2\mathrm{Se} $  nanoflakes can be used as an alternative ETL. Furthermore, we propose  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / 2\mathrm{D - Bi}_2\mathrm{O}_2\mathrm{Se} $  new hybrid ETL to effectively improve the performance of PSCs for the first time. The mechanism of the improvement is characterized by SEM, XRD, AFM, EIS, dark  $ J - V $ , PL etc. We can conclude that introducing 2D  $ \mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm{O}_2\mathrm{Se} $  layer can improve crystallization of perovskite thin film as well as facilitate charge transfer and reduce carrier recombination. The PSCs based on  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / 2\mathrm{D} $   $ \mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm{O}_2\mathrm{Se} $  (20 nm) hybrid ETL exhibit higher average PCE of  $ 19.06\% $ . Besides, the stability of PSCs is enhanced with the application of hybrid ETLs. These results show that  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / 2\mathrm{D} $   $ \mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm{O}_2\mathrm{Se} $  hybrid layer is excellent electronic transport layer in solar cells, and  $ \mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm{O}_2\mathrm{Se} $  material has potential application prospect in optoelectronic devices.
