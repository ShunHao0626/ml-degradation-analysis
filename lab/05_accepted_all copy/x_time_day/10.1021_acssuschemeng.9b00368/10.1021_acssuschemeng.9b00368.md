# Enhanced Electronic Quality of Perovskite via a Novel   $  C_{60}  $   o-Quinodimethane Bisadducts toward Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

ABSTRACT: As one of the more promising potential photovoltaic technologies, planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are arousing worldwide interest for their many advantages. Now, PSCs are being developed toward the direction of high-performance and longevity. However, the defects of the polycrystalline perovskite active layer limit further improvement of the device performance. Seeking simple and efficient strategies to reduce these trap states in the perovskite active layer is highly desirable. Here, a novel nonfunctionalized fullerene   $  C_{60}  $   o-quinodimethane bisadduct   $  [C_{60}(QM)_{2}]  $   was dissolved in chlorobenzene (CB) solvent and introduced into a   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   active layer by antisolvent dripping. Results showed that the introduced   $  C_{60}(QM)_{2}  $   could effectively reduce the trap density of the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   active layer, facilitating carrier extraction/injection from   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   to spiro-OMeTAD. As a result, a highest PCE of 18.4% for the PSC based on   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/C_{60}(QM)_{2}  $   was obtained, which increased by 10.1% compared with 16.7% for the reference device. Meanwhile, the air stability for   $  C_{60}(QM)_{2}  $  -passivated PSCs was also improved significantly. This approach provides a direction for designing highly efficient and air stable PSCs.

KEYWORDS: trap density, high-performance, air stable, perovskite solar cell

# INTRODUCTION

Organic-metal-halides based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are arousing increasing attention in recent years with the great progress in device efficiency, increasing rapidly from 3.8% in 2009  $  ^{1}  $  to the 23.3% in 2018.  $  ^{2}  $  This makes PSCs one of the most promising photovoltaic technologies. In general, for highly efficient PSCs, the perovskite films are usually sandwiched by interfacial transport layers, followed by a metal counter electrode.  $  ^{3-5}  $  The rapid progress that has been made for the PSCs could mainly originate from the excellent photoelectric characteristics of these perovskites, for example, their high hole/electron mobility, large extinction coefficient, and long charge carrier diffusion lengths.  $  ^{6-8}  $

Despite this significant progress, solution-processed polycrystalline perovskite films inevitably have numerous trap states existing in the grain boundaries (GBs) and surface, including under-coordinated halide anions or metal cations induced by the evaporation of methylammonium iodide, as well as ion migration.  $  ^{9-14}  $   These trap states will capture the electron/hole, leading to unwanted carrier recombination. This will cause serious energy loss, which limits the performance of the devices.  $  ^{15}  $   Meanwhile, it has been observed that the degradation of the perovskite polycrystalline films usually

Considering the ionic nature of perovskite crystals, the under-coordinated defects in these materials are typically charged, negatively or positively. Therefore, different passivation molecules equipped with Lewis acid or Lewis base functional groups have been proposed to neutralize these charged defects so that these trap states can be effectively reduced. $ ^{17-20} $  The Lewis base groups include carbonyl ( $ -\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{O} $ ) or cyanide ( $ -\mathrm{C}\equiv\mathrm{N} $ ) etc. that can donate its lone pair of electrons and neutralize charges at the metal cations. Building blocks of organic molecules such as thiophene or pyridine have also been proven to be effective in forming Lewis adducts with positively charged  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  ions, $ ^{21-23} $  while for the Lewis acid, quaternary ammonium ( $ \mathrm{R}_{4}\mathrm{N}^{+} $ ) or ammonium-group ( $ -\mathrm{NH}_{3}^{+} $ ) containing molecules, as well as some strong halogen bond donors (Lewis acid, electron acceptor) can effectively mitigate the coordinatively unsaturated halide anions defects ( $ \mathrm{X}^{-} $ ) and improve charge transport/collection efficiency. $ ^{17,24,25} $  In addition, these Lewis groups were designed to anchor hydrophobic organic molecules to improve the storage stability of device at the same time. Among large selections, many groups chose the fullerene cage as the framework and developed types of functionalized passivators such as bis-PCBM, fluoroalkyl-substituted fullerene ( $ \mathrm{DF-C}_{60} $ ), and so on. $ ^{26-28} $  They were introduced into the perovskite film by a doping (BHJ) or antisolvent process (PHJ) to realize perovskite films with better electronic qualities. However, functionalization imposes a complicated synthetic procedure that is not easy to obtain. In fact, as an extraordinary electron transporting material, fullerene's intrinsic electron-accepting property also plays a critical role in passivating the halide-rich defects by electron transfer, which has not been intensively studied in previous work. $ ^{29} $  Therefore, more effort is required to develop successful nonfunctionalized fullerene passivators and confirm such interaction.

In this work, we introduced novel nonfunctionalized fullerene acceptors  $ (\mathrm{C}_{60}(\mathrm{QM})_2) $  into the perovskite film by the antisolvent deposition method. The results show that  $ \mathrm{C}_{60}(\mathrm{QM})_2 $  could potentially passivate these defects existing in

the polycrystalline  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer and improve the carrier extraction at the perovskite/HTM interface, resulting in an increase in the short-circuit current density  $ (J_{\mathrm{sc}}) $ , open-circuit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{oc}}) $  and fill factor (FF). Consequently, the modified device with  $ \mathrm{C}_{60}(\mathrm{QM})_2 $  yields a higher efficiency of  $ 18.4\% $  in comparison with that  $ (16.7\%) $  of the reference PSCs, and the hysteresis of the  $ \mathrm{C}_{60}(\mathrm{QM})_2 $ -modified device is also suppressed. In addition, introduced  $ \mathrm{C}_{60}(\mathrm{QM})_2 $  could also protect the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film from moisture ingress for its hydrophobic property. Therefore, the stability of the device cooperating with  $ \mathrm{C}_{60}(\mathrm{QM})_2 $  is also improved significantly in the ambient atmosphere, which still maintains over  $ 80\% $  of its original efficiency after 35 days.

# EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

Preparing   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}  $   Precursors. We prepared the   $  TiO_{2}  $   precursor according to the literature.  $  ^{30}  $   First, tetrabutyl titanate (10 mL) was added into absolute ethyl alcohol (90 mL), and the solution was stirred at 25 °C for 0.5 h. Second, equal volumes (10 mL) of acetylacetone and acetic acid were mixed together and then poured into the above solutions, followed by stirring for another 0.5 h. Finally, distilled water (10 mL) was added into the mixed precursors. After being stirred at 25 °C for 10 h, the   $  TiO_{2}  $   precursor was placed in the drying cupboard for 1 day without vibration. The synthesis of   $  SnO_{2}  $   precursor is similar to the reference.  $  ^{31}  $   The 0.05 M   $  SnO_{2}  $   precursor was obtained by dissolving 0.115 g   $  SnCl_{2}\cdot2H_{2}O  $   in anhydrous ethanol (10 mL) with stirring for 120 min.

Devices Fabrication. FTO substrates were cleaned ultrasonically in detergent, acetone, and isopropyl alcohol, each for 15 min. Then these FTO-based glasses were annealed at 500 °C for 1 h in the muffle to remove the residual organics. Before being used, the FTO was treated by ultraviolet-  $  O_{3}  $  for 10 min. Then  $  TiO_{2}  $  precursor was spun onto these FTO bases for 30 s at 3000 rpm, followed by sintering at 130 °C for 10 min and 450 °C for 120 min. When the  $  TiO_{2}  $  films cooled down,  $  SnCl_{2}  $  precursors were spun onto these  $  TiO_{2}  $  films with the same speed and time as that for  $  TiO_{2}  $ . Then these films were sintered for 60 min at 180 °C. Prior to depositing the perovskite films, the as-prepared films were treated with ultraviolet-  $  O_{3}  $  for another 10 min. The perovskite layers can be obtained by antisolvent dripping.  $  ^{32}  $  Perovskite precursor was prepared by dissolving  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  and  $  PbI_{2}  $  (molar ration, 1:1) in the dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO)/N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) mixed solvent with the volume ratio of 1:4. The concentration of the  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  precursor was 1.2 M. After being stirred for 8 h at 25 °C,  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  precursors were spin-coated on these  $  TiO_{2}/SnO_{2}  $  films, then 100 μL CB with and without  $  C_{60}(QM)_{2}  $  was quickly dripped onto the perovskite films to stop the films from becoming turbid. To confirm that the reduced trap states lead to the enhanced device performance, we carried out the photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurement. Figure 4a exhibits the PL spectrum for the bare MAPbI $ _3 $  and MAPbI $ _3 $ :C $ _{60} $ (QM) $ _2 $  films. It is noted that the C $ _{60} $ (QM) $ _2 $  coated perovskite film has stronger PL intensity compared with the bare MAPbI $ _3 $  film. In addition, the PL peak shows slight blueshift with the introduction of the C $ _{60} $ (QM) $ _2 $ , which indicates that the introduced C $ _{60} $ (QM) $ _2 $  could eliminate some defects at the surface or GBs. $ ^{15,18} $  Then the PL spectrum for the glass/MAPbI $ _3 $ /spiro-OMeTAD were measured. From Figure S3, we can see that the PL quenching for MAPbI $ _3 $ :C $ _{60} $ (QM) $ _2 $ /spiro-OMeTAD film is more complete in comparison with the pristine film, suggesting enhanced carrier extraction at the MAPbI $ _3 $ :C $ _{60} $ (QM) $ _2 $ /spiro-OMeTAD interface. This can be interpreted as improved interfacial contact between MAPbI $ _3 $  and Spiro-OMeTAD and reduced trap states at the surface of the MAPbI $ _3 $  passivated by the C $ _{60} $ (QM) $ _2 $ , ensuring that more holes could be rapidly extracted by spiro-OMeTAD layer. To further investigate reasons behind the enhanced carrier extraction at MAPbI $ _3 $ :C $ _{60} $ (QM) $ _2 $ /spiro-OMeTAD interface, the UPS measurement for the MAPbI $ _3 $  and MAPbI $ _3 $ :C $ _{60} $ (QM) $ _2 $  are carried out. It can be seen from Figure S4 that the valence band maxima (VBM) of the MAPbI $ _3 $  and MAPbI $ _3 $ :C $ _{60} $ (QM) $ _2 $  are  $ -5.32 $  eV and  $ -5.59 $  eV, respectively. The deeper VBM for MAPbI $ _3 $ :C $ _{60} $ (QM) $ _2 $  increase the energy difference with the HOMO of Spiro-OMeTAD, which provides stronger driving force for carrier extraction/injection at the perovskite/HTL interface. Therefore, the passivated trap states induced by C $ _{60} $ (QM) $ _2 $ , improved interfacial contact and band alignment of MAPbI $ _3 $ :C $ _{60} $ (QM) $ _2 $ /spiro-OMeTAD lead to the enhanced hole extraction. # CONCLUSIONS

We have demonstrated a facile strategy to fabricate highly efficient and air stable PSCs using   $  C_{60}(QM)_{2}  $   as a passivator. The introduced   $  C_{60}(QM)_{2}  $   can passivate the trap states existing in the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   active layer, effectively facilitating carrier extraction/injection from   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   to spiro-OMeTAD. As a result, the champion PCE of 18.4% for the   $  C_{60}(QM)_{2}  $ -passivated device with less hysteresis is obtained, whereas the efficiency of pristine PSCs is 16.7%. In addition, introduced   $  C_{60}(QM)_{2}  $   can significantly improve the hydrophobic properties of the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   active layer, resisting moisture penetrating into the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   active layer. Consequently,   $  C_{60}(QM)_{2}  $ -containing devices still maintain over 80% of the original efficiency when the device has been exposed to an ambient atmosphere with a humidity of 20% for 35 days, which shows enhanced long-term stability.
