# Abstract

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved remarkable power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) owing to their extraordinary optoelectronic properties. Electron transporting layer (ETL) has been proved to have a significant influence on the photovoltaic performance and stability of cell devices. Herein, for the first time, we prepare a low-temperature processed compact   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   film derived from a highly stable modified indium precursor solution as a promising ETL for stable and efficient air-processed PSCs. The addition of acetylacetone as a chelation ligand in the solution effectively inhibits the hydrolysis reactions by chelating   $  In^{3+}  $ , thus contributing to the formation of compact   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   film at a low temperature of 200 °C. Dense   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite films with many microns-scale grains are fabricated using a scalable doctor-blade method under a harsh ambient condition (relative humidity of 40 ~ 50%). Using the proposed compact   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   film as ETL, the electron extraction and charge transport at the ETL/perovskite interface are significantly improved. As a result, the air-processed PSC based on compact   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   film delivers a champion PCE of 13.97%, greatly outperforming the device with a pristine   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   film (9.81%). In addition to high efficiency, the PSC incorporating proposed compact   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   film exhibits an excellent long-term stability, maintaining 94% of its initial PCE after stored in air for 31 days. This study demonstrates the feasibility of fabricating stable and efficient air-processed PSCs using low-temperature processed   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   ETL, which is expected to have a positive impact in the manufacturing community of solution-processed   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   film as well as air-processed PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

New-emerging inorganic-organic lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have gained broad interests in the past one decade due to their unique optoelectronic properties such as excellent charge carrier mobility, high absorption coefficient and high solar energy conversion characteristics [1-5]. With intensive researches conducted by many researchers all over the world, PSCs have achieved extraordinary power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 25.2\% $  [6]. Electron transporting layer (ETL) plays a crucial role in extracting photo-generated electrons and blocking holes from perovskite films, suppressing charge recombination at the interface of ETL/perovskite film [7-10].  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  has been commonly adopted as an ETL material for PSCs and achieved the remarkable PCEs [1,3,9,11-14]. Nevertheless, an excellent  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film often requires a high-temperature treatment [3,12,13], which is incompatible for the fabrication of flexible devices. In addition, the photocatalytic effect of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  on the perovskite materials under solar illumination accelerates the efficiency decay and reduces the stability of PSCs [15]. To tackle these problems, the low-temperature processed ZnO materials were proposed as a new kind of ETL materials for efficient PSCs [16-19]. However, it is challenging to obtain the stable ZnO-based PSCs owing to the decomposition of perovskite material caused by the proton-transfer reactions at the ZnO/perovskite interface [17]. A surface passivation or modification is indispensable to improve the PCE and long-term stability of cell devices [16,17]. To obtain the efficient and stable PSCs, many efforts have been taken to develop other alternative ETLs for PSCs [20-28].

Among all the alternative ETLs, indium oxide  $ (\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{O}_3) $  thin films have shown great potential to be promising ETL materials for PSCs due to their antireflective characteristic, high electron mobility, wide band gap and high chemical stability [29,30]. Fang et al., firstly reported a solution-processed  $ \mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  ETL for PSCs based on the spin-coated  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  films inside a glove-box [28]. Nevertheless, the

as-fabricated  $ \mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  films exhibited a poor morphology with many pinholes or cracks. This is attributed to the fact that hygroscopic  $ \mathrm{In}^{3+} $  can react vigorously with water molecule in the air to form indium hydroxide during the spin-coating process [31,32], which hinders the preparation of uniform and compact  $ \mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  film using the solution-based method. Usually, it is highly desirable to obtain a perfect ETL with a uniform and dense morphological profile for PSCs because the poor morphology of ETL may lead to the direct contact between perovskite film and FTO, causing severe charge recombination and thus deteriorating the photovoltaic performances of PSCs [9,12,28,33]. To improve the imperfect morphology of  $ \mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  film, Yoon et al. spin-coated indium precursor solution inside a  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $ -filled glove-box to exclude the negative impact of water molecule during spin-coating process for obtaining a pinhole-free  $ \mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  film [32]. However, the indium precursor adopted in their study is strictly required to be dissolved in the anhydrous ethanol to ensure its long-term stability in consideration of hygroscopic nature of  $ \mathrm{In}^{3+} $  [31]. This may limit the development of  $ \mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  film as a potential ETL for PSCs. In addition, a high-temperature annealing treatment is required to enhance the crystallinity of  $ \mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  film to obtain a decent PCE, which increases the energy consumption and production cost for PSCs fabrication. Last but not least, it should be noted that the reported  $ \mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $ -based perovskite films in the open literatures were usually prepared inside a gas-protected glove-box or under the harshly controlled condition with a low relative humidity (i.e.  $ \mathrm{RH} = 25\% $ ) [28,32], which is not suitable for the large-scale industrial production of PSCs. In this regard, it is not only imperative to develop a less sensitive indium precursor solution, but also to achieve the fabrication of excellent  $ \mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  films and corresponding PSCs using the low-cost and energy-saving process in view of mass production in the future.

Herein, for the first time, we report a low-temperature processed compact   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   film derived from

The present work demonstrates that the compact  $ \mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  film fabricated at the low temperature is an alternative and promising ETL material for stable and efficient air-processed PSCs.

# 2. Experimental Section

# 2.1. Preparation of solution

0.5 M modified indium precursor solution was prepared by dissolving indium nitrate hydrate (Aladdin, 99.99%) in the ethanol (Aladdin, 99.8%) containing 0.2 wt% water. Subsequently, acacH was incorporated into the above solution as a chelating ligand to inhibit the hydrolysis of   $  In^{3+}  $   and thus significantly stabilizes the indium precursor solution. The volume ratio of ethanol to acacH (  $  V_{ethanol}:V_{acacH}  $  ) in the modified indium precursor solution is fixed at 1:1 and 2:1. Pristine indium

Journal Pre-proof

precursor solution without the addition of acacH was also prepared for comparison. All the indium precursor solutions were filtered using a PTFE filter (0.45   $  \mu  $  m) and then aged for at least 24 h before to use. To prepare perovskite precursor solution, 553.2 mg of PbI  $  _{2}  $   (TCI, 99.5%) was dissolved in 400   $  \mu  $  L of anhydrous dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO, Sigma-Aldrich, 99.5%) and stirred at 70 °C for overnight. 190.2 mg of CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  I (Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp., 99.5%) was dissolved in 600   $  \mu  $  L of   $  \gamma  $  -Butyroladone (GBL, Aladdin, 99.5%) at room temperature. Finally, the CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  I solution was mixed with PbI  $  _{2}  $   solution to obtain 1.2 M CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $   precursor solution. Hole transporting layer solution was prepared by dissolving 72.3 mg of spiro-OMeTAD (Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp., 99.5%), 28.8   $  \mu  $  l of 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP, TCI, 96.0%) and 17.5   $  \mu  $  l of Li-TFSI solution (520 mg Li-TFSI in 1 mL acetonitrile) in 1 mL of chlorobenzene (Aladdin, 99.5%).

# 2.2. Device fabrication

FTO/glass substrates were etched using zinc powder and 2 M HCl solution. The etched FTO/glass substrates were ultrasonically cleaned in soap, deionized water, ethanol and isopropanol, respectively, followed by an UV-ozone treatment for 15 min. In $ _{2} $ O $ _{3} $  films were spin-coated using the modified indium precursor solution onto the FTO/glass substrates at 3000 rpm for 30 s inside N $ _{2} $ -filled glove-box, then annealed in a muffle furnace for 30 min at different temperatures (150, 200, 300, 400 and 500 °C). To fabricate PSCs, CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3} $  films were doctor-bladed onto the In $ _{2} $ O $ _{3} $  films at a coating stage temperature of 150 °C with a blade gap of 250 μm at a speed of 1 mm/s, and then annealed at 150 °C for a short time of 1 min to remove the residual solvent. The doctor-blade coating and annealing processes of CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3} $  films were conducted in ambient condition with a harsh RH of 40~50%. Afterwards, hole transporting layer solution was spin-coated onto the perovskite film at 4000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, gold electrode was thermally evaporated through a shadow mask to complete the device.

# 2.3. Characterization

X-ray diffraction (XRD) characterization was measured by using an X-ray diffractometer (X'pert Pro MPD) with Cu Kα radiation. A field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM, Hitachi, S-4800) was used to characterize the surface morphology of thin film. The transmittance and absorption spectra of thin film were measured by an UV-vis spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer, Lambda 750). The Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra were obtained by a FT-IR spectrometer (Bruker, Tensor 27). The steady state photoluminescence (PL) and time resolved PL (TRPL) spectra were measured by using fluorescence spectrometer (Edinburgh Instruments, FLS 980). A 405 nm pulsed diode laser was used for the TRPL measurements. The current density-voltage (J-V) curves of perovskite solar cells were measured using an electrochemical workstation (Autolab TYPE □) equipped with an ABET Sun 3000 solar simulator and a Keithley 2420 source measurement unit under 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  illumination (AM 1.5 G conditions). All the measurements of solar cells were performed under ambient conditions at room temperature.

# 3. Results and discussion

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, a uniform and compact   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   film was developed as a promising ETL for stable and efficient air-processed PSCs. Using the stable modified indium precursor solution with the addition of acacH to inhibit hydrolysis of   $  In^{3+}  $ , a dense   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   film with a high transmittance was successfully prepared at a low annealing temperature of 200 °C. The   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films blade-coated onto the   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   film under the harsh RH show many large crystal domains composed of many compactly packed grains. A significant improvement in electron extraction and faster charge transport is observed at the ETL/perovskite film interface. As a result, the PSC based on the low-temperature processed m-  $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   film yields a champion PCE of 13.97% and retains 94% of its initial PCE value over 31 days without encapsulation, whereas the pristine   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $ -based PSC shows a lower PCE of 9.81% with poor stability.

# Highlights

Highly stable modified indium precursor solution was developed.

Uniform and compact   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   thin film was prepared at a low temperature of   $  200\ ^{\circ}C  $  .

➢ Dense   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film with microns-scale grains was bladed in the harsh ambient atmosphere.

➢ Charge transport and dissociation at the   $  In_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   interface was improved.

Air-processed PSC based on compact  $ \mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  film yielded a high efficiency of  $ 13.97\% $ .
