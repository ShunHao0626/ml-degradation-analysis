# Abstract

In this paper, we investigate the feasibility of using a fluoroalkyl-substituted fullerene/perovskite heterojunction (f-FPHJ) to realize efficient and ambient stable perovskite solar cells (PVSCs). The hybrid fluoroalkyl-substituted fullerene,  $ \mathrm{DF - C_{60}} $ , is proven to effectively passivate the defects and grain boundaries in the perovskite film to facilitate charge transport/collection in the derived PVSC. Consequently, the f-FPHJ device yielded an enhanced PCE of  $ 18.11\% $ , outperforming that of the pristine  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  device ( $ 15.67\% $ ). More interestingly, the f-FPHJ PVSC showed a decent PCE of  $ 15.14\% $  (under reverse scan) with small hysteresis even without employing a discrete PCBM electron-transporting layer (ETL), in contrast to the pristine PVSC showing a lower PCE of  $ 14.78\% $  (under reverse scan) accompanied with severe hysteresis. # 1. Introduction

Due to the rapid ascension of power conversion efficiency (PCE), organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite based solar cells (PVSC) have been considered as a very attractive candidate for printable photovoltaics.[1-5] Through the comprehensive investigations of perovskite's material properties and associated process engineering,[6-9] very impressive PCE of  $ 22.1\% $  and  $ 20.5\% $  have been accomplished in the lab-scale  $ (< 1~\mathrm{cm}^2) $  and large-area  $ (1~\mathrm{cm}^2) $  PVSC, respectively.[10,11] Such high performance is comparable to those of the existing inorganic photovoltaics for commercialization.[5] Nevertheless, its anomalous hysteresis and unsatisfactory long-term stability, like thermo-, photo-, and ambient-stability,[12-15] have impeded the rapid transition of this technology to practical applications. In this regard, significant research efforts have been devoted to improving the PVSC reliability and stability.[9,16]

The most appealing feature of PVSC lies on its exceptional semiconducting properties endowed by simple solution process due to the low formation energy of perovskite, affording great merits of cost-effective production and good maneuverability. However, such fast solution deposition produces polycrystalline perovskite film with significant structural

disorder and crystal defects. Those concomitant grain boundaries (GBs) and defects in the perovskite films have been identified to critically affect the resultant optoelectronic properties and related stability. Ginger et al. have unveiled that the GBs in the perovskite films serve as the charge trapping cites to incur trap-assisted non-radiative recombination loss.[17] In addition, the GBs and defects on the perovskite surface can provide the permeation pathways for oxygen and moisture into film's interior, leading to inferior ambient stability.[18] Moreover, myriad studies have lately unraveled the roles of GBs and defects on ion migration in the perovskite film, which is one of the main reasons for causing device hysteresis.[19]

In order to mitigate the adverse effects caused by GBs and defects, various in-situ and post treatments have been exploited to alleviate these undesirable species in perovskite film.  $  ^{[20-24]}  $   Among which, the concept of forming fullerene/perovskite heterojunction (FPHJ) has attracted significant research interests.  $  [20-23]  $   For instance, Sargent and Gong et al. have demonstrated that introducing a small amount of fullerene into the perovskite layer to form a FPHJ can increase its photovoltaic performance with reduced device hysteresis. These fullerene derivatives can passivate the PbI  $  _{3}^{-}  $  anti-site defects at GBs to promote electron transport/collection through effective electron coupling and passivation, and I-induced doping at the perovskite/fullerene interface.  $  [20,21,25-30]  $

Up to now, most of the FPHJ reported do not effectively improve the ambient stability of perovskites. In this respect, the fluoroalkyl-substituted fullerene might be a class of material suitable for FPHJ since it preserves the advantages of fullerene but possess increased hydrophobic properties. Therefore, it is possible the FPHJ derived from fluorinated fullerenes (f-FPHJ) can be employed to improve the ambient stability of perovskites. Recently, Gratzel

9

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

et al. have validated this feasibility by incorporating 1,1,1-trifluoro-ethyl ammonium iodide (FEAI) into the perovskite layer.[31] Meanwhile, we have also recently demonstrated a stable inverted PVSC enabled by a cathode interlayer consisting of fluoroalkyl-substituted fullerene.[32] These results highlight the efficacy of employing hydrophobic fluorinated species in PVSCs to improve their stability against moisture.

In this study, we have successfully developed a fluoroalkyl-substituted fullerene, DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  to form a f-FPHJ and demonstrated its potential to realize efficient and ambient stable PVSC. Similar to the previously reported FPHJs,[20-23] the DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  is proven to effectively passivate the defects in the perovskite film to facilitate the charge transport/collection in the derived PVSC. Consequently, the f-FPHJ device can yield an enhanced PCE of  $ 18.11\% $ , outperforming that of the pristine  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  (MAPbI₃) device ( $ 15.67\% $ ). Moreover, we found out that the f-FPHJ PVSC can deliver a decent PCE of  $ 15.14\% $  (under reverse scan) with small hysteresis even without employing a PCBM electron-transporting layer (ETL), in contrast to the pristine PVSC showing an inferior PCE of  $ 14.78\% $  (under reverse scan) and severe hysteresis.

In addition to the aforementioned passivation effect, we speculate that this discrepancy is correlated with the preferential distribution of  $ \mathrm{DF - C_{60}} $  nearby the surface region of the f-FPHJ film due to its low surface energy, which can partially mimic the PCBM ETL in device to reduce hysteresis. Finally, benefitting from the hydrophobicity of  $ \mathrm{DF - C_{60}} $ , the unencapsulated f-FPHJ device shows better stability against moisture than that of the pristine device. More than  $ 83\% $  of the initial PCE can be retained after being stored in ambient with a relative

We herein designed a new fullerene derivative attached with two perfluoroalkyl side-chains, named as  $ \mathrm{DF - C_{60}} $ , for the proposed f-FPHJ considering its increased hydrophobic property than  $ \mathrm{F - C_{60}} $  reported in our previous work.[32] DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  was synthesized by the Prato reaction between 3,5-bis(perfluorooctyl)benzaldehyde and  $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  as shown in Figure 1a. of the reactions are described in the experimental section. Owing to the perfluoroalkyl groups, DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  is expected to possess a much lower surface energy than PCBM. As shown in Figure 1b, the contact angle for DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  and PCBM is  $ 113.6^{\circ} $  and  $ 65.8^{\circ} $ , respectively. This discrepancy reveals that the attached perfluoroalkyl group effectively decreases the surface energy of  $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  and enhances its hydrophobicity.

The electron-transporting property of  $ \mathrm{DF - C_{60}} $  was examined by a bottom gate/top contact field-effect transistor (FET) device. The transfer and output characteristics presented in Figure 1c clearly demonstrate its decent n-type property. The estimated electron mobility, on/off ratio, and threshold voltage is  $ 1.8\times 10^{-3}\mathrm{cm}^2 /\mathrm{Vs} $ ,  $ 1.6\times 10^{5} $ , and  $ 27.9\mathrm{V} $ , respectively (Table S1). The lower mobility of  $ \mathrm{DF - C_{60}} $  than PCBM ( $ \sim 10^{-2}\mathrm{cm}^2 /\mathrm{Vs} $ ) is due to the two bulky insulating side-chains which prevent tight packing of fullerene.[26] The electron-transporting property of fullerene is slightly compensated in favor of increased hydrophobicity.

To prepare the proposed f-FPHJ, DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  was first dissolved in chlorobenzene and mixed with the precursor solution of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  in different volume to achieve targeted weight concentrations. The details of f-FPHJ film preparation are described in the experimental section. To confirm this, the stable maximum power output for the top-performing  $ 0.025\mathrm{wt}\% $   $ \mathrm{DF - C_{60} - MAPbI_3} $  device was conducted and shown in Figure 5c, wherein a stable photocurrent of  $ \sim 20.2\mathrm{mA / cm^2} $  and PCE of  $ \sim 18.0\% $  can be reached at a voltage of  $ 0.89\mathrm{V} $ , validating the accuracy and hysteresis-free characteristics of the fabricated PVSC. The f-FPHJ device clearly showed higher EQE values than the pristine device across the wavelength ranging from 400 to  $ 700\mathrm{nm} $ , which is consistent with the absorption region of perovskite, indicating enhanced charge separation and collection of the f-FPHJ PVSCs.

As discussed earlier, the DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  is envisioned to distribute nearby the surface region of the derived f-FPHJ film (Figure S2). This preferential distribution might allow it to serve as an ETL to replace PCBM. To examine this hypothesis, the PCBM-ETL-free inverted PVSC with a configuration of ITO/Cu-doped  $ \mathrm{NiO_x / MAPbI_3} $  or 0.025 wt% DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $ -MAPbI $ _3 $ /bis- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $ /Ag was next fabricated. The corresponding  $ J - V $  curves are shown in Figure 6 and the related parameters were summarized in Table S2. Such ETL-free PVSC is useful for the understanding of device operating mechanism and for economizing the production cost.[38,39] Up to now, all the reported ETL-free PVSCs generally encountered large hysteresis.[39] However, in our f-FPHJ system, even without using the PCBM ETL, the 0.025 wt% DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $ -MAPbI $ _3 $  PVSC can still preserve a reasonable PCE of  $ 15.14\% $  under reverse scan and show small hysteresis, for which he PCE obtained under forward scan is  $ 13.56\% $  (Figure 6b). Whereas, the pristine  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  device showed a slight low PCE of  $ 14.78\% $  under reverse scan but exhibited a significant hysteresis, for which the PCE obtained under forward scan is only  $ 10.98\% $  (Figure 6a). The hysteresis ratio defined as  $ \left|\mathrm{PCE}_{\text {reverse}} - \mathrm{PCE}_{\text {forward}}\right| / \mathrm{PCE}_{\text {reverse}} $  is calculated to be around  $ 25.7\% $  and  $ 10.4\% $  for the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and  $ 0.025~\mathrm{wt\%} $  DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $ -MAPbI $ _3 $  devices, respectively. The reduced hysteresis is surely attributed to the passivation effect introduced by DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  (Figure 4). Meanwhile, it can be also ascribed to the preferential distribution of DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  nearby the surface region of the f-FPHJ film as proposed, for which it can partially mimic the PCBM ETL in device to reduce hysteresis.

Owing to the hydrophobic nature of  $ \mathrm{DF - C_{60}} $ , the fabricated f-FPHJ is expected to have improved ambient stability than the pristine perovskite. Moreover, the proposed preferential

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, we have developed a f-FPHJ that shows great potential for achieving efficient and ambient stable PVSCs. Similar to the reported FPHJs,[20-23] the fluoroalkyl-substituted fullerene,  $ \mathrm{DF - C_{60}} $ , can effectively passivate the surface defects and GBs in the perovskite film as evidenced by SEM, PL, and SKPM studies to facilitate charge transport/collection in the derived PVSC. As a result, an enhanced PCE of  $ 18.11\% $  can be realized in the f-FPHJ, outperforming the PCE  $ (15.67\%) $  of the pristine  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  device. More
