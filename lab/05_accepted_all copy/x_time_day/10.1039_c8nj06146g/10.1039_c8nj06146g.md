# Efficient and stable planar perovskite solar cells with carbon quantum dots doped PCBM electron transport layer

Xiaomeng Zhu  $  ^{a}  $  , Jing Sun  $  ^{a}  $  , Shuai Yuan  $  ^{a}  $  , Ning Li  $  ^{a}  $  , Zhiwen Qiu  $  ^{a}  $  , Jinbiao Jia  $  ^{b}  $  , Yining Liu  $  ^{c}  $  , Jia Dong  $  ^{b}  $  , Pin Lv  $  ^{b}  $  , Bingqiang Cao  $  ^{a*}  $

Perovskite solar cell with single light absorption heterojunction has demonstrated power conversion efficiency as high as 23.2% in the past few years. In this work, for the first time, we use quasi-spherical carbon quantum dots (CQDs) doped PCBM film as electron transporting layer of inverted perovskite solar cell. The device structure is FTO/PEDOT:PSS/MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /PCBM:CQDs/BCP/Ag. The optimized PCE of 18.1% is achieved and a 13% enhancement has observed compared to that of pure PCBM-based devices. Various advanced characterizations including ultrafast spectroscopy, conductive atomic force microscopy, and electronic impedance spectroscopy elucidate that quasi-spherical CQDs doping leads to enhanced electrical conductivity, electron mobility, and charge extraction efficiency. Moreover, we find that such CQDs doping help prevent I  $  ^{-}  $  ions from interface diffusing and thus enhance the stability of solar cells.

# Introduction

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells are the most promising photovoltaic technologies due to their high power conversion efficiency (PCE), bipolar carrier transport ability, high absorption coefficients, long carrier diffusion length, and easy fabrication process. To date, the highest PCE value of certified has reached 23.2% for small-scale devices  $  ^{1-9}  $  . However, large-scale industrial production remains to be proven due to its long-term stability and difficulty of large module building. N-i-P (regular) and P-i-N (inverted) heterostructures are most used in perovskite solar cells. For the conventional planar perovskite solar cells, the most widely used n-type electron transport layer (ETL) are   $  TiO_{2}^{10,11}  $  ,   $  ZnO^{12}  $  , and phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM)  $  ^{13-16}  $  . As initial electron transport layer used in N-i-P structure solar cells,   $  TiO_{2}  $   needs a high temperature (  $  >450^{\circ}C  $  ) fabrication process, which is not favorable in the fabrication of P-i-N devices  $  ^{17-19}  $  . In the meanwhile, organic electron transport materials such as PCBM, fullerene  $  ^{20,21}  $  , and PCBDAN  $  ^{22,23}  $   attract researchers' attention and have been wildly applied in P-i-N devices owing to their simple and low-temperature fabrication process.

The intensively use of PCBM is on account of its good electron transport property and relatively high electron affinity. However, employing PCBM as ETL has some significant issues, such as interfacial recombination, current leakage, and easily interface diffusing of the I- from perovskite layer, which results in poor photovoltaic performance and disillusionary long-term stability  $  ^{24}  $  . To overcome these problems, researchers introduced some additives such as graphene oxide  $  ^{25}  $  , graphdiyne  $  ^{26-37}  $  , graphene  $  ^{38-41}  $   and 1H-benzoimidazole  $  ^{42}  $   derivatives to improve the electrical properties of PCBM. Carbon quantum dots (CQDs), a new type of carbon materials, have excellent electron reservoir property and electrical conductivity, which are promising to enhance not merely the PCE but also the stability of devices  $  ^{43}  $  .

In this work, for the first time, we built planar heterojunction (PHJ)  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  solar cell using PCBM with CQDs as electron transport layer. The introduction of CQDs into PCBM led to improved photovoltaic performance with the PCE increased from 16.1 to  $ 18.1\% $ . It was found that doping with CQDs not only could increase the electrical conductivity and charge extraction ability of PCBM ETL, but also contributed to preventing the I- from diffusing into Ag electrode to form AgI, which would suppress the dimerization in fullerene and thus improve the stability of perovskite solar cells. Several characterizations incorporating transmission electron microscopy (TEM), conductive atomic force microscopy (c-AFM), photoluminescence (PL), time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL), external quantum efficiency (EQE), transient photovoltage (TPV), and transient photocurrent decay (TPC), were conducted to give detailed analysis of the doped PCBM film and according the improved device performances. These results demonstrate that the introduction of CQDs dopant into PHJ perovskite solar cells is an effective strategy to enhance device performance and stability.

# Experimental

# Materials Preparation

Methylamine iodide (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I), Poly (3,4-ethylene-dioxythiophene): polystyrenesulfonate (PEDOT: PSS) and [6,6]-phenyl-C $ _{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PC $ _{61} $ BM) were all purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp, and used as received. Lead acetate (Pb(CH $ _3 $ COO) $ _2 $  $ \cdot $ 3H $ _2 $ O) were obtained from Aladdin Industrial Corporation and put it on the hot plate at 80 °C for 24 h to dislodge the bound water. 2,9-Dimethyl-4,7-diphenyl-1,10-phenanthroline (BCP) was obtained from Aladdin Industrial Corporation with purity of 99.8%. Patterned fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass with a sheet resistance of 15 Ω·Sq $ ^{-1} $  was purchased from Nippon Glass. Anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), Chlorobenzene (CB) and isopropanol (IPA) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, and without further purification. MAI and Pb(CH $ _3 $ COO) $ _2 $  (PbAc $ _2 $ ) were dissolved in DMF (1 M) with a 3:1 molar ratio. The precursor solution was stirring for 12 h at room temperature.

# Preparation of CQDs doped  $ \mathsf{PC}_{61}\mathsf{BM} $  solution

CQDs were synthesized by microwave-assisted hydrothermal method. Briefly, the CQDs were synthesized by heating ammonium citrate (0.1 g) at 180 °C for 2 h in an oven, followed by dissolving in 10 mL deionized (DI) water. The obtained CQDs solution was centrifuged at a relative centrifugal force (RCF) of 30 000 g to remove aggregated particles. Subsequently, the solution was dialyzed using a dialysis membrane (MWCO = 1.0 kDa; Float-A-Lyzer G2, Spectrum Laboratories, Rancho Dominguez, CA, USA) for 6 h in DI water. Finally CQDs solution was dried at 70 °C for 12 h. PC  $  _{61}  $  BM solution was prepared by dissolving PC  $  _{61}  $  BM into CB with a concentration of 18 mg·mL  $  ^{-1}  $  and then stirred overnight in a glovebox (MIKROUNA). The CQDs doped solution was prepared by dispersing 3 mg, 5 mg and 8 mg CQDs into PC  $  _{61}  $  BM solution respectively and the optimized content was 5 mg.

# Device Fabrication.

FTO-coated glass substrate was etched with Zn powder and diluted HCI solution. The etched substrate was cleaned with ethanol, saturated KOH solution of isopropanol and water, then dried with clean N2 flow. After that, PEDOT:PSS was spin-coated on the patterned FTO glass substrates at 4000 rpm for 25 s and annealed at 135 °C for 15 min. PEDOT: PSS coated substrates were moved in a glovebox. Perovskite layer was spin-coated on at 2000 rpm for 40 s. Perovskite coated substrates were laid aside for 5 min and then annealed for 10 min. After cooling down, PC  $  _{61}  $  BM solution or CQDs doped PC  $  _{61}  $  BM solution was spin-coated on the perovskite layer at 2000 rpm for 40 s. Soon after, a layer of BCP was deposited on at 4000 rpm for 30 s by using 0.5 mg·mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ethyl alcohol solution. Finally, 55 nm thick Ag electrodes were thermal evaporated onto BCP layer. The active area was defined as the overlap of FTO and Ag electrodes.

# Device characterization

The current density-voltage (J-V) characteristics of the photovoltaic devices were recorded under illumination and dark conditions using a computer-controlled Keithley 2612A source meter under AM 1.5G simulated solar light (0.95 sun due to aging). Before measurement, the lamp was turned on for 15 min and the exact light intensity was calibrated by NREL-traceable KG5 filtered silicon reference cell. The EQE spectra were measured using a QEX10 photoresponse system (PV Measurement Inc.). The wavelength interval is 10 nm, and the intensity of the incident light from monochromator was calibrated with Si photodiode installed inside (1H002). c-AFM imaging was obtained at room temperature in non-contact mode by using a atomic force microscope (Veeco dimension V) and the cantilever coated with Pt/Ir while applying a 5 V bias. All measurements were performed under ambient atmosphere with relative humidity of 25%, and solar cells were tested without any encapsulation. The stability study of solar cells was measured in a drybox.

To investigate the charge transfer process at PCBM: CQDs/perovskite interfaces, PL spectra of perovskite film on different substrates, including   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/glass  $  ,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM/glass  $  , and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM  $  : CQDs/glass, were measured. The results are displayed in Fig. 3(a). An obvious quenching effect can be found when the perovskite layer is coated with PCBM and PCBM: CQDs layer. The PCBM: CQDs coated perovskite layer shows stronger quenching ability than PCBM coated perovskite layer, illustrating the addition of CQDs can extract electrons more efficiently and may decrease the charge recombination  $  ^{44, 45}  $  .

TRPL spectra were used to elucidate the dynamics of charge transfer process at   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/ETL  $   interfaces. The TRPL characteristics probed at 770 nm are shown in Fig. 3(b). The samples were excited with 405 nm picosecond laser pulses and the repetition rate is 20 MHz. The TRPL decay curves can be well fitted with double-exponential model. The fast decay time   $  \tau1  $   (  $  \sim10  $   ns) reflects the quenching of free carriers at the interface between perovskite layer and ETL. The slow decay time   $  \tau2  $   (  $  \sim100  $   ns) reflects the recombination of free carriers via radiative channels. The sample of perovskite spin-coated on PCBM: CQDs film shows a   $  \tau1  $   value of 10.0 ns, while the reference one achieves a value of 14.6 ns. The shorter TRPL lifetime of free carrier indicate that CQDs doping in PCBM exhibit more efficient carrier extraction ability than the pure PCBM substance. The   $  \tau2  $   value of control sample (  $  \tau2 = 20.9  $   ns) is shorter than that of the sample based on PCBM: CQDs (  $  \tau2 = 128.6  $   ns), indicating that doping of CQDs can reduce the bulk recombination process, which is well consistent with the result of steady-state PL measurement in Fig. 3(a).

Impedance spectrum is another effective tool to investigate the charge recombination in a solar cell. Fig. 5(a) shows the Nyquist plots of PCBM: CQDs and pure PCBM based solar cells measured at a forward bias of  $ 1\mathrm{V} $ . Three separated semicircles can be obtained by fitting the frequency curves. The first arc in high-frequency region is related to the charge transport in ETL, HTL, and/or at ETL/FTO, HTL/Ag interface. The second arc in intermediate frequency region is related to the charge recombination process at HTL/perovskite/PCBM/BCP/Ag interfaces. The third arc in low-frequency is associated with a long relaxation process which can be attributed to ions migration in perovskite layer. Fig. 5(b) presents the series resistance  $ (R_{\mathrm{s}}) $  as a function of bias by fitting the Nyquist plots which indicates an Ohmic contact behaviour. The lower  $ R_{\mathrm{s}} $  value may contribute to the augmented short current of the device using PCBM: CQDs ETL. Stability, as a key factor of perovskite solar cells, is a major concern since its appearance. For the pristine organic-inorganic halide perovskite, its decomposition should assume the main responsibility for photovoltaic performance degradation on account of the generation of large   $  PbI_{2}  $   gaps with low conductivity and AgI barriers  $  ^{46,47}  $  . Many strategies have been proposed to solve this problem such as using all-inorganic perovskite layer or charge extraction layers, polymer encapsulation and so on. To compare the device stability, two devices respectively based on PCBM and PCBM: CQDs ETL were placed in a drybox in atmosphere for 20 days. The PCE aging results are shown in Fig. 6(a). We observe that the PCBM: CQDs based devices exhibit enhanced long-term stability. 20 days later, the PCE of PCBM: CQDs based device still remains about 70% versus 20% of pure PCBM based device.

As we all know,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   will decompose at the film grain boundaries with high defect densities, and I- and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}  $  ions can diffuse through PCBM layer. The loss of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   then induces the bulk film decomposing, generating   $  PbI_{2}  $   at the outer margins. Eventually, photoelectrical property of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   thin film and PCE of devices will deteriorate and AgI as a by-product will generate on Ag electrode  $  ^{33}  $  . The molecular structure like PCBM and fullerene appears a severe burn-in phenomenon under illumination, which will decrease solar cell performance severely. Filling electron traps with higher electron density materials is one way to eliminate negative effects of dimerization. Snaith et al. reported that by suppressing dimerization in fullerene with n-type doping could restrain severe burn-in and thus enhance the stability of planar perovskite solar cells  $  ^{48}  $  . It can be explained by that CQDs exhibit electron reservoir properties so that the introduction of CQDs in PCBM will fill the electron traps which originated from dimerization of PCBM upon light exposure, alleviating the negative effect of PCBM dimerization. Based on what we have observed above, we propose that the groups on CQDs surface, such as C–OH, C=O, C–O–C, C–H and so on, can absorb I–ions and prevent their diffusion into Ag electrode through PCBM layer, as shown in Fig. 6(c). As a result, the decomposition of perovskite film is effectively suppressed, improving the device stability.

# Conclusions

In summary, we use CQDs as a dopant adding into PCBM ETL in PHJ  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  solar cells for the first time. With optimized CQDs modification content, the PHJ perovskite solar cell achieves a PCE as high as  $ 18.1\% $  with steady-state PCE of  $ 17.1\% $ . Further study on PL, TRPL, Nyquist plots c-AFM reveals improvement of electron mobility, electron extraction ability, and well-matched energy levers. Moreover, there is a great improvement in long term stability of perovskite solar cells through preventing the diffusion of  $ \mathrm{I}^- $  by adding CQDs into PCBM ETL. The work described here manifests that introducing CQDs into perovskite solar cell is a simple and effective strategy to improve device performances and long term stability.
