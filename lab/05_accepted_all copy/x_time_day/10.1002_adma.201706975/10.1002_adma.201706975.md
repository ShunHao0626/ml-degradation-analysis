# Versatility of Carbon Enables All Carbon Based Perovskite Solar Cells to Achieve High Efficiency and High Stability

Xiangyue Meng, Junshuai Zhou, Jie Hou, Xia Tao, Sin Hang Cheung, Shu Kong So, and Shihe Yang*

Carbon-based perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) without hole transport materials are promising for their high stability and low cost, but the electron transporting layer (ETL) of   $  TiO_{2}  $   is notorious for inflicting hysteresis and instability. In view of its electron accepting ability,   $  C_{60}  $   is used to replace   $  TiO_{2}  $   for the ETL, forming a so-called all carbon based PVSC. With a device structure of fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/  $  C_{60}  $  /methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  )/carbon, a power conversion efficiency (PCE) is attained up to 15.38% without hysteresis, much higher than that of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ones (12.06% with obvious hysteresis). The   $  C_{60}  $   ETL is found to effectively improve electron extraction, suppress charge recombination, and reduce the sub-bandgap states at the interface with MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  . Moreover, the all carbon based PVSCs are shown to resist moisture and ion migration, leading to a much higher operational stability under ambient, humid, and light-soaking conditions. To make it an even more genuine all carbon based PVSC, it is further attempted to use graphene as the transparent conductive electrode, reaping a PCE of 13.93%. The high performance of all carbon based PVSCs stems from the bonding flexibility and electronic versatility of carbon, promising commercial developments on account of their favorable balance of cost, efficiency, and stability.

Organic–inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) have garnered broad attention in the last few years due to their high power conversion efficiency (PCE), low-cost, and solution processability.  $  ^{[1-5]}  $   However, the stability of PVSCs must be addressed before their commercialization is viable.  $  ^{[6]}  $   Among various kinds of PVSCs, carbon-based PVSCs without hole transport materials seem to be the most promising for addressing the stability issue because carbon materials are stable, inherently water-resistant,

PVSCs. In fact, fullerenes, such as phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester and   $  C_{60}  $   have been used as ETLs in PVSCs,  $  ^{[26-29]}  $   and shown to enable highly effective electron extraction when interfaced with the perovskite absorber.  $  ^{[30,31]}  $   Since in carbon-based PVSC devices, the ETL/perovskite interface is the only effective charge separation interface,  $  ^{[8]}  $   using the molecularly tunable fullerene as ETL is clearly advantageous for its efficient charge separation. Moreover, the use of fullerenes also facilitates device fabrication at low temperature (no need for the high temperature calcination step required to obtain highly crystalline metal oxide layers), which is desirable for processing flexible solar cells.

Herein we report on the development of a hysteresis-free all carbon based PVSCs by using   $  C_{60}  $   as ETL with the device structure of fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/  $  C_{60}  $  /methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  )/carbon. The best-performing all carbon based PVSCs fabricated with   $  C_{60}  $   has achieved a PCE of 15.38% without hysteresis. Most importantly, we realized long-term stability for all carbon based PVSCs, which maintained 95% of their initial performance after continuous operation at their maximum power point for 180 h under 1 sun illumination in air with a relative humidity of 40–60%. First, the   $  C_{60}  $   layer was prepared by thermal evaporation in a vacuum environment on the FTO/glass substrate. Vacuum-deposited   $  C_{60}  $   can be packed densely to facilitate intermolecular charge transport, and shows much higher electron mobility and conductivity compared to solution processed fullerenes.  $  ^{[32]}  $   Figure 1a shows the scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of   $  FTO/C_{60}  $   surface and cross-sectional morphology. It is observed from the cross-sectional SEM image that a layer of   $  C_{60}  $   about 30 nm thick is tightly attached to the underlying FTO layer. Though the FTO substrate has a rough surface (Figure S1, Supporting Information), the thermally deposited   $  C_{60}  $   films can fully cover the FTO substrate. The rough surface of   $  C_{60}  $   layer originating from the FTO substrate enlarges the interface area with the perovskite layer, and thus is found to be beneficial for electron extraction and for perovskite film formation. Figure 1b shows the optical transmittance spectra of the   $  C_{60}  $   film (30 nm) deposited on the FTO substrate. The   $  FTO/C_{60}  $   film demonstrates as high transmittance as bare FTO and   $  FTO/TiO_{2}  $   films from 550 to 800 nm, and it decreases less than 10% compared with bare FTO and   $  FTO/TiO_{2}  $   films from 300 to 550 nm due to the absorbance of   $  C_{60}  $ , which has no obvious effect on the performance of the PVSCs fabricated with   $  C_{60}  $ .

We fabricated PVSCs using the prepared   $  C_{60}  $   film as ETL with the all carbon based device structure of FTO/  $  C_{60}  $  /MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /carbon. MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   with full coverage and high crystallinity was prepared by spin-coating CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  I·PbI  $  _{2}  $  ·DMSO adduct in DMF solution with diethyl ether dripping method during the process (Figures S2 and S3, Supporting Information).  $  ^{[33]}  $   Carbon electrode was fabricated by directly printing a commercial carbon paste on the perovskite layer, according to previous procedures.  $  ^{[9,20]}  $   The device configurations of the all carbon based PVSCs are shown in Figure 2a,b. It can be seen that the thickness of carbon electrode is about 20   $  \mu  $  m, which can efficiently prevent the moisture penetration into the perovskite layer. Figure 2c shows the J-V characteristics of the best PVSCs by using   $  C_{60}  $   or TiO  $  _{2}  $   ETLs measured with both reverse and forward scan under AM 1.5G 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $   illumination. It is clear that the device with the TiO  $  _{2}  $   compact layer as the ETL displays obvious hysteresis. But when the device employed   $  C_{60}  $

as the ETL, the J-V hysteresis is eliminated due to the efficient electron extraction from perovskite to   $  C_{60}  $  . As shown in Table 1, the PVSCs with   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer as the ETL showed the best PCE of 12.06% under the reverse scan. The PVSCs fabricated with   $  C_{60}  $   showed higher PCE of 15.38% under the reverse scan with   $  J_{sc} = 23.44 \, mA cm^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{oc} = 1.07 \, V  $  , and FF = 61.25%, which is one of the highest PCE values for the carbon-based PVSCs. The FF of carbon-based perovskite solar cells was commonly below 0.7 due to the poor contact at the perovskite/carbon interface with lots of gaps and inefficient hole selectivity of carbon. To further improve FF and PCE of carbon-based perovskite solar cells, one of the most effective ways is to enhance the perovskite/carbon interface by developing more suitable carbon materials, such as boron doped multiwalled carbon nanotubes, or by employing the embedment method.  $  ^{[16]}  $

The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectrum of the best-performing device based on   $  C_{60}  $   ETL is presented in Figure 2d, which corresponds to an integrated   $  J_{sc}  $   of 23.10 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , in good agreement with the measured   $  J_{sc}  $   from the J-V curves. The best-performing device with the   $  C_{60}  $   ETL also achieved a steady-state efficiency of 15.2% as shown in Figure 2e, with a steady-state current density of 19.0 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   at a constant bias voltage of 0.80 V, which checks well with the small hysteresis and verifies the PCE value from the J-V curves.

Steady state and time resolved photoluminescence (PL) measurements were performed to investigate charge dynamics as shown in Figure 3. Notably,   $  C_{60}  $   shows a higher PL quenching efficiency than   $  TiO_{2}  $ , suggesting more efficient electron transfer from   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   to   $  C_{60}  $ . Furthermore, the time resolved PL was measured by monitoring the peak emission at 768 nm as shown in Figure 3b. By fitting the data to a biexponential decay model, we obtained two time constants, i.e.,   $  \tau_{1}  $   and   $  \tau_{2}  $ , as summarized in Table S1 of the Supporting Information. The shorter decay component of   $  \tau_{1}  $   could be attributed to the charge transfer from the perovskite to the ETL quencher and the nonradiative charge recombination within the perovskite layer, while the longer decay component of   $  \tau_{2}  $   should be related to the radiative recombination of trapped charges. Compared to the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}/MAPbI_{3}  $   films, both   $  \tau_{1}  $   and   $  \tau_{2}  $   were shortened for the   $  C_{60}/MAPbI_{3}  $   film. Impedance spectroscopy (IS) was used to further study the internal charge recombination behaviors in the devices. Figure 3d shows the Nyquist plots for the PVSCs based on   $  C_{60}  $   or   $  TiO_{2}  $   measured under dark condition at the applied bias voltage (  $  V_{appl}  $  ) of 0.8 V. Only a single distinct semicircle (corresponding to a single RC element) was observed, which represents a parallel combination of the recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) and capacitance;  $  ^{[35]}  $   a higher   $  R_{rec}  $   is associated with a lower carrier recombination. By fitting the IS results using the equivalent circuit in the inset of Figure 3d, the   $  R_{rec}  $   for the device based on   $  C_{60}  $   is 71.1 kΩ, which is obviously higher than that based on   $  TiO_{2}  $   (17.2 kΩ), highlighting the role of   $  C_{60}  $   in abating the carrier recombination. Conceivably, fullerenes can effectively passivate charge trap states at the interface with   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  ,  $  ^{[28]}  $   resulting in reduced occurrence of trap-assisted recombination. Notably, the   $  R_{rec}  $   values measured at different   $  V_{appl}  $   follow the same sequence as those measured at 0.8 V (Figure S5, Supporting Information), further verifying the suppression of charge recombination in the all carbon based PVSCs with   $  C_{60}  $   as the ETL. The charge recombination suppression characteristics of   $  C_{60}  $   are advantageous for improving   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF values of the all carbon based PVSCs.

It can be seen that a significant loss (80%) of the efficiency was observed for the PVSCs with the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL after 20 d. In conclusion, we have successfully demonstrated an all carbon based PVSC by using   $  C_{60}  $   as the ETL with the device structure of   $  FTO/C_{60}/MAPbI_{3}/carbon  $  . We observed stronger and faster PL quenching from perovskite to   $  C_{60}  $   indicating enhanced electron extraction. Photothermal deflection spectroscopy confirmed the lower sub-bandgap absorption level for   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   on   $  C_{60}  $   substrate than that on   $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate, which results in lower energetic disorder at the   $  C_{60}/MAPbI_{3}  $   interface. We also showed that   $  C_{60}  $   could effectively reduce the charge recombination at the interface with   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   by using the impedance spectroscopy measurements. Due to these advancements, the all carbon based PVSC fabricated with   $  C_{60}  $   has achieved a PCE of 15.38% without hysteresis, compared favorably with that of the   $  TiO_{2}  $ -based PVSC (12.06% with obvious hysteresis), as well as the best performing carbon-based solar cells reported to date. Moreover, the   $  C_{60}  $ -based devices exhibit superior stability to humidity and continuous light illumination, which maintained nearly 95% of their initial PCEs after continuous operation at their maximum power point for 180 h under 1 sun illumination in air with a relative humidity of 40–60%. Furthermore, graphene-based transparent conductive electrode was successfully applied for fabricating the real all carbon based PVSCs, and a decent PCE up to 13.93% has been achieved. This is a dramatic exploitation of carbon's ability to be made p-type or n-type semiconducting or metallic on account of its

bonding versatility. Our results point to new opportunities for making cost-efficient, flexible, and stable PVSCs toward market deployment.
