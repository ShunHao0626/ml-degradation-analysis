# Metal-organic Compound Branched MoS  $  _{2}  $  for Achieving High Performance Perovskite Solar cells

Ruina Dai, Yangyang Wang, Jie Wang and Xianyu Deng*

Abstract:   $  MoS_{2}  $   as a graphene-like 2D material shows a large potential to replace even overcome graphene in various important applications due to its perfect properties of electrical, optical, frictional, and tunable band gap. However, low solubility in the most of common solvents makes it difficult to prepare via a simple solution process. Here we introduce a metalorganic compound to modify   $  MoS_{2}  $  . Phenyl acetylene silver (PAS) functionalized   $  MoS_{2}  $   compound is easily dispersed in the solvent of DMF and water. A conductive polymer PEDOT: PSS blend with the   $  MoS_{2}  $   leads to a significant enhancement of the performance of planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells. The solar cells have a high power conversion efficiency of 16.47% as well as largely increased stability. This provides a feasible method on large-scale yield of the   $  MoS_{2}  $   for wide applications in various electric devices.

Molybdenum disulfide (MoS $ _2 $ ), a typical graphene-like two-dimensional material, has attracted much attention due to its large application prospect in some important areas, such as electrochemistry, bio-imaging, sensing and electronic devices[1-4]. Although MoS $ _2 $  has excellent electrical, optical and frictional properties, it is still a challenge to achieve large-scale yield. It is also usual insoluble in the most of common solvents except for some specific ones such as hot concentrated sulfuric acid and aqua regia[5-7]. This seriously limits the application range of MoS $ _2 $ . The methods of mechanical exfoliation and chemical vapor deposition (CVD) are generally employed to grow a monolayer MoS $ _2 $ . However, these methods have various inevitable shortcomings[8-12], for examples, CVD is high-cost and intricate while mechanical exfoliation can only realize a small-scale production.

To overcome these problems, a simple method usually used is to optimize the properties of   $  MoS_{2}  $   by a physical hybrid. In the physical hybrid, it has obtained obvious results in some applications such as lithium batteries and gas sensors. Li et al reported the hybrid of   $  MoS_{2}  $   and MoC-MWCNT structure with 23%   $  MoS_{2}  $   to apply in lithium ion batteries, whose specific capacities are obviously higher than those of   $  MoS_{2}  $  -MWCNTs with   $  \sim70\% MoS_{2}  $    $  ^{[13]}  $  . Choi and Kang used   $  MoS_{2}  $  -amorphous carbon (  $  MoS_{2}  $  -AC) composite as battery electrode, where the macroporous microspheres of   $  MoS_{2}  $  -AC make Li- and Na-ion have an excellent storage ability  $  ^{[14]}  $  . Cho et al have reported a 2D heterostructure of graphene/  $  MoS_{2}  $   as gas sensors that can

detect a very low   $  NO_{2}  $   concentration and show superior stability of gas detection  $  ^{[15]}  $  .

Compared with a physical blend, a chemical modification on the surface of   $  MoS_{2}  $   even more helps to reveal its 2D characteristics. A primary character of   $  MoS_{2}  $   is that with the transform from multiple layers into a single layer, the property of its bandgap will change from an indirect bandgap into a direct bandgap, accompanying with the bandgap changing from 1.29 eV to 1.9 eV  $  ^{[16-18]}  $  . Thanks to the special electronic structure of the monolayer   $  MoS_{2}  $ , it has excellent magnetic, electrical and optical properties, which makes it can be widely used in various fields, especial for electrical devices in recent  $  ^{[4,7]}  $  . According to previous reports in literatures, it mainly contains two reaction mechanics that S defects are linked with inorganic atoms, and S coordinates with transition metal atoms  $  ^{[19]}  $  . In these applications,   $  MoS_{2}  $   is usually dispersed in the solvents of NMP (N-Methyl pyrrolidone), CHP (N-Cyclohexyl-2-pyrrolidone), DMF (N, N-Dimethylformamide) or IPA (Iso-propyl alcohol) owing to their similar surface tensions  $  ^{[20]}  $  . Chou and his colleagues have reported ligand (-OH, -COOH) conjugation of chemically exfoliated   $  MoS_{2}  $   using thiol chemistry  $  ^{[21]}  $  . Backes demonstrated a simple method to functionalize   $  MoS_{2}  $   with metal salts (M(OAc)  $  _{2}  $  ) and thiol cysteine  $  ^{[22,23]}  $  . Besides Liu et al has investigated   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanosheets chemically cross-linked with a carboxylate-containing polymeric (PMMA) via copper-ion coordination  $  ^{[24]}  $  . Even so, it is still a key difficult for chemical modification that   $  MoS_{2}  $   does not react with most chemical reagents owing to its chemical inertness. Therefore, seeking feasible approaches on chemically modifying the   $  MoS_{2}  $   will be a long-term topic in future related studies.

Here we reported a chemical modification of  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  with a metallorganic compound of phenyl acetylene silver (PAS) by the mechanic of S coordination with transition metal atoms. S atoms in  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  have lone pair electrons while transition metals, such as Cu, Zn and Ag, has unoccupied orbital so that they can form coordination bond with each other. On the other hand, because the addition of transition metal ion will cause deposition of dispersed  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  directly, we design to employ the covalent compounds containing metal atoms to modify  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $ . After the modification, the solubility was largely enhanced. Another consideration is that metal organic compounds are original functional materials with good fluorescence or phosphorescence emission. The  $ \pi-\pi $  conjugative structure in PAS makes it show outstanding optical and electrical properties. These combined with the property of  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  with monolayer, the modified  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  could exhibit some remarkable properties for wide applications in some important fields such as opto-electrical devices.

Perovskite solar cells have been attracted much interest during the past years due to its unexpected high efficiency and ease process. In order to achieve low temperature fabrication to fit for flexible and even wearable devices, an inverted device of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/Perovskite/PCBM/Metal is considered as an ideal structure. Besides, its hydrophobicity will reduce the harm from water and be in favor of the stability of devices. To prove the possibility of the modified MoS  $  _{2}  $   in this study used in electrical devices, we used it to blend with PEDOT:PSS as the hole transport layer in perovskite solar cells. PAS is synthesized by phenyl acetylene and silver nitrate  $ (\mathrm{AgNO}_3) $  at room temperature. In the reaction as shown in Figure 1a, Ag atom from  $ \mathrm{AgNO}_3 $  replaces H atom on  $ \mathrm{C} \equiv \mathrm{C} $  in the molecule of phenyl acetylene. Then the PAS is introduced onto the surface of  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  through solvent exfoliation. The modification process is shown in Figure 1b. Firstly, we dispersed  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  powders into the solvent of DMF or NMP, and then the

solution was ultra-sonic treated in water for 4 h. Secondly, PAS was added into the pretreated solution sequentially with an ultrasonic treatment for 4 h. Lastly, the dispersed solution of MoS  $  _{2}  $  with the modification of PAS was obtained.

In order to confirm the above reaction and the format of the   $  MoS_{2}  $   after modification, some measurements were carried out, the results of which are shown in Figure 2. The fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra, as shown in Figure 2a, shows that the spectra of   $  MoS_{2}  $  -PAS appear some peaks at   $  2924\ cm^{-1}  $   and   $  2131\ cm^{-1}  $  , which indicates the coordination bond between   $  MoS_{2}  $   and PAS. The two peaks at   $  2924\ cm^{-1}  $   and   $  2131\ cm^{-1}  $   are attributed to the peak shift of stretching vibration peak of C-H (  $  3050\ cm^{-1}  $  ) and C≡C (  $  2924\ cm^{-1}  $  ) in benzene ring, respectively. In addition, the stretching vibration peak of C≡C shifts from   $  2010\ cm^{-1}  $   to   $  2131\ cm^{-1}  $  . These results imply that the chemical environment of PAS and   $  MoS_{2}  $   changes because of the coordination reaction of S in the   $  MoS_{2}  $   with Ag in the PAS.

Figure 2b shows the Raman spectra of MoS $ _2 $  and modified MoS $ _2 $ . MoS $ _2 $  has two characteristic peaks, one of which is the in-plane vibration peak E $ _{2g} $  $ ^1 $ , and the other of which is out-of-plane vibration peak A $ _{1g} $ . The A $ _{1g} $  is sensitive to electronic coupling and easily produce on the surface of MoS $ _2 $ , but has almost no effect on inter-layer action. The E $ _{2g} $  $ ^1 $  is influenced by the Coulomb force between the layers of Mo atoms $ ^{17} $ . After the modification, the peak position of A $ _{1g} $  is blue-shift and its intensity relative to the peak of E $ _{2g} $  $ ^1 $  is obviously decreased, indicating a strong electron coupling effect to prove the occurring of chemical reaction between the MoS $ _2 $  and the PAS. The blue-shift of E $ _{2g} $  $ ^1 $  indicates that PAS can promote the aggregation of MoS $ _2 $  during drying process. On the other hand, the difference between E $ _{2g} $  $ ^1 $  and A $ _{1g} $  for MoS $ _2 $ -PAS was slightly lower than the one for pure MoS $ _2 $  that indicated that the last MoS $ _2 $  modified by PAS had fewer layers $ ^{[17]} $ .

Figure 2c compares the thermogravimeric analysis of   $  MoS_{2}  $  , PAS and PAS modified   $  MoS_{2}  $  . Because the decomposition of PAS will produce a mass loss, thermogravimeric analysis can quantify the amount of PAS on the surface of   $  MoS_{2}  $  . By this, it can also indirectly prove the adsorption format of PAS on the surface. As shown in Figure 2c, the mass loss of   $  MoS_{2}  $   is nearly negligible between 100 °C to 500 °C because   $  MoS_{2}  $   keeps thermostable at the temperature low than 600 °C. However, PAS shows a sharp decrease after the temperature increased up to 197 °C. Meaningwhile, the PAS modified   $  MoS_{2}  $   only has a stable mass loss about 5% after 300 °C, which is attributed to the decomposition of PAS integrated on top of   $  MoS_{2}  $  . From the thermogravimeric curve, the decomposition temperature of the PAS modified   $  MoS_{2}  $   is about 275 °C which is much higher than that of the pure PAS about 197 °C. The comparison proves that the S and Ag forms chemical bond that increases the decomposition temperature of PAS.

Figure S1 shows the XRD spectra of   $  MoS_{2}  $   and   $  MoS_{2}-PAS  $  . There are new peaks at   $  24.12^{\circ}  $   and   $  38.91^{\circ}  $   for   $  MoS_{2}-PAS  $  , which suggests a new crystal structure that is formed on the surface of   $  MoS_{2}  $  . The results verify that   $  MoS_{2}  $   and PAS performed chemical reaction during the process of ultrasound. In addition, the increase of peak of   $  64.75^{\circ}  $   is observed, which suggests the increase of crystal sizes owing to the reaction of   $  MoS_{2}  $   and PAS.

In summary, we have designed a facile method to functionalize   $  MoS_{2}  $   with PAS via a simple solution process. The organic metal compound was successfully functionalized on the surface of   $  MoS_{2}  $   through coordination of surface S atoms with Ag of the PAS. The production of   $  MoS_{2}  $  -PAS is fully characterized by UV/Vis, FT-IR, TGA, XRD and Raman spectroscopy, which provides strong evidence for the chemical functionalization of surface. The   $  MoS_{2}  $   functionized in solvents DMF shows a well dispersibility in both DMF and water, which contributes to N atoms of DMF coordination with Ag of PAS. By adding the   $  MoS_{2}  $  -PAS into PEDOT:PSS as hole transport layer, solar cells based on planar perovskite heterojunction shows significant enhanced power conversion efficiency and stability. This work provides a feasible method to large-quantity produce the 2D materials of   $  MoS_{2}  $   that can be widely used in electric devices.

# Sample preparation

0.3 g silver nitrate was dissolved in 6 ml deionized water. Silver nitrate solution was added into 0.170 g phenyl acetylene, stirring for 20 min to full reaction. The white solution was centrifuged at 4,000 rpm for 5 min to obtain white solid powder. The white solid power was washed three times with deionized water to remove the excess of silver nitrate. MoS  $  _{2}  $   nanosheets were produced by liquid exfoliation. Firstly, an ultrasonic treatment was carried out on bulk MoS  $  _{2}  $   powder in 100 ml NMP under 40 °C for 1 h. The dispersion solution was centrifuged at 4,000 rpm for 20 min. The supernatant containing impurities was discarded and the grey sediment was collected for further sonication. After a successive sonication for 5 h, MoS  $  _{2}  $   dispersion was mixed with phenyl acetylene silver, followed with a continuing ultrasonic treatment for 10 h. After this process, the solution was centrifuged at 8,000 rpm for 30 min and a black solid powder was obtained. The black solid power was washed three times with NMP to remove the excess of phenyl acetylene silver. The washed black powers are dried at 60 °C to get final products.

# Materials characterization

The products were characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Rigaku D/Max 2500 PC) and Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR, Nicolet 6700). The XRD were done from  $ 5^{\circ} $  to  $ 80^{\circ} $  with the step of  $ 6^{\circ} / \mathrm{min} $ . The FT-IR tests were carried out after the samples were blended with KBr with the volume ratio of 1:100 and then tableted with bead machine. Thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA) measurements were performed using a synchronous thermal analyzer (STA, STA449F3Jupiter) under nitrogen atmosphere at the temperature from  $ 25^{\circ}C $  to  $ 600^{\circ}C $ .

# Fabrication and Characterization of Perovskite Solar Cells

PEDOT:PSS (4083) or PEDOT:PSS blend with MoS₂ was spin-coated on ITO with a speed of 3,000 rpm for 30 seconds, and annealed on a hot plate at 130 °C for one hour in a N₂ glove box. The MAPbI₃ and DEACl mixture solution was deposited on top of the substrates with the PEDOT:PSS layer to the produce perovskite film via the process described in our previous paper[32]. A mixture solution with 1 mmol CH₃NH₃PbI₃ and 0.4 mmol DEACl dissolved in 1 ml DMF was spin coating on PEDOT:PSS films with the speed of 4,000 rpm and then annealed on a plate for 10 min at temperature of 130 °C. After the perovskite film was formed, an electronic transfer material (15 mg of PCBM was added into 1 ml of chlorobenzene) was spin-coated on top of the perovskite film at a speed of 1,500 rpm for 30 seconds. Finally, a 120 nm silver (Ag) was deposited on top of the PCBM layer via a thermal-evaporation process in a vacuum chamber with a pressure of less than 10⁻⁴ Pa. The typical active area of the devices was approximately 0.12 cm², which was determined by a shadow mask during the top electrode evaporation. The solar light source was simulated by AM1.5 sun light with an intensity of 100 mW/cm² provided by a solar simulator. The current density (J)-voltage (V) was recorded using a Keithley 2400 source meter unit in N₂ glove box. Then the devices were tested for 10 days by 2 days once.
