# Performance Improvement of Perovskite Solar Cells Based on PCBM-Modified ZnO-Nanorod Arrays

Yang Xu, Yi Wang, Jichao Yu, Bingjie Feng, Hai Zhou, Jun Zhang, Jinxia Duan, Xi Fan, Peter A. van Aken, Peter D. Lund, and Hao Wang

Abstract—Here, we report a promising perovskite solar cell structure based on a modified ZnO-nanorod interfacial structure with improved stability. An organic surface modifier, i.e., [6,6]-phenyl   $  C_{61}  $   butyric acid methyl ester (PCMB), was employed between the perovskite and the ZnO-nanorod arrays to modify the interface condition. As a result, the optimized structure improves the light absorption, accelerates the transfer of the carriers, and reduces the electron and hole recombination losses in the solar cell. A perovskite solar cell with a structure of FTO/ZnO-nanorods/PCBM/  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  /Spiro-OMeTAD/Au gives an 18.8% enhancement in the short-circuit current density than a device without PCBM. Moreover, the open-circuit voltage was improved by 18.5% to 0.96 V. A power conversion efficiency of 11.67% was reached in cell with PCBM modification; results indicate that a modified ZnO-nanorod perovskite structure could have potential for even higher performance.

Index Terms—Light absorption, perovskite solar cell (PSC), power conversion efficiency (PCE), surface modification, ZnO-nanorod, [6, 6]-phenyl C  $  _{61}  $  butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM).

# I. INTRODUCTION

ORGANOMETAL trihalide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have recently received much attention due to their rapid development   $  [1-5]  $  . It was first used as dye sensitizer in a sealed liquid dye-sensitized solar cell in 2009 with the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of only 3.8%   $  [6]  $  . A breakthrough was achieved when a solid-state hole conductor spiro-OMeTAD was introduced using a   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticle film as a mesoporous scaffold, which leads to a PCE of 9.7% in 2012   $  [7]  $  . Extensive research in recent years has resulted in PSCs with an amazing 20.1% efficiency   $  [8, 9]  $  . These state-of-the-art new cells

surpass other third-generation solar cells and have become one of the most promising candidates for silicon-based solar cells. The hybrid perovskite has a formula   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbX_{3}  $   (X = I, Br, Cl). It presents a direct bandgap, large absorption coefficients over a broad range, and a tunable optical bandgap. The assembled PSCs have a high light adsorption, low exciton binding energy, long diffusion length, and outstanding charge carrier's mobility, which are important factors to reach good solar conversion efficiency [10–18]. The theoretical short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of the assembled PSCs is up to 27.2 mA/cm² in the visible range [19]. However, currently, the   $  J_{sc}  $   of best PSCs is around 20 mA/cm² [20]. One of the main issues is the poor coverage of the perovskite on the scaffold [21, 22]. One way to improve the coverage is doping the electrode or modifying the surface of the scaffold to enhance the adsorption ability. Another way to improve the coverage of the perovskite   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbX_{3}  $   is exploring novel scaffold configuration to load more perovskite. A scaffold could be composed of semiconductor nanoparticles to provide a large surface area for adsorbing the perovskite, but the poor infiltration of perovskite in a nanoparticle scaffold limits the thickness of the scaffold layer and accordingly suppresses the light adsorption. The thickness of the scaffold layer is generally about 400–600 nm [23], although the electron–hole diffusion lengths were reported to be larger than 175 μm in solution grown   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbX_{3}  $   [24]. Furthermore, the boundary effects in the nanoparticle scaffold also cause recombination between carriers and reduce the photovoltaic performance of the PSCs. One-dimensional nanorod arrays could be a promising alternative to the conventional nanoparticle scaffold because they provide straight channels for   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbX_{3}  $   to infiltrate into the scaffold and a direct route for electron transportation. Recently, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 17.9 mA/cm² and a PCE of 6.52% were shown for PSCs assembled with   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanotubes [25]. In comparison with   $  TiO_{2}  $   1-D arrays, ZnO-nanorod arrays have several advantages over   $  TiO_{2}  $ , such as solution process ability, high electron mobility, and ease of fabrication of crystallized ZnO in a variety of structures [26]. PSCs based on ZnO-nanorod arrays scaffold have also been reported. In 2013, a ZnO-nanorod PSC was first introduced by Bi et al., however, only a PCE of 5% was obtained for the pure ZnO-nanorod arrays [26]. By improving the fabrication method of ZnO-nanorod arrays, a PCE of 8.9% was achieved in the ZnO-nanorod-based PSC [27]. Until 2014, Son et al. improved the PCE to 11.13% by optimizing the length of ZnO-nanorod arrays to 1 μm [28]. Recently, PSCs with ZnO-nanorods have reached a PCE of 14.35% and a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 19.44 mA/cm² by using a colloidal solution of zinc acetate to prepare the seed layer for subsequent growth of ZnO-nanorods [29]. As a fullerene derivative, [6,6]-phenyl   $  C_{61}  $   butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) is an excellent electron acceptor and was generally used in planner structure PSCs as an electron transfer layer [31, 32]. Kyaw et al. also reported that the introduction of PCBM can effectively modify the interfacial properties of ZnO-nanorod arrays to improve the PCE of small-molecule solar cells [33]. The   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.06 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and a PCE of 11.67% were achieved in the PSC based on the PCBM-modified ZnO-nanorod arrays. More importantly, the modification of ZnO-nanorod arrays with PCBM resulted in several effects on the PSCs.

1) It creates a rough surface on the  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  arrays to loading more  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and hence increases the  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  of the cells.

2) It provides a proper energy levels served as a ladder to reduce the energy difference between perovskite and ZnO-nanorod arrays and, hence, accelerates the separation and transfer of carriers.

3) The PCBM modification may repair the cracks between ZnO-nanorods and, hence, suppresses the carriers' recombination and improves the photovoltaic performance of cells as well.

# II. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

# A. Growth and Modification of ZnO-Nanorod Arrays

The ZnO-nanorod arrays were grown on the FTO glass substrates through the water bath method   $  [33, 34]  $  . Prior to the growth, the FTO glass substrates were cleaned by ultrasonics in a detergent and followed by deionized (DI) water, acetone, and ethyl alcohol sequentially for 20 min, respectively. The substrate was then treated with UV-ozone for 15 min to get rid of organic materials. ZnO seed layer was first prepared as follows. A 2 mM methyl alcohol solution of zinc acetate dihydrate   $  (\mathrm{Zn}(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{COO})_{2} \times 2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}, \text{Aldrich}, 98\%)  $   was spin-coated onto the cleaned FTO glass at a spinning rate of 3000 r/min for 15 s. Then, the samples were annealed at 350 °C for 30 min. The solution for growing ZnO-nanorod arrays was prepared by dissolving equal molar zinc nitrate hexahydrate   $  (\mathrm{Zn}(\mathrm{NO}_{3})_{2} \times 6\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}, \text{Aldrich}, 98\%)  $   and hexamethylenetetramine (HMTA, Aldrich, 99%) in 150 ml DI water. The solution concentration was adjusted to 50 mM. Then, 0.6 g Polyetherimide (PEI, Aldrich, 98%) was added in the solution to control the diameter of the ZnO-nanorod arrays. Finally, a certain amount of ammonia   $  (\mathrm{NH}_{3} \times \mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}, \text{Aldrich}, 25–28\%)  $   was added to adjust the pH to   $  \sim10.3  $  . The FTO substrates with ZnO seed layers were immersed in the solution at 85 °C for 3 h. When the FTO was taken out, the ZnO-nanorod film was washed with DI water and ethanol several times and, finally, annealed at 350 °C for 2 h.

Fig. 1. Schematic configuration of the whole PSC.

For modification of ZnO-nanorod arrays, a 1 mM chlorobenzene solution of PCBM was dropped onto the ZnO-nanorod arrays (the length of ZnO-nanorod arrays was 0.9–1   $  \mu  $  m) on the FTO substrate. After waiting for 15 s, the sample was spun at 4000 r/min for 10 s and, finally, dried at 80 °C for 10 min. All steps were done in a glove box. Fig. 1 shows the schematic structure of the PCBM-modified ZnO-nanorod arrays. The small red spheres represent PCBM particles, and they have been introduced onto the surface of the ZnO-nanorod arrays.

# B. Assembly of Perovskite Solar Cells

Methylammonium lead iodide,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , was deposited on the ZnO-nanorod film by a two-step spin-coating method [35]. Methylamine (  $  CH_{3}NH_{2}  $  ) (13.5 mL, 40 wt% in aqueous solution, Alfa Aesar) and hydroiodic acid (HI) (15.0 mL, 57 wt% in water, Alfa Aesar) were stirred at 0 °C under a nitrogen atmosphere for 2 h. After the reaction, the solvent was removed using a rotary evaporator. This reaction produced the white powder, methylammonium iodide (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  ). The white precipitate was washed with diethyl ether (Sigma-Aldrich) three times and dried at 60 °C in a vacuum oven overnight. A   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution (dissolved in N, N-dimethylformamide at a concentration of 460 mg/ml  $  ^{1}  $   for overnight) was spin-coated on the ZnO-nanorod arrays layer at 2000 r/min for 15 s. After drying at 70 °C for 10 min, the sample was cooled down in air, and the substrate was dipped into a solution of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   in 2-propanol (10 mg/ml  $  ^{1}  $  ) for 50 s and dried under a flow of air for 15 min. Subsequently, the spiro-OMeTAD-based hole-transfer layer (80 mg spiro-OMeTAD, 28.5 ml 4-tertbutylpyridine and 17.5 ml lithium-bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) solution (520 mg Li-TFSI in 1 ml acetonitrile) all dissolved in 1-ml chlorobenzene) was deposited by spin-coating at 3000 r/min for 10 s. Finally, a layer of   $  \sim  $  40-nm-thick Au was deposited using thermal evaporation at a deposition rate of 0.8 Å/s under a base pressure of   $  9.0 \times 10^{-4}  $   Pa. Fig. 1 also shows the schematic configuration of the whole PSC. The ZnO-nanorod arrays and PCBM would be completely covered by perovskite after deposition of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   and spiro-OMeTAD.

# III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The XRD pattern of the pure ZnO-nanorod arrays is shown in Fig. 2. Except for the weak peaks from the FTO substrate, it shows a wurtzite structure with a strong (0 0 2) peak. The relatively weak wurtzite ZnO (1 0 0), (1 0 1), (1 0 2), and (1 0 3) peaks can be also observed in this pattern. Fig. 3(a) shows the SEM image of pure annealed ZnO-nanorod arrays. Highly ordered hexagonal ZnO-nanorod arrays of 0.8–1  $ \mu $ m (most of nanorods is in the range of 0.9–1  $ \mu $ m) in length and with relatively smooth surface were formed, which is good for electron transmission. Fig. 3(b) shows the ZnO-nanorod arrays after being modified with PCBM. The ZnO-nanorod arrays are wrapped up by small grains and the cross-sectional shape of the nanorods is more cylindrical than hexahedral. The ordered ZnO-nanorod arrays configuration, in comparison with the nanoparticle scaffold, is beneficial for better penetration of CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3} $  into scaffold, which improves the loading of CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3} $ , and accordingly effectively enhances the light adsorption of the PSCs [26]. However, the smooth surface makes the arrays still not the best scaffold candidate for loading CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3} $ . After depositing with the PCBM particles, the surface of the nanorods is much rougher compared with the pure ZnO-nanorod arrays, which is helpful for a high loading of CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3} $ .

The fabrication process of perovskite is also critically important to the photovoltaic performance of the PSCs. A two-step spin-coating method was utilized to synthesize the perovskite  $ (\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3) $  film. The SEM image in Fig. 3(c) shows that the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  films are uniformly distributed on the ZnO-nanorod arrays, in the form of flat polygonal grains with triple grain boundaries [see the inset of Fig. 3(c)], which may lead to improved charge transport properties [37]. The cross-sectional SEM image of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film is shown in Fig. 3(d), which also demonstrates that the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  has fully permeated into the ZnO-nanorod arrays. This configuration is beneficial to the transfer of the carriers because the ordered arrays provide direct transfer route for electrons and the transmission distance of the electrons from  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  to ZnO-nanorods in this PSC is much shorter than that in a planar heterojunction [20]. The fast transfer of carriers can improve the device photovoltaic performance [38].

The modification of ZnO-nanorod arrays by PCBM could accelerate the electrons transfer and improve the  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  of the PSCs, which will be further discussed below.

To investigate the recombination situation of the cells, the dark current study of these two configurations was investigated and results are presented in Fig. 5(b). Due to the rise of Fermi level under forward bias (  $  V_{applied} > 500 \, mV  $  ) [7], the dark current is dominated by the recombination of excitons. Excitons are generated in the light absorbing layer and the recombination process of excitons can affect the   $  V_{oc}  $   of the devices negatively [20]. From Fig. 5(b), it can be observed that when the scanning potential is below 0.4 V, the dark current for both samples is still very small, indicating good device properties. However, at around 0.5 V, dark current of samples without PCBM modification starts to increase compared with that of the cell based on the PCBM modified nanorods, which introduces more interfaces between ZnO-nanorods and perovskite crystals, and hence strengthens the recombine process of the charge carrier. Dark current of the latter ones keeps steady until around 0.9 V, exhibiting better interface and suppressing the charge recombination process greatly. Therefore, the   $  V_{oc}  $   of the device is increased. Similar results are also confirmed in Fig. 6(a). This implies that the PCBM modification on the ZnO-nanorod arrays suppresses the recombination of carriers in the PSCs. This may be attributed to the fact that the cracks between ZnO-nanorods and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   were repaired by the PCBM particles, resulting in better interface contacts between ZnO-nanorods and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  . A similar phenomenon has been observed in dye-sensitized solar cells [46].

Fig. 5(c) gives the UV–visible absorption spectra in the range of 350–800 nm of pure ZnO-nanorod arrays,   $  ZnO/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   arrays, and ZnO/PCBM/  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   arrays. The absorption of the three kinds of arrays is in the order of ZnO-nanorod <   $  ZnO/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   < ZnO/PCBM/  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   in the visible range. The PCBM modification yields a 66% enhancement at 450 nm over the pure ZnO-nanorod case. Obviously, the significant improvement on 400–500 nm in Fig. 5(c) comes mainly from the better loading of perovskite crystal. The modified ZnO-nanorod scaffold has a rough surface to loading more perovskite material, which can further improve the adsorption in visible range. Furthermore, some papers also reported that PCBM can contribute light absorption in 300–650 nm somewhat [47], which partly enhances the final light absorption.

In addition, since   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   and HTM are same in our case,   $  V_{oc}  $   is mainly dependent on the quasi-Fermi level of the semiconductor [48]. At open circuit, the rate of electron injection is equal to the recombination. The quasi-Fermi level, and thus the   $  V_{oc}  $ , is determined by the steady-state electron concentration in the semiconductor, which can be increased by increasing the rate of electron injection (is related to   $  J_{sc}  $ ) or decreasing recombination. Since the   $  J_{sc}  $   of ZnO-nanorod arrays modified by PCBM is generally higher than that of the pure ZnO-nanorod PSC, it is reasonable to expect a larger   $  V_{oc}  $   obtained in the PCBM-modified ZnO-nanorod arrays PSC. As stability is a major issue of PSCs, the device performance shown in Fig. 8 was monitored in a glove box for 50 days of solar exposure. We found that the maximum efficiency of 11.67% was obtained in the cell displayed in glove box for ten days. This may be attributed to the fact that   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM  $

had not completely permeated into the ZnO-nanorod arrays yielding a lower filling factor (FF) in the beginning of test, but this improved over time because the better permeation of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM  $   into ZnO-nanorod arrays may repair the cracks between   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM  $   and ZnO in the deep part of arrays, which improves the carriers transportation, suppresses the recombination, and enhances the FF of the cells. In addition, in comparison with the traditional nanoparticle scaffold, the ordered ZnO-nanorod arrays have channels perpendicular to the conductive substrates, resulting in better   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM  $   loading and confinement of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   particle size, which reduces the path length of the electron and hole before their collection. On the other hand, the lifetime trace of cells based on   $  ZnO/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   has also been presented in Fig. 8. The   $  J_{sc}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $  , FF, and PCE of the cells all exhibit similar trends over time as the ZnO/PCBM/  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   cells, although there is some deviation in current density and voltage. The increase of the PCE is mainly caused by the increase of the FF. Previous literature reported a similar phenomenon [51] and attributed the improved PCE, as well as the FF values, to the better penetration of the perovskite crystal into the ZnO-nanorods. However, over time, the perovskite starts to decompose, which decreases the cell efficiency for both situations. Clearly, the special nanoconfiguration of ZnO-nanorod arrays exhibits a better stability over the traditional cells. This behavior has been confirmed by an early report in [52].

# IV. CONCLUSION

A PSC based on PCBM-modified ZnO-nanorod arrays has been developed. It was found that the decoration of ZnO-nanorod arrays with PCBM creates a rough surface on the ZnO-nanorod arrays, increasing the loading of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   crystal and enhancing the   $  J_{sc}  $   of the cells. PCBM also provides proper energy levels between ZnO and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   to serve as a ladder to reduce the energy difference and, hence, accelerates the carrier separation and transfer. Furthermore, the PCBM particles may repair the cracks between nanorods and suppress the recombination of carriers in PSCs and improve the photovoltaic performance as well. As a result, the cell showed a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 21.92 mA/cm² and a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 0.96 V. The PCE of the PCBM modified PSC was even slightly improved to 11.67% for ten days displaying in the glove box, which is mainly attributed to the better permeation of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM  $   into ZnO-nanorod arrays over time. Despite the above result, much still needs to be done to fabricate better PSCs.
