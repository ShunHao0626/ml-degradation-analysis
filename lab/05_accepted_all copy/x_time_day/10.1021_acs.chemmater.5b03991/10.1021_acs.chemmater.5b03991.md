# Room-temperature Solution-Processed n-Doped Zirconium Oxide Cathode Buffer Layer for Efficient and Stable Organic and Hybrid Perovskite Solar Cells

Chih-Yu Chang  $  ^{*†}  $  , Wen-Kuan Huang  $  ^{\dagger}  $  , Jhao-Lin Wu  $  ^{\ddagger}  $  , Yu-Chia Chang  $  ^{\dagger}  $  , Kuan-Ting Lee  $  ^{\dagger}  $  , Chin-Ti Chen  $  ^{*\ddagger}  $

$  ^{\dagger}  $   Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Feng Chia University, Taichung, Taiwan 40724, R.O.C.

$  ^{\ddagger}  $  Institute of Chemistry, Academia Sinica, Taipei, Taiwan 11529, R.O.C.

ABSTRACT: In this study, we present a simple and effective method to improve the performance and stability of organic and hybrid perovskite solar cells by the incorporation of solution-processed cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB)-doped zirconium oxide  $ (\mathrm{ZrO_x}) $  as cathode buffer layer (CBL). This novel n-doped  $ \mathrm{ZrO_x} $  CBL possesses several remarkable features, including easy of fabrication without the need of thermal annealing or any other post-treatment, reasonable electrical conductivity  $ (2.9\times 10^{-5}\mathrm{Scm}^{-1}) $ , good ambient stability, effective work function modulation of Ag electrode, relative weak thickness-dependent performance property, and wide applicability in a variety of active layers. Compared with  $ \mathrm{ZrO_x} $  CBL without CTAB dopant, CTAB-doped  $ \mathrm{ZrO_x} $  can significantly improve the power conversion efficiency (PCE) from  $ 0.57\% $  to  $ 2.48\% $  in organic solar cells based on diketopyrrolopyrrole-thiophene-bezothiadazole low bandgap polymer (PDPP-TBT):[6,6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{71} $ -butyric acid methyl ester  $ (\mathrm{PC}_{71}\mathrm{BM}) $  blend. With this n-doped  $ \mathrm{ZrO_x} $  CBL, organic solar cells based on polythieno(3,4-b)-thiophene-alt-benzodithiophene (PTB7): $ \mathrm{PC}_{71}\mathrm{BM} $  blend deliver a record high PCE of  $ 9.3\% $ . The effectiveness of this novel CBL also extends to perovskite solar cells, and a high PCE up to  $ 15.9\% $  is demonstrated, which is superior to those of the devices with undoped  $ \mathrm{ZrO_x} $  and state-of-the-art CBL zinc oxide nanoparticles film. In addition, this approach is applicable to the development high-performance semitransparent solar cells. More significantly, the long-term ambient stability of the resulting devices can be secured without the need of rigorous encapsulation.

# INTRODUCTION

Clean and inexhaustible solar energy is one of the most promising alternative energy sources to supplement and eventually replace non-renewable fossil fuels. Photovoltaic solar cells are the devices that make direct conversion of solar photons into electricity. Nowadays, the solar industry today predominately produces solar panels made from inorganic materials such as crystalline silicon and other thin film vapor-phase deposited semiconductors. However, they are far from full-scale commercialization due to their prohibitive high cost and complex manufacturing process. Solution-processable solar cells utilizing organic semiconductors or organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite compounds (e.g.   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  ) as the photoactive materials have garnered an explosion of interest, thanks to their reasonable performance and the low costs in their materials and processing.  $  ^{1-5}  $   The device performance is governed not only by the electronic energy levels, absorption characteristics, and charge carrier mobility of the photoactive materials, but also by the effective charge extraction from the photoactive layer and then collection by the electrodes.  $  ^{1-7}  $   A typical device structure comprises substrate/indium tin oxide (ITO) anode/poly(styrene sulfonate) (PSS) doped poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) (PEDOT:PSS)/photo-active layer/cathode.  $  ^{1-4}  $   To ensure ohmic contact for maximizing achievable open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) and minimizing energy barrier for charge extraction without causing excessive interface recombination, low work function (WF) metals such as Ca (~2.9 eV) or Al (~4.1 eV) are usually used.  $  ^{1-4}  $   Despite high device performance achieved, the realization of long-term stable device remains a pivotal challenge because of the high susceptibility of low WF metals to atmospheric moisture or oxygen. Although the use of more ambient-stable high

WF metals such as Au (  $  \sim  $  5.1 eV) or Ag (  $  \sim  $  4.7 eV) as cathode is highly desirable, the mismatch between the WF of these metals and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level of active layer often compromises the device performance.  $  ^{6-11}  $

An effective approach to address this issue is to incorporate an additional cathode buffer layer (CBL) between the active layer and high WF electrode. Several solution-processed CBL materials, including metal oxides or cationic molecules, have been applied to improve the contact properties and consequently enhance the device performance.  $  ^{6-11}  $   The n-type inorganic metal oxides such as sol-gel-based zinc oxide (ZnO) and   $  TiO_{x}  $   have become the predominant options for CBL in organic and perovskite solar cells due to their desirable properties such as low cost, high optical transparency, excellent ambient stability, and good electron transport ability.  $  ^{4,6,7,11}  $   The relative high refractive index of these metal oxides can also function as the optical spacer to enhance the light absorption in the photoactive layer via adjusting the field distribution within the devices.  $  ^{6}  $   Nevertheless, the implementation of these materials into organic and perovskite-based solar cells remains challenging due to the fact that the formation of these CBLs generally requires an annealing step at relatively high temperature (  $  \geq400\ ^{\circ}C  $  ) to ensure high film quality.  $  ^{4,12}  $   This requirement also impedes the progress towards the development of flexible solar cells and high-performance multi-junction tandem solar cells. Despite the fact that recent efforts have been devoted to developing relatively low-temperature processing of metal oxide CBLs via the modified sol-gel route, the resulting device performance is usually inferior to those of the devices with high-temperature processed metal oxide CBLs,  $  ^{12,13}  $   possible due to the insufficient electrical conduc

To overcome intrinsic materials limitations and to improve the overall device performance, an effective strategy is to incorporate charge donating dopants into the host metal oxides (i.e., n-doping) to alter the carrier concentrations and hence the electronic state of the materials. Although several solution-based processing of alkali-metal compounds such as cesium carbonate or lithium fluoride have been utilized as n-type dopants to enhance the electrical conduction of metal oxides and improve the interface properties,  $  ^{15,16}  $   the samples are generally suffer from degradation issues due to the highly hygroscopic nature of these dopants. An effective approach to circumvent this problem is to incorporate relative stable halogen anion such as bromine (Br) or fluorine (F) as the dopant.  $  ^{17-22}  $   These dopants can preferentially substitute the oxygen sites and/or occupy oxygen vacancies, thus contributing to enhanced conductivity. However, their applications to optoelectronic devices are rarely reported.  $  ^{23}  $

In this study, for the first time, we demonstrate an effective, low-temperature, solution-processed n-doped zirconium oxide (ZrO $ _x $ ) CBL by incorporating relatively stable cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB; see Figure 1a for the chemical structure) as the dopant into zirconium acetylacetonate (ZrAcac) precursor solution. We choose ZrAcac as the precursor material because it has been proven as a good candidate for use in low-temperature fabrication of high-quality ZrO $ _x $  film. $ ^{24-26} $  The applicability of this CBL in solution-processed organic and hybrid perovskite solar cells is also studied, where high WF Ag is used as the cathode layer to ensure long-term environmental stability (see Figure 1b and 1c for the device structures). Despite ZrO $ _x $  CBL possesses several advantages over the traditionally used metal oxide CBLs such as facile preparation, appropriate energy level to ensure efficient charge selectivity, and general applicability to different materials systems, $ ^{26} $  our results indicate that the devices with undoped ZrO $ _x $  CBL show only moderate device performance due to the presence of the energetic barrier at the ZrO $ _x $ /Ag interface, while this issue can be resolved after doping ZrO $ _x $  with CTAB. In addition, the electrical conductivity of ZrO $ _x $  film can be enhanced by  $ \sim $ 4 orders of magnitude upon doping with CTAB. Notably, CTAB-doped ZrO $ _x $  CBL also possesses good ambient stability and high compatibility with various active materials. With this CBL, organic and perovskite-based solar cells deliver PCE up to 9.3% and 15.9%, respectively, greatly outperforming the devices with undoped ZrO $ _x $  film and state-of-the-art CBL ZnO nanoparticles (NPs) film. Importantly, the long-term ambient stability of the resulting devices can also be secured without the need of rigorous encapsulation. The applicability of this novel CBL in high-performance semitransparent solar cells is also demonstrated. This work demonstrates a simple and effective

strategy for improving device performance and stability through interface engineering.

First, we studied the influence of CTAB doping on the electrical properties of   $  ZrO_{x}  $   film. The electrical conductivities of   $  ZrO_{x}  $   film at varied dopant concentration were measured the four-point probe technique, and the results were depicted in Figure 2a. As the concentration of CTAB increased, the conductivity first increased dramatically: the conductivity increased from   $  4.3\times10^{-9}  $   S cm  $  ^{-1}  $   (undoped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   film) to a maximum of   $  2.9\times10^{-5}  $   S cm  $  ^{-1}  $   (with 10 mol% CTAB doping), which is comparable to those of the other organic conducting materials such as PEDOT:PSS (  $  10^{-5}-10^{-6}  $   S cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for Clevious P VP CH 8000) and conjugated polyelectrolyte (  $  \sim10^{-5}  $   S cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ).  $  ^{[27,28]}  $   This enhancement in electrical conductivity is consistent with previous studies showing that the incorporation of halide anions into metal oxides can preferentially substitute the oxygen sites to generate free electrons and/or occupy oxygen vacancies to eliminate electron trap sites, leading to efficient n-type doping.  $  ^{[17-20]}  $   Analysis of X-ray photoemission spectroscopy (XPS) revealed that the Zr 3d spectra for CTAB-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   shifted toward a lower binding energy by   $  \sim0.32  $   eV in comparison to the value for undoped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   (Supporting Information (SI) Figure S1), suggesting the formation of partially reduced Zr ions. This discrepancy in the XPS spectra is in good agreement with previous findings and can be explained by the possible formation of doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   material.  $  ^{[29]}  $   When the doping concentrations were further increased beyond 10 mol%, however, the conductivity decreased gradually (Figure 2a). This deterioration could be ascribed to the tendency of excess CTAB-caused aggregation. It should be noted that the influence of the dopant with short alkyl chain (hexyltrimethylammonium bromide; see SI Figure S2 for the chemical structure) on the electrical properties of   $  ZrO_{x}  $   film was also investigated. As shown in SI Figure S2, the samples showed very similar results to those obtained for CTAB-doped films. The electrical conductivity reached a maximum value of   $  3.6\times10^{-5}  $   S cm  $  ^{-1}  $   (10 mol% doping), which was comparable to that of CTAB-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   film.

To gain more insight into the morphological alteration of the films, atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements were carried out in tapping mode. The obtained topography images of 5   $  \mu  $  m × 5   $  \mu  $  m scans of the films were depicted in Figure 3. For the films with doping concentration below 10 mol%, smooth surface morphology (rms roughness ~0.6 nm) with no obvious phase separation was observed (Figure 3), indicating high miscibility between   $  ZrO_{x}  $   and CTAB. Further increases in the doping concentration of CTAB beyond 10 mol% revealed a greater surface roughness (rms roughness = 1.7-3.8 nm) with more pronounced phase-separated domains. In addition to the reasonable conductivity, we observed that the doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   film (10 mol% doping) possessed good ambient stability: ~40% of its initial conductivity could be retained upon exposure to ambient air (30 °C, ~60% relative humidity).

The effect of CTAB doping on the work function of Ag electrode was then investigated by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurement. As shown in Figure 4a, the work function value of bare Ag electrode was determined to be 4.69 eV, which is very close to the reported values (  $  \sim  $  4.7 eV).  $  ^{30}  $   When Ag electrode was modified with undoped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   film, the WF was reduced to a moderate value of 4.21 eV. Given that the LUMO level of electron-accepting material (e.g. [6,6]-phenyl-  $  C_{6i}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (  $  PC_{6i}BM  $  ) or [6,6]-phenyl-  $  C_{7i}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (  $  PC_{7i}BM  $  )) is approximately at 4.10 eV,  $  ^{31}  $   which is slightly lower than the work function of   $  ZrO_{x}/Ag  $   electrode (4.21 eV). This led to unfavorable electron extraction from the active layer to the electrode because of the Schottky-barrier effect, as illustrated in Figure 4b. On the other hand, modification of Ag electrode with CTAB-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   layer (10 mol% doping) effectively reduced WF to 3.72 eV. The low WF contact at the cathode interface provides better energy level matching with electron-accepting   $  PC_{6i}BM  $   or   $  PC_{7i}BM  $   layer and forms ohmic contact (Figure 4b), which is preferable for efficient electron extraction as described previously.  $  ^{1-4}  $   This effective WF modulation would be beneficial for improving the built-in potential in device thus contributes to high open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ), as discussed later. The significant decrease in the WF via CTAB doping can be attributed to the formation of favorable interfacial dipole created by the strong interaction between the ammonium cations and Ag electrode surface, as several recent studies have demonstrated that the introduction of ammonium cations-based molecules at the cathode interface can induce interfacial dipoles toward the active layer that can significantly elevate the vacuum level of the cathode for efficient electron extraction.  $  ^{8,10}  $

To investigate the effect of CTAB-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   CBL on the photovoltaic performance of bulk heterojunction type organic solar cells with a configuration of glass substrate/ITO/PEDOT:PSS/polythieno(3,4-b)-thiophene-alt-benzodithiophene (PTB7) blended with   $  PC_{71}BM/CTAB-doped\ ZrO_{x}  $   (10 mol% doping)/Ag was fabricated. The chemical structures of PTB7 and   $  PC_{71}BM  $   were shown in Figure 5a. A control device with undoped ZrOx CBL was also fabricated for comparison. The solar-to-electrical PCE were evaluated by recording the current-voltage (J-V) characteristics under simulated AM 1.5 G conditions (intensity = 100 mWcm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). The average values and the corresponding standard deviations of photovoltaic parameters including   $  V_{oc}  $  , short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ), fill factor (FF), and PCE were summarized in Table 1. The J-V characteristics of the best performing devices were also depicted in Figure 5b, and the detailed photovoltaic parame-

ters were listed in Table 1. For the control device employing undoped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   as CBL (device A), an average PCE of 2.51% was attained, with   $  J_{sc}  $   of 12.09 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $   of 0.53 V, and FF of 39.38% (Table 1 and Figure 5b). Although 2.51% PCE was moderate, it was still higher than that of the device without CBL (device B; PCE = 0.1%), as shown in Table 1 and Figure 5b. This result is consistent with what is observed from UPS measurement (Figure 4a) that   $  ZrO_{x}  $   film can modify the work function of Ag electrode even without adding dopants. When using CTAB-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   film (10 mol% doping) as CBL (device C), a substantial improvement in PCE (by ~3.5 fold) was demonstrated as a result of the simultaneously increased all the parameters. As shown in Table 1, device C exhibited an average PCE of 8.89%, with   $  J_{sc}  $   of 16.73 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $   of 0.73 V, and FF of 72.83%. In particular, the best performing device showed a PCE of 9.3%, with   $  J_{sc}  $   of 16.87 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $   of 0.73 V, and FF of 73.56% (Table 1 and Figure 5b). Note that the measured   $  J_{sc}  $   of device C agreed very well (within 10% error) with the   $  J_{sc}  $   values integrated from incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra (SI Figure S3), which confirmed the accuracy of our reported PCE values. To the best of our knowledge, 9.3% is among the highest reported PCE for PTB7:PC  $  _{71}  $  BM-based solar cells.  $  ^{32}  $   It should be noted that the photovoltaic performance of the device using CTAB as CBL (i.e., without   $  ZrO_{x}  $  ; device D) was found to be inferior to that of doped device (Table 1 and Figure 5b), presumably due to the insufficient CTAB film coverage, as can be seen from AFM topography images shown in SI Figure S4. These results clearly indicate the effectiveness of using CTAB-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   film as the viable CBL in organic solar cells.

To gain further insight into the PCE improvement afforded by CTAB dopant, the series resistance   $  (R_{\mathrm{s}})  $   and shunt resistance   $  (R_{\mathrm{sh}})  $   of the devices were investigated. The   $  R_{s}  $   and   $  R_{sh}  $   are calculated from the inverse slope of the J-V characteristics under dark conditions close to o and 2 V, respectively (Figure 5c). Compared to the control device (device A), the device with doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   CBL (device C) exhibited a lower series resistance (1.6   $  \Omega  $     $  cm^{2}  $   vs. 2366   $  \Omega  $     $  cm^{2}  $   for device A) while maintaining a high shunt resistance (  $  \sim174  $     $  K\Omega  $     $  cm^{2}  $  ), leading to higher rectification ratio (  $  \sim10^{5}  $  ). This improvement was consistent with what was observed from the electrical conductivities measurements that the conductivity could be enhanced significantly upon the addition of 10 mol% CTAB as the dopant (Figure 2a). These results elucidate that CTAB-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   CBL can provide interface modification superior to that of undoped film, ensuring efficient electron extraction and preventing undesirable electron-hole recombination between active layer and Ag electrode.

The applicability of CTAB-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   CBL to hybrid halide perovskite solar cells based on methylammonium lead iodide (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  ) was also demonstrated. device G was summarized in Figure 6b. Importantly, more than 90% of the integrated devices delivered PCE above 14.5% with low standard deviation (Table 1 and Figure 2c), indicating good reproducibility. We envisage that the reproducibility of the devices can be further improved by controlling the morphology of the perovskite layer with a new fabrication method.  $  ^{34}  $   In addition, given that planar heterojunction PSCs have been shown to be prone to hysteresis,  $  ^{3,4}  $     $  J-V  $   characteristic of the device with doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   CBL (device G) measured under different scan directions was then analyzed. Encouragingly, we observed that the hysteresis was negligible under reverse and forward scan directions and various voltage sweep rates were then analyzed. As shown in Figure 6c and d, our device exhibited photocurrent hysteresis-free J-V characteristics with different scanning directions and/or voltage sweep rates, which can be ascribed to effective passivation of trap states in the perovskite afforded by   $  PC_{6i}BM  $   capping layer.  $  ^{35,36}  $

Controlling the thickness of CBL usually plays an important role for performance optimization of perovskite solar cells. Here the thickness effects of the undoped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   and CTAB-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   (10 mol% doping) films on the PCEs of perovskite solar cells were investigated. For the devices with undoped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   CBL, a strong correlation between the device performance and CBL thickness was observed. The PCE rapidly declined from 7.12% to 4.17% when CBL thickness was increased from the optimal thickness of 10 nm to 20 nm (Table 4 and Figure 7a). When employing doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   film as CBL, however, the device performance exhibited a more slowly decline with respect to the ones with undoped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   CBL (Table 4 and Figure 7b). Even with a high-thickness of doped CBL (~60 nm), the device still remained a reasonable PCE of 11.64% (Table 4 and Figure 7b). The diminished thickness-dependent performance property in the case of doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   CBL could be ascribed to superior electrical conduction capability, as evidenced by the conductivities measurement shown in Figure 2a. This thickness-insensitive property would be beneficial for mass production techniques, such as blade coating or inkjet printing.

The shelf stability of the perovskite devices employing CTAB-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   CBL (device G) was also examined by monitoring the evolution of their PCE as a function of time during storage under ambient conditions (30 °C, ~60% relative humidity). No extra package or encapsulation layer was used for these devices. Encouragingly, over 80% of the initial PCE could be retained after being exposed to more than 20 days (Figure 8a). This might be associated with superior air stability afforded by inorganic   $  ZrO_{x}  $   film, as discussed previously (Figure 1d). On the other hand, semitransparent (ST) solar cells have created a new market for the development of novel value-added products such as electricity-generating windows that can harvest solar energy to generate electricity without obscuring the view.  $  ^{40-42}  $   Although many transparent organic- or perovskite-based solar cells have been reported previously, the development of appropriate interfacial layers to ensure the devices with both high PCE and satisfactory visible transmittance remain desirable.  $  ^{43,43}  $   Given that CTAB-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   CBL significantly improved the contact properties between the active layer and Ag electrode, its applicability in ST perovskite solar was examined. The fabrication procedure and device structure was the same as for the opaque device (device G) except that Ag thickness was lowered to 12 nm. As shown in Figure 9a, the best performing ST device delivered a high PCE of 12.21%, with   $  J_{sc}  $   of 17.20 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $   of 0.99 V, and FF of 71.75%. Note that the measured   $  J_{sc}  $   agreed very well (within 10% error) with the   $  J_{sc}  $   value integrated from IPCE spectra (SI Figure S5), which confirmed the accuracy of our reported PCE value. Importantly, this ST device also possessed a reasonable average visible transmittance (AVT; calculated between 380 and 750 nm) of 16.1%, as shown in Figure 9b. A photographic image of this device was also shown in the inset of Figure 9b, where the campus building can be visualized through this device. A comparison between our results and the characteristics of state-of-the-art ST perovskite solar cells were also depicted in Figure 9c. Encouragingly, the ST device demonstrated in this study represent the highest PCE compared with other ST devices with similar transmittances (Figure 9c). Again, we observed that the resulting ST device without the encapsulation layer possessed good ambient stability, as depicted in Figure 9d. These remarkable results can be ascribed to the fact that CTAB-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   CBL exerts multiple positive effects on the device characteristics, including superior charge selectivity, reasonable electrical conductivity, good moisture barrier property, and excellent optical transmittance (AVT ~90%; see SI Figure S9).

# CONCLUSION

A facile and effective method to improve the performance and stability of organic and hybrid perovskite solar cells is demonstrated by the incorporation of room-temperature solution-processed n-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   as CBL. With this CBL, PTB7:PC  $  _{7i}  $  BM organic solar cells deliver a PCE up to 9.3%, which is among the highest reported value for PTB7:PC  $  _{7i}  $  BM blend system. Significant improvement in the performance of perovskite solar cells is also demonstrated, and a remarkable PCE up to 15.9% with high reproducibility (average PCE = 14.9% with a standard deviation of 0.4%) is achieved, greatly outperforming the device with undoped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   (7.1%) and the state-of-the-art CBL ZnO NPs (10.5%). In addition, this approach is applicable to the development high-performance ST solar cells, yielding a record efficiency of 12.2% with a reasonable AVT of 16.1%. More significantly, the resulting devices possess good ambient stability without the need of rigorous encapsulation. These results clearly indicate that CTAB-doped   $  ZrO_{x}  $   film is a promising CBL for highly-efficient and stable organic and perovskite solar cells. This study put forward a promising strategy to the interfacial modification in low-temperature solution-processed solar cells.

Electrical analysis on corrosion Ca film: The structure of the device used for Ca test was shown in SI Figure S8. 250-nm Ca film and 100-nm Ag contacts were deposited by thermal evaporation through shadow masks on the glass substrates. All of these parts were sealed with epoxy resin (Electron-lite Corp., ELC-2500), which is cured under UV-lamp (365 nm wavelength) inside the glove-box. $$
\mathrm{P} = - \mathrm{n} \delta \rho \left(\frac {l}{w}\right) \left(\frac {M _ {r e a g e n t}}{M _ {C a}}\right) \left(\frac {d (C)}{d t}\right)
$$

where n is the molar equivalent of the degradation reaction which is assumed as n = 2 from the chemical reaction of Ca with water molecules.   $  \delta  $   is the density of calcium (1.55 g cm  $  ^{-3}  $  ),   $  \rho  $   is the is the specific resistance of Ca (9 × 10  $  ^{-8}  $   Ω·m),   $  M_{reagent}  $   and   $  M_{Ca}  $   correspond to the molar masses of the permeating reagent and Ca, respectively. l and w are the effective length and width of Ca layer, respectively. C represents the conductance. The initial resistance of Ca layer is ~8.4 × 10  $  ^{-8}  $   Ω·m, and l and w are 3 and 0.5 cm, respectively.

Characterization: The current-voltage characteristics of as-fabricated solar cells were measured under ambient conditions using a Keithley 2400 source measurement unit, and the scan rate was set at  $ 0.15\mathrm{Vs}^{-1} $  unless otherwise stated. An Oriel xenon lamp (450 W) with an AM1.5 G filter was used as the solar simulator. A Hamamatsu silicon solar cell with a KG5 color filter, which is traced to the National Renewable Energy Laboratory, was used as the reference cell. To calibrate the light intensity of the solar simulator, the power of the xenon lamp was adjusted to make the  $ J_{sc} $  of the reference cell under simulated sun light as high as it was under the calibration condition. IPCE spectra were measured using a lock-in amplifier with a current preamplifier under short-circuit conditions with illumination of monochromatic light from a 250 W quartz-halogen lamp (Osram) passing through a monochromator. The work functions of the electrodes were measured with a ULVAC-PHI PHI 5000 Versaprobe II X-ray photoelectron spectrometer employing a monochromatic focused Al-Kα X-ray source and hemispherical analyzer. The surface morphology of the films was studied using the tapping mode AFM from Digital Instrument D3100CL. The high-resolution of XPS were carried out in a XPS spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) with a monochromatic Al Kα X-ray source (1486.6 eV).
