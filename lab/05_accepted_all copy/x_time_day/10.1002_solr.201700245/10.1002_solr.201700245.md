# Metal Oxide   $  CrO_{x}  $   as a Promising Bilayer Electron Transport Material for Enhancing the Performance Stability of Planar Perovskite Solar Cells

Shizhao Zheng, Wenlang Li, Tongtong Su, Fangyan Xie, Jian Chen, Zhiyong Yang, Yi Zhang,* Siwei Liu, Matthew P. Aldred, King Young Wong,* Jiarui Xu, and Zhenguo Chi*

The stability of both the perovskite material and the interfacial layer is a key issue toward commercializing perovskite solar cells, due to the detrimental effects of water and oxygen under ambient conditions. Here, we report a solution-processed organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cell that employs a bilayer   $  PC_{61}BM/CrO_{x}  $   as the electron transport layer. Use of an additional   $  CrO_{x}  $   interfacial layer can enhance the stability of a device in air without encapsulation by more than 10 times compared to that for a device using only a   $  PC_{61}BM  $   electron transport layer. The device with a   $  CrO_{x}  $   interfacial layer can also improve the power conversion efficiency by 17.5% compared to that using   $  PC_{61}BM  $   only. The improved performance is attributed to the better matching of the energy levels of the active layer with the metal electrode and more effective electron collection when using interfacial   $  CrO_{x}  $   as the bilayer electronic transport layer. It was also found that use of   $  CrO_{x}  $   as a passivating layer could resist the diffusion of water and oxygen into the active layer and prevent the reaction of the metal electrode with the perovskite layer.

[6,6]-Phenyl   $  C_{61}  $   butyric acid methyl ester (  $  PC_{61}BM  $  ) is an efficient and commonly used ETL material in inverted perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{[28-29]}  $   However,   $  PC_{61}BM  $   is not stable in air, so that the perovskite solar cells constructed from   $  PC_{61}BM  $   generally have poor device stability. It is well known that metal oxides have much higher charge-carrier mobilities and superior stabilities compared to the organic materials. Therefore, using   $  TiO_{2}  $  ,  $  ^{[2]}  $     $  ZnO  $  ,  $  ^{[17,30]}  $     $  SnO_{2}  $  ,  $  ^{[20]}  $   or   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}/Cr^{[31-33]}  $   as the ETL material could improve the device performance and stability of perovskite solar cells. Herein, we report that good device stability can be achieved for inverted perovskite solar cells when employing   $  PC_{61}BM  $   as the ETL. By using a bilayer structure consisting of a combination of   $  PC_{61}BM  $   and   $  CrO_{x}  $ , the inverted perovskite solar cell shows enhanced device performance and good stability. The passivation mechanism is also discussed.

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted much research interest due to their high power conversion efficiency (PCE), ease of fabrication by solution processing and low materials cost.  $  ^{[1-5]}  $   The PCE of organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells has exceeded 20% in recent years.  $  ^{[6-8]}  $   However, the stability issue remains a key challenge for their commercial applications because the organic-inorganic halide perovskites are unstable in the presence of moisture and oxygen and degrade gradually with time in air.  $  ^{[9-12]}  $   Minimizing the detrimental effects of the moisture and oxygen by interface engineering at the perovskite surface is an important strategy to improve the device performance and stability.  $  ^{[13-27]}  $

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were performed to investigate the chemical composition information of the deposited   $  CrO_{x}  $   thin film. The XPS spectra are shown in Figure 3. In Figure 3a, the peak at 577.2 eV can be assigned to Cr   $  2p_{3/2}  $   of Cr(III) oxide (  $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  ), while the peak at 586.9 eV corresponds to Cr   $  2p_{1/2}  $   of   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  .  $  ^{[36-37]}  $   The peak of metallic chromium could not be found. In Figure 3b, the O 1 s spectrum is composed of two peaks. The peak at a binding energy of 530.7 eV corresponds to O in   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  ,  $  ^{[36-37]}  $   and the other peak at a binding energy of 532.2 eV is attributed to the defects in   $  CrO_{x}  $   layer or organic contaminations. The XPS result shows that the thin film deposited by thermal evaporation of Cr metal mainly consists of   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $  . It is well known that Cr metal is easily oxidized during the thermal evaporation process.   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   is a very stable semiconductor with a conduction band at 4.0 eV,  $  ^{[38-39]}  $   which is between the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of   $  PC_{61}BM  $   (3.95 eV) and the work function of Ag (4.3 eV).

Therefore, a   $  Cr_{2}O_{3}  $   layer is energetically favorable to transport electrons from   $  PC_{61}BM  $   to the cathode, as shown in Figure 1b. Its low-lying highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level could also efficiently block the passage of holes from the photoactive layer. In addition, the chromium oxide layer serves as an optical spacer to redistribute light intensity in the perovskite layer and increase the short circuit current density as previously reported for   $  TiO_{x}  $  .  $  ^{[34]}  $

Ultroviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) was used to measure the energy level change due to the addition of the  $ \mathrm{CrO_x} $  thin film. Figure S5, Supporting Information shows the low energy cut-off region of the UPS spectra from both the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  and  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} / \mathrm{CrO_x} $  surfaces. The work function of the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  surface was measured to be  $ 4.83\mathrm{eV} $ , which is significantly larger compared to the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} / \mathrm{CrO_x} $  surface ( $ 3.76\mathrm{eV} $ ). The work function becomes very close to the LUMO everyg level ( $ 3.75\mathrm{eV} $ ) of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  when  $ \mathrm{CrO_x} $  is added on top of  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $ . It is evident that adding a  $ \mathrm{CrO_x} $  layer on top of the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  layer could balance the energy level alignment between the perovskite layer and the metal electrode, and consequently benefit the electron transportation from the perovskite layer to the metal electrode. A larger built-in potential corresponds to an enhanced driving force for the separation of photogenerated carriers. It also explains the slight improvement of the  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  and enhanced short circuit photocurrent density resulting from inserting  $ \mathrm{CrO_x} $  interlayer on top of the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  layer.

The morphology and phase images from atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements for samples with and without a  $ \mathrm{CrO_x} $  interlayer are shown in Figure 4. The root mean square roughness for a film with  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  only is  $ 10.3\mathrm{nm} $  (Figure 4a and b). The root mean square roughness for the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} / \mathrm{CrO}_x $  bilayer film, as shown in Figure 4c, was  $ 7.61\mathrm{nm} $ . The data show that the  $ \mathrm{CrO_x} $  interfacial layer effectively smoothens the surface of the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  interface and with a better coverage. The  $ \mathrm{CrO_x} $  layer, with an averaged particle size of around  $ 30\mathrm{nm} $ , fully covers the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  surface. This fully covered  $ \mathrm{CrO_x} $  layer can efficiently isolate the Ag metal anode from diffusion into the perovskite photoactive layer.

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) can be used to analyse the recombination losses and charge transport

properties between different interfaces.  $  ^{[40]}  $   We compare the interfacial recombination resistance upon adding CrOx interlayer between the   $  PC_{61}BM  $   and silver electrode. In the EIS measurements, device impedance as a function of frequency was swept from 100 Hz to 1 MHz under dark conditions. The Nyquist plot is shown in Figure 5. The low-frequency regime correlates with slow ionic relaxation and diffusion, and the high-frequency regime originates from the charge transport and recombination kinetics.  $  ^{[41-42]}  $   The high frequency impedance is correlated with the recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ). The fitting results show that the device with   $  PC_{61}BM/CrO_{x}  $   bilayer film exhibits a much higher recombination resistance (1648 Ω) compared to the device with only   $  PC_{61}BM  $   layer (480 Ω). The   $  PC_{61}BM/CrO_{x}  $   bilayer interface leads to reduced recombination losses. These measurements demonstrate that the presence of a   $  CrO_{x}  $   interlayer between the electron transport layer and metal electrode in a planar heterojunction perovskite solar cell can affect the interfacial recombination losses and device performance.

We measured the stability of perovskite solar cell devices that employed either   $  PC_{61}BM  $   only or a bilayer   $  PC_{61}BM/CrO_{x}  $   as the FTL. In all of the measurements, the devices without encapsulation were tested in an ambient environment at 25 °C and with a 30–50% relative humidity. A dramatic decrease in device efficiency was observed for the device without a   $  CrO_{x}  $   interlayer. We also measured the EIS after aging for 21 days by storing in an ambient environment at 25 °C and with a 30–50% relative humidity. The corresponding Nyquist plot is shown in Figure 5. One possible reason for the degradation of the device using only a   $  PC_{61}BM  $   interlayer is that the   $  PC_{61}BM  $   layer itself can absorb oxygen and water from ambient air.  $  ^{[47]}  $   It was found that   $  PC_{61}BM  $   can interact with water and the degraded   $  PC_{61}BM  $   film develops a large contact resistance with the adjacent metal layer.  $  ^{[45]}  $   Another possible reason is that the coverage of the perovskite film by the   $  PC_{61}BM  $   may be incomplete, and this subsequently leads to insufficient protection of the perovskite layer such that a rapid chemical reaction between the perovskite layer and the Ag electrode occurs under ambient conditions. When the thermally deposited Ag metal diffuses into the perovskite layer, it can cause a chemical decomposition of the perovskite photoactive layer and also damages the metal electrode.  $  ^{[48]}  $   By depositing a   $  CrO_{x}  $   thin film onto the   $  PC_{61}BM  $   layer to form the bilayer for electron transport, the device stability is shown to be significantly enhanced. The metal oxide   $  CrO_{x}  $   exhibits good stability in ambient air. The   $  CrO_{x}  $   thin film is not only able to aid the device resist the attack by oxygen and water, but can also passivate the perovskite photoactive layer from the Ag metal electrode.

In summary, we have demonstrated efficient perovskite solar cells using an electron transport layer that is composed of a bilayer of  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  and  $ \mathrm{CrO_x} $ . It was found that the devices containing  $ \mathrm{CrO_x} $  metal oxide not only exhibit enhanced PCE, but also shows better stability in ambient conditions compared to the

devices without the   $  CrO_{x}  $   metal oxide layer. The presence of the   $  CrO_{x}  $   metal oxide provides an ideal energy-band matching and favorable electron collection properties in combination with   $  PC_{61}BM  $  . More importantly, the use of   $  CrO_{x}  $   as a passivating layer can protect the perovskite layer from the attack of water and oxygen and prevent the metal anode from diffusion into the perovskite photoactive layer. Therefore,   $  CrO_{x}  $   metal oxide is a promising material for efficient and stable perovskite solar cells in commercial applications.

# Experimental Section

Materials:   $  PbCl_{2}  $   was purchased from Alfa Aesar and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (MAI) was purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. To prepare the perovskite precursor solution, powders of MAI and   $  PbCl_{2}  $   were mixed and dissolved in anhydrous dimethylformamide (DMF) with concentrations of 2.64 M for MAI and 0.88 M for   $  PbCl_{2}  $   to give a molar ratio of 3:1. The solution was stirred at 40 °C overnight in a glovebox, and was filtered with a 0.45 μm polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) filter before the device fabrication.

Device Fabrication: The patterned FTO glass substrates with a sheet resistance of 7 Ω/sq were first cleaned with acetone and detergent, and then further treated with oxygen plasma for 10 min to remove the organic impurities. Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulphonate) (PEDOT:PSS) (Clevios P VP Al 4083) was spin-coated on the FTO substrate at 2000 rpm for 60 s and then annealed at 150 °C for 10 min. The substrates were transferred into a glovebox for thin-film perovskite layer coating. The PbCl₂/CH₃NH₃I (1:3 molar ratio) precursor solution was spin-coated on top of the PEDOT:PSS layer at 1500 rpm for 40 s and then annealed at 90 °C for 2 h. Subsequently, the PC₆₁BM layer was deposited by spin coating a PC₆₁BM solution with a concentration of 20 mg mL⁻¹ in chlorobenzene at 1000 rpm for 40 s. The thin CrOₓ layer with an approximate thickness of 5 nm was deposited by thermal evaporation of chromium metal under a low vacuum environment. With the presence of oxygen inside the evaporation chamber at low vacuum, the chromium metal is easily oxidized to form CrOₓ upon evaporation at high temperature. Finally, a film of Ag with a thickness of 100 nm was thermally evaporated on top of the device. The active area of the device was 0.1 cm² and for all the devices no external encapsulation was applied after the device fabrication.

Characterization: The photocurrent density–voltage (J–V) measurements of the devices were recorded by a Keithley 2400 source-measure unit under ambient conditions at room temperature. The illumination intensity was calibrated to AM 1.5G, 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  using a ABET Sun 3000 solar simulator. The reference used was a 15 150 calibrated Si reference cell manufactured by ABET Technologies, Inc. The active area of the device was 10 mm  $  ^{2}  $ . External quantum efficiency (EQE) was measured by a PV Measurements QEXL solar cell quantum efficiency measurement system. The device test was under ambient conditions at 25 °C and 30–50% relative humidity. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was carried out using a Thermo Fisher Scientific ESCALAB 250 system. A monochromatic Al K  $  _{a}  $  (1486.6 eV) X-ray beam was used as the excitation source. The work functions of the surfaces were determined by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurements using a Thermo Fisher Scientific ESCALAB 250 system with He(I) excitation (hv=21.22 eV) under 5 × 10  $  ^{-8}  $  mbar. Atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements of the surface morphology was conducted on a Bruker Multimode 8 SPM system. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was performed by BioLogic Science Instruments VMP-300 multipotentiostat.
