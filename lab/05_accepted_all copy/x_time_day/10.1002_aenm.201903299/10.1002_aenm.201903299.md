# MACl-Assisted Ge Doping of Pb-Hybrid Perovskite: A Universal Route to Stabilize High Performance Perovskite Solar Cells

Gyu Min Kim,  $  ^{*}  $  Ayumi Ishii, Senol Öz, and Tsutomu Miyasaka  $  ^{*}  $

Interfacial engineering, grain boundary, and surface passivation in organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (HyPSCs) are effective in achieving high performance and enhanced durability. Organic additives and inorganic doping are generally used to chemically modify the surface contacting charge transport layers, and/or grain boundaries so as to reduce the defect density. Here, a simple but tricky one-step method to dope organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite with Ge for the first time is reported. Unlike Ge doping to all-inorganic perovskites, application of  $ \mathrm{Gel}_2 $  in organic-inorganic perovskite precursors is challenging due to the extremely poor solubility of  $ \mathrm{Gel}_2 $  in hybrid perovskite ink, leading to failure in the formation of uniform films. However, it is found that addition of methylammonium chloride (MACl) into the precursor remarkably increases the solubility of  $ \mathrm{Gel}_2 $ . This MACl-assisted Ge doping of hybrid perovskites produces high-quality crystalline film with its surface passivated with nonvolatile  $ \mathrm{Gel}_2 $  ( $ \mathrm{GeO}_2 $ ) and the volatile MACl additive also improves the uniformity of  $ \mathrm{GeO}_2 $  distribution in the perovskite films. The resulting Ge-doped mixed cation and mixed halide perovskite films with composition  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.83}\mathrm{MA}_{0.17}\mathrm{Ge}_{0.03}\mathrm{Pb}_{0.97}(\mathrm{l}_{0.9}\mathrm{Br}_{0.1})_3 $  show superior photoluminescence lifetime, power conversion efficiency above  $ 22\% $ , and greater stability toward illumination and humidity, outperforming photovoltaic properties of HyPSCs prepared without the Ge doping.

# 2. Results and Discussions

Mixed cation and halide perovskites comprising   $  FA_{0.83}MA_{0.17}Ge_{x}Pb_{1-x}(I_{0.9}Br_{0.1})_{3}  $   were prepared by doping Ge to Pb-perovskite in a one-step solution process. Precursor solutions were prepared by containing   $  GeI_{2}  $   as a Ge source in a mixture of FAI, MAI,   $  PbBr_{2}  $  , and   $  PbI_{2}  $   dissolved in a DMF/DMSO mixed solvent. Here, solubility of   $  GeI_{2}  $   is the key to the preparation of homogeneous clear solution.

Perovskite films were coated on   $  SnO_{2}  $  -deposited ITO glass substrates. Halide anion (I) was also mixed with Br to optimize the photovoltaic performance as described hereinafter.

The change of the preferential oriented growth and morphologies caused by   $  GeI_{2}  $   affected photoluminescence (PL) lifetimes (Figure S7, Supporting Information). The average lifetimes (  $  \tau  $  ) were calculated by fitting the curves using biexponential decay function composed of   $  A_{1}  $  ,   $  A_{2}  $  ,   $  \tau_{1}  $  , and   $  \tau_{2}  $   (Table S3, Supporting Information). We observed that the first component (  $  A_{1}  $  ), commonly known as fast decay related to nonradiative recombination, becomes less dominant by increasing the concentration of   $  GeI_{2}  $   up to 5%. As confirmed from the increase in the value of   $  A_{2}/A_{1}  $  , nonradiative recombination near grain boundaries/surface was suppressed by   $  GeI_{2}  $  , testifying the passivation effects of the additive. However, the lifetime for second decay (  $  \tau_{2}  $  ) starts to decrease when the concentration of   $  GeI_{2}  $   exceeds 5%. We attributed this to nonuniform small perovskite grains caused by large amounts of   $  GeI_{2}  $   (Figure 2) because the second components are closely related to the bulk perovskite condition. Thus, the highest   $  \tau  $   (average) was obtained for the perovskites

with 3% of   $  GeI_{2}  $   with significant improvements for both first and second components,   $  \tau  $   recorded as 18 846 ns.

We also fabricated a quadruple cation-based (K, Cs, MA, FA) HyPSC. The original performances without   $  GeI_{2}  $   already showed high efficiency reaching 22.06% of PCE as the quadruple compositions have been known to be highly efficient with less hysteresis (Figure 6d). Further improvement of performances was recorded using   $  GeI_{2}  $  . The perovskites having composition of   $  K_{0.03}Cs_{0.05}(FA_{0.87}MA_{0.13})_{0.92}(Ge_{0.03}Pb_{0.97})(I_{0.9}Br_{0.1})_{3}  $  , yielded the PCE of 22.7% by reverse scan. High PCE is mainly due to the increase in   $  V_{oc}  $   and less hysteresis, recording 1.18 V, which is a high value despite small amounts of Br (10%) compared to Br amounts in general studies (17%). This implies that the application of “  $  GeI_{2}  $   and MACl” can be extended to other organic-based perovskite compositions for further improvements of PCE and stability.

# 3. Conclusion

We have elucidated the significant effect of Ge cation doped to Pb halide perovskites in improving the quality of perovskite grains and the photovoltaic performance through defect passivation. To elicit this effect for MA and FA-based high PCE mixed cation perovskite absorbers, it was essential to overcome the insolubility issue of   $  GeI_{2}  $   in organic cation (  $  FA^{+}  $  ,   $  MA^{+}  $  ) precursors at first step, which was realized in this study by the simultaneous use of   $  GeI_{2}  $   and MACl for precursor preparation. The adequate amounts of   $  GeI_{2}  $   and MACl in the precursors used as nonvolatile and volatile additives produced uniform perovskite films with   $  GeI_{2}  $   passivation layer. The resulting HyPSCs consisting of Ge–Pb (1–3% Ge) demonstrated high PCEs over 22% with stable steady power output without any preconditions such as encapsulation, temperature adjustment, and UV-filter. To the best of our knowledge, the Ge–Pb system introduced here is the first application in organic–inorganic HyPSCs with the help of MACl. The use of   $  GeI_{2}  $   and MACl is believed to be universal to organic-based perovskites by the improvements of perovskite/HTL interface leading to the increase in   $  V_{oc}  $  . This opens up space for further improvements of organic-based perovskites with variable compositions.
