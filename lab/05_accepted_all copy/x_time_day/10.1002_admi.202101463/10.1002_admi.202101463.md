# Efficient and Stable Carbon-Based CsPbIBr  $  _{2}  $  Perovskite Solar Cells by 4-Aminomethyltetrahydropyran Acetate Modification

Huayan Zhang, Zhongliang Yan, Yuan Xu, Xin Wang, Jihuai Wu, and Zhang Lan*

Inorganic cesium lead halide perovskite solar cells have attracted widespread attention owing to their excellent stability relative to organic-inorganic solar cells. However, all-inorganic perovskite solar cells without hole transport layers and using carbon layers as electrodes have serious energy level mismatch problems. To overcome this problem, here, the  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $  surface is treated with 4-aminomethyltetrahydropyran acetate to form a gradient energy band on the  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $  perovskite/carbon interface. As a result, the hole extraction efficiency is successfully improved, and the morphology and crystallization of the perovskite layer are also improved. Moreover, the nonradiative recombination inside the perovskite and the charge recombination at the interface are effectively inhibited. Therefore, the power conversion efficiency of  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $  solar cell is enhanced to  $ 10.12\% $ , and the high photovoltage of  $ 1.32\mathrm{V} $  is obtained under one solar illumination, which are both higher than the pristine one ( $ 7.79\% $ ,  $ 1.23\mathrm{V} $ , respectively).

# 1. Introduction

Since the Miyasaka team first used perovskite materials as sensitizers in solar cells, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has grown from 3.8% to more than 25%.  $  ^{[1-6]}  $   Organic–inorganic hybrid PSCs have become a revolutionary light-absorbing semiconductor due to their excellent photoelectric properties.  $  ^{[7]}  $   However, the instability of organic–inorganic hybrid PSCs under environmental stress (such as moisture, oxygen, light, and heat) hinders the development of devices, and the use of precious metal and expensive organic hole transport materials electrodes further hinders the commercial application of PSCs.  $  ^{[8]}  $   The use of inorganic cation cesium (Cs  $  ^{+}  $  ) to replace organic components, carbon

CsPbI $ _3 $  has the narrowest bandgap, and the PCE of devices has exceeded 19%, but there is a problem of phase instability. $ ^{[22-23]} $  The photoactive  $ \alpha $  phase (bandgap 1.73 eV) is easily converted to nonperovskite  $ \delta $  phase (bandgap 2.82 eV), causing serious PCE loss. $ ^{[24]} $  While CsPbBr $ _3 $  has the highest environmental stability, but its wide bandgap of 2.3 eV limits efficiency. Compared with CsPbI $ _2 $ Br, CsPbIBr $ _2 $  not only has a suitable bandgap (2.05 eV), but also has higher environmental stability. In this work, a 4-aminomethyltetrahydropyran acetate (4-AMTHP-Ac) is used to treat the  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $  surface. The 4-AMTHP-Ac modification passivates the surface defects of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $  film and forms a beneficial energy level arrangement, which prevents electron transfer on the  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $  perovskite/carbon interface and facilitates hole extraction, thereby significantly inhibits the charge recombination in  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $  PSCs. # 2. Results and Discussion

4-AMTHP-Ac is obtained by the reaction of 4-AMTHP and acetic acid. According to 1H nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra (Figure S1, Supporting Information), the 4-AMTHP-Ac spectrum shows a new single peak at  $ \delta $  6.76, which is derived from the ammonium ion on the ammonium salt that reacts with the carboxyl group of acetate, indicating that acetic acid reacts with 4-AMTHP to form a salt.

Compared with the pristine CsPbIBr₂, the XRD patterns of the 4-AMTHP-Ac modified CsP-bIBr₂ are stronger than those of the pristine one, indicating that the 4-AMTHP-Ac modified CsPbIBr₂ has higher crystallinity and fewer crystal defects. Figure 1b,c shows the UV-visible absorption spectra and the related Tauc plots of the samples. And Figure 7c demonstrates the excellent thermal stability of the device, which can provide stable PCE performance within 100 h in air at 80 °C and 40% RH.

# 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, we have demonstrated that the 4-AMTHP-Ac modification is an effective strategy for preparing high efficiency and good stable carbon-based all-inorganic  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $  PSCs. The 4-AMTHP-Ac modified  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $  film shows favorable interface energy level arrangement, improved charge transport, inhibited charge recombination, and enhanced stability, which finally contribute to obviously enhanced photovoltaic performance and good long-term stability. The champion 4-AMTHP-Ac modified  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $  PSC can achieve a higher PCE of  $ 10.12\% $  with a high  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  of  $ 1.32\mathrm{V} $ , which are both higher than the pristine one  $ (7.79\%, 1.23\mathrm{V}) $ .
