# Influence of polymer additives on the efficiency and stability of ambient-air solution-processed planar perovskite solar cells

Deng Wang $  ^{a}  $ , Lei Zhang $  ^{a}  $ , Kaiming Deng $  ^{a}  $ , Weina Zhang $  ^{a}  $ , Jing Song $  ^{a}  $ , Jihuai Wu $  ^{a}  $ , Zhang Lan $  ^{a,*}  $

# Abstract:

It is challenging to fabricate efficient and stable perovskite solar cells (PSCs) in ambient air conditions without special controlled humidity. Here, we report a facile polymer additive route to overcome the problem. We find that the polyvinyl butyral (PVB) and polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) additives can both obviously enhance the quality of the perovskite films compared with the pristine one. Especially, the perovskite film with PVB additive shows clearer improved quality because of the formation of larger crystal grains which can go from bottom to top and smoother surface with the lower root-mean-square roughness of  $ 23.4\mathrm{nm} $  than that of the one with PVA additive (45.6 nm). The improved quality of the perovskite films with PVB additive contributes to better photovoltaic performance and long-term stability of the devices. The PSCs with PVB additive can achieve a champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 19.10\% $  and a small hysteresis index of 0.039, which are both better than those of the pristine ones ( $ 15.14\% $  and 0.148, respectively). Furthermore, the PSC with PVB additive can maintain a relatively high PCE of  $ 12.43\% $  after being stored in a humid ambient air condition without any encapsulation, whereas the pristine one drops to a very low PCE of  $ 5.18\% $ . This work demonstrates the valuable PVB additive route for developing efficient and stable PSCs in ambient air conditions.

# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted tremendous

attention due to their rapidly increased power conversion efficiency (PCE) from original 3.8 % to a certified 22.1 % in a few years' time and the possibility of adopting low-energy-consuming solution-processed fabricating methodology [1-4]. Unfortunately, the organic components in the  $ \mathrm{ABX}_3 $  structure of organic-inorganic perovskite materials, where A = [Cesium Cs $ ^+ $ ; Rubidium, Rb $ ^+ $ ; methylammonium (MA)  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3^+ $ ; formamidinium (FA)  $ \mathrm{CH}_3(\mathrm{NH}_2)_2^+ $ ; B = ( $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $ ;  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $ ); and X = (Cl $ ^- $ ; Br $ ^- $ ; I $ ^- $ ), are very sensitive to moisture due to their hygroscopic nature [5-7], so it is challenging to fabricate efficient and stable PSCs in ambient air conditions without special controlled humidity. With additive strategies the problem may be overcome [8-10]. For instances, we recently reported a novel multi-cycle short-time dipping reaction (STDR) method to prepare high quality perovskite films in ambient air conditions with the aid of n-butyl amine additive. The best-performance PSC achieves an average PCE of 17.56 % with slight hysterisis, good reproducibility and moderate stability [11]. Chang, et al. firstly reported the utilization of polymer additive [poly(ethylene glycol, PEG)] to tune perovskite morphology, which finally achieved a 25 % increase in PCE of the PSCs [12]. And Zhao, et al. further used PEG to fabricate uniform perovskite films based on a novel polymer-scaffold architecture and the PSCs exhibited PCE about 16 % with small variation. Especially, these unencapsulated PSCs retained high output of up to 300 h in highly humid environment [70 % relative humidity (RH)] and showed strong humidity resistant and self-healing behavior [9]. # Results and Discussion

It is reported that polymer additives have significant influences on the morphologies of perovskite films   $  [12, 16, 17]  $  , for instance, Bi et al. found that poly (methyl methacrylate) could play as a template to control nucleation and crystal growth of perovskite and form shiny smooth perovskite films with excellent electronic quality   $  [18]  $  . Therefore, the scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of the perovskite films with PVB and PVA additives (5 mgmL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) were measured and presented in Figure 1. The top views from Figure 1a, b and c show that the PVB and PVA additives can both induce bigger sizes of crystal grains in the perovskite films compared with that of the pristine sample; and the utilization of PVB additive can form the largest crystal grains and the smoothest surface of the perovskite film among the three samples. Correspondingly, the cross-sectional SEM images from Figure 1 d, e and f show that the perovskite grains for the sample with PVB additive are more monolithic, namely, they tend to go from bottom to top, than those of the sample with PVA additive. Whereas, the perovskite grains of the pristine sample tend to stack on top of each other.

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) images from Figure 2 further confirm the morphology features of the three samples. The parameter of root-mean-square roughness (RMS) can well reflect the surface smoothness of the perovskite films. It is found that the RMS of the sample with PVB additive is  $ 23.4\mathrm{nm} $ , which is the lowest value among the three samples as those of the one with PVA additive is  $ 45.6\mathrm{nm} $  and the pristine one is  $ 54.7\mathrm{nm} $ . The formation of monolithic crystal grains that can go from bottom to top and pinhole-free smooth perovskite films is significantly important for achieving high photovoltaic performance of the PSCs, especially the planar PSCs [19], so the enhanced photovoltaic performance of the PSCs with PVB additive can be expected.

Furthermore, the influence of the PVB additive on the long-time stability of the PSCs is studied through monitoring PCEs of the PSCs with/without PVB additive stored in a humid ambient air condition (RH about 60~80 %) without any encapsulation. As shown in Figure 9c, the two PSCs both show a little increased PCE after 12 h (0.5 day) interval time of the first test of photovoltaic performance, mainly owing to the improved interfacial contacting properties and the sufficient oxidation of spiro-OMeTAD during this time. Therefore, it is concluded that the PVB additive can obviously enhance the long-term stability of the PSC in a humid atmosphere.

# Device fabrication

The  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETLs were prepared on the cleared and patterned FTOs by spin-coating toluene solution of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanoparticles (20 mgmL $ ^{-1} $ ) at a rate of 4000 rpm for 30 s. Then, the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETLs were heated at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 30 min and  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 30 min. The STDR was used to fabricate perovskite layers on the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETLs

according to our recently reported method [11]. Firstly, 60 μL of the mixture of 1.3 g PbI $ _2 $ , 0.9 mL dimethyl sulfoxide, 0.45 mL n-butyl amine and 2.1 mL N,N-dimethylformamide was spin-coated on the TiO $ _2 $  ETLs at 3500 rpm for 30 s and then heated at 70 °C for 10 min. Secondly, after being cooled down to room temperature, the lead iodide layers were dipped into a n-butanol solution of mixed FAI, MABr and polymer additive (148 mg FAI, 19.33 mg MABr, 20 mL n-butanol and different amounts of PVB, PVA or without) for 30 s and then washed with 0.5 mL n-butanol by spinning at 4000 rpm for 10 s. The processes were done for 5 cycles to completely convert lead iodide into perovskite. Finally, the perovskite layers were heated at 150 °C for 30 min. All of the above mentioned processes were done in indoor lab ambient air conditions with RH about 60~80 %. # Characterization

The SEM and AFM measurements were conducted using a SU8000 SEM and a Bruker Multimode 8, respectively. The PL spectra were acquired using a fluorescence spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific Lumina). The TRPL spectra were acquired using an Omin-  $ \lambda $  Monochromator/Spectrograph with the time-correlated single-photon counting method (Zolix). The EIS measurements were conducted on a Zennium electrochemical workstation (IM6) in dark conditions.

The J-V characteristic curves of PSCs were recorded with a computer-controlled Keithley 2400 source meter under simulated AM 1.5 G solar illumination at  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $  with #94043A solar simulator (PVIV-94043A, Newport, USA) in air. The voltage step and delay time were  $ 20\mathrm{mV} $  and  $ 10\mathrm{ms} $ , respectively. The forward scan started from  $ -0.1\mathrm{V} $  to  $ 1.2\mathrm{V} $ , while reverse scan from  $ 1.2\mathrm{V} $  to  $ -0.1\mathrm{V} $ . The stabilized current density and power output were recorded close to the maximum power point, which was extracted from the J-V characteristic curves. The IPCE curves were measured as a function of wavelength from 300 to  $ 850\mathrm{nm} $  using the Newport IPCE system (Newport, USA). The PSCs with the active area of  $ 0.12\mathrm{cm}^2 $  (calibrated with a black metal mask with the area of  $ 0.3\times 0.4\mathrm{cm}^2 $ ) and without any encapsulation were prepared for measurements. One batch of 30 PSCs for each changed condition was prepared and measured.

We report a facile polymer additive route to prepare potentially available efficient and stable planar PSCs. The polyvinyl butyral (PVB) and polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) additives can obviously enhance the quality of perovskite films. Especially, the perovskite film with PVB additive shows clearer improved quality because of the formation of larger crystal grains which can go from bottom to top and smoother surface than that of the one with PVA additive. Furthermore, the device with PVB additive shows superior photovoltaic performance, reduced hysteresis and greatly improved air stability.
