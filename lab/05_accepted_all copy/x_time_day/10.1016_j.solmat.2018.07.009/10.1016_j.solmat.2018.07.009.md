# 1. Introduction

The organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cell (PSC) has attracted tremendous interest because of its combined advantages of high conversion efficiency and low processing cost. The solar-to-electrical conversion efficiency of the PSC has been rapidly improved from 3.8% to 22.7% by improving the quality of the interfaces, carrier transport layers, and perovskite films [1–6]. In spite of this rapid development, organic-inorganic hybrid PSC still suffer from poor stability due to the inferior stability of the hybrid perovskite materials to air (oxygen and moisture), heat, and ultraviolet light [6–9], which hinders their practical application. One effective way to enhance the stability of PSC is to use inorganic materials to replace labile organic components. Recently, all-inorganic halide perovskites of the type   $  CsMX_{3}  $   (M = Pb or Sn; X = I, Br, Cl or mixed halides) have been drawing great attention for use in PSC to improve their stability. The all-inorganic PSC   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   [10–13],   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   [14–17],   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   [18,19],   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   [20,21],   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   [22–28],   $  CsPb_{0.9}Sn_{0.1}IBr_{2}  $   [29], and   $  Cs_{0.925}K_{0.075}PbI_{2}Br  $   [30] have been realized, and better device performance has been achieved.

# ABSTRACT

Organic–inorganic hybrid halide perovskites materials have inspired enormous interest in the photovoltaic research community; however, they are currently plagued by insufficient environmental stability. To overcome this problem, all-inorganic halide perovskites have been developed and exhibit significantly improved stability. Here, vacuum thermal evaporation deposition of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite films is developed by dual-source coevaporation of CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   precursors. The evaporation rate ratio of CsBr to   $  PbBr_{2}  $   determines the stoichiometry of the deposited   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films, and the substrate temperature and post annealing temperature affect the crystallinity of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films. Through systematic optimization of the deposition parameters, high-quality   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films with good crystallinity and uniformity are obtained. Planar   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells with high efficiency of 6.95% in small size (0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) and 5.37% in large size (1 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) are fabricated. Moreover, the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   solar cells show good long-term stability under ambient conditions without encapsulation.

Additionally, Kulbak's study shows that all-inorganic versions of the lead bromide perovskite materials perform as well as the organic one [23]. Among these inorganic PSCs, the  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  PSC has the best stability.

The use of a particular method for preparing inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   or   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  -based perovskite films is important for obtaining good film quality, high device performance. Previously, almost all the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films/solar cells were fabricated by a solution process [22–28]. Although the solution process does not require expensive vacuum equipment, it is more challenging to obtain consistently uniform, large-area, high-quality   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films. Vacuum thermal evaporation is a mature technique used in the coating industry that can easily deposit multiple layers of thin films over large areas, and the deposited films have good uniformity and flatness. Compared with solution processes, there are relatively few studies on the fabrication of perovskite solar cells using vacuum thermal evaporation [3,31–34]. Liu et al. first used dual-source vacuum thermal evaporation to fabricate a high-efficiency (15%)   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   PSC [3]. Their studies show that the vapor-deposited films are extremely uniform, while the solution-processed

# 3. Results and discussion

Fig. 1a shows an illustration of the vacuum thermal coevaporation deposition setup.   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films were deposited by thermal coevaporation of CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   precursors in vacuum with subsequent thermal annealing. The as-deposited and annealed films appear yellow and demonstrate large-scale uniformity (Fig. S1, Supporting information). The stoichiometry and crystallinity of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films are crucial in determining its photovoltaic properties. The substrate temperature and post-annealing temperature have large effects on the crystallinity of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films, while the relative evaporation rate ratio of CsBr to   $  PbBr_{2}  $   would alter the stoichiometry of the resultant product. First, we studied the effect of substrate temperature (25–300 °C) on the crystallinity of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films as determined through X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements, as shown in Fig. 1b. The characteristic peaks of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   located at   $  2\theta = 16.26  $  , 21.6, 26.34, 30.37, 37.74, and 44.15 deg

correspond to the (100), (110), (111), (002), (211), and (220) crystal planes, respectively (PDF#18-0364). The two main diffraction peaks, (110) and (211), have their highest intensities when the substrate temperature is 300 °C, indicating that the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film deposited at this temperature has the best crystallinity. It should be noted that when the substrate temperature exceeds 300 °C (e.g. 350 °C) the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films cannot be deposited on the substrate because when the temperature is too high, the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   evaporates from the substrate.

Fig. 1c shows the effect of the evaporation rate ratio of CsBr to   $  PbBr_{2}  $   on the crystallinity of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films. When the evaporation rate ratio is 0.7:1, the (110) diffraction peak is stronger than for the other ratios, meaning the deposited   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film has the best crystallinity and an optimum stoichiometry. This is further demonstrated by the energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) measurement (Fig. S2 and Table S1). When the evaporation rate ratio is 0.7:1, the atomic percentages of Cs, Pb, and Br in the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film are 17%, 16%, and 67%, respectively. The corresponding ratio of the number of Cs, Pb, and Br atoms is 1.1:1:4.2 (there is a considerable error in the EDX measurements), which is the nearest to 1:1:3 of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   among all the evaporation rate ratios. This indicates that an evaporation rate ratio of 0.7:1 for CsBr to   $  PbBr_{2}  $   is suitable for obtaining relatively correct stoichiometry of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film. We think the cause for needing a higher evaporation rate of   $  PbBr_{2}  $   is that some   $  PbBr_{2}  $   arrived on the substrate evaporated again from the substrate (due to a high substrate temperature of 300°C).

After annealing, the preferential crystal orientation changed to (100) and (002), reflecting a more stable structure. Additionally, as the annealing temperature increases the XRD peaks from (100) and (002) strengthen, demonstrating improvement of the crystallinity of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films.

The influence of annealing on the morphology of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films was investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and atomic force microscopy (AFM). Fig. 2a-f shows the surface morphologies of the as-deposited   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films (a and d) as well as those annealed at 400 °C (b and e) or 500 °C (c and f). The   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films are composed of dense   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   grains and there is full surface coverage. All the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films have good continuity and homogeneity. The as-deposited   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film has average grain size of ~450 nm, and thermal annealing leads to growth of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   grains up to ~560 nm at 400 °C and up to ~640 nm at 500 °C. Fig. 2g–i show cross-section SEM images of the as-deposited, 400, and 500 °C-annealed   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films, respectively. For the as-deposited   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film, the grain size is smaller and there are some grains stacked on others along the film growth direction. In this case, the longitudinal grain boundaries will cause substantial carrier recombination and lead to the deterioration of solar cell performance. For the annealed   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films, the grains grew larger until they were large enough to span the entire film thickness. Further, the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film annealed at 500 °C has the largest grains, and they are intimately connected, which produces fused grain boundaries. This means that photocarriers are able to travel to opposite electrodes in a single crystal domain without passing through grain boundaries, which is believed to be desired for photovoltaic devices. The 3D AFM images shown in Fig. 2j-l also demonstrate a significant decrease in the film roughness after annealing (the root mean square roughnesses (Rqs) of the as-deposited, 400, and 500 °C-annealed   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films are 60.6, 56.6, and 43.7 nm, respectively). The   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film annealed at 500 °C has the best flatness. A combination of the SEM and AFM data suggests an increase in the crystallinity of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films upon annealing, which is in accordance with our XRS measurements. When the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film was annealed at 550 °C, the grains became much larger (2–8 μm). However, some large voids (1–3 μm) appeared in the film, which resulted from migration and fusion of the small grains to form large grains (Fig. S4).

The effect of annealing on the optical and photoelectric properties of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films is given in Fig. 3. Fig. 3a shows the UV–Vis absorbance spectra of the as-deposited, 400, 500 and 550 °C-annealed   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films. All films exhibit an absorbance onset at 520 nm, which is characteristic of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite [23,24]. As the annealing temperature increases, the absorbance (below 520 nm) of the films decreases. The defects (such as vacancy, interstitials, or antisite) in the films can also absorb light [37,38]. Since the annealing improved the crystallinity of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films, in our case, we think that one cause for the drop of the absorbance is a decrease of the defect densities in the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films due to annealing. For the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film annealed at 550 °C, the other cause is that the film has considerable voids, which leads to some direct light transmission, decreasing the light absorbance of the film. Based on the absorbance spectra, the optical band gaps of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films can be calculated by the Tauc plot method assuming a direct gap, as shown in Fig. 3b. All the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films have an optical gap of 2.35–2.36 eV (which is near the reported value of 2.3 eV [23]), and the gap slightly decreases as the annealing temperature increases.

The photoelectric quality of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films was examined by photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL decay measurements, and the results are given in Fig. 3c and d, respectively. In Fig. 3c, all the

PL peaks of the as-deposited, 400, 500 and 550 °C-annealed   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films are located at 547 nm (2.26 eV), which is close to their optical band gap. The PL intensity increases as the annealing temperature increases from 25 °C (the as-deposited) to 500 °C and then drops as the annealing temperature further increases to 550 °C. The   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film annealed at 500 °C has the strongest PL intensity, demonstrating this   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film has the best optoelectronic quality. In other words, the film shows the highest photogenerated carrier efficiency and less carrier recombination at defects [39–42]. Fig. 3d shows the photoluminescence (PL) decay traces of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films annealed at different temperatures. Using a biexponential decay function   $  F(t) = A_{1} \exp(-t/\tau_{1}) + A_{2} \exp(-t/\tau_{2}) + A_{0}  $  , the PL decay traces were fitted to determine the decay times of the fast (  $  \tau_{1}  $  ) and slow (  $  \tau_{2}  $  ) components. The decay is attributed mainly to trap-assisted recombination at the defects [43–45]. The average carrier lifetimes were calculated to be 0.5, 1.4, 2.6, and 1.2 ns for the as-deposited, 400, 500 and 550 °C-annealed   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films, respectively. The 500 °C-annealed   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film has the longest carrier lifetime, reflecting the best photoelectric quality.

To quantitatively estimate the density of defects in the annealed and non-annealed   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films, we fabricated FTO/  $  TiO_{2}/CsPbBr_{3}/PCBM/Ag  $   devices, and their dark current-voltage (I-V) curves are shown in Fig. 3e. The current increases linearly with the voltage up to a kink point. Further increasing the voltage beyond the kink point, the current shows a more rapid exponential rise. The sharp rise of the I-V curve correlates with a trap-filled limit (TFL), where all of the defects are occupied by charge carriers. The applied voltage at the kink point is the trap-filled limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ). The defect density can be calculated according to the equation   $  N_{trap} = ed^{2}/(2\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0})V_{TFL}  $   [46], where d is the film thickness, e is the elementary charge, and   $  \varepsilon  $   and   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   are the dielectric constants of the perovskite and the vacuum permittivity, respectively. We estimated the defect densities,   $  N_{trap}  $  , to be   $  8.86 \times 10^{15}  $   and   $  1.09 \times 10^{16}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $   for the annealed and non-annealed   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films, respectively. The defect density was substantially decreased by annealing, which is in agreement with the PL and TRPL results.

The deposited   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films were used to fabricate planar   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells with the structure of FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}/CsPbBr_{3}/SpiroMeOTAD/Au  $   and area of   $  3 mm \times 3 mm  $  , and the schematic diagram, a typical cross-sectional SEM image, and the energy level diagram of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   solar cell are shown in Fig. 4. In Fig. 4b, the thickness of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   layer is approximately 600 nm (the optimized thickness), and the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   grains are very large (500 nm  $  ^{-1}  $     $  \mu  $  m). The effects of the evaporation rate ratio of CsBr to   $  PbBr_{2}  $   and annealing temperature on the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   solar cells are shown in Fig. 4d and e, respectively. When the evaporation rate ratio of CsBr to   $  PbBr_{2}  $   is 0.7:1 and the annealing temperature is 500 °C, the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   cell has the highest PCE. This is in agreement with the previous XRD, SEM, AFM, PL, and defect density measurements. Although the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film annealed at 550 °C has the best crystal quality, the PCE of its solar cell is not the highest. This is due to considerable voids in the high-temperature-annealed   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film, which resulted in direct contact of the spiroMeOTAD and the   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer in the voids, which leads to a leakage current.

In our experiments, the heating temperature of the CsBr source and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   source is   $  \sim490^{\circ}C  $   and   $  \sim290^{\circ}C  $  , respectively, which is less than their melting point (630°C for CsBr and 370.6°C for   $  PbBr_{2}  $  ). evaporation deposition, CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   vapor are from the sublimation of the CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $ , respectively. The CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   vapors encounter each other, and the molecules of CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   react to generate   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   on the   $  TiO_{2}/ITO  $   glass substrate:   $  CsBr + PbBr_{2} \rightarrow CsPbBr_{3}  $ . The molecules of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   migrate and combine to form   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   grains and film. Thus, there are two key factors that determine the quality of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films: one is the stoichiometry, and the other is the crystallinity of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films. The evaporation rate ratio of CsBr to   $  PbBr_{2}  $   influences the stoichiometry, and the substrate temperature and post-annealing temperature affect the crystallinity. Therefore, to obtain high-quality   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films from dual-source thermal coevaporation deposition, the evaporation rate ratio of CsBr to   $  PbBr_{2}  $ , substrate temperature, and post-annealing temperature should be optimized.

# 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we have demonstrated that high-quality inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite films and high-efficiency planar   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   solar cells can be fabricated by dual-source vacuum thermal coevaporation of CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $  . The effects of substrate temperature, evaporation rate ratio of CsBr to   $  PbBr_{2}  $  , and annealing temperature on the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film quality and its photovoltaic performance were studied. The optimal deposition conditions of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films are a substrate temperature of 300 °C, evaporation rate ratio of CsBr to   $  PbBr_{2}  $   of 0.7:1, and post-annealing temperature of 500 °C for 15 s. The deposited films are extremely uniform and have large grains. The best-performing device fabricated from the deposited   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film achieved a stabilized high conversion efficiency of 6.95% in small size (0.09 cm²) and 5.37% in large size (1 cm²). Furthermore, the fabricated   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   cell has good long-term stability. The dual-source thermal coevaporation is an effective approach for preparing high-quality   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films.
