# Sodium Benzenesulfonate Modified PEDOT:PSS with Improved Wettability and Work Function for Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Wei Li, Huaxin Wang, Xiaofei Hu, Wensi Cai, Cong Zhang, Ming Wang, Zhigang Zang*

W. Li, Dr. H. Wang, X. Hu, Dr. W. Cai, C. Zhang, M. Wang and Prof. Z. Zang

Key Laboratory of Optoelectronic Technology & Systems (Ministry of Education), Chongqing University, Chongqing 400044, China  
E-mail: zangzg@cqu.edu.cn

Keywords: perovskite solar cells, PEDOT:PSS, sodium benzenesulfonate, energy-level alignment

The p-type conducting polymer poly (3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) is widely utilized as the hole transport layer (HTL) for solution-processed planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with a p-i-n structure. However, the inverted PSCs based on PEDOT:PSS HTL suffer from the deficient open-circuit voltage  $ (\mathrm{V_{OC}}) $  and poor stability issues, because of the high electron affinity and acid nature of PEDOT:PSS. In this work, the sodium benzenesulfonate  $ (\mathrm{C_6H_5SO_3Na}) $  (SBS) is applied to modify the PEDOT:PSS (SBS-PEDOT:PSS) layer to form a smoother surface and better energy level alignment with the perovskite layer. In addition, the SBS-PEDOT:PSS layer with improved hole extraction capacity and suppressed charge recombination, significantly increases the grain size and crystallinity of the  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.8}\mathrm{FA}_{0.2}\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  perovskite film. Consequently, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) and  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $  of the inverted PSCs are improved from  $ 18.07\% $  and  $ 1.04\mathrm{V} $  to  $ 19.41\% $  and  $ 1.08\mathrm{V} $ , respectively, after SBS modification. Moreover, the efficiency of the unencapsulated PSCs based on SBS-PEDOT:PSS remains above  $ 90\% $  after storing in ambient condition for 20 days. This research provides an accessible route to surmount the intrinsic imperfection of PEDOT:PSS and ameliorate the performance of PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

The increasing interest in high-performance and economical photovoltaic materials has heightened the emergency to develop the organic-inorganic hybrid halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Since the debut of PSCs proposed by Miyasaka et al. in 2009, the PSCs community has achieved great success such as the rocketing power conversion efficiency (PCE) from the initial 3.8% to the latest 25.5% in 2020 in single-junction architectures, and to 29.1% in silicon-based tandem cells.  $  ^{[1-5]}  $   This eye-catching breakthrough is contributed by the numerous merits of perovskite material such as long carrier-diffusion length, proper bandgap (1.5 – 1.6 eV), and facile solution processing.  $  ^{[6-10]}  $   Generally, the PSCs are classified by architecture into normal (n-i-p) and inverted (p-i-n) structures, where the perovskite layer is sandwiched between the electron transport layer (ETL) and hole transport layer (HTL).  $  ^{[11,12]}  $   For the normal-structured PSCs, metal oxides such as   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}  $   are commonly used as the bottom ETL, whereas the organic materials such as Spiro-OMeTAD are employed as HTL.  $  ^{[13,14]}  $   The PSCs feature high efficiency up to 24.82% by introducing fluorinated isomeric analogs in Spiro-OMeTAD.  $  ^{[15]}  $   However, the normal-structured PSCs with high PCE are usually based on mesoscopic n-type   $  TiO_{2}  $   or insulating   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   to accelerate electron mobility, while the high sintering temperature (>450 °C) limits the application in low-cost flexible devices.  $  ^{[16,17]}  $   Meanwhile, the upper HTL using organic materials is extremely costly and unstable in air. On the contrast, the bottom HTL of inverted (p-i-n) PSCs is low-temperature processable (<100 °C) and cheaper. Therefore, the inverted planar PSCs have a wider application prospect on account of low-temperature preparation, excellent reproducibility, and negligible hysteresis.  $  ^{[18-19]}  $   Besides, the efficiency of the small-area inverted planar PSCs has reached a certified value of 22.3%.  $  ^{[20]}  $   And the record efficiency of over 18% for a mini-module has been achieved in an aperture area of 19.276 cm  $  ^{2}  $  .  $  ^{[21]}  $   These results indicate that the inverted structure may be more suitable for manufacturing

large-area cells with long-term stability and outstanding efficiency, which means inverted planar PSCs have a better prospect in commercialization.

The most common structure of the inverted PSCs is indium tin oxide (ITO)/Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene): polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS)/ perovskite/PCBM/metal electrode.  $  ^{[22,23]}  $   PEDOT:PSS is the most extensively used hole transfer material in inverted PSCs owing to its optical transparency, high thermal stability, and good mechanical flexibility.  $  ^{[24,25]}  $   Nevertheless, the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of the inverted PSCs with PEDOT:PSS HTL is still less than 1.0 V by reason of the too-high electron affinity of PEDOT:PSS to separate hole and electron effectively.  $  ^{[24]}  $   Furthermore, the acidic nature of PEDOT:PSS also causes instability issues in PSCs.  $  ^{[26-28]}  $   Therefore, the modification of the HTL/perovskite interface plays an important role in achieving efficient charge transfer, reducing recombination, and controlling the morphology of the perovskite film.  $  ^{[29-31]}  $   To solve the acidic issue, Hytham et al. reported a strategy to tune the acidic nature of PEDOT: PSS by adding urea, where the PCE was increased from 14.4% to 18.8%.  $  ^{[32]}  $   Wang et al. applied a mild base of imidazole to tune the pH value of the PEDOT:PSS, which can neutralize the acidity and thus largely improving the long-term stability of PSCs.  $  ^{[33]}  $   To obtain better energy level alignment, Zuo and Ding modified PEDOT:PSS with polymer electrolyte PSS-Na to improve the work function and obtained an outstanding   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.52 V in the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbBr_{3}  $  -based PSCs.  $  ^{[34]}  $   Another effective strategy is doping inorganic halometallate (such as NaCl, RbCl, and CsI) into PEDOT:PSS to suppress the charge recombination and increase the stability.  $  ^{[35-37]}  $   Meanwhile, adding metal salts to PEDOT:PSS to form PEDOT:PSS-metal oxide composite can enhance the work function and achieve higher   $  V_{OC}  $   and PCE of PSCs  $  ^{[38,39]}  $  . A previous study reported a self-organized PEDOT:PSS comprising perfluorinated ionomer (PFI) that can successfully increase the   $  V_{OC}  $   and PCE.  $  ^{[40]}  $   However, few works have focused on disposing of multiple issues, i.e., energy alignment, surface energy modulation, and morphology modification, simultaneously. Therefore, it is crucial to find some additives which can overcome the

shortcomings of PEDOT:PSS and enhance the efficiency of the inverted planar perovskite solar cells.

Herein, we proposed a facile and effective method to manufacture high-performance inverted PSCs by adding sodium benzenesulfonate (SBS) into PEDOT:PSS to achieve an SBS-PEDOT:PSS composite hole transport layer (HTL). We systematically investigated the effects of SBS on surface morphology, chemical composition, and work function of PEDOT:PSS by using atomic force microscopy (AFM), X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), and ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS). Compared with the pristine PEDOT:PSS film, the SBS-PEDOT:PSS film demonstrates smaller roughness, more efficient hole extraction, and improved work function for desirable energy level alignment. The perovskite layer spin-coated on SB-PEDOT:PSS film has better crystallinity, larger grain size, and fewer grain boundaries. The improved perovskite film morphology contributes to the reduced surface recombination and the passivated trap states. Eventually, using  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.8}\mathrm{FA}_{0.2}\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  as the light-absorbing layer and SBS-PEDOT:PSS as the HTL, we successfully improved the  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $ , short-circuit current density ( $ \mathrm{J_{SC}} $ ), fill factor (FF), and PCE from  $ 1.04\mathrm{V} $ ,  $ 21.24\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ ,  $ 81.32\% $  and  $ 18.07\% $  to  $ 1.08\mathrm{V} $ ,  $ 21.57\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ ,  $ 83.30\% $ , and  $ 19.41\% $ , respectively. Besides, the SBS-modified device shows less hysteresis effect and enhanced long-term stability.

# 2. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

To investigate the influence of introducing SBS on the PEDOT:PSS surface morphology, we measure the topographic AFM of different PEDOT:PSS films. The different PEDOT:PSS films are denoted as SBS-x, where x is the SBS concentration with a unit of mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  . As shown in Figure 1a and b, the roughness of the pristine PEDOT:PSS film is 1.05 nm, which is higher than the roughness of SBS-9 film (0.82 nm). The smoother HTL film will be instrumental in forming high-quality perovskite film.  $  ^{[38]}  $   Furthermore, the bright regions in AFM images refer to the hard phase of

PEDOT, and dark regions represent the soft phase of PSS.  $  ^{[32]}  $   For the pristine PEDOT:PSS film, the PEDOT and PSS tend to assemble to form larger and separated regions, which results in discontinuous and inefficient charge transport. While after SBS modification, the PEDOT:PSS film reveals a more uniform and nanosized fibrous structure, contributing to accelerating the charge transport. The uniform distribution of bright and dark regions can be observed in SBS-3 and SBS-15 PEDOT:PSS films (Figure S1). Afterward, we test the transmittance properties of the different HTLs on the ITO/glass substrates (Figure S2). All the films possess well transmittance (  $  >80\%  $  ) in the visible region, ensuring the perovskite layer absorb plentiful light. Then the current density - voltage (J-V) curves of the device with the structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS (or SBS-PEDOT:PSS)/Ag are tested to investigate the conductivity of different HTLs. According to the J-V curves presented in Figure S3, the SBS-9 PEDOT:PSS film shows the highest current density at the same bias, which suggests its higher conductivity and verifies that the SBS promotes the hole transporting ability.  $  ^{[41]}  $

Afterward, the XPS is employed to investigate the variation of chemical state after SBS modification. As illustrated in Figure 1c, a strong signal located at  $ 1071\mathrm{eV} $  referring to Na 1s orbit is detected in SBS-9 film but absent in the pristine film, suggesting the  $ \mathrm{Na^{+}} $  has been introduced successfully. Besides, we investigate S 2p orbit by XPS as shown in Figure 1d. According to the reported studies,[42-44] the lower binding energy corresponds to the sulfur atoms in the PEDOT complex and the higher binding energy relates to the sulfur atoms in the PSS complex. Besides, both peaks compose of spin splitting doublet states S (2p3/2) and S (2p1/2). As depicted in Figure 1d, an obvious shift of S 2p peaks from both PEDOT chains and PSS chains toward low binding energy is observed in SBS-9 PEDOT:PSS film, suggesting the phase segregation of PEDOT and PSS.[43,45] And the S 2p peaks of PSS shifting to lower binding energy indicates the sulfonic acid groups  $ (-SO_3H) $  change to corresponding sodium salts  $ (-SO_3Na) $ .[45] Besides, PEDOT chains might not be wrapped by insulating PSS chains, which leads to better charge hopping between conductive

Then the surface properties of different HTLs are evaluated by measuring the contact angles of DMSO droplets on PEDOT:PSS, SBS-3-PEDOT:PSS, SBS-9-PEDOT:PSS, and SBS-15-PEDOT:PSS films. The insets of Figure 2a-d indicate that the contact angles are closely related to the additive concentration, where the SBS-9-PEDOT:PSS has the smallest contact angle with a value of  $ 9.3^{\circ} $ . The smaller angle represents higher DMSO-wettability of substrates, facilitating the formation of compact and uniform perovskite film.[50,51] Furthermore, the top-view SEM and AFM images of perovskite films deposited on PEDOT:PSS and SBS-x-PEDOT:PSS film are presented in Figure 2 and Figure S5. All the perovskite layers spin-coated on different HTLs fully cover the substrates without pinholes. However, the perovskite films coated on the SBS-modified HTLs exhibit larger and more uniform grain size, where the grain size distribution is shown in Figure S6. Particularly, the perovskite layer on SBS-9-PEDOT:PSS film has the largest grain size as a result of smoother surface and better DMSO-wettability of the SBS-9-PEDOT:PSS film.[52] However, the higher concentration of SBS additive will lead to the decrease of grain size as shown in Figure 2d and Figure S6. Large grain size means better crystallinity and fewer grain boundaries, which are essential for the suppressed carrier recombination and enhanced charge separation.[53] Besides, the AFM images (Figure S5) of perovskite films cross-checked the SEM results in Figure 2. The root-mean-square (RMS) values of the perovskite films spin-coated on modified HTLs are significantly reduced. Specifically, the perovskite layer on the SBS-9-PEDOT:PSS film shows the smoothest morphology with the roughness value of  $ 11.1\mathrm{nm} $ . Moreover, Figure S5 demonstrates the larger grain size of the perovskite layer on SBS-9-PEDOT:PSS film than that on pure PEDOT:PSS film.

Once the SBS concentration reaches  $ 15\mathrm{mg~mL}^{-1} $ , the diffraction peak intensity of perovskite film is slightly decreased, suggesting a tiny deterioration in the perovskite crystallinity. # 3. Conclusion

To conclude, we have demonstrated a facile and efficient method to promote the photoelectric performance and stability of the inverted planar perovskite solar cells by adding sodium benzenesulfonate into PEDOT:PSS hole transport layer. Compared with the PEDOT:PSS film, the

WILEY-VCH

modified PEDOT:PSS film has obtained higher conductivity to speed up hole transportation, smaller surface roughness for better perovskite crystallization, and higher work function to the well-matched energy level alignment. With these advantages, the perovskite film deposited on modified PEDOT:PSS film exhibits larger grain size, more uniform and pinhole-free morphology, less trap-state intensity, and suppressed undesirable recombination. As a result, with the optimal SBS concentration of 9 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , V  $  _{OC}  $  , J  $  _{SC}  $  , FF and PCE of MA  $  _{0.8}  $  FA  $  _{0.2}  $  PbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $   solar cells increased from 1.04 V, 21.24 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , 81.32% and 18.07% to 1.08 V, 21.57 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , 83.30% and 19.41%, respectively. Besides, the perovskite solar cells based on SBS-9-PEDOT:PSS has negligible hysteresis and long-term stability with over 90% of the initial PCE after 20 days stored in ambient condition. Our work provides an easy way to optimize inverted planar perovskite solar cells.

Device fabrication: The ITO-coated glass substrates were washed by deionized water, detergent, alcohol, acetone and isopropanol for 20 min, respectively. Then the substrates were blown dry with nitrogen and cleaned by UV-zone for 30min. C $  _{6}  $ H $  _{5}  $ SO $  _{3}  $ Na was dissolved into PEDOT:PSS solution to get different concentrations (0, 3, 9, 15 mg mL $  ^{-1}  $ ). Then all the samples were transferred into a glove box filled with pure nitrogen. 50 μL perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated onto the top of PEDOT:PSS film first at 1200 rpm for 15 s and then at 4500 rpm for 45 s. 1 mL toluene was dropped on the perovskite film when 15 s is left for spin coating. The perovskite film was then thermally annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. The ETL was obtained by spin-coating with PC $  _{61}  $ BM solution with a concentration of 23 mg mL $  ^{-1}  $  at 4000 rpm 30 s then transferred to a hotplate for annealing at 100 °C for 10 min. A thin layer of RhB101 was spin-coated onto the PC $  _{61}  $ BM.
