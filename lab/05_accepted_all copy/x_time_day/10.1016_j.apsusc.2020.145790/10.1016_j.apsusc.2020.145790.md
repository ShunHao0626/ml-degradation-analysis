ABSTRACT: Metal halide perovskite solar cells have drawn a lot of attention due to their excellent photovoltaic properties. However, a simple method to prepare perovskite films with high quality in ambient air remains a big challenge, which has become an obstacle for the commercialization of PSCs. Here we propose a facile method to prepare efficient  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_x(\mathrm{SCN})_{3 - x} $ -based planar PSCs in ambient conditions and ammonium salt ( $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $ ,  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $ ) are used to regulate the microstructure of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_x(\mathrm{SCN})_{3 - x} $  perovskite film prepared in ambient air. At the optimal concentration, the devices with  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  or  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $  additives achieve the champion efficiency of  $ 14.71\% $  and  $ 16.61\% $  respectively, which are much higher than the  $ 12.97\% $  of the reference device. The stability of the unsealed devices with additives in ambient

The conductive atomic force microscopy and photoluminescence measurement are used to characterize photoelectric properties of perovskite film. The results suggest that the improved photovoltaic characteristics and stability may be attributed to the improved quality of perovskite films, the reduced trap-states and the suppressed charge recombination.

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic perovskite materials are one of the most popular light absorbing materials due to their many advantages, such as wide absorption range, long carrier diffusion length, excellent semiconducting properties and so on [1-4]. In the last few decades, the research on organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has achieved unprecedented development [5-8]. The certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) has reached  $ 25.2\% $  [9]. However, most of the PSCs are fabricated under anhydrous conditions and the instability issue of perovskite material itself in ambient air is still unresolved [10-12]. To deposit the perovskite film with high quality by a simple and scalable process is one of the most important challenges for the commercialization of PSCs. To overcome these issues, much work has been done. For example, some researchers have devoted to improve the photoelectric properties of PSCs by interface engineering and additives [13, 14]. To prepare more stable devices, some efforts have been done to fabricate PSCs based on the 2-dimensional perovskite materials. But the PCE of these devices is lower than most of the 3-dimensional PSCs prepared in the glove box [15-18].

Thiocyanate (SCN) anions are often used as pseudo-halide additives to improve the moisture resistance and efficiency of perovskite PSCs [19-21]. Moreover, several lead sources such as  $ \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{PbCl_2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_2} $  were used to replace  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  to prepare perovskite films. Among them,  $ \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2} $  is one of the important candidates. When the  $ \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2} $  is deposited on the compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film in the two-step method, it tends to form a dense film and cannot react completely with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $ , resulting in the

worse performance of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}(\mathrm{SCN})_x $  PSCs [19, 20, 22, 23]. As it is well-known that the microstructure of the perovskite film has a significant impact on the photovoltaic performance of PSCs and tuning the morphology of perovskite film can improve the photoelectric properties of PSCs [24]. In order to improve the quality of perovskite film, many methods have been used such as solvent reprocessing [25], gas evaporation treatment [26], additive assistance [27] and so on. Among them, the introduction of proper additives in the perovskite precursor is one of the most widely used methods. As reported in our previous work, the introduction of NaSCN and KSCN additives into the perovskite layer can significantly improve the performance and stability of PSC [23]. It is found that the addition of ammonium cation  $ (\mathrm{NH}_4^+) $  into organic PSCs can regulate and optimize the microstructure of perovskite films, leading to the enhanced performance and stability of the PSC [14, 22, 23, 28-30]. Moreover, the  $ \mathrm{NH}_4^+ $  cation can better passivate the trap states in PSCs compared to the  $ \mathrm{Na}^+ $  or  $ \mathrm{K}^+ $  due to its larger ion radius [31]. On the other hand, the  $ \mathrm{SCN}^- $  anion can also contribute to improve the PCE and stability of PSCs [23, 32]. The ammonium chloride  $ (\mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{Cl}) $  has also been used as an additive to improve performance of PSCs [33-35]. Furthermore, it is necessary to transfer the preparation process of PSCs from a glove box to air to develop the lower cost photovoltaic technology. To further improve the performance of device and compare the effects of ammonium thiocyanate  $ (\mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{SCN}) $  and  $ \mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{Cl} $  additions on  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}(\mathrm{SCN})_x $ -based PSCs fabricated in ambient air,  $ \mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{SCN} $  and  $ \mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{Cl} $  are used as additives for  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}(\mathrm{SCN})_x $ -based PSCs in this work. Moreover, the involved mechanism has been systematically investigated.

On this base, our PSCs with the structure of  $ \mathrm{FTO / TiO_2 / CH_3NH_3PbI_{3-x}(SCN)_x / Spiro-OMeTAD / Ag} $  have been fabricated in an ambient air. The  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $  and  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  are selected as additives to optimize the microstructure of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3-x}(\mathrm{SCN})_x $  films, respectively. The effects of  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $  and  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  additives on the microstructure of perovskite layer and photoelectric properties of devices have been systematically studied. Three types of films/PSCs have been analyzed in this work: the Reference film/PSC that no any additive is included, the  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN}- $  and  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl}- $  film/PSC where the  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $  or  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  are added to fabricate the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3-x}(\mathrm{SCN})_x $  films/PSCs. The results show that the as-prepared  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN}- $  and  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $ -film do show larger grain size, homogenous morphology and lower trap-state density, resulting in the improved photovoltaic performance and moisture stability. The highest PCE of the reference PSC is  $ 12.97\% $  while the  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN}- $  and  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $ -PSC achieve the champion PCE of  $ 16.61\% $  and  $ 14.71\% $ , respectively. Moreover, the moisture stability of  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl}- $  PSC and  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $ -PSC in ambient atmosphere has also been significantly improved compared to the reference PSC. The improvement of PCE and stability in the  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN}- $  and  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $ -PSCs may be due to the optimization of microstructure and the reduced trap-state density in the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3-x}(\mathrm{SCN})_x $  films. Significantly, all PSCs in our work are prepared by two-step method in ambient air and all processes are carried out at a relatively low temperature, which will reduce cost.

# 2. Experimental methods

# 2.1 Device fabrication

Fluorine doped tin oxide (FTO, 15 Ω/squares, NSG) substrates were etched using

3

Journal Pre-proofs

Zn powder and diluted hydrochloric acid, then those FTO glass was washed with detergent, acetone, deionized water and ethanol, successively. For   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer, the FTO glass was immersed in titanium tetrachloride aqueous solution at 70 °C for 55 min, then   $  TiO_{2}  $   films were annealed at 200 °C for 30 min in air. The   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}(SCN)_{3-x}  $   films were deposited on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer by a two-step sequential spin-coating method. The   $  \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_{2}}  $   solution (500mg/ml dissolved in dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO, Sigma-Aldrich) was first spin-coated on the   $  TiO_{2}  $ -coated FTO (FTO/  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) film at 4000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 90 °C for 30 min. For the modified devices, different amounts of   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   (Aladdin) or   $  NH_{4}SCN  $   (Aladdin) were introduced into the   $  \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_{2}}  $   precursor solutions. Then, the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   solution (8 mg/ml dissolved in 2-propanol) was spin-coated on   $  \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_{2}}  $   layers at 3000rpm for 60s and annealed at 80 °C for 20 min to form a   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}(SCN)_{3-x}  $   layer. Subsequently, the Spiro-OMeTAD layer was spin-coated on the cooled substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s. The Spiro-OMeTAD solution can be obtained by dissolving 72 mg Spiro-OMeTAD, 28.6μL 4-tertbutylpyridine and 17.5μL Li-TFSI solution (520 mg/mL in acetonitrile) in 1 ml chlorobenzene. Finally, Ag electrode with the thickness of about 100 nm was thermally evaporated on the top of the devices.

The schematic draw of the device structure is presented in Fig. 1a. To investigate the effect of  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $  or  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  additives on crystallinity and morphology of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_x(\mathrm{SCN})_{3 - x} $  films, the XRD patterns and top-view SEM images of the three kinds of perovskite films (with or without additives) are provided. The XRD patterns of reference,  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $ - and  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $ -film are shown in Fig. 1b. It can be seen that no new diffraction peaks appear in the  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $ - and  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $ -film, suggesting that the

$ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $  or  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  additives does not change the crystal structure of perovskite film. Moreover, all the films exhibit the characteristic peaks at  $ 14.08^{\circ} $ ,  $ 28.41^{\circ} $  and  $ 31.9^{\circ} $  which are corresponded to the (110), (220) and (310) crystal planes [22]. It is noted that the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the (110) plane in  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $ -film (0.365) is narrower than that of the  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $ -film (0.371) and reference film (0.376), which indicates that the  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  and  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $  additives increase the crystallinity of perovskite films. In addition, the intensity of the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  peak ( $ 12.5^{\circ} $ ) becomes weaker compared to the reference [22] and there is no  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  peak in the XRD pattern for  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $ -film. These results confirm that the  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $  or  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  addition can suppress the formation of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . The enhanced crystallinity and the reduced  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  will facilitate the charge separation and extraction [22, 36], resulting in the improved photovoltaic performance of devices. Fig. 2a-c shows the top-view SEM images of the three perovskite films, respectively. The corresponding distribution of grain size was shown in Fig. 2d-f. It can be seen that the  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $ -film exhibits the maximum average grain size (407 nm) with respect to those of the  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $ -film (366 nm) and reference film (328 nm). The increased grain size will not only decrease the series resistance of devices but also facilitate to reduce charge trapping or recombination [37], resulting in the enhanced photovoltaic parameters. They will be confirmed by the data of J-V and trap-state density ( $ \mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{trap}} $ ) of the three films, which will be discussed in the following.

The electron   $  D_{trap}  $   of perovskite films has an important impact on device performance. Thus the   $  D_{trap}  $   of the three types of perovskite films was investigated. To get the kink point (trap-filled limit voltage,   $  V_{TFL}  $  ) exactly, six electron-only devices with the structure of FTO/  $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite/PCBM/Ag were fabricated. The average values of   $  V_{TFL}  $   for the three devices are shown in Table S3, respectively. The I-V curves corresponding to the average   $  V_{TFL}  $   are shown in Fig. 6a-c, respectively. At low bias, the

linear relationship of the I-V curve demonstrates the ohmic response, which means the region is the trap-states filling stage. When the applied bias is higher than that of   $  V_{TFL}  $  , the current dramatically increases. This means that the trap-state filling process has completed [49, 50]. The values of   $  D_{trap}  $   can be obtained by the flowing equation (1) [49]:

$$
\mathrm{V} _ {\mathrm{TFL}} = \frac {\mathrm{eD} _ {\text {trap}} \mathrm{L} ^ {2}}{2 \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0}}
$$

(1)

Where  $ \varepsilon $  is the relative dielectric constant of perovskite material,  $ \varepsilon_0 $  is the vacuum permittivity ( $ \varepsilon_0 = 8.854 \times 10^{-12} $  F/m), L is the thickness of perovskite films, and e is the electron elementary charge (e=1.6 × 10 $ ^{-19} $  C). The thicknesses of the three perovskite films were measured and presented in Fig. S4. The values of V $ _{\text{TFL}} $  for the reference, NH $ _4 $ Cl- and NH $ _4 $ SCN-film are 0.67, 0.48 and 0.26V, respectively. According to the equation (1), the calculated D $ _{\text{trap}} $  is  $ 4.29 \times 10^{16} $ ,  $ 2.4 \times 10^{16} $  and  $ 1.24 \times 10^{16} $  cm $ ^{-3} $  for reference-film, NH $ _4 $ Cl- and NH $ _4 $ SCN-film, respectively. Obviously the values of D $ _{\text{trap}} $  decrease after the NH $ _4 $ Cl and NH $ _4 $ SCN addition, suggesting that the defect in perovskite layer was effectively passivated by NH $ _4 $ SCN or NH $ _4 $ Cl additive. They will contribute to enhance the photovoltaic parameters of NH $ _4 $ Cl- and NH $ _4 $ SCN-PSC [51]. This is consistent with our J-V measurements, confirming that the lower D $ _{\text{trap}} $  can help to achieve better photovoltaic performance of PSCs [50].

# Conclusions

In summary,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}(SCN)_{3-x}  $  -based planar PSCs have been fabricated in ambient air and the   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   and   $  NH_{4}SCN  $   are used to regulate the microstructure and photoelectric properties of perovskite layer. After the addition of   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   and   $  NH_{4}SCN  $   additives, the PCE of PSCs is enhanced to 14.71% and 16.61% respectively, compared with the 12.97% of the reference device. Moreover, the stability of PSCs has been significantly improved. The results show that the   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   and   $  NH_{4}SCN  $   additives can increase the grain size, improve the crystallinity and light absorption, reduce the trap-state density and suppress charge recombination of perovskite film, resulting in the improved performance and stability.
