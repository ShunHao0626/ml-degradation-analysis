# Achieving 20% Efficiency for Low-Temperature-Processed Inverted Perovskite Solar Cells

Daobin Yang, Takeshi Sano,* Yuma Yaguchi, He Sun, Hisahiro Sasabe, and Junji Kido*

Low-temperature-processed inverted perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) attract increasing attention because they can be fabricated on both rigid and flexible substrates. For these devices, hole-transporting layers (HTLs) play an important role in achieving efficient and stable inverted PVSCs by adjusting the anodic work function, hole extraction, and interfacial charge recombination. Here, the use of a low-temperature (  $  \leq  $  150 °C) solution-processed ultrathin film of poly[(9,9-dioctyl-fluorenyl-2,7-diyl)-co-(4,4'-(N-(4-secbutylphenyl) diphenylamine)] (TFB) is reported as an HTL in one-step-processed CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  )-based inverted PVSCs. The fabricated device exhibits power conversion efficiency (PCE) as high as 20.2% when measured under AM 1.5 G illumination. This PCE makes them one of the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -based inverted PVSCs that have the highest efficiency reported to date. Moreover, this inverted PVSC also shows good stability, which can retain 90% of its original efficiency after 30 days of storage in ambient air.

# 1. Introduction

In the past nine years, perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) experience a rapid development and show great potential as a very promising next-generation photovoltaic technology because of their excellent power conversion efficiency (PCE) and low-cost fabrication.  $  ^{[1-6]}  $   Two types of device architectures have been widely used for fabricating PVSCs, i.e., mesoporous structure and inverted structure. The current record PCE of the device based on mesoporous structured PVSCs is over 23%.  $  ^{[7]}  $   Such a device is typically constructed with the following architecture of FTO/thin-barrier-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /mesoporous-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /perovskite layer/hole-transporting layer (HTL)/Au. Therefore, the stability of PEDOT:PSS-based inverted PVSCs is poor.  $  ^{[14,15]}  $   To overcome this drawback, inorganic semiconductors, such as   $  NiO_{x}  $  ,  $  ^{[16]}  $   CuI,  $  ^{[17]}  $   CuSCN,  $  ^{[18]}  $     $  Cu_{2}O  $  ,  $  ^{[19]}  $   and   $  V_{2}O_{x}  $  ,  $  ^{[20]}  $   have been used as HTLs. They present excellent chemical stability without corrosion to ITO substrates, good energy level alignment with the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy level of the perovskite layer, high hole mobility benefiting hole extraction, and low-cost fabrication. Among them,   $  NiO_{x}  $   is the most popular inorganic HTL that is used in inverted PVSCs. To date, the   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based inverted PVSCs exhibit PCE of around 20% and excellent stability.  $  ^{[21]}  $   Organic semiconductor HTLs, for example, poly(4-butylphenyldiphenylamine) (poly-TPD) and poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethyl-phenyl)amine] (PTAA),  $  ^{[22,23]}  $   are developed to address this problem because of their low-temperature solution processability. At present, PTAA is one of the most representative organic HTLs in inverted PVSCs. So far, the highest PCE of PTAA-based inverted PVSCs fabricated with   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   is 20.3%,  $  ^{[24]}  $   but this high PCE can be only achieved in two-step-processed   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  -based devices. However, for one-step-processed   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  -based devices, the PCE is only around 15%.  $  ^{[25]}  $   Therefore, it is important to develop low-temperature-processed HTLs to achieve highly efficient and stable inverted PVSCs.

An organic semiconductor polymer of TFB (poly((9,9-dioctylfluorenyl-2,7-diyl)-co-(4,4'-(N-(4-secbutylphenyl)diphenylamine)), its chemical structure is shown in Figure S1 in the Supporting Information, is widely used as an HTL in organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs).  $  ^{[26-28]}  $   Although TFB is used in mesoporous structured PVSCs, the PCE of this device is only 12.8%.  $  ^{[29]}  $   Most importantly, TFB has never been used in inverted PVSCs. According to the previous literature,  $  ^{[30,31]}  $   TFB has suitable energy levels (HOMO = -5.4 eV, lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) = -2.6 eV), high hole mobility (  $  \approx 3 \times 10^{-3}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $  ), high optical transmittance, and solution processability, indicating that TFB may be an ideal HTL for inverted PVSCs. Herein, we use a low-temperature (drying at 150 °C) solution-processed ultrathin film of TFB as an HTL in one-step-processed MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -based inverted PVSCs. By optimizing the thickness of TFB, the inverted PVSCs based on this design exhibit PCE as high as 20.2% with low hysteresis when measured under AM 1.5 G illumination.

Compared to the device without HTL, the distinctly enhanced PCE of the TFB-controlled device is attributed to its simultaneously much higher  $ V_{oc} $ ,  $ J_{sc} $ , and FF. In addition to the PCE, hysteresis is still a large controversial issue in PVSCs. As shown in Table 1, a large hysteresis (3.8%) could be observed in the without HTL-controlled device. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of the corresponding devices were measured, and they are shown in Figure 5. The  $  J_{sc}^{\mathrm{EQE}}  $  values calculated from the integration of the EQE spectra agree well with the  $  J_{sc}  $  values obtained from the  $  J - V  $  curves with less than  $  5\%  $  mismatch, indicating all of the values are within the reasonable errors. The broad and efficient EQE spectra covered the entire visible range from 300 to  $  800~\mathrm{nm}  $ . As expected, the TFB-controlled devices showed higher EQE values than their counterparts in the nearly whole spectral range, resulting in higher  $  J_{sc}  $ . In particular, the TFB-based device showed the maximum EQE value of nearly  $  100\%  $  at  $  360~\mathrm{nm}  $ , leading to such high  $  J_{sc}  $ .

To probe the charge transfer dynamics that occurred at the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{TFB} $  interface, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra for  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films on ITO, and TFB-treated ITO substrates were measured. As shown in Figure S4 in the Supporting Information, the PL spectra of the perovskite layer on the ITO surface exhibited an emission peak at  $ 766~\mathrm{nm} $ , which is typical of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ . However, the peak intensity was obviously lower than that of the TFB-treated ITO. The TFB layer clearly quenched the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  emission, which confirmed that TFB facilitated hole extraction and transport from the perovskite layer to the HTL, leading to high  $ J_{sc} $  and FF.

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, a low-temperature solution-processed TFB was demonstrated as an ideal HTL to push the PCE of one-step-processed  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based inverted PVSCs up to  $ 20.2\% $ . The TFB-based inverted PVSCs showed obviously higher PCE and lower hysteresis than the HTL-free inverted PVSCs. This high PCE is ascribed to the simultaneously enhanced  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $ ,  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $ , and FF. Furthermore, this PCE is remarkably higher than those of PEDOT:PSS- and PTAA-controlled devices. The good energy level alignment between the TFB and perovskite layers, large grain sizes of perovskite layers, good hole extraction and transport are attributed to such enhancement. Materials:   $  C_{60}  $  (>99.5%) was purchased from Luminescence Technology Corp.; the   $  C_{60}  $   was sublimated three times before use. TFB was purchased from American Dye Source.   $  PbI_{2}  $   (99.99%) and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (>98.0%) were purchased from Tokyo Chemical Industry Co., Ltd. and Wako Pure Chemical Industries, respectively. All the chemical reagents were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich and used as received.

Hole-Transporting Layers: TFB Layer: TFB (90   $  \mu  $  L) solutions with a concentration of 0.2, 0.4, and 0.6 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in p-xylene were spin-coated on the ITO substrates at 5000 rpm for 15 s, followed by drying at 150 °C for 10 min. The corresponding thicknesses of the TFB layers are 0.8, 1.5, and 2.5 nm, respectively, which were measured by Dektak 8 Stylus Profiler and SHIMADZU UV-3150 UV–vis–NIR spectrophotometer together.

PEDOT:PSS Layer: PEDOT:PSS (Clevios P VP AI 4083, filtered at 0.45 μm with PVDF) was spin-coated at 4000 rpm for 40 s on to the ITO surface, and the substrates were heated at 150 °C for 20 min before being moved into the glovebox. The thickness of the PEDOT:PSS film is around 30 nm, which was measured by Dektak 8 Stylus Profiler.

PTAA Layer: PTAA (100   $  \mu  $  L) solutions with a concentration of 1.5, 2, 3, and 5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in toluene were spin-coated on the ITO substrates at 4000 rpm for 30 s, followed by drying at 100 °C for 10 min. The corresponding thicknesses of the PTAA layers are 4, 6, 9, and 15 nm, respectively, which were measured by Dektak 8 Stylus Profiler and SHIMADZU UV-3150 UV–vis–NIR spectrophotometer together.

Devices Fabrication and Characterization: Inverted PVSCs devices were fabricated using ITO coated the glass as a substrate with the sheet resistance of 10 Ω sq  $  ^{-1}  $  . The thickness of the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  film is around 550 nm, which was measured by Dektak 8 Stylus Profiler. Then, the films were heated at 100 °C for 10 min.
