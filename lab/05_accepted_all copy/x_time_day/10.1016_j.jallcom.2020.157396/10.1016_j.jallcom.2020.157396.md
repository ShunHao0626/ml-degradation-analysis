# Abstract

Here, anti-solvent-assisted deposition method coupled with the additive engineering is employed to fabricate the perovskite solar cells (PSCs). The utilization of 1-3-methylimidazole iodized salt (DMII) additive in the precursor solution during the anti-solvent-assisted deposition process is helpful to get the high-quality perovskite films with the improved the morphology, crystallization and photophysical properties. Various analytical examinations prove that the perovskite film by this strategy possesses of the excellent crystallinity, enlarged grain size, less crystal boundary, and smoother surface. Base on the high quality of the perovskite films, the efficiency of electron generation and transportation in the PSCs is enhanced, which promote the efficient photovoltaic conversion for PSCs. As a result, the best power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the PSCs has reached  $ 14.56\% $  contrasting with the reference pristine cell achieved  $ 12.80\% $ , respectively. It is demonstrated that the additive engineering cooperating with the anti-solvent-assisted process is a convenient and effective method to get the high-quality light absorption layer and the superior photovoltaic performance PSCs.

Keywords: Perovskite solar cells; additive engineering; Anti-solvent-assisted process; Crystal growth; Film quality.

# 1. Introduction

Journal Pre-proof

The attention of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is unprecedented increased in ascribed largely to the high-power conversion efficiency (PCE) and the convenient preparation technology [1-5]. Recently, the PCE of PSCs has increased more than  $ 25.2\% $ , reaching the same level with polycrystalline silicon, CISG, and CdTe solar cells [6]. In these thin film solar cells, the quality of the light harvesting film is the important factor affecting the performance of the devices [7]. It has been testified that the composition, crystallinity, and morphology of the light harvesting film seriously influence the light harvesting, carrier transport, collection, and recombination in the PSCs [8-11]. Thus, great efforts have been put to enhance the light harvesting film quality in PSCs, such as solvent annealing method, Pb precursor modification, adding anti-solvent during spin-coating, and adding additives [12-18]. Among these methods above, adding the additive is an effective way to promote grain growth or reduce the interface defects by participating in the process of nucleation and crystallization, thus possibly achieving high PCEs [19-21].

Various additives influence the morphology and the physical property of the perovskite by different mechanisms: inorganic acids such as HI, HBr and formic acids as additives in the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  layers retard the rapid reaction between the organic part and the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  [21,22]; Additives with oxygen donor (O-donor), sulfur donor (S-donor), and nitrogen donor (N-donor) provide the Lewis acid and base reaction to form an adduct for the perovskite fabrication, including dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), thiourea and so on [23-26]; Some polymer additives such as 1,8-diiodooctane, 1-cyclohexyl-2-pyrrolidone (CHP) with the high-boiling-point promote the perovskite crystallization to obtain the large grain [27-30]. These additives have been demonstrated to be particularly useful in one-step spin coating method, two-step sequential deposition, and the vapor-assisted solution method. The anti-solvent-assisted method has the advantages of room temperature process, large-area uniform deposition, and smoothness surface of film [31,32]. But, the rapid crystallization of perovskite at the extraction process leads to an uncontrolled reaction getting the poor-quality film [33]. To overcome this issue, some research groups have published many reports about the additives introduced in the perovskite precursor or the antisolvent. Tavakoli et al. added the chloride into the precursor solution to control the perovskite grain size and to suppress the formation of bulk and defects during the anti-solvent-assisted process [34]. Huang et al. reported that the methylamine (MA) additive used in antisolvent to obtain the large grain and low trap density perovskite film [35]. Yang et al. added the methanol additive in the antisolvent to fabricate the desirable perovskite film under the moist conditions [36]. Ionic liquids as additives have

been added in the precursor for the perovskite preparation process such as one-step spin coating method and two-step sequential deposition. Owing to the advantages of the high-boiling-point and the chemical bonding, ionic liquid additives, such as trimethylammonium chloride and 1-Butyl-3-methylimidazolium iodide, efficiently improved the perovskite film quality with high-quality crystallization, and large-grained microstructure [13, 37, 38]. But until now, the research on the ionic liquid in the precursor for the anti-solvent-assisted method is still not systematically studied.

In this work, we added the ionic liquid 1-3-methylimidazole iodized salt (DMII) (Fig. 1b) as the additive in anti-solvent-assisted method for altering the crystallization kinetics of the perovskite to significantly improve the performance of the PSCs. The morphology, crystallization and photophysical properties of the perovskite film were improved by adding the additive of DMII, resulting in the low defect states density in the light harvest layer. The high-quality perovskite film further promotes the electron transport and restrain the carrier recombination. Moreover, we also explored the optimum proportion of the DMII, which shown that the perovskite films can be well controlled by the molar ratios of  $ 1\% $ . As a result, and the PCEs of the best solar cells were enhanced from  $ 12.80\% $  to  $ 14.56\% $  under  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $  illuminations. We confirm that this ionic liquid additive modified the anti-solvent-assisted process is a desirable way to enhance the quality of perovskite light harvesting film.

# 2.1 Materials

Substrate is fluorine-doped tin oxide conducting glass (FTO) (Pilkington, 15  $ \Omega $ /square). Methtylammonium iodide ( $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $ ) was purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. DMII (Monils Chem. Eng. Sci. & Tech.),  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  (Sigma-Aldrich), traisopropyl titanate (Sigma-Aldrich), N-methyl-2-pyrrolidinone and  $ \gamma $ -butyrolactone (Alfa Aesar) 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis (N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobifluorene (spiro-MeOTAD, Lumtec), tert-butylpyridine (TCI) and bis(trifluoromethylsulphonyl)imide lithium (Sigma-Aldrich) are used directly after purchased and never further take purification.

# 2.2 PSCs fabrication

The patterned FTO glasses were cleaned and further treated by ozone-ultraviolet to remove organic contaminants.  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  blocking layer  $ (\mathrm{bl - TiO_2}) $  was prepared on cleaned FTO substrates by spin coating the traisopropyl titanate (isopropanol) solution with  $ 0.15\mathrm{M} $  in ethanol at 2500 r.p.m. for  $ 20~s $ , and then was annealed at  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $ . The ml-  $  TiO_{2}  $  layer about 200-300 nm thickness was achieved, with dried 10 min at 100 °C in the oven and then annealed at 500 °C for 30 min. The precursor contained MAI and  $  PbI_{2}  $  (1.2:1, molar ratio), which was dissolved in the mixed solvent, containing N-methyl-2-pyrrolidinone and  $  \gamma  $ -butyrolactone (7:3, weight ratio). The additives DMII (0, 0.5, 1, 2 and 5% with respect to the molarity of  $  PbI_{2}  $ ) were mixed in the precursor solution. The perovskite film formation as show in Fig. 1a comprised of 1) spin coating precursor at 3500 r.p.m. for 20 s, 2) immersing into the diethylether bath for 150 s and 3) grain growth by heating plate at 150 °C for 15 min. The hole transport layer (HTM) solution contains 1 mL spiro-OMeTAD solution (72.3 mg spiro-OMeTAD in 1 mL chlorobenzene), 17.5  $  \mu  $ L lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) solution (520 mg Li-TFSI in 1mL acetonitrile) and 28.8  $  \mu  $ L 4-tert-butyl pyridine (tBP). After the perovskite film deposition, the HTM layer is prepared by spin coating the solution above at 2000 r.p.m. for 30 s and the 300-400 nm thickness HTM layer is achieved. Finally, 70-80 nm of Au electrode was thermally evaporated at  $  10^{-7}  $  Torr atmospheric pressure.

# 2.3 Device characterization

The SEM images of the perovskite films were observed using a FEI Quanta 250 field-emission scanning electron microscope (SEM). X-ray diffraction (XRD) was examined by the ESCALAB 250 spectrometer with the  $ \mathrm{hv} = 1486.6\mathrm{eV} $  Al Kα radiation. UV-vis absorption spectra were measured by an UV-vis spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer Lambda 850). Photoluminescence (PL) measurement was conducted by the J-Y Horiba UV-lamb micro-Raman spectrometer. The excitation is  $ 488~\mathrm{nm} $  He-Cd laser after back-scattering. Photoluminescence transients were recorded with a FLS920 fluorescence spectrophotometer (Edinburgh Instruments, UK) using a  $ 405~\mathrm{nm} $  laser at a pulse frequency of 0.5-10 MHz. In order to obtain the contrast samples in the same conditions, the perovskite films were spin-coated on the mesoporous  $ \mathrm{Al_2O_3} $  film. An apparatus designed by Institute of Physics; Chinese Academy of Sciences was used to test the incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE). To examine the semiconductor optical property of the perovskite films prepared with additive, various optical characterization measurements were carried out, including the UV-vis absorption, PL patterns and time-resolved PL decay. From the absorption spectra of the samples with and without DMII in Fig. 4(b), we find that all the spectra cover the visible light region and show similar intensity from  $ 500~\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 700~\mathrm{nm} $ , which suggests that adding additives do not impact the films thickness and do not change the perovskite loading amount on the

Thus, we can conclude that the crystallization of perovskite was enhanced effectively by using the modified anti-solvent-assisted deposition method with the 1% DMII additive, resulting in the enhancement of the film quality and the semiconductor optical property.

The DMII additive effectively decelerated the chemical reaction rate in the anti-solvent-assisted deposition process. In addition, this work helps to provide the guidance for the selection or exploration of new additives.

# 4. Conclusions

We have demonstrated that adding the DMII additive in anti-solvent-assisted method facilitated the coarsening of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  grains, improved the film crystallinity, and enhanced the film optical characterizations and hydrophobic performances. Moreover, by the steady-state and transient-state PL examinations, the interfacial charge transfer efficiency in the  $ 1\% $  DMII treating perovskite is obviously better than the pristine one, which is owing to the less grain boundary in the DMII film. As a result, the  $ 1\% $  DMII-assisted process improved the PCE of the PSC from  $ 12.8\% $  to the reference device of  $ 14.56\% $ . Thus, based on our idea of ionic liquid additive modified anti-solvent-assisted process provided a convenient and efficient strategy to get the devices with high PCEs and stabilities.
