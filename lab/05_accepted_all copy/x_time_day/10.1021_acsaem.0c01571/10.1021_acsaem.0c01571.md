# Mechanistic Understanding of Cetyltrimethylammonium Bromide-Assisted Durable  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  Film for Stable ZnO-Based Perovskite Solar Cells

Yan Guo, Xin Li,  $  ^{*}  $   Leilei Kang,  $  ^{*}  $   Chuanqi Cheng, Xiong He, Xin Liu, Jingyan Liu, Yuanchao Li, and Cunku Dong  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Long-term stable perovskite solar cells (PSCs) fabricated in ambient conditions are highly desirable. However, the stable perovskite film has yet to be easily controlled in open air, especially employing ZnO as an electron transportation layer. Here, the roles of cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTABr) were further investigated on ZnO-based PSCs, including more details of the grain boundaries and the interaction mechanism between CTABr and perovskite layer. The average efficiency of the unsealed CTABr-modified PSCs could remain at 93.2% of their original level after exposing to the environment with 60–65% relative humidity for as long as 2150 h. Meanwhile, the efficiency of the PSCs with the structure of fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/ZnO/MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -CTABr/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au was also increased obviously. To deepen the understanding of the roles of CTABr, various powerful

techniques such as synchrotron-based grazing incidence X-ray diffraction, Hall effect measurement, space-charge-limited current, and density functional theory calculations were used. Our work would deepen the understanding of the enhancement mechanism of CTABr on PSCs.

# INTRODUCTION

The booming development of organometal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) shows great potential for clean energy. For large-scale commercialization, the golden triangle of efficiency, cost, and stability is urgent to be considered, the gaps of which need to be narrowed significantly.  $  ^{1}  $   Numerous studies mostly focus on improving the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs, surging from 3.8 to   $  \sim25\%  $   in the past decade.  $  ^{2-4}  $   However, the stability issue has been becoming a critical bottleneck for commercializing PSCs. In particular, it is difficult to prepare stable PSCs under atmospheric conditions. Therefore, understanding of the essence of the stability of PSCs is urgent.  $  ^{5-7}  $

Many factors, including external factors (moisture, oxygen, heat, light, etc.) and internal factors (ion migration, architecture, interface, etc.), can affect the stability of PSCs.  $  ^{8}  $   External factors can exert influence on PSCs' performance through changing intrinsic elements, which is the primary cause of instability. Owing to the hygroscopic nature of the organic cations and weak interaction between organic and inorganic components,  $  ^{9}  $   ion migration within PSCs has been considered as a major factor for the origin of the stability issue. The motion of   $  I^{-}  $  ions and   $  I^{-}  $  -ion vacancies in perovskite film have been determined by previous

As a hydrophobic insulating layer, CTABr self-assembled monolayer (SAM) on the surface and GBs of perovskite film can significantly inhibit the moisture penetration and improve air stability of the devices. # RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The CTABr SAM was achieved over the surface of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   film by a facile spin-coating method, which would form spontaneously on the perovskite film by virtue of van der Waals interactions of the long alkyl chains.  $  ^{26}  $   For brevity, the perovskite film treated by CTABr is defined as   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  -CTABr X, where X represents the concentration of CTABr. As a control, the FTO/ZnO/MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film spin-coated by the same amount of 2-propanol (IPA) solvent is denoted as   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  -IPA. Notably, all fabrication processes were conducted under ambient conditions. The details are depicted in the experimental section of the Supporting Information. As shown in Figure S1a,b, the Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  -CTABr films present two intense bands within the range of 2950–2850 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , which can be assigned to the asymmetric and symmetric stretching vibrations of C–CH  $  _{2}  $   in methylene chains.  $  ^{27}  $   Also, the intensities of the IR bands enhance with the increase of the concentration of the CTABr solution. This result suggests that CTABr molecules have been deposited onto the surface of the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   films. To investigate the effect of CTABr on the morphology of the perovskite film, scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and atomic force scanning probe microscopy (AFM) were conducted (Figures 1c–f, S2, and S3). Besides the presence of   $  PbI_{2}  $   nanosheets, the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   films treated by CTABr solutions become more compact and smooth compared to the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  -IPA film. Obviously, the introduction of CTABr did form a coating over the surface of perovskite film, where the thickness of the coating increases with CTABr concentrations. However, once the concentration of CTABr solution is more than 5 mM, local aggregation would occur, concomitant with the increased interface barrier and resistance for PSCs.  $  ^{21,28}  $   Thus, 5 mM CTABr solution should be the exact concentration to form a homogeneous monolayer.  $  ^{29}  $

To explore the details of the GB regions without   $  PbI_{2}  $   nanosheets, high-resolution scanning transmission electron microscopy (HR-STEM) was carried out. As shown in Figure 1g,l, the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   crystals treated with 5 mM CTABr are more uniform than the one without any treatment, where the entire surface of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   grains is preserved by a dense coating. A clear amorphous band marked by the red line between well-crystallized perovskite phases can be found, which should be CTABr self-assemblies for patching up the GBs (Figure 1m). In contrast, only disordered crystals can be found in the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  -IPA film, of which the perovskite crystals have been destroyed to some extent (Figure 1h). The above results reveal that the grain-locking effect can also be realized by the CTABr modification.  $  ^{22}  $   To identify the components of perovskite films, scanning transmission electron microscopy–energy-dispersive X-ray (STEM–EDX) spectra and EDX mappings were collected

We can see that I, Pb, and Br elements evenly distribute over the MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5 film, while no obvious signal of the Br element can be detected, but noise in the control sample. In another sense, the diffusion depth of Br $ ^- $  ions is an important factor that needed to be considered. The cross-sectional SEM–EDX line scanning from the complete MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5-based PSC device strongly proves that Br $ ^- $  ions not only reside on the surface but also can permeate the perovskite film (Figure S5). It has been reported that the perovskite lattice would shrink when I $ ^- $  ions are substituted by smaller Br $ ^- $  ions. $ ^{15} $  As a result, the interactions between organic cation and lead halide become stronger, suppressing ion migration and in turn promoting the stability of perovskite crystals. $ ^{19,30} $  In our case, both the HR-STEM images and the corresponding fast Fourier transform (FFT) patterns indicate that the lattice parameter of the (220) plane in tetragonal MAPbI $ _3 $  slightly decreases from 0.3026 to 0.2894 nm after treating by CTABr solution (Figure S6). $ ^{31} $  We guess that the trace amounts of Br $ ^- $  ions have embedded into I $ ^- $  vacancies and/or exchanged with I $ ^- $  ions, generating a I–Br mixed perovskite. $ ^{15,32} $  This conjecture can be further validated by the slight

blue shift of the absorption edges in the ultraviolet–visible (UV–vis) absorption spectra (Figure S7).

The synchrotron-based two-dimensional GIXRD (2D-GIXRD) was measured, aiming to investigate the influence of CTABr SAM on crystal features of perovskite film. Figure 2a,b displays the 2D-GIXRD patterns of the MAPbI $ _3 $ -IPA and MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5 films, respectively. It is worth noting that the perovskite films used for testing had been stored in air for 15 days. Both of them present the typical diffraction rings of tetragonal MAPbI $ _3 $  crystal, which involves (110) ( $ q = 10.0 \text{ nm}^{-1} $ ), (220) ( $ q = 20.0 \text{ nm}^{-1} $ ), and (310) ( $ q = 22.4 \text{ nm}^{-1} $ ) crystal plane diffractions. $ ^{33} $  By comparison, the diffraction intensities of MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5 film are much stronger than that of the MAPbI $ _3 $ -IPA film, especially for the (110) crystal plane. This indicates that the introduction of CTABr SAM could significantly improve the crystallinity and orientation of perovskite crystals. $ ^{31,33} $  Meanwhile, a distinct diffraction ring indexed to the PbI $ _2 $  (001) plane ( $ q = 9 \text{ nm}^{-1} $ ) appears in the MAPbI $ _3 $ -IPA film, suggesting that obvious decomposition has occurred. $ ^{34,35} $  In contrast, the diffraction features of PbI $ _2 $  are negligible in the MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5 film. Moisture is one of the main reasons that resulted in the decomposition of perovskite film, which is closely related to the durability of PSCs.  $  ^{19,38}  $   The pathways that water interacts with perovskite film include contacting with the film surface as well as further penetrating into film through GBs. Therefore, avoiding the direct contact between moisture and perovskite film has been certified as one of the most effective ways to improve the stability of PSCs.  $  ^{39}  $   Considering that a large amount of hydrophobic long alkane chains shielded over the perovskite film, we speculate that the CTABr SAM-decorated MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film ought to be capable of moisture resistance. To corroborate this assumption, static water contact angle measurements were performed. As shown in Figure 3a, we can see that the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -IPA film presents a water contact angle of 24.7° that decreases quickly by about 20% within 60 s, suggesting that water molecules can easily infiltrate into the perovskite layer. Exhilaratingly, the contact angle enormously jumps to 97° and slightly declines to 93° when introducing CTABr solution (Figure 3b). To further assess the feasibility of water resistance, the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films with and without CTABr treatment were exposed to water vapor. The color changes of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -IPA and MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -CTAB 5 films are shown in Figure 3c,d, where both films were sprayed twice by water vapor for 60 s and healed for different time periods. The MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film without CTABr has severely broken down upon the first water vapor spraying. After healing for 180 s, many distinctly irreversible dots appear. After the second loop, the film is damaged more seriously and the FTO substrate can even be seen. As expected, only a small fraction of the CTABr-modified MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film turns yellow, given the same operation conditions. Interestingly, it is able to recover to the original state within less than 45 s. The videos in Supporting Information Movies S1 and S2 vividly exhibit this surprising self-healing behavior. This phenomenon suggests that the introduction of the CTABr SAM could restrain the production of the nonstoichiometric defects by reducing the loss of the volatile moieties in perovskite crystals even under extreme conditions.  $  ^{9}  $   The changes of the perovskite crystals were investigated by X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements. The main diffraction patterns of the fresh MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -CTABr 5 film, attributed to the (110), (220), and (310) planes at 14.09, 28.42, and 31.88°, respectively, are identical to that of the fresh MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -IPA film, indicating that both procedures generate the same perovskite crystals with tetragonal structure (Figure 3e,f).  $  ^{40}  $   However, the peak intensities of the (110) and (220)

planes of the MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5 film are much stronger than those of the MAPbI $ _3 $ -IPA film. Additionally, the variation trend of intensity ratios of (110)/(310) and (220)/(310) is well consistent with that of the (110) and (220) peak intensities. These results suggest a better crystallinity and more ordered grain orientation in the MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5 film (Figure S8), which agree well with the results of GIXRD. $ ^{41} $  Upon exposing to water vapor and healing for 10 min, a prominent diffraction peak indexed to PbI $ _2 $  crystals appears in the MAPbI $ _3 $ -IPA film, while only a weak PbI $ _2 $  peak can be seen for the MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5 film. Interestingly, the diffraction peak of PbI $ _2 $  in the MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5 film becomes weaker with prolonging healing time to 20 h. In contrast, the variation of the sprayed MAPbI $ _3 $ -IPA film is negligible with the extension of healing time. This result provides a robust evidence for the excellent moisture-resistant and self-healing behavior of the CTABr SAM-modified perovskite film.

The precise control of the interfaces is critical to improve the performance of PSC devices. Thus, we preferentially investigated the effect of CTABr concentration on the device photovoltaic performance. Figure S9a shows the photocurrent density–photovoltage   $  (J-V)  $   curves of devices with the different concentrations of CTABr-treated perovskite films. The corresponding parameters including power conversion efficiency (PCE), short-circuit current density   $  (J_{\mathrm{SC}})  $  , open-circuit voltage   $  (V_{\mathrm{OC}})  $  , and fill factor (FF) are listed in Table S1. A volcano trend in device performance was obtained with the increase of the CTABr concentration. The PCE of the device increases from 14.36 to 16.51% and then decreases to less than 7%. In this regard, interfacial engineering should be affordable. A more compact and smoother interface can help to decrease the interface resistance, accelerate the charge transfer, and improve the device performance as well as hysteresis, and vice versa.  $  ^{42}  $   In accordance with the results of the SEM and AFM characterizations (Figures S3 and 1c–f), the device based on the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -CTABr 5 film exhibits the best performance. By comparing the cross-sectional SEM images, we can find that the contacts of perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD interface and perovskite grain GBs within MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -CTABr 5-based PSC (Figure 1a) are more compact than that of the control device (Figure S10). Recently, it was reported that the wide-band-gap halide (n-hexyl trimethyl ammonium bromide) stacked onto the perovskite surface can effectively reduce recombination at the interface of perovskite and hole transportation layers.  $  ^{43}  $   Similarly, the recombination of charge carriers at the interface should also be decreased by CTABr SAM. To have a deep insight into the effect of CTABr on the charge transfer processes, electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) were further recorded. The results and the corresponding equivalent circuit are plotted in Figure S9b, and the final ZView2 fitting data are summarized in Table S2. The equivalent circuit used for fitting Nyquist plots consists of series resistance   $  (R_{\mathrm{s}})  $   and chemical capacitance (CPE1) in parallel with charge transfer resistance   $  (R_{\mathrm{CT}})  $   at perovskite/ZnO and perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD interfaces relative to the carrier transport.  $  ^{44}  $   The result shows that the CTABr 5-based PSC reveals a distinctly lower   $  R_{CT}  $   (115.37 Ω) compared to that of the control device (184.4 Ω), while the   $  R_{CT}  $   value of CTABr 20-based PSC is as high as 331.62 Ω. In principle, the lower   $  R_{CT}  $   indicates a more efficient charge extraction/transport and weaker carrier recombination,  $  ^{28,41,44}  $   which could well explain the promoted performance of CTABr 5-based PSC and the deteriorative performance of CTABr 20-based PSC.

To verify the reliability of the CTABr modification, we fabricated 50 individual devices based on MAPbI $ _3 $ -IPA and MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5 films, respectively. The statistic distribution diagrams of their parameters extracted from  $ J-V $  curves are plotted in Figure S11, and the corresponding average values are listed in Table S3. We are delighted to find that the champion PCE is significantly increased to 17.02% from 14.85% upon the CTABr treatment. In addition, the hysteresis index (HI) of the target device decreases to 0.09 from 0.14, which is calculated using eq S1 in the Supporting Information.

To more precisely estimate the PCE and hysteresis behavior, we further traced the steady-state photocurrent output of the champion MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5-based PSC and MAPbI $ _3 $ -IPA-based PSC at constant bias values of 0.85 and 0.80 V, respectively. As shown in Figure 4b, the steady-state photocurrent density and PCE of the champion MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5-based PSC, and MAPbI $ _3 $ -IPA-based PSC are 19.90 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , 16.89% and 18.24 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , 14.61%, respectively, close to the values from their  $ J-V $  curves (Figure 4a). Besides, the shorter saturation time of the target device indicates a weaker hysteresis, in line with the results of the  $ J-V $  measurements. During 300 s of continuous illumination, the output photovoltaic parameters of the target device are more stable, signifying a better working stability. Furthermore, the corresponding incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra were measured

(Figure 4c). The IPCE absorption edge of the champion MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5-based PSC delivers a slight spectral shift, which is in agreement with the observed blue shift in the UV–vis spectra (Figure S7). Also, an improved IPCE value for the MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5-based PSC can be observed, manifesting that the introduction of CTABr could perfect the photonic carrier collection processes. $ ^{32} $  The integrated  $ J_{\text{SC}} $  values of the champion devices with and without CTABr from the IPCE spectra are 19.90 and 18.45 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , respectively, approaching the  $ J_{\text{SC}} $  values from the  $ J-V $  curves. As studied above, the CTABr SAM endows the MAPbI $ _3 $  film with excellent moisture resistance and self-healing ability. As a proof of concept, we subtly fabricated the PSCs employing the healed MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5 film as a light absorption layer, which had been exposed to water vapor for 120 s. From the  $ J-V $  curves in Figure 4d, we can see that PCE of the device utilizing the healed MAPbI $ _3 $ -CTABr 5 film is almost comparable to that of the device using the fresh one. This unique moisture-proof and self-healing properties can undoubtedly ease the effect of moisture on the device performance, which should be beneficial for improving the stability of PSCs in practical working environment.

# CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, the multifunctional roles of CTABr were further demonstrated on ZnO-based PSCs, in which the CTABr SAM endows the perovskite film with moisture-resistant and self-healing behavior and high crystallinity. The long alkyl chains of the CTABr SAM and vertically distributed   $  PbI_{2}  $   are capable of passivating the surface and GBs of the perovskite film, thereby blocking the external interference. Meanwhile, the strong interaction between   $  CTA^{+}  $  and   $  I^{-}  $  ion ameliorates the migration of   $  I^{-}  $  ions in the perovskite film. Besides, the free   $  Br^{-}  $  ions would patch the   $  I^{-}  $  vacancies induced by ion migration, further reducing the defect density and promoting the stability. As a result, the efficiency increased by 13%, and the averaged efficiency of the unsealed CTABr-modified PSC devices could nearly remain at 93.2% of their original level after exposing to highly humid (60–65% RH) conditions for as long as 2150 h. This work further deepens the understanding of the enhancement mechanism of CTABr on PSC devices.
