# ABSTRACT

High efficiency organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells have attracted significant attention and experienced a rapid development in recent years. Lithium-doped spiro-OMeTAD is one of the most commonly used hole transporting material, however, the hygroscopicity of lithium dopant usually causes serious moisture instability of devices. Herein, we demonstrate a dopant-free spiro-OMeTAD as hole transporting layer to improve the ambient stability of planar perovskite solar cells. With the optimization of the thickness of spiro-OMeTAD layer, the dopant-free spiro-OMeTAD based device achieved a comparable device performance with a champion power conversion efficiency of  $ 16.92\% $ . Moreover, the unencapsulated

dopant-free device showed significantly improved stability, which still maintained 95% of its initial efficiency after storage in ambient environment for 60 days. This work provides a simple and valid approach to overcome the instability issue of spiro-OMeTAD based devices, paving a way to manufacture more stable and efficient perovskite photovoltaics.

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted great attention in the field of photovoltaics since they were reported by Miyasaka in 2009 [1]. After rapid development in recent years, PSCs have become one of the most promising photovoltaic devices due to their simple fabrication methods [2-7] and excellent photovoltaic properties of perevskite, such as low exciton binding energy [8], high absorption coefficient [9, 10] and long carrier diffusion length [11, 12]. Nowadays, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has exceeded  $ 23\% $  [13].

Several architectures of PSCs have been reported, but the most efficient and common one is n-i-p structure, where the perovskite layer was fabricated on the electron transporting layer (ETL) and then covered with the hole transporting layer (HTL) [14]. 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) as a commonly used hole transporting material (HTM) usually needs dopants such as 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) and lithium bis(trifluoromethyl sulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) to enhance the hole transportation [15-19]. Unfortunately, Li-dopant has been found to be detrimental to long-term performance of PSCs because of its hygroscopic nature [20, 21]. Besides, there would be more pinholes in doped spiro-OMeTAD film due to evaporation of tBP and hydration of Li-TFSI [22, 23]. Therefore, lots of studies [24-34] focused on finding an alternative stable dopant or another dopant-free HTM to replace spiro-OMeTAD. Xu and co-workers reported a copolymer dithiophene-benzene (DTB) as a highly efficient HTM to replace doped spiro-OMeTAD to improve the stability of devices [24]. Snaith and co-workers replaced Li-TFSI by molybdenum tris(dithiolene)s as p□dopants of spiro-OMeTAD HTL, and fabricated high-performance PSCs with stabilized PCE greater than  $ 16\% $  and improved long-term stability under thermal stressing [25]. Luo et al. reported 2,3,5,6-tetrafluoro-7,7,8,8-tetracyanoquinodimethane (F4-TCNQ) as an alternative dopant to replace the commonly used Li-dopant for spiro-OMeTAD. The PSCs based on optimal F4-TCNQ doped spiro-OMeTAD exhibited a comparable PCE of  $ 12.93\% $  and better stability [26]. In all these researches stated above, the device stability was

improved, however, the PCE was still lower than that of the devices based on classical Li-doped spiro-OMeTAD.

In this work, we simply employed the pristine spiro-OMeTAD as dopant-free HTM in planar PSCs to abandon the negative effects of Li-dopant. The thickness of spiro-OMeTAD was reduced to achieve both effective hole transmission and complete isolation of perovskite and electrode. By optimizing the thickness of spiro-OMeTAD layer, the devices based on dopant-free spiro-OMeTAD with thickness of  $ \sim 50 $  nm (solution concentration of  $ 30~\mathrm{mg / mL} $ ) showed significant improved ambient stability, which retained  $ 95\% $  of their initial efficiency after storage in ambient air for 60 days, while the PCE of control device dropped to  $ 77\% $  after 60 days' aging in the same storage condition. Moreover, the optimal dopant-free device achieved a champion PCE of  $ 16.92\% $ , which was comparable to that of doped devices.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Materials

Indium tin oxide (ITO) glass substrates with ITO thickness of 180 nm and sheet resistance of 8 Ω/sq were purchased from Huayulianhe Co., Ltd. SnO₂ colloid precursor (tin (IV) oxide, 15% in H₂O colloidal dispersion), anhydrous N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF), anhydrous dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and chlorobenzene were obtained from Alfa Aesar. PbI₂ (99.9985%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Co., Ltd. Spiro-OMeTAD was obtained from Shenzhen Mason Technology Co., Ltd. Methylammonium iodide (MAI, 99.5%) was acquired from Borun New Material Technology Co., Ltd. Lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulphonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) were obtained from Aldrich. All these commercially available materials were used as received without any further purification.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

The   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloid precursor ultrasonically diluted by   $  H_{2}O  $   (1:6 volume ratio) was

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

spin-coated onto the cleaned and ultraviolet-ozone treated glass/ITO substrates at 3000 rpm for 30 s, then baked on a hot plate at 150 °C for 30 min. The perovskite precursor solution (1.0 mol/L) was prepared by mixing PbI $ _2 $ , MAI and DMSO (1:1:1 molar ratio) in DMF. After complete dissolution, the perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated onto the cooled SnO $ _2 $ /ITO substrate at 1000 rpm for 1 s and 4000 rpm for 6 s. Then the sample was quickly moved to a small sample chamber which pumped immediately to 10 Pa in about 40 s. A brown and transparent gas-pump dried film was obtained and then taken out to be annealed at 100 °C for 10 min.

The HTL was fabricated on the perovskite film by spin-coating the spiro-OMeTAD solution at 4000 rpm for 40 s. For doped spiro-OMeTAD noted as spiro-Li below, 1mL spiro-OMeTAD/chlorobenzene (72.3 mg/mL) solution was employed with the addition of 54 μL Li-TFSI/acetonitrile (170 mg/mL) and 28.8 μL tBP. For dopant-free spiro-OMeTAD, the thickness of spiro-OMeTAD was adapted by the solution concentration. Spiro-OMeTAD was dissolved in chlorobenzene with different concentration of 10 mg/mL, 30 mg/mL, 50 mg/mL or 70 mg/mL, noted as spiro-10, spiro-30, spiro-50 or spiro-70, respectively. All processes were performed in ambient atmosphere so far. Finally, an 80 nm Au back electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation at a pressure of  $ < 2 \times 10^{-3} $  Pa. The active area was 0.09 cm².

# 2.3. Characterization

The current density-voltage (J-V) curves were measured with a scan rate of 20 mV/s under 100 mW/cm² AM 1.5G simulated illumination using a Keithley 2611 Semiconductor Characterization System with a Newport solar simulator as a light source, and the current density was revised with a mask. Incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectrum was observed using a lock-in amplifier (Model SR830 DSP) coupled with a 1/4 m monochromator (Crowntech M24-s) and 150 W tungsten lamp (Crowntech). Scanning electron microscope (SEM) images were collected with a Hitachi S-4800. Atomic force microscopy (AFM) images were taken by an Agilent Series 5500. Photoluminescence (PL) was measured with NaonLog

infrared fluorescence spectrometer (Nanolog FL3-2Ihr) with excitation at 470 nm. Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) was carried out by an ultrafast lifetime spectrometer (Delta flex) with excitation at 470 nm and emission at 770 nm. The absorption spectrum was recorded with a UV-visible spectrophotometer (Agilent 8453). The water contact angle was measured by a contact angle meter (OCA2O Dataphysics). All of the measurements were performed in ambient atmosphere at room temperature without encapsulation.

# 3. Results and discussion

As we know, Lithium salt was doped to increase the hole mobility of spiro-OMeTAD. It's necessary to decrease the thickness of spiro-OMeTAD layer to maintain efficient hole transportation in dopant-free device. Therefore, doped spiro-OMeTAD and several dopant-free spiro-OMeTAD solutions with decreased concentration were prepared, and the corresponding devices with the structure of ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /MAPbI $ _3 $ /spiro-OMeTAD/Au were fabricated. Fig. 1 shows the SEM image of perovskite and the cross-sectional SEM images of devices. The perovskite film fabricated with our gas-pump method is pretty dense with a homogeneous and smooth surface, which contributes to the excellent performance of devices consequently. As seen in the cross-sectional SEM images, the thickness of spiro-OMeTAD decreases as the solution concentration decreases. AFM measurements were conducted to evaluate the roughness of the perovskite film with or without spiro-OMeTAD. The root mean square (RMS) roughness of pristine perovskite film was 12.70 nm, while the RMS roughness of perovskite coated with spiro-Li, spiro-10 and spiro-30 reduced to 3.08 nm, 5.56 nm and 3.67 nm, respectively. The surface roughness of perovskite/spiro-30 film is close to that of perovskite/spiro-Li film, which indicates that the spiro-30 is enough to mitigate the unevenness of perovskite film effectively.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we demonstrate efficient and stable PSCs utilizing dopant-free spiro-OMeTAD as HTM. In precondition of complete coverage of perovskite layer, the disadvantage of poor hole transmission of pristine spiro-OMeTAD was mitigated to some extent by optimizing the thickness of spiro-OMeTAD layer. With the solution concentration of  $ 30~\mathrm{mg / mL} $ , the dopant-free device achieved a comparable PCE of  $ 16.92\% $  to that of Li-doped device. Moreover, the ambient stability of unencapsulated devices was greatly improved due to the abandonment of Li-dopant. Our findings provide a simple and valid approach to obtain efficient and stable PSCs based on spiro-OMeTAD.
