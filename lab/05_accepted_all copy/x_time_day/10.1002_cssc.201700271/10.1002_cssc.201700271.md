# ABSTRACT:

It is well known that the surface trap states and electronic disorders in the solution-processed  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite film affect the solar cell performance significantly and moisture sensitivity of photo-active perovskite material limits its practical applications. Herein, we show surface modification of perovskite film with a solution-processable hydrophobic polymer namely poly(4-vinylpyridine) (PVP), which passivates the under-coordinated lead (Pb) atoms (on the surface of perovskite) via its pyridine Lewis base side chains and thereby eliminates surface trap states and non-radiative recombination. Besides, it acts as an electron barrier between the perovskite and hole transport layer (HTL) to reduce interfacial charge recombination, which led to improvement in open-circuit voltage ( $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $ ) by 120 to  $ 160\mathrm{mV} $  while the standard cell fabricated in same conditions showed  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  as low as  $ 0.9\mathrm{V} $  due to dominating interfacial recombination processes. Consequently, power conversion efficiency increased by  $ 3\% $  to  $ 5\% $  in the polymer modified devices ( $ \mathrm{PCE} = 15\% $ ) with  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  more than  $ 1.05\mathrm{V} $  and hysteresis-less J-V curves. Advantageously, hydrophobicity of the polymer chain was found to protect the perovskite surface from moisture and improved stability of the non-encapsulated cells, which retained their device performance up to 30 days of exposure to open atmosphere ( $ 50\% $  humidity).

# INTRODUCTION

Emergence of inorganic-organic lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) as a highly potential alternative to conventional photovoltaic cell has pushed the photovoltaic research to a different level. A rapid growth in its power conversion efficiency (PCE), from 3.8%  $  ^{[1]}  $  to certified efficiency of 22.1 %  $  ^{[2]}  $  has been witnessed in just few years. Since its birth in photovoltaic devices, many strategies including various deposition methods  $  ^{[3-6]}  $ , solvent engineering  $  ^{[7]}  $ , tuning of the morphology  $  ^{[8,9]}  $ , and incorporation of additives into the precursor solution  $  ^{[10,11]}  $  have been employed to improve the PCE. Also, interfacial modification of perovskite and its adjacent layers  $  ^{[12,13]}  $  have been recognized as an efficient way to boost the overall PCE, as the interfacial changes dramatically affect charge carrier dynamics and photovoltaic properties. After light absorption by photoactive layer, the generated carriers pass through the interfaces existing between electron transporting layer (ETL) and hole transporting layer (HTL). Therefore, the property of these interfaces and the contact between the layers play a crucial role in determining overall photovoltaic performance of the cell  $  ^{[14]}  $ . The surface trap states and defects at the interfaces not only reduce short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) but more significantly lead to loss in open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) and fill factor (FF). Some reports have shown modifications at interfaces of ETL/perovskite  $  ^{[15-17]}  $ , and ETL/metal electrode  $  ^{[18]}  $  that improved the cell performance by reducing leakage current and recombination. For instance, incorporation of graphene quantum dots  $  ^{[16]}  $  between ETL and perovskite layer and amino polymer (PN4N)  $  ^{[19]}  $  incorporation at the cathode interface have been found to lessen current leakage. Recombination suppression at interfaces has also been successful by introducing chlorine capping layer on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanocrystal, [22] self-assembled monolayers (SAMs)[23] and 3-aminopropyltriethoxysilane (APTES)[24] at  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ /perovskite interface. Other recent reports also highlight the importance of interfacial engineering of perovskite [30]. Furthermore, for minimization of surface trap states, Lewis base treatment of perovskite film has been performed by Snaith and co-workers [31], where they observed suppressed non-radiative recombination with increased photo luminescence and carrier life time up to  $ 2\mu s $ .

Albeit methyl ammonium lead perovskite is one of the best photovoltaic absorbers that has outshined among many solar materials, it is still facing challenges in terms of stability against moisture. It shows rapid degradation in humid atmosphere as methyl ammonium cation (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3^+ $ ) in its lattice can be removed catalyzed by water [32] and the cation simply diffuses out from the structure when exposed to moisture. Therefore, for its practical applications, stability is the essential issue for improvement [33]. Till date, a limited amount of work on the stability improvement has been carried out [34]. Considering issues of stability, perovskite surface defect states due to uncoordinated ions, and the interfacial recombination between  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  or perovskite/HTL interface, here we introduce the hydrophobic polymer poly(4-vinylpyridine) (PVP) that strongly improves the stability of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ . As a Lewis base, pyridine moiety (side chain) of the PVP polymer passivates the surface defects which are caused by under-coordinated lead ion [31] and fill the iodine vacancies trap states over the surface of perovskite film. Also, the thin insulating layer can suppress the interfacial charge recombination by selectively blocked holes and tunneled electron [28]. Moreover, the hydrophobicity of polymer plays the role of encapsulation of the perovskite film to prevent it from the moisture, which enhances the stability of perovskite solar cell.

# 2.1 PSC fabrication

The fabrication of PSCs was carried out by the conventional sequential deposition   $  [4]  $  . Succinctly, patterned FTO glass substrates were obtained by etching FTO layer with Zn-power and 3M HCl (Sigma-Aldrich). Then, the substrates were cleaned by ultrasonication sequentially in 2% hallmax soap solution, distilled water, isopropanol and ethanol. # 2.2 Characterization

The absorption properties, structural analysis and morphological studies for both suitably annealed standard and PVP modified perovskite films have been performed by using Shimadzu UV-2450, X-ray diffractometer (Brucker, D8 Discover, CuKα beam) at scan rate   $  5^{\circ}  $   min  $  ^{-1}  $   and scanning electron microscope (SEM) (SU8000, Hitachi) respectively. The measurement for I-V characteristics of cells was performed by using solar simulator (Peccell Technologies, Inc.,) with the set illumination condition (AM 1.5 G, 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). Peccell PEC-S20 set up was used to measure incident photon to current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra at room temperature under monochromatic light. Impedance was measured on Solartron potentiostat/galvanostat Model SI 1287. Horiba iHR320-Synapse CCD was used to obtain the steady state photoluminescence (PL) spectra at wavelength 680nm, 100 kHz frequency, and pulse duration of 200ns.

This method has an advantage in performing perovskite crystallization under ambient 20%-40% humidity to obtain the larger grains of perovskite as compared to one step procedure in the same environment   $  [38]  $  . The purpose of this study is to examine the effect of PVP interlayer on improvement of device performance by reducing recombination.

As mentioned earlier, the photo active material,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , is very sensitive to moisture. After reacting with water, the methyl ammonium cation is eliminated from the framework of perovskite, leading to the decomposition of the 3D structure. Therefore, color of the cell (perovskite film) changes from dark brown to yellow   $  {}^{[47]}  $   (  $  PbI_{2}  $   color) after exposing to humidity. The extent of degradation in a cell can be easily detected by color changes in the film, which can be quantitatively measured by optical absorption spectroscopy. The time dependent variations in UV-Vis absorption spectra   $  {}^{[48]}  $   of both unmodified and PVP modified samples were studied to observe the direct effect of humidity on perovskite films, as the quality of perovskite layer majorly affects the device performance, as shown in Figure 5a and 5b respectively. For UV-Visible spectroscopy measurement, both FTO/  $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite and FTO/  $  TiO_{2}  $  /Perovskite/PVP substrates were prepared and the measurement was taken on the samples immediately after preparation (referred as day 0) and after storing them for 7, 15, 23 and 30 days under open atmosphere. The time dependent absorption spectrum peak of unmodified substrate was shifted towards the shorter wavelength, as displayed in Figure 5a. The onset wavelength  $  ^{[49]}  $   (0 day) at 780nm shifted to blue side and eventually the absorption edge was found to be approximately at 530 nm, matching the absorption spectrum of   $  PbI_{2}  $  . This confirmed that the unmodified perovskite film degraded to   $  PbI_{2}  $   when stored in open air atmosphere (humidity 50%) for about 23 days. In contrast to that, the time dependent UV-Vis spectra of the PVP modified perovskite film showed almost no change with time, which clearly indicated that the stability of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film was enhanced remarkably by PVP modification. # CONCLUSION:

In this study, we demonstrated the effect of deposition of a hydrophobic polymer containing the Lewis base side chain (i.e. PVP) as an interlayer between the perovskite film and HTL on the cell performance. The thin PVP layer made by spin-coating method (a) decreased the non-radiative recombination by reducing the surface trap states accomplished by its Lewis base side chain, (b) acted as an insulating barrier to suppress the interfacial charge recombination, which

significantly improved the open circuit voltage and device performance, and advantageously (c) prevented water diffusion into the perovskite lattice due to its hydrophobic property, which remarkably enhanced the stability of PSC. After such modification, the device efficiency of 15% was observed in comparison to the control device having 11.2% efficiency. In addition, the PVP modified perovskite cells showed significant improvement in long term stability against moisture (under 50% humidity), retaining 85% of the initial efficiency after 30 days. By this simple and effective interfacial modification, high voltage perovskite cell with high stability (without further encapsulation) has been achieved.
