# Performance improvement of inverted two-dimensional perovskite solar cells using a non-fullerene acceptor as the trap passivator†

Zhihai Liu  $  ^{a}  $  and Eun-Cheol Lee  $  ^{*b}  $

Recently, Ruddlesden–Popper two-dimensional (2D) perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been intensively studied, owing to their high power-conversion-efficiency (PCE) and excellent long-term stability. In this work, we improved the performance of 2D PSCs by adding a non-fullerene acceptor (indacenodithiophene, IDIC) to the perovskite layer. We found that IDIC efficiently passivates the charge traps in the perovskite film and enhances the electron transport properties of PSCs. After adding 1.5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  IDIC to the perovskite layer, the average PCE of the 2D PSCs significantly improved, from 10.19% to 12.78%, corresponding to the 25% enhancement. The PCE improvement was mainly caused by the simultaneous enhancement of the short-circuit current density and fill factor. Moreover, the long-term stability of the studied 2D PSCs also improved, with the PCE degradation after 10 days significantly reduced, from 14.9% to 9.5%, which can be attributed to the enhanced moisture resistance of the perovskite layer. The best 2D PSC with added IDIC exhibited a PCE of 13.23%, with a stable power output and negligible hysteresis. Our results demonstrate that the addition of IDIC to 2D perovskite is a simple and effective method for fabricating high-performance 2D PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Recently, organic-inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskite-based solar cells have been widely investigated and rapidly developed, owing to their advantages such as high power-conversion-efficiency (PCE), low cost, light weight, and convenient fabrication.  $  ^{1-4}  $   Recently, a certified PCE of 25.5% has been reported for single perovskite solar cells (PSCs),  $  ^{5}  $   making them very promising for future commercialization.

However, despite the high performance of PSCs, the problem of low environmental stability of their devices has to be solved, which strongly precludes the commercialization of PSCs.  $  ^{6,7}  $   In humid environments, conventional 3-dimensional (3D) perovskites (for instance,   $  MAPbX_{3}  $  ,   $  MA = CH_{3}NH_{3}  $  , X = Cl, Br, or I) can be easily converted into hydrated perovskite phases, and eventually decompose to   $  PbI_{2}  $  .  $  ^{8,9}  $   To overcome this challenge, novel and more stable perovskite materials are needed. Consequently, all-inorganic perovskite materials (for instance,   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   and   $  CsPbI_{3}  $  ) have been used for fabricating PSCs, in which MA was replaced by cesium.  $  ^{8,10,11}  $   However, the band gap of all-inorganic perovskites is in the 1.8–2.3 eV range, which is

much wider than that (1.5 eV) of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  .   $  ^{2,8,10,11}  $   The poor light absorption property of these perovskites further limits the photovoltaic performance of relevant PSCs.  $  ^{8,10,11}  $   Recently, low-dimensional perovskite materials with high light absorption coefficient have been intensively studied.  $  ^{12-22}  $   For example, Ruddlesden–Popper 2D perovskites, particularly   $  (\mathrm{BA})_{2}(\mathrm{MA})_{n-1}-\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{3n+1}  $   (n is an integer and BA is   $  \mathrm{CH}_{3}(\mathrm{CH}_{2})_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}  $  ) series, have been widely used in PSCs, owing to their tunable band gaps and high intrinsic stability.  $  ^{14-22}  $   Although the PCE (<15%) of   $  (\mathrm{BA})_{2}(-\mathrm{MA})_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{3n+1}  $  -based 2D PSCs is still lower than that of conventional 3D PSCs, their long-term stability is significantly better.  $  ^{1-4,14-22}  $   Therefore, further improving the performance of 2D PSCs is highly desired for satisfying their commercialization requirements.

In conventional 3D PSCs, MAI is typically easily released from perovskite at high temperatures, leaving vacancies in the perovskite crystal.  $  ^{23-25}  $   Thus, passivating these defects in perovskites using molecules could significantly improve the PCE and stability of 3D PSCs, which has been a method of choice in recent years.  $  ^{23-25}  $   For example, Shao et al. introduced phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) as a defect passivator into inverted 3D PSCs, dramatically improving the PCE and reducing the hysteresis effect in PSCs.  $  ^{26}  $   Li et al. used an ultrathin PCBM layer to passivate trap states of MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $   perovskite, which leads to an improved PCE from 17.23% to 20.07%.  $  ^{27}  $   Zheng et al. reported the use of choline chloride and choline iodide for passivating the ionic defects of perovskites, thus significantly improving the PCE, from 17.1% to 20.0%, for

MAPbI $ _3 $ -based PSCs. $ ^{28} $  On the other hand, owing to their easily tunable energy levels, broadened absorption, and facile synthesis process, fused-ring non-fullerene-based electron acceptors have been demonstrated as good electron transport layers (ETLs) and defect passivators for fabricating PSCs. $ ^{29-38} $  For example, Jiang et al. used combined TiO $ _2 $  and 3,9-bis(2-methylene-(3-(1,1-dicyanomethylene)-indanone))-5,5,11,11-tetrakis(4-hexylphenyl)-dithieno[2,3-d:2',3'-d']-s-indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b'] dithiophene (ITIC) as double ETLs, enhancing the PCE of PSCs up to 20.08%. $ ^{34} $  Qin et al. added 3,9-bis(2-methylene-(3-(1,1-dicyanomethylene)-indanone))-5,5,11,11-tetrakis(5-hexylthienyl)-dithieno[2,3-d:2',3'-d']-s-indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b']dithiophene (ITIC-Th) into perovskite films as a defect passivator and perovskite stabilizer, improving the PCE (from 18.07% to 19.20%) and long-term stability of PSCs. $ ^{35} $  Compared with ITIC and ITIC-Th, indacenodithiophene (IDIC) is another fused-ring-based non-fullerene acceptor with even higher electron mobility ( $ \mu_{\text{electron}} $ ,  $ \sim $ 1.1  $ \times $  10 $ ^{-3} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ ). $ ^{36-38} $  As a result, IDIC has proven to be an excellent ETL and defect passivator, in both standard and inverted 3D PSCs. $ ^{36,37} $  Considering the relatively low PCE of 2D PSCs, it is very important to investigate the effect of defect passivation on the performance of 2D PSCs.

In this work, we report the use of a non-fullerene acceptor IDIC as a defect passivator, for improving the performance of   $  (\mathrm{BA})_{2}(\mathrm{MA})_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{4}\mathrm{I}_{13}  $  -based 2D PSCs. Following the addition of IDIC to perovskite films, the trap density significantly reduced from   $  7.3 \times 10^{15}  $   to   $  2.3 \times 10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  , owing to the Lewis base nature of IDIC, which allows it to form coordinate bonds with under-coordinated Pb in perovskite. The electron mobility of the 2D perovskite layer also increased, from   $  5.3 \times 10^{-4}  $   to   $  7.8 \times 10^{-4}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $  , with the addition of IDIC. Consequently, the average PCE of the studied 2D PSCs significantly improved, from 10.19% to 12.78%, which was mainly owing to the simultaneous enhancement of the short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) and the fill factor (FF). Moreover, the long-term stability of the studied 2D PSCs also improved, with the PCE degradation significantly reduced, from 14.9% to 9.5%, after 10 days of storage; this was attributed to the enhanced moisture resistance of the perovskite films. The best 2D PSC based on IDIC exhibited a high PCE of 13.23% with a stable power output and negligible hysteresis. Our results suggest that IDIC-induced defect passivation is an effective technique for improving the performance of 2D PSCs.

# 3. Results and discussion

The SEM cross-section image in Fig. 1(b) reveals the well-condensed layer-by-layer structure of the fabricated 2D PSCs. The SEM image indicates that the thicknesses of the perovskite and PCBM layers were approximately 350 nm and 55 nm, respectively. Fig. 1(c) shows the energy alignment in the fabricated PSCs, which indicates that electrons can be transported through IDIC to PCBM and collected by the Ag cathode, while holes are transported to PTAA and collected by the ITO anode. The lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level of IDIC is -3.9 eV,  $  ^{38}  $  which is slightly lower than that of perovskite (-3.8 eV).  $  ^{15}  $  A suitable energy alignment ensures efficient electron extraction from the perovskite absorber to PCBM, using IDIC. Fig. 1(d) shows the molecular structure of IDIC, which is a π-conjugated non-fullerene acceptor with a Lewis base (C=O and C≡N) at the ends of the IDIC molecule.  $  ^{36,37}  $

As shown in Fig. 4(a), the calculated slopes for the dependence of   $  J_{sc}  $   on the light intensity, for the PSCs without and with IDIC were 0.92 and 0.99, respectively, indicating suppressed bimolecular recombination in the PSCs.  $  ^{46,47}  $   As discussed above, the use of IDIC in perovskite could facilitate electron transport from perovskite to PCBM (Fig. 1(c)), weakening the recombination at the interface of perovskite/PCBM. The dependence of   $  V_{oc}  $   on the light intensity in Fig. 4(d) shows that the PSCs without IDIC exhibited a high slope of 1.67 kT/q, where k is the Boltzmann constant, T is the absolute temperature, and q is the elementary charge. On the other hand, the PSCs with IDIC exhibited a shallower slope of 1.38 kT/q, indicating the reduced trap-assisted recombination in the PSCs.  $  ^{46,47}  $   This can also be explained by the trap passivation effect of IDIC, which reduced   $  n_{trap}  $   and facilitated the charge transport in the PSCs. The analysis of SCLC, EIS, and light intensity dependence results explained well the improved   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF of the studied PSCs. Moreover, the   $  V_{oc}  $   of the PSCs can be described by the Shockley equation:  $  ^{48-50}  $

$$

# 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we improved the performance of inverted 2D PSCs by adding a non-fullerene acceptor IDIC into their perovskite layers. The coordinate bonding of IDIC with undercoordinated Pb in perovskite can efficiently passivate vacancy defects, which is beneficial for charge transport in PSCs. The average PCE of the studied 2D PSCs significantly improved, from  $ 10.19\% $  to  $ 12.78\% $ , which was mainly attributed to the simultaneous improvement of  $ J_{sc} $  and FF. The SCLC, PL, and EIS analyses demonstrate efficient trap passivation and enhanced charge transfer by using IDIC. The light intensity dependence results indicate suppressed charge recombination in the PSCs upon using IDIC. The long-term stability of the studied 2D PSCs also improved, with the PCE degradation significantly reduced from  $ 14.9\% $  to  $ 9.5\% $  after 10 days, which could be attributed to the enhanced moisture resistance of perovskite films. The best 2D PSC based on IDIC exhibited a high PCE of  $ 13.23\% $  with a stable power output and negligible hysteresis. Our results indicate that using IDIC as a defect passivator in 2D perovskite films is an effective way to improve the performance of 2D PSCs.
