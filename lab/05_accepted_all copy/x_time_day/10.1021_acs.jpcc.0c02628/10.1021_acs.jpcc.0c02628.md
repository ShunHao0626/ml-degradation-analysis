# Interface Modification for Enhanced Efficiency and Stability Perovskite Solar Cells

Haoyue Li, Qintao Wang, Haimin Li*, Jia Zhuang, Heng Guo, Xingchong Liu, Hanyu Wang Ronghong Zheng, Xiaoli Gong

School of New Energy and Materials, Southwest Petroleum University, Chengdu 610500, P. R. China

*Corresponding author: Haimin Li, Email: lucialee@126.com (H.L.), Tel: (+86)28-83037409

Abstract: As superstar of photovoltaic devices, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have appealed plenty of interests due to their superior characters. However, many defects, such as carrier recombination, inferior stability, poor interface contact, have prevent its further development. Here, we demonstrate a novel approach of interface engineering to form a compact perovskite layer with decreased defects on  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  film by adding Tris(pentafluorophenyl)boron (TPFPB) as interfacial modification layer, which validly improves the interface performance and enhances the crystallinity of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ . Hence the planar  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  PSCs with TPFPB modification show fast charge transfer and low trap state density with an enhanced champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) from the original of  $ 16.92\% $  to  $ 19.41\% $ , as well as long-term stability with  $ 80.7\% $  of its initial PCE after  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  aging in  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $  atmosphere without encapsulation, while the pristine one only shows  $ 68.9\% $  of original PCE. The results reveal that TPFPB can be used as an effective interface modification layer for high efficiency and stability PSCs, and it maybe also be used in other devices due to its superior interface modification for high quality crystallinity thin films.

Meantime, several effective ETLs have been attempted in PSCs, such as   $  SnO_{2}  $ , PCBM, ZnO and so on. Among all of these materials,   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL is the most promising alternative to   $  TiO_{2}  $   for its wide optical bandgap (3.6-4.0 eV), high bulk electron mobility (240 cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $ ), preferable band alignment with perovskite and low-temperature process.  $  ^{5}  $   The efficiency based on   $  SnO_{2}  $  -planar PSCs has been reported over 23%.  $  ^{6}  $   However, achieving high performance and stability of   $  SnO_{2}  $  -PSCs remains huge challenge. Factors such as low electron mobility, weak chemical interaction, interface and irradiative recombination would affect the optical properties of PSCs.  $  ^{7-8}  $   The interface recombination caused by the poor interface between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite is the mainly issue to restrain the further development of the structure. Therefore, enhancing the transport of carriers between the interfaces of perovskite and   $  SnO_{2}  $ , minimizing the charge recombination, have become increasingly significant.  $  ^{9-10}  $

In recent years, some strategies, such as additive engineering and interfacial modification, have been demonstrated to reduce the irradiative recombination and trap-state density in PSCs. For example, Guojia Fang and co-works reported the doped Mg into   $  SnO_{2}  $   as an electron selective layer (ESL), which suppressed the carrier recombination and improved the extraction of electrons from perovskite to ESLs. Tris(pentafluorophenyl)boron(TPFPB), a fluorine-containing electron acceptor, is usually used as an additive to accept anion in electrolyte in lithium batteries, and have been proved that it can effectively enhance the crystallinity and stability of the electrolyte, and also improve the   $  Li^{+}  $  transference numbers.  $  ^{12-13}  $   In recent years, TPFPB was used as a dopant in hole transport layer in PSCs.  $  ^{14-15}  $   And also, new study has found that TPFPB is also suitable for perovskite surface passivation. Moisture stability was also tested under RH 70±5%, the PSCs with optimized TPFPB interfacial modification layer reveal much better humidity stability, indicating that TPFPB interface modification between   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL is an effective way to greatly improve the efficiency and the stability for PSCs. The results demonstrate that TPFPB can be used as superior interface modification layer, and it may also be used in other devices for it can greatly restrain the interface defects.

# 2.1 Materials and reagents

Stannic oxide was purchased from Alfa Aesa (15% in   $  H_{2}O  $   colloidal dispersion). N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%), chlorobenzene (CB, 99.9%) and acetonitrile (99.9%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. TPFPB (97%) was bought from Aladdin. methylamine iodine (MAI, 99.98%), lead iodine (  $  PbI_{2}  $  , 99.98%), lithium salt (Li-TFSI, 99%), Spiro-OMeTAD (99.8%) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP, 96%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. All the materials and reagents were used without further purification.

# 2.2 Device fabrication

The ITO glass was washed in demonized water, acetone and ethanol for 15 min, sequentially. Then the ITO was exposed to UV-ozone for 20 min. After that, the clean ITO was spin coated with   $  SnO_{2}  $   solution and annealed at   $  150^{\circ}C  $   for 30 min. After the spin coating, the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film was fabricated, followed by spinning the TPFPB-IPA solution with the same method, and then heated the TPFPB layer at   $  100^{\circ}C  $   for 20 min. The concentrations of TPFPB were 0, 10, 12 and 14 mg   $  ml^{-1}  $  , respectively. After that, perovskite layer was prepared by anti-solvent method: the solution of perovskite precursor was applied to the substrate by drops, and CB was added to the sample after rotating to 8-15s. After the rotary coating, the black and bright perovskite film can be obtained by heating the plate at   $  100^{\circ}C  $   for 20 min. Perovskite precursor solution was prepared by dissolving 0.5532 mg   $  PbI_{2}  $   and 0.1908 mg MAI in 1mL DMF. The hole transport layer was prepared by spiral-coating doped Spiro-OMeTAD chlorobenzene solution (72.3 mg Spiro-OMeTAD, 28.8 ml TPB and 17.5 mmL lithium acetonitrile solution dissolved in 1.0 ml chlorobenzene). Finally, 90 nm Ag electrode was deposited onto the Spiro-OMeTAD coated film by sputtering technique to finish the whole device fabrication process.

In summary, TPFPB has successfully modified the interface between  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite and  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ETL, which greatly enhanced the morphology and crystallinity of perovskite layers, decreased the trap density and irradiation recombination of carriers.
