# Simultaneous Power Conversion Efficiency and Stability Enhancement of   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $   Lead-Free Inorganic Perovskite Solar Cell through Adopting a Multifunctional Dye Interlayer

Xiaoqing Yang, Yonghui Chen, Pengyun Liu, Huimin Xiang, Wei Wang,* Ran Ran, Wei Zhou, and Zongping Shao*

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are highly promising next-generation photovoltaic devices because of the cheap raw materials, ideal band gap of  $ \approx 1.5 $  eV, broad absorption range, and high absorption coefficient. Although lead-based inorganic-organic PSC has achieved the highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 25.2\% $ , the toxic nature of lead and poor stability strongly limits the commercialization. Lead-free inorganic PSCs are potential alternatives to toxic and unstable organic-inorganic PSCs. Particularly, double-perovskite  $ \mathrm{Cs_2AgBiBr_6} $ -based PSC has received interests for its all inorganic and lead-free features. However, the PCE is limited by the inherent and extrinsic defects of  $ \mathrm{Cs_2AgBiBr_6} $  films. Herein, an effective and facile strategy is reported for improving the PCE and stability by introducing an N719 dye interlayer, which plays multifunctional roles such as broadening the absorption spectrum, suppressing the charge carrier recombination, accelerating the hole extraction, and constructing an appropriate energy level alignment. Consequently, the optimizing cell delivers an outstanding PCE of  $ 2.84\% $ , much improved as compared with other  $ \mathrm{Cs_2AgBiBr_6} $ -based PSCs reported so far in the literature. Moreover, the N719 interlayer greatly enhances the stability of PSCs under ambient conditions. This work highlights a useful strategy to boost the PCE and stability of lead-free  $ \mathrm{Cs_2AgBiBr_6} $ -based PSCs simultaneously, accelerating the commercialization of PSC technology.

Actually, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has increased quickly to 25.2% in the last 8 years.  $  ^{[3]}  $  However, many issues including current–voltage hysteresis, poor stability, toxicity of lead, and inferior water resistance are stumbling blocks to the commercial road for PSCs.  $  ^{[4,5]}  $  Refer to above challenges, all-inorganic lead-free perovskites were proposed considering their better stability and environmental friendliness.

In this work, we put forward a simple strategy for improving the PCE and operating stability of   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $  -based PSC without the need to change the available matured method for cell fabrication. The key point for current strategy is to apply an interlayer of N719 dye (di-tetrabutylammonium cis-bis(isothiocyanato) bis(2,2'-bipyridyl-4,4' dicarboxylato) ruthenium (II)) on the surface of   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $   films. As a result, a PCE up to 2.84% (short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ): 5.13 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ; open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ): 1.06 V) was reached by the   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $  -based PSC with the N719 dye interlayer. In addition, the obtained cells showed obviously improved stability against the surrounding atmosphere. Our work provides an efficient and convenient approach of improving the photoelectric efficiency and operational stability of   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $  -based lead-free PSCs, which may also be applicable to other perovskite materials.

It shows that the average PCE of the PSCs with the N719 dye interlayer was improved obviously as compared to the dye-free ones. It is well known that the PCE of a PSC is determined by many factors such as the sunlight absorption capability, energy level alignment of different layers, photo-generated carrier transport properties, and recombination behavior as well as the defect density. The broadened absorption wavelength ending point of EQE spectrum suggests the additional light absorption capability of the N719 dye, which was also confirmed by the absorption profiles of the   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $   films with and without the N719 dye interlayer (Figure 2f). The   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $   exhibited a sharp absorption peak at about 438 nm and a weak tailing absorption edge at around 610 nm due to the indirect band gap property of   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $ , which led to the lower   $  J_{sc}  $   of the devices. After the introduction of the N719 dye interlayer, the sunlight absorption intensity was enhanced obviously. We also measured the absorption capability of pure N719 film as shown in Figure S8, Supporting Information, a much broader adsorption wavelength was found as compared to the   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $   perovskite. Therefore, the improvement of   $  J_{sc}  $   and PCE of   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}/N719  $   film-based PSCs was partially due to the contribution of additional sunlight absorption of the N719 dye.

It is well known that a proper energy level alignment of the different layers in PSC is critical to accelerate the hole extraction process, which will then enhance the PCE of PSC.  $  ^{[31]}  $   Figure 1b displays the energy band diagrams of the different layers in the dye-modified PSC. Here, the position of Fermi level (  $  E_{F}  $  ) and valence band maximum (  $  E_{VBM}  $  ) of the as-prepared   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $   film was determined by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurement as shown in Figure S9, Supporting

Information, which was turned out to be -4.08 and -6.13 eV, respectively. Figure S10, Supporting Information shows the Tauc plot of the   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $   film from which phonon assisted energies of 1.74 and 2.30 eV were determined. Therefore, the indirect band gap of the prepared   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $   film in this study was 2.02 eV (an average of the phonon assisted energies). The position of conduction band minimum (  $  E_{CBM}  $  ) of -4.11 eV was then calculated according to the relationship: band gap =   $  E_{CBM} - E_{VBM}  $  . The energy band structures of other materials were obtained from the literature.  $  ^{[24,32]}  $   It was found the energy band structure between   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $   (  $  E_{VBM}, E_{CBM}: -6.13, -4.11  $   eV) and Spiro-OMeTAD (  $  E_{VBM}, E_{CBM}: -5.20, -2.20  $   eV) was mismatched, which will hinder the hole extraction process in PSCs. The N719 dye is a conventional sunlight absorbing material in dye-sensitized solar cells with the   $  E_{VBM}  $   and   $  E_{CBM}  $   of -5.34 and -3.01 eV, respectively. According to suitable energy level alignment, the N719 interlayer will accelerate the hole extraction process between   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $   and Spiro-OMeTAD, and then enhance the PCE of   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $ -based PSC. Figure 1c,d shows the schematic diagram of charge transfer in the   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $ -based PSCs without and with the N719 interlayer, respectively. For the dye interlayer-free cell, there were many traps among perovskites particles, resulting in a serious charge carrier recombination at the interface. After introducing the N719 interlayer, more photogenerated electrons and holes were produced. In addition, the amount of surface traps was reduced and a suitable energy level diagram was formed, which effectively reduced the charge carrier combination and accelerated the hole extraction ultimately.

The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectrum and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements of   $  Cs_{2}Ag-BiBr_{6}  $   films deposited without and with the N719 interlayer were employed to understand the photo-generated charge carrier transport properties. Figure 3a shows the PL measurement spectra, reflecting the indirect band gap property due to the broad PL emission peak at around 607 nm, which was consistent with the Tauc plots. The PL emission peak intensity of   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $   film was reduced after the deposition of the N719 interlayer due to the enhanced hole extraction capability of the N719 dye. Moreover, the PL emission peak intensity of perovskite/N719/Spiro-OMeTAD film was lower than that of perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD, showing that the N719 interlayer accelerated the charge transfer from the perovskite to Spiro-OMeTAD, agreeing with the enhanced energy level alignment by the addition of N719 interlayer. As shown in the TRPL decay curves of various films (Figure S11, Supporting Information), the PL decay lifetimes were calculated by biexponential fitting and the corresponding   $  \tau  $   values were summarized in Table S3, Supporting Information. It was found that the average   $  \tau  $   value of the perovskite/N719/Spiro-OMeTAD film was the lowest one, indicating the charge extraction capability was enhanced after the addition of the N719 interlayer, which was in good agreement with the PL results in Figure 3a.

In general, the ideality factor  $  n  $  trends to unity for ideal solar cells. The calculated  $  n  $  values were 2.31 and 1.87 for the devices without and with the N719 interlayer, respectively, indicating that the introduction of the N719 interlayer reduced the intrinsic defect density and suppressed the Shockley–Read–Hall (SRH) recombination. Thus, the increase in the  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  value of the cell with the N719 interlayer can be attributed to the following two aspects. On the one hand, the introduction of the N719 interlayer between the  $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $  layer and Spiro-OMeTAD layer led to an appropriate energy level alignment, accelerating the hole extraction process. On the

other hand, the N719 interlayer passivated the surface defects of   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $  , reducing the charge carrier recombination. These two effects together with the additional sunlight absorption by the dye then contributed to the enhanced PCE of the cell after adopting the dye interlayer.

Humidity sensitivity of PSCs is a big concern for practical application. The long-term stability of both devices with and without the dye interlayer was also comparatively studied in the dry glovebox and in ambient air without encapsulation with the results shown in Figure 4. Both devices stored in   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glove box showed no significant change in the PCE. However, the PCE of the device without the N719 interlayer decreased to 75% of the initial PCE after storage in ambient air for 10 days (temperature: 20–30 °C; relative humidity: 30–40%), which was resulted from the decreased   $  J_{sc}  $   and   $  V_{oc}  $   (Figure S12, Supporting Information). On the other hand, the device with the N719 interlayer displayed no obvious degradation after storage in ambient air for 16 days at the same conditions, showing a much better moisture stability. The N719 interlayer likely hindered the water molecules to penetrate into the perovskite layer, thus increasing the moisture resistance of the PSC.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we have demonstrated that the introduction of a multifunctional N719 dye interlayer remarkably improved the PCEs and operational stability of   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $  -based PSCs simultaneously. The multifunctional N719 interlayer broadened the light absorption spectrum, reduced the charge carrier recombination, accelerated the hole extraction, passivated surface defects, and constructed an appropriate energy level alignment at the same time. As a result, the device optimized with the N719 interlayer showed a superior PCE of 2.84%, which was much higher than that of the device without the N719 interlayer. This superior PCE value is among the highest PCE values of double perovskite-based PSCs reported so far. Moreover, the devices with the N719 interlayer showed a much enhanced stability under the ambient conditions as compared with the pristine devices without the N719 interlayer. Our work provides an effective and convenient strategy for enhancing the PCE and operational stability of environmentally friendly lead-free   $  Cs_{2}AgBiBr_{6}  $  -based PSCs, which can accelerate the commercialization of PSC technology.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: CsBr (99.999%, Sigma-Aldrich), AgBr (99.8%, Alfa Aesar), BiBr₃ (99%, Alfa Aesar), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, anhydrous, 99.9%, Aladdin), N719 dye (Dyesol Industries Pty Ltd), 75% titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate in isopropoxide (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.999%), Li-bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI, 99.95%, Sigma-Aldrich), 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP, 96%, Aladdin), chlorobenzene (anhydrous, 99.9%, Aladdin). TiO₂ paste (30NR-D), Spiro-OMeTAD (99.8%), and Tris(2-(1H-pyrazol-1-yl)-4-tert-butylpyridine)-cobalt (III)Tris(bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide)) (FK209, 99.5%) were obtained from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. All these materials in experiments were used as received without any further purification.

Device Fabrication: The FTO substrate (7 Ω sq  $  ^{-1}  $  , 2.2 mm thick) were cleaned by acetone, isopropanol, deionized water, and ethanol for 30 min, respectively. The FTO substrate was treated with an oxygen plasma cleaner for 5 min before the usage. First, the compact TiO  $  _{2}  $   (c-TiO  $  _{2}  $  ) layer was deposited by spin-coating 0.15 M isopropanol solution of titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) at 3000 rpm for 30 s for two times and with a calcination at 500 °C for 30 min. Second, the m-TiO  $  _{2}  $   layer was deposited by spin-coating the TiO  $  _{2}  $   paste (30NR-D)/ethanol mixture (1:2 w/w) at 3000 rpm for 30 s on the surface of c-TiO  $  _{2}  $ , which was then calcined at 500 °C for 45 min. After that, the Cs  $  _{2}  $  AgBiBr  $  _{6}  $   precursor solution was synthesized by dissolving 0.2128 g of CsBr, 0.0939 g of AgBr, and 0.2244 g of BiBr  $  _{3}  $   in 1 mL of DMSO with stirring at 70 °C until the salts were fully dissolved. The as-prepared substrates were also treated by oxygen plasma as described before. The perovskite layer was deposited by two-step heating process. The substrate and perovskite precursor solution were preheated at 80 °C for at least 10 min before the spin-coating, and then was spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 30 s onto the m-TiO  $  _{2}  $   film, followed by an annealing at 280 °C for 5 min. The N719 interlayer was prepared onto the Cs  $  _{2}  $  AgBiBr  $  _{6}  $   film by spin-coating N719-ethanol solutions at 2000 rpm for 30 s and then heated at 80 °C for 5 min. All the above experimental procedures were conducted under ambient condition.

The substrate with above layers were placed to the dry glove box to prepare the hole transfer layer. 85 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  of Spiro-OMeTAD-chlorobenzene solution were prepared (additives: 28.5 μL of tBP, 19.3 μL Li-TFSI-acetonitrile solution (1.8 M), 13.9 μL FK209-acetonitrile solution (0.25 M) for 1 mL of chlorobenzene). The Spiro-OMeTAD–chlorobenzene solution was spin-coated onto the N719 interlayer at 4000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, ≈100 nm thick Ag electrodes were deposited on the topside by thermal evaporation. The active area of the devices is 0.0625 cm  $  ^{2}  $  in this study.

Characterization: XRD patterns were obtained with an X-Ray Diffractometer (Riguku. Co. Ltd.) using Cu Kα radiation in the range of 10–50° (2θ) at a scanning rate of 10° min  $  ^{-1}  $  . The top-view and cross-sectional morphologies were investigated by SEM (Hitachi S-4800) with an EDX (Octane Ultra). The XPS spectra was recorded by XPS spectrometer (PHI5000 Versa Probe) using an Al Kα X-ray source. The surface smoothness of films was obtained through AFM (Horiba, SmartSPM). The absorption curves were collected by a UV–vis-NIR spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer, Lambda 750S). The steady-state PL measurements were measured with a fluorescence spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer, FL6500) with an excitation wavelength of 418 nm. The time-resolved PL decay curves were performed on a fluorescence spectrophotometer (Edinburgh Instruments, FLS980). The J–V curves were performed under simulated AM 1.5 Solar Simulator (Zolix, SS150A) with a Keithley 2400 source meter. The sunlight intensity was calibrated with a certified reference silicon solar cell. The EQE was measured by the solar cell QE/IPCE measurement system (Zolix, Solar Cell Scan100). The EIS measurement was conducted by an electrochemical station (CHI760) and the measuring frequency ranged from 10  $  ^{6}  $  to 0.1 Hz.
