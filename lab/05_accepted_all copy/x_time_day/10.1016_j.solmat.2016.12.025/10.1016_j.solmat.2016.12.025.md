# 1. Introduction

Since the first report on solid state perovskite-sensitized solar cells in 2012, enormous attention has been focused on perovskite solar cells due to their high power conversion efficiency (PCE) attained by a low-cost and facile fabrication process, as a result, a PCE of 20.1% was certificated until 2014 [1-4]. In which,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   structure have attracted scientists' most interests because of their unique properties such as long charge carrier's diffusion length, strong light absorptivity, ambipolar charge transport, high open circuit voltage, and solution processibility [5-9]. In addition, the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  -based solar cells with p-i-n structure has been recognized and widely studied in previous. It should include metal electrode, electronic transport layer (ETL), hole transport layer (HTL) and perovskite active layer [10-13]. Several novel ETL and HTL materials have been investigated to improve the device performances, but the high quality   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film with optimized film morphology was still a key link for high device performances [14-16]. So far, the drawback for   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films in device can be summarized as: discontinuous coverage, pinholes and   $  PbI_{2}  $   residue in the film, which not only caused electrical shorting, but also deleteriously impacted charge dissociation/transport/recombination in devices [17-19].

In recent years, several methods have been investigated to construct   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films for excellent optoelectronic devices, such as: thermal evaporation deposition, chemical vapor deposition, solution spin-coating, and so on [20–22]. # 2. Experimental

Methylammonium iodide (MAI) was synthesized according to the literature reported in previous.   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   was synthesized in ice bath by adding hydroiodic acid (HI, 57 wt% in water, Sigma-Aldrich) into methylamine (33 wt% in ethanol, Sigma-Aldrich) and stirring for 2 h. The precipitate was washed by dissolving in a minimum amount of ethanol and then adding a large amount of diethyl ether. The precipitate was collected by filtration, then, the procedure was repeated three times to get pure MAI. The resulting product (white powder) was dried at 60 °C. N. N-dimethylformamide (DMF; 99.99%, Sigma-Aldrich) was dried by molecular sieve and deposited in anhydrous environment. 461 mg of Lead iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  , 99%, Sigma-Aldrich) was dissolved in 1 mL of DMF solution in a brown bottle at 70 °C under magnetic stirring. A clear and transparent   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution would be achieved after the   $  PbI_{2}  $   power was dissolved completely. Then, left it quietly on the flat table at room temperature for several hours, many needle-like yellow   $  PbI_{2}  $   crystals would gradually precipitate and cover the inside of the bottle following the standing time. After that, the   $  PbI_{2}  $   crystals was stirred and heated again until it turned to clear and transparent once more, which was ready for device fabrication. The process engineering are shown in Scheme 1.

FTO substrates were cleaned sequentially with detergent, deionized water, acetone, ethanol, and isopropyl alcohol under ultrasound for 10 min.   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer was deposited on the substrates by aerosol spray at 450 °C using a commercial titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) solution (75 wt% in 2-propanol, Sigma-Aldrich) diluted in ethanol (1:39, volume ratio) as precursor and oxygen as carrier gas. The mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer composed of 20-nm-sized particles was deposited by spin-coating at 5000 rpm for 30 s using a commercial   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste (Dyesol 18-NRT, Dyesol) diluted in ethanol (2:7, weight ratio). After drying at 125 °C, the   $  TiO_{2}  $   films were gradually heated to 500 °C, baked at this temperature for 15 min, which can be used after it turn down to room temperature.   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution obtained as described above was spin-coated on top of the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer at 4000 rpm for 30 s, then   $  PbI_{2}  $   film would be achieved after dried on 70 °C hot plate for 15 min. The substrate with   $  PbI_{2}  $   film was dipped into   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   solution in 2-propanol (10 mg ml  $  ^{-1}  $  ) for 40 s, drip washing with IPA solution and then dried on 70 °C hot plate for 30 min. The Spiro-OMeTAD-based hole-transfer layer (80 mg Spiro-OMeTAD, 28.5 ml 4-tert-butylpyridine and 17.5 ml lithium-bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) solution (520 mg Li-TFSI in 1 ml acetonitrile) all dissolved in 1 ml chlorobenzene) was deposited by spin coating at 4000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, a 100-nm-thick gold electrode layer was deposited by thermal evaporation. All device

# 4. Conclusions

An effective and simple method has been investigated to improve the performance of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  -based solar cells, the PCE of which can reach nearly 15% repeatedly in ambient conditions. The excellent device performance was attributed to the high quality   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films constructed by two-step method using the   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution after dissolution-crystallization-dissolution treatment. More important, excellent device stability was also exhibited, which was remained at more than 77% efficiency under 50% humidity for 10 days. In another, EIS measurements has been carried out to characterize and understand the charge transfer dynamics and microscopic mechanisms of PSCs.
