# 4-Tert-butylpyridine Free Organic Hole Transporting Materials for Stable and Efficient Planar Perovskite Solar Cells

Bo Xu, Zonglong Zhu, Jinbao Zhang, Hongbin Liu, Chu-Chen Chueh, Xiaosong Li, and Alex K.-Y. Jen*

4-Tert-butylpyridine (tBP) is an important additive in triarylamine-based organic hole-transporting materials (HTMs) for improving the efficiency and steady-state performance of perovskite solar cells (PVSCs). However, the low boiling point of tBP (196 °C) significantly affects the long-term stability and device performance of PVSCs. Herein, the design and synthesis of a series of covalently linked Spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene] (SFX)-based organic HTMs and pyridine derivatives to realize efficient and stable planar PVSCs are reported. One of the tailored HTMs, N2,N2,N7,N7-tetrakis(4-methoxyphenyl)-3',6'-bis(pyridin-4-ylmethoxy) spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene]-2,7-diamine (XPP) with two para-position substituted pyridines that immobilized on the SFX core unit shows a high power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 17.2% in planar CH₃NH₃PbI₃-based PVSCs under 100 mW cm⁻² AM 1.5G solar illumination, which is much higher than the efficiency of 5.5% that using the well-known 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxy-phenyl-amine)9,9'-spirobifluorene (SpiroOMeTAD) as HTM (without tBP) under the same condition. Most importantly, the pyridine-functionalized HTM-based PVSCs without tBP as additive show much better long-term stability than that of the state-of-the-art HTM SpiroOMeTAD-based solar cells that containing tBP as additive. This is the first case that the tBP-free HTMs are demonstrated in PVSCs with high PCEs and good stability. It paves the way to develop highly efficient and stable tBP-free HTMs for PVSCs toward commercial applications.

However, tBP also induced instability of the PVSC device because of its relatively low boiling point (196 °C), which is easily evaporated during device fabrication and long-term storage.  $  ^{[10]}  $   Han and co-workers further confirmed that tBP tends to corrode perovskite to form a new complex with PbI  $  _{2}  $  , leading to chemical decomposition of the perovskite and poor charge transport between the perovskite and HTL.  $  ^{[10]}  $   Although recent reports have demonstrated that tBP caused poor stability of PVSCs, very few works were presented to address this problem.  $  ^{[11]}  $   The presence of tBP in HTM is still necessary in order to achieve high-efficiency PVSCs.

In this work, we have designed and synthesized a series of spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene] (SFX)-based organic HTMs with covalently linked pyridine groups (Figure 1) to realize tBP free organic HTMs for stable and efficient planar PVSCs. One of the tailored HTMs N2,N2,N7,N7-tetrakis(4-methoxyphenyl)-3',6'-bis(pyridin-4-ylmethoxy) spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene]-2,7-diamine (XPP) with two para-substituted pyridines on the SFX core unit shows a high PCE of 17.2% in planar CH₃NH₃PbI₃-based PVSCs without tBP as additive, which is much higher than the PCE (5.5%) of device using well-known Spiro-OMeTAD as HTM (without tBP) under the same condition. In addition, the XPP-based devices also exhibit a remarkable PCE of 19.5% in mix-ion[12] [HC(NH₂)₂]₀.₈₅[CH₃NH₃]₀.₁₅Pb[I₀.₈₅Br₀.₁₅]₃-based planar PVSCs without using tBP. Most importantly, the PVSCs with pyridine functionalized HTM showed much better long-term stability than that of Spiro-OMeTAD-based solar cell containing tBP as additive. This work paves the way to develop high-efficiency and stable tBP-free HTMs for PVSCs toward commercial applications.

# 2. Results and Discussion

It has been shown that SFX is an excellent 3D core for constructing high-performance HTMs for PVSCs, due to its easy synthesis and excellent film-forming ability.  $  ^{[13]}  $   We have used SFX as the core to construct three pyridine-functionalized HTMs, termed N2,N2,N7,N7-tetrakis(4-methoxyphenyl)-3',6'-bis(pyridin-2-ylmethoxy)spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene]-2,7-diamine (XOP) (ortho-position), N2,N2,N7,N7-tetrakis(4-methoxyphenyl)-3',6'-bis(pyridin-3-ylmethoxy) spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene]-2,7-diamine (XMP) (meta-position), and XPP (para-position) as tBP-free HTMs for PVSCs. The phenyl-substituted one-3',6'-bis(benzyloxy)-N2,N2,N7,N7-tetrakis(4-methoxyphenyl)spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene]-2,7-diamine (XDB) and Spiro-OMeTAD were also used as reference in this study. The synthetic routes of the pyridine containing HTMs are quite straightforward as shown in Scheme 1. The core spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene]-3',6'-diol (SFX-2OH) was prepared via the condensation reaction of fluorenones and resorcinols using p-toluenesulfonic acid monohydrate as catalyst.  $  ^{[14]}  $   The final products were made by using the Buchwald–Hartwig cross-coupling reaction with the 4,4'-dimethoxydiphenylamine as starting materials.  $  ^{[15]}  $   The detailed synthetic information and materials characterization can be found in the Experimental Section;   $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR and high-resolution mass spectra (MALDI-TOF) are shown in Figure S1–S12 (Supporting Information).

This is also the first case that a tBP-free HTM has been demonstrated in PVSCs with such high PCE of 19.3%.

In order to determine the origin of the photovoltaic characteristics of the HTMs XOP, XMP, XPP, and Spiro-OMeTAD, the interface charge transfer between these HTMs and the perovskite film, as well as their hole-transporting property, were systematically investigated by steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence measurements. The charge collection efficiency can be affected by the hole transfer process at the perovskite/HTM interface. This process can be studied by steady-state PL, as displayed in Figure 4b. It is clearly shown that the pyridine substituted HTM-XOP, XMP, and XPP demonstrate lower PL intensity than the devices based on Spiro-OMeTAD. This indicates that the interfacial hole transfer between pyridine-based HTMs and perovskite is more efficient than that of Spiro-OMeTAD/perovskite interface. As shown in Figure 4c, the time-resolved PL measurements further verified the improved hole transfer using XPP as HTM, which introduces a shorten PL decay time (  $  \tau  $  ) of 27.68 ns compared to that of the Spiro-OMeTAD/perovskite film with a   $  \tau  $   of 43.23 ns (Table S1, Supporting Information). These results further highlight the superior hole extraction and charge dissociation from perovskite to the newly designed HTMs.

To better identify the chemical and electrical interactions among different HTM molecules with MAPbI $ _3 $  surface, we have utilized a combined cluster and periodic model approach to reveal the HTM/perovskite interaction, especially the chemical coordination between the nitrogen atom of the pyridine on the HTM molecular and the exposed Pb atom of perovskite clusters surface. $ ^{[6,18]} $  The binding energy levels of the HTMs were calculated with perovskite, respectively, and the detailed simulation results can be obtained in the Experimental Section (Supporting Information). Due to the steric hindrance effect of the molecular geometry, both the pyridine's nitrogen atoms of XPP can easily form a strong chemical bonding with the Pb atoms, while the XMP and XOP molecule can only contribute one nitrogen atom to bind with Pb atom. As shown in Figure 4a, the simulation results show that XPP exhibits the largest binding energy with a value of 144.71 kcal mol $ ^{-1} $  with the perovskite, while XOP and XMP molecules only have the value of 86.77 and 96.39 kcal mol $ ^{-1} $ , respectively.

The strong binding correlation and enhanced interfacial coupling between our designed HTMs and perovskite should passivate surface traps through the pyridine substation and further reduce the charge recombination. However, the nonpyridine substituted HTM-XDB and Spiro-OMeTAD do not have this additional interaction between hole-transporting molecules and the perovskite surface. This result could also explain why the devices with para-substituted pyridine HTM-XPP exhibit higher PCE than those of the ortho- and meta-immobilized XOP and XMP-based solar cells. These efficiencies are also much higher than those of the nonpyridine-substituted HTM-XDB and the Spiro-OMeTAD-based devices without tBP additive.

Stability of the devices based on new HTMs is very important for PVSCs. It has been previously studied that the tBP can control the morphology of the HTMs and avoid the lithium salt aggregation in the HTM film as well as enhance the performance of the related devices. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, a series of SFX-based organic HTMs substituted with different pyridine groups were successfully designed/synthesized and applied as tBP-free HTMs in PVSCs. One of the tailored HTMs XPP with two para-position substituted pyridine shows a high PCE of 17.2% in tBP-free planar

$ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  -based PVSCs under  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $  AM 1.5G solar illumination, which is clearly much higher than that of the efficiency of  $ 5.5\% $  that using the well-known Spiro-OMeTAD as HTM (without tBP) under the same condition. In addition, XPP-based devices also exhibit a record remarkable PCE of  $ 19.5\% $  in mix-ion-based planar PVSCs without tBP as additive. Furthermore, the results of steady-state PL and transient photocurrent decay measurements clearly indicate that pyridine-functionalized HTMs have comparably much better hole extracting and transporting capability, leading to better photovoltaic performance. Most importantly, the pyridine-functionalized HTM-based PVSCs showed much better long-term stability than that of the state-of-the-art HTM Spiro-OMeTAD-based solar cell that containing tBP as additive. This work paves the way to develop high-efficiency and stable tBP-free HTMs for PVSCs toward commercial applications.

Device Fabrication: The  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  precursor solution was prepared by dissolving  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  (MAI) and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  with a molar ratio of  $ 1\mathrm{M}:1.08\mathrm{M} $  in DMF and DMSO (volume ratio of 4:1). The  $ [\mathrm{HC}(\mathrm{NH}_2)_2]_{0.85}[\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3]_{0.15}\mathrm{Pb}[i_{0.85}\mathrm{Br}_{0.15}]_3 $  solution was mixed with  $ \mathrm{HC}(\mathrm{NH}_2)_2\mathrm{I} $  (FAI),  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ ,  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{Br} $  (MABr), and  $ \mathrm{PbBr}_2 $  in a mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO solution (volume ratio 4:1) with the molar concentration of  $ 1.35\mathrm{M} $   $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  ( $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{PbBr}_2 $ , the molar ratio of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 / \mathrm{PbBr}_2 = 85 / 15 $ ,  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 / \mathrm{FAI} = 1.05 $ ,  $ \mathrm{PbBr}_2 / \mathrm{MABr} = 1 / 1 $ ). The typical synthesised HTM solution such as XPP was prepared  $ 50~\mathrm{mg~mL^{-1}} $  in dry chlorobenzene by adding  $ 10~\mu \mathrm{L} $  Li-TFSI/acetonitrile ( $ 170~\mathrm{mg~mL^{-1}} $ ) additives. The Spiro-OMeTAD solution was prepared  $ 70~\mathrm{mg~mL^{-1}} $  in chlorobenzene by adding  $ 10~\mu \mathrm{L} $  Li-TFSI/acetonitrile ( $ 170~\mathrm{mg~mL^{-1}} $ ) and  $ 10~\mu \mathrm{L} $  tBP solution additives. All solutions were stirred at  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  overnight and filtered with a  $ 0.45~{\mu\mathrm{m}} $  PTFE filter before use. All chemicals and reagents were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used as received from commercial sources without purification and purchased.

Indium tin oxide (ITO)-coated glass substrates were cleaned with isopropanol, acetone, and distilled water under ultrasonic 20 min, respectively. These substrates were treated with UV–ozone for 20 min before use. An ≈30 nm thick SnO₂ compact layer was deposited onto the substrates by spin-coating an ethanol solution with 0.2 M SnCl₂ at 4000 rpm and 60 s, then followed by thermal annealing 180 °C for 1 h. The PCBM layer (10 mg mL⁻¹ in chlorobenzene) was deposited on the prepared SnO₂ layer by spin-coating at 3000 rpm for 30 s and 4000 rpm for 5 s. Both the perovskite precursor solutions prepared above were spin-coated onto the prepared SnO₂/PCBM substrates at 1500 rpm for 15 s and 5000 rpm for 45 s. During the last 15 s of the second spin-coating step, the substrate was treated with chlorobenzene drop-casting (0.1 mL). The as-spun films were then annealed at 100 °C for ≈45 min. Next, HTM solution such as the synthesised HTM and Spiro-OMeTAD was spin-coated on top of the perovskite layer at 4000 rpm for 30 s. All

of the prepared films were then annealed at   $  100\ °C  $   for 10 min. Finally, a 10 nm think of   $  MoO_{3}  $   and 70 nm thick top Ag electrode was evaporated under high vacuum (  $  <2\times10^{-7}  $   Torr). For all studied devices, the device area was defined as   $  11.2\ mm^{2}  $   by a metal shadow mask.

Device Characterization: The J-V curves in this study were recorded by using Keithley 2400 source measurement unit and the scan rate was kept at 0.05 V s  $  ^{-1}  $  . The photocurrent was measured under illumination from a 450 W thermal Oriel solar simulator (AM 1.5G). The EQE spectra were measured by combining a monochromated 450 W xenon lamp (Oriel) with a sourcemeter (Keithley 2400) and calculated using a calibrated Si photodiode (OSI-Optoelectronics). The SCLC method was used to test the charge-carrier mobilities (  $  \mu  $  ) calculated from the J-V characteristics using the Mott–Gurney equation. The steady-state PL spectra were measured by using a Horiba Fluorolog FL-3 instrument with an excitation wavelength of 550 nm. The time-resolved PL was measured by laser diode with a wavelength   $  \lambda = 375  $   nm; the combined (source + instrumental response function) time resolution was   $  \Delta t \approx 0.13  $   ns. The surface morphology of samples was characterized using SEM-FEI-Sirion-XL30.
