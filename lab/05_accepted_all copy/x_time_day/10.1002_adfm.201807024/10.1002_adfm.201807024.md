# Highly Efficient Sn/Pb Binary Perovskite Solar Cell via Precursor Engineering: A Two-Step Fabrication Process

Xiaomei Lian, Jiehuan Chen, Yingzhu Zhang, Minchao Qin, Jun Li, Shuoxun Tian, Weitao Yang, Xinhui Lu, Gang Wu,* and Hongzheng Chen*

Regulation of the crystallization of perovskite films and avoiding the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   during the deposition process are very important for achieving Sn/Pb binary perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) with high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and producibility. In this work, a high-quality   $  HC(NH_{2})_{2}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   (  $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $  ) film deposited from the two-step solution process by introducing methylammonium thiocyanate (MASCN) as a bifunctional additive into the precursor solution containing   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  SnI_{2}  $   is reported. MASCN can not only tune the morphology of the perovskite film but also stabilize the precursor solution via retarding the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   through a strong coordination between   $  SCN^{-}  $  and   $  Sn^{2+}  $ . The Sn/Pb binary inverted PVSCs based on   $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   present a high fill factor of 0.79 and the best PCE of 16.26% in the case of 0.25 MASCN addition. The device fabrication producibility is also greatly improved due to the stabilized precursor solution with the aid of MASCN. The PCE of the device is almost independent of the storage time of the precursor solution within 124 d in the   $  N_{2}  $ -filled glove box. These results indicate that the precursor engineering with multifunctionality additive is an effective approach toward highly efficient and producible PVSCs for future commercialization.

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites have emerged as a revolutionary class of photovoltaic materials.  $  ^{[1-9]}  $   The Pb-based perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) have achieved an impressive power conversion efficiency (PCE) over 23.2%  $  ^{[10]}  $   in the past few years. Considering the toxicity and the resulting threat to environment and human health, fabricating Pb-free or low-Pb PVSCs has attracted increasing attention in recent years. The oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   induced self p-type doping and the resulting intensified recombination will be harmful to the performance of the Sn/Pb binary PVSCs.  $  ^{[29,30]}  $   Prevention of the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   is one of the key issues for fabrication of the Sn/Pb binary perovskite film with high quality and producibility.

The crystallization of the Sn/Pb binary perovskite film fabricated by the one-step solution-process method can be tuned by precursor engineering.  $  ^{[30-33]}  $   The single-junction Sn/Pb binary PVSCs based on   $  (\mathrm{FASnI}_{3})_{0.6}(\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3})_{0.4}  $   have achieved a PCE of 18.03%.  $  ^{[22]}  $   Besides, both adding   $  SnF_{2}  $   additives  $  ^{[34]}  $   and hydrazine   $  (\mathrm{N}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{4})  $   treatment  $  ^{[12]}  $   can retard the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $  . Recently, Jen and co-workers found that ascorbic acid (AA) can simultaneously retard   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation and modulate the crystallization of Sn/Pb binary perovskite. The PVSCs based on the   $  MA_{0.5}FA_{0.5}Pb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{3}  $   film with AA assisted one-step deposition exhibited a PCE of 14.01%.  $  ^{[35]}  $

Compared to the one-step method, two-step deposition process was regarded to be helpful to control the morphology of the perovskite films because the uniform and full coverage   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer could be easily obtained.  $  ^{[36,37]}  $   For the Sn/Pb binary

Herein,   $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   film was prepared by a two-step solution-processed method. Considering that the high reactivity of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   is the origination of the difficulties in crystallization regulation and oxidation prevention, we introduced methylammonium thiocyanate (MASCN), for the first time, into the precursor solution containing   $  PbI_{2}/SnI_{2}  $  . MASCN can act as strong ligand, coordinating with   $  Sn^{2+}  $  , to avoid the direct contact between   $  Sn^{2+}  $   and reactants (organic ammonium and oxygen) and reduce the corresponding reaction activity. Dual effects of MASCN in tuning the film morphology and preventing the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   was expected. The   $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   film, with optimized amount of MASCN addition, exhibits full coverage, intensified crystallinity with large grains, and low trap density. The inverted planar structure of indium tin oxide/Poly(3,4-thylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate)/  $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}/2  $  -Phenylethylammonium iodide/(6,6)-Phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester/Bahocuproine/Ag (ITO/PEDOT:PSS/FAPb  $  _{0.7}  $  -  $  Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $  /PEAI/  $  PC_{61}BM  $  /BCP/Ag presented the best PCE of 16.26%, much higher than the record PCE (10.8%)[40] of the FA-based Sn/Pb binary PVSCs. It is also the highest value among the Sn/Pb binary PVSCs fabricated by two-step deposition method. Because of the protection of MASCN to   $  Sn^{2+}  $  , the device fabrication producibility was greatly improved due to the excellent stability of the precursor solution.

# 2. Results and Discussion

A schematic planar heterojunction PVSC device structure, ITO/PEDOT:PSS/FAPb $ _{0.7} $ Sn $ _{0.3} $ I $ _3 $ /PEAI/PC $ _{61} $ BM/BCP/Ag, is presented in Figure 1a. The FAPb $ _{0.7} $ Sn $ _{0.3} $ I $ _3 $  film was prepared by a two-step solution-processed deposition method. In the first step, PbI $ _2 $ /SnI $ _2 $  film was deposited from the precursor solution containing metal iodides (PbI $ _2 $ :SnI $ _2 $  = 0.7:0.3 by molar ratio) and  $ x $  MASCN.  $ x $  MASCN refers to the molar ratio of MASCN to metal iodides, where  $ x $  = 0, 0.15, 0.25, 0.4. Then FAI/IPA solution was dropped onto the PbI $ _2 $ /SnI $ _2 $  film, and annealed at 130 °C for 15 min to obtain FAPb $ _{0.7} $ Sn $ _{0.3} $ I $ _3 $  film. Figure 1b displays the  $ J-V $  curves of the PVSCs with various addition amounts of MASCN. The corresponding photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table 1. Without MASCN addition, the device exhibits a PCE of 12.84% with short-circuit current density ( $ J_{sc} $ ) of 23.50 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  and fill factor ( $ FF $ ) of 0.73.

0.15 MASCN addition elevates the PCE to 13.71%. In the case of 0.25 MASCN, the device exhibits the best PCE of 16.26%, with enhanced   $  J_{sc}  $   of 26.46 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and FF of 0.79. Excessive MASCN ratio of 0.4 decreases the device performance. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectrum and the corresponding integrated photocurrent (  $  J_{cal}  $  ) curve of the device with 0.25 MASCN addition are shown in Figure 1c. The calculated   $  J_{cal}  $   from EQE spectrum is 25.60 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , consistent well with the   $  J_{sc}  $   value (26.46 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) derived from J-V curve. The photocurrent density and PCE output as a function of time at the maximum power point (0.66 V) are shown in Figure 1d. The device shows stable photocurrent and PCE output toward the 300 s scan, demonstrating a stable PCE of 15.60%. The PVSC device with 0.25 MASCN addition presents a best PCE of 16.26% with an average value of 15.81% based on 30 individual devices. As shown in the PCE histograms (Figure 1e, red columns), over 70% of the devices exhibits a PCE >15.70%, indicating good device fabrication producibility. Without MASCN addition, the PCEs of the devices present lower values with a wider dispersion. The reverse–forward scan J-V curves of the devices with and without MASCN addition are shown in Figure 1f. Table S1 in the Supporting Information summarized the corresponding photovoltaic parameters. The PVSC device with 0.25 MASCN addition shows less hysteresis than that without MASCN. The hysteresis index (HI) was calculated according to the previous report.  $  ^{[41]}  $   The MASCN addition decreases the HI to 1.72% from 13.3%, indicating less defect density.

The improved device performance could be attributed to high quality of the   $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   film. Figure 2 presents the corresponding scanning electron-microscope (SEM) images. As shown from the cross-sectional SEM images (Figure 2a–d), the   $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   film with rough surface is constructed by compactly packed random small crystalline grains without MASCN addition. With the aid of MASCN, large brick-shaped grains with less grain boundaries along the vertical direction can be clearly observed. From the top-view SEM images (Figure 2e–h), the full coverage and the increase of grain size from 250 nm (0 MASCN) to 700 nm (0.25 MASCN) can be observed. Higher ratio of MASCN up to 0.4 results in cobblestone-like grains with obvious gaps between the adjacent grains. The corresponding XRD patterns of above   $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   films are presented in Figure 3a. The diffraction peaks at around 14.1° and 28.2° can be ascribed to the (110) and (220) lattice planes of the   $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   crystals, respectively.  $  ^{[15]}  $   Figure 3b shows the intensity and the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the (110) diffraction peak as a function of MASCN addition ratio. With increasing MASCN ratio, the peak intensity strengthens from   $  4.5 \times 10^{4}  $   cps to   $  2.2 \times 10^{5}  $   cps and the FWHM value narrows down from 0.174° to 0.122°, indicating improved crystallinity of the perovskite film. These results indicate that high quality   $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   film with full coverage, large grain size, less grain boundary, and high crystallinity is formed with the aid of 0.25 MASCN addition, leading to improved charge transport and decreased nonradiative recombination, which are responsible for the increased   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF values in the PVSC with 0.25 MASCN.  $  ^{[42,43]}  $   Figure S1 in the Supporting Information shows the Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra of the perovksite films with different amounts of MASCN. In addition to retarding the reaction between   $  Sn^{2+}  $   with FAI, MASCN also prevents the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   in the precursor solution. To evaluate the antioxidation capability of MASCN, we exposed precursor solutions with/without MASCN to air atmosphere for different times. Initially, the as-prepared precursor solutions with/without MASCN exhibited a light yellow color as shown in Figure 6a. Just after 2 min, the color of the precursor solution without MASCN turned into light orange color and gradually changed into dark orange with the prolonged exposure time, which is attributed to the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $  .[35] In comparison, the precursor solution with MASCN remained the light yellow color within 30 min exposure to air and turned into light orange color after 50 min. The similar phenomenon was also observed in the precursor solutions containing higher   $  SnI_{2}/PbI_{2}  $   ratios of 0.5/0.5 M, 0.7/0.3 M, 0.9/0.1 M, and 1/0 M (see Figure S4, Supporting Information). The results show that the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   is strongly avoided in the precursor solution with MASCN, indicating the antioxidation effect of MASCN. The protection of MASCN to   $  Sn^{2+}  $   might be attributed to the coordination between   $  SCN^{-}  $  and   $  Sn^{2+}  $ , which retarded the contact between   $  Sn^{2+}  $   and oxygen.

# 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, high quality   $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   film with large grains, high crystallinity, and low trap density was fabricated by two-step solution-processed deposition method with the aid of bifunctional additive, MASCN, mixed in precursor solution. Through the strong coordination between   $  SCN^{-}  $  and metal iodide, MASCN regulates the morphology of the   $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   film by suppressing the crystallization and compactness of the metal iodide film on one hand. On the other hand, MASCN can also prevent the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   and the degradation of   $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   during the fabricating process. In the case of 0.25 MASCN addition, the Sn/Pb binary perovskite based PVSCs with inverted planar structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/  $  FAPb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}/PEAI/PC_{61}BM/BCP/Ag  $   presented the best PCE of 16.26%, which is the highest value among the Sn/Pb binary PVSCs fabricated by two-step deposition method. The device fabrication producibility was greatly improved due to the protection of coordinated   $  SCN^{-}  $  to   $  Sn^{2+}  $   and the resulting antioxidation. PCE of the optimized device is almost independent on the storage time of the precursor solution within 124 d in the   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glove box. This work gives the new route to fabricate Sn/Pb binary PVSCs with high efficiency and producibility.

Then the substrates were transferred into glove box for active layer deposition and further device fabrication. The two-step Sn $ _{0.3} $ Pb $ _{0.7} $ I $ _{2} $  precursor solution (1 M) was prepared by dissolving SnI $ _{2} $ , PbI $ _{2} $ , and MASCN in molar ratio of 0.3:0.7:x (x = 0, 0.15, 0.25, 0.4, referred as 0 MASCN, 0.15 MASCN, 0.25 MASCN, 0.4 MASCN) into the mixture of 1 mL DMF and 71 μL DMSO, and the solution was stirred at 70 °C overnight in N $ _{2} $  glove box. Thirty microliters of the mixed Sn $ _{0.3} $ Pb $ _{0.7} $ I $ _{2} $  precursor solution was spin-coated at 3000 rpm for 30 s onto the PEDOT:PSS coated ITO (ITO/PEDOT:PSS) substrate. Then, the FAI solution was spun on the substrate at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Afterward, perovskite film was obtained after 130 °C annealing for 15 min. For device fabrication, PEAI (1 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  in IPA) was deposited on the perovskite film with 5000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at 100 °C for another 15 min. PC $ _{61} $ BM (20 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  in chlorobenzene) and BCP (0.5 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  in ethanol) were deposited subsequently at 2000 rpm for 30 s and 3000 rpm for 30 s, respectively. Finally, Ag (100 nm) was deposited in a vacuum chamber under a high vacuum of  $ 5 \times 10^{-4} $  Pa. The device area is 0.06 cm $ ^{2} $  with the metal mask.
