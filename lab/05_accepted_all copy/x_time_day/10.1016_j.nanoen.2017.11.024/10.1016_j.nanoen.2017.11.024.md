Abstract: Polystyrene (PS) is introduced to stabilize the perovskite component, grains and microstructures by forming PS-capped perovskite grains. It is found that the PS coverage has significant advantages in suppressing the component loss and phase separation, obstructing moisture corrosion and promoting crystal self-healing of perovskite films. Besides, with the PS involvement, exceeding  $ 20\% $  efficiency of a planar  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  based cell has been achieved by decreasing charge traps and nonradiative recombination.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have developed rapidly in the past few years due to the outstanding semiconductor photoelectric properties of organic-inorganic perovskite materials and tremendous efforts in perovskite material and device investigation.  $  ^{[1-6]}  $   Its latest certified efficiency of 22.1% is comparable to those of the multicrystalline silicon and chalcogenide solar cells, which makes it a promising alternative to low cost photovoltaics.  $  ^{[7]}  $   However, compared to its high efficiency, stability issue has remarkably limited its further step toward commercialization.  $  ^{[8]}  $   Many theoretical and experimental works have been done to reveal the stability-related physics-chemistry mechanisms.  $  ^{[9-14]}  $   The organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials are found to be very sensitive to humidity and heat.  $  ^{[15-18]}  $   They can easily degrade when exposed into the moisture and show intrinsic thermal instability. Moreover, phase separation, which has been widely observed in inorganic perovskite systems,  $  ^{[19-20]}  $   could also exist in the multiple-component organic-inorganic perovskite absorber, causing energy band fluctuation, carrier accumulation and transport scattering.  $  ^{[21-25]}  $   This phase separation can be induced by non-stoichiometric component change caused by moisture, heat and imbalanced ion migration. For these instability issues, the exposed surface and grain boundaries of the perovskite polycrystalline films are always the initial positions of degradation.

Grain boundaries as well as surface of the perovskite film have low crystallization quality, more atomic coordination deficiency and traps and high surface area,  $  ^{[26]}  $   which provide a large number of channels for moisture and heat corrosion. Some strategies have been proposed to improve the device stability from the perspective of surface and grain boundary engineerings.  $  ^{[27-33]}  $   Metal oxide or organic polymer insulating tunneling layer has been used to cover on the perovskite surface to reduce interface recombination and isolate the moisture for improved stability.  $  ^{[27-30]}  $   Phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) and its derivatives are also introduced into the grain boundaries to passivate trap states and resist the moisture corrosion, whereas they are usually sensitive to the ambient conditions.  $  ^{[31-32]}  $   Recently, long-chain organic cations have been incorporated into the film to enhance the stability by forming quais-3D perovskite structures or passivating the grain boundaries.  $  ^{[33-34]}  $   However, this approach usually needs extra material synthesis processes, and the long-chain organic cations could affect the carrier transport. Thus, an easier-processed and robust surface and grain boundary engineering method is desired to improve the device stability while avoiding negative side influences.

In this work, the polystyrene (PS) is directly employed to assist the film deposition and to protect the perovskite. The PS could cover on the film surface and be pushed to the grain boundaries during the perovskite crystallization process, forming PS-capped perovskite grains. This PS coverage could block the mass exchange between perovskite grains and external environment, thus suppressing the organic component evaporation and water corrosion. Finally, the perovskite film

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

shows better stability in chemical phase, morphology and microstructure under extreme conditions and can fast heal itself after water degradation. Moreover, it is found that this PS assisted film deposition could help to reduce the charge traps and nonradiative recombination of the perovskite semiconductor. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PS-based planar cells is thus promoted to 20.2% with a steady-state efficiency of 19.3%, and could retain its 90% after 30 days kept in the ambient conditions compared to 78% of the control cells.

# 2.1 Microstructure of perovskite films

Figure 1(a) and (b) give schematic diagrams of the PS-assisted deposition of perovskite films and the energy level structure of the complete cell. A certain amount of PS is dissolved in chlorobenzene, which is then used as an anti-solvent in a single-step method to accelerate the perovskite nucleation as literature reported. $ ^{[35-36]} $  For clarity, the perovskite film derived from this process is labeled as ‘PS- $ x $ ’, where  $ x $  is the concentration of the PS solution. Figure 1(c) gives the top-view scanning electron microscope (SEM) image of the PS-5 perovskite film. As can be seen, the perovskite grains with sizes of approximately 300 nm connect tightly to each other. No obvious differences in the grain shape and size could be distinguished between the PS-0 and PS-5 films. Obviously, the introduction of PS does not influence the crystal nucleation and growth process, which is different from the polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA). $ ^{[37]} $  However, the PS involved perovskite films, especially the grain

boundaries, show worse conductivity in SEM measurements, indicating that the insulating PS indeed covers the perovskite film. Besides, because the PS is introduced into the film before the perovskite crystal growth, it can be softened during the heat treatment. It is thus suggested that the surface tension could push the PS to infiltrate along the grain boundaries of the polycrystalline films. After the films cooling down, the PS would form a cured vertical network to protect the perovskite grains and the whole films, just as depicted in Figure 1(a). Figure 1(d) gives the cross-sectional SEM image of the complete planar cell. This PS coverage could give a relatively closed space for the perovskite crystal, helping to suppress the component loss due to volatility or ion migration and to resist the corrosion of moisture, as Figure 1(b) shows. To track the depth profile of PS along the grain boundaries, time of flight secondary ion mass spectroscopy (TOF-SIMS) measurement is carried out, where the perovskite film is deposited on the silicon substrate. # 2.2 Device performance

We further investigate the influence of PS involvement on the composition and trap properties of the perovskite film by X-ray diffraction (XRD), light absorption and steady-state PL measurements (Figure S2). The films show almost the same absorption features with an onset of  $ 783~\mathrm{nm} $ . Neither difference in the XRD peak position can be found. These indicate that the compositions of perovskite films with and without PS involvement are similar to each other. The PL peak position of the PS-0 film is located at  $ 788.6~\mathrm{nm} $ , which is red-shifted about  $ 6\mathrm{nm} $  compared to its

absorption onset and the PL peak position of the PS-5 film. Moreover, the PL intensity of the PS-0 perovskite is only about half of that of the PS-5 perovskite, indicating that the charge trap density in the PS-0 film is a little higher than that in the PS-5 film. Thus, the PS involvement could help to decrease the charge trap of the perovskite film, which may come from the crystal improvement in grain surface and boundaries. On the other hand, it has been found that an ultrathin insulating interface layer in this cell could help to enhance carrier collection and decrease recombination.  $  ^{[29]}  $   The results of electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) also indicate this (Figure S4 and Table S2). All these positive influences indicate that the cell efficiency can be enhanced by the PS assisted film deposition.

In this section, we will give a detailed investigation on the stability of compositions, chemical phase, morphology and microstructure of the perovskite films under extreme conditions and the influence of the PS involvement. Here, the best performed PS-5 perovskite is chosen as the sample.

Firstly, the perovskite films annealed at  $ 160^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  in the ambient conditions are used to study the thermal stability. To get more information about the influence of composition change on the perovskite semiconductor properties, light absorption and steady-state PL measurements are performed. It is found that, after heat treatment, the absorption edge is red-shifted, which is attributed to the decrease in MA component. This agrees with the XRD and XPS results. Besides the thermal decomposition, moisture catalytic degradation is another critical factor affecting the perovskite material stability. A recent work found that the grain boundary rather than the film surface is more easily corroded by the moisture. $ ^{[17]} $  In this work, the PS, which has an excellent hydrophobic capacity, infiltrates into the grain boundaries and covers the surface of the perovskite film. This film microstructure is supposed to have better humidity stability. Firstly, the contact angle (CA) between the perovskite film and the water is measured. As shown in Figure 7(a), the CA of the bare perovskite/water interface is approximately  $ 69.4^{\circ} $ . For the PS-5 film, the CA is significantly increased to  $ 104.9^{\circ} $ , only a little smaller than that of the pure PS/water interface. This means that the perovskite surface is almost covered by the PS and that less-area surface will contact with the water in the same humidity conditions.

To further investigate the humidity stability, a moisture degradation experiment is designed, as shown in Figure 7(c) and (d). The perovskite film is put above boiling water with directly facing the water. The water vapor would condense onto the film surface and degrade the perovskite. As can be seen in Figure 7(c), the bare PS-0 perovskite film immediately degrades into  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  within 1 s while no obvious color change is observed for the PS-5 film. Then, the color of the PS-5 film gradually changes into yellow after 8 s. However, the degradation rate of the PS-5 perovskite is much slower, as shown in Figure 7(d). This indicates that the PS coverage can effectively prevent the water directly contacting with the perovskite surface or infiltrating into the grain boundaries. This result has been further demonstrated by

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

directly immersing the films into the isopropanol bath, where the PS-5 perovskite film also shows much slower degradation rate. After 60 s, both the films change to yellow, implying a complete degradation occurs from the perovskite to  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . Interestingly, for a longer time, the yellow PS-5 film can partly transform back into dark color. It is thus suggested that the PS-5 film may have an excellent self-healing ability even in high humidity conditions. To observe this self-healing behavior, the films are taken away from the water vapor to the ambient condition with humidity of about  $ 30\% $  after 120 s corrosion. Figure 7(e) shows evolutions of the film color at different time. At 0 s, large water droplets on the PS-0 film surface are clearly seen while no obvious droplets are found on the PS-5 film. After that, the area of yellow color gradually decreases, indicating a self-healing process. Obviously, the PS-5 film shows a much faster and better healing ability. It completely changes back into the dark perovskite after 50 s. Although the PS-0 film can also heal itself, some irreversible degradation still occurs. This may be due to that the large-area water coverage causes component inhomogeneity in the macroscopic scale or that part of the components has been catalytically degraded irreversibly. It is thus suggested that the PS involvement has outstanding ability to keep the macroscopic-scale component stabilized by forming closed spaces for perovskite grains. It will thus improve the microstructure stability of the film.

Based on the above discussions, the introduction of PS can help to enhance the perovskite phase and microstructure stability in extreme conditions, thus the device stability is supposed to be promoted. Therefore, the performance of perovskite solar cells without and with PS coverage in different conditions has been investigated. As

shown in Figure 9(a), the PS-5 cell still keeps its  $ 90\% $  efficiency after 30 days kept in the ambient conditions  $ (25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and  $ 30\% $  humidity) without encapsulation and any other protection. For the PS-0 film, only  $ 78\% $  efficiency is kept. When under high humidity of  $ 80\% $ , both the cell efficiencies decay rapidly. After 60 hours, only  $ 46\% $  efficiency is kept for the PS-0 cell, while a much higher  $ 73\% $  efficiency is kept for the PS-5 cell. In fact, although the PS coverage can protect the perovskite being corroded by the moisture, the spiro-OMeTAD and its additives are also sensitive to water, which can also decay the cell performance. It is worth noting that the cell may show better stability even with higher concentration PS coverage (e.g. PS-10 and PS-20). We further investigate the charge dynamics of the cell after the decay test. As shown in Figure 9(c), the PS-5 cell shows negligible decay in the charge recombination property. For the PS-0 cell, the recombination lifetime decreases from 4 to  $ 1.6~\mathrm{ms} $ , it implies that the trap density of the perovskite absorber obviously increases. This is due to the moisture degradation or ion diffusion between the perovskite and HTM. Thus, it is supposed that protecting the perovskite itself is the first step toward enhancing the device stability. In the meantime, an interface protection between the perovskite and HTM is also necessary for high device stability.

# 3. Conclusions

In this work, the polystyrene (PS) has been employed to assist the deposition of perovskite films in a single step method. PS-capped perovskite grains have been obtained to protect perovskite films. It is found that the PS does not obviously

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

influence the perovskite grains size and component; however, the charge trap and nonradiative recombination of the absorber are suppressed. Thus, an improved PCE of as high as  $ 20.2\% $  and a steady-state efficiency of  $ 19.3\% $  for the planar perovskite cell have been achieved. Further research indicates that the PS coverage has significant advantages in improving the stabilities of the perovskite material and the device in extreme conditions. It can significantly suppress the perovskite component volatilization at high temperatures, thus increase the perovskite phase stability. It can protect perovskite films from the water corrosion, thus significantly enhancing the humidity stability. And it helps to stabilize the perovskite microstructure and component distribution, beneficial for perovskite self-healing after degradations. With these benefits, the PS based cell shows impressive charge dynamics stability. Our work provides an effective method toward improving the device stability by stabilizing the perovskite component, grains and microstructures.
