# Abstract

Ruddlesden–Popper type two-dimensional (2D) and quasi-2D perovskite-based photovoltaics have been attracting increasing attention due to their high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term stability. In this work, we fabricated inverted 2D perovskite solar cells (PSCs) using a non-fullerene acceptor as electron transport material. Employing a 55-nm thick 3,9-bis(2-methylene-(3-(1,1-dicyanomethylene)-indanone))-5,5,11,11-tetrakis(5-hexylthienyl)-dithieno[2,3-d:2',3'-d']-s-indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b']dithiophene (ITIC-Th) as electron transport layer, an average PCE of 8.4% was achieved for the 2D PSCs, which was comparable to that of 2D PSCs based on phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM). However, the long-term stability of the 2D PSCs was improved as the degradation of PCE was reduced from 19.8 to 14.6% following a 15-day duration, which can be attributed to the strong hydrophobic property of the ITIC-Th. The best sample based on the 55-nm thick ITIC-Th exhibited a high PCE of 8.9% with a stable power output and negligible hysteresis, indicating the superior electron transport property of ITIC-Th. Our results demonstrate that ITIC-Th is an effective alternative to PCBM to fabricate highly efficient and stable 2D PSCs.

Keywords: Non-fullerene acceptor, electron transport layer, 2D Perovskite solar cells, Stability, Power conversion efficiency

# 1. Introduction

Organic–inorganic hybrid metal halide-based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have emerged as one of the most promising next-generation photovoltaics, owing to their high performance, low cost, and convenient fabrication process   $  [1-4]  $  . The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the PSCs has rapidly been improved from 3.9 to 22.7% within just a few years   $  [5]  $  , indicating a high potential for future commercialization.

In addition to the PCE, the long-term stability of the devices is another important issue that has to be considered simultaneously to satisfy the requirements of commercialization [6, 7]. However, conventional three-dimensional (3D) perovskite materials (for instance,  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbX}_3 $ ,  $ X = \mathrm{Cl} $ , Br, or I) typically suffer from the poor stability [6-8]. For example, they can be easily destroyed by moisture in the air, which can be attributed to their low formation energy and high hydroscopic property [9, 10]. To overcome this issue, in addition to using nanomaterials, developing novel perovskite materials with good stability is highly desired [11-13]. Recently, the application of quasi 2D perovskite materials in PSCs, particularly the  $ (\mathrm{BA})_2(\mathrm{MA})_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_n\mathrm{I}_{3n+1} $  (where  $ n $  is an integer, BA is  $ \mathrm{CH}_3(\mathrm{CH}_2)_3\mathrm{NH}_3 $ , and MA is  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3 $ ) series, have been intensively investigated [14-21]. To date, the highest PCE value of the quasi 2D perovskite based solar cells (2D PSCs) has been  $ 13.7\% $  reported by X. Zhang and coworkers [14]. Although the PCE of 2D PSCs is still lower than that of conventional 3D PSCs, the long-term stability has been significantly improved [14-20]. According to the position of charge transport layers in the device, PSCs can be fabricated as standard and inverted structures [1-4, 22-25]. For standard structured PSCs,  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  is typically employed as the electron transport material, resulting in the high performance of PSCs [1-4, 14]. However, the high sintering temperature of  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  ( $ \sim 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) limits its application in large-scale industrial production [26-28]. To lower the fabrication temperature, inverted PSCs have been actively studied, in which phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) is typically used as electron transport material [22, 23]. Nevertheless, owing to the conveniently tunable energy levels, broadened absorptions, and facile synthesis process, non-fullerene acceptor materials have been

demonstrated to be suitable choices to replace PCBM to fabricate high-performance organic solar cells (OSCs) [29–35]. Recently, the PCE of OSCs using non-fullerene acceptors is even higher than that of PCBM-based devices as indicated by previous studies [29–35]. For 3D PSCs, non-fullerene acceptors have also been actively used, because they not only transport electrons effectively, but also passivate traps in perovskite films [36–38]. For example, Huang et al. introduced a π-conjugated non-fullerene electron transport material (IDIC) into inverted 3D PSCs, and greatly improved the PCE from 13.1 to 19.4% [36]. Zhou et al. used IDIC to replace TiO₂ in standard PSCs, and improved the PCE up to 19.1% [37]. Liu et al. reported the application of combined TiO₂ and 3,9-bis(2-methylene-(3-(1,1-dicyanomethylene)-indanone))-5,5,11,11-tetrakis(4-hexylphenyl)-dithieno[2,3-d:2',3'-d']-s-indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b']dithiophene (ITIC) as electron transport layer, resulting in a high PCE of 20.08% [38]. Compared with ITIC, 3,9-bis(2-methylene-(3-(1,1-dicyanomethylene)-indanone))-5,5,11,11-tetrakis(5-hexylthienyl)-dithieno[2,3-d:2',3'-d']-s-indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b']dithiophene (ITIC-Th) has even higher electron mobility, which is more suitable for electron transportation [32]. Therefore, Zhan et al. added a small amount of ITIC-Th to the 3D perovskite precursor and simultaneously improved the PCE and stability of the relevant PSCs [39]. However, up to now, no study is available on the application of non-fullerene acceptors in 2D PSCs. Considering the different properties of 2D and 3D perovskites, the characterization of the effects of using non-fullerene in the fabrication of 2D PSCs is very important.

In this work, we fabricated quasi 2D  $ (\mathrm{BA})_{2}(\mathrm{MA})_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{4}\mathrm{I}_{13} $  based PSCs using ITIC-Th (a non-fullerene acceptor) as electron transport layer. We controlled the thickness of the ITIC-Th layer by varying the concentration of the ITIC-Th precursors and found that using a  $ 55\mathrm{-nm} $  thick ITIC-Th for the fabrication of 2D PSC showed the highest performance. The fullerene-free 2D PSCs exhibited an average PCE of  $ 8.4\% $ , which is comparable to that of PSCs using PCBM. The long-term stability of the 2D PSCs was improved when the degradation of PCE was reduced from 19.8 to  $ 14.6\% $  in a duration of 15 days, which could be attributed to the strong hydrophobic property of the ITIC-Th. # 2. Experimental section

# 2.1 Device fabrication

ITIC-Th and methylammonium iodide (MAI) were purchased from Ossila Ltd (UK) and Ying Kou You Xuan Trade Co., Ltd (China), respectively. Butylammonium iodide (BAI), lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2) $ ,  $ N,N $ -dimethylformamide (DMF), 2-propanol, and chlorobenzene (CB) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (USA). Poly(bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine) (PTAA) and bathocuproine (BCP) were purchased from EM Index (Korea) and Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. (China), respectively. As shown in Fig. 1(a), our devices were fabricated on pre-cleaned patterned indium-tin oxide (ITO) coated glasses, using a glass/ITO/PTAA/perovskite/ITIC-Th/BCP/Ag architecture. First, the PTAA solution (in CB,  $ 10\mathrm{mg~mL}^{-1} $ ) was spin coated onto the ITO glass at 3500 rpm for 45 s. Then the samples were thermally annealed at  $ 80^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  to fully remove the solvent. The 2D perovskite precursor was prepared by dissolving BAI, MAI, and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  (molar ratio = 2:3:4) in DMF at a total concentration of  $ 35\mathrm{wt}\% $ . The perovskite precursor was spin coated onto the PTAA layers at 4000 rpm for 45 s in a glove box filled with  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $ . Then the quasi 2D  $ (\mathrm{BA})_2(\mathrm{MA})_3\mathrm{Pb}_4\mathrm{I}_{13} $  perovskite films were formed after being thermally annealed at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 5 min. Following this, the ITIC-Th solution (6, 9, 12, and  $ 15\mathrm{mg~mL}^{-1} $  in CB) was spin coated onto the perovskite films at 1000 rpm for 60 s. Then the BCP solution (in 2-propanol at  $ 0.5\mathrm{mg~mL}^{-1} $ ) was spin coated onto the ITIC-Th layers at 4500 rpm for 45 s. The fabrication of our 2D PSCs was finished by thermally evaporating the 100-nm thick Ag onto the samples. The effective working area of our 2D PSCs was  $ 0.1\mathrm{cm}^2 $ , defined by a shadow mask.

the  $ J - V $  characteristics for SCLC devices can be divided into three regions. In the low-voltage range, the current density increased linearly with the increasing voltage, indicating an ohmic response [42]. In the intermediate-voltage range ( $ \sim 0.4 - 0.8\mathrm{V} $ ), the current exhibited a rapid non-linear increase, indicating a trap-filled limit, where all the available trap states are filled by the injected carriers [42-44]. The voltage between the ohmic contact and the trap-filled regime is known as the trap-filled limit voltage ( $ V_{\mathrm{TFL}} $ ), which is determined by the trap states. Hence, the trap density ( $ n_{\mathrm{trap}} $ ) can be calculated using the following equation [42-44]:

$$
V _ {\mathrm{TFL}} = \frac {e n _ {\mathrm{trap}} L ^ {2}}{2 \varepsilon_ {0} \varepsilon},
$$

(1)

where L is the thickness of the perovskite film,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative dielectric constant of the perovskite,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity, and e is the elementary charge. As shown in Fig. 5, the   $  V_{TFL}  $   values of the devices are 0.48 and 0.42 V when using PCBM and ITIC-Th, respectively. The average corresponding   $  n_{trap}  $   values were calculated as   $  5.3\times10^{15}  $   and   $  4.6\times10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  , respectively, indicating a reduction of 13% in the number of traps when ITIC-Th was used. All the   $  n_{trap}  $   values of 12 individual devices were shown in Fig. S8, which confirmed the decreased number of traps upon using ITIC-Th. This can be explained by the molecular structure of ITIC-Th, which contains a Lewis base (C=O and C≡N) at the end, as shown in Fig. 1(c). Previous studies demonstrated that, compared with PCBM (Lewis acid), electron transport materials containing a Lewis base (C=O and C≡N) can effectively passivate the traps at the surface of the perovskite by forming coordinate bonding with the under-coordinated Pb in the perovskite [36, 37, 40, 41], which is in good agreement of our PL and SCLC results. In the high-voltage range, the current increased quadratically with the voltage in the Child's regime [42–44]. In addition, the electron mobility can be determined by the Mott–Gurney law [45–47], using equation:

$$
J = \frac {9}{8} \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0} \mu \frac {V ^ {2}}{L ^ {3}},
$$

The lower  $ \mu_{\mathrm{electron}} $  value of the device might be the result of the intrinsic lower  $ \mu_{\mathrm{electron}} $  value of the ITIC-Th layer compared with that of the PCBM [32-34]. # 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we fabricated high-performance inverted 2D PSCs using a non-fullerene acceptor (ITIC-Th) as electron transport layer. The excellent electron extraction property of ITIC-Th in 2D PSCs was demonstrated by the PL and TRPL analysis. Based on a 55-nm thick ITIC-Th, the fullerene-free 2D PSCs exhibited an average PCE of  $ 8.4\% $ , which is comparable to that of PSCs using PCBM. The long-term stability of the 2D PSCs was improved as the PCE degradation was reduced from 19.8 to  $ 14.6\% $  following a 15 days duration, which can be attributed to the strong hydrophobic property of the ITIC-Th. The best sample based on the 55-nm thick ITIC-Th exhibited a high PCE of  $ 8.9\% $  with a stable power output and negligible hysteresis, demonstrating the superior performance of the application of ITIC-Th. Our results indicate that using the non-fullerene acceptor ITIC-Th as electron transport material is a simple and effective method to fabricate high-performance 2D PSCs.
