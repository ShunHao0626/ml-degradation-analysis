# 1. Introduction

To meet the increasing demand for energy, the rate of fossil fuels depletion has accelerated, that increases the emission of carbon dioxide and other detrimental gases. Therefore, the greenhouse effect and climate change, has motivated a worldwide increase in development of renewable energy resources. Among all renewable energy sources, perovskite solar cell (PSC) are an emerging and promising photovoltaic technology for solar energy harvesting that shows a rapid and remarkable development, from the first reported PCE of 3.8% to a certified PCE of up to 25.5%  $  ^{[1]}  $  over recent years owing to its intrinsic properties, such as excellent ambipolar charge transport, high absorption coefficient, long carrier diffusion length and tunable direct bandgap.  $  ^{[2]}  $  Generally, high-efficiency PSCs consist of a multicomponent structure where the light absorbing layer (perovskite) is sandwiched between hole transporting materials (HTMs) and electron transporting materials

For highly efficient and stable perovskite solar cells (PSCs), small molecular hole transporting materials (HTMs) with high glass transition temperature, hydrophobicity, excellent film-formation and excellent hole mobility is highly desirable. However, developing a single molecule, which could meet all the above mention properties, is challenging. Herein, novel small molecules were design and synthesized, which show a suitable HOMO energy level and fulfill the above mentioned properties simultaneously. For this purpose, we incorporate a hydrophobic trifluoropropoxy-terminal group to diphenylamine in SGT-405(3,6), a carbazole-based promising non-spiro-type small molecular HTM with excellent performance and low-cost, leading to two new HTMs (SGT-405s(C₂,CF₃) and SGT-405d(C₂,CF₃)). Among them SGT-405s(C₂,CF₃) showed an excellent hole mobility of 3.32 × 10⁻⁴ cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹, a remarkable high T₈ of 183 °C and increased water contact angle of 87° compared to that of spiro OMeTAD (77.7°). Due to these superior properties, compared to those of spiro-OMeTAD, PSCs based on SGT-405s(C₂,CF₃) showed a remarkable power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 20.14%, which is higher than that of spiro-OMeTAD (18.97%), together with enhanced long-term and thermal stability. Our work revealed for the first time that trifluoropropoxy is a potential terminal group and an effective strategy for the design of new HTMs possessing superior properties in terms of glass transition temperature, hydrophobicity and film formation, for realizing efficient PSCs with enhanced moisture and thermal stability, simultaneously.

High-quality perovskites have been enabled by tremendous efforts in perovskite compositions engineering,  $  ^{[3,4]}  $   versatile solution deposition methods,  $  ^{[5]}  $   interface engineering  $  ^{[6,7]}  $   and optimization of the morphology of perovskite layers,  $  ^{[8]}  $   and stable and affordable ETMs (e.g.,   $  TiO_{2}  $   or   $  SnO_{2}  $  ) have been successfully developed for high performance PSCs. HTMs play an important role in extracting the hole from the valence band (VB) of perovskite layer while blocking the electrons.[9] Numerous reports have been dedicated to the design of various efficient HTMs such as organic polymers, small molecules and inorganic components (CuSCN).[10] Among them, small molecular HTMs possess the following advantageous characteristics, such as facile synthetic routes, exact molecular weight, tunable band gap, and good reproducibility. To date, spiro-OMeTAD is still recognized as the most efficient and broadly used HTM for PSCs, owing to its amorphous nature and energy levels that match with the VB of perovskite. However, its inferior charge mobility (without dopants), complicated synthesis and tedious purification renders it unattainable for commercialization.

Long-term stability is still one of the challenging issue encountered in PSCs as perovskite films suffer degradation upon moisture exposure, which could not be resolved by using spiro-OMeTAD. For spiro-OMeTAD, usually chemical doping is required by an external chemical dopant, which induces PSC degradation, due to the hygroscopic nature of the dopant. To solve the aforementioned issues, the development of efficient hydrophobic HTMs[35,36] is inevitable as a moisture protection layer for the underlying perovskite films via preventing the diffusion of external moisture. Long alkoxy[37] or alkyl chains[38,39] are widely added to HTMs in order to improve their hydrophobicity. However, it has been observed that the incorporation of long alkoxy or alkyl chains in HTM molecules significantly decreased the  $ \mathrm{T_g} $  of HTMs.[22,37,38,40] Iwan et al. investigated the effect of alkyl chain length on the thermal properties of HTMs and found that the  $ \mathrm{T_g} $  of ATT-OBu is  $ 131^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and decreased to  $ 95^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for ATT-OHex, and further decreased to  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for ATT-ODec, demonstrating that the increase of alkyl chain length significantly decreased the  $ \mathrm{T_g} $  of HTMs.[37] The  $ \mathrm{T_g} $  values of some representative HTMs varying in alkyl chain length are shown in Fig. 1 and Table S2. It was also found that the HTM substituted by long alkyl chains may suffer poor film formation, due to additional van der Waals interactions stemming from the side chains.[37] A similar phenomenon was also observed in our previous work,[21] where the introduction of OHex in SGT405(3,6) HTM results in poor film formation. However, a high  $ \mathrm{T_g} $  value of HTMs is highly desirable for the stability of HTM morphology upon thermal stress or heating in PSC devices.[41-43] As far as we know, a single HTM possessing multiple superior properties at once (e.g., high  $ \mathrm{T_g} $ , excellent film formation and hydrophobicity) is

relatively unexplored, to date.

Herein, we introduced a hydrophobic trifluoropropoxy group to terminate diphenylamine in SGT-405(3,6), a promising small molecular non-spiro HTM with low-cost and outstanding performance, for replacing the existing synthetically-challenging and expensive spiroOMeTAD in PSCs.[21] The number of trifluoropropoxy units was controlled to tune the HOMO energy level and film forming properties of HTMs. Unlike the strong electron-withdrawing group  $ \mathrm{OCF}_3 $ .[44] the trifluoropropoxy unit is a very weak electron-withdrawing group, and can reasonably down-shift the HOMO level of HTMs. The effects of the trifluoropropoxy unit on the wetting properties of the HTM, as well as the electrochemical and optical properties, thermal properties, hole mobility, film formation and device performance of PSCs were systematically studied. The introduction of an appropriate number of trifluoropropoxy groups to the HTMs not only improved the hydrophobicity of HTM, but also showed excellent film formation properties without aggregation behavior observed in other long alkyl chain-substituted HTMs, benefiting from the repulsion properties of the trifluoropropoxy group. Furthermore, density functional theory (DFT) and space-charge-limited current (SCLC) studies demonstrated that the trifluoropropoxy-substituted SGT-405(3,6) exhibited lower hole reorganization energy and higher hole mobility vs the OHex group-substituted material. To the best of our knowledge, for the first time, we have designed and synthesized a novel HTM via substitution of trifluoropropoxy groups in carbazole derivatives, providing excellent film formation, suitable HOMO energy levels, hydrophobicity, high  $ \mathrm{T_g} $ , high hole mobility, and enhanced long-term moisture and thermal stability.

Hole mobility is one of the key parameters in the design of novel HTMs. In this work, the hole mobility of new HTM together with spiro-OMeTAD was measured and estimated using the hole-only devices having the device structure of FTO (fluorine-doped tin oxide)/PEDOT: PSS/HTMs/Au by the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method (Fig. 9a). [51,52] The following equation is used for the fitting:

$$
J = \frac {9}{8} \mu \varepsilon_ {0} \varepsilon_ {r} \frac {V ^ {2}}{d ^ {3}}

Long-term device stability is an important key aspect for the long-term operation of PSCs in the practical application. In the case of n-i-p type PSC device configuration, hydrophobic HTM films spin-coated on top of perovskite films can not only serve as a hole extraction and transport layer but also act as a protecting layer for perovskite film toward environmental moisture. SGT-405s(C₂,CF₃) shows higher water contact angle compared to that of SGT-405(3,6) and spiro-OMeTAD indicating its better hydrophobicity (see Fig. 11a-d). When the device stability of PSCs based on SGT-405s(C₂,CF₃), SGT-405(3,6) and spiro-OMeTAD without encapsulation were investigated in ambient conditions, SGT-405s(C₂,CF₃)-based PSCs exhibited higher stability compared to that of SGT-405(3,6) and spiro-OMeTAD based PSCs, as shown in Fig. 11e. The improved stability of the SGT-405s(C₂,CF₃)-based device is ascribed to the combination results of the uniform HTM protecting layer and its greater hydrophobicity.[57] The thermal stability of the PSCs based on SGT-405s(C₂,CF₃), SGT-405(3,6) and spiro-OMeTAD at 60 °C for 21 h and consecutively 70 °C for 52 h on the hot plate under air condition (~20% RH) were monitored (Fig. 11f). Under this testing condition, the efficiency of PSC based on spiro-OMeTAD exhibited rapid decrease. It is acknowledged that deformation of spiro-OMeTAD film rises from crystallization upon heating at high temperature or long time operation.[21,48] Conversely, there is no apparent performance decrease in PSC based on SGT-405s(C₂,CF₃) and SGT-405(3,6) under identical experimental condition, indicating that PSC based on SGT-405s(C₂,CF₃) exhibited significantly enhanced thermal stability than that of spiro-OMeTAD, benefiting from the effective asymmetric HTM structure via the trifluoropropoxy substitution.

# 3. Conclusions

To develop HTMs with hydrophobicity and fine-tune the optical and electronic properties, two different carbazole-based derivatives, terminated by trifluoropropoxy substituents, were developed, and their electrochemical and thermal properties were systematically investigated. Both  $ \mathrm{SGT - 405s(C_2,CF_3)} $  and  $ \mathrm{SGT - 405d(C_2,CF_3)} $  exhibited suitable HOMO energy level of  $ -5.26 $  and  $ -5.27\mathrm{eV} $ , that shows a good energy level alignment with the valence band of perovskite films, which is beneficial for hole extraction. This work also found that  $ \mathrm{SGT - 405d(C_2,} $   $ \mathrm{CF}_3) $ , possessing twelve trifluoropropoxy groups, resulted in several gaps in the thin film after spin-coating, and, thus, showed a very poor

efficiency. However, SGT-405s(C $ _2 $ ,CF $ _3 $ ) exhibits the best efficiency of 20.14%, mainly due to the combined contribution of good film formation properties and high hole mobility of  $ 3.32 \times 10^{-4} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ . Furthermore, the termination of diphenylamine with trifluoropropoxy on one side can results in asymmetric SGT-405s(C $ _2 $ ,CF $ _3 $ ) with better hydrophobicity and completely amorphous state film with much higher T $ _g $  compared to that of spiro-OMeTAD, simultaneously; thus, PSCs based on SGT-405s(C $ _2 $ ,CF $ _3 $ ) showed improved stability toward moisture and thermal stress compared to those based on spiro-OMeTAD. In summary, novel properties of trifluoropropoxy-functionalized SGT-405s(C $ _2 $ ,CF $ _3 $ ) can simultaneously improve the efficiency and stability of PSCs.
