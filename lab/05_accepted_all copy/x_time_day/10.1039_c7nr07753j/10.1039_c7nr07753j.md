# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have received so much attention recently due to their excellent optoelectronic properties such as tunable band gap, high absorption coefficient, balanced charge carrier transport, and long charge carrier lifetime and diffusion length.  $  ^{1-11}  $   The perovskite solar cell efficiency has been improved tremendously from 3.8 % to over 20 % within past 5 years by tuning the perovskite compositions, using interface engineering and optimizing the film deposition methods.  $  ^{10-22}  $   The high efficiency is approaching to those long term established inorganic thin film solar cells based on copper indium gallium selenium (CIGS) and cadmium telluride (CdTe).  $  ^{23}  $   Although the devices have achieved impressive power conversion efficiency (PCE) values, some important problems such as stability, hysteresis issues still exist in the generally used devices.  $  ^{24}  $   Without encapsulation, the device tends to degrade within a few hours or days under air conditions, which is a big obstacle for commercialization of the PSCs. Perovskite/PCBM heterojunction has been proved to be an efficient route for fabricating high performance and long-term stable perovskite solar cells. In this study, the efficient perovskite/PCBM heterojunction was formed via conventional sequential deposition and one-step formation process. Compared to conventional deposition process, the one-step process is much simpler, and the perovskite thin film quality is substantially improved due to the fullerene passivation. Moreover, the resulting perovskite/PCBM heterojunction exhibits more efficient carrier transfer and extraction, and reduced carrier recombination. The perovskite solar cell device based on the one-step formed perovskite/PCBM heterojunction exhibited a much higher maximum PCE of  $ 17.8\% $  compared to conventional method  $ (13.7\%) $ . The device also showed exceptional stability with retained  $ 83\% $  of initial value after 60 days' storage in ambient air condition.

For the device fabrication, the planar structure including n-i-p or p-i-n type is usually used since it could avoid mesoporous scaffold layer, and improve the reproducibility.  $  ^{32-33}  $   The most important thing is that this structure is compatible with flexible substrates since the metal oxide interlayer could be processed at low temperature or replaced with some organic interlayers. Among these structures, perovskite/fullerene especially phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) heterojunction has been widely used in PSCs due to the simple fabrication, less hysteresis effects, as well as the high device performance.  $  ^{34-37}  $   For example, Huang's group has reported PCBM passivation effects in some studies. It was reported that the fullerene small molecules could diffuse into the perovskite layer through the grain boundaries, and further passivate the shallow trap states.  $  ^{38}  $   Hence, the resulting PSC device usually exhibits high fill factor up to 0.80 and negligible hysteresis effect. In order to simplify the fabrication process, perovskite-PCBM bulk heterojunction by using   $  \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_2+MAI+PCBM}  $   precursor solution has been reported.  $  ^{39}  $   However, due to the low solubility of PCBM in DMF solvent, the content in formed perovskite heterojunction is limited. Moreover, all these methods require an additional electron transporting layer deposition process for efficient charge extraction and transport to achieve a high device performance.

In this study, we adopted a unique solvent engineering method to form perovskite/fullerene heterojunction in one step. Moreover, this process could not only passivate the perovskite grain boundaries with fullerene small molecules, but also form electron transporting layer, and hence simplify the fabrication step. # Results and discussion,

In order to form this kind of perovskite/fullerene heterojunction structure, the perovskite thin films were deposited as shown in Figure 1. The thin film was formed onto PEDOT:PSS surface with solvent engineering methods since this method has been proved to be an efficient route to retard the crystal growth for obtaining perovskite by forming a complex MAI-DMSO-  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  immediate phase and optimizing the perovskite quality. During the spin coating, the PCBM in CB solution was dripping onto the perovskite surface. Then the thin films were annealed at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 20\mathrm{min} $ . After that, the devices were finished by thermally evaporating  $ 100\mathrm{nmAg} $  in high vacuum ( $ \leq 5\times 10^{-4}\mathrm{Pa} $ ) through a shadow mask.

Figure 1. Processing scheme of in-situ one-step formation of the perovskite/PCBM heterojunction in inverted PSCs and schematic device structure of the perovskite solar cell in this study (ITO/PEDOT:PSS/perovskite/PCBM/BCP/Ag).

The influence of PCBM content on device performance is shown in Figure 2, and the corresponding device performance parameters are summarized in Table 1. When PCBM content was lower during film formation, the performance was much poorer due to discontinuous formation of the electron transporting layer. For example, the 5mg-PCBM PSC showed a short circuit current density  $ (J_{\mathrm{SC}}) $  of  $ 16.44\pm 0.76~\mathrm{mA / cm^2} $ , fill factor (FF) of  $ 63.5\pm 1.1\% $ , and the average PCE of about  $ 10.7\% $  (PCE of the best device is  $ 11.2\% $ ). When gradually increasing the PCBM content, the device performance was enhanced. When PCBM content was  $ 10~\mathrm{mg / mL} $ , the PCBM could form a continuous thin film for efficient electron transport. The best  $ 10\mathrm{mg - PCBM} $  PSC showed an improved device performance with a  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  of  $ 21.90~\mathrm{mA / cm^2} $ , an open circuit voltage  $ (V_{OC}) $  of  $ 1.02\mathrm{V} $ , a FF of  $ 69.3\% $ , and the overall PCE of  $ 15.6\% $ . The device had a best performance when PCBM content was  $ 20~\mathrm{mg / mL} $ . The device performance parameters were significantly improved with a  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  of  $ 21.71~\mathrm{mA / cm^2} $ , a  $ V_{OC} $  of  $ 1.02\mathrm{V} $ , a FF of  $ 78.1\% $ , and the overall PCE of  $ 17.8\% $ . This was also much higher than the conventional device which exhibited a  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  of  $ 18.78\pm 1.21~\mathrm{mA / cm^2} $ , a  $ V_{OC} $  of  $ 0.99\pm 0.02\mathrm{V} $ , and a FF of

71.0±1.5 %, and a corresponding average PCE of 13.3% (best device shows a PCE of 13.7 %). The increased device performance was contributed to all parameters enhancement. It was thought that PCBM plays important roles in the heterojunction device with the function of transporting electrons and blocking holes. Since PCBM possesses lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy level of -4.2 eV which matches with perovskite and relatively deep highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy level (-6.0 eV) which acts as the hole blocking layer in inverted PSCs. Meanwhile, a thin PCBM layer between the perovskite and the cathode can facilitate electron transport and reducing the charge recombination efficiently. Thus, a thin continuous PCBM layer should be formed between the perovskite layer and the cathode so that it can avoid the direct contact between the perovskite layer and the cathode to ensure a full coverage to reduce the leakage current. Meanwhile, the appropriate thickness of PCBM should be as thin as possible to reduce the series resistance (  $  R_{S}  $  ). Moreover, the in-situ formed perovskite/PCBM heterojunction has better film passivation between grain boundaries compared to conventional device which use two step depositions.  $  ^{37}  $

The IPCE curves of the conventional device and one-step method based device are shown in Figure S1. The higher IPCE values in the wavelength range from 350 nm to 750 nm also confirm the higher photon to electron conversion efficiency. The integrated current density were   $  18.9 \, mA/cm^{2}  $   and   $  21.5 \, mA/cm^{2}  $   for the conventional and one-step method based devices, respectively, which is close to the   $  J_{sc}  $   value obtained from current density – voltage (J - V) scans.

The hysteresis behaviors were also checked by scanning the devices under different scan rates (a high scan rate with the delay time of 10 ms and a low scan rate with the delay time of 100 ms) and scan directions (reverse scan: from a positive bias 1.1 V to a negative bias -0.2 V, and forward scan: from a negative bias -0.2 V to a positive bias 1.1 V). Negligible photocurrent hysteresis and highly consistent  $  J - V  $  characteristics regardless of the scan rates and scan directions were observed. These results are consistent with the literature reported that PCBM can significantly reduce the hysteresis effect. This is easy to understand that the carrier extraction at the PCBM and perovskite interfaces is very efficient and this can effectively alleviate the carrier accumulation at the interfaces. Moreover, the diffused PCBM into the perovskite layer can effectively passivate the trap states and tie up the mobile ions. The steady state outputs for the current density and PCE as a function of time were also checked (Figure 3c-d). The devices exhibited a steady-state PCE output of  $  13.5\%  $  and  $  17.2\%  $  for conventional device and one-step method based device, respectively. The results confirm that the device performance are stable and without photocurrent hysteresis in the devices. All these results verify the absence of photocurrent hysteresis in the devices.

To further investigate the formed perovskite/PCBM heterojunction effect on the device performance and related recombination mechanism,   $  V_{OC}  $   and   $  J_{SC}  $   versus light intensity measurements were conducted by measuring   $  V_{OC}  $   and   $  J_{SC}  $   at various light intensities from 100 to 0.1 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $  . As shown in Figure 4, the   $  V_{OC}  $   as a function of light intensity in semi-logarithmic exhibits a linear relationship with a slope of kT/q, where k, T, and q are the Boltzmann constant, temperature in Kelvin, and the elementary charge, respectively. The slopes extracted from the curves are 2.31 and 1.65 for conventional and one-step method based devices, respectively. As we know, when the additional trap-assisted recombination is involved, the slope of this plot is greater than kT/q. Hence, the lower slope value indicates reduced density of grain boundaries related traps and suppressed trap-assisted recombination. The   $  J_{SC}  $   showed a power law dependence on illumination intensity   $  J_{SC} \propto I^{a}  $  , where l and   $  \alpha  $   are the light intensity and the exponential factor, respectively. The   $  \alpha  $   values extracted from the curves are 0.82 and 0.94 for conventional and one-step method based devices, respectively. This means that the on-step approach could efficiently passivate the perovskite grain boundaries, enhance charge carrier transport and reduce the recombination.

The device stability of different approaches was investigated by storing the un-encapsulated devices in an ambient environment at 22 °C at about 30% humidity. From Figure 5, the PCE of the conventional device reduced to 25% of the original value after 60 days. While the one-step method based device still kept about 83 % of the original value after 60 days. The enhanced stability was due to both the passivation effect for the traps and mobile ions in the perovskite layer and hydrophobic property for protecting the perovskite layer against the moisture. Besides, the nature of the perovskite material itself which has a much larger and denser crystal grains should provide a dynamic barrier towards water molecules in the ambient air.

In order to illustrate the one-step formation method effect on the final perovskite thin film quality, various characterization techniques were employed to analysis the thin film properties. The thin film crystallinity was checked by X-ray diffraction

(XRD). Diffraction peaks at the XRD patterns around 14.21°, 28.51°, and 31.88° are assigned to the (110), (220), and (310) lattice planes of the tetragonal perovskite structure, respectively, indicating that the perovskite film is formed. The PbI₂ peak around 12.8° was not observed in the one-step formed film, indicating that the formed perovskite thin film had good quality. The enhanced diffraction intensities of perovskite peaks at 14.21°, 28.51°, and 31.88° indicate the improved crystallinity of the perovskite film. It should be mentioned that using one-step formation method did not change the perovskite crystal structure as no observable peak shift was observed. The UV-vis absorption provides similar result that using one-step formation method did not affect the perovskite thin film absorption (Figure 6b).

In order to have a better understanding on charge extraction based on perovskite/PCBM heterojunction structure, the charge carrier dynamics were further investigated by steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL measurements. # Experimental

# Materials

All the materials, Methylammonium iodide (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  , 99.8%, Dyesol), Lead iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $   crystals, 99.999%, Sigma), phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM, 98%, nano-c), poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS, Clevios PVP Al 4083), Dimethyl sulphoxide (99.8%, Sigma), Chlorobenzene (99.8%, Sigma), Toluene (99.8%, Sigma-Aldrich), and γ-Butyrolactone (99.8%, Aladdin) were used as received without further purification.

# Film formation and device fabrication

Here, an inverted planar heterojunction structure (ITO/PEDOT:PSS/Perovskite/PCBM/BCP/Ag) was used. Inverted planar PSCs were fabricated on pre-patterned ITO glass substrates (around 2×2.5 cm $  ^{2}  $  in size, 10 Ω per square). The patterned ITO glass substrates were sequentially ultrasonic cleaned with detergent, de-ionized water, acetone, and isopropyl alcohol at 50 °C for 20 min, respectively. Then, the ITO substrates were dried with nitrogen gun and treated in a UV ozone oven for 15 min before a thin layer of PEDOT:PSS was spin-coated on the substrates at 7000 rpm for 40 s and annealed at 140 °C for 15 min. After that, the substrates were transferred into a nitrogen-filled glovebox. A MAPbI $  _{3-x}  $ Cl $  _{x}  $  precursor solution containing 1.26 M PbI $  _{2}  $ , 0.14 M PbCl $  _{2}  $  and 1.35 M MAI was prepared in the cosolvent of DMSO:GBL (3:7 v/v), and stirred for 2 h at 75 °C. Various concentration of PCBM solutions (2 mg/ml, 5 mg/ml, 10 mg/ml, 20 mg/ml, and 30 mg/ml) were prepared by dissolving PCBM in anhydrous chlorobenzene and stirred for 12 h. The spin-coating process for the perovskite precursor solution was programmed to run at 1000 rpm for 20 s and then 4000 rpm for 40 s. When the spinning was at 50 s, 150 μl anhydrous chlorobenzene or

# Materials and device characterization

The samples for the material characterization were prepared as the same process of device fabrication. The morphology measurement of the perovskite layers was measured by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (JSM-7800F). X-ray diffraction (XRD) test was conducted on Bruker D8 Advance XRD. The UV-visible absorption spectra were recorded with an UV-visible spectrophotometer (Perkin-Elmer Lambda 950). Photovoltaic performances were measured by using a Keithley 2400 source meter under simulated sunlight from XES-70S1 solar simulator matching the AM 1.5G standard with an intensity of  $ 100\mathrm{mW/cm^2} $ . The system was calibrated against a NREL certified silicon reference solar cell. Incident photo-to-current conversion efficiencies (IPCEs) of PSCs were measured by the solar cell quantum efficiency measurement system (SCS10-X150, Zolix instrument. Co. Ltd). All the measurements of the solar cells were performed under ambient atmosphere at room temperature without encapsulation. Time-resolved photoluminescence (TR-PL) were measured using the EDINBURGH INSTRUMENTS FLS 980 by using a  $ 450~\mathrm{nm} $  picosecond pulsed laser (EPL-450). Transient photocurrent (TPC) measurement was performed with a system excited by a  $ 532~\mathrm{nm} $  ( $ 1000\mathrm{Hz} $ ,  $ 3.2\mathrm{ns} $ ) pulse laser. Transient photovoltage (TPV) measurement was performed with the same system excited by a  $ 405~\mathrm{nm} $  ( $ 50\mathrm{Hz} $ ,  $ 20\mathrm{ms} $ ) pulse laser. A digital oscilloscope (Tektronix, D4105) was used to record the photocurrent or photovoltage decay process with a sampling resistor of  $ 50\Omega $  or  $ 1\mathrm{M}\Omega $ , respectively.

# Conclusions

In conclusion, the perovskite/PCBM heterojunction thin film could be formed by using one-step formation method. By choosing proper PCBM content during the film formation, the perovskite film quality could be significantly improved, charge extraction and collection were enhanced, charge carrier recombination was reduced, and hence the device performance and stability were significantly improved. The best device showed a high PCE of  $ 17.8\% $ ,  $ 30\% $  higher than the conventional device. Further performance enhancement could be expected by carefully optimizing the perovskite compositions and the film interfaces. It is obviously that this approach could simplify the device fabrication, and hence, lower down the fabrication cost. These results provide a facile way towards the widespread use of the one-step formation of the photoactive and electron transporting layers of high efficiency and low-cost PSCs for commercial applications.
