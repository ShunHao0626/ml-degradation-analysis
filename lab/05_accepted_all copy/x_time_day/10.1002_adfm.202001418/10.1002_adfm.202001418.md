# Vertical Phase Separated Cesium Fluoride Doping Organic Electron Transport Layer: A Facile and Efficient “Bridge” Linked Heterojunction for Perovskite Solar Cells

Jianxing Xia, Junsheng Luo, Hua Yang, Fengjiao Zhao, Zhongquan Wan, Haseeb Ashraf Malik, Yu Shi, Keli Han, Xiaojun Yao, and Chunyang Jia*

In perovskite solar cells (PSCs), the interfaces of the halide perovskite/electron transport layer (ETL) and ETL/metal oxide electrode (MOE) always attract and trap free carriers via the surface electrostatic force, altering quasi-Fermi level ( $ E_{\text{Fq}} $ ) splitting of contact interfaces, and significantly limit the charge extraction efficiency and intrinsic stability of devices. Herein, a graded “bridge” is first reported to link the MOE and perovskite interfaces by self vertical phase separation doping (PSD), diminishing the side effect of notorious ionic defects via both reinforced interface  $ E_{\text{bi}} $  and the vacancies filling. Experimental and theoretical results prove that the inhomogeneous distribution of CsF in the bulk or surface of PC $ _{61} $ BM would not only form metal–oxygen (M–O) dipole on MOE, reinforcing the interface  $ E_{\text{bi}} $ , but also create a graded energy bridge to alleviate the disadvantage of band offset raised by the enhanced interface  $ E_{\text{bi}} $ , which significantly avoid the carrier accumulation and recombination at defective interfaces. Employing PSD, the power conversion efficiency of the devices approaches 21% with a high open-circuit voltage (1.148 V) and delivers a high stability of 89% after aging 60 days in atmosphere without encapsulation, which is the highest efficiency of organic electron transport layers for n–i–p PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Solar cells based on halide perovskite (PSCs) have attracted vast interest in recent years ascribe to their excellent optical and electronic properties, such as intense absorption in visible region, long charge carrier diffusion lengths and lifetimes.  $  ^{[1,2]}  $   The power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of PSCs surpassed 25%  $  ^{[3-11]}  $   and became the most potential candidates for next-generation solar cells, since the first reported by Miyasaka in 2009.  $  ^{[12]}  $

In addition, some works focus on the interfacial buffer layers (IBLs) to reinforce the interface build-in field ( $ E_{bi} $ ) $ ^{[31-33]} $  and tuning the interfacial energy level by the inorganic alkali metal compound doping ETLs to enhance the transfer of interface carriers, $ ^{[34]} $  paving important ways to suppress the interface recombination of ETLs/perovskite. However, merely the single interface of ETLs/perovskite involved by IBLs and the doping strategies, as well as are not consideration to the interface defects of MOE/ETLs, which is another important factor for the recombination of carriers during charge collection. Researchers modified the electrode work function ( $ W_F $ ) to get better match with the ETLs to reduce the interface recombination. $ ^{[35]} $  Employing two steps to simultaneously modify both interfaces of MOE/ETLs and ETLs/perovskite may be the possible way to obtain perfect interface of MOE/perovskite. Nevertheless, the multistep of interface modifications are complicated and unavoidably induce superfluous defects during the multistep processes. Most importantly, the band offset invariably tends to rise with the  $ E_{bi} $ , making the interface of MOE/perovskite is hard to simultaneously achieve high  $ E_{bi} $  and low interface barriers at the function of IBLs. $ ^{[36]} $  Thus, developing a facile strategy to fabricate perfect MOE/perovskite heterojunction interface should take into consideration.

Here, the vertical phase separation of cesium fluoride (CsF) n-type dopant in   $  PC_{61}BM  $   is first performed to bridge the MOE/perovskite interface which enhances the electron transfer from perovskite to the MOE by enhanced   $  E_{bi}  $   and defects passivation. Experiments and theories certify that CsF will accumulate at ITO surface in the   $  PC_{61}BM:CsF  $   mixture via the self-phase separation which driven by polar surface and its oxygen vacancies, as well as the surface energy difference between the polar of CsF and the nonpolar organic   $  PC_{61}BM  $ . This efficient interface attributes to 1) the bottom accumulated CsF form metal–oxygen (M–O) dipole at MOE surface, which transfer surface charges and enlarge the   $  E_{bi}  $   of MOE/perovskite; 2) bulk of CsF as the n-type dopant upshift   $  E_{F}  $   of   $  PC_{61}BM  $   which reduces the side effect of band-offset caused by reinforced   $  E_{bi}  $   of MOE/perovskite; 3) surface remnants CsF passivated the undercoordinated ionic defects which reduced the interface carrier recombination. As a result, the n–i–p PSC based on   $  PC_{61}BM  $   exhibits exceptional efficiency of 20.93% which is the highest PCE among the organic electron transport layers (OETLs) to date. Further, the device also delivers high stability after aging 60 days in atmosphere without encapsulation due to the intrinsic factors, which suppress interfaces charge accumulation and the escape of   $  MA^{+}  $  cation from perovskite.

Nevertheless, there are also disadvantages that the CsF will not consider to the important ETL/perovskite interface, and the unduly enhancement of the interface  $ E_{\text{bi}} $  will also increase the band offset which

In the architecture based on PSD (Figure 2n), the enriched CsF on the ITO and the remained CsF on the PC $ _{61} $ BM surface not only respectively reinforce the  $ E_{\text{bi}} $  of heterojunction interfaces of ITO/ETL and the ETL/perovskite due to the surface M–O dipole and the n-type doping of PC $ _{61} $ BM, but also reduce the band offset by the gradient “bridge” energy level which concatenate the two interfaces of ITO and perovskite. This adjective energy alignment could enhance the free carrier transfer at these interfaces under higher  $ E_{\text{bi}} $  simultaneously with small barriers, avoid the carrier trapping at these vacancy defects enriched interfaces. Optimizing the doping concentrations of PSD would attain the efficient energy level interfaces. Thus, the PSD may provide a better route to reduce the recombination at these indispensable interface defects and pave a way to bridge the heterojunction interface for transportation.

Additionally, the steady-state fluorescence spectrum (PL) of the CsF based architecture (Figure 3b) exhibits significant quenching behavior which is consistent with the above mentioned more remarkable carrier transportation properties. It is worth noting that the blueshifted PL peak from 756 to 754 nm after the CsF doping of the PC $ _{61} $ BM, which indicates that the existence of surface CsF could passivate the surface defects of perovskite and density of trap states decrease from  $ 1.68 \times 10^{16} $  to  $ 8.96 \times 10^{15} $  cm $ ^{-3} $  (Figure S13, Supporting Information) as compared to the bare PC $ _{61} $ BM-based sample.

# 2.4. Surface Contact Defects Passivation

To further explore the mechanism of passivation, the typical undercoordinated ionic defect sites which certified to generate gap charge states in the organic-inorganic perovskite film, $ ^{[14]} $  a cation of Pb-dimer and the anionic I-dimer on the MAI termination and PbI $ _2 $  termination of (001) perovskite surfaces with  $ 2 \times 1 $  periodicity in the  $ x-y $  plane are in respectively to further investigate the role of residual CsF on the surface trap passivation in PSD strategy. The side of lowest unoccupied conduction band states and the highest occupied valence band states with or without the passivation are exhibited in Figure 4a-h, and the top side of key slabs corresponding with Figure 4 is placed in Figure S16 (Supporting Information). It is distinct that the Pb-dimer defects give rise to the localization of the lowest unoccupied conduction band states on the perovskite surface, inverse the charge localization of I-dimer defects dominating in the highest occupied valence band states due to the both nonequilibrium surface defects charge. After the CsF adsorbs on the defect sur-

faces, it can be seen from the constructions that the F atoms is prone to exclude the I  $  ^{-}  $  anions and attract the Pb  $  ^{2+}  $  cations, as well as the N atom of the MA  $  ^{+}  $  component by the N–H  $  \cdots  $  F bond due to the stronger electronegativity compared to the I atoms (Figure S16d, Supporting Information), which may fill the vacancies of I atoms. Nevertheless, the Cs atom is prone to fill the surface absent vacancies of MA  $  ^{+}  $  and interact with the I atoms at the center of the tetrahedral halide cage and the surface I-dimer, leading to a surface coordination (Figure S16c, Supporting Information).  $  ^{[53]}  $  The extra surface ionic interactions with the defect slabs may result in a shift of the defects states, increasing the overlap between the wavefunctions of the dimer defects and the surface slabs which eliminate the gap defects states and avert the trapping of free charges, in addition inhibiting the ions vacant motion and improve the intrinsic stability of PSCs via the reinforced interface interactions. To get more detailed change of the defects by the function of surface CsF, the key partial density of states (PDOS) of surface perovskite units is given in Figure 4i,j, and the full PDOS is exhibited in Figure S17 (Supporting Information). In the all perovskite slab surfaces which without defects, with dimer defects and with passivation (Figure S17, Supporting Information), the I 5p and Pb 6p contribute the major of valence band (VB) and conduction band (CB), respectively. However, there are new gap states in the defect slabs which are mainly contributed by the p orbitals of Pb-dimer and I-dimer, as well as serve as deep traps due to the states been occupied by excess electron and hole. After passivation, the gap states of slab with I-dimer defects move to the VB and the p orbitals of I-dimer exhibit a new hybridization with the surface slab which bridge the trap states with states of the VB edge (Figure 4i), opposite in the Pb-dimer based slab, the Pb-dimer 5p is become lower and majorly dedicate to the CB edge, resulting in the reduction of defect states which avoid the charge trapping (Figure 4j). Except surface passivation of the remaining CsF, the incorporation of the F  $  ^{-}  $  ions in the perovskite crystal also had been considered. The result shows that the activation energy (  $  \Delta E_{A}  $  ) of MA  $  ^{+}  $  vacancies in the F  $  ^{-}  $  ions incorporated lattice is 34.39 kcal mol  $  ^{-1}  $  which is much higher than that in the pristine perovskite crystal, indicating the difficulty in formation of vacancies defect sites (Figure S18, Supporting Information). These DFT results indicate that the surface CsF in phase separation strategy could further passivate the trap states of perovskite, consisting with the experimental results. In contrast to the perovskite side, the accumulated F  $  ^{-}  $  ion via self-phase separation at the ITO side will fill the notorious oxygen vacancies and reduce the surface accumulation of charge density which act as the defect sites (Figure S19, Supporting Information). Thus, in the PSD strategy, the “bridge” between the perovskite and ITO is not only involved to the energy level, but also relate to the decreasing of interface defects.

# 2.6. Stability

The stability is also the key obstacle which limit PSCs for further applications. Hence, the stability of unencapsulated PSCs which stored in the ambient atmosphere were further examined, and the corresponding normalized photoelectric properties decay fabricated on the different ETL films were shown in Figure 6a and Figure S26a–c (Supporting Information). It shows that the PSC based on PSD retains about 89% of the initial PCE after aging 60 days; however, the device based on the bare PC  $  _{61}  $  BM exhibits faster decline in PCE and retains about 77% of the initial efficiency under the same degradation conditions. To explore the reason of the improved stability under ambient atmosphere, the compared perovskite crystal characteristics of degraded devices carried by 2D-GIXD (Figure 6c,d). All the devices which fabricate on PSD and bare PC  $  _{61}  $  BM under

the ambient atmosphere at the same aged time of 40 days exhibit semblable perovskite crystal reflections of (110), (220), and (310) at  $  q \approx 10, 20  $ , and  $  22.6~\mathrm{nm}^{-1}  $ , as well as no additional reflections of perovskite hydrate. It implies the ultrahigh stability of perovskite crystals and the moisture almost not influence the perovskite films in these devices which are extremely related to the hydrophobic LAD in PTAA HTL rather than the hydrophilic dopant of Li-TFSI.[50,54] The results prove that the ambient oxygen and moisture may not the pivotal reasons for the influence of stability in the both type of devices.

Except the alteration of the crystal structure, the ionic migration has been attracted wide attention in PSCs including the investigations through both experimental and theoretical methods which could change the charge equilibrium of contact interface, decreasing the performance of PSCs during aging. It demonstrates that the organic groups of perovskite (such as MA $ ^{+} $ ) are easy to migrate via the point defects even at room temperature due to the much low activation energy for migration. $ ^{[55-57]} $  In the PSD, the surface MA $ ^{+} $ organic ions will rotate toward to the F atom in the optimization process and exhibit a stronger N–H $ \cdots $ F bond comparing to the I atom due to the higher electronegativity of F atom (Figure 6e–g). It implies that the more robust interaction at the ETL/perovskite interface which should suppress the escape of organic ions in the perovskite lattice. In addition, the severe charge accumulation should localize at these defects, which would hinder the formation of stable electric field distribution within the devices during aging, presumably resulting in much faster degradation of performances. By employing PSD, the interface of ETL/perovskite, ITO/ETL show lower defects states and faster carrier transfer which finally avoid the capture and accumulation of interface charge. Therefore, the inhibition of intrinsic ionic migration and charge accumulation may be the fundamental of the improved intrinsic stability in this PSD strategy, emphasized the role of interface on the stability of PSCs. $ ^{[58]} $  Furthermore, in the 1 Sun illumination (AM 1.5G) condition (Figure 6b; Figure S26d–f, Supporting Information), the PSC based on the PSD also exhibits a high photostability which still retain 92% of the initial efficiency under the illumination 1440 min. In the bare PC $ _{61} $ BM based device, the photodegradation is faster than the devices based on PSD and retains about 83% of the initial efficiency at the same illumination time, which further confirm the improved stability of PSC based on PSD strategy.
