# 1. Introduction

Since the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite solar cells (PSCs) was first introduced in 2009 (Kojima et al., 2009), organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs have aroused considerable interests and displayed rapid progress (Petrus et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2018b; Yip and Jen, 2012). The solar-to-electrical power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has reached to over  $ 23\% $  (Green et al., 2018; Jeon et al., 2018). Two structures, i.e. inverted (p-i-n) and regular (n-i-p), were primarily used for solid thin-film PSCs (Chueh et al., 2015). The typical inverted structure in perovskite solar cells consist of hole transport layer (HTL) (PEDOT:PSS), light-harvesting active layer (perovskite) and electron transport layer (ETL) (PCBM). The inverted structure has many advantages over the regular structure, e.g. simple preparation process, low preparation temperature, negligibly hysteresis behavior and ideal stability (Luo et al., 2018; Yip and Jen, 2012). So the inverted device was closer to commercialization, as compared with the mesoporous regular architecture. Furthermore, Zhu et al. improved the efficiency of inverted planar heterojunction PSCs devices, which has reached  $ 21\% $  (Luo et al., 2018).

Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene): poly(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT: PSS) as the most extensive organic hole transport material, was used in the inverted devices for its low temperature process, suitable valance band maximum, good electrical conductivity and good flexibility (Chen

The power conversion efficiency (PCE) reported for the inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is still less than that of the regular structure, and low open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) is one of the crucial problems for achieving high PCE in inverted PSCs. In this study, a new modification strategy was developed by doping poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)-poly-(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT: PSS) with the cetyl trimethyl ammonium bromide (CTAB) to passivate the bottom of perovskite layer and enhance properties of PEDOT:PSS. The work function of PEDOT: PSS was altered from -4.18 to -4.21 eV, the Fermi level of CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS better fitted to the valence band of perovskite layer, thereby leading to a higher build-in voltage. As a result, the   $  V_{oc}  $   increased by 70 mV, up to 0.90 V and the PCE of the device increased from 10.21% to 12.53%. Furthermore, the device based on CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS still retained over 75% of the initial PCE after exposing the device in the ambient condition (20–40% humidity, 10–30 °C) for 30 days.

# 2. Experimental section

Materials: lead (II) iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2) $  and Methylamine iodide (MAI) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS,PVP AI4083), Chlorobenzene (CB) and Cetyl trimethyl ammonium bromide (CTAB) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Anhydrous N,N-Dimethylformamide (DMF), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and [6,6]-Phenyl-C61-butyric Acid Methyl Ester  $ (\mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM}) $  were purchased from Aladdin.

Fabrication of the device: ITO layers on glass substrates, with a sheet resistance of   $  8\Omega/\square  $  (Pilkington), were cleaned with alkaline detergent, acetone, absolute ethanol and deionized water for 15 min, the cleaned substrates were UV-ozone treated for 15 min, respectively, before they were used for spin-coating. The PEDOT:PSS solution with

different amount of CTAB was spin-coated onto the ITO substrates at 4000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 150 °C for 10 min. MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  precursor solution was prepared by mixing 159 mg CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  I, 462 mg PbI  $  _{2}  $  in anhydrous DMF:DMSO (9:1, v/v), then filtered with 0.22 μm nylon filter to obtain a clear solution. The MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  precursor solution was spin-coated on the PEDOT: PSS coated substrates at 3000 rpm for 30 s and 200 μL chlorobenzene was slowly dripped onto the surface of the film 10 s after the beginning of spin-coating. Perovskite layer was annealed at 100 °C for 20 min resulting in a thickness of 300 nm. After cooling to room temperature naturally, 20 mg/ml PC  $  _{61}  $  BM dissolved in CB was spin-coated onto the perovskite films at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, Ag electrode was deposited using thermal evaporation at a constant evaporation rate of 0.1 nm/s. Except for the fabrication of PEDOT:PSS layer, the whole process is carried out in glove-box under Ar condition at room temperature.

Characterization: Atom Force Microscope (AFM) images were obtained using a Multimode 8 atomic force microscope using tapping mode. The photovoltaic performance of PSCs was recorded using a Keithley 2400 source meter with a delay time of  $ 100\mathrm{ms} $  under one-sun,

# 3. Results and discussion

The chemical structures of CTAB and PEDOT: PSS are shown in Fig. 1(a) (b). Long carbon chain, ammonium group and  $ \mathrm{Br}^{-} $  can be observed in the structure of CTAB. The PEDOT:PSS was the polymer composite integrated through Coulomb interaction between a negatively charged PSS and a positively charged PEDOT (Fan et al., 2018; Huang et al., 2017; Ha et al., 2018). Furthermore, the role of CTAB at the interface of perovskite and PEDOT:PSS layer is shown in Fig. 1(c), the  $ \mathrm{Br}^{-} $  in the CTAB could diffuse into the bottom-side of the perovskite

layer, leading to higher   $  V_{oc}  $   (Long et al., 2017) and the ammonium group in CTAB participated in the passivation process (Cai et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2019, 2018d).

The transmittance spectra of PEDOT:PSS and CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS films is shown in Fig. 2(a). High optical transmittance of the PEDOT:PSS in the range of 400–800 nm was required for high performance PSCs. The results show that the incorporation of small amount of CTAB had no effect on the optical transmittance of PEDOT:PSS film. Over 90% optical transmittance in the visible region and low transmittance in the UV region was observed for the PEDOT:PSS and CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS films.

The I-V characteristics are shown in Fig. 2(b) to analyze the effect of CTAB on the conductivity of PEDOT:PSS film. Besides the sandwich structures of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/Au and ITO/CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS/Au were used to measure I-V characteristics of PEDOT:PSS and CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS films. Atomic force microscopy (AFM) images of the PEDOT: PSS film with and without CTAB are shown in Fig. 2(d), the results demonstrate that both PEDOT: PSS and the CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS were uniform and homogeneous. The roughness of the hole transport layer below the perovskite layer affected the quality of perovskite grain growth (Wang et al., 2016; Xue et al., 2018; Yang et al., 2017; Zhao et al., 2016), the roughness of PEDOT: PSS film and the CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS film were concluded as  $ 1.63\mathrm{nm} $  (pure PEDOT: PSS) and  $ 1.33\mathrm{nm} $  (the CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS), respectively. Furthermore, the corresponding images of water droplets on the surface of the PEDOT: PSS and the CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS films are given in Fig. 2(e). The contact angle of PEDOT: PSS film significantly increased from  $ 5^{\circ} $  to  $ 27^{\circ} $  with the incorporation of CTAB because CTAB is a kind of quaternary ammonium salt with a certain degree of hydrophobicity, the larger contact angle of PEDOT:PSS film may be beneficial to the growth of perovskite layer (Yang et al., 2018).

The results of ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) are shown in Fig. 3(a) (b) to measure the work function and valence band of the PEDOT: PSS and CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS films. The band gap calculation using the Tauc plot is presented in Fig. 3(c). The corresponding energy level diagram of the PEDOT:PSS or CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS films is presented in Fig. 3(d). It can be obtained from these figures suggest that the work function (WF) of the PEDOT:PSS film

shifted from -4.18 eV to -4.21 eV and the valence band varied from -5.20 eV to -5.27 eV with the introduction of CTAB. In Fig. 3(c), the band gaps of PEDOT:PSS and CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS films were 4.00 eV and 4.03 eV. The formation of deep WF in the PEDOT:PSS reduced the energy offsets and barrier at the interface of PEDOT:PSS/perovskite (Ameen et al., 2018; Huang et al., 2017).

SEM images of perovskite layer deposited on PEDOT: PSS and CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS are given in Fig. 4(a), (b). The results demonstrate that the perovskite films deposited on PEDOT:PSS and CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS films were both uniform and homogeneous without any hole and defect, which may be favorable to photovoltaic performance of PSCs. In order to explore the effect of contact angle on the growth of the perovskite films, the distribution of the grain size was measured using Nano-Measurer software. The average grain size of the perovskite film deposited on PEDOT: PSS (w/o CTAB) was  $ 190\mathrm{nm} $ . The average grain size of the perovskite film deposited on CTAB-complexed PEDOT: PSS (w CTAB) was  $ 220\mathrm{nm} $ . This indicates that the CTAB-complexed PEDOT: PSS hole transport material might be beneficial to achieve the large grain size. Cross-section image of the device is shown in Fig. 4(e), the structure of the device was ITO/PEDOT:PSS/MAPbI3/PCBM/Ag, the thickness of each layer has been optimized ( $ \sim 200\mathrm{nm} $  for ITO,  $ \sim 50\mathrm{nm} $  for PDEOT:PSS,  $ \sim 300\mathrm{nm} $  for perovskite and  $ \sim 80\mathrm{nm} $  for PCBM film).

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, a new strategy was reported to improve the interface of the perovskite and PEDOT:PSS layer by using CTAB. The work function of PEDOT: PSS was altered from -4.18 to -4.21 eV, the Fermi level of the CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS better fitted to the valence band of perovskite layer, leading to a higher build-in voltage. Moreover, the ammonium group in CTAB would passivate the bottom surface of perovskite film, Br  $  ^{-}  $  in CTAB would diffuse into the perovskite film and exchange with iodide ion during the annealing process. As a result, PCE of the PSCs increased from 10.21% to 12.53% with the increase of  $  J_{sc}  $  and  $  V_{oc}  $ . Besides, the long-term stability for 30 days of the device with CTAB was also enhanced, the device based on CTAB-doped PEDOT:PSS still retained over 75% of the initial PCE. This work illustrates that optimizing the traditional functional layers is still an effective way to increase the efficiency of perovskite solar cells.
