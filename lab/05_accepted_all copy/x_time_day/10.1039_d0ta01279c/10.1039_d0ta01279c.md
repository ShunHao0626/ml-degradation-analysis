# Conjugated polyelectrolyte with potassium cations enables inverted perovskite solar cells with an efficiency over 20%

Luozheng Zhang, $ ^{abc\dagger} $  Xianyong Zhou, $ ^{bc\dagger} $  Jiaming Xie, $ ^{bc} $  Shi Chen, $ ^{abc} $  Sanghoon Bae, $ ^{d} $  Jeehwan Kim $ ^{d} $  and Baomin Xu $ ^{bc*} $

Defect-passivation functional groups have been extensively explored in perovskite solar cells (PSCs) in recent years; however, most of these groups were organic, and the integration of inorganic functional groups with organic matrixes bearing other functions has rarely been reported. Here, to demonstrate the effective of this integration, the coalition of an inorganic group,  $ \mathsf{K}^+ $  ion, and an organic conjugated polyelectrolyte (CPE) matrix which serves as a hole transporting material (HTM) is attempted. The resulting CPE material TB(K) presents much stronger ability of defect-passivation and hole extraction, and thus yields a much lifted power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 20.01\% $  and an increased long-term stability when applied in the inverted PSC devices compared with its control polymer TB(Na), where only the inorganic cations are varied from a defect-passivation group to a common one.

After ten years of explosive growth, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have reached a new record power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.2%, which surpasses most of the traditional solar cells based on multicrystalline silicon, CuInGaSe, and CdTe.  $  ^{1}  $   This big progress is believed to be achieved based on the device with a regular n-i-p configuration, while the PSCs with an inverted p-i-n structure still lag behind,  $  ^{2}  $   thus a large quantity of research has been devoted to improve their efficiency as well as long-term stability.  $  ^{3-9}  $   As a typical hole transporting material (HTM) in inverted devices, poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS)

could erode its underneath conductive layers like fluorine doped tin oxide (FTO) and indium tin oxide (ITO) due to its acidic dopant PSS, leading to a poor stability, and thus various alternatives have been widely explored, including organic  $  ^{10-21}  $  and inorganic ones  $  ^{22-27}  $ . Among these, conjugated polyelectrolytes (CPEs) have been paid special attention since its high hydrophilicity and wettability to the superjacent perovskite layer, which is supposed to be compatible to the ready casting of perovskite solution during the future industrial fabrication processing.  $  ^{28-30}  $  The mechanical flexibility of CPE also renders it a promising HTM once PSCs are integrated into wearable devices. CPE was first introduced as an HTM in 2015, where a superior long-term stability and a higher PCE of 12.51% were demonstrated.  $  ^{15}  $  Thereafter, a number of CPE materials were designed and investigated as HTMs. Polythiophene with COONa sidechains,  $  ^{18}  $  CPE based on benzene and thiophene units with  $  SO_{3}Na  $  sidechains,  $  ^{16}  $  CPE composed of benzothiadiazole and benzene units with  $  SO_{3}Na  $  sidechains,  $  ^{20}  $  and CPE constructed by fluorine and triphenylamine units with  $  SO_{3}Na  $  sidechains  $  ^{21}  $  were successively reported. As we noticed, sidechains of  $  SO_{3}Na  $  and COONa were frequently chosen to provide the polymers with water/alcohol solubility; however, no any other featured functions were designed or even mentioned for these sidechain groups, and a PCE of only 16.6% was obtained.  $  ^{18,21}  $  The situation changed in 2017, when an organic cation, methylammonium ion (MA  $  ^{+}  $ ), was incorporated for the first time, and the corresponding CPE material P3CT-COOMA accomplished a high PCE of 19.6% owing to its improved solubility and outstanding defect-passivation ability.  $  ^{19}  $  In 2019, our group reported a CPE named TB(MA) whose PSC device yielded a PCE of 19.76%, which is the highest PCE so far realized for CPE based PSCs.  $  ^{31}  $  Moreover, the defect-passivation superiority of MA  $  ^{+}  $  ions over Na  $  ^{+}  $  ions was quantitatively demonstrated for the first time. These results suggest that defect-passivation capability and any other functions beyond solubility should also be in mind when one designs a CPE material, especially at the current stage when no CPE materials reaching a PCE of 20% have been released.

Meanwhile, with the continuous pursuit of high efficiency and stability in recent years, defect-passivation has attracted more and more attention, and consequently various functional groups with defect-passivation effects emerged.  $  ^{32}  $   Nevertheless, most of these groups are organic, while the inorganic groups are very limited even though a remarkable performance enhancement was often attained.  $  ^{32}  $   Furthermore, the materials bearing these functional groups dominantly served as a pure defect passivator, and the combination with other functions like charge transportation has rarely been reported.  $  ^{33-35}  $   Therefore, it would be very desirable if inorganic groups could be integrated with organic matrix materials even organic charge transporting materials, and then more extensive applications can be definitely expected. As mentioned above, we have proved that the copolymer constructed with benzene and thiophene as the main chain units could be a decent HTM,  $  ^{34}  $   and its CPE analog TB(MA) has shown a prevailed performance over PEDOT:PSS.  $  ^{31}  $   As a result, the copolymer based on benzene and thiophene units could be a promising matrix to demonstrate the feasibility of integration with inorganic defect-passivation groups. On the other hand, as one of the typical inorganic groups, K  $  ^{+}  $   ion, which gradually became flourishing in last two years, has shown its strong passivation effect when doping charge transporting materials  $  ^{36-39}  $   or perovskite layers  $  ^{40-44}  $   with inorganic potassium halides  $  ^{37-44}  $   or hydroxide  $  ^{36}  $  . As a consequence, the alliance of K  $  ^{+}  $   with the copolymer could set an example for the integration of inorganic functional groups with organic substrates, and is expected to exhibit exciting properties.

In this work, we incorporate   $  K^{+}  $  into organic materials for the first time and a CPE HTM based on the copolymer of benzene and thiophene units (noted as TB(K), Figure 1a) is designed and synthesized as a demonstration. Thanks to its outstanding defect-passivation and hole-extraction ability, TB(K) presents a much superior photovoltaic performance than its analogue TB(Na) with   $  Na^{+}  $  as the cation. The PSC devices based on TB(K) display a very high PCE of 20.01%, which is the first report for CPE based PSCs with a PCE of over 20%, and an increased long-term stability relative to those TB(Na) devices. These results indicate that   $  K^{+}  $  could be explored in the organic charge transporting materials, and more importantly inorganic functional groups could also cooperate with organic matrixes to broaden their functions and the corresponding applications.

The molecular structures of CPE polymers TB(Na) and TB(K) are shown in Figure 1a, which were synthesized through the similar procedures to the reported ones except the MA ion was replaced by Na or K ion.  $  ^{31}  $   Their work functions were characterized by the ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) and calculated to be 5.09 eV and 5.10 eV for TB(Na) and TB(K) film coated on ITO glass, respectively (Figure S1), suggesting both of them could work as HTMs and efficiently extract the photogenerated holes from the perovskite layer in view of their matched energy levels. As for the TAS test, it is widely used to quantitatively evaluate the distribution of trap states, and is carried out based on the exactly same device configuration of PSC. The integrated trap densities are summarized in Table S3, where the concentration of deep traps is obviously reduced but that of shallow traps is slightly increased after introducing   $  K^{+}  $  . Since the deep traps are recognized to arise from the defects on the surface of perovskite film, the remarkably decreased number of defects indicates that the interface of CPE/perovskite is defect-passivated due to the presence of   $  K^{+}  $  . As we know, there is a tolerance factor requirement that one material has to meet if it would like to be of perovskite crystal. The ionic radii of   $  Na^{+}  $  and   $  K^{+}  $  are 95 pm and 133 pm, respectively, and the Goldschmidt tolerance factors of MPbI  $  _{3}  $   based on M=Na and M=K are calculated to be 0.65 and 0.73, respectively.  $  ^{47}  $   As a consequence, both   $  Na^{+}  $  and   $  K^{+}  $  ions cannot be used to form a perovskite crystal. But with a closer value to the tolerance factor requirement (0.8-1.0),  $  ^{47}  $     $  KPbI_{3}  $   is closer to perovskite in the respect of crystal lattice than   $  NaPbI_{3}  $  . Therefore, the interface of CPE/perovskite for TB(K) could be more ordered than that for TB(Na), leading to less defects and more efficient hole-extraction at the TB(K)/perovskite interface as well as the resulting better photovoltaic performance. This is also implied by the increased shunt resistance (  $  R_{sh}  $  ) value (Table 1) which reflects the suppressed charge recombination at the CPE/perovskite interface. On the other hand, the concentration of the shallow traps which are believed to come from these defects in the bulk of perovskite layer is lifted, agreeing well with the conclusion from the PL assessment. This increased concentration may be attributed to the more grain-boundaries existing in the perovskite film atop TB(K) layer, as illustrated in Figure S3c and S3d. To sum up the above evaluation results, we can see that the hole-extraction ability and the defect-passivation at the interface of CPE/perovskite could be apparently improved after replacing   $  Na^{+}  $  with   $  K^{+}  $  . Although the defect in the bulk of perovskite film suffers a slight increase, the sharply lessened surface defects make the total concentration of defects decreased by 16.5%, and therefore the photovoltaic performance is reasonably remarkably enhanced to a superior PCE of 20.01%, which is much higher than the previous record (19.76%) for PSC devices based on CPE HTMs.  $  ^{31}  $

The stability of the devices was also surveyed. As seen in Figure 3f, the TB(K) device could maintain 84% of its original PCE after storing in air with a relative humidity of 60%, which is better than its counterpart based on TB(Na). Since the defects could act as active centers for degradation,  $  ^{48}  $  the less defects for TB(K) device may render it more stable than its control device based on TB(Na).

# Conclusion

In summary,  $ \mathsf{K}^+ $  ion and the copolymer based on benzene and thiophene units were chosen as an example to demonstrate

the practicability of cooperating inorganic functional groups with organic matrixes for the first time, and thus a CPE HTM with  $ \mathsf{K}^+ $  was intentionally designed. The resulting polymer TB(K) possessed a superior ability of hole-extraction and defect-passivation, and all of the photovoltaic parameters were improved compared with its control polymer, yielding a PCE of as high as  $ 20.01\% $  and a prolonged long-term stability. Our results confirmed the prosperity of choosing  $ \mathsf{K}^+ $  as the cation in the design of organic charge transporting materials, and significantly indicated the broad future of designing and application of the integration of inorganic functional groups and organic substrates in optoelectronic devices.

Preparation of perovskite precursor solutions: The FA0.85MA0.15Pb(Br0.15I0.85)3 precursor solution was prepared by mixing MABr (0.15 mmol), FAI (0.85 mmol), PbI2 (0.85 mmol) and PbBr2 (0.15 mmol) powder in 593 ul DMF and 148 ul DMSO.

Patterned ITO coated glass substrates were sequentially cleaned in detergent, deionized water, acetone, as well as in isopropanol.
