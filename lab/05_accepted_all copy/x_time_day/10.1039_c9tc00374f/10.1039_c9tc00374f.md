# Efficient and carbon-based hole transport layer-free   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   planar perovskite solar cells using PMMA modification

Xiang Zhang, $ ^{a} $  Yang Zhou, $ ^{a} $  Yuzhu Li, $ ^{a} $  Jiawen Sun, $ ^{a} $  Xubing Lu, $ ^{a} $  Xingsen Gao, $ ^{a} $  Jinwei Gao, $ ^{a} $  Lingling Shui, $ ^{b} $  Sujuan Wu $ ^{*a} $  and Jun-Ming Liu $ ^{a,c} $

In this work, the planar inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with the simple structure of glass/ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /CsPbI $ _2 $ Br/C have been fabricated. The solution-processed poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA) is selected to modify the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film. The effects of PMMA modification on the microstructure of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film and the photoelectric properties of inorganic PSCs have been systematically investigated. The concentrations of PMMA solution have been optimized. At the optimal concentration, the modified carbon-based hole transport layer (HTL)-free CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs demonstrate the improved open-circuit voltage (V $ _{oc} $ ) and fill factor (FF), and achieve a champion efficiency of 10.95% with less hysteresis behavior, being much higher than 9.14% of reference PSCs. Moreover, the unencapsulated CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs modified by PMMA demonstrate the improved stability and remain about 95% of its initial efficiency after being exposed in air (relative humidity of 25%-35%) for 20 days. The enhanced performance is mainly attributed to the reduced trap state density and suppressed charge recombination. This work provides a simple route to fabricate efficient and stable carbon-based HTL-free inorganic PSCs.

# Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials have attracted lots of attention in the field of perovskite solar cells (PSCs), due to their excellent photoelectric properties such as long carrier diffusion length,  $  ^{1-3}  $  large absorption coefficient,  $  ^{4-6}  $  high carrier mobility  $  ^{7,8}  $  and so on. Until now, the certified efficiency of organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs has exceeded 23%.  $  ^{9}  $  However, the device stability still needs to be improved. It has been reported that the intrinsic stability of perovskite materials can be improved by replacing the organic cation with inorganic Cs*.  $  ^{10,11}  $  Although CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  material has reasonable bandgap of 1.73 eV, its cubic phase is unstable in ambient atmosphere.  $  ^{11,12}  $  The CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $  material shows the better stability, but its large bandgap (E  $  _{g}  $  = 2.3 eV) is not suitable for photovoltaic applications.  $  ^{11,12}  $  Fortunately, the mixed-halide CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br material has an appropriate bandgap of 1.91 eV and exhibits a better phase stability in ambient air.  $  ^{11}  $  The theoretical efficiency of CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br PSCs is predicted up to 22.1%.  $  ^{13}  $  Obviously the CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br material is a promising candidate for photovoltaic applications. A high-quality CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br film is necessary for the efficient charge extraction and transport in PSCs.  $  ^{14}  $  However, most of the CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br films are

fabricated by a simple one-step spin-coating method.  $  ^{15, 16}  $   It is difficult to prepare a pinhole-free and uniform   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film due to its slow crystallization.  $  ^{11}  $   Moreover, it is reported that most of the charge recombination and material degradation occur at the grain boundary and interface.  $  ^{17}  $   To improve the performance of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs, a series of works have been done to control the crystallization process,  $  ^{18-23}  $   reduce the trap states  $  ^{24-26}  $   or optimize the interface.  $  ^{10, 12, 27, 28}  $   For example, Liu et al. have synthesized Lewis base adducts as precursors to fabricate   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with the efficiency of 14.78%.  $  ^{20}  $   Tian et al. have achieved an efficiency of 12.34% by reducing the trap states on the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film using   $  \mathrm{Pb(NO_{3})_{2}}  $ .  $  ^{25}  $   Jin et al. have attained an efficiency of 14.45% by modifying the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br/PTAA  $   interface using   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   quantum dots.  $  ^{10}  $   Recently, the efficiency of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs has been enhanced to 16.07%.  $  ^{23}  $   Regrettably, the organic hole transport layer (HTL) and evaporated gold (Au) electrode are used in these efficient   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. This not only increases the cost, but also reduces the stability of PSCs.

To solve these problems, the all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs based on carbon (C) electrode have been developed.  $  ^{[11, 29]}  $   But the efficiency of carbon-based   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs is still much lower than that of the organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs. Although the carbon-based inorganic PSCs have achieved a efficiency over 10%, it is still lower than that of the inorganic PSCs based on the evaporated metal electrode and organic HTL.  $  ^{[29, 30]}  $   It is reported that the lower efficiency in carbon-based inorganic PSCs is closely related to the high trap state density.  $  ^{[11, 29, 30]}  $   The trap states can not only cause the ion migration, current density-voltage (J-V) hysteresis and device degradation in ambient environment,  $  ^{[31]}  $   but also generate shunt-leakage paths due to direct contact between C electrode and electron transport layer (ETL), resulting in the large charge recombination and the reduced open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) and fill factor (FF) of PSCs.  $  ^{[32, 33]}  $   It is necessary to remove or passivate the trap states in order to obtain

It is found that charge recombination can be suppressed by incorporating polymers with Lewis base groups.  $  ^{34}  $   Up to now, the various polymers have been used to reduce the trap states in organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs to promote charge extraction and collection.  $  ^{17, 32, 35-39}  $   It has been identified that PMMA can passivate the trap states at grain boundary and interfaces, resulting in the reduced charge recombination and improved performance in organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs.  $  ^{40-42}  $   However, the PMMA has not been used to improve the performance of inorganic PSCs, to the best of our knowledge.

Based on these considerations, the inorganic PSCs with the simple structure of glass/ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /CsPbI $ _2 $ Br/C were fabricated. The PMMA solution was spin-coated on the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film. For convenience of presentation, two types of carbon-based CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs are discussed in this work: reference-film/PSC in which CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film/PSC without PMMA modification, PMMA-film/PSC in which CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film/PSC with PMMA modification. The effects of PMMA modification on the microstructure of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film and the photoelectric properties of carbon-based CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs have been investigated. The results indicate that the PMMA modification can effectively improve the performance of carbon-based CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs. At the optimal PMMA solution concentration, the champion efficiency of PMMA-PSC is improved to 10.95% from 9.14% of reference-PSC. Moreover, the PMMA-PSC show the less J-V hysteresis behavior and improved stability. A series of measurements have been carried out to investigate the related mechanisms for the enhanced performance. It is found that the PMMA modification can effectively suppress the non-radiative recombination induced by the trap states in CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film and the recombination at CsPbI $ _2 $ Br/C interface, resulting in the promoted charge extraction. This work provides a facile and feasible strategy to prepare efficient and stable carbon-based HTL-free inorganic PSCs.

# Experimental

# Materials

Cesium iodide (Csl) (99.999%, metals basis), cesium bromide (CsBr) (99.999%, metals basis), PMMA (molecular weight: 43982), chlorobenzene (99.9%, spectrophotometric grade) and tin(IV) oxide (  $  SnO_{2}  $  ) solution (15 wt% in   $  H_{2}O  $   colloidal dispersion) were bought from Alfa Aesar. Lead(II) iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  ) (99.99%) and lead(II) bromide (  $  PbBr_{2}  $  ) (99.99%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (≥ 99.9%) and indium-doped tin oxide (ITO) glass substrate (15 ohm/square) were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich and Nippon Sheet Glass (NSG) Company Limited of Japan, respectively. The conductive carbon paste (printing ink) (CH-8 (MOD2)) was purchased from Jujo printing supplies & technology (pinghu) co., Ltd., and its related properties are as follows: surface resistivity: 10.0   $  \Omega/cm^{2}  $   (measured by four-probe method); volume resistivity:   $  1.0\times10^{-2}\Omega\cdot cm  $   (measured by four-probe method); specific gravity: 1.15 (20 °C); adhesion: 100/100; heat resistance: 150 °C/72 hour with the resistance changing within 10%; moisture resistance: 90% relative humidity/240 hour, 40 °C with the resistance changing within 10%. All the materials were directly used without any further purification.

# Device fabrication

The glass/ITO substrate was patterned by etching with diluted hydrochloric acid solution and zinc powder, then 9 successfully cleaned with detergent, deionized water, acetone, isopropanol and ethanol via ultrasonic process for 15 min, respectively. Fig. S1 shows the schematic drawing of the fabrication of PMMA-PSC. After the glass/ITO substrate was dried with nitrogen (  $  N_{2}  $  ) flow and UV-ozone treatment for 5min, the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film was prepared by spin-coating a   $  SnO_{2}  $   solution (3 wt%) on the glass/ITO substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at 150 °C for 30 min in air. After cooling down to the room temperature, the glass/ITO/  $  SnO_{2}  $   substrate was treated with UV-ozone for 15 min, then the mixture   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution of CsBr: CsI:   $  PbBr_{2}  $ :   $  PbI_{2}  $   (molar ratio = 0.5:1:0.5:1) in DMSO at 0.8 M (filtered with 0.22 μm nylon filter) was spin-coated on glass/ITO/  $  SnO_{2}  $   substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 260 °C for 10 min to get the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film in air (relative humidity of 25-35%). For the PMMA-PSC, the PMMA chlorobenzene solution with different concentrations were respectively spin-coated on the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film at a speed of 3000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. Subsequently, the commercial carbon paste was doctor-bladed on the sample surface. Finally, the sample was annealed at 120 °C for 20 min. The size of carbon-based   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs was 0.085 cm².

# Characterization

The J-V characteristics of PSCs were measured using a source meter (Keithley 2420) under an illumination of  $ 100\mathrm{mW/cm^2} $  (Newport 91160, AM 1.5G) calibrated by a standard silicon solar cell (certified by NREL) as the reference. For J-V measurements, the scan voltage was from  $ -0.2\mathrm{V} $  to  $ 1.5\mathrm{V} $ . The scan rate and delay time were  $ 100\mathrm{mV/s} $  and  $ 50~\mathrm{ms} $ , respectively. The crystallinity of perovskite film was investigated by X-ray diffractometer (XRD) (PANalytical X' Pert PRO) using  $ \mathrm{CuK}_{\alpha} $  radiation source. The morphology and contact angle of perovskite film were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, ZEISS ULTRA 55) and video-based optical contact angle measurement instrument (Dataphysics OCA Pro 15, Germany), respectively. The UV-vis absorption spectra were obtained using SHIMADZU UV-2550 spectrophotometer. A standard EQE system (Newport 66902) was used to measure the external quantum efficiency (EQE). Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM) (Asylum Research, Cypher) was employed to investigate the surface morphology and local contact potential difference (CPD) of perovskite films. The current-voltage (I-V) curves were acquired by Keithley 2420 source meter with a scan rate of  $ 100\mathrm{mV/s} $  in the dark. The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra were measured by fluorescence spectrophotometer (HITACHIF-5000) excited at  $ 322\mathrm{nm} $ . The time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) decay spectra were recorded with a time-correlated single photon counting method by fluorescence spectrophotometer (Fluorolog-3, Horiba) excited at 473 nm. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurement was carried out with an electrochemical workstation (Zahner Zennium, Germany) under the  $ 10\mathrm{mW/cm^2} $  white LED light, applying a  $ 10\mathrm{mV} $  AC signal with the frequency ranging from  $ 1\mathrm{Hz} $  to  $ 1\mathrm{MHz} $  under a voltage bias of  $ -1.1\mathrm{V} $ . All samples without any encapsulation were stored and tested in air (relative humidity of  $ 25\%-35\% $ ).

The J-V characteristics (  $  V_{oc}  $  ,   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF) as a function of light intensity (  $  P_{light}  $  ) have been measured to further investigate the charge recombination mechanisms in PSCs. The linear relationship of the   $  V_{oc}  $   and natural logarithm of   $  P_{light}  $   can be plotted according to the following equation (5):  $  ^{63}  $

$$
\mathrm{V} _ {\mathrm{oc}} = \frac {\mathrm{nkT}}{\mathrm{q}} \ln \left(\mathrm{P} _ {\text { light }}\right) + \text { constant }
$$

(5)

where n, k, T and q are the ideal factor, Boltzmann constant, absolute temperature and elementary charge, respectively. There is no charge extraction under open-circuit condition, indicating that all photogenerated charge carriers recombine within PSCs.  $  ^{64}  $   The slope of   $  V_{oc}  $   versus   $  P_{light}  $   will be equal to kT/q when trap-assisted recombination is absent or bimolecular recombination determines the performance of PSCs.  $  ^{23, 64, 65}  $   In contrast, if the trap-assisted recombination is involved in the device operation, the slope would be larger than kT/q.  $  ^{64}  $   As shown in Fig. 9a, the reference-PSC and PMMA-PSC show a slope of 1.75 kT/q and 1.57 kT/q, respectively. Moreover, the   $  V_{oc}  $   of reference-PSC demonstrate a stronger dependence on   $  P_{light}  $  .  $  ^{64}  $   This result indicates that the PMMA modification reduces the trap-assisted recombination in PSCs.  $  ^{12, 20, 64}  $   The dependence of   $  J_{sc}  $   on   $  P_{light}  $   could be described by power law relationship (6):  $  ^{63}  $

$$
\mathrm{J} _ {\mathrm{sc}} \propto \mathrm{P} _ {\text { light }} ^ {\alpha} \quad (\alpha \leq 1)
$$

(6)

where   $  \alpha  $   is an exponential factor. Here, the bimolecular recombination is expected to be the minimum for the maximum carrier sweep out (  $  \alpha \approx 1  $  ) at the short circuit.  $  ^{63}  $   As displayed in Fig. 9b, the values of   $  \alpha  $   for both reference-PSC (  $  \alpha = 0.96  $  ) and PMMA-PSC (  $  \alpha = 0.98  $  ) are close to 1, indicating the negligible bimolecular recombination in devices.  $  ^{66}  $   The dependence of FF on   $  P_{light}  $   is presented in Fig. 9c. At the relatively low   $  P_{light}  $   (just under 0.5 sun), the FF of reference-PSC and PMMA-PSC appears steady state around a certain constant and almost remains unchanged. As the   $  P_{light}  $   continues to increase, the FF of reference-PSC appears more obviously decreased than that of the PMMA-PSC. This can be attributed to the trap-assisted recombination.  $  ^{67}  $   All of these characterizations indicate that the PMMA modification at   $  CsPbI_{2}Br/C  $   interface can reduce the charge recombination and contribute to improve the photovoltaic performance of PSCs.

# Conclusions

In summary, we have successfully demonstrated that the PMMA modification is an effective and facile method to improve the photovoltaic performance of inorganic PSCs. At the optimal PMMA concentration, the PMMA-PSC achieves the highest PCE of  $ 10.95\% $  and a steady-state PCE of  $ 10.89\% $ , as well as less J-V hysteresis behavior and improved device stability. The improved performance can be attributed to the reduced non-radiative recombination induced by the trap states in  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  film and recombination at  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br / C} $  interface, resulting in the promoted charge extraction. This work provides a new and simple route to fabricate efficient and stable carbon-based HTL-free inorganic PSCs.
