# Efficient and UV-Stable Perovskite Solar Cells Enabled by Side Chain-Engineered Polymeric Hole-Transporting Layers

Chang-Hung Tsai, $ ^{a+} $  Nan Li, $ ^{b+} $  Chia-Chen Lee, $ ^{a} $  Hung-Chin Wu, $ ^{a} $  Zonglong Zhu, $ ^{c*} $  Liduo Wang, $ ^{b} $  Wen-Chang Chen, $ ^{a,d} $  He Yan, $ ^{c*} $  Chu-Chen Chueh $ ^{a,d*} $

Biaxially-extended octithiophene-based conjugated polymers are demonstrated as effective polymeric hole-transporting layers (HTLs) to simultaneously enhance the efficiency and UV-photostability of perovskite solar cells (PVSCs). The intense UV absorption contributed from the bearing conjugated side-chain groups of such polymers facilitated its function of UV filtering when serving as the bottom HTL in device. Through rationally tailoring polymer backbone structure, a highly efficient (PCE:  $ 18.34\% $  ) PVSC with much improved UV-photostability and ambient stability was successfully achieved

In the past few years, the astonishing surge of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) has posed a new page for the development of transformative photovoltaic techniques.  $  ^{1-4}  $   In parallel to the exploration of the fundamental properties of perovskite materials and their compositional optimization,  $  ^{5-8}  $   continuous efforts at the device's level have also been dedicated to the simplification of the manufacturing procedures,  $  ^{9,10}  $   the scaling up of the device's size,  $  ^{11-13}  $   as well as the improvement of the power conversion efficiency (PCE)  $  ^{10-15}  $   and the long-term stability  $  ^{11, 14, 16, 17}  $   in order to facilitate the commercialization progress of PVSCs.  $  ^{4, 18}  $

Among the device engineering of PVSCs, the exploration of functional charge-transporting interlayers (CTLs) has played a pivotal role in enhancing both device's PCE and associated stability  $  ^{19-22}  $   in addition to the evolution of device architectures.  $  ^{23, 24}  $   Note that the ambient/thermal/photostability of PVSCs has been identified as the main bottleneck for their practical applications owing to the fragility of

perovskite film in response to the external stresses.  $  ^{18}  $   Myriads of studies have disclosed the severe degradation of perovskite film while exposed to a highly humid environment or continuous illumination, resulting in the decay of device's performance. Therefore, rational employment of functional CTLs together with compositional engineering of perovskite materials has been vigorously investigated to address these issues since it could enhance the robustness of perovskite layer in both intrinsic and extrinsic aspects.  $  ^{14, 17}  $

Among the versatile CTLs exploited for PVSCs thus far, organic materials have attracted intensive research interest since they not only possessed low-temperature, facile solution processability and flexible structural design but also have demonstrated promising potential to largely improve PVSC's ambient stability owing to their hydrophobic nature.  $  ^{19, 25}  $   For example, for a conventional n-i-p PVSC, it has been proven that an appropriately designed organic hole-transporting layer (HTL) could form an effective and dense barrier to prevent the permeation of moisture into the underlying perovskite layer, thus improving the overall moisture resistance.  $  ^{26, 27}  $   Similar enhancement has also been confirmed in the proper design of the organic electron-transporting layer (ETL) for an inverted p-i-n PVSC.  $  ^{28, 29}  $   Moreover, when they served as the bottom CTL in the device, their relatively non-polar feature might promote the crystallinity of the perovskite layer above to mitigate the penetration of moisture through grain boundary or defects.  $  ^{30, 31}  $

However, besides the ambient stability, the exploration pertaining to utilizing organic CTLs to improve the photostability of PVSCs was rarely reported.  $  ^{14}  $   The inferior photostability of PVSCs under continuous illumination has been suspected to partially originate from the ultraviolet (UV) radiation in the solar spectrum.  $  ^{32, 33}  $   Such high-energy photons seem to beget the formation of associated defects inside the perovskite crystals to accelerate its degradation.  $  ^{32, 33}  $   To resolve this issue, the most straightforward method is to reduce the UV-radiation intensity coupling into the perovskite layer. Based on the above principle, a few of functional layers or CTLs with decent UV absorption were recently exploited and employed in PVSCs to enhance the UV-photostability.  $  ^{34-38}  $   For instance, an n-type   $  BaSnO_{3}  $   ETL with respectable UV absorption was developed to replace the regular   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL in a conventional n-i-p PVSC, which manifested a clear inhibition of UV-induced decomposition of perovskite.  $  ^{34}  $   Noteworthily, the PL emission of   $  BaSnO_{3}  $   was located within the absorption region of the perovskite layer, which thus realized the down-conversion of UV light to promote the light-harvesting efficiency of device. More recently, inorganic nanophosphors   $  [(SrAl_{2}O_{4}:Eu^{2+}, Dy^{3+})  $  , SAED] and delafossite-type   $  CuCrO_{2}  $   ETL with broad and intense UV absorption were respectively exploited and employed to improve the UV-photostability of the derived PVSCs.  $  ^{37, 38}  $   Interestingly, the characteristic long-persistent luminescence of SAED endowed the derived PVSC with certain capability to store the solar energy after illumination.  $  ^{37}  $   Despite those achievements, all the current exploitation of CTLs with UV-filtering capability are mainly centered around the inorganic materials and the exploration of organic counterparts are still lacking, especially compatible to the inverted p-i-n PVSCs.  $  ^{14}  $

In this study, we successfully exploited low-temperature solution processable organic HTLs to improve the UV-photostability of inverted PVSCs by using biaxially-extended octithiophene-based conjugated polymers. It was revealed that the conjugated side-chain groups of such polymers contribute to the intense UV absorption  $  ^{39,40}  $  and thus enables the UV-filtering function when serving as the bottom HTL in device. Through rationally tailoring the backbone structure of the polymers, polymeric HTLs with both high mobility and decent UV-radiation blocking capability were further developed and employed to realize an efficient (PCE: 18.34%) PVSC with much improved UV-photostability and ambient stability. The device architecture of the studied inverted PVSC and the chemical structures of the biaxially-extended octithiophene-based polymers, including P8TTT, PII2T8T, and PII2T8TSi, were displayed in Figure 1a. The detailed synthesis of these three polymers was reported in our previous works. Their thermal properties such as thermogravimetric analysis (TAG) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) curves were presented in Figure S1 and the relevant optoelectronic properties were summarized in Table S1.  $  ^{39,40}  $   As shown, all polymers exhibited high decomposition temperature (  $  T_{d}  $   @ 5% weight loss) around or above 400 °C, revealing good thermal stability. Besides, only P8TTT showed a clear glass transition temperature (  $  T_{g}  $  ) around 70 °C. Note that as portrayed in Figure 1b, due to the p-type characteristic of the backbone, P8TTT possessed a suitable HOMO level to serve as a HTL for the   $  (\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3})_{0.85}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})_{0.15}  $  -based PVSC.  $  ^{41}  $   Furthermore, thanks to the contribution from the constituent bulky side-chain group, P8TTT exhibited broad and intense absorption in the region of UV radiation (< 400 nm). This interesting feature highlights the feasible design of organic CTL with UV-radiation blocking capability through side-chain engineering. However, P8TTT only possessed an mediocre hole mobility of   $  5.0 \times 10^{-5}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $   as extracted from a top-contact/bottom-gate transistor (Table S1), possible due to the weak intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) inside the backbone and the unsatisfactory molecular packing arising from the bulky side-chain groups.  $  ^{39}  $   Such inferior charge-transporting properties of P8TTT might require proper doping when it serves as a HTL in the device.

In this regard, we have further tailored the backbone structure of the biaxially-extended octithiophene-based polymer with a donor-acceptor (D-A) design strategy in order to realize dopant-free HTLs, wherein an electron-withdrawing moiety, isoindigo with octyldodecyl (II) or siloxane-terminated alkyl chains (IISi), was used to prepare PII2T8T and PII2T8TSi.  $  ^{40}  $   As seen in Figure 1c, the ICT feature of these D-A type polymers was largely increased as exemplified by new absorption characteristic around   $  \sim  $  625 nm compared to the P8TTT. This feature promotes the hole mobility of PII2T8T and PII2T8TSi to   $  1.3 \times 10^{-3} cm^{2} V^{-1} s^{-1}  $   and   $  3.0 \times 10^{-2} cm^{2} V^{-1} s^{-1}  $   (Table S1) while preserving suitable HOMO levels to serve as the HTLs for the   $  (\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3})_{0.85}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})_{0.15}  $  -based PVSC (Figure 1b).  $  ^{40}  $   Notably, the broad and intense absorption in the region of UV radiation (< 400 nm) contributed from the side-chain group of the constituent donor moiety was not affected by the main-chain structural tailoring. Both high mobility and decent UV-radiation blocking capability of PII2T8T and PII2T8TSi thus afford promising potential for enhancing the UV-photostability of the derived PVSCs (Figure S2).

Provided these appealing results, we fabricated the inverted PVSCs (ITO/HTL/perovskite/PC $ _{61} $ BM/BCP/Ag) using these studied HTLs. For a fair comparison, the regular PEDOT:PSS-based PVSC was fabricated as the reference device. Figure 3a presents the best current density-voltage ( $ J-V $ ) curves of the fabricated devices measured under AM 1.5 G solar irradiance (100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $ ) in both reverse and forward scan direction and the related photovoltaic parameters, including open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{oc} $ ), short-circuit current density ( $ J_{sc} $ ), and fill factor ( $ FF $ ), were summarized in Table 1.

As can be seen, the PII2T8T-based PVSC delivered a champion PCE of 18.34% (measured under reverse scan direction) with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.09 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 21.53 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , a FF of 0.78, and negligible J-V hysteresis, outperforming the performance (PCE 16.92%;   $  V_{oc}  $  : 1.02 V;   $  J_{sc}  $  : 21.38 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ; FF: 0.78) of the reference PEDOT:PSS-based PVSC. The improved   $  V_{oc}  $   of the PII2T8T-based device mainly contributed to the enhanced PCE, which can be inferred from its deeper-lying HOMO level (  $  \sim -5.4  $   eV) than the value (  $  \sim -5.1  $   eV) of PEDOT:PSS. The obtained high FF might benefit from the promoted crystallinity of perovskite layer induced by PII2T8T and the efficient charge-transfer at the PII2T8T/perovskite interface as discussed earlier. It is important to note that the absorption

characteristic around   $  \sim  $  625 nm arising from the ICT feature of PII2T8T does not affect the resultant photocurrent when a thin thickness (  $  \sim  $  30 nm) is used although we indeed observed the energy loss induced by parasitical absorption while using a thicker polymeric HTL. At such optimized thickness, the intense light-harvesting characteristic of perovskite layer and the improved charge-transfer at the PII2T8T/perovskite interface seems to compensate the expected energy loss.  $  ^{42,43}  $

Unlike PII2T8T, the PII2T8TSi-based PVSC delivered a lower PCE of 15.15 % with a  $  V_{oc}  $  of 1.05 V, a  $  J_{sc}  $  of 18.79 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and a  $  FF  $  of 0.77. It again suggests that the bulky insulating siloxane groups of PII2T8TSi impede the charge transport/transfer at the PII2T8TSi/perovskite interface. Although PII2T8TSi afforded better solubility and higher hole mobility than PII2T8T (Table S1), this result clearly highlights the significance of the side-chain engineering when designing polymeric HTLs for PVSCs. Among the studied HTLs, the P8TTT-based PVSC yielded the poorest PCE with conspicuous  $  J-V  $  hysteresis, which is kind of expectable as it possessed the lowest mobility among the studied HTLs.

Figure 3b compared the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of the studied PVSCs. As seen, both PII2T8T- and PEDOT:PSS-based devices showed high photo-response across the wavelengths from 400 nm to 800 nm, validating the high photocurrent. Note that, despite the fact that the PII2T8T-based device recorded lower EQE values across the wavelengths from 300 nm to 400 nm, it is difficult to conclude its UV-radiation blocking at this stage and it necessitates more detailed investigations that will be discussed later. It is important to note that the high-energy photon generally possesses a shorter penetration depth in the film compared to the photons with lower energy. This enables the thin polymeric HTL at the front side in the device to absorb relatively large portion of UV radiation. However, the photons absorbed by the polymeric HTLs seem not to be efficiently down-converted to the energy that can be reabsorbed by the perovskite film, which reveals there is still room for further improvement in the structure design. Besides the UV-photostability, the ambient stability of both devices was also examined as shown in Figure 4b, wherein the devices were kept at room temperature with a relative humidity of   $  50 \pm 10\%  $   without under illumination. The PII2T8T-based device remained over 90% of its initial PCE after 15 days while the PCE of the reference device showed fast decay to almost zero after 10 days. The acidic and ionic nature of PEDOT:PSS HTL has been reported to engender the degradation of perovskite film.  $  ^{42,48}  $   Thus, the enhancement in ambient stability might benefit from the promoted crystallinity of perovskite film induced by the PII2T8T HTL and the more inactive interactions at the HTL/perovskite interface. Figure S7 presented the characterization of device's ambient stability by tracking its maximum power point under working condition after different storage time. As shown, the result is consistent with the result shown in Figure 4b, which not only suggests the well reliability of the fabricated devices but also confirms the improved stability of the PII2T8T-base devices.

# Conclusions

We herein have successfully manifested low-temperature solution processable organic HTLs with UV-radiation blocking capability to simultaneously enhance the efficiency and stability of inverted PVSCs for the first time, by using biaxially-extended octithiophene-based conjugated polymers. We revealed that the bearing conjugated side-chain groups of such polymers contributed to the intense UV absorption, which thus facilitated the UV-filtering function when serving as the bottom HTL in device. Through rationally tailoring the polymer backbone structure, the PII2T8T HTL owing both high mobility and decent UV-radiation blocking capability was developed to yield a highly efficient (PCE: 18.34%) PVSC. Moreover, this device showed much improved UV-photostability, for which the PCE remained unchanged under continuous irradiation by a 365-nm UV lamp (5 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) at 25 °C (relative humidity: 50 ± 10 %) for 200 mins, in contrast to the quick degradation of the reference PEDOT:PSS-based device. The unencapsulated PII2T8T-based device also remained over 90% of its initial PCE after 15-day storage at room temperature with a relative humidity of 50 ± 10 % while the PCE of the reference device showed fast decay to almost zero after 10 days. This work not only demonstrates the effectiveness of using organic CTLs to enhance the UV-photostability but also highlights the significance of side-chain engineering while designing functional organic CTL for efficient and stable PVSCs.

# Efficient and UV-Stable Perovskite Solar Cells Enabled by Side Chain-Engineered Polymeric Hole-Transporting Layers

Chang-Hung Tsai, Nan Li, Chia-Chen Lee, Hung-Chin Wu, Zonglong Zhu,  $  ^{*}  $  Liduo Wang, Wen-Chang Chen, He Yan,  $  ^{*}  $  Chu-Chen Chueh  $  ^{*}  $

Keywords: UV-radiation blocking, side-chain engineering, hole-transporting layer, perovskite solar cells, photostability.

PII2T8T

PII2T8TSI

Biaxially-extended octithiophene-based conjugated polymers are demonstrated as effective polymeric hole-transporting layers (HTLs) to simultaneously enhance the efficiency and UV-photostability of perovskite solar cells. The intense UV absorption contributed from the bearing conjugated side-chain groups of such polymers facilitated its function of UV filtering when serving as the bottom HTL in device. Through rationally tailoring polymer backbone structure, a highly efficient (PCE:  $ 18.34\% $  ) PVSC with much improved UV-photostability and ambient stability was successfully achieved.
