# Bandgap Alignment of   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   Quantum Dots as Efficient Inorganic Hole Transport Materials in Planar Perovskite Solar Cells with Enhanced Stability

# Abstract

With the rapid development of the perovskite solar cells (PSCs), it is critical to exploit efficient and low-cost inorganic hole transport materials (HTMs) to solve the instability of the traditional organic HTM of spiro-OMeTAD. Herein,   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   quantum dots (QDs) are applied as inorganic HTMs in n-i-p planar PSCs, the bandgap alignment of the QDs is designed by the modulation of the QDs size to accelerate the hole transport from perovskite layer to QDs, and the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 14.13% is achieved for 5.7 nm sized QDs. IPCE and UV-vis spectra indicate that the light absorption intensity of   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs based PSCs is slightly higher than that of spiro-OMeTAD based one, which enables the enhancement of   $  J_{SC}  $   (21.85 mA/cm²) for PSCs. The excellent hole transport behavior of   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs is proven that   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs may be considered as an efficient and potential candidate of HTMs in PSCs. Moreover, the stability of the   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs based PSCs HTM is significantly improved comparing with that of the spiro-OMeTAD based one due to the high stability of   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs and the efficiency retains 91% of the initial PCE after 30 days in ambient air.

Keywords:  $ \mathrm{Cu_{12}Sb_4S_{13}} $  QDs, hole transport materials, perovskite solar cells, bandgap alignment, photovoltaic efficiency, stability

# Introduction

Energy and environment are facing huge crisis, a great number of researches on photocatalysts and solar cells have been made to solve the problems.  $  ^{1-4}  $   Since 2009, the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted tremendous attention of researchers, which leads to the rapid increasing of the photoelectric conversion efficiency from 3.8% to 23.2%.  $  ^{5-10}  $   Hole transport materials (HTMs) play a significant role for achieving high efficiency in PSCs. So far, the widely-used organic HTMs are small-molecule and polymer ones, such as spiro-OMeTAD (2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-4-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene), PTAA (poly-[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine]), etc. In particular, the most popular and traditional HTM is spiro-OMeTAD due to its high solubility in organic solvent, which makes it an ideal material for spin-coating with remarkable efficiency in PSCs.  $  ^{11, 12}  $   However, the above organic HTM is extremely expensive and owns unsatisfied long-term stability as well as discontented carrier mobility. To improve its hole conductivity, some efforts have been employed in spiro-OMeTAD by doping of LiTFSI and tBP.  $  ^{13}  $   However, the dopant in organic HTMs is hydrophilic and may deteriorate the perovskite layer, which further induces the poor long-term stability.  $  ^{14, 15}  $

To solve the above problems of organic HTMs, a number of inorganic HTMs have emerged to improve the chemical stability and charge mobility. As reported previously, CuI, $ ^{16} $  CuSCN $ ^{17, 18} $  and NiO $ ^{19} $  have been commonly used in PSCs with excellent long-term stability. Nevertheless, the photovoltaic efficiency of the PSCs

based on inorganic HTMs is still lower than that of organic HTMs. Therefore, it is needed to explore the novel inorganic HTMs to further promote the photovoltaic efficiency.

As the typical inorganic HTMs, quantum dots (QDs) are the potential materials to meet the requirements of low cost, splendid charge mobility and chemical stability simultaneously. Based on the above characteristic properties, QDs materials have been widely used in the field of solar cells and photodetectors. $ ^{20-22} $  It is well known that QDs possess a lot of unique and distinctive advantages as HTMs in PSCs, such as tunable bandgap, hot electron transfer, high absorption coefficient and multiple exciton generation, etc. $ ^{23-26} $  Li et al. have reported PbS QDs as HTMs, achieving a PCE of  $ \sim 8\% $ . $ ^{27} $  Lv et al. have applied colloidal CuInS $ _2 $  QDs as inorganic HTMs in PSCs, leading to a PCE of  $ 8.38\% $ . $ ^{28} $  Later on, Khanzada et al. have introduced an effective engineering of surface ligands for  $ \mathrm{Cu_2ZnSnS_4} $  (CZTS) QDs, and it is found that CZTS QDs with ligands removal exhibit excellent hole transporting properties as HTMs in PSCs. $ ^{29} $  Till now, the emergence of new QDs as HTMs in PSCs becomes a current research hotspot. As the typical p-type direct bandgap semiconductors, there are two non-occupied states in the valence band of  $ \mathrm{Cu_{12}Sb_4S_{13}} $  QDs, which induces their quite excellent hole mobility, and their appropriate position of valence band has a good matching with that of the perovskite layer. On account of these advantages,  $ \mathrm{Cu_{12}Sb_4S_{13}} $  QDs will be recognized as extraordinary HTMs in PSCs. $ ^{30-32} $  Recently, Muthusamy et al. have used copper antimony sulfide (CAS,  $ \mathrm{Cu_{12}Sb_4S_{13}} $ ) tetrahedrite nanoparticles as the inorganic HTMs in PSCs, and obtained a PCE of  $ 6.5\% $ . $ ^{33} $

However, the low photovoltaic efficiency with the low   $  V_{OC}  $   value of 0.8 V may be originated from the poor charge separation efficiency due to the band energy level of the used CAS HTMs in the PSCs. Therefore, the bandgap alignment of   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs should be considered to favor for the charge transfer to further improve the photovoltaic efficiency of the PSCs.

In the present work, we introduce the  $ \mathrm{Cu_{12}Sb_4S_{13}} $  QDs as the efficient HTMs in n-i-p planar PSCs. To modulate the charge transfer as well as the photovoltaic efficiency of QDs in PSCs, the bandgap alignment is proposed by adjusting the QDs size. Finally, the photovoltaic efficiency and stability of  $ \mathrm{Cu_{12}Sb_4S_{13}} $  QDs based PSCs are effectively improved. This work may provide potential and efficient HTMs in PSCs.

# Results and discussion

In this work, various sized   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs have been synthesized by controlling the reaction temperatures (110 °C, 130 °C and 150 °C) using a simple hot-injection method. The corresponding crystal structures are confirmed by the XRD patterns shown in Figure 1a, which suggest the cubic structure of   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   phase (JCPDS No. 42-0561). High-resolution transmission electron microscope (HRTEM) images observe the microstructures of QDs (Figure 1b-1d). It demonstrates that the as-prepared   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs are single crystals and monodisperse, and the sizes of QDs increase with the increasing of the reaction temperature. The average diameters of QDs at the reaction temperatures of 110 °C, 130 °C and 150 °C are measured to be 4.5 nm, 5.7 nm and 7.9 nm, respectively.

# Conclusions

In summary, the size-dependent   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs are prepared using a simple hot-injection method, which are successfully applied as the efficient HTMs in PSCs. The result reveals that the 5.7 nm sized   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs achieve the best photovoltaic efficiency of 14.13%, which is comparable with that of the PSCs using spiro-OMeTAD HTMs. What is deserved to be mentioned is that an admirable   $  J_{SC}  $   value of 21.85 mA/cm² is obtained for the PSCs using 5.7 nm sized   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs as HTMs, which is originated from the extra light absorption contributions of   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs as proven by UV-vis spectra. UPS measurements prove that the energy level of   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs may be varied by the size, and 5.7 nm sized QDs have the most proper bandgap alignment for the PSCs in our case. In addition, EIS, steady-state PL and TAS measurements provide the valid evidences that   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs based PSCs own the more efficient hole injection behavior from perovskite layer to HTMs, which is favorable for the reduced recombination possibility of the photo-excited electrons and holes, and the result also implies that   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs possess the superb hole transport property than the traditional spiro-OMeTAD Furthermore, the stability of the   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs based PSCs is markedly improved comparing with that of the spiro-OMeTAD based one due to the higher stability of inorganic QDs,  $  ^{34}  $   which proves that   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs may be efficient HTMs in PSCs.

# Synthesis of   $  Cu_{12}Sb_{4}S_{13}  $   QDs

Hexane (5 mL) was used to re-disperse the QDs, and the dissolved solution was centrifuged again at 8000 rpm for 3 min to obtain the liquid supernatant for the follow-up work. # Device Fabrication

The etched FTO glasses were cleaned by ultrasound for 15 min with detergent, deionized water and ethanol in sequence. Next, the cleaned FTO glasses were used as substrate to deposit an ultrathin   $  TiO_{2}  $   film, which was prepared by hydrolysis of 50 mM   $  TiCl_{4}  $   solution at 70 °C for 1 h. After washed by deionized water, the substrates were annealed on a hotplate at 450 °C for 10 min. The above fabrication process of   $  TiO_{2}  $   film was cycled again to deposit a compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   film. To prepare the perovskite layer, 231.4 mg   $  PbI_{2}  $   and 80 mg   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I_{3}  $   were dissolved in 0.5 mL DMF to form a 1 M perovskite precursor solution, and shaken in a spiral mixer for 10 min to form a homogeneous and clear yellow solution. An anti-solvent method was used to prepare a smooth, pinhole-free and high quality perovskite film. The perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated on   $  TiO_{2}  $   film at 5000 rpm for 30 s with chlorobenzene as an anti-solvent after plasma pre-treatment for 120 s. Then, the prepared perovskite layer was annealed at 100 °C for 10 min, and the film color would turn from yellow to dark. Specially, to obtain a larger perovskite crystal, the traces of   $  \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_{2}}  $   were added in perovskite precursor solution.  $  ^{36}  $   Whereafter, QDs solution dispersed in hexane was centrifuged at 8000 rpm for 3 min again, and the precipitate was redispersed in hexanethiol to form the precursor solution of QDs. To optimize the thickness of QDs layer, the concentration of QDs solution and the deposited QDs layer were controlled in the range of 5-15 mg/mL and 1-3 layers, respectively. The formed QDs layer was annealed at 110 °C for 3 min. For comparison, the spiro-OMeTAD precursor solution consisted of 41.6 mg spiro-OMeTAD, 7.81 μL LiTFSI, 16.88 μL tBP and 0.5 mL chlorobenzene was prepared after shaken for 5 min. Subsequently, the spiro-OMeTAD solution was spin-coated on perovskite layer at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, the counter electrode Au was thermally evaporated on HTLs with a thickness of 80 nm. The preparations of perovskite, QDs and spiro-OMeTAD were conducted in a glovebox filled with high purity argon to prevent the deterioration of perovskite.

# Measurement and Characterization

The crystal structures and microstructures of the as-prepared QDs were characterized by X-Ray Diffraction, (XRD, Pert-Pro, PANalytical, Netherlands) and High-resolution Transmission Electron Microscope (HRTEM, JEM-2100F, JEOL, Japan). UV-vis absorption (UV-2550, Shimadzu, Japan) in the wavelength range from 300 nm to 800 nm was recorded to characterize the optical absorption properties of the samples. Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscope (FESEM, JSEM-5610LV, Hitachi, Japan) was performed to observe the morphology of the materials and PSCs. Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS) (Autolab Potentiostat 30, PGSTAT100, Netherlands) and steady-state PL spectra (LabRam HR, HORIBA Jobin Yvon, France) were taken to evaluate the hole mobility of QDs in PSCs, and the films
