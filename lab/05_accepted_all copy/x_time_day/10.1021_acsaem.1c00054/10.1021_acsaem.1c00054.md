# One-Step Spray-Coated All-Inorganic CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br Perovskite Solar Cells

fu-Tien Yu, Shun-Hsiang Yang, Li-Hui Chou, Itaru Osaka, Xiao-Feng Wang, and Cheng-Liang

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Here, we report a scalable ultrasonic spray deposition under ambient conditions to fabricate a   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based all-inorganic perovskite thin film for photovoltaic applications. With the cooperated aid of vacuum extraction of the as-fabricated perovskite film and the incorporation of lead(II) acetate (  $  Pb(Ac)_{2}  $  ) additive to the precursor solution, the quality of the perovskite film dramatically improved from the filled pinholes between the crystal grain with enhanced perovskite crystallinity. We found that the champion device for the 5%   $  Pb(Ac)_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film combined with vacuum extraction achieved the best power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 10.06%, arising from the efficient defect dissipation within the film and the reduction in the charge recombination loss. Simultaneously, the thermal stability was boosted with the incorporation of   $  Pb(Ac)_{2}  $   after aging at 85 °C for 10 days. We believe that the spray-coating process combined with the optimization of the all-inorganic perovskite film will help in the fabrication of a photoabsorbing layer for the future industrial upscaling of perovskite solar cells.

KEYWORDS: all-inorganic perovskite, spray coating, solar cells, additive, vacuum extraction

# INTRODUCTION

The emergence of halide perovskite solar cells for a decade has been a breakthrough in solar energy conversion.  $  ^{1-4}  $   Despite the considerable progress in efficiency achieved in recent years, the instability of these cells under thermal stress is a crucial bottleneck for practical deployment, which can be mainly attributed to the decomposition of the high-volatility organic component. The all-inorganic lead halide counterparts with cesium as the A-site inorganic cation   $  (\mathrm{CsPbX}_{3})  $   are supposed to be alternative light-harvesting materials in solar cells with remarkable thermal stability even though their performance still falls behind those of the equivalent hybrid compounds.  $  ^{5-14}  $   Simultaneously compromising the tradeoff between the phase stability and a suitable band gap, the all-inorganic mixed-halide   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite, with a band gap of   $  \sim1.92~eV  $   for light-harvesting and stable structural stability against conversion into the yellow   $  \delta  $  -phase,  $  ^{15}  $   has been considered an optimized candidate for an efficient and stable photoabsorber.  $  ^{16-18}  $   Developing a reliable and scalable process for the all-inorganic perovskite film production while achieving good quality and phase stability of perovskite crystallites is expected to advance the progress of perovskite-based photovoltaics.

A high-quality perovskite layer with controlled morphologies such as large grain size and a high film coverage is a prerequisite to enhance the efficiency output and stability of the all-inorganic perovskite solar cells. The solar cells with a spray-coated hybrid mixed halide perovskite film from the precursor solution containing methylammonium iodide (MAI) and   $  PbCl_{2}  $   showed an efficiency of 11.1%.  $  ^{34}  $   Later on, Ho-Baillie and co-workers demonstrated the feasibility of the spray-assisted deposition process to fabricate the all-inorganic   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   films for perovskite solar cells with a stabilized efficiency of 6.3% to overcome the poor solubility of the bromide ion precursor solution.  $  ^{49}  $   CsI was sprayed onto a spin-coated   $  PbBr_{2}  $   film in air, and the effects of the substrate temperature during the spraying and the post-annealing temperature were found to manipulate the perovskite quality. A multistep spraying method was further developed to fabricate all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite solar cell devices by Tang's group.  $  ^{50}  $   High-quality   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films can be deposited by spin-coating of a   $  PbBr_{2}  $   layer and the subsequent spraying of a   $  CsBr_{3}  $   multilayer in ambient air. Upon the optimization of four spraying cycles, the efficiencies are determined to be 6.80 and 4.12% for the 0.04 and 1 cm  $  ^{2}  $   devices, respectively. Considering all-inorganic perovskite solar cells with high efficiencies based on the spin-coating process, it is indeed of importance for mass production given that the precursor solution developed for the lab-scale spin-coating process can be transferred to the scalable deposition technology. Following the abovementioned discussion, the understanding of controlled crystallization of inorganic perovskite films and their correlation with the scalable one-step spray deposition technique is crucial for achieving high-quality films with efficient and stable photovoltaic properties.

In this study, inspired by the previous work and compatible with upscaling fabrication, we demonstrated the one-step ultrasonic spray deposition of the all-inorganic perovskite film and explored the synergetic effect of vacuum extraction and the

$ \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_2} $  additive on the device performance of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  perovskite solar cells with a planar cell configuration. Vacuum pumping on the perovskite film can help to precisely control the nucleation and crystallization during the perovskite film formation. The film morphologies can be controlled by the doping quantity of  $ \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_2} $  and effectively tune the energy of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  perovskite, which manipulates the energy level offset relative to that of the charge transport layer, thus boosting the device performance. Combined with these two morphological control strategies and the ultrasonic spray-coating process on the perovskite film, an optimized PCE of  $ 10.06\% $  was achieved for  $ 5\% $   $ \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_2} $ -doped  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $ , which is the first example reported for the spray deposition of all-inorganic perovskite solar cells with a PCE of above  $ 10\% $  to date. The device also exhibited good thermal stability.

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The exceptional conformability of spray-coating methodologies for a high-speed coating has been widely used in the solution-based fabrication of thin films for a wide range of applications and is suitable for the scaling up of perovskite thin films and the compatible roll-to-roll casting technique. The ultrasonic spraying platform was developed for the deposition of the all-inorganic perovskite thin film, as shown schematically in Figure 1a, including an ultrasonic spray nozzle with X–Y movements, which are precisely driven by a computer-controlled servo motor. The vertical working distance between the nozzle and the substrate is fixed. For the work reported here, the all-inorganic perovskite precursor solution was fed to the nozzle at a constant rate and then ultrasonically assisted atomized into fine spraying droplets. These liquid droplets reached the target surface, spread, dried, and finally adhered onto the substrate. The spray deposition parameters were responsible for the overall quality of the deposited perovskite films and were optimized during this study (see the Experimental Methods for details). With the simultaneous compromise of the light-harvesting ability and phase stability, the inorganic component,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  , was used as a photoabsorber in the spray-coated perovskite layer, as the theoretical calculation revealed that the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   alloy film with a suitable band gap (  $  \sim1.9~eV  $  ) provided the structurally proximal and stable cubic phase

The morphologies and grain size of the perovskite film have been found to be the key factors ensuring sufficient light-harvesting and effective carrier extraction, which can be controlled by the optimization of crystallization and growth affected during the film formation process.  $  ^{54,55}  $   The one-step solution-processed   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film is widely used, and the homogeneity, pinhole-lessness, and the large grain size of the perovskite film can be realized by the adjustment of the solvent evaporation conditions in the vacuum extraction procedure. To explore the working mechanisms of vacuum-assisted thermal annealing in the all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite solar cells, the properties of both the perovskite films and the related devices were explored. To gain insight into the impact of vacuum extraction on the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite crystal growth, the fabricated film morphology with thermal annealing under ambient conditions (280 °C for 10 min) or in a vacuum chamber (40 °C for 3 min and 280 °C for 3 min) was recorded by SEM (Figure 1b–e). The thickness of the stacked perovskite films was controlled to   $  \sim  $  400 nm, according to the cross-sectional SEM images. The ambient-annealed   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film without the assistance of vacuum extraction featured numerous loosely packed crystals surrounding irregular pinholes and unfavorable surface coverage due to the as-fabricated wet perovskite films prepared from the low volatile, high-boiling-point N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP). These undesired morphological features may be driven from the thermal kinetics with a simultaneous competition between the perovskite crystal nucleation and growth under high-temperature annealing. In contrast, when the films were annealed in vacuum, the evaporation of NMP enhanced with decreasing pressure, thereby contributing to the formation of significantly fewer pinholes and dense   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films. Similarly, the coffee-ring phenomenon and the flat/dense surface appear in the photographs taken in the perovskite film without and with the vacuum extraction process, respectively, as shown in Figure S1. The unavoidable problems of the coffee-ring effect during the perovskite spraying have been discussed in the recent literature by Dai and co-workers.  $  ^{56}  $   A solution with a high-boiling-point solvent will lead to the shrinkage of the wet films and the formation of the film-thickness variation because of the coffee-ring effect. Fewer pinholes were formed when vacuum extraction was applied to assist the growth of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film. The crystalline structure of the perovskites was determined by X-ray diffraction (XRD; Figure S2). The use of vacuum extraction (at 40 °C, 2 min) resulted in the crystallization of the intermediate   $  PbI_{2}-NMP  $   adduct with diffraction peaks at 8.3°, similar to that previously reported.  $  ^{57}  $   The adduct was expected to slow down the crystal growth rate and improve the quality of the perovskite film.  $  ^{58}  $   The intermediate phases were transformed into the target α-phase   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite phases after the post-vacuum-assisted thermal annealing process (280 °C), located at 14.6, 21.0, and 29.7° for the (100), (110), and (200) crystal planes, respectively. Both the

perovskite samples with and without vacuum extraction represented the cubic crystal structures of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  ,  $  ^{59}  $   and the unchanged peak positions indicated the negligible influence of the perovskite crystal lattice.

The structural evolution of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   crystals under vacuum extraction is schematically illustrated in Figure S3. The all-inorganic perovskite precursor film was first deposited onto the indium tin oxide (ITO)/  $  SnO_{2}  $   substrate by spray coating, and excess solvent and precursor components were present in the wet film. Then, the as-fabricated perovskite film was placed under vacuum, and the excess NMP solvent escaped from the film when the lead compound precursor and the NMP solvent coordinated to form the intermediate state.  $  ^{60-62}  $   Finally, further vacuum-assisted thermal annealing causes the residual NMP to volatilize from the intermediate complex, and the crystals grow simultaneously along the nuclei to transform into the target crystalline   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film.

# CONCLUSIONS

In summary, this work demonstrated the ultrasonic spray deposition of a   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   all-inorganic perovskite film for a photovoltaic performance assessment. The critical role of the synergetic effects of vacuum extraction and   $  \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_{2}}  $   additive on the increasing PCE of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite solar cells was discussed. We found that a combination of the 5%   $  \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_{2}}  $   additive and vacuum extraction during the processing could distinctly provide an improved quality of the perovskite film with full coverage and long carrier lifetime, thereby reducing the defect density and the nonradiative recombination loss as compared to the reference device cell. The best-performing   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   n-i-p perovskite device achieved a high PCE of 10.06%. Good thermal stability was achieved for the 5%   $  \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_{2}}  $ -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , with 76% of the initial PCE after 10 days of annealing at 85 °C. This work thus realized the proof-of-concept on the deposition of the all-inorganic perovskite film by one-step upscaling the spray-coating method as well as a feasible route to optimize the crystalline morphologies and phase stability of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films toward the photovoltaic industry.

# EXPERIMENTAL METHODS

Preparation of the Perovskite Film. A 14% CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite precursor solution was prepared by mixing PbI $ _2 $  (Alfa Aesar, 99.9985%), PbBr $ _2 $  (Alfa Aesar, 99.999%), and CsI (Alfa Aesar, 99.9%), in a molar ratio of 1:1:2; then anhydrous N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP; Sigma-Aldrich, 99.5%) was added to the solution. For extrinsic lead(II) acetate, Pb(Ac) $ _2 $ -doped perovskite and lead(II) acetate trihydrate (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.999%), with a 2.5, 5, 7.5, and 10% weight ratio to PbI $ _2 $ , were added to the pristine perovskite solution. To form the perovskite layer on the SnO $ _2 $ /ITO substrates, CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite without and with Pb(Ac) $ _2 $  was subsequently deposited by customized ultrasonic atomization-assisted spray coating, as described in our previous work. The system was equipped with an atomizing spray nozzle with a fixed nozzle-to-substrate distance of 4 cm. The spraying solution was held inside the syringe pump connected to the nozzle with a feeding rate of 18 mL h $ ^{-1} $ . During the spraying process for each device, the substrates were fixed on the heating plate at a controlled temperature of 40 °C, and low-pressure nitrogen gas at 0.01 MPa was introduced into the spraying nozzle to assist the shaping of the droplet beams. The spraying nozzle moved in line to scan over the substrates at 0.5 cm s $ ^{-1} $  in a single spraying pass. Then, the films were immediately placed under vacuum in a chamber for 2 min and heated to 280 °C for 10 min under vacuum. After being cooled to room temperature, the samples were transferred to a N $ _2 $ -filled glove box for the next step.

Device Fabrication. The ITO substrates were cleaned with detergent, deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol in a sonication bath for 10 min at each step. Then, the cleaned substrates were treated with plasma for 6 min before use. Both the bottom electron transport layer and the perovskite layer were fabricated under ambient conditions (relative humidity (RH) controlled at   $  \sim40\%  $   and room temperature at 25   $  {}^{\circ}  $  C). Then, a layer of   $  SnO_{2}  $   was spin-coated from an aqueous tin(IV) oxide colloidal dispersion (Alfa Aesar, 15%), diluted with ultrapure water (1:4, v:v), and annealed at 150   $  {}^{\circ}  $  C for 30 min as the electron transport layer. After the deposition of the perovskite photoabsorber, following the procedure described above, 15 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   of poly(triarylamine) (PTAA; Ossila,   $  M_{n} = 12\,200  $  , polydispersity index (PDI) = 2.7) in anhydrous toluene (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.8%) was spin-coated onto the perovskite film, and the films were dried at 40   $  {}^{\circ}  $  C for 5 min inside the glove box. For the final step, an 80-nm-thick Au layer was evaporated at low pressure (  $  \sim5 \times 10^{-7}  $   Torr).

Characterization. A Bruker D8 Advance (D8A) diffractometer system with a Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda = 1.54 \AA  $  ) was used to obtain the XRD patterns of the film in the study. The film morphologies were imaged using a field-emission scanning electron microscope (FE-

SEM; Hitachi SU8200) and an atomic force microscope (AFM; Seiko SPA400). The UV–vis absorption spectra were collected on a Hitachi U-4100 UV–vis–NIR spectrophotometer. The ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) results were obtained using a Thermo VG-Scientific system with monochromatized He I (21.22 eV) in an ultrahigh vacuum system (base pressure:   $  5 \times 10^{-8}  $   Torr). The Fourier transform infrared spectra (FTIR) were recorded using the PerkinElmer One FTIR spectrometer. Steady-state and transient photoluminescence (PL) measurements were performed on a UniRAM spectrometer system integrated by Protrustech Co., Ltd. The current density–voltage (J-V) measurements were performed with illumination from a Xe lamp (AAA class) with 1-sun intensity (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , AM 1.5G) calibrated with a reference diode. A black metal mask with an aperture area of 3 mm × 3 mm was used to accurately define the active area of the devices. The statistical device performance data of the perovskite solar cells can be collected by 15 cells in different batches. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) of the perovskite solar cells was measured using the Enlitech QE-R3011 quantum efficiency measurement system.
