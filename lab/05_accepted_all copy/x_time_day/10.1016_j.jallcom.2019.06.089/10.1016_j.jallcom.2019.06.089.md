# Abstract

Minimizing the interface loss of perovskite solar cells is critical to achieving high photovoltaic performance, and intensive research is underway on interfacial engineering in this regard. In this work, we introduce a ZnO nanoparticles (ZnO NPs) interlayer between phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) and a metal electrode in order to reduce the interface loss due to charge recombination and device degradation, and also investigate the dependence of device performance on the thickness and morphology of the PCBM and PCBM/ZnO electron transport bilayer. After achieving optimized PCBM and ZnO thickness, the PCBM/ZnO bilayer-based devices reached an average power conversion efficiency of  $ 15.63\% $  (Max.  $ 16.39\% $ ) with an open circuit voltage of  $ 1.05\mathrm{V} $ , short circuit current density of  $ 18.69\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , and fill factor of  $ 79.95\% $ . In addition, hysteresis behavior and atmospheric stability are significantly improved by the incorporation of a PCBM/ZnO bilayer. Therefore, the implementation of a PCBM/ZnO electron transport bilayer is a promising approach toward achieving a high-efficiency PSC with stable power output (low J-V hysteresis) and durability.

Keywords: ZnO nanoparticles; electron transport bilayer; interfacial engineering; interface loss; high-efficiency perovskite solar cells; stable perovskite solar cells

# 1. Introduction

In recent years, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials with the chemical formula   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbX_{3}  $   (X = I, Br, Cl) have attracted much attention because of their excellent photoelectric properties and high photovoltaic efficiency [1-6]. The organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite material has an appropriate band gap (1.5 eV), a high absorption coefficient, a long hole-electron diffusion length (100~1000 nm), and excellent charge-carrier mobility (  $  \sim10\ cm^{2}\ V^{-1}s^{-1}  $  ); furthermore, the band gap and hole–electron diffusion length can be adjusted by mixing in various halides or by replacing the methylammonium organic species with other constituents such as formamidium and ethylammonium [7-13]. Interestingly, in a very short period of time, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has rapidly improved from 3.8% in 2009 to 23.7% in 2018 through intensive research on compositions, crystal growth control and morphologies, interfacial engineering, and device architecture [13-21]

PSCs with various device architectures have been explored, but recently, p-i-n type planar PSCs have begun to receive more attention [22-26]. This is because the mesoporous-structure and n-i-p type planar PSC exhibit good performance, but the formation of a mesoporous or compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer requires high-temperature (>450 □) processing; as a result, the potential for scale-up manufacturing and tandem and flexible substrate applications remains limited [13, 22, 27-30]. Further, the n–i–p type planar PSC with an   $  n-TiO_{2}  $   as an electron transport layer exhibits a large hysteresis depending on the forward or reverse direction sweep [31, 32]. In contrast, due to the passivation effect of fullerene derivative deposited on the perovskite, the p-i-n type planar PSC having the typical structure of a transparent conductive electrode/hole transport layer (HTL)/perovskite/electron transport layer (ETL)/metal electrode shows substantially reduced hysteresis behavior compared to the n-i-p type planar

PSC [33-36].

Phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) is commonly used as an ETL in PSCs of the p-i-n type planar structure, due to its high electron mobility and dissolution in non-polar solvents [22]. In addition, the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy level (3.9 eV) of PCBM can be well matched to the conductive band level of an organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite material, which allows for efficient electron transport between the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite and PCBM interfaces [6, 37, 38]. However, it is difficult to form a uniform and defect-free PCBM film on the perovskite layer due to the low solubility and low viscosity of the solution, as well as the aggregation behavior of PCBM, which results in poor surface morphology and uncovered perovskite surfaces [39-41]. Uncovered perovskite surfaces are in direct contact with metal and perovskite, resulting in charge recombination and device degradation [13, 22]. Thus, there has been much research into inserting an interlayer (e.g., Ca, LiF, C60/bathocuproine (BCP), poly[(9,9-bis(30-(N,N-dimethylamino)propyl)-2,7-fluorene)-alt-2,7-(9,9-dioctylfluorene)] (PFN), and metal oxides) between the PCBM and the metal electrode in order to avoid such undesirable shunt paths and to promote electron transfer [39, 42-47]. Among these interface materials, zinc oxide (ZnO) has attracted attention as an electron transport interlayer due to its high electron mobility, good optical transparency, and environment stability, and its bilayer structure with PCBM has been studied in order to improve the performance of PSCs. Sai et al. reported the successful production of highly efficient, stable and reproducible planar heterogeneous  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  solar cells with power conversion efficiency (PCE) of up to  $ 15.9\% $  by introducing PCBM/ZnO electron transporting interlayers [48]. Jie et al. reported that ZnO can effectively modify the PCBM/Ag interface and increase the  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $  values, resulting in improved efficiency [49]. Weiming et al. reported a  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_x\mathrm{Cl}_{3 - x} $  based perovskite solar cell with PCE above  $ 14\% $  by taking the advantage of a PCBM/ZnO double ETL as well as a

short-time air aging step [46]. Soyun et al. reported the hybrid interlayer, which is a mixture of ZnO nanoparticles with   $  PC_{70}BM  $   electron transport material, could improve the   $  J_{SC}  $   value of perovskite solar cells due to enhanced charge-generation efficiency and reduced contact resistance [50]. Recently, Sawanta et al. reported that a high efficiency (> 19% PCE) p-i-n PSC with   $  (FAPbI_{3})_{0.85}(MAPbBr_{3})_{0.15}  $   and PCBM/ZnO ETL by optimizing architectures and thickness of   $  np-NiO_{x}  $   [51]. The above studies indicate that when ZnO is introduced between PCBM and Ag electrode, the device performance can be significantly enhanced by improving the energy level alignment and effectively reducing charge recombination and contact resistance. In particular, Sawanta et al. has demonstrated the importance of optimizing the thickness of the interface for effective charge transport and reduction of recombination [51]. However, detailed studies on the effect of each layer thickness of PCBM/ZnO bilayer on the performance of a p-i-n type planar PSC with   $  ITO/NiO_{y}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM/ZnO  $   NPs/Ag architecture under various process conditions have not yet been thoroughly investigated and have not been compared so far.

Therefore, in this work, we systematically studied the dependence of its PSC-performance on the thickness and morphology of the PCBM and PCBM/ZnO electron transport bilayer. When the interlayer of ZnO NPs was inserted between the PCBM and the metal electrode, the charge recombination and device degradation were e-ectively suppressed, and as a result the PCE and air stability of the device were improved. Furthermore, we optimized the PCBM/ZnO bilayer and demonstrated the fabrication of a highly improved p-i-n type planar PSC with an ITO/NiO $ _{x} $ /CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3} $ /PCBM/ZnO NPs/Ag architecture; the p-i-n type planar PSC based on a PCBM/ZnO bilayer showed a PCE of 15.63% with an open circuit voltage (V $ _{OC} $ ) of 1.05 V, a short circuit current density (J $ _{SC} $ ) of 18.69 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and a fill factor (FF) of 79.95% with negligible hysteresis, and the highest PCE of 16.39% was achieved.

# 2. Experimental section

The pre-patterned ITO glass-substrates (10 Ohm sq $ ^{-1} $ , Samsung Corning) were sequentially cleaned with acetone, deionized water, and isopropyl alcohol under an ultrasonic bath for 20 min, then dried in an oven at 80 □ for 10 min. Next, the substrates were exposed to UV-ozone for 30 min. For the NiO $ _x $  layer, the NiO $ _x $  precursor was spin-coated on ITO substrates at 3000 rpm for 40 s, then annealed at 350 □ for 30 minutes in air. The NiO $ _x $  precursor (0.1M) was prepared according to a modified procedure reported in the literature; briefly, 0.248 g nickel acetate tetrahydrate (Ni(OCOCH $ _3 $ ) $ _2 $  $ \cdot $ 4H $ _2 $ O, Sigma-Aldrich) was dissolved in 1 ml ethanol with ethanolamine (NH $ _2 $ CH $ _2 $ CH $ _2 $ OH, Sigma-Aldrich), where the mole ratio of Ni $ ^{2+} $ :ethanolamine was maintained at 1:1 in solution and the solution was stirred at 70 □ until it was dark green [52]. A CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  perovskite precursor solution of a 40 wt% concentration was prepared by dissolving 490 mg Lead(II) iodide (PbI $ _2 $ , Alfa Aesar) and 155 mg methylammonium iodide (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I, Dyesol) in 0.8 ml diemthylformamide (DMF, Sigma-Aldrich) and 0.2 ml dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, Sigma-Aldrich) in an N $ _2 $ -filled glove box. The perovskite precursor was spin-coated at 500 rpm for 5 s, then accelerated at 6000 rpm and held for 45 s. At the same time, in order to form a uniform thin film, 700 ml of a toluene solvent was dropped 20 s after the start of acceleration rotation, then annealed at 100 □ for 10 minutes in an N $ _2 $ -filled glove box. The PCBM (20 mg ml $ ^{-1} $  solution in chlorobenzene) layer was spin-coated on the prepared perovskite film at 3000, 5000, and 7000 rpm for 45 s in an N $ _2 $ -filled glove box. ZnO NPs dispersion (Dispersed in IPA at a concentration of 2.5 wt%, Zinc oxide nanoparticle < 10 nm, Nano Clean Tech) was spin-coated on the PCBM layer at 2000, 5000, and 8000 rpm for 40 s in air. Finally, the Ag electrodes (thickness 100 nm) were

thermally deposited on the ZnO electron transporting layer using a thermal evaporator (<   $  1 \times 10^{-7}  $   Torr), and the active area of   $  4.64 \, mm^2  $   was defined using a shadow mask. The device performances were measured using a source measurement unit instrument (Model 2400 SourceMater, Keithley Instruments) and a solar simulator (SoI3A Class AAA, Oriel Instruments) under the standard AM 1.5G condition (  $  100 \, mW cm^{-2}  $  ). The surface morphology and roughness of the thin film were observed using a field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM, Hitachi, SU-70/S-4800) as well as atomic force microscopy (AFM, Park system, XE7). The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) was measured using spectrofluorophotometer (SHIMADZU, RF-6000). Transient photovoltage (TPV) and transient photocurrent (TPC) measurement were performed using multi-functional organic semiconductor parameter test system (T4000, McScience) with oscilloscope (DPO-2014B, Tektronix). TPV and TPC were measured in the ambient air by irradiating short pulse of light under the short circuit condition using a 0.5 W white-LED for bias light illumination, and transient charge decay was calculated by fitting the TPV and TPC curves using a bi-exponential decay function.

Figure 1 shows a schematic illustration of the device configuration for the p-i-n type planar PSC, a typical cross-section SEM image of the device, and an energy level diagram of each layer in the device. The perovskite layer was sandwiched with a  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  layer (HTL) and PCBM/ZnO bilayer (ETL) to form a p-i-n structure, as shown in Figure 1(a) and (b). The  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film fabricated on top of the  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  layer has a very smooth surface morphology and is composed of well-developed grains each of a few hundred nanometers in size; SEM and AFM measurements showed that the perovskite film had a film thickness of about 200 nm and a root mean square (rms) roughness of about  $ 11.11~\mathrm{nm} $  (Figure S1). Thus, the perovskite layer formed in this work is considered to be very low in roughness, allowing for a thin ( $ <60~\mathrm{nm} $  thick) PCBM layer (Figure S2). The PCBM/ZnO bilayer was fabricated simply through spin-coating a ZnO NPs dispersion solution onto the thin PCBM layer, without thermal annealing. From the energy level difference between the conduction band of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of the PCBM and/or the conduction band of ZnO, it can be seen that this configuration is suitable for efficient exciton dissociation and electron transport, and also that the hole-blocking capability can be dramatically improved due to the deep valence band level of ZnO [48]. We therefore expected that the free charge carriers (or excitons) generated in the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  layer would be more efficiently extracted (or dissociated) and transferred to the electrode via the PCBM/ZnO bilayer than the PCBM layer.

In order to investigate the effects of the PCBM and PCBM/ZnO bilayer thickness on device performance and to find the optimal combination of PCBM and ZnO layer thickness, we fabricated p-i-n structure PSCs with fixed thicknesses of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  layer but varying thicknesses of the PCBM and ZnO layer. The PCBM layer thicknesses were reduced to 57.6, 47.6, and  $ 31.7\mathrm{nm} $  as the spin-coating rates increased to 3000, 5000, and 7000 rpm, respectively (Figure S2). As can be seen from the cross-section SEM images in Figure S2, the

PCBM layer coated on the perovskite layer is very smooth and appears to cover the entire  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  surface, even though the thickness is less than about  $ 57~\mathrm{nm} $ . However, AFM measurements showed that the surface roughness of PCBM coated on the perovskite layer increased to 1.40, 1.74, and  $ 2.06~\mathrm{nm} $  as the spin-coating rate increased to 3000, 5000, and  $ 7000~\mathrm{rpm} $ , respectively (Figure S3). It can additionally be assumed that this can also induce direct contact between the perovskite and the metal electrode [13, 22].

Figure 2(a) shows the changes in PCEs depending on the thickness of the single PCBM (w/o) layer and the PCBM/ZnO bilayer with PCBM and ZnO thin films of various thicknesses; the thicknesses of PCBM and/or ZnO layer were controlled through controlling the spin-coating rate. The average photovoltaic parameters are given in detail in Table S1. The average device efficiency increased from  $ 11.37\% $  to  $ 12.09\% $  as the PCBM layer thickness decreased from  $ 57.6~\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 47.6~\mathrm{nm} $ , and decreased  $ 10.06\% $  as the PCBM thickness further decreased to  $ 31.7~\mathrm{nm} $ . As shown in Table S1, a similar tendency is observed for the  $ \mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{SC}} $ ,  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $ , and FF. Thus, optimal device performance was obtained for a thin PCBM layer having a thickness of  $ 47.6~\mathrm{nm} $ , and the maximum PCE was  $ 13.43\% $ . These results are well explained by the results of previous studies [13]; if the PCBM layer is thick, the dissociated carrier must travel a greater distance to reach the electrode, where the probability of recombination increases and the resulting high series resistance can result in a lower current density and fill factor. On the other hand, if the thickness of the PCBM layer is too thin, a leakage current is generated due to the direct contact between the perovskite layer and the metal electrode, and thus  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $  and FF can be greatly reduced.

Interestingly, when the ZnO thin films of various thicknesses was introduced and formed

a PCBM/ZnO bilayer as shown in Figure 2(b), the performance of the device was remarkably improved compared to those of single PCBM layer (see Figure 2(a) and Table S1). In addition, a similar tendency is also observed for the  $ \mathrm{J_{SC}} $ ,  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $ , and FF. Therefore, it can be seen that the improvement of the device performance is due to the increase of  $ \mathrm{J_{SC}} $ ,  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $ , and FF by the PCBM/ZnO bilayer, which can be attributed to efficient exciton dissociation and electron transport by the favorable energy level alignment between the perovskite and PCBM/ZnO (see Figure 1), as well as the hole-blocking capability due to the deep valence band level of ZnO [9, 48]. Furthermore, compared to the other conditions, when ZnO with varying thickness was coated on a  $ 47.6~\mathrm{nm} $ -thick (5000 rpm) PCBM layer, the overall device performance was excellent. Specifically, the average device efficiency increased from  $ 13.49\% $  to  $ 15.25\% $  as the ZnO layer thickness decreased from  $ 51.3~\mathrm{nm} $  (2000 rpm) to  $ 33.5~\mathrm{nm} $  (5000 rpm), and decreased  $ 13.19\% $  as ZnO thickness further decreased to  $ 14.9~\mathrm{nm} $  (8000 rpm). Similar trends were also observed for the FF under the same condition, which suggests that the change of efficiencies is mainly due to the changes of FF. The average FF increased from  $ 69.44\% $  to  $ 77.99\% $  as the ZnO layer thickness decreased from  $ 51.3~\mathrm{nm} $  (2000 rpm) to  $ 33.5~\mathrm{nm} $  (5000 rpm), and decreased  $ 67.53\% $  as ZnO thickness further decreased to  $ 14.9~\mathrm{nm} $  (8000 rpm). Therefore, it is also reasonable to consider that the change in FF is due to the thickness and morphology change of the ZnO thin film. As can be seen from the SEM and AFM images of Figure 3, when the thickness of ZnO decreased to  $ 14.9~\mathrm{nm} $ , the surface became rough (the rms increased to  $ 2.84~\mathrm{nm} $ ) and pinholes were observed. This leads to direct contact between the PCBM and the electrodes, thereby reducing the charge transfer capability and consequently reducing the FF [46, 55, 56]. On the other hand, when the thickness of ZnO layer is thick, the electron collection ability is deteriorated due to an increase in series resistance ( $ \mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{s}} $ ), and the FF decreases [57, 58].

In order to investigate the charge carrier dynamics when the ZnO interlayer is introduced between PCBM and Ag electrode, we compared the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) of the corresponding perovskite films. Figure 5(a) shows the steady-state PL spectra of the glass/perovskite and glass/perovskite/PCBM layer with/without the ZnO interlayer. The excitation light wavelength is  $ 700~\mathrm{nm} $  and the PL emission is collected from the PCBM or PCBM/ZnO side. As shown in Figure 5(a), the pristine perovskite film exhibits a strong luminescence peak at around  $ 768~\mathrm{nm} $ , which is in good accordance with the bandgap of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  [60, 61]. When the PCBM ETL was introduced on the perovskite film, the PL peak intensity was drastically reduced. Further, when the PCBM/ZnO bilayer was formed by introducing ZnO on the PCBM, the PL peak intensity was further reduced. The results indicate that the extracting and transporting charge carriers from the perovskite to the ETL is more efficient in the PCBM/ZnO bilayer and that the non-radiative carrier recombination is suppressed by the PCBM/ZnO bilayer [60, 62], which is consistent with the above-mentioned J-V characteristics and EQE results mentioned above and is an important factor in the  $ \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{SC}} $  increase. In addition, the blue shift of the PL peak indicates that spontaneous radiation recombination is suppressed and the trap density is low, which can lead to improvements in the  $ \mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{OC}} $  [60].

Further, to verify this result, we performed the transient photovoltage (TPV) and transient photocurrent (TPC) measurement of device. TPV and TPC technique can provide additional information on carrier dynamics at the interface, which allow to observe the generation of charge carriers and its decay in solar cell; TPV allows to monitor the relevant bulk lifetime

encapsulation. This is because the J-V characteristic as a function of the scan direction, so-called J-V hysteresis, is an important indicator for evaluating the actual performance and stable power output of the device. In addition, one of their main concerns is whether perovskite solar cells are durable in ambient conditions and applications. As shown in Figure 6(a) and Figure S4, the typical J-V curve obtained by different scanning directions (forward and reverse) for the PCBM/ZnO bilayer-based device showed negligible hysteresis similar to that of the PCBM based device, which indicates that there is no change in hysteresis behavior due to the introduction of the ZnO layer and the formation of the PCBM/ZnO bilayer. In addition, as shown in Figure 6(b), the PCBM/ZnO bilayer-based devices showed slow degradation rates in terms of device performance as the exposure time in the ambient environment increased. The PCBM/ZnO bilayer-based PSCs maintained more than ~ 54% of the PCE compared to the original value after exposure to the ambient environment for 20 days. However, the PCBM based PSCs exhibited relatively fast degradation rates in device performance with the increase in exposure time to the ambient environment, resulting in the PCEs of the PCBM based PSCs not being measured after exposure to the ambient environment for 20 days. It has been found that when using the PCBM layer as an ETL of PSCs, the device performance can be degraded in ambient atmosphere through the adsorption of oxygen or water by PCBM [55, 66]. In addition, incomplete thin film coverage of the PCBM can lead to direct contact between the perovskite and the metal electrode, thereby reducing performance [13, 22, 55]. These results clearly show that the PCBM/ZnO bilayer-based PSCs has better J-V hysteresis characteristics and stability than PCBM based PSCs in the ambient conditions, thus suggesting that the PCBM/ZnO electron transport bilayer is a promising approach for a high-efficiency PSC with stable power output (low J-V hysteresis) and durability.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, we successfully demonstrated high performance p-i-n type planar perovskite solar cells incorporating a PCBM/ZnO electron transport bilayer simply through spin-coating a ZnO NPs dispersion solution onto the PCBM layer without thermal annealing. It can be seen that the PCBM/ZnO bilayer improves the   $  J_{SC}  $  ,   $  V_{OC}  $  , and FF of the device by boosting charge extraction and charge carrier transfer as well as suppressing charge carrier recombination, resulting in a dramatic improvement in the device performance of PCBM/ZnO bilayer-based PSC. When an optimal thickness of PCBM/ZnO bilayer was formed, the PCBM/ZnO bilayer-based device was reached an average PCE of 15.63 with   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.05 V,   $  J_{SC}  $   of 18.69 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and FF of 79.95%, and the highest PCE of 16.39% was achieved. More importantly, it was found that the PCBM/ZnO bilayer-based devices showed a significant improvement in hysteresis behavior and stability in ambient conditions, as compared to single PCBM based devices. These results show that the PCBM/ZnO electron transport bilayer is a promising approach for high-efficiency PSC with stable power output (low J-V hysteresis) and durability.

# Highlights

● ZnO nanoparticles were introduced on the PCBM layer to reduce interfacial losses.

● PCBM/ZnO bilayer reduced interface resistance and leakage current of the device.

- Optimum thickness PCBM/ZnO bilayer-based PSC achieved a high PCE of 16.39%.

● Hysteresis and air-stability are greatly improved by introducing PCBM/ZnO bilayer.
