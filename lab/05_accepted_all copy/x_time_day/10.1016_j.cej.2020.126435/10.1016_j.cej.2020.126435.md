# 1. Introduction

High-power-conversion-efficiency (PCE) perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been considered as promising candidate for commercial photovoltaic applications owing to the excellent properties of perovskite, such as high defect tolerance, long diffusion, excellent light harvesting, and facile tuning of the bandgap [1-5]. The PCE of PSCs has increased from  $ 3.8\% $  to over  $ 25\% $  through developing new functional materials, improving device preparation technology and interfacial engineering [3-8]. PSCs based on the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  mesoporous structure have been most successful in high-efficiency photovoltaic devices [8,9]. However, the strong photocatalytic effect of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  can accelerate decomposition of the perovskite material under ultraviolet light illumination [10-12]. In addition, high-temperature ( $ >400^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) annealing of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film is also a disadvantage to attain low production costs and

The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with   $  SnO_{2}  $   as an electron transport layer, achieve over 25%. However, the long-term stability and reduction of hysteresis are still great challenges. To solve these problems, different from the traditional treatment on ITO by UV or ozone, we introduce a simple, clean solvent engineering method of water spin-coating in the preparation of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer to obtain a compact and highly uniform   $  SnO_{2}  $   film. It is confirmed that after water treatment, the interface defects of the perovskite film are greatly reduced, and the electron transportation is also accelerated due to the near-perfect   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer. As a result, an improved PCE above 19% is obtained with triple cation (Cs/FA/MA)-mixed PSCs compared with   $  \sim16\%  $   PCE of the controlled devices. More importantly, the initial PCE of unwrapped PSCs with the water treatment can maintain 90% of their initial efficiency after exposure to ambient conditions for 360 h, while, the PSCs based on a conventional spin-coated   $  SnO_{2}  $   film degrade   $  \sim30\%  $  . Therefore, it is believe that this water spin-coating pre-treatment on   $  SnO_{2}  $   film we introduced can be a valid and practical method to obtain high-performance PSCs based on   $  SnO_{2}  $  .

Other teams optimised electron transport between the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL and perovskite layer using phenyl–C61–butyric acid methyl ester, graphene, and other self-assembled monolayers [27–30]. However, increased complexity of processing is a general disadvantage of utilising these new procedures and materials to improve the quality of   $  SnO_{2}  $   films, and hence, reduce the hysteresis of PSCs.

We developed a facile and environmentally friendly water treatment method to obtain a more compact modified   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL (M–  $  SnO_{2}  $  ) with less pinhole, compared with the traditional spin-coated   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL (T-  $  SnO_{2}  $  ). # 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Material

The commercial colloidal dispersion of   $  SnO_{2}  $   was obtained from Alfa Aesar (tin (IV) oxide, 15% in   $  H_{2}O  $   colloidal dispersion). Methylammonium bromide (MABr), formamidinium iodide (FAI), lead iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  ), lead bromine (  $  PbBr_{2}  $  ) 4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP), Li-bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI), [6,6]-phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (  $  PC_{61}BM  $  ), cobalt(III), and tris(bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide) (FK209) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Ethyl acetate (EA) was purchased from Aladdin. CsI, chlorobenzene (CB), dimethylformamide (DMF), and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) were obtained from Sigma Aldrich. 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-dip-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (SpiroOMeTAD) (99.9%) was obtained from Shenzhen Feiming Science and Technology Co., Ltd. All chemicals were used without further purification.

# 2.2. Fabrication of   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL

Before spin-coating the commercial   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloidal dispersion, ITO glass substrates of size 2 cm × 2 cm, were cleaned with water, then acetone, and ethanol for 15 min in turn, and subsequently treated with UV-ozone for 30 min. The as-purchased   $  SnO_{2}  $   commercial colloidal dispersion was diluted with DI water (1:4 wt). T-  $  SnO_{2}  $   ETLs were formed on the cleaned ITO by spin-coating the diluted   $  SnO_{2}  $   commercial colloidal dispersion at 3000 rpm for 30 s, followed by heating at 100 °C for 30 min. M-  $  SnO_{2}  $   ETLs were produced by first spin-coating the ITO with 1–3 ml of DI water at 3000 rpm for 10 s (without subsequent annealing), followed by spin-coating the diluted   $  SnO_{2}  $   commercial colloidal dispersion. The schematic diagram of the water treatment process is shown in Fig. S1, and the all experimental environment is under ambient air temperature with 25 ± 5 °C humidity with 30 ± 2%.

# 2.3. Fabrication of perovskite films

Perovskite solutions (1.0 M) were prepared by dissolving CsI (0.05 M), FAI (0.81 M), MABr (0.14 M), PbI $ _2 $  (0.845 M), and PbBr $ _2 $  (0.155 M) in anhydrous DMF: DMSO 4:1 (V:V). The mixed perovskite solutions were stirred continuously overnight at room temperature (20 °C25°C). Perovskite solutions with other concentrations (1.2–2.0 M) were prepared by the same process. Perovskite films were produced by solvent engineering method, as follows. The perovskite solutions were

spin-coated in two steps, at 2000 rpm for 10 s, and 4000 rpm for 30 s. Subsequently, 300   $  \mu  $  L of ethyl acetate (EA)/chlorobenzene (CB) was poured on the spinning substrate over 1–2 s quickly during the second step after 5 s. The perovskite films were heated at 100 °C for 30 min.

NOTE: All processes were carried out under a nitrogen atmosphere in a glove box unless specified.

# 2.4. Fabrication of spiro-OMeTAD hole transporting layer (HTL) and conductive coating

The spiro-OMeTAD solution and detailed process can be found in our earlier reports   $  [31,32]  $  . A gold layer of thickness 60–80 nm was deposited on the HTL.

# 2.5. Characterisation and measurements

The surface morphologies and roughness of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   films were determined by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (FESEM, JEOL JSM-7100F) and atomic force microscopy (AFM) (SPM9700, Shimadzu, Japan). The microstructure of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanoparticles was characterised by high-resolution TEM (JEOL JEM 2010, Japan) and X-ray diffraction (XRD, Brucker D8). UV–vis spectroscopy (UV-3600, Shimadzu) was used to determine visible light absorption and reflectance of the perovskite and   $  SnO_{2}  $   films. The photovoltaic parameters (J-V-curves/I-V-curves) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) were determined with a digital multimeter (Keithley 2402) under AM1.5G 100 mW/cm² illumination using a solar simulator (Newport). Electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) of the PSCs were recorded with an electrochemical workstation (CHI-660D) under dark conditions. The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL were measured on a fluorescence spectrometer (PicoQuant Fluotime 300) using a 514 nm picosecond pulsed laser.

Cyclic voltammograms and membrane impedance were completed in a one-compartment cells on electrochemical workstation (CHI-660D). Pt wire as the counter electrode and the reference electrode is Ag/ AgCl (sat'd KCl). The electrolyte solution is 1 mM K $ _{4} $ Fe (CN) $ _{6} $  + 1 mM K $ _{3} $ Fe(CN) $ _{6} $  in aqueous 0.5 M KCl, pH 2.5, scan rate: 50 mV/s.

# 3. Results and discussion

The quality of   $  SnO_{2}  $   is greatly dependent on the hydrophilicity of the ITO. A   $  SnO_{2}  $   film with less pinhole is predicted to be beneficial to obtain a continuous, high-coverage perovskite film and high-efficiency PSCs. For ITO, UV or ozone treatment is commonly used to increase the ITO surface hydrophilicity and produce a compact ETL with reduced pinholes [20,26]. Different from the conventional T-  $  SnO_{2}  $   film coated on the UV treated ITO, in our work, the modified M-  $  SnO_{2}  $   was spin-coated on the ITO treated by deionised water without further treatment. To investigate the hydrophilicity of ITO with different treatment, the contact angles between the ITO surface and water were tested and the results were presented in Fig. 1a and b. Without water treatment, the contact angle between water and ITO is 30.5°. However, it decreased to 17.8° when the ITO was treated by water, confirming that the hydrophilicity of the ITO surface was enhanced. The reason could be that the hydrophilic group (-OH) could be added on the surface of ITO substrate by water-pretreatment, which can be benefit for the ductility of   $  SnO_{2}  $   slurry to form a flat film with high-crystallinity. At the same time, a better interface contact of   $  SnO_{2}  $   with ITO was achieved. The morphological changes in the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film that resulted from the different spin-coating processes were observed by SEM as shown in Fig. 1c–f. Compared with the T-  $  SnO_{2}  $ , the density of the pinholes in M-  $  SnO_{2}  $   is significantly decreased, and the M-  $  SnO_{2}  $   film is more compact and uniform due to the improved hydrophilicity of ITO. Furthermore, the lower roughness of 0.614 nm with the M-  $  SnO_{2}  $   than T-  $  SnO_{2}  $   film in Fig. S10 indicate that a more smooth and compact surface have obtained.

$$

$$

(1)

where  $ \mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{REV}} $ ,  $ \mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{FOR}} $ , and  $ \mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{AVG}} $  represent the PCE measured under the reverse and forward scan directions. Based on calculation, the HI of

Capacitance-frequency (C-F) characteristics were also measured to further investigate the effect of water treatment on hysteresis. This result further confirms that the accumulation of electrons and ionic charges in the layer interface were reduced, and the electronic trap states at the interface of perovskite/SnO  $  _{2}  $  were also decreased [25].

$$
N _ {t} = \frac {2 \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0} V _ {\mathrm{TFL}}}{e I ^ {2}}

(2)

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, we developed a simple, environmentally friendly, and low-cost method to obtain compact and smooth  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  ETL  $ (\mathrm{M - SnO_2}) $  by water spin-coating treatment. Due to the improved qualities of  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  ETL, the  $ \mathrm{M - SnO_2} $  film exhibited more efficient charge extraction and transportation compared with the control sample  $ (\mathrm{T - SnO_2}) $ . Moreover, based on the  $ \mathrm{M - SnO_2} $  film, defects in the coated perovskite layer were sharply reduced and the charge recombination in the interface between perovskite and  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  film was well-suppressed. Benefiting from these characteristics, the assembled PSCs based on  $ \mathrm{M - SnO_2} $  obtained a stable output PCE of  $ 19.3\% $  with almost negligible hysteresis, and both the stability and reproducibility of high-efficiency planar PSCs were enhanced. This high quality of the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  film under low-temperature process bound to promote the application of flexible optoelectronic devices. Therefore, we also believe that the high-quality  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  ETL obtained by this water treatment can accelerate the commercial application of high-efficiency and stable PSCs.
