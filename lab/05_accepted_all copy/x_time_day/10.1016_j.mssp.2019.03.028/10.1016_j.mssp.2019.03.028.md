# 1. Introduction

Nowadays, with the increasing consumption of fossil fuels, energy shortage and environment protection have become important factors to limit the future development of human society. Solar energy, an efficient, clear and inexhaustible energy, has attracted a tremendous amount of attention. As the reason of its long carrier diffusion length, high absorption coefficient, and superior ambipolar carrier conduction properties, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite is widely studied in recent years   $  [1-4]  $  . The efficiency using these materials as solar cell devices have increased from 3.8% in 2009   $  [5]  $   to 23.3% in 2018   $  [6]  $  , making the fastest record of solar technology development up to now. Despite enormous advantage in perovskite solar cells, challenges are still in the way to restrict the commercial application in large scale.

The solids perovskite structure compound with an  $ \mathrm{ABX}_3 $  composition in which A and B are cations with different sizes and X is an anion. The B and X ions make up  $ \mathrm{BX}_6^{4-} $  octahedral, meanwhile, the A cation is located in the cavity between the four  $ \mathrm{BX}_6^{4-} $  octahedral and is surrounded by 12 nearest neighbors. In spite of the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the PSCs has been rapidly improved, many changes, such as defects, poor morphology and expensive hole transporte materials (HTM), are still on the way to commercial application. In this paper, CuSCN was used as the HTM to substitute the popular Spiro-OMeTAD, because of its high hole transfer capability and much lower cost. A simple, planar construction device of FTO/c-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /CuSCN/Au has been prepared, yielding a best PCE of 10.41%. To improve the stability and performance of the device, 1 mol % CuSCN was innovatively introduced into the absorber layer. The grain size of the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   layer has increased obviously and the defects in the device were also reduced. The fast charge transportation and extraction make the short-circuit currents increased from 18.39 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   to 22.18 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  . The PCE of the CuSCN doped perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have increased 21.4% compared to the control device without added. In the meantime, the hysteresis and stability have also been improved effectively. The PCE of the CuSCN doped MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   device only dropped 28% from the initial efficiency after 500 h stored in an ambient air with temperature of 25°C and humidity of 20%, whereas the device without CuSCN doped dropped 48%. This result indicates that CuSCN has great potential as a doping to improve the morphology of perovskite films and enhance the performance of the PSCs.

The  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  films with PbSe-doped maintained the perovskite structure, and the device exhibited excellent stability in ambient atmosphere. However, the introduction of PbSe into the perovskite layer further aggravates the environmental impact of Pb, while the price of Se is relatively high. Inspired by the work mentioned above, looking for environment friendly, low cost, stable and efficient material is an efficient method for improving the performance of PSCs.

The poor environmental stability and high cost of the Spiro-MeOTAD (2,2',7,7'-Tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spiro-bifluorene), which was widely used as hole transport material has also hindered the development of the PSCs. Recently, Wuyou Fu and Hari Bala et al. used the hole-conductor-free   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Pb(I_{1-x}Br_{x})_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells with carbon counter electrode and received a high PCE (9.99%) and excellent stability [13]. Copper thiocyanate (CuSCN), a p-type material which was used as hole conductor due to its high hole mobility, affordable price, good stability and simple preparation process [14–16]. What's more, as the reason of copper (Cu) have similar ionic radius to Pb and thiocyanate (SCN) have similar chemical behaviors and properties to true halide [17]. Therefore, there are many reports on Cu and SCN substitution in PSCs [18–22]. In 2015, Cui et al. reported two Cu-based hybrid perovskites,   $  (\mathrm{p}-\mathrm{C}_{6}\mathrm{H}_{5}\mathrm{C}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{4}\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{2}\mathrm{CuBr}_{4}  $   and   $  (\mathrm{CH}_{3}(\mathrm{CH}_{2})_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{2}\mathrm{CuBr}_{4}  $   with PCE of 0.51% and 0.63%, respectively [18]. Liang and his co-workers [20] added a small amount of   $  \mathrm{Pb(SCN)}_{2}  $   into   $  PbI_{2}  $   and received larger-sized crystals and less traps which had high reproducibility and good stability, and they got the highest PCE of 11.07%. So far, however, there is no report on CuSCN being introducing into perovskite film. In this paper, CuSCN was not only used as hole transport layer but also dopant, a small amount of CuSCN was doped into the perovskite layer to improve the charge transportation and extraction, the   $  J_{sc}  $   was improved about 20.6%, and other photovoltaic properties were also highly improved. The device with 1 mol% CuSCN doped   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   has increased grain size, improved morphology and reduced defects. The average PCE of the 1 mol% CuSCN doped   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   increased 21.4% compared to the control devices without CuSCN doped (from 9.60% to 11.52%), and the stability and hysteresis were also improved effectively.

# 2. Results and discussion

X-ray diffraction (XRD) characterizations were used to evaluate the effect of CuSCN doping in the perovskite lattice and crystallinity. As shown in Fig. 1a, both the XRD patterns of perovskite films with or without CuSCN doped samples showed obvious diffraction peaks located at approximately  $ 14.1^{\circ} $ ,  $ 28.4^{\circ} $ , and  $ 31.9^{\circ} $ , which are corresponded to the (110), (220), and (310) planes of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ . The high unity characteristic peaks in perovskite films implied that the incorporated 1 mol % of CuSCN in  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite layer didn't change the perovskite phase and the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite with high purity were successfully prepared. We also note that the diffraction peaks in 1 mol% CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film are sharper than that of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film (see Fig. 1b), which implying the increased crystallinity with better appearance. To validate the existence of CuSCN in perovskite film, energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) was used to analyze the chemical composition of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  [23] and 1 mol% CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films. The surface morphologies of the prepared  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / 1\mathrm{mol}\% $  CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films were depicted in Fig. 2a and b. Although the SEM images of both the perovskite layers shows high surface coverage, the 1 mol% CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film exhibited more compact, uniform and smooth morphology with larger grain size. As shown in Fig. 1c, carbon, nitrogen, lead, and iodine elements are derived from the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film, and the uniform distribution of copper and sulphur elements showed in 1 mol% CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film demonstrated that the CuSCN does

existed in the final perovskite film.

We further explore the chemical states and interaction of CuSCN with perovskite film by using X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS) measurement. The full survey spectrum of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and  $ 1\mathrm{mol}\% $  CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite layers are shown in Fig. 2a. The emergence of Cu 2p and S 2p peaks in  $ 1\mathrm{mol}\% $  CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film, is further evidence of CuSCN introduce into perovskite layer what is consistent with EDX result. Fig. 2b and c shown the XPS of Cu 2P and Pb 4f, respectively. For the bare  $ \mathrm{MABbI}_3 $  film, the binding energies of  $ \mathrm{Pb}4\mathrm{f}_{7/2} $  and  $ \mathrm{Pb}4\mathrm{f}_{5/2} $  are 138.5 and  $ 143.4\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. Nevertheless, the binding energies of  $ \mathrm{Pb}4\mathrm{f}_{7/2} $  and  $ \mathrm{Pb}4\mathrm{f}_{5/2} $  are 137.5 and  $ 142.3\mathrm{eV} $  in  $ 1\mathrm{mol}\% $  CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film, respectively, exhibiting a little shift in binding energy. There are many researchers reported that surface states of perovskite films and Pb 4f binding energy could be changed by interacting between perovskite and other materials [24,25]. In this paper, the binding energy of Pb 4f shift to lower values indicated that the changed chemical environment and the under-coordinated Pb interaction between CuSCN in the perovskite film. Moreover, Fig. 2d illustrates the calibrated Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) spectra of CuSCN powder,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ , and  $ 1\mathrm{mol}\% $  CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  thin films, which to probe the possible electronic interactions between  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and CuSCN. We focused on the spectral region around  $ 3000~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ , the frequencies of the C-H vibration modes, which are affected by the intermolecular C-H···N hydrogen bonds (H-bonds) [26]. Thus, the formation of the H-bonds causes the blue shift in this region, which are attributed to the nitrogen atoms in thiocyanate and ammonium hydrogen atoms. This result is in accordance with the previously reported role of thiocyanate anion in hybrid halide perovskite [27].

The surface morphologies of the prepared  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / 1\mathrm{mol}\% $  CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films were depicted in Fig. 3a and b. Although the SEM images of both the perovskite layers shows high surface coverage, the  $ 1\mathrm{mol}\% $  CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film exhibited more compact, uniform and smooth morphology with larger grain size.

Fig. 3d shows the grain size distribution of the perovskite film, it is apparently that the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film grains are concentrated in  $ 100 - 200\mathrm{nm} $ , while the  $ 1\mathrm{mol}\% $  CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film with an average size of  $ 300\mathrm{nm} $ . The increased grain size reveals the positive effect of CuSCN doping in  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film. This is because the larger grain with the better crystallinity and morphology are certainly conducive to improve the photo-induced charge carrier generation and transportation. Fig. 3c shows the cross-section SEM image of the complete device, which clearly reveals the thickness of the absorber and hole extraction layers to be about  $ 650\mathrm{nm} $  (about  $ 500\mathrm{nm} $  and  $ 150\mathrm{nm} $ , respectively).

The UV–vis absorption spectra obtained for perovskite films showed very similar profiles in both samples. As Fig. 3a shows that compared with the MAPbI $ _3 $  films, the absorption peak of the 1 mol% CuSCN doped MAPbI $ _3 $  has a little blue shift. The Kubelka-Munk function was used to determine the optical band gap, i.e.,  $ (\alpha h\nu)^{1/2} $  versus the photon energy of the exciting light ( $ h\nu $ ), where  $ \alpha $  is the absorption coefficient,  $ h $  is Planck's constant, and  $ \nu $  is the light frequency [28]. The band gap of 1 mol% CuSCN doped MAPbI $ _3 $ , which shows in Fig. 3b increased from 1.55 to 1.56 eV after 1% CuSCN was introduced into the MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite precursor solution. This slightly change suggests that there was no markedly improve in light absorption after introducing 1 mol% CuSCN in MAPbI $ _3 $  film.

In order to understand the recombination behavior of photo-excited carriers in the perovskite films, photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) were measurement as shown in Fig. 4c and d. Compared to the non-doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film deposited on the FTO substrate, a large rise in PL intensity was obtained in  $ 1\mathrm{mol}\% $  CuSCN doped samples. So much strong emission in case of latter entirely caused by the reduced defects and surface trap states in the perovskite film [29], which are responsible for non-radiative recombination in PSCs. The Schematic of the standard device structure can be seen from Fig. 5a. Fig. 5b presents the current density-voltage (J-V) curves of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and different CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  based solar cells recorded under simulated air mass (AM) 1.5G solar illumination by scanning the voltage from 1.2 to  $ -0.2\mathrm{V} $  at a rate of  $ 0.1\mathrm{V / s} $ . The best  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  solar cell exhibits a short-circuit current density  $ (\mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{sc}}) $  of  $ 18.39\mathrm{mA} / \mathrm{cm}^2 $ , an open-circuit voltage  $ (\mathrm{V_{oc}}) $  of  $ 0.935\mathrm{V} $  and a fill factor (FF) of  $ 59.87\% $ , yielding a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of

Compared to the best CuSCN based PSCs, which PCE exceeding 20% [31], the lower efficiency of our cells mainly resulted from the poor quality of the interface, different components and device structures, what's more, the different film preparation techniques. As we all know, the preparation of perovskite solar cell devices is interfered by many factors, such as materials, equipment, environment and unavoidable artificial and experience difference. Anyway, we note that compared with controls, 1 mol% CuSCN doped MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  devices shows increased J  $  _{sc}  $  and FF which all contribute to the improvement of PSCs. This is because the larger grain size in SEM image (Fig. 3b) reduces the grain boundary, carrier transport resistance and recombination, and facilitates electron extraction and transmission.

The photovoltaic characteristics and the distribution of results for the different CuSCN ratios in the perovskite solar cells are plotted in Fig. 6. All the devices were tested under 1 sun AM1.5G illumination with a mask of  $ 0.16\mathrm{cm}^2 $ . After incorporating CuSCN with a relatively low doping ratio  $ (0.5\% -1\% $  mol), the device performance of PSCs is enhanced. Continue to increase the doping ratio to  $ 10\% $  mol, however, the devices performance decreases obviously, which is even lower than that of control device. Among the photovoltaic parameters, the PCE enhancement upon CuSCN incorporation is primarily originated from the increase of  $ \mathrm{J_{sc}} $  and FF (Fig. 6b and c). The increase of  $ \mathrm{J_{sc}} $  is verified by EQE values of the devices with and without CuSCN doped as shown in Fig. 5d. And the enhanced FF may benefit from improved film morphology and interface contact.

# 3. Conclusion

In this study, CuSCN was not only used as hole transport materials but also was innovatively introduced into the perovskite absorber layer as dopant. By optimizing the ratio of CuSCN in  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film, we have demonstrated that the average PCE of  $ 1\mathrm{mol}\% $  CuSCN doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  based PSCs have improved  $ 21.4\% $  compare to the samples without CuSCN doped. The increased number of hydrogen bonds that between the nitrogen atoms in thiocyanate and ammonium hydrogen atoms also making a progress in the stability of the devices. This work provides a new idea for the application of CuSCN in perovskite solar cells.

# 4. Methods

# 4.1. Solar cell fabrication

The device we fabricated with an architecture of fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) coated glass substrates/compact-  $  TiO_{2}/MAPbI_{3}/CuSCN/Au  $  . The FTO glass (15Ω/sq, YingKou, China, OPVTech New Energy Co.) was successively cleaned with ethanol, deionized water, acetone, ethanol and finally blow dry with nitrogen before used. Sol-gel approach was used to deposited the compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   electron transport layer. Come from this object, a mixture of 2 M HCl (28 μl) and ethanol (2 ml) was added dropwise to a solution of 292 μl of titanium isopropoxide (SIGMA, 99.999%) in ethanol (2 ml) under vigorous stirring. A clear solution could be achieved after stirring the mixture for 2 h, which was then filtered with a 1 ml syringe attached to a 0.22 μm nylon filter, and sealed in the refrigerator. A compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   (c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) was deposited on the cleaned FTO substrate by spin-coating the above solution at 2000 rpm for 50 s, and then annealing on a hot plate at 150 °C for 15 min. The   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer was completely sintered at 500 °C in muffle furnace for 30 min. The film was immersed in a closed container containing   $  TiCl_{4}  $   solution (Aladdin AR, 99.0%) (0.45 M) and dried in a 70 °C oven for 30 min. Deionized water and ethanol were used to wash the substrate, successively. And then the washed substrates were dried with nitrogen and placed in a muffle furnace and annealed at 500 °C for 30 min.

The absorber layer that 1.2 M solution of   $  PbI_{2}  $   and MAI (Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp) with different CuSCN (Aladdin, 99%) concentrations in N,N-dimethylformamide(DMF,SIGMA, 99.8%) was deposited via one-step spin coating at 3000 rpm for 55 s in a nitrogen-filled glovebox. # 5. Characterization
