# Single-Phase Brookite   $  TiO_{2}  $   Nanoparticle Bridge Enhances the Stability of Perovskite Solar Cells

Md. Shahiduzzaman, $ ^{a,c\#*} $  Ashish Kulkarni, $ ^{g\#} $  Sem Visal, $ ^{e} $  LiangLe Wang, $ ^{b} $  Masahiro Nakano, $ ^{b} $  Makoto Karakawa, $ ^{a,b} $  Kohshin Takahashi, $ ^{b} $  Shinjiro Umezu, $ ^{f} $  Atsushi Masuda, $ ^{a} $  Satoru Iwamori, $ ^{c} $  Masao Isomura, $ ^{e} $  Tsutomu Miyasaka, $ ^{g} $  Koji Tomita $ ^{d*} $  and Tetsuya Taima $ ^{a,b*} $

Titanium dioxide (TiO $ _2 $ ) based planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs) suffers from poor long-term stability and hysteretic behaviour in the device characteristic curve. In addition to the bulk exceptional properties of perovskite, the performance and stability are also highly dependent on the conduction band energy, conductivity, and electronic trap states of TiO $ _2 $  compact layer (CL). In this work, single-phase brookite (BK) TiO $ _2 $  nanoparticles (NPs), synthesized via hydrothermal method by water-soluble titanium complex, is incorporated as a bridge between perovskite and TiO $ _2 $  CL. This resulted in uniform large perovskite grain growth with enhanced crystallinity, significant reduction in trap/defect sites and interfacial recombination as revealed by scanning electron microscope (SEM), photoluminescence (PL), and impedance spectroscopy results respectively. The resulting PSCs show highly reproducible power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) up to  $ \sim $ 18.2% (vs.  $ \sim $ 15%) with stable performance of 18% under continuous light illumination (1 Sun) at maximum power point tracking (MPPT) in contrast to only TiO $ _2 $  CL based planar devices. To the best of our knowledge, this is so far the best photo-stability data reported for brookite NPs based PSCs. Based on our present study, at the end; we provide further direction to enhance the stability of planar PSCs.

# Introduction

Sourcing renewable energy is one of the most important problems of this century, and solar energy can be an important contributor to future energy production and demand. Organometal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs), show rapid rise in power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 3.8%  $  ^{1}  $  to certified

25.2%,  $  ^{2}  $  have emerged as the fastest growing technology in history of photovoltaics. With the rapid development of perovskite-based optoelectronics devices, the lifetime (stability) has become new goals besides high efficiency. The stability of PSCs is affected by many aspects such as extrinsic (environmental) and intrinsic factors. Environmental factors such as moisture, oxygen, light and heat are responsible for degradation of the photoactive perovskite component into its non-photoactive component.  $  ^{3}  $  On the other hand, the intrinsic stability of perovskite highly depends on the defects in perovskite itself and at the interface between perovskite and charge transport layers (CTLs).  $  ^{4}  $  Hygroscopicity of the organic cations, under coordinated lead atoms, thermal instability, and ion migration are some of the vital intrinsic features that dominate perovskite instability. The hygroscopicity is related to the environmental factors, which can be resolved by encapsulation.  $  ^{5}  $  The thermal instability can be addressed by developing the perovskite material through compositional engineering and even by developing all-inorganic perovskite materials and inorganic HTMs.  $  ^{6-8}  $  Recently, Miyasaka et al. reported the main issues in design and preparation of the high-quality photovoltaic films of all-inorganic lead-free perovskite and perovskite-like materials for efficiency enhancement.  $  ^{9}  $  The ion migration which is caused by illumination-induced electric field can be hindered or even stopped by passivating the grain boundary, that is, through interfacial modification.  $  ^{10}  $  Moreover, it can be also reduced via engineering the packing density of the crystal lattice by ion substitution.  $  ^{11}  $  Besides this, interfacial modification also mitigates the recombination centers

Furthermore, interfacial modification layers not only improve the contact and efficient charge transfer between perovskite and electron transport layer (ETL)/hole transport layer (HTL), that is, suppress the interfacial recombination but also beneficially change the perovskite morphology further enhancing the photovoltaic performance besides stability.  $  ^{13}  $

Among various device architectures explored, titanium dioxide   $  \left(\mathrm{TiO}_{2}\right)  $   compact layer (CL) based planar structured device attracts attention due to their simplicity in the fabrication process. However, perovskite device with this device architecture suffers from poor long-term stability and hysteretic behavior in the device current density (J)-voltage (V) characteristic curve. Hence, interface modification of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   CL has drawn attention to produce highly efficient PSCs. For instance, researchers have modified the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL through interface engineering with anatase   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs,  $  ^{14}  $   alkali-doping,  $  ^{15}  $   Nb-doping,  $  ^{16}  $   and solvent engineering.  $  ^{17,18}  $   Yang et al.  $  ^{19}  $   reported an efficient PSCs with a PCE of 20.7% by utilizing multifunctional fullerene derivative on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer (as an interfacial modifying layer) resulting in enhanced device stability under ambient conditions. Previously, we have fabricated PSCs by modifying the interface between the perovskite and amorphous compact   $  TiO_{x}  $   layer with fullerene and N-phenyl[60]fulleropyrrolidines (PNP), as a result the efficiency significantly improved.  $  ^{20,21}  $   By replacing PCBM ([6,6]-phenyl   $  C_{61}  $   butyric acid methyl ester) with perylene diimide glass as a   $  TiO_{x}  $   modifier, Adhikari et al.  $  ^{22}  $   improved the efficiency of PSCs by 39%. Li et al.  $  ^{23}  $   presented that using an amine based fullerene as interfacial modifier, performance and light stability of planar PSCs can be greatly improved. Zheng et al.  $  ^{24}  $   have demonstrated that introduction of   $  NH_{4}I  $   layer as a novel interfacial modifier between the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL and perovskite layer, not only enhances the PCE over 18% but also significantly reduces the hysteresis and enhances the perovskite device stability against moisture and UV-light. Yang et al.  $  ^{25}  $   reported that using yttrium-doped tin-dioxide nanosheets as the ETL improved the device efficiency from 16.25% to 17.29% with hysteretic less performance. Tan et al.  $  ^{26}  $   reported that using chlorine-capped   $  TiO_{2}  $   colloidal nanocrystal films as the ETL in PSCs improved their efficiency up to 19.5% along with long term stability. Therefore, the selection of appropriate materials to form a stable contact with perovskites is highly desirable to gain high performance and stability.

On the other hand, incorporation of brookite (BK)   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticles in PSCs have attracted recent attention because the   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer can be obtained at relatively low temperature, contrasting the case of anatase   $  TiO_{2}  $  . Additionally, anatase and BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   polymorphs significantly differ in several crucial parameters, such as the energy position of the conduction band, the distribution and density of the trap states, electron mobility, and the rate constant of the electron transfer rate.  $  ^{[27,28]}  $   Kogo et al. firstly fabricated compact   $  TiO_{x}  $  /mesoporous BK   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based PSCs with the initial efficiency of 14%.  $  ^{[29]}  $   Recently, we

compare the effectiveness of single-phase anatase or brookite and heterophase anatase–brookite or brookite anatase as ETLs in PSCs.  $  ^{30}  $   We found that single phase brookite NPs can solely acts as an effective ETL (without   $  TiO_{2}  $   CL) which exhibited PCE of 14.92% which was comparable to the first work reported by Kogo et al.  $  ^{27}  $   but without any CL. This further suggests that single phase BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   can solely block the holes effectively due to its highly compact nature and internecking particle. In addition to this, the large surface area of BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs facilitates efficient electron injection and subsequent transport, which can balance the electron current density and hole current density in the resultant PSCs and, therefore, can beneficially influences the performance. Even though reports have shown the beneficial effect of BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   on the device performance, investigation on the effect of brookite   $  TiO_{2}  $   on the perovskite thin film growth, and their influence on planar device performance compared to only   $  TiO_{2}  $   CL (without BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs) based planar PSCs and their stability have not been yet demonstrated.

In this study, we employ synthesized single-phase BK TiO $ _2 $  (deposited by low-temperature process) that act as a bridge between perovskite and TiO $ _2 $  CL for improving charge transport in planar PSCs. Pure-phase BK TiO $ _2 $  NPs were obtained by adjusting the pH of the solution to 10 by addition of NH $ _3 $  or ethylenediamine that uses one-step hydrothermal route. $ ^{31} $  The results revealed that a weakly basic environment was essential for the formation of BK TiO $ _2 $  while ammonium ions did not play an important role. From an environmental viewpoint, water-soluble titanium complexes are favorable precursors of titanium-containing functional materials due to their ability to reduce the carbon content per titanium atom. The surface of highly crystalline BK TiO $ _2 $  facilitates interparticle necking and the formation of compact scaffolds. Moreover, highly crystalline BK TiO $ _2 $  facilitates high driving force with a more negative flat-band potential and efficient charge-transfer kinetics and resistance $ ^{28} $  and exhibits higher conductivity and higher stability compared to anatase TiO $ _2 $  phase. $ ^{30} $  The perovskite layer grown on BK-TiO $ _2 $  NPs showed uniform grain growth with large grain size, reduced bulk and interfacial defects, in contrast to perovskite thin film grown on only TiO $ _2 $  CL. As a result, the BK-TiO $ _2 $ /perovskite planar devices exhibited improved device performance up to 18.2% with high reproducibility and enhanced stability under continuous light illumination, light soaking and ambient atmosphere contrasting only TiO $ _2 $  CL based devices. This can be credited to the high conductivity of BK TiO $ _2 $  NPs which decreases the charge accumulation at the interface of BK TiO $ _2 $ /perovskite, resulting in enhanced performance.

# Synthesis of brookite TiO  $  _{2}  $  NPs

Fine powder (5 mmol) of titanium was dissolved in a mixed solution of 30%   $  H_{2}O_{2}  $   (aq.) and 28%   $  NH_{3}  $   (aq.). After adding glycolic acid (7.5 mmol), the solution was dried at 50 °C in order to remove the excess   $  H_{2}O_{2}  $   and   $  NH_{3}  $ , and the Ti–glycolate complex solution (0.25 M) was prepared by adding distilled water. Sodium acetate (45 mmol) was dissolved in the Ti complex solution, and the resultant mixture solution was heat-treated in an autoclave at 200 °C for 5 h. The formed   $  TiO_{2}  $   particles were separated by using centrifugation and resulted in washed twice with distilled water. The   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs were re-dispersed in the distilled water to make a colloidal suspension for BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs thin-film.

# Fabrication of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / BK} $   $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NP ETL films and devices

Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)-patterned glass substrates were cleaned thoroughly and sequentially with commercial detergent in water, deionized water, acetone, and ethanol in a sonication bath. The washed substrates were dried in   $  N_{2}  $   and then treated by UV–ozone treatment for 10 min prior to use. Then,   $  TiO_{2}  $   CL was deposited on the FTO-glass at an optimized 40 nm as follows. As reported by Miyasaka et al.,  $  ^{32}  $   the   $  TiO_{2}  $   CL was deposited via spin coating of precursor solution of 0.15 M   $  Ti(acac)_{2}(OiPr)_{2}  $   in a 2-propanol solution at 2000 rpm for 30 s, whereas the substrate was on a hot plate at 120 °C for 5 min then cooled down to room temperature. Additional two repeated coatings with higher concentration of   $  Ti(acac)_{2}(OiPr)_{2}  $   (0.3 M) solution were spin-coated at the same spinning conditions (2000 rpm, 30 s) and dried at 120 °C for 5 min. Since then the substrate was baked at 450 °C for 30 min in a muffle furnace and then left to cool to room temperature. The as-synthesized, pure-phase BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs colloidal suspension were deposited on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   CL by spin-coating at 2000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 100 °C for 5 min, followed by baked at 180 °C for 60 min in a muffle furnace. The BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs was reserved as a bridge between perovskite and   $  TiO_{2}  $   CL. Perovskite precursor solutions were prepared from 1 M   $  PbI_{2}  $   and 1 M   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   in a mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO (4:1) and stirred at 70 °C for 60 min prior to spin-coating. The as-prepared precursor solution was spin-coated in three steps: with a first step at 0 rpm for 10 s then in second step at 1000 rpm for 10 s then in third step at 5000 rpm for 20 s. In the third step a 500 μL of chlorobenzene solution was dripped at just 12 s before the spin-coating stops.  $  ^{33,34}  $   The samples were directly transferred to a hot plate at 100 °C for 60 min and cooled to room temperature. For the hole-transport layer (HTM), a spiro-OMeTAD precursor solution was prepared by dissolving spiro-OMeTAD (72.3 mg; Merck, Germany), lithium salt (9.1 mg), cobalt (8.7 mg) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (28.8 μL; Sigma-Aldrich) in 1000 μL chlorobenzene by vigorous stirring at 800 rpm at RT for 6 h. As prepared precursor solution was spin-coated on the perovskite layer at 4000 rpm for 30 s and obtained optimized thickness was 250 nm.  $  ^{35,36}  $   To complete the device, 100-nm thick gold (Au)

electrode was thermally evaporated on top of the HTL layer. The optimized thicknesses of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  CL, BK  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs (three-cycle coating), perovskite layer, HTL, and Au layers were measured approximately as 40, 60, 300, 250, and  $ 100\mathrm{nm} $ , respectively.

# Characterization

The surface morphology was characterized by field emission scanning electron microscopy (S-4800, Hitachi High-Tech, Tokyo, Japan). The ultraviolet–visible near-infrared absorption spectra were measured using a spectrophotometer (V-670, Jasco Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). The particulate surface areas of the BK TiO  $  _{2}  $  NPs were determined using Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) analyses (Bell sorp mini II, Microtrac BEL, Osaka, Japan). The TiO  $  _{2}  $  nanocrystals and perovskite films were analyzed by X-ray diffraction (XRD) using an X-ray diffractometer (D8 Discover, Bruker AXS GmbH, Karlsruhe, Germany) with an X-ray tube (Cu Kα radiation,  $  \lambda = 1.5406  $  Å). Charge transport properties with and without BK TiO  $  _{2}  $  NPs-based perovskite films were measured by Photoluminance (PL) (FP-8600, Jasco Corporation, Tokyo, Japan) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) (Agilent E4980A Precision LCR Meter, Tokyo, Japan). The current density versus voltage (J-V) curves of the resulting devices was measured in forward-scan (FS; -0.2 to 1.2 V) and reverse-scan (RS; 1.2 to -0.2 V) modes at a scan speed of 0.05 V/s. The active scan area was 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  under 100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $  light (AM1.5, 1 sun intensity) generated by a solar simulator using a Keithley 2401 digital source meter. The incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) of the resulting devices was determined using a monochromatic xenon arc light system (Bunkoukeiki, SMI-250JA).

# Results and Discussion

The surface area analysis of BK-TiO $ _2 $  nanoparticles were carried out by Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) analysis which revealed that BK TiO $ _2 $  NPs are uniform with a large specific surface area of 83.71 m $ ^2 $  g $ ^{-1} $  as shown in Figure S1a. The device architecture was used to examine the performance and stability of the PSCs as shown in the schematic diagram (Figure 1a). Scanning transmission electron microscope (STEM) was performed to gain understanding into the nanoscale morphology and sizes of brookite TiO $ _2 $  NPs (inset Figure 1a). Figure 1b shows the XRD patterns of the perovskite films deposited on TiO $ _2 $  Cl without and with the BK TiO $ _2 $  layers. The diffraction peaks of the perovskite film with and without BK TiO $ _2 $  NPs were observed at 2θ = 14.12°, 28.40°, and 31.91°. These peaks were assigned to the (110), (220), and (310) crystal planes, respectively, consistent with the major phases of the perovskite. $ ^{37} $  It should be noted that the PbI $ _2 $  peak at 12.65° was absent in both XRD patterns, indicating that all PbI $ _2 $  is consumed. Additionally, the intensity of the diffraction peak at 2θ = 14.12° was slightly stronger and sharper for BK TiO $ _2 $  NPs case than only TiO $ _2 $  CL case, indicating improved perovskite crystallinity. The higher crystallinity of perovskite can be attributed to the under layer effect of BK TiO $ _2 $  NPs (as mean crystal size of 10.85 nm) $ ^{31} $ . From Figure S2a it can be seen that   $  TiO_{2}  $   CL covers the entire FTO substrate and from Figure S2b it is evident that BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs is homogeneously distributed on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   CL. The domain size distribution of the perovskite without and with BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs is shown in Figure S3. From Figure S3, it is clear that the perovskite shows small grain size when deposited on   $  TiO_{2}  $   CL and the grain size improves with the incorporation of BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs. This large-grained, uniform perovskite morphology (in BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs case) influenced few grain boundaries, reducing the number of traps and thereby the charge carrier losses at the grain boundaries.  $  ^{40}  $   The average grain size values are consistent with the values estimated from FWHM (Scherrer's plot) (Table S1). The perovskite film crystallinity and quality directly relate to the efficiency of the photovoltaic performance and the properties of the cell.  $  ^{33,41}  $   In addition, the cross-sectional SEM images of perovskite device without and with BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs are

shown in Figure 1d and Figure 1f respectively. From Figure 1d, it can be seen that the perovskite layer (when deposited on TiO  $  _{2}  $  CL) shows non uniform morphology with random crystal growth which hampers efficient light absorption. On the other hand, with the influence of BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $ , perovskite crystallization occurred in vertical direction as can be seen from Figure 1f. This vertical perovskite crystallization is beneficial for enhanced light absorption, reduced recombination, and efficient charge transport within the bulk and across the interfaces and hence can be effectively enhance the performance of the PSCs  $  ^{42}  $ .

To get further insights into the influence of  $ \mathrm{BKTiO_2} $  in enhancing crystallinity of perovskite and its uniform crystal growth water contact angle measurement was performed and the results are shown in Figure 2. Surface wetting is an important characteristic for the solution coating procedure. Water contact angles were measured at three different points on each film surface, and an average value is reported. From Figure 2b it can be seen that the water contact angle is lower for the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  CL/BK TiO $ _2 $  NPs layer (4.1°) than for the only TiO $ _2 $  CL (13.2°) (Figure 2a). This can be attributed to single phase, and inter-necking of the BK TiO $ _2 $  NPs. Low water contact angle indicates enhanced hydrophilicity of the surface which is favorable for perovskite crystallization and grain growth. These results are in line with XRD (Figure 1b) and SEM (Figure 1 b-f) results, that is, high surface wettability (as in case of BK-TiO $ _2 $  NPs) can beneficially help the perovskite grain growth leading to enhanced grain size, and crystallinity.

To check the effect of BK TiO $ _2 $  NPs layer on the device performance, the devices were fabricated (with and without BK-TiO $ _2 $  interlayer) and photovoltaic properties were measured. Figure 3a depicts current density ( $ J $ ) – voltage ( $ V $ ) characteristic curve and the corresponding device parameters are summarized in Table 1 and the comparison of the FS and RS results are summarized in Table S3. For the TiO $ _2 $  CL case, the short-circuit current density ( $ J_{sc} $ ) and fill factor (FF) were comparatively lower than that of BK-TiO $ _2 $  NPs based devices (Figure S4). Additionally, large hysteretic behavior was observed in the device  $ J $ - $ V $  curve (Figure S4). Long-term stability is one of the most vital complaints for the future commercialization and market acceptance of PSCs. The perovskite device degrades when exposed to moisture, oxygen, light and heat.  $  ^{47}  $   Even though, some good approach such as an encapsulation, PSCs degradation cannot be basically eliminated, mostly due to the intrinsically irreconcilable disputes of the electronic processes, which take place therein.  $  ^{48}  $   In this work, we measured the device stability under continuous light 1 Sun illumination, light soaking and under ambient atmosphere as shown in Figure 6. The best performing devices (with and without BK-  $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs) are exposed to continuous light illumination, that is, at maximum power point tracking (MPPT) and the results are shown in Figure 6a. # Conclusions

Single-phase BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NP and large specific surface area (  $  \sim83.71~m^{2}~g^{-1}  $  ) allows for the surface morphology tuning of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   films. This approach can efficiently improve the photovoltaic performance of PSCs as well as stability. After three layers of BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs on   $  TiO_{2}  $   (thickness 60 nm), the PCE of the fabricated PSCs was maximized at 18.14%, suggesting that 60 nm is the optimal thickness of the BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs layer. The PSCs with the   $  TiO_{2}  $   CL/BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NP layer exhibited excellent photovoltaic performance, photostability under continuous light 1 Sun illumination, that is, at MPPT period than standard PSCs, because the photogenerated electrons were quickly transported to their respective electrodes. These highly conductive NPs were assumed to mainly contribute to the device stability. The outstanding performance of the BK   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs layer ETL-based device was attributed to the enlarged numbers of photogenerated charge carrier sites and reduced agglomeration and trapping at the   $  TiO_{2}  $   interface, as evidenced in the PL and EIS analysis. This work will advance an alternative approach to anatase   $  TiO_{2}  $ -based PSCs applications, and their photovoltaic device performance and preservation stability were enhanced.
