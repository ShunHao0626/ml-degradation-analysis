# Abstract

Thermo-evaporated organic semiconductors with suitable frontier molecular orbitals are potential hole transport materials (HTMs) for perovskite solar cells (PSCs), owing to their high crystallinity associated with high carrier mobility. In this study, we employ typical organic semiconductors, namely pentacene and perylene, as the HTMs in PSCs with an inverted structure. We gradually regulate the crystallographic morphologies of pentacene and perylene thin films by elevating the target substrate temperature from room temperature to 120 °C and 80 °C, respectively. We observe a clear dependence of the photovoltaic performance of inverted PSCs on the substrate temperature. We obtain the optimal power conversion efficiency (PCE) for pentacene and perylene at the substrate temperatures of 40 °C and 60 °C, respectively. The PSCs based on perylene show the best PCE of 13.5%, which is much higher that based on pentacene (5.7%). Perylene-based device shows better stability than PEDOT:PSS-based one. This study substantially deepens our knowledge regarding PSCs based on thermo-evaporated HTMs.

Keywords: Pentacene; Perylene; Hole transport materials; Substrate heating temperature; Crystal growth

# 1. Introduction

In recent years, the growth of the global economy accompanied by energy shortages and environmental pollution has led to the need for rapid development of renewable energy necessary. Thus far, solar cells are one of the most efficient methods of utilizing solar energy, which is the most abundant renewable energy known. Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are known as one of the most promising photovoltaic

technologies and the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has been rapidly increased from the initial value of 3.8%  $  ^{[1]}  $  in 2009 to 23.2%  $  ^{[2]}  $  nowadays. Most PSCs can be divided into the mesoscopic structure  $  ^{[3-6]}  $  and planar structure  $  ^{[7-9]}  $  types, depending on the layer morphology of perovskite materials. Moreover, the planar structure PSCs can be divided into the traditional n-i-p structure  $  ^{[10, 11]}  $  and inverted p-i-n structure  $  ^{[12-14]}  $  types. In particular, inverted planar PSCs usually have a smaller hysteresis effect in comparison to PSCs with other structures. Thus, they have been drawing more and more attention. In inverted structure PSCs, poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)-poly(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) is often used as the hole transport material (HTM)  $  ^{[15-17]}  $ . However, the acidic nature of PEDOT:PSS causes the corrosion of the perovskite layer during the operating period. Therefore, in order to solve this problem, other HTMs that are more stable during the processing of the perovskite layer are desirable as alternatives to PEDOT:PSS in PSCs with an inverted planar structure.

In addition to porphyrins- and phthalocyanines-based small molecules being employed as potential HTMs for PSCs due to their good absorption and energy transfer abilities   $  [18-23]  $  , typical organic semiconductors, such as pentacene and perylene, have been investigated extensively in organic photovoltaics, owing to their high hole mobility and ease of synthesis. Additionally, pentacene and perylene can be prepared through thermal evaporation with low solubility in organic solvents. It has been reported that crystalline perylene thin film formed by thermal vapor deposition method can ensure a high hole mobility   $  [24-26]  $  . Whereas solution-processed amorphous

3

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

organic HTMs, for instance, spiro-OMeTAD and conductive polymers, for which, additives of charged molecules or ions are required to achieve sufficient hole conductivity   $  [27-30]  $  . These features of pentacene and perylene strongly suggest their potential as HTMs in PSCs. In this study, we prepared pentacene and perylene films on an indium tin oxide (ITO) substrate through thermal evaporation. Their crystallographic morphologies were monitored by substrate heating at different temperatures. We optimized the perovskite crystallization on the HTM layer and obtained the PCE of the inverted planar structure PSCs by varying the substrate temperature. We obtained the highest PCE when the substrate temperature was 40 °C and 60 °C, for pentacene and perylene, respectively. The highest PCE was obtained for the PSCs based on perylene as the HTM, with a value of 13.5% and a substrate temperature of 60 °C. Importantly, PSCs based on perylene HTM give much better PCE and stability as compared to those based on PEDOT:PSS reference, suggesting a great potential of perylene in the future application.

# 3. Results and Discussion

It has been known that the crystal growth of organic semiconductors by thermo-evaporation depends on the surface temperature of the target substrates. In order to observe the effect of the substrate heating temperature on the crystalline structures of pentacene and perylene, we prepared the pentacene thin films on ITO substrates with different temperatures, such as RT, 40 °C, 80 °C, and 120 °C, and prepared perylene thin films on ITO substrates with different temperatures of RT, 40 °C, 60 °C, and 80 °C. Figure 1 shows the XRD patterns of pentacene and perylene films prepared by the thermo-evaporation of these materials on the target ITO substrate preheated to different temperatures. Additionally, we measured the XRD pattern of the bare ITO as a reference of the XRD results. It was obvious that there were three additional diffraction peaks in the XRD patterns of pentacene films at 2θ near 5°, 11°, and 17°, corresponding to the crystallite surface of (001), (002), and (003), respectively [31]. Similarly, we could easily identify two identical diffraction peaks of perylene films at 2θ near 8.5° and 17.5°, corresponding to the (001) and (002) crystallite surfaces, respectively.

Although similar diffraction peaks could be observed in the ITO substrates preheated at different temperatures for both pentacene and perylene, we realized that there were tiny diffraction peak shifts on the XRD patterns of these films. Additionally, it is noteworthy, under preheating temperature of  $ 40^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , the width of the XRD peaks of pentacene film is significantly narrower than that under other heating conditions. Most importantly, the PSC device based on perylene exhibits much better stability compared to that based on PEDOT:PSS, i.e., even after 15 days aging under operation condition of the PSCs, the PCE of perylene-based PSC remains  $ 90\% $  of its initial value. The inferior stability of PEDOT:PSS-based device is most likely caused by the corrosion of the perovskite layer and the ITO substrates due to the acidic nature of PEDOT:PSS [34]. The excellent stability of perylene-based PSCs is a big plus for future real-world applications.

# 4. Conclusion

We systematically investigated the growth of both pentacene and perylene thin films on preheated ITO substrates and applied these films as HTLs to PSCs with an inverted architecture. We observed the clear dependence of pentacene and perylene film morphologies and resultant PSC performances on preheating temperatures. The preheating temperatures of 40 °C and 60 °C provided the best photovoltaic performances for PSCs based on pentacene and perylene, respectively. PSCs using perylene as HTM generally showed much higher efficiencies than those using pentacene. This could be interpreted with the relatively deeper HOMO level of perylene than that of pentacene, as well as more uniform grain growth of perylene than that of pentacene under their respective optimal conditions. The highest photovoltaic performance was obtained when depositing 50 nm thickness of perylene
