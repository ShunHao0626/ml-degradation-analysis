# Crystallization Manipulation and Morphology Evolution for Highly Efficient Perovskite Solar Cell Fabrication via Hydration Water Induced Intermediate Phase Formation under Heat Assisted Spin-Coating

Xianyong Zhou  $  ^{ab\dagger}  $  , Yong Zhang  $  ^{a\dagger}  $  , Weiguang Kong  $  ^{a}  $  , Manman Hu  $  ^{a}  $  , Luozheng Zhang  $  ^{a}  $  , Chang Liu  $  ^{a}  $  , Xiangnan Li  $  ^{a}  $  , Chunyue Pan  $  ^{b*}  $  , Guipeng Yu  $  ^{b}  $  , Chun Cheng  $  ^{a*}  $  , and Baomin Xu  $  ^{a*}  $

A delicate control of crystallization and morphology of perovskite absorber is critical to obtain high performance hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, we have developed a novel crystallization strategy which involves hydration water induced intermediate phase formation under heat assisted spin-coating processing (HASP) to manipulate the morphology and grain size of perovskite films, and thus can avoid the use of toxic and volatile antisolvents used in conventional one-step solution process to make PSCs. During HASP, impressive film morphological evolution occurs with the formation of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3\cdot \times \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  perovskite intermediate phases. With ingenious control of the crystallization kinetics, highly crystalline perovskite films with less defect states and high carrier lifetimes are obtained. The corresponding PSCs exhibit a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 19.12\% $  with stabilized power output efficiency of  $ 18.89\% $  at the maximum power point, which is  $ 70\% $  enhancement compared to that of the conventional one-step process based PSCs. Furthermore, the PSC fabricated by the HASP method achieved a PCE of  $ 15.47\% $  with an active area of  $ 1.2~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ . Moreover, the flexible PSCs fabricated by the HASP method achieved a record efficiency of  $ 14.87\% $  with negligible hysteresis for PEDOT:PSS-based flexible inverted PSCs. Our work opens up a new avenue for morphology and crystallization control through hydration water induced intermediate phase formation under HASP, which paves the way toward further enhancing the device performance of perovskite solar cells.

# 1 Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have received broad interest in both the academic and industrial sectors in the past eight years due to the low fabrication cost, high efficiency and ease of processibility.  $  ^{1-5}  $   In terms of photovoltaic performance, the highest certified power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of perovskite solar cells have risen to 22.1% in single-junction devices.  $  ^{6}  $   The dramatic increase of efficiency has been mainly attributed to the perfecting of the perovskite film quality to be pin-hole free,  $  ^{7-8}  $   uniform,  $  ^{9,10}  $   and smooth  $  ^{11,12}  $   with larger crystal grain.  $  ^{13,14}  $   Engineering the dynamic crystallization of perovskite from precursors is considered to be the most crucial factor for perovskite film quality, which raises a variety of strategies to govern the nucleation and crystal growth of the perovskite films. Organic-cation displacement, antisolvent engineering, and intramolecular exchange have been developed to yield the

desired morphologies of the perovskite.  $  ^{15-17}  $   Among these strategies, antisolvent engineering is a representative of the "one-step" solution process that turns out to be an effective method favoring good-performing PSCs.  $  ^{18-25}  $   However, the antisolvent is usually dropped on the center of the substrate, creating a radial gradient over-saturation and spatially inhomogeneous nucleation of the perovskite,  $  ^{26}  $   which is unfavorable for large-area solar cell fabrication and device reproducibility. In addition, the most commonly used antisolvents, such as chlorobenzene (CB) and toluene are toxic and harmful to the environment and operators in manufacturing and scientists in the lab, hampering PSCs' industrial application. For the scalable fabrication of PSCs, antisolvent engineering is currently far from perfect.  $  ^{27,28}  $   Hence it is crucial to develop alternative scalable processes to prepare perovskite films. Generally, preparation of perovskite precursor solutions is conducted in an anhydrous environment. However, recent studies discovered that moisture could assist the perovskite grain growth, leading to a better device performance.  $  ^{29-35}  $   Wu et al. reported the use of   $  H_{2}O  $   as an additive in   $  PbI_{2}/DMF  $   solution to prepare a full-coverage, good-quality   $  PbI_{2}  $   film on PEDOT:PSS coated ITO substrate. A high-quality   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   film was then formed by adding MAI/IPA on the top of   $  PbI_{2}  $   using a two-step spin coating method. Unfortunately, the first attempt of adding liquid water in perovskite precursor solution which was prepared with methylammonium iodide (MAI) and lead chloride at a molar ratio of 3:1 and dissolved in dimethylformamide (DMF) did not boost the one-step processed PSCs' performance.  $  ^{36}  $   These seemingly inconsistent experimental results implied that the role of water molecule in the perovskite crystallization process still remains unclear. Recently, lead acetate (  $  PbAc_{2}  $  ) has been introduced as a new budget promising lead source for highly efficient PSCs without using antisolvents.  $  ^{34,37}  $   For instance, Zhang et al have reported using methylamine iodide (MAI) and lead acetate at a molar ratio of 3:1 dissolved in DMF solvent to fabricate smooth   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   thin films by one-step solution process.  $  ^{13}  $   Zhu et al have further reported that the incorporation of methylamine bromide as an additive in the lead acetate based   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite precursor solution enabled to controllable ultrasmooth films, and the device efficiency exceeding 18%.  $  ^{37}  $   The results of the above studies are based on anhydrous lead acetate as lead source. Recently, Zheng et al have reported lead acetate trihydrate (  $  PbAc_{2}\bullet3H_{2}O  $  ) as a new lead source to fabricate   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   films, but the device efficiency is only 9.5%.  $  ^{34}  $   With decreasing the contents of hydration water in the perovskite precursor solutions, the PCEs of devices increased gradually to 12.3%. When the precursor solution contains only anhydrous lead acetate, the PCE of devices is only 9.3%.  $  ^{34}  $   It implies that appropriate contents of hydration water is beneficial to improve the performance of PSCs. However, the specific mechanism of hydration water is not very clear in the one-step perovskite crystallization process. It is crucial to further understand the underlying mechanism and adapt a new crystallization strategy to improve the performance of PSCs.

In this paper, we have developed a novel crystallization strategy which involves hydration water induced intermediate phase formation under heat assisted spin-coating process (HASP) to manipulate the morphology and grain size of perovskite films, and thus high performance PSC devices can be obtained using one-step perovskite crystallization process with hydrous lead acetate as lead source. Using the HASP method to fabricate perovskite precursor films with different contents of hydration water in the precursor solutions, impressive film morphological evolution occurs with the formation of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3\cdot \mathrm{xH}_2\mathrm{O} $  perovskite intermediate phases and suitable evaporation state of the by-products  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $ , MA-Ac and DMF, which induces rapid orderly crystallization at an appropriate temperature. We observed that the crystallization kinetics is obviously changed due to the formation of perovskite intermediate phases, yielding highly crystalline perovskite films with less defect states and longer carrier lifetimes. The perovskite solar cells with the reconstructed films exhibit the highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to  $ 19.12\% $  under 1 sun AM 1.5G irradiance, which is  $ 70\% $  enhancement compared to that of the conventional one-step process based PSCs. Also, the perovskite solar cells with HASP show negligible hysteresis behavior and a stabilized power output efficiency of  $ 18.89\% $  obtained at the maximum power point. In addition, we have

fabricated large area PSCs by the HASP method, the efficiency of the cell device with  $ 1.2\mathrm{cm}^2 $  active area has achieved  $ 15.47\% $ . Hence, the present study demonstrates up-scaling potential for the assembly of modules in solar cells. Moreover, the flexible PSCs fabricated by the HASP method have achieved a record efficiency of  $ 14.87\% $  with negligible hysteresis reported so far for PEDOT:PSS-based flexible inverted PSCs, and remarkable power per-weight of  $ 25.20\mathrm{Wg^{-1}} $ . At the same time, we tested the stability of the prepared rigid devices. For the HASP-treated rigid cells, we can see that over  $ 80\% $  of the initial PCEs remained after about 20 days in air ( $ 30\% $  humidity) without encapsulation, and after 60 days when vacuum sealed.

# 2 Results and discussion

Hydration water induced intermediate phase formation under heat assisted spin-coating process (HASP). The proposed strategy includes three major steps as shown in Figure 1a. First, the perovskite precursor solution containing 3MAI:  $  PbAc_{2}\cdot3H_{2}O  $  in DMF was prepared and kept at different temperatures from 70 to 95 °C with an interval of 5 °C, respectively. Secondly, the hot precursor solution was spin-coated onto the substrate which was pre-heated at 90 °C (according to the experiment of the substrate temperature we have explored before, it is ideal that it is 90°C, and when the precursor solution temperature continues to rise to 100 to 120 °C, due to the too fast evaporation state of  $  H_{2}O  $ , MA·Ac and DMF, the quality of the perovskite film gets worse.) to form a light brown intermediate complex film. Finally, the complex film was annealed at 90 °C for 10 min to obtain controllable ultrasmooth and large-grain perovskite layer. Compared to the conventional one-step process (abbreviated as CP in this paper, where the precursor solution and substrate were maintained at room temperature, see details in Experimental Section), significant changes in the film color were observed during the HASP, typically, as shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information). Before thermal annealing, the homogeneous intermediate complex films prepared by the HASP method gradually show light brown color with the precursor solution temperature increasing from 80 °C to 85 °C. When the precursor solution temperature reaches 95 °C, the intermediate complex films prepared by the HASP method become a non-uniform light brown film before thermal annealing. After annealing at 90 °C for 10 min, the homogeneous intermediate complex films are transformed into smooth, compact and highly crystalline perovskite layer with bright dark brown color after gradually losing remnant water molecules, but the non-uniform intermediate complex films are converted into rough and fogged low-quality films. In contrast, the intermediate complex films prepared by the CP method show a light yellow color before thermal annealing, and then are transformed into the perovskite layer with dark brown color after thermal annealing. The film formation mechanism is closely related to the contents of hydration water in lead acetate. When MAI and  $  PbAc_{2}\cdot xH_{2}O  $  (where x≠0) are mixed at molar ratio 3:1, the white powder immediately turns into yellow, as shown in Figure 1b, while no color change was observed for case of x = 0. The formation of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}\bullet xH_{2}O  $   intermediate complex was further confirmed from X-ray powder diffraction (XRD) patterns (Figure 1c and 1d). The conversion rate of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}\bullet xH_{2}O  $   complex was monitored by UV-vis spectra in Figure S3 (Supporting Information). We characterized the spin-coated films before and after thermal annealing, respectively. Figure 1c depicts the XRD patterns of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}\bullet xH_{2}O  $   intermediate phase film with similar thickness before thermal annealing. The films showed peaks at both 14.1° and 28.6°, corresponding to the (110) and (220) faces of the perovskite tetragonal crystal as illustrated in previous work.  $  ^{33}  $   The appearance of perovskite peaks suggests that the precursors were directly converted into the perovskite tetragonal structure. However, as compared with the films prepared by the anhydrous lead acetate through the CP method, the films with hydration water prepared by CP method show three new peaks at 11.83°, 12.51° and 26.6° respectively, and the films with hydration water fabricated by HASP method have three new peaks at 8.8°, 10.83° and 26.6° respectively. This indicates that the presence of hydration water induces the crystallographic orientation of the perovskite, and the crystal orientation of the film prepared by HASP is different from that of the CP-prepared film. The appearance of the peaks at 26.6° and lower diffraction angels are attributed to the formation of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}\bullet xH_{2}O  $   intermediate phase. All these peaks disappeared after conversion into the perovskite films with annealing on hot plate (Figure 1d). Upon annealing at 90 °C for 10 min, the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}\bullet xH_{2}O  $   intermediate phase transforms into the smooth, compact and highly crystalline perovskite layer by losing remnant water molecules. Figure 1d depicts the XRD patterns of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}\bullet xH_{2}O  $   intermediate phase film with similar thickness after thermal annealing. The XRD peaks after thermal annealing for the perovskite film prepared from 3MAI:  $  PbAc_{2}\bullet3H_{2}O  $   precursor solution by the HASP method show the strongest and sharpest diffraction peak from the (110) plane, whereas the perovskite film from CP method exhibits the weakest XRD intensity. Since all the measurements are performed under the same parameters and all the perovskite films are of similar thickness, the results from XRD further confirm the improvement of crystallinity for the perovskite film prepared with 3MAI:  $  PbAc_{2}\bullet3H_{2}O  $   precursor solution by the HASP method. These results clearly show that hydration water induced perovskite intermediate phase formation under HASP condition is an effective crystallization strategy to produce less

defect and highly crystalline perovskite film. It is worth to mention that the intermediate phase obtained by HASP in the presence of hydration water is unprecedented in literature. Thus, our work opens up a novel mechanism to convert the precursor films into perovskite.

Based on the above results, we propose a thin film growth mechanism as shown in Figure 1e. Six species including Pb $  ^{2+}  $ , Ac $  ^{-}  $ , Ma $  ^{+}  $ , I $  ^{-}  $ , H $  _{2}  $ O, DMF coexisted the perovskite precursor solution. When the precursor solution was pre-heated at 80-85 °C, the hot precursor solution was spin-coated onto the substrate which was pre-heated at 90 °C to form a light brown homogenous intermediate complex film (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The homogenous intermediate complex film mainly including CH $  _{3}  $ NH $  _{3}  $ PbI $  _{3}  $ •xH $  _{2}  $ O, MA·Ac and residual DMF was annealed at 90 °C to obtain controllable ultrasmooth and large-grain perovskite layer. Here, a suitable evaporation state of H $  _{2}  $ O, MA·Ac and DMF at appropriate temperature which induced a grain growth and suppressed nucleation process is speculated to be responsible for forming the large-grain and compact perovskite film at that specified temperature. As the temperature of the precursor solution further increased, the quality of the perovskite film gets worse, which may be due to the too fast solvent evaporation and the bad diffusion state of H $  _{2}  $ O, MA·Ac and DMF. Since the boiling point of water and the decomposition temperature of MA·Ac (85-97 °C) are lower than that of DMF (bp 153 °C), when the temperature of the precursor solvents is at a higher level (>90 °C), the above-mentioned preferred evaporation state is broken. The rates at which water runs out increase, associating with the decompose of the MA·Ac, the remaining DMF and by-product N-methylacetamide (bp 204 °C) will govern the crystallization of perovskite and result in poor film quality. At a lower temperature (<70 °C), the evaporation of H $  _{2}  $ O, MA·Ac and DMF kept at a slow rate, the excessive remaining H $  _{2}  $ O, DMF and MA·Ac will hinder the crystallization of perovskite and result in poor film quality containing numerous pinholes and small-grain.

# 3 Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated that the crystallization strategy of the hydration water induced intermediate phase formation under HASP is an effective method to manipulate crystallization and control the morphology of the perovskite absorber for highly efficient perovskite solar cells. This novel method is effective to fabricate the perovskite thin film with pure and stable crystal phase at a relatively low annealing temperature, yielding uniform and compact perovskite thin films with excellent optoelectronic properties. Ultimately, the best PCE of 19.12% was achieved in the inverted planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells based on the HASP fabrication method, and a stabilized output efficiency of 18.89% was obtained at the MPP, which is 70% enhancement compared to that of the conventional one-step process based PSCs. Moreover, PCE of as high as 15.47% was achieved for the final PSCs with an active area of 1.2 cm² in single-junction devices. The optimized device showed negligible J-V hysteresis. In addition, the flexible PSCs fabricated by the HASP method achieved a record efficiency of 14.87% with negligible hysteresis reported so far for PEDOT:PSS-based flexible inverted PSCs, and remarkable power per-weight of 25.20 W g⁻¹. Furthermore, the hydration water presence together with HASP simplifies the device fabrication steps and avoids the use of toxic antisolvent, which opens a new approach to the scalable fabrication of PSCs in the near future. It is worth to mention that the intermediate phases obtained by HASP together with the presence of hydration water is unprecedented in literature. Thus, our work opens up a novel mechanism to convert the precursor films into perovskite, and a new avenue for morphology and large-grain control, which paves the way toward further enhancing the device performances of perovskite solar cells.
