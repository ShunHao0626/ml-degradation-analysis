# Surfactant Sodium Dodecyl Benzene Sulfonate Improves the Efficiency and Stability of Air-Processed Perovskite Solar Cells with Negligible Hysteresis

Wenyuan Zhang, Lang He, Dongyan Tang, and Xin Li*

The device performance of organic-inorganic hybrid halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is highly dependent on the quality of perovskite layer. Herein, a multifunctional chemical additive strategy is reported to simultaneously improve the efficiency and stability of fully air-processed PSCs. The planner methylammonium lead trihalide  $ (\mathrm{MAPbI}_3) $ -based PSCs incorporating sodium dodecyl benzene sulfonate (SDBS) exhibit a champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 19.20\% $  and negligible hysteresis, which is one of the top efficiencies of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based PSCs made in air. The increased efficiency is due to the reduction of defects and inhibition of ion migration in the perovskite films. Furthermore, the enhancement of device performance and stability can also be ascribed to highly preferred and efficient perovskite crystals protecting the perovskite films from humidity. The corresponding unencapsulated device retains  $ 92.34\% $  of its initial efficiency after 90 days ( $ >2100\mathrm{h} $ ) storage in air and maintains  $ 85.20\% $  of its original PCE after being exposed to  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 27\mathrm{h} $ . The results indicate that SDBS is a promising chemical additive to enhance the performance of air-processed PSCs for future applications.

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been projected to be promising candidates in the field of photovoltaics. In the last few years, the remarkable leap of power conversion efficiency (PCE), surging from 3.8% to 25.2%, has validated its promise.  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   However, further enhancing the device efficiency toward the theoretical limit is getting harder and harder.  $  ^{[4]}  $   Apart from PCE, the issues of stability and cost are two main barriers for commercialization.  $  ^{[5-7]}  $

Among various passivation techniques, additive engineering is an effective way to passivate perovskite materials. A wide range of additives, including polymers, zwitterions, fullerene and its derivatives, metal halide salts, organic halide salts, inorganic acids, water, and caffeine, have been introduced to perovskite precursor solution.  $  ^{[9,25-29]}  $   On the contrary, the morphology, crystallization behavior, and crystal structure of perovskite film can significantly affect the device performance.  $  ^{[30]}  $   Chemical additive-assisted strategies are also considered to be an effective solution to controlling crystallinity of perovskite film.  $  ^{[31]}  $   Despite the performance improvement of perovskite film, multifunctional additives are rarely reported so far.

Apart from light harvesting, perovskite layer largely determines charge carrier diffusion due to the nature of ambipolar semiconductor.  $  ^{[8,9]}  $   In general, polycrystalline perovskite film with low crystallization energy barrier can be easily obtained by solution-processed approaches.  $  ^{[10]}  $   Similar to other soft ionic solids, polycrystalline perovskite film features a wide variety of defects.  $  ^{[11]}  $   Such inevitable defects can result in energy loss owing to nonradiative charge recombination, and thus reduce device performance and stability.  $  ^{[12,13]}  $   For example, the defects associated with uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   can induce the deep-level traps and low built-in potential, leading to inefficient charge extraction and charge recombination.  $  ^{[14-17]}  $   Furthermore, some crystal defects can drive ion or vacancy to easily migrate in perovskite structures.  $  ^{[18]}  $   Recent

Although all-inorganic PSCs and lower dimensional perovskite structures exhibit excellent water resistance and superior stability, these strategies are at the expense of the efficiency of PSCs.  $  ^{[34-38]}  $   Introduction of a thin buffer interlayer between the perovskite layer and the HTL is another promising way to prevent moisture ingress.  $  ^{[39,40]}  $   A limitation of this method is high-cost and complex fabrication processes, which hamper the future practical applications of PSCs. To achieve future industrialization of PSCs, exploring a fabrication technology that is less sensitive to the processing environment is required.  $  ^{[41]}  $   All air-processed PSCs without any conditional control are of great significance.  $  ^{[42-50]}  $   However, it is often nontrivial to transplant technique innovations involved in inert atmosphere to the device fabrication in ambient air. Thus, there is still great room to push the performance of fully air-processed PSCs.

Herein, inspiration from structure-directing effect, we first show that, by using sodium dodecyl benzene sulfonate (SDBS) as a multifunctional chemical additive, it is likely to obtain fully air-processed PSCs with an optimal PCE of 19.20% and negligible hysteresis without adding complex preparation processes. As an anionic surfactant, SDBS has abundant functional groups, is earth abundant, and has a low cost of production. Specifically, we select SDBS based on fourfold. First, the introduction of SDBS can slow down the crystallization rate, which favors highly preferred crystal structure. Second,   $  SO_{3}^{-}  $  could coordinate with the uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $ , not only reducing induced deep-level traps and carrier recombination, but also increasing built-in potential. Third, the interaction between sodium ion (  $  Na^{+}  $  ) and   $  I^{-}  $  could greatly prevent iodine migration, which is beneficial for reducing hysteresis and improving stability devices. Fourth, the long alkyl chains are capable of passivating grain boundaries (GBs) and increasing the hydrophobicity of perovskite films, avoiding air-processed perovskite films from moisture damage during the preparation process, thereby blocking the external interference and improving device stability. The corresponding unencapsulated device retains 92.34% of its initial efficiency after 90 days (>2100 h) storage in air and maintains 85.20% of its original PCE after exposing to 85 °C for 27 h.

# 2. Results and Discussion

As we all know, the quality of perovskite films has great influence on the PSCs performance. In this work, the SDBS was doped into perovskite precursor solution (see Supporting Information for details), and then the perovskite films were prepared by one-step spin-coating strategy using ethyl acetate green antisolvent, as shown in Figure 2a. As previously reported in the literature, the  $ \mathrm{SO}_3^- $  in SDBS can coordinate with the  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  due to the lone electron pair on the oxygen (O) atom of the  $ \mathrm{SO}_3^- $  group and empty electron orbits of  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $ . To verify the effect of SDBS on the morphology of perovskite film, scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and atomic force microscopy (AFM) observations of pristine MAPbI $ _3 $  and MAPbI $ _3 $  with SDBS films (defined as MAPbI $ _3 $ -SDBS films) are performed. According to the top surface SEM images shown in Figure 2c,d, there are conspicuous differences between the different perovskite films. Even though both films completely cover the SnO $ _2 $  substrate, the MAPbI $ _3 $ -SDBS films become more uniform and smooth comparing to the MAPbI $ _3 $  film. As shown in Figure S2, Supporting Information, the smaller root-mean-square roughness ( $ R_q $ ) value of MAPbI $ _3 $ -SDBS film compared with MAPbI $ _3 $  film further determines that SDBS can indeed produce a smoother perovskite film. Low roughness can effectively reduce the surface defects of perovskite films and facilitate better contact with HTL, which is positive for device performance. $ ^{[52]} $  Moreover, it can be seen from the SEM images that the grain size increases significantly after introducing SDBS; larger grain size is generally beneficial for all properties because it is generally accompanied by the reduction of GBs, and is expected to reduce the density of defects. $ ^{[53]} $

To confirm the interaction between  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and SDBS, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) analyses were performed. As shown in Figure S4, Supporting Information, the S=O stretching vibration peak in sulfonic group of SDBS film appeared at  $ 1041.85\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ . Simultaneously, it is worth noting that the I 3d peaks are also shifted according to Figure 4b. Compared with the results for the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film, the I  $ 3\mathrm{d}_{3/2} $  peak of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  with SDBS film shifted from 630.31 to  $ 629.75\mathrm{eV} $ , and the I  $ 3\mathrm{d}_{5/2} $  peak shifted from 618.76 to  $ 617.25\mathrm{eV} $ .[63] This peak shifting was caused by the increase in the electron cloud density of  $ \mathrm{I}^- $  ions interacting with  $ \mathrm{Na}^+ $  ions, which indicated the interaction between  $ \mathrm{Na}^+ $  with  $ \mathrm{I}^- $ . This may inhibit  $ \mathrm{I}^- $  migration, further reducing hysteresis and improving the stability of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  with SDBS film.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we demonstrate an additive engineering strategy to passivate defects, and improve crystallinity and the hydrophobicity of perovskite films by introducing SDBS additives. The   $  SO_{3}^{-}  $  in SDBS can coordinate with the   $  Pb^{2+}  $ , which can not only slow down the crystallization rate, promoting the preferential crystal

orientation growth of MAPbI $ _3 $ , but also effectively passivate the uncoordinated Pb $ ^{2+} $ , thereby reducing the defect density. Simultaneously, the interaction between Na $ ^+ $  and I $ ^- $  could significantly prevent iodine migration, which is beneficial for reducing hysteresis and improving stability devices. Moreover, the long alkyl chains of the SDBS are capable of passivating GBs and increasing the hydrophobicity of perovskite films, thereby blocking the external interference. As a result, the champion device with the architecture of FTO/SnO $ _2 $ /MAPbI $ _3 $ -SDBS/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au presents a large  $ V_{oc} $  of 1.10 V, and yields a champion PCE of 19.20% with suppressed hysteresis, which is an increase of 9.87% compared with that of the MAPbI $ _3 $  device (17.92%). Furthermore, the optimized PSCs exhibit excellent long-term air stability and thermal stability; unencapsulated device maintains 92.34% of original PCE after 90 days (>2100 h) and maintains 85.20% of initial value after exposing to 85 °C for 27 h. This demonstrated strategy is expected to be of great benefit to the design and the development stable and efficient PSC devices in open air.
