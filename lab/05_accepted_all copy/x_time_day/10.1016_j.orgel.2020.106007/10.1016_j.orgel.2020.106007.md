# Natural Passivation of the Perovskite Layer by Oxygen in Ambient air to Improve the Efficiency and Stability of Perovskite Solar Cells Simultaneously

Zhenlong Zhang  $  ^{a}  $ , Yanyan Liu  $  ^{a}  $ , Peiyu Zhang  $  ^{a,*}  $ , Yanli Mao  $  ^{a,b,*}  $

$  ^{a}  $   School of Physics and Electronics, Henan University, Kaifeng 475004, China

$  ^{b}  $   Institute of Micro/Nano Photonic Materials and Applications, Henan University

*Corresponding author: +86-371-23881602; E-mail:memszhang@yahoo.com (P.Y. Zhang); ylmao1@163.com (Y.L. Mao)

Abstract: For the hybrid organic-inorganic halide perovskite (HHPs) material, oxygen passivation could enhance the photoluminescence intensity, while it also deteriorates the stability of HHPs film or device. It is a challenge how to use oxygen passivation to improve the efficiency and stability of the HHPs solar cells simultaneously. Here we reported a novel and simple method for natural passivation of the perovskite layer with oxygen in ambient air by exclusion of light illumination. The HHPs solar cells were fabricated by deposition of hole transport layer after oxygen passivation. By optimizing the passivation conditions, the power conversion efficiency of the solar cells increases to  $ 20.1\% $  from  $ 17.3\% $  for those without passivation. Meanwhile, the stability of the HHPs solar cells with passivation was improved. This work is the first report applying oxygen passivation to improve the efficiency and stability of HHPs solar cells simultaneously.

Key Words: perovskite solar cells, oxygen passivation, efficiency, stability

# 1. Introduction

Hybrid organic-inorganic halide perovskite (HHPs) solar cells have been paid more attentions from the scientific community due to their superior properties, such as larger optical absorption coefficient, longer carrier diffusion length, and tunable direct band gap [1-4]. To further improve the efficiency and stability of the HHPs solar cells, Some researchers have proposed passivation strategies to reduce the surface defects, such as the introduction of metal ions [6], functional molecules [7, 8], and halide ions [9]. Liu et al. applied an amino-containing polyhedral additive  $ (\mathrm{NH}_2\text{-POSS}) $  to modify the surface of the perovskite film, which induced the efficiency increasing to  $ 20.5\% $  from  $ 18.1\% $ . Moreover, the passivation of the perovskite film significantly improves the stability of the HHPs solar cells [10].

In addition to the abovementioned strategies, the oxygen in ambient air can also be used to passivate the surface defects of the perovskite layer. Some researchers have reported that oxygen passivation enhances the photoluminescence (PL) intensity of the perovskite due to the decrease of the nonradiative recombination [11-14]. Although the positive effect of oxygen on the properties of the perovskite material, there are few reports on the fabrication of HHPs solar cells by applying oxygen passivation of the perovskite layer. Aristidou et al. reported that oxygen could deteriorate the stability of the HHPs solar cells [15], which means the negative effect of oxygen on the stability of PSCs. While some researchers reported that oxygen passivation could enhance the stability of all-inorganic halide perovskite (IHPs) solar cells [16, 17], which indicates the positive effect of oxygen on the stability of IHPs solar cells. Therefore it is a challenge how to use oxygen passivation to improve the efficiency and stability of the HHPs solar cells simultaneously. The degradation of the HHPs material can be observed under oxygen and light illumination. It is believed that the degradation is due to the reaction of oxygen molecule and photogenerated electron to decompose the organic moieties [18-20]. If the HHPs layer expose to oxygen by exclusion of light illumination, the halide vacancies could be passivated by oxygen atoms, meanwhile the reaction between oxygen molecule and photogenerated electron might be prevented [21], which will lead to the improvement of efficiency and stability for the HHPs solar cells simultaneously.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1 Materials

N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.99%), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.99%), chlorobenzene (CB, 99.99%) and isopropanol (IPA, 99.5%) were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich. The   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloid solution, bis(trifluoromethane) sulfonamide lithium salt (Li-TFSI), and 4-tertbutylpyridine (t-BP) were from Alfa Aesar. Methylammonium chloride (MACl), Formamidinium iodide (FAI), Methylammonium bromide (MABr), CsCl (beads, 99.99%), CsI (99.99%), Formarmidinium bromide (FABr), Spiro-OMeTAD, and   $  PbI_{2}  $   (beads, 99.99%) were obtained from Xi'an Polymer light Technology Corp.

# 2.2 Preparation of Perovskite Precursor Solution

The perovskite precursor was obtained with the method presented in the literature [22]. In brief,   $  PbI_{2}/CsCl  $   solution was formed by dissolving 1293 mg   $  PbI_{2}  $   beads and 20.7 mg CsCl beads in 1.76 ml DMF and 0.24 ml DMSO, and stirring at 80°C overnight. The perovskite seed solution was obtained by mixing   $  PbI_{2}  $   beads (1200 mg),   $  PbBr_{2}  $   powder (73.5 mg), FAI powder (378.4 mg), MABr powder (33.6 mg), and CsI powder (78 mg) in 2 ml mixture solvent (DMF:DMSO=4:1, v/v) and stirring at 60°C for 30 min. The   $  PbI_{2}/CsCl  $   seed mixture solution was prepared by adding 0.13 ml perovskite seed solution into the 1ml   $  PbI_{2}/CsCl  $   solution. The MACl/FAI/MABr solution was prepared by dissolving 115 mg MACl, 1100 mg FAI and 110 mg MABr in 15 ml IPA and stirring for 1 h at room temperature.

Finally, a back contact electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation of aurum.

# 2.4 Characterization

Current density-voltage (J-V) measurements were conducted by applying a Keithley 2400 source meter with an illumination of a Newport Oriel Solar Simulator with AM 1.5 irradiation. Calibration of the illumination was carried out with a silicon photodiode before J-V measurement. The scans were performed at a rate of  $ 100\mathrm{mV / s} $  for both forward scan (FS) and reverse scan (RS). The active area of solar cells for measurement is  $ 0.1~\mathrm{cm}^2 $  as determined by a shadow mask. The photovoltaic parameters of the solar cells were obtained by an average of the values from 20 pieces of devices. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were measured with a DX-2700 diffractometer. The UV-vis absorption spectra were conducted using a Cary 5000 spectrophotometer. The morphology and nanostructure of materials were obtained using scanning electron microscope (SEM). The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra were obtained on the FLS 980E spectrometer with an excitation laser beam at  $ 515~\mathrm{nm} $ . # 3. Results and Discussion

In the present study, HHPs solar cells with a structure of ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au were fabricated. The J-V curves of the solar cells were measured with forward scan (FS) and revers scan (RS), from which the PCE was calculated by an average of the values from FS and RS. Figure 1a displays the dependence of PCE on the oxygen passivation time in ambient air, in which the PCE of the solar cells were obtained by an average of the values from 20 pieces of devices. With the increase of the passivation time, the PCE increases at the beginning, and then decreases. A maximum value was achieved at the passivation time of 4 days. Hereinafter we referred passivation for 0 day as without passivation, and passivation for 4 days as with passivation. Table 1 presents the detailed photovoltaic parameters of the solar cells without and with passivation. Compared with the solar cells without passivation, the solar cells with passivation present enhanced performance. The short circuit densities (J $ _{sc} $ ) for both of the solar cells are nearly the same, while the open circuit voltage (V $ _{oc} $ ) and fill factor (FF) of the solar cells with passivation increase largely, which induce its average PCE increased to 20.1% from 17.3% for the solar cells without passivation. Figure 1b displays the J-V curves of the best performance solar cells based on perovskite layers without and with passivation. Both of the J-V curves exhibit negligible hysteresis. The photovoltaic parameters are presented in the inset of Figure 1b. The PCE of the best performance solar cell with passivation increased to 20.9% from 18.2% for that without passivation.

To understand the PCE enhancement deeply, some investigations were carried out. Figure 2a shows the XRD of the perovskite layers without and with passivation. The peaks marked with black dots belong to the ITO/SnO $ _2 $  substrate. The peaks denoted with black stars are attributed to the perovskite material, and the peaks located at  $ 12.4^{\circ} $  and  $ 38.4^{\circ} $  (denoted with inverted black triangles) are attributed to  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  [22]. In this work, the perovskite layer was prepared according to the method in the report [22], in which the high efficiency of  $ 21.3\% $  was obtained. In this method, the perovskite material is nonstoichiometric in which the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  is excessive. This XRD pattern of perovskite layer is similar to that in the report. The intensity ratio of the peak at  $ 12.4^{\circ} $  ( $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  phase) and its neighbouring peak at  $ 13.7^{\circ} $  (perovskite phase) is 0.81 for XRD of the perovskite layer without passivation, while that is 0.75 for the perovskite layer with passivation. This indicates that the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  in the sample phase decreases after oxygen passivation, which could be useful for the solar cell performance [23]. It has been reported that moderately access  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  in perovskite is beneficial for high efficiency of solar cells due to its passivation of defects in the perovskite layer [24, 25]. Kim et al. [24] fabricated the PSCs using excess  $ 5.7\mathrm{mol}\% $   $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  to obtain high PCE of  $ 20.1\% $ . Jiang et al. [25] reported a planar PSCs with a high PCE of  $ 21\% $  using moderate amount of residual  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . Figure 2c presents the photoluminescence (PL) spectra of the perovskite layers without and with passivation. The PL peaks of the perovskite layer are located at about  $ 804~\mathrm{nm} $ , which agrees with the report [22]. The peak position shifts to  $ 802~\mathrm{nm} $  for the perovskite layer with passivation from  $ 804~\mathrm{nm} $  for that without passivation, which could be due to the passivation of perovskite layer by the oxygen in ambient air [16]. The PL intensity of the perovskite layer is increased after oxygen passivation, which could be due to the reduction of nonradiative recombination [11-14]. Moreover, the enhancement of PL intensity is the direct evidence of perovskite layer passivation by oxygen. In this work, the perovskite layer was kept in a dry ambient air (25□,  $ \mathrm{RH} = 3\% $ ) which is mainly composed of oxygen and nitrogen. Tian et al. [14] demonstrated that oxygen can enhance the PL intensity of perovskite material, while the enhancement of PL intensity does not exist in nitrogen atmosphere. Therefore the enhancement of PL intensity indicates the perovskite layer was passivated by oxygen but not by nitrogen, which is the proof for the oxidation of perovskite layer. Liu et al. [16] prepared perovskite layer in dry ambient air for PSCs to obtain high PCE of the solar cells, and they demonstrated that the perovskite layer was passivated by the oxygen in ambient air. The mechanism on the positive effects of oxygen on the perovskite properties was investigated with theoretical calculation [11], which demonstrated that superoxide and peroxide are the common forms of oxygen interacting with the perovskite and that oxygen most readily interacts with iodine vacancies on the perovskite surface.

To investigate the effect of oxygen passivation on the stability of the HHPs solar cells, we placed the unencapsulated solar cells without encapsulation in ambient air (25°C, RH=30%) for aging, and measured the J-V curves at certain interval. Figure 5a shows the variation of the PCE of the HHPs solar cells on the aging time. For the solar cells without passivation, the PCE decreases to 77.3% of the original value after aging for 100 days, while the PCE of the solar cells with passivation maintains 87.4% of the initial value. The same phenomenon was observed for the FF and   $  V_{oc}  $   shown in Figure 5b and c. While there is no obvious difference between the   $  J_{sc}  $   values as shown in Figure 5d, which could be due to the little effect of oxygen passivation on the   $  J_{sc}  $   as demonstrated in the J-V measurements displayed in Figure 1b. # 4. Conclusion

In this paper, the HHPs perovskite layer was naturally passivated by the oxygen in ambient air by exclusion of light illumination. The HHPs solar cells based on the perovskite layer with oxygen passivation were fabricated. The maximum value of the PCE was achieved with oxygen passivation time of 4 days. The PCE of the solar cells with passivation increases to  $ 20.1\% $  from  $ 17.3\% $  for the solar cells without passivation. Meanwhile, the stability of the HHPs solar cells with passivation was also improved. The PCE decreases to  $ 77.3\% $  of the initial value for the solar cells without passivation after aging for 100 days, while the PCE of the devices with passivation maintains  $ 87.4\% $  of the initial value. This work provides a novel and simple method using oxygen passivation to improve the efficiency and stability of HHPs solar cells
