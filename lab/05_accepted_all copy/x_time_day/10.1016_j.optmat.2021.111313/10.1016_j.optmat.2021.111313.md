# 1. Introduction

Despite the significant rate of the power conversion efficiency (PCE) obtained from the porous structure of perovskite solar cells (PSCs), the recorded value of  $ 24.8\% $  is far from the predicted value  $ (-31\%) $  [1]. Such features as the fast electron-transfer ability, high hole-blocking capability, low optical dissipation, stability as well as easy and inexpensive fabrication of electron-transport layers (ETLs) play important roles in the fabrication and performance of PSCs [2,3]. Of all the ETLs ever used to make PSCs, mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $   $ (\mathrm{mTiO_2}) $  has been the most favored one. The use of  $ \mathrm{mTiO_2} $ , however, is not without problems. In this regard, one can refer to such problems as the recombination of charges and the low ability of ETLs to transport charge carriers. A serious deterring point about this material is its sensitivity to light, which makes the device unstable and limits its application [1,4]. One way to overcome these problems and, thus, to promote the optical and electrical

In perovskite solar cells (PSCs), the transportation of photogenerated electrons can be controlled by the modification of electron-transport layers. In the present study,   $  TiO_{2}  $   doped with reduced graphene oxide (RGO) and   $  C_{60}  $   was used as an electron-transport layer in hole-transport-free porous perovskite solar cells. The PSCs were fabricated in the laboratory by a single-step method. As the measurements showed, the use of a mixed electron-transport layer in perovskite cells helps to decrease the absorption in perovskite layers in the visible range, while the electron transport is increased. The scanning electron microscopy images and the X-ray diffraction patterns also showed that large perovskite crystals have fewer holes and better morphology than the reference cells fabricated with pure mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $  . Appropriate amounts of RGO and   $  C_{60}  $   were used to compare the parameters of the modified fabricated cells with those of the reference cells. Moreover, the stability of the fabricated cells was examined by the comparison of them to the reference cells. This comparative study showed an increase of efficiency from 5.15% to 9.10% for the untreated and the treated samples respectively. Less cell destruction was also observed over a 32-day period for the treated samples, indicating the improvement of the shelf stability of PSCs through the suggested ETL modification.

Fullerene and its derivatives have been another set of materials widely used in the field of ETL fabrication. With their high efficiency in electron transfer, they have proved to be capable of reducing hysteresis, passivating the deep trap states of   $  TiO_{2}  $   and enhancing the stability of PSCs. These features can improve the performance of perovskite solar cells to a large extent. Fullerene has also been used in the structure of ETLs in planar PSCs. In this case, it has shown a notable photovoltaic performance with high power-conversion efficiency   $  [10–12]  $  .

As the literature suggests, some researchers have sought to improve the performance of perovskite solar cells by using various materials. In this respect, one may refer to Wolf et al. [13] who used four fullerene combinations in ETLs, Lin et al. [11] who used  $ C_{60} $  and  $ C_{60} / C_{70} $  in ETLs, Mundhaas et al. [14] who benefited from  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 / C_{60}} $  as an ETL and Chen et al. [10] who placed a buffer layer between an ITO electrode and a  $ C_{60} $ /ETL.

This study aims at the effect of ETL1 (mTiO  $  _{2}  $  ) and ETL2 (mTiO  $  _{2}  $   + RGO + C  $  _{60}  $  ) on porous perovskite solar cells without HTLs. The general structure of FTO/ETL (cTiO  $  _{2}  $  /ETL (1or 2)/CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $  /Au) was applied for the cell fabrication. The application of this structure in an ETL resulted in the enhanced electron transport between mTiO  $  _{2}  $   and FTO, which was due to the reduced energy barrier between mTiO  $  _{2}  $   and FTO, thus preventing the charge recombination at the interface. The cell stability was also enhanced due to fewer defects in the mTiO  $  _{2}  $   layer and C  $  _{60}  $   hydrophobicity; the latter may prevent accumulation and trapping by C  $  _{60}  $  . As the mesoporous TiO  $  _{2}  $   was doped, the efficiency rose from 5.15% to 9.10%.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Precursor preparation

Methylammonium iodide,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (MAI) and   $  PbI_{2}  $   were synthesized as reported in Refs. [15,16]. The solvents used to fabricate a precursor included anhydrous DMF (99.5%) from Samchun Co. and DMSO (99%) from Exir Co. Also,   $  mTiO_{2}  $   paste was purchased from Solar Sharif Co. and diluted in ethanol at the mass ratio of 1:5. The perovskite solution contained 461 mg of   $  PbI_{2}  $ , 159 mg of MAI in 0.635 mL of DMF and 0.071 mL of DMSO. The solution was stirred for more than 45 min.

# 2.2. Solar cell fabrication

FTO-coated glass substrates were patterned by etching with hydrochloric acid (HCl) and zinc (Zn) powder and sequentially cleaned with soap and water, distilled water, acetone, ethanol and isopropanol for 15 min each. A compact layer of   $  TiO_{2}  $   was deposited via spin-coating at 4000 rpm for 60 s. All the samples were placed on a hot plate, heated at 100 °C for 20 min and then sintered at 500 °C for 60 min. Pure   $  mTiO_{2}  $   scaffold (ETL1) and   $  mTiO_{2}  $   doped with   $  C_{60}  $   and RGO at the volume ratio of 100:1:1 (ETL2) were spin-coated on compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers at 4000 rpm for 60 s and annealed at 500 °C for 60 min.   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   was synthesized using the one-step rotation method [17], and the subsequent experiments were performed in the open air. Then, 0.040 mL of the perovskite solution was spin-coated on the ETLs at 4000 rpm for 30 s. The anti-solvent was poured on the intermediate perovskite layers at the 25th second. The samples were annealed at 100 °C for 45 min. Finally, a gold electrode was deposited with the sputtering method. All the cells were kept in a dry compartment in dark, and their parameters were measured at room temperature and 20–35% humidity.

# 3. Results and discussion

# 3.1. X-ray diffraction analysis

The XRD patterns of the perovskite layers grown on two types of ETLs are shown in Fig. 1. The two samples have a similar crystalline structure. The X-ray diffraction peaks located at 2θ of 14.2°, 20.1°, 23.6, 24.6°, 28.6° and 32° can be indexed to the (110), (112), (211), (202), (220) and (310) planes respectively. This suggests a tetragonal crystal structure, which is consistent with the reported results. The three extra Xray diffraction peaks appear at 2θ of 26.6°, 35° and 37.8° corresponding to anatase titanium dioxide, which is consistent with previous research [18]. The higher intensity and the lower width of the peaks for the perovskite grown on ETL2 reflect better morphology and crystallinity as well as a larger perovskite particle size, which are of benefit for the modification of perovskite layers without pinholes. This is because the charge transfer is accelerated and the electron-hole recombination is reduced [19–22]. The existence of a PbI₂ peak (12.6°) can be attributed to the incomplete PbI₂ reaction with CH₃NH₃I₃ or the perovskite degradation by air during the layering process through spin coating.

# 3.2. Absorption spectrum analysis

As shown in Fig. 2, the absorbing ability of the ETL2 layer in the visible region of the spectrum increased due to the presence of RGO and  $ C_{60} $ , which is in agreement with the previous reports [4].

The diagrams drawn in Fig. 3 are the absorption spectra of FTO/  $  cTiO_{2}/ETL  $  (1 and 2)/  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  structure. As it can be understood, the absorption of the perovskite decreases due to FTO/  $  cTiO_{2}/ETL  $  . It is also suggested that, although the absorption of light by the perovskite layer decreases in the visible range in this structure, the electron transport of the cells increases. This results in an increase in the overall cell performance.

# 3.3. Photoluminescence (PL) analysis

As shown in Fig. 4, there is a characteristic luminescence peak recorded under excitation at  $ \lambda = 405\mathrm{nm} $ . For the perovskite solar cells with the ETL2 substrate, the intensity of PL is reduced. This reduction can be attributed to the improvement of the crystalline properties of the perovskite as observed in the XRD patterns (Fig. 1) and the SEM images (Fig. 5). There is also a significant change in the morphology of the perovskite surface. It indicates that RGO and  $ C_{60} $  substantially affect the electronic properties of titanium in addition to its optical capability, i.e., decreasing the transparency of the  $ \mathrm{mTiO_2} $  layer. It is, thus, concluded that RGO and  $ C_{60} $  can facilitate electron transport and hamper the electron-hole recombination at the interface of ETL/perovskite layers [4,5,23].

Based on the ImageJ software, the ETL coverage by the perovskite is  $ 95.4\% $  for the cells with an ETL1 substrate and  $ 98.3\% $  for those with an ETL2 substrate. Thus, the use of ETL2 can increase the average particle size and the coverage of this layer by perovskite [24,25].

According to the SEM images and the ETL coverage by perovskite, the perovskite layer fabricated over the ETL2 substrate provides a highly compact perovskite film with very few surface defects (confirmed by its 98.3% surface coverage). Such a desirable perovskite layer leads to less charge accumulation and, thus, reduced charge recombination. Less recombination, in turn, improves the solar cell performance [22,26].

# 3.5. Current-voltage analysis

Fig. 7 shows the J-V curves of the best-efficiency perovskite solar cells made with two different ETLs on the second day after construction. The short-circuit current density (Jsc), open-circuit voltage (Voc), fill factor (FF) and PCE of the champion perovskite solar cells with ETL1 were found to be   $  14.95 \, mA cm^{-2}  $  , 0.75 V, 0.46 and 5.15% respectively. The Jsc of   $  22.72 \, mA cm^{-2}  $  , Voc of 0.81 V, FF of 0.50 and PCE of 9.10% were also achieved for the champion perovskite solar cells containing ETL2. As it can be seen in Table 1, the photovoltaic parameters were improved by ETL doping. This improvement in the performance can be attributed to the reduction of the energy barrier between the perovskite and mTiO  $  _{2}  $   due to the presence of C  $  _{60}  $   and RGO in the mTiO  $  _{2}  $   layer. Indeed, the reduction of the energy barrier between mTiO  $  _{2}  $   and cTiO  $  _{2}  $   points to the higher porosity of this layer and, thus, the better perovskite penetration. These factors increase the efficiency of electron transport and prevent charge recombination [27].

# 3.6. Aging examination

Fig. 8 shows the mean daily PCE (normalized to the first day) for the two types of the fabricated cells over a 32-day period. All the cells were kept in a dry compartment in dark, and the parameters were measured at room temperature under 20–35% humidity. The cells made with ETL1 and ETL2 had the initial efficiency of 54% and 92% after 32 days respectively. The improvement made in the cells with ETL2 can be attributed to the deeper perovskite penetration into the ETL and the improved contact between the perovskite and the mesoporous layer due to the existence of RGO and   $  C_{60}  $   in mTiO  $  _{2}  $  .

# 4. Conclusion

In this study,  $ \mathrm{mTiO_2} $  was doped with RGO and  $ C_{60} $  and used as an ETL in porous perovskite cells without an HTL. Through the ETL modification, the micromorphology of the perovskite layer was improved and the surface defects in it were reduced. Besides, due to the existence of RGO and  $ C_{60} $  dopants in the ETL, the charge recombination at the interface of the ETL/perovskite layer was suppressed but the electron transport process was facilitated in the photovoltaic system. The cell parameters were enhanced due to the improvement of the energy barrier between the ETL and the  $ \mathrm{mTiO_2} $  perovskite layer. The efficiency of the fabricated cells was, thus, increased from  $ 5.15\% $  to  $ 9.10\% $ . Moreover, the stability
