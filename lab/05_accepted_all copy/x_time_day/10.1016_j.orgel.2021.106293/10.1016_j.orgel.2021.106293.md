# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have become a research hotspot in the field of photovoltaics due to their fast-growing power conversion efficiency (PCE) [1], low cost, and solution processability [2,3]. Recently, the certificated PCE of PSCs reaches over 25% [4–6], which is exceeding copper indium gallium selenium and cadmium telluride solar cells and approaching the record of silicon-based solar cells. Inorganic-organic perovskite possesses advantages of adjustable band gap and high carrier mobility of both electrons and holes, so it is suitable for fabricating the photovoltaics devices with planar architecture. In a typical planar PSCs, perovskite layer is sandwiched between an electron transport layer (ETL) and a hole transport layer (HTL) to construct an n-i-p stacked structure. # ABSTRACT

Perovskite solar cell (PSC) has attracted great attention due to its high power conversion efficiency (PCE), low cost and solution processability. The well-designed interface and the modification of electron transport layer (ETL) are critical to the PCE and long-term stability of PSCs. In this article, a fused-ring electron acceptor is employed as the interfacial material between  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and the perovskite in rigid and flexible PSCs. The modification improves the surface of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , which decreases the defects of ETL surface. Moreover, the modified surface has lower hydrophilicity, and thus is beneficial to the growth of perovskite with large grain size and high quality. As a result, the interfacial charge transfer is promoted and the interfacial charge recombination can be suppressed. The highest PCE of  $ 19.61\% $  is achieved for the rigid PSCs after the introduction of ITIC, and the hysteresis effect is significantly reduced. Flexible PSC with ITIC obtains a PCE of  $ 14.87\% $ , and the device stability is greatly improved. This study provides an efficient candidate as the interfacial modifier for PSCs, which is compatible with low-temperature solution process and has a great practical potential for the commercialization of PSCs.

However, its rough surface with a large number of surface defects leads to serious charge recombination and large leakage current, resulting in efficiency loss and nonnegligible hysteresis effect   $  [13,14]  $  . Besides,   $  TiO_{2}  $   would catalyze the decomposition of perovskite and decrease the stability of PSCs under ultraviolet irradiation. The approaches of optimization include element doping, surface treatment, nanocomposite assembly and interface engineering   $  [15–18]  $  . The FREAs are used as the electron acceptors in organic solar cells and their structures are based on an electron donating fused ring with strong electron withdrawing groups, which contributes to adjustable optical band gap and energy level structure, good electronic transfer capability, excellent thermal and photochemical stability   $  [21,22]  $  . In this paper, a typical FREAs, ITIC, is deposited on top of   $  TiO_{2}  $   film as the interfacial modifier for planar PSCs with forward structure. ITIC has a well-match LUMO energy level (-3.93 eV) to the perovskite (Fig. 1) and good electron transport ability [23]. The influence of ITIC on the morphology of perovskite crystals and the electrical properties of the device are studied comprehensively. The flexible device remains 80% of its original PCEs after 500h stored in ambient humidity of 30–40%, which shows significantly superior stability to the unmodified device.

# 2.2. Fabrication of PSCs

Firstly, the ITO glass substrate was ultrasonic cleaned with detergent, deionized water, acetone and isopropanol for 15 min, and then dried with ultraviolet ozone for 10 min. The   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticle solution (20 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) was spin-coated onto the ITO substrate at 4000 r min  $  ^{-1}  $   for 50 s. The desired thickness of   $  TiO_{2}  $   film was obtained by repeating spinning process for three times, and the samples were annealed in the air under ambient pressure at 120 °C for 60 min. The following coating steps were performed under argon atmosphere inside a glovebox. ITIC/chlorobenzene solution (1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) was spin-coated onto the surface of   $  TiO_{2}  $   film at 2000 r min  $  ^{-1}  $   for 50 s and then annealed at 120 °C for 20 min. For the perovskite layer, the   $  PbI_{2}  $   (DMSO) (1.5 mol  $  ^{-1}  $  ) complex dissolved in DMF was preheated at 70 °C, and the solution was spin-coated on the top of the ITIC modified layer at 2000 r min  $  ^{-1}  $   for 45 s. Subsequently, a mixed FAI:MACl solution in 2-propanol (70 mg mL-1) was spin-coated on the top of the   $  PbI_{2}  $  (DMSO) film at 2000 r min  $  ^{-1}  $   for 40 s. The substrates were then annealed on a hot plate at 120 °C for 45 min. After cooling to room temperature, Spiro-OMeTAD was then deposited on the top of the perovskite layer by spin-coating at 2000 r min  $  ^{-1}  $   for 50 s. The Spiro-OMeTAD solution was prepared by dissolving Spiro-OMeTAD in chlorobenzene (60 mM), with the addition of 30 mM Li-TFSI (520 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in acetonitrile), 200 mM 4-tBP, and 1.8 mM FK209 (300 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in acetonitrile). Finally, 100 nm of silver was deposited by thermal evaporation using a shadow mask to pattern the electrodes at a base pressure of   $  4 \times 10^{-4}  $   Pa; the active area of this electrode was fixed at 0.1 cm  $  ^{2}  $  . To explore the role of the ITIC modified layer, the control samples without ITIC coating were prepared under the same conditions. For the fabrication of flexible PSCs, the above process was applied on patterned ITO-PEN substrates with the active area fixed at 0.06 cm  $  ^{2}  $  .

# 3. Results and discussion

# 3.1. Effect of ITIC on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $

In order to study the effect of ITIC, ITIC with different thickness is deposited on   $  TiO_{2}  $   by spin coating with different spin speed. 6000 r/min, 5000 r/min, 4000 r/min, 3000 r/min and 2000 r/min are applied, corresponding to an increasing thickness of ITIC on   $  TiO_{2}  $  . The reference sample based on pristine   $  TiO_{2}  $   is fabricated by an annealing-free aqueous process. A Hall effect measurement system is employed to test the effect on the electrical properties of these films, and the results are listed in Table S1. It is found that the sheet resistance (Rs) first decreases and then increases as the spin speed of ITIC decreases. The lowest Rs achieves   $  5.23 \Omega/cm^{2}  $   when the spin speed is 4000 r/min, which is much lower than   $  5.81 \Omega/cm^{2}  $   of ITO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   sample. However, it should be noted that when the spin speed reaches 2000 r/min, the Rs becomes larger than that of the reference sample. The deduced reason of the tendency is illustrated in Fig. S1. When the speed is relatively high (6000–4000 r/min), only a small amount of ITIC loads on   $  TiO_{2}  $  , which flattens the rough surface of   $  TiO_{2}  $   and fills tiny cracks of   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticles without increasing the thickness of the film obviously. Attributed to improved electron pathway, the Rs of the ITO/  $  TiO_{2}  $ /ITIC samples decreases remarkably. But further increasing of ITIC on   $  TiO_{2}  $   thickens the entire film, leading to a larger Rs. This indicates that a suitable thickness of ITIC improves the surface and the conductivity of the entire ETL, which benefits the extraction and the transportation of electrons. Best electrical properties are obtained by spinning at 4000 r/min, which would be applied in the following sections unless specified.

Top-view SEM images exhibit the difference of surface morphology directly. As shown in Fig. 2a and b, the surface of ITIC modified  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film is dense and uniform, and is much more compact and smoother than unmodified film. The thickness of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / ITIC} $  ETL is about  $ 69~\mathrm{nm} $  after the introduction of ITIC  $ (4000\mathrm{rmin}^{-1}) $ , without significant increase of ETL thickness (Fig. 2c and d). The result shows that ITIC fills the cracks and defects of the rough  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film, gaining a higher quality of film, which corresponds to the above results of electrical properties. The modification would enhance the contact of ETL with perovskite and reduce the charge recombination at the interface [27]. The water contact angles (Fig. 2e and f) of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / ITIC} $  ETLs are investigated to explore the surface wettability, which would dramatically influence the crystallization process of perovskite. Pristine  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  exhibits definitely hydrophilicity with the water contact angle of  $ 27^{\circ} $ , and it turns to  $ 48^{\circ} $  after the modification of ITIC. The ITIC modification reduces the hydrophilicity of ETL films to a moderate level, ensuring well deposition of the precursor solution of perovskite. Moreover, according to the literatures [28,29], the increased hydrophobicity of ETL could promote the grain boundary migration and the growth of large grains by suppressing heterogeneous nucleation of perovskite grains [29].

Surface topographic image of   $  TiO_{2}/ITIC  $   ETL is also studied by atomic force microscopy (AFM). The root mean square (RMS) roughness of   $  TiO_{2}/ITIC  $   ETL is 2.26 nm, demonstrating a favorable smooth surface for perovskite deposition (Fig. 2g). Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) is used to investigate the contact potential difference (CPD) via a Ti/Ir (5/20) coated Si cantilever tip. The work function (W.F.) of the tip is 4.97 eV, which is calibrated to single-crystal silicon wafer (100). The average surface potential value of the modified ETL is 418 mV (Fig. 2g), corresponding to the work function (W.F.) of 4.55 eV, which is very close to the W.F. of perovskite (4.6 eV) [30]. Small W.F. difference between the ETL and the perovskite suggests a minimized band bending and energy barrier, inhibiting the charge transport losses at the interface [31].

The transmittance of ETL also plays an important role in n-i-p PSCs with forward architecture. Fig. S2(a) shows the UV–vis transmission spectra of ITO,   $  ITO/TiO_{2}  $   and   $  ITO/TiO_{2}/ITIC  $   films. The transmittance of

$ \mathrm{TiO_2 / ITIC} $  ETL increased slightly between  $ 450~\mathrm{nm} $  and  $ 550~\mathrm{nm} $  and decreased slightly between  $ 550~\mathrm{nm} $  and  $ 750~\mathrm{nm} $ . Overall, its transmittance almost remains unchanged in the main absorption wavelength of  $ (\mathrm{FAPbI}_3)_\mathrm{x}(\mathrm{MAPbCl}_3)_{1 - x} $  from  $ 450~\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 850~\mathrm{nm} $ . ITIC has two absorption peaks between  $ 550~\mathrm{nm} $  and  $ 750~\mathrm{nm} $  after modification (Fig. S2b), which accords with the literature [32].

# 3.2. Perovskite on different ETLs

Fig. 3a and b displays (FAPbI $ _3 $ ) $ _x $  (MAPbCl $ _3 $ ) $ _{1-x} $  crystals on different ETLs by the two-step spin coating method. The average grain size of perovskite crystals on pristine TiO $ _2 $  is about 300 nm, but the homogeneity and the quality of the film are insufficient. The crystals grow to 600–700 nm after the introduction of ITIC, and the perovskite is well distributed with high quality. The surface of modified ETL is less hydrophilic, on which the nucleation density can be lowered and thus form larger perovskite crystals. The cross-sectional SEM images validate the difference of the grain size, and the thickness of perovskite layers on different substrates is determined to be 400 nm equally. Intensive crystal boundaries are found because of the smaller grain size of perovskite on pristine TiO $ _2 $ , which means that the carries should encounter several boundaries in this film (Fig. 3c). However, the grain size of perovskite on ITIC/TiO $ _2 $  substrate is obviously larger than the thickness of the film, so the perovskite grains run through the whole layer in the longitudinal direction. The crystal boundaries are known as the accumulation areas of charge traps and defects, which would greatly hinder the effective charge extraction and transportation. As a consequence, ITIC modification is beneficial to the growth of crystals and the quality of perovskite film.

The optical properties of the perovskite determine the light absorption of the device, which is crucial to obtain high performance. Fig. 4a displays the UV–vis spectra of   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based and   $  TiO_{2}/ITIC  $  -based perovskite films. Briefly, the absorption of both films is strong in short wavelength region, but weak in long wavelength region.   $  TiO_{2}/ITIC  $  -based perovskite film shows dramatically stronger absorption in short wavelength region than unmodified film, attributing to the improved quality and morphology of the perovskite layer. The spectra almost overlap in the long wavelength region, illustrating that the absorption of ITIC is negligible. Steady-state fluorescence spectrum of the perovskite film on ITO,   $  ITO/TiO_{2}  $   and   $  ITO/TiO_{2}/ITIC  $   is measured to compare the charge extraction at the interface of the ETL/perovskite (Fig. 4b). The photoluminescence (PL) emission intensity of the perovskite film without ETL is the highest, and decreases significantly after inserting   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer and   $  TiO_{2}/ITIC  $   layer. The quenching effect is caused by the electron transfer from perovskite to the ETL [33]. The fluorescence intensity of   $  ITO/TiO_{2}/ITIC  $  /perovskite is the lowest, indicating that the ITIC modification enhances the PL quenching ability of ETL. The introduction of ITIC improves the electron extraction and reduces the charge recombination at the perovskite/ETL interface effectively [34].

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, the fused ring electron acceptor ITIC is deposited on the conventional ETL   $  TiO_{2}  $   as the interfacial modifier. ITIC fills the cracks and suppresses the defects of   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface, which restrains the charge accumulation and recombination at the interface. Due to the decreasing hydrophilicity of the surface, large perovskite crystals with high quality can be obtained on the modified ETL. Therefore, the PCE of the PSCs reaches 19.61% on the rigid substrate and 14.87% on the flexible substrate after the introduction of ITIC. Moreover, the PCE of   $  TiO_{2}/ITIC  $  -based flexible devices remains 80% after 500 h, whose stability is significantly superior to that of the   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based device. The application of FREA as the ETL modification layer is one of the technical approaches to further improve the performance of PSCs.
