# Abstract

Although carbon based hole-conductor-free perovskite solar cells have acquired interest in the scientific community in the past few years, some issues with regard to relatively low efficiency and toxicity need to be addressed before their commercialization. For the first time, the environmentally friendly ammonium acetate  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COONH}_4 $  ( $ \mathrm{NH_4Ac} $ ) and  $ \mathrm{Zn(CH_3COO)_2} $  ( $ \mathrm{ZnAc_2} $ ) were adopted as additives for perovskite preparation. By introducing a very small amount (0.08%) of volatile  $ \mathrm{NH_4Ac} $  through a simple one-step spin coating method, high-quality perovskite layer with lower defect density, larger grain size and pure perovskite composition without alien atom was obtained, resulting in a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 13.9%, which is ~25% enhancement compared to that of the device without additive. Moreover, stability was also investigated, 6% degradation appeared in PCE of  $ \mathrm{NH_4Ac} $  sample after being stored for 1900h in dark. The promoted interface contact of carbon/perovskite results in higher efficiency and longer stability of  $ \mathrm{NH_4Ac} $  added perovskite solar cell (PSC). On the other hand, addition of non-volatile  $ \mathrm{ZnAc_2} $  only lead to a slight enhancement of the efficiency. The factors affecting the properties will be discussed in detail.

# Introduction

Organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cell (PSC) have drawn worldwide attention over the past few years with the maximum power conversion efficiency (PCE) approaching over 23%. [1-7] High light absorption coefficient,[3] suitable bandgap,[4] bipolar charge transport,[5] long charge diffusion length and charge carrier lifetime have been proved to be effective in the excellent hybrid perovskite materials properties.[6,7] However, expensive and air-sensitive hole transportation materials (HTM) are widely used and a noble metal electrode (eg. Au) are deposited by complicated vacuum fabrication techniques in traditional PSCs.[8] Carbon materials can be used not only in other fields such as thermal storage materials [9-11], but also as electrodes to replace precious metals in perovskite soalr cells. To stimulate the industrial development and promote the commercial competitiveness of perovskite solar cells, carbon-based hole-conductor-free PSCs (C-PSCs) were developed for its low cost. However, the PCEs of C-PSCs still largely lag behind those of traditional PSCs with HTMs. The performance was not obviously improved mainly because of the poor contact at the perovskite/carbon interface, which seriously inhibit hole extraction and decrease fill factor (FF) [12,13]. In order to tackle this problem, it is necessary to fabricate an high quality perovskite layer to increase the interfacial contact. Up to now, great efforts have been made on this point, such as the control of precursor ratio, solvent engineering, investigation on the crystallization kinetics of the perovskite and varying fabrication methods such as one-step, two-step and thermal vapor deposition [14-18]. The one-step methods nowadays become essential in the fabrication of compact perovskite films [19,20] and extensive efforts have been made to enhance the quality (morphology, crystallization and size) of the film made by one-step method [21, 22]. For instance, Han et al. [23] mixed CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3} $  (MAPbI $ _{3} $ ) perovskite with LiCl by a one-step drop-coating approach, leading to better electronic properties due to the improved crystallinity and morphology of the perovskite layer.

Alex K.-Y. Jen [19] introduced  $ \mathrm{SrCl}_2 $  in the precursor solution to modify  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite and found that the incorporation of  $ \mathrm{SrCl}_2 $  rendered the formation of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3(\mathrm{SrCl}_2)_x $  perovskite, leading to inhibiting trap generation and improving pore filling. Jen et al. [24] added  $ \mathrm{CaI}_2 $  into perovskite precursor solution, resulting in dense, lower defect concentration and surface-smooth perovskite film. As can be concluded that most of the additives investigated are halogen additives and may be retained in the final film after fabrication, leading to complex perovskite component and may produce byproduct retarding the long time performance of C-PSCs. Thus, it is necessary to develop an approach which do not alter the composition of the perovskite by one-step method. Besides, to further improve their performances, molecular adsorption approach has been adopted to modify the halide perovskite surface/interface structures[25], with an aim to enhance the power conversion efficiency and stability of halide perovskite solar cells. Da Yun et al. [26] quantitatively assess the energy losses in planar perovskite solar cells in terms of the underlying physical mechanisms. The coupled optical and electrical modeling is developed to explore the working principle of the perovskite solar cells. IlariaCardinaletti et al. [27] report on the launch of the stratospheric mission OSCAR, which demonstrated for the first time organic-based solar cell operation in extra-terrestrial conditions. Recently,  $ \mathrm{Pb(CH_3COOH)_2} $  with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COO}^- $  was adopted as an additive in the perovskite precursor, resulting in better morphology and larger grains of perovskite films[28].  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $  with  $ \mathrm{NH_4^+} $  was also employed to retreat the preformed perovskite film, leading to an improved PCE of  $ 19.44\% $  [29]. However, both  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $  and the produced thiocyanic acid (HSCN) are toxic. In addition, excess  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $  can lead to the precipitates easily and it is harmful to the preformed perovskite film. The former researches encourage us to choose an environmentally friendly  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COONH}_4 $  as precursor to fabricate perovskite film by one-step method. It is speculated that both  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COO}^- $  and  $ \mathrm{NH_4^+} $  may left from the perovskite film after fabrication. For comparison, the effect of nonhalogen additive left in the film was also considered. Recently,  $ \mathrm{ZnCl}_2 $  has been used as an additive to control the film morphology and improved the performance of the device [31,32]. But the zinc ion

In our study, non-volatile  $ \mathrm{Zn(CH_3COO)_2} $  was employed to explore the synergistic effect of  $ \mathrm{Zn}^{2+} $  and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COO}^- $  on the performance of C-PSCs.

In addition, the storage stability of the devices were also investigated.

# 2. Experiment section

Materials: Imethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, anhydrous, 99.5%), N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF, anhydrous, 99.8%), chlorobenzene (99.9%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ , 99%) and CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Ammonium acetate (NH $ _4 $ Ac) and Zinc acetate (ZnAc $ _2 $ ) were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co, Ltd.

Perovskite precursor solution: To prepare  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  solution,  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  at a 1:1 molar ratio were stirred in a mixture of DMF and DMSO (9:1 v/v) with final concentrations of  $ 1.4\mathrm{M} $  in a  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $  condition.  $ \mathrm{NH_4Ac} $ -perovskite solution mixture of methylammonium iodide (MAI) / $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  /  $ \mathrm{NH_4Ac} $  (1:1:x, x=0.04%, 0.06%, 0.08%, and 0.1%) and  $ \mathrm{ZnAc_2} $ -perovskite solution mixture of MAI / $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  /  $ \mathrm{ZnAc_2} $  (1:1-x:x, x=5%, 7%, 9%, and 11%).

Device Fabrication: Fluorine–doped tin oxide coated glasses (FTO, TEC-15, NSG) were firstly patterned by etching with zinc powder and diluted hydrochloric acid (2 mol L  $  ^{-1}  $  ). and then cleaned with deionized (DI) water, alcohol, acetone, isopropanol. A compact-TiO  $  _{2}  $   was deposited on FTO by spin-coating at 4000 rpm for 30 s using a precursor solution of titanium isopropoxide in ethanol. After drying at 100☐, the film

# 3. Results and Discussion

The carbon based perovskite solar cells were constructed with a stacking sequence of  $ \mathrm{FTO / TiO_2} $  perovskite with and without additives/Carbon, as shown by SEM images of the sample cross-section in Figure 1a and 1b. The electrons and holes are transported to  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer and carbon layer through the sandwiched perovskite layer, respectively. According to Figure 1a and b, the thicknesses of the mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , perovskite and carbon layer are about  $ 400\mathrm{nm} $ ,  $ 350\mathrm{nm} $  and  $ 20~\mu \mathrm{m} $ , respectively.

# 3.1 Microstructure and properties of perovskite films

The surface morphology of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite films (plane view, without carbon layer) fabricated with and without additives were investigated by atomic force microscopy (AFM), as shown in Figure S1. The measured mean square root roughness of pristine  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film is  $ 13.2\mathrm{nm} $ . It decreases to 10.2 and  $ 12.6\mathrm{nm} $  after introducing  $ \mathrm{NH_4Ac} $  and  $ \mathrm{ZnAc_2} $ , respectively. The results show that  $ \mathrm{NH_4Ac} $  additive has a smoother surface than those of the  $ \mathrm{ZnAc_2} $  added and pristine perovskite film. The detailed surface morphology of the films were uncovered by SEM (Figure 1c and d). As presented,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film with and without  $ \mathrm{NH_4Ac} $  additive both affords a dense film with a good surface coverage on the substrate. A slightly larger average grain size (Figure 1e) is obtained in  $ \mathrm{NH_4Ac} $  added film. Larger grain size means a reduction of grain boundaries, which is beneficial for the transportation and collection of charges. Compared with the pristine perovskite film, obvious morphology change can be seen for the  $ \mathrm{ZnAc_2} $  modified perovskite films. Pinholes/Voids and irregular shaped crystals (extremely large and small crystals) can be seen in the  $ \mathrm{ZnAc_2} $  doped film. There are many nano-sized protrusions with white color (marked by the circles) in the SEM images of the  $ \mathrm{ZnAc_2} $  doped film. XRD patterns have been demonstrated the presence of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , thus we speculated the protrusions are  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  crystals which is consistent with the previous literature[30]. We speculated that the very large crystal grain size is mainly related to Zn which can affect the nucleation and consecutive crystal growth processes, similar as a phenomenon reported by Chen et. al[31] who incorporating Zn to the perovskite precursor. # 4. Conclusion

In summary, we have compared two additives including  $ \mathrm{NH_4Ac} $  and  $ \mathrm{ZnAc_2} $  based on the one step method to obtain high quality perovskite films for the carbon based hole-conductor-free PSCs. By introducing a very small amount (0.08%) of  $ \mathrm{NH_4Ac} $ , we obtained uniform, compactness and pure perovskite layer, indicating that the incorporation of  $ \mathrm{NH_4Ac} $  additive is very effective in improving crystallinity. This perovskite film endowed the carbon based hole-conductor-free PSCs with a much better interfacial contact at the perovskite/carbon interface. What's more, the high

18

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

quality perovskite layer presented lower defect density and lower carrier recombination rate, resulting in enhancing photovoltaic efficiency. The champion device based   $  NH_{4}Ac  $   additive achieved a high PCE of 13.9%, which is obviously higher than those of the devices based on pristine   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   and   $  ZnAc_{2}  $   additive. Although the   $  ZnAc_{2}  $   additive makes the perovskite film own higher optical absorption and endows the device with smaller series resistance, it did not induce a higher efficiency on account of the cracks in the perovskite. Furthermore, the   $  NH_{4}Ac  $   additive based PSC owns better air stability in comparison with the   $  ZnAc_{2}  $   additive based one and the pristine one. Our results indicate that   $  NH_{4}Ac  $   is a more effective additive to promote crystallization of perovskite films fabricated by the one step method and improve the PSCs performance without changing the perovskite composition. With the advantage of low cost, easy fabrication process together with considerable stability and efficiency, our study provides impetus to encourage the commercialization and improve the performance of carbon based PSCs.
