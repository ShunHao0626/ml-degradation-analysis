# Control of the morphology of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  films for efficient perovskite solar cells by strong Lewis base additives

# Abstract

Two-step method is widely used to fabricate high efficient perovskite solar cells. In the typical two-step method,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite are fabricated from   $  PbI_{2}  $   films through intercalation reaction between   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $ , which usually have rough surface and residual   $  PbI_{2}  $   due to compact   $  PbI_{2}  $   structure. Here, we introduce some strong Lewis bases into   $  PbI_{2}/DMF  $   solutions in the first step, which is helpful to control the morphology of   $  PbI_{2}  $   films with mesoporous structure. The mesopores in the   $  PbI_{2}  $   films provide not only space for accommodating the volume expansion during the reaction between   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $ , but also channels for   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   solution to diffuse in the   $  PbI_{2}  $   films, which helps to eliminate the residual   $  PbI_{2}  $   and the dissolution and recrystallization process. As a result, smooth perovskite films without residual   $  PbI_{2}  $   are easily obtained. The power conversion efficiency, stability and reproducibility of the perovskite solar cells fabricated from the optimized mesoporous   $  PbI_{2}  $   films improved simultaneously due to the improvement in perovskite films. This work reveals the key roles of solvents (boiling point, Lewis basicity) in controlling the microstructure of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   films.

# 1. Introduction

Organometallic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted much attentions due to their high power conversion efficiency, low cost, and ease of fabrication.  $  ^{1-3}  $   To achieve highly efficient PSCs, a series of methods, such as one-step method,  $  ^{4}  $   two-step method,  $  ^{5,6}  $   dual-source evaporation,  $  ^{7}  $   and vapor-assisted deposition,  $  ^{8,9}  $   have been developed to fabricate high quality perovskite films. Among these methods, the two-step method is the most used due to high coverage and uniform grains of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) films. In the typical two-step methods, PbI  $  _{2}  $   films are first deposited on substrate by spin-coating from dimethylformamide (DMF) solution followed by annealing, and then exposed to MAI/2-propanol (IPA) solution. The formation of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   through the two-step method can be regarded as intercalation reaction between the parent PbI  $  _{2}  $   crystallites and MAI.  $  ^{10}  $   After the intercalation, the volume of PbI  $  _{2}  $   lattice are expanded about 2.1 times.  $  ^{11}  $   Meanwhile, some small grains might extrude from the surface, leading to rough perovskite films. Obviously, the structure of the parent PbI  $  _{2}  $   films, including the morphology, crystallinity, and density, influence the quality of the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films significantly.

In order to observe the reaction process of perovskite, we deposit   $  PbI_{2}  $   films on the compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   film from different solutions. Some approaches have been proposed to prepare smooth perovskite films without residual   $  PbI_{2}  $  . Wang et al. developed a hybrid deposition method for precisely controlling the flow of MAI and the deposition rate of metal halide. This hybrid deposition method allows growth of perovskite films with smooth surface.  $  ^{15}  $   Zhang et al. fabricated smooth perovskite films through applying stronger air flow on the wet films.  $  ^{16}  $   Kerner et al. obtained ultrasmooth perovskite thin films via sol–gel processing.  $  ^{17}  $   Zhao et al. prepared   $  PbI_{2}  $  -free perovskite films through three-step sequential solution process.  $  ^{18}  $   Zhang et al. obtained smooth perovskite film through suppressing the finally volume expansion ration via using a novel volume-expansion adjustable   $  PbI_{2}\cdot xMAI  $   precursor to replace pure   $  PbI_{2}  $   in the two-step method.  $  ^{19}  $   Recently, we reported a modified two-step method to obtain smooth perovskite films by reducing volume expansion during the molecular exchange via pre-expanding lattice of   $  PbI_{2}  $  .  $  ^{20}  $

Here, we develop a new route to obtain smooth perovskite films without residual   $  PbI_{2}  $   via typical two-step method by fabricating porous   $  PbI_{2}  $   films before the intercalation reaction. The morphology of   $  PbI_{2}  $   films are controlled by introducing some additives with strong Lewis basicity into the   $  PbI_{2}/DMF  $   solutions. The conversion rate of   $  PbI_{2}  $   to   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   is boosted significantly in the porous   $  PbI_{2}  $   films. # 2. Experimental

# Fabrication of the devices

FTO glass (TEC7, Pilkington) were subsequently cleaned with detergent, deionized water, acetone, 2-propanol, ethanol, deionized water, and dried by nitrogen gas. The glass was exposed to a UV ozone cleaner at 150 °C for 15 min before use. A compact layer of TiO₂ was spin coated from titanium isopropoxy solution in ethanol at 2000 rpm for 30 s, and then annealed at 500 °C for 30 min. A mesoporous TiO₂ layer was then made from depositing a diluted TiO₂ paste (Dyesol-18NRT, Dyesol) in ethanol (2:7, weight ratio) on the compact TiO₂ layer by spin-coating at 4500 rpm for 30 s, and then annealed at 500 °C for 30 min. The mesoporous PbI₂ films were made from PbI₂/DMF solution (1.0 M) with different amount of Lewis basicity additive by spin coating at 3000 rpm for 30 s, the film was left to dry at room temperature and then annealed at 70°C for 30 min to remove solvent. After cooling down, the PbI₂ films were dipped into a solution of CH₃NH₃I (MAI) in 2-propanol with a concentration of 10 mg mL⁻¹ for 10 min to prepare perovskite films, and then annealed at 100 °C for 60 min. A hole transport layer was prepared by dissolving 100 mg Spiro-MeOTAD, 40 μL TBP, 36.3 μL TFSI (520 mg/mL in acetonitrile) and 60 L FK102 (300 mg/mL in acetonitrile) in 1 mL chlorobenzene, and deposited by spin-coating at

# Characterization

The   $  PbI_{2}  $   and perovskite films were characterized by field-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM, MERLIN VP Compact), atomic force microscope (AFM5500, Agilent), X-ray diffraction (XRD, D8-Advance), UV/Vis absorption spectrometer (Cary 5000 UV-vis-NIR, Agilent Technologies) in a range from 400 nm to 900 nm. The incident photon-to-current efficiency (IPCE) was characterized by using a QEX10 solar cell quantum efficiency measurement system (QEX10, PV measurements, USA). Current-voltage (J-V) curves of the PSCs were measured by a Keithley 4200-SCS parameter analyzer sweeping from -0.1 to 1.2 V at a scan rate of 5 mV/s, illuminated under a solar simulator (AM 1.5G, 100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , 91195, Newport).

Donor number (DN) is usually used to evaluate the Lewis basicity of a solvent. The larger DN, the stronger Lewis basicity. The DN of binary solution can be modulated by changing the component.  $  ^{21}  $   DMF, N-Methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP), dimethyl sulphoxide (DMSO), and hexamethylphosphoric triamide (HMPA) are typical O-donor Lewis bases strong

Lewis base solvent with DN of 26.6, 27.3, 29.8, and 38.8, respectively.  $  ^{22,23,14-15}  $  The molecule structure of the solvents is shown in FigureS1. In our previous work, we revealed that it forms Lewis adducts of PbI $ _2 $ ·xSol when the PbI $ _2 $  solution is spin-coated on the mesoporous TiO $ _2 $  substrate, $ ^{20} $  where PbI $ _2 $  acts as Lewis acid, solvent molecule acts as Lewis base. Those solvent molecules can coordinate to Pb $ ^{2+} $  through Pb-O bond, and Pb $ ^{2+} $  prefer to coordinate to solvent with stronger coordinating ability in binary solvents. $ ^{24} $  The strength of the Pb-O bond increases as the DN increasing of additive. $ ^{25,26} $  The Lewis adducts of PbI $ _2 $ ·xSol convert to PbI $ _2 $  by removing solvent molecule via annealing treatment. Thus, the morphology of the PbI $ _2 $  films depend on the Lewis basicity of the solutions.

We first optimize the concentration of these strong Lewis base additives in the   $  PbI_{2}/DMF  $   solution to obtain continuous and mesoporous   $  PbI_{2}  $   films. The optimized concentration of the strong Lewis base is 30%, 20%, 5% for NMP, DMSO, HMPA, respectively. Figure 1 shows top-viewed and cross-sectional SEM images of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   films fabricated from the solutions with optimized concentrations of Lewis base additives. The   $  PbI_{2}  $   film fabricated from the pure DMF solution exhibits densely packed structure (Figure 1a). The cross-sectional SEM image in Figure 1b further confirms the compact structure of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film fabricated from the pure DMF solution. The compact structure restrains MAI to diffuse into the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film, leading to some residual   $  PbI_{2}  $   (Figure 2a). By adding some strong Lewis base additives in the   $  PbI_{2}/DMF  $   solutions, the compact   $  PbI_{2}  $   films transform to loose ones with mesoporous structure. The size and density of

the pores in the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film depend on the Lewis basicity of the additives. With increasing Lewis basicity of additives, both of the pore size and density increase evidently (Figure 1c to 1h). The boiling point of the DMF, NMP, DMSO, and HMPA are 152, 202, 189, 235 °C, respectively.  $  ^{[27, 28]}  $   The strong Lewis base additives have higher boiling points than DMF to reduce the evaporation of the solvent during the spinning process. Furthermore, the strength of Pb-O bonds in Lewis adduct increase as increasing of Lewis basicity of the solvent. Therefore, the higher boiling point and stronger Pb-O in the Lewis adducts retard the escape of solvent from   $  PbI_{2}  $  -based Lewis adduct (  $  PbI_{2}\cdot xSol  $  ) films.  $  ^{[25]}  $   The residual solvent molecule will be released when the   $  PbI_{2}\cdot xSol  $   are annealed at high temperature. the release of amount solvent molecule is the mechanism behind the appearance of the pores in   $  PbI_{2}  $   film.

Figure 4 shows SEM and AFM images of the MAPbI $ _3 $  films fabricated from different Lewis basicity solutions. The perovskite films fully cover the TiO $ _2 $  substrate for the samples prepared from pure DMF, 30% NMP, and 20% DMSO (see Figure 4a, 4c, 4e). However, there are many holes in the perovskite film fabricated from the HMPA solution (see Figure 4g). The high coverage of the MAPbI $ _3 $  film are ascribed to two times volume expansion of PbI $ _2 $  during the intercalation reaction. $ ^{11} $  However, if the PbI $ _2 $  film contains large pore size or has high pore density (see Figure 1g), it might lead to inhomogeneous and even discontinuous perovskite film (see Figure 4g). The roughness of the perovskite films is characterized by AFM. The average roughness (Ra) are 40.1, 27.4, 24.5, 47.2 nm for the perovskite films fabricated from pure DMF, 30%NMP, 20%DMSO, 5% HMPA, respectively. Perovskite films with smooth surface are desired for high efficient PSCs. As mentioned above, the reaction between PbI $ _2 $  and MAI is an intercalation reaction. During the intercalation of MAI into the PbI $ _2 $ , the volume expansion about 2.1 times. $ ^{11} $  Meanwhile, some small grains extrude to the surface due to the expansion, which increases the roughness. Furthermore, the reaction between PbI $ _2 $  and MAI is also a solid-liquid interfacial reaction. $ ^{12} $  The MAPbI $ _3 $  formed at the surface of the densely packed PbI $ _2 $  films, which suppresses the interfacial reaction. As a result, I $ ^- $  ions accumulate at the surface of perovskite film. The excess amount I $ ^- $  ions can provide the driving force that dissolve the initial formed MAPbI $ _3 $  and un-reacted PbI $ _2 $  to form

$ \mathrm{PbI}_4^{2-} $  complex in iodine rich environment through dissolution and recrystallization growth mechanism  $ ^{12} $  as the following formula:

$$
\mathrm{CH} _ {3} \mathrm{NH} _ {3} \mathrm{PbI} _ {3} (\mathrm{s}) + \mathrm{I} ^ {-} (\mathrm{sol}) \rightarrow \mathrm{CH} _ {3} \mathrm{NH} _ {3} ^ {+} (\mathrm{sol}) + \mathrm{PbI} _ {4} ^ {2 -} (\mathrm{sol})
$$

$$
\mathrm{PbI} _ {2} (\mathrm{s}) + \mathrm{I} ^ {-} (\mathrm{sol}) \rightarrow \mathrm{PbI} _ {4} ^ {2 -} (\mathrm{sol})
$$

The  $ \mathrm{PbI}_4^{2-} $  ions can react with  $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $  ions to grow cub-like  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  on the surface by the following reaction

$$
\mathrm{PbI} _ {4} ^ {2 -} (\text {sol}) + \mathrm{CH} _ {3} \mathrm{NH} _ {3} ^ {+} (\text {sol}) \rightarrow \mathrm{CH} _ {3} \mathrm{NH} _ {3} \mathrm{PbI} _ {3} (s) + \mathrm{I} ^ {-} (\text {sol})
$$

The cub-like  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  grains (see light dots in Figure 4a and 4b) also increase the roughness of the perovskite film fabricated from compact  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film. This phenomenon becomes more obvious by prolonging reaction time or increasing the concentration of MAI (See Figure S3). Therefore, the smooth perovskite film fabricated from  $ 20\% $  DMSO additive film can be ascribed to dual-functional of the pores in the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film. One hand, the pores provide space for accommodating the for lattice expansion during conversion from  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  to  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ . On the other hand, the pore provides channels for  $ \mathrm{I}^- $  ions, which is responsible for increase conversion rate, and the elimination of dissolution and recrystallization process.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, mesoporous  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  films with controllable pore size and density are prepared by introducing some strong Lewis bases in the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2/\mathrm{DMF} $  solutions.

The mesoporous  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  films convert to perovskite easier and faster than the compact one. The pores in the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  films provide not only channels for the penetration of MAI, but also space to accommodate volume expansion during the intercalation reaction. Therefore, smooth perovskite films without residual  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  are easily obtained by adding suitable strong Lewis bases in the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2/\mathrm{DMF} $  solution. The perovskite solar cells fabricated from the mesoporous  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  films exhibit improvement in power conversion efficiency, reproducibility and stability.
