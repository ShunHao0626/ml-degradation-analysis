# Abstract

Regardless of the excellent improvement in the assembling of perovskite solar cells (PSCs), the photon-harvesting performance of these devices is inadequate through the disproportionate recombination of generated charge carriers. The improvement of the charge carrier mobility can significantly reduce the recombination and help the perovskite devices reach the theoretical power conversion efficiency (PCE). The modification of charge selective contacts is one of the most effective approaches for reducing the carrier recombination. Herein, we introduce a facile and effective doping engineering approach based on graphene quantum dots (GQDs) for the modification of  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 / ZnO} $  bilayer electron transport layer (ETL). A comparative evaluation of perovskite films deposited on  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 / ZnO} $  layers with altered concentrations of GQDs employing to significantly enhance the opto-electronic properties. The integration of GQDs into ETL indicates a potential for improving the charge carrier transporting in PSCs. Overall, PSC employing  $ 4\% $  GQDs modified ETL yields a PCE of  $ 19.81\% $  with a striking open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ ) of 1.17 V. Besides,  $ 4\% $  GQDs modified ETL-based devices enhance the long-term ambient and thermal stability.

# Introduction

Based on the auspicious features of the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials, including their tremendous optical and electronic features, for instance, broad spectral absorption, long charge lifespan, intrinsic low trap density, and solution processability, they deliberated as a talented applicant for photovoltaic applications [1-8]. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has augmented intensely from  $ 3\% $  to over  $ 25.5\% $  [9-11]. It was noted that the thermally unstable and volatile nature of the organic cations in the perovskite structure, despite their excellent results, is a major anxiety for large-scale commercialization of perovskite photovoltaics [12, 13]. Different attempts have been applied to minimize the energy loss in the assembled cells including adapted crystallization, surface passivation, doping, as well as architecture engineering [14, 15]. Incorporating two-dimensional (2D) layered perovskites into 3D hybrid perovskites is a viable strategy for enhancing stability while maintaining high performance [16, 17]. The 2D fragment appears to act as an impurity, obstructing electron transport and increasing electron recombination. Inorganic cesium  $ (\mathrm{Cs}^{+}) $  perovskites have acquired tremendous attention because of their increased thermal stability features [18]. The addition of formamidinium (FA) and/or methylammonium (MA) to inorganic  $ \mathrm{CsPbX_3} $ -based  $ (\mathrm{X} = \mathrm{I}^{-}, \mathrm{Br}^{-}, \mathrm{Cl}^{-}) $  perovskite absorbers can progress crystallinity and the stability of the fabricated layers [19-21]. Recently, a 2D/3D heterostructure with butylammonium incorporation in the caesium-formamidinium lead halide perovskite is introduced to improve the PCE and increase the stability of the fabricated devices [22].

The creation of suitable charge-selective contacts, along with engineering the perovskite layer, is considered another important approach in defining system features for the reason that photo-induced carriers must move through the interface before reaching the electrodes. Numerous n-type metal oxide materials may be applied as an electron transport layer (ETL) in PSCs, with

zinc oxide (ZnO), titanium dioxide  $ (\mathrm{TiO}_2) $ , tin dioxide  $ (\mathrm{SnO}_2) $ , and tungsten trioxide  $ (\mathrm{WO}_3) $ . Academic studies on  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  and ZnO ETLs for perovskite photovoltaics have been widely published [23, 24]. Aside from the traditional ETLs, a lot of work has gone into forming novel ETL materials [25-27]. For instance, Seok and colleagues reached a PCE above  $ 21\% $  in PSCs by using  $ \mathrm{BaSnO}_3 $  as an ETL, but  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  remained at only  $ 1.12\mathrm{V} $  [28]. Very recently, planar PSCs with the efficiency of  $ 19.3\% $  have been reported with zinc stannate  $ (\mathrm{Zn}_2\mathrm{SnO}_4) $  ETL material, presenting a  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of only  $ 1.09\mathrm{V} $  [27]. The quality of  $ \mathrm{Zn}_2\mathrm{SnO}_4 $  layer is enhanced via a chemical bath post-treatment. The treated ETL promoted a high PCE of  $ 21\% $  because of suppressed defects and a conformal development of perovskite. Furthermore, graphene-based materials are known as game changer in perovskite photovoltaics, in particular, may gain conductivity and align the band position when used as a dopant or interlayer in different configurations of PSCs [24].

Herein, we fabricated PSCs based on mixed   $  Cs_{0.05}(FA_{0.95}MA_{0.05})_{0.95}\mathrm{Pb(I_{0.95}Br_{0.05})_{3}/BABr}  $   3D/2D perovskite and using   $  SnO_{2}/ZnO  $   bilayer film modified with graphene quantum dots (GQDs) additives. These 3D/2D perovskite materials fabrication methodologies provide a humble and collaborative settling for effective as well as air-stable perovskite film and system assembling. The incorporation of different concentrations of GQDs (2, 4, and 6%) additives to the ETLs displayed a role in reducing the trapped-defect density, improving the carrier lifetime, and increasing the built-in potential, resulting in a high-quality ETL and help in modifying the ETL/perovskite interface to form high-efficiency PSC with outstanding stability. The acquired results demonstrate that the devices with 4% GQDs doped   $  SnO_{2}/ZnO  $   bilayer ETLs show the best photovoltaic parameters as PSC reveal a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.172 V, a   $  J_{SC}  $   of 22.85 mA/cm², an FF of 74%, and a PCE of 19.81%. Moreover, the corresponding devices retained ~82% of their original performance after aging in ambient conditions for 56 days.

# Results and Discussion

The schematic structure and energy level diagram of fabricated PSCs are demonstrated in Figure 1. The configuration of the fabricated device in this study is as follows: glass/FTO/GQDs doped  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 / ZnO} $  ETL/perovskite/BABr/spiro-OMeTAD/Au (Figure 1a). Moreover, the schematic of the layer-by-layer assembled device process is revealed in Figure S1, in the supporting information (SI). Besides, the potential-energy level diagram of each layer in the cell, as well as the possible transport of photo-generated carriers, electrons  $ (\mathrm{e}^{-}) $  and holes  $ (\mathrm{h}^{+}) $ , are shown in Figure 1b. The carrier separation in the perovskite film is found to be possible through a good match between band energies [29].

The photovoltaic features in addition to the stability of the mixed perovskite/BABr based PSCs were improved through modification of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  bilayer film with GQDs additives. To this end, first, the impact of the thickness of the ZnO interlayer on PSC performance was investigated to optimize our proposed strategy. After that, mixed 3D/2D PSCs were synthesized via a two-step sequential deposition approach (see the Experimental part in the SI). Furthermore, GQDs have a size of  $ < 5\mathrm{nm} $  in each dimension, indicating that they are zero-dimensional material, according to the high-resolution TEM image (Figure S2a, SI). Besides, the GQDs are evenly spaced and have normal spherical dots. The Raman spectrum of the GQDs is also shown in Figure S2b, SI, with the Raman bands (D-band at  $ 1335.2~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $  and G-band at  $ 1582.4~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ ) indicating the crystal nature of the prepared GQDs [1]. In addition, GQDs show a wide photoluminescence (PL) peak at  $ 501~\mathrm{nm} $ , as shown in Figure S1c, SI. The absorption pattern of GQDs (Figure S2d, SI) shows two bands at 245 and  $ 332~\mathrm{nm} $ , which may be due to an electronic transition of GQDs [30]. Evidently, the desired GQDs have been synthesized with suitable features that allow them to be used as dopant materials for the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  bilayer.

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) was employed to assess the possessions of diverse   $  SnO_{2}  $   layers on the morphology of the perovskite layers, Figure 3a-c. Besides, the perovskite layer deposited on the pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   (Figure 3a) showed small grains with many grain boundaries on the surface. The root mean square (RMS) values corresponding to the perovskite layer deposited on the pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   were ~15.0 nm. The AFM image (Figure 3b,c) of perovskite layer deposited on the   $  SnO_{2}/ZnO  $   and 4% GQD-doped   $  SnO_{2}/ZnO  $   ETL showed larger grains on the surface with RMS of ~13.4 nm and ~9.8 nm, respectively. Such a smooth film brings a more suitable contact between perovskite and HTL films, indicating the enhanced hole transport processes at the 3D/2D perovskite/HTL interfaces [21]. The grain boundaries disappeared for the perovskite layer prepared on the 4% GQD-doped   $  SnO_{2}/ZnO  $   ETL. An optimized amount of GQDs provide to better crystallized perovskite film. The reason is that GQDs seize oxygen groups like -OH and C=O, which can produce strong bonds with the perovskite, ensuring a closer connection with perovskite crystals and better crystallization [1]. The results showed that the intensity of the characteristic perovskite (110) and (220) peaks increase as perovskite films deposited on the modified   $  SnO_{2}  $ . The formation of perovskite crystallization contains two steps: nucleation and crystal growth. With GQDs can serve as the heterogeneous nucleation centers of perovskite. Moreover, we investigated the stability of the 3D/2D mixed devices based on   $  SnO_{2}/ZnO  $   bilayer with and without GQDs additives. The PSCs without any encapsulation were maintained in the ambient environment with a relative humidity (RH) of 30–40%. Four representative PSCs were used from different batches of the respective triple-cation perovskite devices with J-V plots recorded at various time intervals. Figure 5a shows a set of normalized PCEs as a function of aging time. The GQDs modified PSCs revealed excellent PCE over the whole test period and retained 82% of the original PCE after 56 days, while the PCE of the untreated PSCs gradually reduced to 40%. Besides, the thermal stability of unencapsulation PSCs was assessed at 80 °C with ambient humidity (Figure 5b). The GQDs treated devices have better thermal stability than other pristine devices, maintain 88% of the initial PCE after 30 days, while the pristine PSCs quickly dropped to 67% of its initial PCE. # Conclusions

We have investigated the effects of GQDs additives to the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 / ZnO} $  bilayer on the performance of the PSCs. After determining the optimal ZnO thickness using ALD technique, we evaluated the surface properties of the perovskite films before and after GQDs modification. The addition of  $ 4\% $  GQDs to the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 / ZnO} $  ETL improved the surface morphology as well as the crystallinity of the fabricated perovskite layers. PL intensity significantly reduced for the perovskite layer deposited on the ETL with  $ 4\% $  GQD, suggesting the lower charge recombination. The improvements of the perovskite film manifested in better photovoltaic parameters, where the PCE increased from  $ 17.50\% $  to  $ 19.81\% $  for the GQD-modified ETL-based device. From the stability point of view, it was observed that PSCs deposited on the GQD-modified ETL were stable over time, preserving  $ 82\% $  of its initial performance after 56 days in ambient conditions. This work introduces an effective method for reducing the carrier recombination by incorporating GQDs into  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 / ZnO} $  ETL and thus a facile modification technique to fabricate PSCs close to the theoretical performance.
