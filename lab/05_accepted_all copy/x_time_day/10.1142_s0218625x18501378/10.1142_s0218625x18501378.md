# THE ROLE OF Br AS DOPANT ON THE STRUCTURAL AND CHARGE TRANSPORT PROPERTIES IN  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x - y}\mathrm{Br}_x\mathrm{Cl}_y $  MIXED-HALIDE PEROVSKITE FOR HYBRID SOLAR CELLS

JINFENG WANG, BENGUANG ZHAO, LEI ZHU* and JIAN SONG† School of Materials Science and Engineering, China University of Mining and Technology, Xuzhou, Jiangsu 221116, P. R. China *lzhu@cumt.edu.cn †jsoong@cumt.edu.cn

Received 25 September 2017  
Revised 12 December 2017  
Accepted 19 December 2017  
Published 8 February 2018

A series of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x-y}Br_{x}Cl_{y}  $   mixed-halide perovskite were fabricated as light harvesters for organic–inorganic planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells (PSCs). This paper aimed at investigating the morphology and structural properties of Br doped mixed-halide perovskites and the influence on the corresponding photovoltaic performances. We found that Br incorporation in an I/Cl-based structure dramatically improved the charge transport within the perovskite layer. The average efficiency of the planar device was 13.6% with a minimal standard deviation of   $  \pm1.53\%  $   and best efficiency as high as 15.13% was achieved. Moreover, the device showed superior stability over 20 days with little degradation   $  <9\%  $   when stored under ambient conditions, indicating the outstanding performances of planar heterojunction solar cells based on this material. The results highlight the crucial role of the Br doping on the performance of the PSCs and pave the way for further progress on this field.

Keywords: Perovskite solar cell; doping; Br; photovoltaic performances; stability.

# 1. Introduction

Organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite materials   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbX_{3}  $   (X = Cl, Br, I) have garnered enormous attention as highly promising semiconductors for thin-film photovoltaic applications due to their unique photo-electronic properties as excellent absorbers: high absorption coefficient, high charge carrier mobility, long charge-carrier lifetime, long charge carrier diffusion length and apparent tolerance of defects.  $  ^{1-3}  $   Since the first report of 3.8% by

Miyasaka in 2009, there has been tremendous progress in achieving high performance perovskite solar cells (PSCs) of  $ \sim 22.1\% $  over the past eight years, showing that the PSCs have the potential to compete with the traditional silicon solar cells. Besides their low cost and ease of fabrication, perovskite materials offer a wide tunable ability on composition by adjusting the mixed-halide anion.

$ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{Pb(I,Cl)}_3 $  has been wildly investigated as an absorber, including I and Cl in mixed-halide PSCs. Stranks et al. found that the diffusion lengths were

In this paper, on the basis of our previous research, we report a detailed investigation on the role of Br as dopant on the charge transport and structural properties of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Pb(I,Br,Cl)_{3}  $   ternary mixed perovskites. Owing to the self-assembling of halide elements in perovskite, independent of the components' ratio in the precursor solution, the chemical formula of the mixed-halide perovskite can be identified as   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x-y}Br_{x}Cl_{y}  $  . The cells fabricated with   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x-y}Br_{x}Cl_{y}  $   with x=0.1 performed better than the other compositions, and the partial substitution of I with Br also led to an improved efficiency with a maximum power conversion efficiency of 15.13% under AM 1.5G illumination of 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $   intensity. The J-V hysteretic behavior was investigated and proper addition of the Br element in the perovskite film could effectively suppress the hysteresis. The stability test indicated that the PCE of the device with Br doped content x=0.1 was retained up to 91% after exposing in dry air for 20 days at room temperature.

# 2. Experimental Section

# 2.1.  $ CH_{3}NH_{3} $  I synthesis

$ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  (MAI) was synthesized by mixing the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_2 $  solution (33 wt.% in absolute ethanol, J&K)

and the HI solution (57 wt.% in water with 1.5% hypophosphorous acid, J&K) with 1:1 molar ratio in a round-bottom flask at 0°C for 2 h under stirring. The solvent was evaporated and the precipitate was washed with diethyl ether. The precipitate was dried at 60°C for 24 h in a vacuum oven. Synthesis method is reported in the literature.  $  ^{10}  $

# 2.2. Device fabrication

Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO, Pilkington, TEC 15) glass substrate was partially etched with Zn powder and 2 M HCl to pattern the electrode. Then, the substrate was cleaned with deionized water, ethanol, isopropanol and absolute ethyl alcohol for 20 min, respectively. As in our early report, a 50 nm thick dense blocking   $  TiO_{2}  $   was deposited via the spray pyrolysis method, followed by a heating process at 450°C for 60 min according to the literature.  $  ^{[11]}  $   The perovskite layer was deposited by the one-step method reported in the literature.  $  ^{[9]}  $   The mixed-halide perovskite   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x-y}Br_{x}Cl_{y}  $   solutions were prepared by mixing the synthesized   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   powder,   $  PbCl_{2}  $   (Aldrich) and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   (Aldrich) with corresponding amount in 0.5 mL N, N-Dimethylformamide (DMF) respectively. The specific quality of various ingredients was shown in Table S1. After that, the mixed solution was stirred and heated for 2 h at 75°C in the glove box. The solution was then coated onto the (cp)   $  TiO_{2}/FTO  $   substrate by spin coating at 2000 rpm for 60 s and then dried on a hot plate at 60°C for 10 min, 80°C for 10 min, 100°C for 50 min in sequence. The hole transport material solution was prepared by mixing spiro-OMeTAD powder (72.3 mg) with 17.5 μL Li-bis (trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI), 28.8 μL 4-tert-butylpyridine (4-TBP) and 1 mL chlorobenzene. The hole transporting layer (HTL) was deposited using the above solution by spin coating at 3000 rpm for 30 sec. Finally, 50 nm thick Ag was thermally evaporated on top of the HTL to form the back contact. The active area of device was 10 mm² determined by a black mask with dimensions of 2 mm × 5 mm.

To find out the variation of optical properties in the alloyed hybrid perovskite, we measured the UV–Vis absorption spectra of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x-y}Br_{x}Cl_{y}  $   as shown in Fig. 4(a). It should be noted that this blue-shift to higher photon energy originates from the differing spin orbital coupling between Pb–I and Pb–Br as well as a simultaneous structural transition.  $  ^{17}  $   The energy band gap value was obtained from the intersection of the linear extrapolation of the edge of the absorption band and the horizontal axis. As the Br content increased from x = 0 to x = 0.2, the energy band gap value improved from 1.56 eV to 1.62 eV and the monotonically increasing relationship also was showed from Fig. 4(b). The range of energy band gap result is consistent with the literature.  $  ^{8}  $

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we report a facile halide exchange route with one-step spin-coated method based on planar structure, and we gain   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x-y}Br_{x}Cl_{y}  $   film with simultaneously tunable morphologies and compositions. The PCE of the planar structure PSCs with the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x-y}Br_{x}Cl_{y}  $   films can be optimized to 15.13% and have a higher   $  V_{oc}  $   than that of the device with the parent   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film. We found that the incorporation of a small amount of Br in   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Pb(I, Cl)_{3}  $   film would have a significant effect on the morphology and performance of the perovskite film. For planar structure used by us, the optimized additional content of Br is x=0.1 (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x-y}Br_{x}Cl_{y}, x=0.1  $  ). These series of researches will enable further improvement of the current family of perovskite materials for future practical applications with excellent perspectives.
