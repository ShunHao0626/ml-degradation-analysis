# Dopant-Free F-substituted Benzodithiophene Copolymer Hole-Transporting Materials for Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Xiangyu Kong  $  ^{a}  $ , Yue Jiang  $  ^{*a,f}  $ , Xiayan Wu  $  ^{a}  $ , Cong Chen  $  ^{a}  $ , Jiali Guo  $  ^{a}  $ , Shengjian Liu  $  ^{*b}  $ , Xingsen Gao  $  ^{a}  $ , Guofu Zhou  $  ^{c}  $ , Jun-Ming Liu  $  ^{a,d}  $ , Krzysztof Kempa  $  ^{a,e}  $ , and Jinwei Gao  $  ^{*a}  $

Polymeric hole transporting materials (HTMs), with low production cost and versatile wet processing procedures have been important choice for efficient and stable perovskite solar cells. In this paper, three F-substituted Benzodithiophene copolymers are introduced into perovskite solar cells as dopant-free efficient HTMs. Among, perovskite solar cells based on dopant-free PBDT[2F]T have achieved a comparable power conversion efficiency (PCE) (17.52%) to that of the well-known p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD (~20.2%). More importantly, the solar cells with pristine PBDT[2F]T HTMs present impressive stability in contrast to that with the doped spiro-OMeTAD as a HTM in ambient atmosphere and even in high humidity. A detailed study shows that the dopant-free PBDT[2F]T HTM presents an exceptional ability to extract holes from perovskite layer, simultaneously the capability to reduce trap-assisted recombination at the interface of perovskite / PBDT[2F]T. These findings pave the way in developing dopant-free polymeric HTMs for cost-effective and stable perovskite solar cells.

# Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs), as one kind of the most promising alternative energy sources have attracted widespread research attentions due to their simplicity, low-cost and fast-growing power conversion efficiency (PCE).  $  ^{1-5}  $   The PCEs of PSCs were enhanced from 3.8% in 2009 to recent, a certificated 25.2%,  $  ^{6}  $   which is mainly due to the great efforts on the device structure optimization and new material exploration. In the state-of-the-art PSCs, the light-absorbing perovskite layer is sandwiched between an electron transporting material (ETM) and a hole transporting material (HTM). Particularly, the HTM plays a key role in extracting and transporting the photogenerated holes from perovskite to contact electrodes as well as in suppressing charge recommendation.  $  ^{7,8}  $   Although the HTM-free PSCs have been proposed, their PCEs are far below those of HTM PSCs, suggestive of the indispensability of hole transporting materials for high-efficiency PSCs.  $  ^{9,10}  $

A wide range of HTMs, including organic and inorganic materials have been studied recently.  $  ^{[11-17]}  $   Among those, 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N'-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) is the most commonly used and has been well commercialized.  $  ^{[18]}  $   However, due to the rather tedious procedures of synthesis and purification of spiro-OMeTAD, the production cost is quite expensive.  $  ^{[19]}  $   Moreover, to enhance the electrical conductivity and hole mobility, pristine spiro-OMeTAD requires additional dopants,  $  ^{[20]}  $   such as bis(trifluoromethane) sulfonimide lithium (LiTFSI), which could adversely accelerate the device degradation due to its high hygroscopicity and migration of Li  $  ^{+}  $   into the perovskite layer.  $  ^{[18,21-23]}  $

Aiming to bypass these drawbacks, polymeric and small molecular HTMs have been well utilized in perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{11,24}  $   In respect of some small molecules, the dopant-free HTMs have recently been reported with a comparable PCE and stability to that with doped spiro-OMeTAD HTM.  $  ^{25-29}  $   Regarding to polymers, however, they have been much less explored than small molecules as HTM in perovskite solar cells, albeit they offer excellent properties, such as cost effectiveness, thermal stability, etc., and most of them gave superb performance in OPVs and OLEDs.  $  ^{30}  $   One of the best known polymeric HTMs is poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)-amine] (PTAA). Even though PTAA gives the PCE up to 22.1%  $  ^{31}  $   and shows improved stability at higher temperatures, the high cost and dopant requirement obviously limit the scale-up and long-term stability.  $  ^{32}  $   Simultaneously, mostly reported PCEs based on pristine polymer HTMs were at a low level,  $  ^{31,33,34}  $   only a few examples up to 19%,  $  ^{35,36}  $

The fluorination of   $  \pi  $  -conjugated polymer backbones has been proven a particularly effective strategy in the design of p-type and high hole mobility polymers. A broader consensus emphasizes that F-substituted p-type polymers are prone to adopt planar backbone conformations, favorable electronic coupling patterns, and preferential polymer backbone face-on orientation with respect to substrate which are the essential properties for efficient polymer HTM. Previously, Beaujuge et al. has reported a set of wide-bandgap polymers composed of benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']dithiophene (BDT), and thienyl ([2H]T) or 3,4-difluorothiophene ([2F]T) motifs as donor materials for polymer solar cells with PCEs more than 9.8%.  $  ^{37,38}  $   These F-substituted polymers PBDT[2F]T and PBDT(T)[2F]T present many desirable merits, for example, the appropriate HOMO energy levels of 5.29 eV and LUMO energy level of 3.19 eV, high hole mobility of   $  (9.2\pm0.4)\times10^{-6}\mathrm{cm}^{2}\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1}  $   and good film formability, thus, may be a good HTM candidate and could be examined in perovskite solar cells.

In this study, we explore those materials as alternative HTMs for high-efficiency and stable perovskite solar cells. We used three pristine, dopant-free copolymers, PBDT[2F]T, PBDT[2H]T and PBDT(T)[2F]T (structures in Fig. 1a) as HTMs and fabricated the n-i-p type planar PSCs. Among, PBDT[2F]T as HTM delivered a decent  $ PCE $  of  $ 17.52\% $ . Systematic studies show that PBDT[2F]T, as an HTM exhibits an excellent ability in extracting holes, simultaneously passivating surface traps, making it a good competitor of doped spiro-OMeTAD. In addition, the hydrophobic nature of PBDT[2F]T renders the corresponding PSCs a better ambient stability ( $ 91.34\% $  after 16 days),

compared with device based on doped spiro-OMeTAD (64.87% after 16 days).

Further, the polymer chain packing of three HTMs were investigated by grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS), as illustrated in Fig. 4. The PBDT[2F]T and PBDT(T)[2F]T films exhibited (010) scattering signals in the out-of-plane (OOP) direction while no obvious signal could be observed in PBDT[2H]T film, suggesting their better π-π stacking as well as the higher crystallinity. Specifically, the signals of PBDT[2F]T is the strongest, demonstrating the prominently favorable face-on orientation of polymer chains.  $  ^{11,40,41}  $   Hence, despite the obtained hole mobility of PBDT[2F]T is lower than that of PBDT(T)[2F]T,  $  ^{42}  $   PBDT[2F]T favors the effective hole transportation in the vertical direction, resulting in the higher FF and PCE (Fig. 2a).

The hole extraction properties of three HTMs were studied with steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (PL and TRPL) spectra of FTO/perovskite/HTMs. The PL peaks at 770 nm is originated from   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , which were prominently quenched upon coating HTMs on top (Fig. 5a). Notably, with PBDT[2F]T and spiro-OMeTAD, the PL intensity is essentially undetectable, suggesting the excellent hole extracting capability of the dopant-free PBDT[2F]T.  $  ^{43}  $   On the other hand, the emission lifetime of different HTMs atop perovskite/glass was fitted by two-exponential decay model from TRPL spectra (Fig. 5b).  $  ^{20,44}  $   The fast decay time   $  \tau_{1}  $   stands for the trap-assisted recombination and equals to 2.29, 2.38, 2.97 and 3.18 ns for devices with spiro-OMeTAD, PBDT[2F]T, PBDT[2H]T and PBDT(T)[2F]T, respectively (Table S3 in Supporting Information). Therefore, the smaller   $  \tau_{1}  $   of device with PBDT[2F]T demonstrated a reduced trap-assisted recombination.

The charge transfer properties of three HTMs were further confirmed with the electrical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) of the corresponding PSCs under illumination. Nyquist plots in Fig. 5c show a semi-circle at high frequency region corresponding to the transfer resistance  $ (R_{tr}) $ . The smaller  $ R_{tr} $  of PBDT[2F]T (147  $ \Omega $ ) than those of PBDT(T)[2F]T (204  $ \Omega $ ) and PBDT[2H]T (281  $ \Omega $ ) based devices also declares the efficient charge transfer process at perovskite/PBDT[2F]T interface.

Fig. 5d shows the relationship between   $  V_{OC}  $   and light intensity with three HTMs. According to the formula:   $  V_{OC} = n k T \ln(I)/q + C  $  , in which C is a constant, k represents the Boltzmann constant, T is absolute temperature, q is the elementary charge, n is an ideal factor related to monomolecular recombination.  $  ^{45,46}  $   The ideal factors n were determined to be   $  \sim 1.25  $  , 1.29, 1.54 for PBDT[2F]T, PBDT(T)[2F]T and PBDT[2H]T, respectively. It has been suggested that smaller ideal factor means less trap-assisted recombination involved in the device operation.  $  ^{36}  $   Therefore, the trap-assisted recombination in perovskite/PBDT[2F]T HTM has largely been suppressed compared with the other two cells.

# Conclusions

In summary, we have explored a series of dopant-free polymeric HTMs, PBDT[2F]T, PBDT[2H]T and PBDT(T)[2F]T, which were utilized to fabricate the high-performance PSCs. The best perovskite solar cell with PBDT[2F]T HTM showed a decent efficiency of  $ 17.52\% $ , higher than those of devices based on the

other two polymers, and comparable with that of cells with the doped spiro-OMeTAD. This enhanced photovoltaic performance is mainly attributed to the introduction of PBDT[2F]T HTM with higher hole mobility, favorable orientation and better morphology, further resulting in the efficient hole extraction process, as well as the reduced trap-assisted recombination in perovskite/PBDT[2F]T interfaces. Moreover, due to the hydrophobic nature of PBDT[2F]T, the PSCs exhibited an excellent long-term stability, even in high humidity environment, presenting its great practical potential as cost-effective, dopant-free HTMs for stable PSCs.
