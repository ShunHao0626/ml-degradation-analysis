# Abstract

Nickle oxide (NiO) with excellent stability and favorable energy band are desirable candidate for hole selective contact (HSC) of inverted perovskite solar cell (PSC). However, current works focused on addressing interfacial issues, which would be induced by the poor NiO/perovskite contact or other defects, are scarce. Herein, a facile one-step hydrothermal strategy is demonstrated to develop 3D NiO nanowall (NW) film as promising HSC. Such newly-designed NiO NWs HSC exhibits robust, homogenous mesoporous network structure, which would improve NiO/perovskite interface contact, passivate the interfacial defect and promote the quality of perovskite film. Hence, the optimized interface features enable the power conversion efficiency (PCEs) approaching ~18%. Moreover, the additional diethanolamine (DEA) interlayer was introduced to further passivate the intrinsic defect of NiO surface, thereby fabricating the better charge transfer with suppressed recombination loss. As a result, the champion PCE of NiO NWs/DEA based device was boosted up to 19.16% with high open-circuit voltage (~1.11 V) and fill factor (> 80%), which is prominent in MAPbI₃-based inverted PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are emerging as new generation of photovoltaic device due to their remarkable photoelectric characteristics and excellent power conversion efficiency (PCE). [1-5] Among the developed device architectures, the inverted p-i-n PSCs attracts widespread attention, which benefits from the advantages of facile preparation process and negligible hysteresis. [6, 7] As the indispensable components of inverted PSCs, hole selective contacts (HSCs) play an important role in the rapid hole extraction, critical for device performance. To date, the most popular hole transporting material (HTM) is poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS). However, the disadvantages of high cost and undesirable device stability are inevitable for such PEDOT:PSS based device. [8-10] Therefore, numerous efforts have been devoted to developing p-type inorganic materials as a candidate for stable, efficient HTMs.

Typically, solution-based NiO nanocrystal (NC) has been adopted as the suitable HTMs with merits of high transmittance, favorable bandgap and energy level alignment.  $  ^{[11-15]}  $   And such NiO-based devices exhibit excellent long-term moisture/thermal stability.  $  ^{[16-18]}  $   However, to our best of knowledge, the majority of current work mainly focuses on doping NiO to further improve its intrinsic conductivity for better device performance.  $  ^{[19-23]}  $   As previously reported, this type of solution-based NCs are prone to aggregate and lead to rough/discontinuous film morphology, which would further create the imperfect NiO/perovskite interface. The induced interfacial problems, such as poor contact at HTL/perovskite interface or unsatisfactory

interfacial charge kinetics interfered by defects, would significantly degrade the device performance. [24-27] Hence, the significance of interface engineering, which could passivate interfacial defect and improve recombination behavior, is getting increasing awareness. Nevertheless, the interface engineering was widely investigated in conventional n-i-p device, while only a few works has been done for the inverted PSCs. And the current strategies tend to reduce the negative effects of poor interfacial contact by introducing modification layer to enhance NiO/perovskite interface stability and charge transfer ability. [28-30] For instance, Zhang et al. employed 5-aminovaleric acid (5-AVA) to modify the NiO film, which effectively passivates the intrinsic surface defect of NiO and even improve the deposited perovskite film quality. As a result, these improved interface properties contribute to the decreased interfacial energy loss. [29, 31] Despite the considerable results, the interfacial contact problem induced by poor quality of NiO film is still to be addressed. Therefore, it is of great significance to develop additional strategies to optimize NiO film quality and related NiO/perovskite interface characteristics.

As far as we know, morphology of selective contact is critical for the charge transfer and interfacial properties of device. Specially, available evidences suggest that the mesoporous oxide film could be served as the promising passivation/active scaffold, thus promoting the deposition of perovskite film, optimizing the interface contact and passivating the interfacial defect density.  $  ^{[32, 33]}  $   Therefore, the utilization of mesoporous NiO film seems to be the feasible strategy to address interfacial contact issues of NiO/perovskite interface.

In this work, we demonstrated the hydrothermal design of 3D mesoporous structure of NiONW HSCs as interface engineering strategy for efficient inverted PSCs. As is known, the hydrothermal method is effective approach to develop the high-quality metal oxide film featuring

This contributes to the better charge transport/extraction with lower interfacial energy loss. As a result, the device yields the efficiency of approximately ~18%. To further passivate the intrinsic defect of NiO surface, the additional DEA interlayer was introduced between NiO NWs and perovskite layer. With the synergistic improvement of passivation strategies, the champion PCEs was boosted up to 19.16% with high open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  , ~1.11 V) and fill factor (FF, >80%). Meanwhile, the excellent stability and suppressed hysteresis behavior were also achieved for such NiO NWs/DEA device. This work demonstrates the great potential of morphological design of selective contacts in device performance optimization.

# 2. Results and discussion

Herein, the reliable fabrication of NiO NWs hole selective contact (HSC) was studied by regulating hydrothermal growth time. As displayed in Figure S1, the NiO compact layer was formed on FTO substrate during the initial stage of hydrothermal process (1 h). With the increase of hydrothermal time, the covered film gradually developed into the NiO NWs arrays with desirable porous network structure (3-9 h). Notably, excessive growth would induce the formation of poor, complex morphology (12 h). For simplicity, this work focuses on the effect of expected NiO NWs films (3-9 h) on subsequent device performance. Figure 1a presents the detail morphology of NiO NWs film. Obviously, as the hydrothermal time increases from 3 h to 9 h, the NiO NWs film become dense with decreased porosity. From the perspective of film morphology, the optimum growth time is 6 h because of their more impressive, homogeneous mesoporous structure. Moreover, the atomic force microscopy (AFM) was employed to further investigate the surface roughness of perovskite film (Figure 2a, b). In comparison, the NiO NWs film (6 h) exhibits the better surface features (uniformity and coverage) with the lower roughness of 12.2 nm. Here, the XRD pattern was utilized to characterize the crystallinity of these perovskite films. [29, 36] Specially, the stronger (110) peak intensity of perovskite film on NiO NWs-6 h film determines its better crystallinity, agrees with the SEM and AFM results. Besides, Figure 2d presents the corresponding absorption spectra of perovskite film. As expected, the film (6 h) exhibits the better light absorption, which could be also ascribed to the improved film quality.

Here, the space-charge limited current (SCLC) method was applied to evaluate charge trap density at NiO/perovskite interface. The hole-only devices with structure of FTO/NiO NW/perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Ag were fabricated, and corresponding I-V curves were displayed in Figure 3a. According to the equation (1):

To further clarify the effects of NiO NWs film, the photovoltaic performance of these PSCs was compared in detail (Figure 3e and Table S2). Obviously, the optimal PCE of  $ 17.80\% $  with  $ \mathrm{J_{sc}} $  of  $ 21.39\mathrm{mA} / \mathrm{cm}^2 $ ,  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  of  $ 1.097\mathrm{V} $ , and FF of  $ 75.85\% $ , was achieved for the device based on NiO NW HSC (6 h). This is remarkably higher than  $ \sim 12.77\% $  efficiency of NiO NCs device. Detailed comparisons of various photovoltaic parameters (each 60 individual devices) are presented in Figure 3f. It can be found that the better performance of NiO NW-6 h devices is mainly attributed to the significantly improved  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  and FF value. As is known, the  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  and FF are mainly related to the recombination behavior within the perovskite layer and the interface. The presence of bulk/interface defect would induce severe charge recombination and energy loss.[27, 41] As discussed above, the unavoidable film issues for the pristine NiO NCs film (agglomeration or discontinuity), would increase the defect density in NiO/perovskite interface, thus resulting in the unnecessary  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}}/\mathrm{FF} $  loss. Besides, the J-V hysteresis were also investigated to further evaluate the device performance (Figure 4a, b). Here the quantified hysteresis index can be defined by the equation of  $ (PCE_{reverse} - PCE_{forward}) / PCE_{reverse} $ . As presented in Figure 4c, the statistical results demonstrate the weaker hysteresis is found for NiO NWs device. It is well-known that the hysteresis behavior mainly originates from severe interfacial charge accumulation. In principle, such hysteresis could be improved by better charge dynamics behavior or lower interfacial defect density.[42, 43] Hence, benefitting from the effectively interface passivation effect, the optimal NiO NW device exhibits the enhanced charge transport/extraction with suppressed hysteresis. The result indicates that such hydrothermally treated NiO NWs film would be the reliable choice for high-performance inverted NiO device.

To our best of knowledge, some Ni vacancy defects are common on the surface of NiO, which would induce the generation of highly reactive O dangling bonds. They may lead to the poor chemical stability of NiO/perovskite interface, and energy loss of interfacial charge transfer process. [27, 44] It has been demonstrated that surface modification is an effective strategy to minimize such negative influence, such as 5-AVA or alkali halide. [29, 30] Here, we introduced a DEA interlayer to passivate intrinsic defects of NiO surface for further improving device performance (Figure 5a). As illustrated in Figure 5b, the interactions between functional groups of DEA molecule and NiO would improve the interface properties. Specially, the DEA could be complexed to the NiO surface by forming Ni-NH bond. While, the hydroxyl group (-OH) were known to interact with Pb atoms of perovskite. [28, 29, 45] Hence, the more stable and reliable NiO NWs/perovskite interface contact can be achieved. The FTIR and XPS results synergistically proved the presence of these interactions (details in Figure S6). Moreover, benefitting from these interactions, the perovskite crystallization and corresponding film quality were further improved [45]. As displayed, the film roughness was decreased to  $ 9.7\mathrm{nm} $  (Figure 5c). And the SEM and XRD results synergistically demonstrates the improved crystallinity with increased grain size of  $ \sim 300\mathrm{nm} $  (Figure 5d and Figure S7). The corresponding cross-sectional SEM image of such NiO NWs/DEA device was presented in Figure 5e.

# 3. Conclusion

In this work, we have developed one-step hydrothermal strategy to fabricate high-quality NiO NWs film as efficient HSCs of inverted PSCs. This approach overcomes the disadvantages of easy agglomeration of the conventional solution-processed NiO NCs film. And such newly-designed NiO film HSC exhibits the continuous, homogeneous mesoporous network structure and excellent crystallinity. Hence, the improved interfacial contact with low defect density and enhanced perovskite film quality can be achieved. Moreover, the additional DEA interlayer was introduced to further passivate the intrinsic defect induced by Ni vacancy on NiO

surface, which would be beneficial for the better charge transfer with suppressed recombination loss. As a result, the NiO NWs/DEA based PSCs demonstrates the promising PCEs (19.16%, with high   $  V_{oc}  $   of ~1.11 V and FF of 80.23%) and enhanced stability. This work demonstrates the great potential of such hydrothermal NiO NWs film as HSCs for efficient, stable inverted PSCs.
