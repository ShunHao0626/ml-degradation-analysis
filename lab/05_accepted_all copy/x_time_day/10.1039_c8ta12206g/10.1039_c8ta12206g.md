# Pyridine-functionalized Fullerene Additive Enabling Coordination Interactions with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  Perovskite towards Highly Efficient Bulk Heterojunction Solar Cells

Jieming Zhen  $  ^{\#a}  $  , Weiran Zhou  $  ^{\#a}  $  , Muqing Chen  $  ^{*\#a}  $  , Bairu Li  $  ^{a}  $  , Lingbo Jia  $  ^{a}  $  , Mingtai Wang  $  ^{b}  $  , and Shangfeng Yang  $  ^{*a}  $

Incorporating n-type fullerene derivatives into the perovskite layer affording bulk heterojunction (BHJ) perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is an effective strategy to passivate trap state, consequently reducing the current-voltage hysteresis and improving the device performance. Herein, we report the synthesis of a novel pyridine-functionalized fullerene derivative  $ (C_{60} - PyP) $  via a 1, 3-dipolar cycloaddition reaction, and the unambiguous determination of its molecular structure by X-ray single crystal diffraction. Upon incorporating  $ C_{60}-PyP $  as an additive with an optimized doping ratio of  $ 0.13\mathrm{wt}\% $  into  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  (MAPbI₃) perovskite precursor, the regular-structure bulk heterojunction PSC devices exhibit a best power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 19.82\% $ , which is dramatically enhanced relative to that of the control devices  $ (17.61\%) $ .  $ C_{60}-PyP $  additive renders heterogeneous nucleation sites, leading to the decrease of the nucleation Gibbs free energy and consequently enlarged grain size and improved crystallization of MAPbI₃ perovskite film. Besides, incorporation of  $ C_{60}-PyP $  results in enhanced crystalline orientation as confirmed by grazing-incidence X-ray diffraction (GIXRD) measurement. Meanwhile, the coordination interaction between the N atom of the pyridine moiety within  $ C_{60}-PyP $  and  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2+}} $  ion within MAPbI₃ leads to the passivation of trap states of perovskite layer, jointly contributing to the improved device performance and markedly suppressed current-voltage hysteresis. Moreover, the ambient stability of the devices is improved upon  $ C_{60}-PyP $  incorporation due to the hydrophobic nature of  $ C_{60}-PyP $  molecule, which presumably reside at the grain boundaries.

# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are regarded as one of the most promising photovoltaic devices due to the superior properties of perovskite materials including large absorption coefficient, high carrier mobility, long carrier recombination lifetime and adjustable bandgaps etc.  $  ^{[1-6]}  $   Recently, a record power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 23.7% has been achieved.  $  ^{[7]}  $   Among the factors determining device performance, film quality of the perovskite absorber layer is one of the most determinative ones. Additive engineering via incorporating exotic additives such as small molecules,  $  ^{[8-11]}  $   polymers,  $  ^{[12,13]}  $   fullerene derivatives  $  ^{[14-17]}  $   into perovskite layer has been proved to be effective in improving the quality of perovskite film via crystallization control and/or defect passivation. In particular, incorporating n-type fullerene derivative into the perovskite

layer affording bulk heterojunction (BHJ) PSCs is an effective strategy to passivate trap state, and fullerene derivatives represent an intriguing type of additives with multiple functions, including, (1) facilitating electron extraction and transfer due to its strong electron-accepting ability; (2) passivating the trap states via the interaction of fullerene cage with Pb-I antisite defects; (3) acting as heterogeneous nucleation sites to tune grain quality of perovskite, which jointly contribute to improved PCE and stability.  $  ^{[14-19]}  $   [6,6]-phenyl-C  $  _{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) is the first fullerene derivative which was incorporated into CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) precursor solution, and PCBM was observed to locate homogeneously at the perovskite grain boundaries (GBs), passivating the Pb-I antisite defects, resulting in a decent PCE of 14.4% with suppressed current-voltage hysteresis.  $  ^{[14]}  $   A fluoroalkyl-substituted fullerene derivative (DF-C  $  _{60}  $  ) was recently synthesized and incorporated into MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   layer to form DF-C  $  _{60}  $  /perovskite heterojunction in PSCs, yielding an impressive PCE of 18.11% relative to that of the control device (15.67%).  $  ^{[15]}  $   Very recently, polyethylene glycol (PEG) functionalized fullerene derivatives were incorporated into MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   precursor, yielding a best PCE of 16.41% with reduced hysteresis index and improved ambient stability.  $  ^{[16]}  $   Note that, among them only few fullerene derivatives such as DF-C  $  _{60}  $   as additives were reported to achieve higher PCEs (18.11%) than that based on PCBM additive (14.4%). Therefore, it is highly desired to develop novel fullerene additives bearing other functional groups, which render reinforced interactions with perovskite layer.

Herein, we synthesized a novel pyridine-functionalized fullerene derivative (abbreviated as   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP) and applied it as an exceptional additive affording coordination interactions with MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   via the pyridine moiety, resulting in dramatically improved PCE of bulk heterojunction PSC device with suppressed current-voltage hysteresis.   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP was synthesized via a facile 1, 3-dipolar cycloaddition reaction, and its molecular structure is unambiguously confirmed by X-ray single crystal diffraction. # Results and Discussion

# Synthesis and structure characterization of the pyridine-functionalized fullerene derivative (  $  C_{60}  $  -PyP)

Very recently, by applying several fullerene derivatives bearing pyridine moieties as electron transport layer (ETL) of inverted planar heterojunction PSCs, we found that the grafted pyridine moiety fulfilled the coordination interactions with the   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ion of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite whereas no such coordination interaction was observed for PCBM ETL, leading to the enhanced device performance relative to that based on PCBM ETL.  $  ^{[22]}  $   Stimulated by the peculiarity of the pyridine moiety, in this work we manage preferably to design and synthesize another novel pyridine-functionalized fullerene derivative (  $  C_{60}-PyP  $  ) in which pyridine moiety is grafted in a different manner.   $  C_{60}-PyP  $   was synthesized via a facile 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition reaction of   $  C_{60}  $   with excess amount of N-(4-chlorophenyl)picolinohydrazonoyl chloride (ca. 2-fold) and few drops of triethylamine in chlorobenzene solution at 80 °C (see Scheme 1). The detailed synthetic procedure was described in the experimental section. The solubilities of   $  C_{60}-PyP  $   in chlorobenzene and N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) are about 6 mg/ml and 4 mg/ml, respectively.

The chemical structure of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP was characterized by   $  {}^{13}  $  H and   $  {}^{13}  $  C NMR spectroscopy. According to the   $  {}^{13}  $  H-NMR spectroscopic result, the proton signals of phenyl, pyrazole and pyridine groups within   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP are observed in the range of 7.32-8.51 ppm (see Supporting Information Figure S1). The   $  {}^{13}  $  C NMR spectrum of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP shows clearly the   $  sp^{2}  $  -carbon signals of the   $  C_{60}  $   cage in the range of 135.64-151.76 ppm. Besides, the   $  sp^{2}  $  -carbon signals of pyridine ring overlap with those of the   $  sp^{2}  $  -carbon of   $  C_{60}  $   cage. The   $  sp^{2}  $  -carbon signals of chlorobenzyl moiety is in the range of 124.94-135.63 ppm. Specially, the lone pair of nitrogen in pyrazole ring of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP is located at the benzene ring containing addition sites,  $  ^{[23]}  $   resulting in the carbon signals of addition sites shift to low field at 123.31 and 123.36 ppm (see Supporting Information Figure S2). The mono-adduct nature of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP is confirmed by matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time of flight (MALDI-TOF) mass spectrometry, showing the dominant ionic peak at m/z=949, which is coincident with the molecule weight of mono-adduct (Supporting Information, Figure S3).

Furthermore, the molecular structure of  $  C_{60}  $ -PyP is unambiguously determined by X-ray single crystal diffraction. Shown in Figure 1a is the X-ray single crystal structure of  $  C_{60}  $ -PyP, unveiling that the addition reaction occurs at a [6, 6]-bond junction with the bond length of 1.60 Å for C1-C2, which keeps closed. In the pyrazole ring grafted onto fullerene cage, the bond length of C1-N2 is 1.501 Å, which is much larger than that of C3-N1 (1.279 Å), indicating the double bond character of C3-N1. In addition, the bond length of C2-C3 and N1-N2 are 1.538 Å and 1.366 Å, respectively, falling into the range of single bond. Interestingly, the packing structure of  $  C_{60}  $ -PyP shows that fullerene cage are prone to form ordered pathway (Figure 1b), which is expected to facilitate electron transport along such a pathway.

# Photovoltaic performance of PSC devices using   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP as additive.

To evaluate the function of  $  C_{60}  $ -PyP as an additive doped in MAPbI $ _3 $ , we fabricated the regular-structure (n-i-p) planar heterojunction PSC devices with the structure of ITO/TiO $ _2 $ /MAPbI $ _3 $  + C $ _{60} $ -PyP/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au (Figure 2d). C $ _{60} $ -PyP was dissolved in DMF and was doped into the perovskite precursor solution with different doping ratio (0.05 wt%, 0.13 wt%, 0.25 wt% and 1.0 wt%) to determine the optimized additive concentration. The current density-voltage (J-V) curves of the PSC devices with and without C $ _{60} $ -PyP additive obtained

under 1 sun illumination are compared in Figure 3a. The corresponding photovoltaic parameters including open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ), short-circuit current (  $  J_{sc}  $  ), fill factor (FF), PCE, series resistance (  $  R_{s}  $  ), and shunt resistance (  $  R_{sh}  $  ) are summarized in Table 1. The control device without   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP shows an average PCE of 17.04% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.083 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 20.92 mA·cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and a FF of 75.24%. After incorporating   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP additive with a relatively low doping ratio (0.05 wt% - 0.25 wt%), the device performance of PSCs is enhanced obviously, and the optimized doping ratio is determined to be 0.13 wt%, under which an average PCE of 19.04%, calculated from a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.090 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.31 mA·cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and FF of 78.26%, is obtained. The champion device exhibits a PCE of 19.82%, which is enhanced by ~12% relative to that of the control device. Upon increasing the   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP doping ratio to 1.0 wt%, however, the device performance decreases obviously, which is even lower than that of the control device. The variation tendency of PCE as well as other photovoltaic parameters is confirmed further by comparing the statistical photovoltaic data obtained for 15 independent devices (see Supporting Information Figures S6 and S7 for the histograms and box plots of the photovoltaic parameters).

Among the three photovoltaic parameters, the PCE enhancement upon   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP incorporation is primarily originated from the increase of   $  J_{sc}  $   (from 20.9 to 22.3 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , ~6.7% enhancement), whereas the enhancement ratios of both FF (from 75.24% to 78.26%, ~4.0% enhancement), and   $  V_{oc}  $   (from 1.083 to 1.090 V, ~0.6% enhancement) are much smaller. The increase of   $  J_{sc}  $   is verified by comparing the external quantum efficiency (EQE) values of the devices with and without   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP additive as shown in Figure 3b. The perovskite device with 0.13 wt%   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP additive shows higher EQE response in the region of 620-780 nm compared to that of the control device, indicating more effective photon utilization, as evidenced from the enhanced integrated   $  J_{sc}  $  .

The current-voltage hysteresis is further examined by measuring the J-V curves in different scan directions (Figures 3c, 3d). The control device suffers from a severe hysteresis with a hysteresis index of  $ 20.3\% $ , while the  $ 0.13\mathrm{wt}\% $   $ C_{60} $ -PyP-doped device exhibits negligible hysteresis with the hysteresis index of  $ 0.53\% $  (see Supporting Information Table S2). We further verify this result by measuring the J-V curves at different scan rates, revealing that the control device without  $ C_{60} $ -PyP suffers from considerable variations whereas the fluctuation for the 0.13 wt%  $ C_{60} $ -PyP-doped device is much smaller (see Supporting Information Figure S8).

# Effect of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP on morphology and crystallinity of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film.

In order to elucidate the correlation between the MAPbI $ _3 $  film quality with the photovoltaic property of the PSC device, we carried out morphological characterizations by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). The control perovskite film shows compact morphology with relatively small grain size of ca. 300 nm. Interestingly, when 0.13 wt% C $ _{60} $ -PyP was incorporated, the average perovskite grain size increases obviously up to 600 nm, which however decreases to 450 nm after incorporating 1.0 wt% C $ _{60} $ -PyP additive (see Figures 4a-c and Supporting Information S9 for grain size distribution). Interestingly, when the C $ _{60} $ -PyP doping ratio increases to 1.0 wt%, some exotic particles appear, which aggregates mostly along the grain boundaries (Figure 4c). This phenomenon is similar to the case of incorporating PCBM additive into MAPbI $ _3 $  film, $ ^{[14]} $  and the exotic particles are presumably assigned to the aggregate of C $ _{60} $ -PyP molecules. Nevertheless, the amount of C $ _{60} $ -PyP additive incorporated into MAPbI $ _3 $  film has little influence on the thickness of the MAPbI $ _3 $  film (ca. 300 nm, Figures 4d-f). The pristine   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite generally exhibits homogeneous nucleation manner, followed by growth of the perovskite seed crystal (Figure 6a).  $  ^{[17]}  $   After incorporating   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP into perovskite precursor,   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP may serve as heterogeneous nucleation sites for perovskite crystallization as shown in Figure 6b, and this provides much lower nucleation barrier than the homogeneous nucleation manner due to the decreased Gibbs free energy, facilitating the growth of larger perovskite crystalline grains.  $  ^{[17, 34,35]}  $   In this sense, addition of excess   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP (1.0 wt%) results in the existence of excessive nucleation sites, and this is unfavorable for crystallization of perovskite, consequently leading to decreased crystallinity and grain size.

Given that   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ion has strong coordination ability with Lewis bases due to its 6p empty electron orbitals,  $  ^{[36]}  $   it is intriguing to investigate whether the incorporated   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP can interact with   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  . We carried out X-ray photoelectron spectroscopic (XPS) characterization to monitor the change of the binding energy of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ion within   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   after incorporating   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP additive. According to the comparison of the high-resolution Pb 4f XPS spectra of the pristine   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   and   $  MAPbI_{3} + C_{60}  $  -PyP (1.0 wt%) films (Figure 5d), the characteristic Pb 4f  $  _{7/2}  $   and Pb 4f  $  _{5/2}  $   signals at 143.38 and 138.52 eV  $  ^{[37,38]}  $   detected for the pristine   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   shift obviously towards the lower core-level binding energies of 143.20 and 138.35 eV respectively after   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP incorporation. This result suggests the decrease of cationic charge of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions within   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ , which is due likely to the donation of the lone electron pair on the N atom of the pyridine moiety within   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP to the empty 6p orbital of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   via coordination bonding.  $  ^{[22,35]}  $   Notably, the metallic Pb signal at 136.0 eV and 141.3 eV in perovskite film disappeared after incorporation of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP,  $  ^{[39,40]}  $   which is due to the reduction of iodine vacancy defect induced by the bonding effect between   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP and uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $ .

It has been reported that the Pb-I antisite defects are the main deep-level defects in perovskite films, where the uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions as trap states at grain boundaries readily capture transported electrons.  $  ^{[18]}  $   After   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP incorporation enabling coordination interactions with the   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ion of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  , such uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   trap states can be effectively passivated. As a result, the undesirable non-radiative recombination process can be prohibited, contributing to the increase of   $  J_{sc}  $  .  $  ^{[24]}  $   Besides, passivation of the trap states at grain boundaries is beneficial for the inhibition of ion migration and consequently reduced current-voltage hysteresis.  $  ^{[41-44]}  $

# Effect of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP on the stability of PSC device.

To unveil the effect of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP additive on the stability of the PSC device, the steady-state photocurrent output at the maximum power point was measured (Supporting Information, Figure S11 and Table S5). The PCE of the   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP-doped PSC device can be stabilized at 18.63% during the illumination period of 300 s, which is much higher than that of the control device (15.61%). This tendency agrees with that obtained from J-V curves (see Table 1), confirming the reliability of the measured PCE.

The ambient stability of the PSC device is further evaluated by storing the device without any encapsulation under ambient in dark (temperature:  $ 20^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , relative humidity:  $ 35\% $ ). As shown in Figure 8, the performance of the control device is quite susceptible to the ambient environment with a PCE drop of ca.  $ 30\% $  after 30 days, whereas the  $ \mathrm{C_{60} - PyP} $ -doped PSC device exhibits a better stability with the PCE drop of ca.  $ 17\% $ . The underlying degradation mechanism is further studied by monitoring the compositional change of perovskite film by XRD measurements, indicating that the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  phase for the control device decreases obviously along with the appearance of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  phase. Whereas upon  $ \mathrm{C_{60} - PyP} $  incorporation, negligible  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  phase is detected in the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film after 30 days (see

Supporting Information Figure S12). This indicates that the  $ C_{60} $ -PyP additive could protect perovskite from degradation due to the hydrophobicity of  $ C_{60} $ . In addition, we carried out SEM measurements to monitor the morphology change during the degradation of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film. After storage at a high humidity (80%) for 24 h, the control  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film begins to decompose from grain boundaries along with the appearing of a large number of pinholes. However, upon  $ C_{60} $ -PyP incorporation, the MAPbI3 film still retains the perfectly dense and smooth morphology without obvious pinholes (see Supporting Information Figure S13). This can be explained that the defects accumulated at the grain boundaries of perovskite film are effectively passivated, thus the decomposition of perovskite film by moisture erosion can be effectively suppressed.

# Conclusion

In summary, a novel pyridine-functionalized fullerene derivative (  $  C_{60}  $  -PyP) was synthesized via a facile 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition reaction, and its molecular structure was unambiguously determined by X-ray single crystal diffraction.   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP was incorporated as an additive into   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   layer to construct bulk heterojunction PSC devices, and a dramatic PCE enhancement from 17.61% to 19.82% is obtained under the optimized   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP doping ratio of 0.13 wt%, with suppressed current-voltage hysteresis and improved ambient stability. Incorporation of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP renders heterogeneous nucleation sites, leading to the decrease of the nucleation Gibbs free energy and consequently enlarged grain size, improved crystallization and enhanced crystalline orientation of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite film. Besides, the coordination interaction between the N atom of pyridine moiety within   $  C_{60}  $  -PyP and   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ion within   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   leads to passivation of the trap states of perovskite layer, contributing to the improved   $  J_{sc}  $  .
