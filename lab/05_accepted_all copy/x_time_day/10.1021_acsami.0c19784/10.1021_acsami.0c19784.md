# Reconstruction of the   $  (EMIm)_{x}MA_{1-x}Pb[(BF_{4})_{x}I_{1-x}]_{3}  $   Interlayer for Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Zequn Zhang, Tonghui Guo, Haobo Yuan, Luting Yu, Rui Zhao, Zhiqiang Deng, Jing Zhang,  $  ^{*}  $   Xiaohui Liu, Ziyang Hu, and Yuejin Zhu  $  ^{*}  $

Cite This: ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 2021, 13, 727–733

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Defective grain boundaries (GBs) and surface trap states are detrimental to the efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). In this research, ionic liquid (IL) is used to control the defect states at the perovskite surface and GBs. The newly formed  $ (\mathrm{EMIm})_x\mathrm{MA}_{1 - x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_4)_x\mathrm{I}_{1 - x}]_3 $  interlayer promotes secondary grain growth to diminish GBs; besides,  $ \mathrm{EMIM^{+}} $  and  $ \mathrm{BF_4^-} $  fill the vacancies of  $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $  and  $ \mathrm{I^{-}} $  and also passivate undercoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2 + }} $  trap states. The newly formed interface largely reduces the nonradiative recombination, thus enhancing the solar-cell performance to  $ 19.0\% $  (AM 1.5, 1 sun) with higher photovoltage and fill factor than the control device. Due to the hydrophobicity of the  $ (\mathrm{EMIm})_x\mathrm{MA}_{1 - x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_4)_x\mathrm{I}_{1 - x}]_3 $  interlayer, the unencapsulated device stability in 30 days is much better than the

control device under relative humidity (RH) = 20%. This work highlights IL-induced secondary grain growth and a defect passivation method for efficient and stable PSCs.

KEYWORDS: perovskite solar cells, ionic liquid, interface reconstruction, defect passivation, crystal reconstruction

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Recently, the inorganic–organic hybrid PSCs have become a star in the field of solar cells due to their high performance and simple preparation process.  $  ^{1-5}  $   Due to the low thermal stability of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite (PVK), MA vacancy and under-coordinated Pb atoms at the film surface and GBs are the main trap states,  $  ^{6}  $   which cause serious nonradiative recombination in the solar-cell devices. Furthermore, defective GBs and surface are easy to be attacked by heat and moisture, which induce device instability.  $  ^{7}  $   Aiming for high PCE, high-quality large perovskite crystals with few GBs are pursued to reduce the defect states in perovskite.  $  ^{8-12}  $   Moreover, the GB and surface with large dangling bonds are modified to further passivate the defect states.  $  ^{13,14}  $   Owing to passivation of defects, robust chemical bonding interlayer, and moisture-resistant interface at the rear surface of the device, the stability of the device is largely improved.  $  ^{15-18}  $

Ionic liquid (IL) has good ionic conductivity, high thermal stability, and chemical stability and so it is a promising candidate liquid in PSCs. IL is generally composed of larger asymmetric organic cations (i.e., pyridine, phosphine, and imidazole) and organic or inorganic anions (i.e.,   $  Cl^{-}  $  ,   $  COOH^{-}  $  ,   $  PF_{6}^{-}  $  , and   $  BF_{4}^{-}  $  ). It plays a positive role in uniform nucleation, surface defect modification, and energy level adjustment when it is used as an additive in the perovskite active layer.  $  ^{19}  $   At the same time, IL also shines through the application of interface control additives. # 2. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

As shown in the scheme in Figure 1a, the use of  $ \mathrm{EMImBF_4} $  IL in methanol/isopropanol modification of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  surface is not a direct and simple deposition process but a desorption-reconstruction process. In the presence of methanol, the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  surface is transformed to be Pb-rich surface because  $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $  and  $ \mathrm{I^{-}} $  are desorbed from the surface by the methanol solvent. When the defective Pb-rich surface interacts with the  $ \mathrm{EMImBF_4} $  IL, the  $ \mathrm{BF_4}^- $  anion will fill the  $ \mathrm{V}_{1} $  and  $ \mathrm{EMIm^{+}} $  fills the  $ \mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{MA}} $  vacancies. The phase formation in this process is proved by X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurement, as shown in Figure 1d. To examine the desorption process by methanol, methanol/isopropanol in the same ratio as the modification solution is added on the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  PVK surface. It is observed that the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  phase is formed in PVK with a new diffraction peak emerging at  $ 12.4^{\circ} $ , which proved that  $ \mathrm{MA}^{+} $  and  $ \mathrm{I}^{-} $  are desorbed from the PVK surface by the methanol solvent.

From the XRD of the PVK surface treated with 0.003–5 mg IL, it can be found that the IL treatment can improve the crystallinity, as shown in Figure 1d. The crystallinity is optimized at a concentration of 0.03 mg IL because of the highest XRD diffraction intensity in the sample. The sample with highest XRD diffraction is denoted as IL-treated. It is also found out that a new diffraction peak at   $  10^{\circ}  $   emerged in 5 mg IL-treated PVK. In the reported reference, it is announced that the formation of lead halide/imidazolium halide complexes referred to the new diffraction peak at   $  10^{\circ}  $   and was reported in a published study.  $  ^{25}  $   The XRD diffraction peak of the interlayer might be too low to be detected in the 0.003–0.3 mg IL-treated sample. To determine whether the newly formed phase is derived from lead halide complexes or halogenated imidazole complexes, we prepared the   $  (\mathrm{EMIm})_{x}\mathrm{MA}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_{4})_{x}\mathrm{I}_{1-x}]_{3}  $   perovskite solution by introducing x EMImBF  $  _{4}  $   IL and reducing MAI by x (MA/IL/PbI  $  _{2}  $   = 1 - x::x:1). When x = 1.5%, the XRD (Figure S1, Supporting Information) of the resulting compound also shows a new diffraction peak at   $  10^{\circ}  $  . Therefore, we confirm that the   $  (\mathrm{EMIm})_{x}\mathrm{MA}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_{4})_{x}\mathrm{I}_{1-x}]_{3}  $   interlayer is formed after the treatment with the EMImBF  $  _{4}  $   methanol/isopropanol solution.

As shown in Figure 2a,b, the surface morphology of the perovskite after modification with different concentrations of IL was studied by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). It can be observed that when the modified concentration is  $ 0.3\mathrm{mg}/ $  mL, the size of the crystal grain is much larger than that of the

control film; the grain boundary is therefore reduced. When the concentration increases, some bright strip-shaped crystal grains are found (Figure S2). This may be due to a large number of   $  (\mathrm{EMIm})_{x}\mathrm{MA}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_{4})_{x}\mathrm{I}_{1-x}]_{3}  $   crystallites on the PVK surface. The morphology map after atomic force measurement (AFM) characterization (Figure S3) is consistent with SEM results. The root-mean-square (RMS) values of the control, IL-treated, 5 mg/mL samples are 14.4, 13.9, and 17.2 nm, respectively, which indicates that the   $  (\mathrm{EMIm})_{x}\mathrm{MA}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_{4})_{x}\mathrm{I}_{1-x}]_{3}  $   interlayer changed the morphology of the PVK film. Under the optimal concentration modification, the surface roughness decreased, which may be because the ionic liquid induces a secondary crystallization process, causing the sharp crystal domain edges to fuse together to obtain a smoother morphology.

Differing from the doping induced crystallization process,   $  EMImBF_{4}  $   IL is added on the formed PVK crystalline layer, where the secondary grain growth process occurs to influence the crystallinity of the formed PVK. According to thermal dynamics, new interactions must be induced between IL and PVK to allow the second crystallization process to occur. To further explore the kind of interaction existing between   $  EMImBF_{4}  $   and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ , we used the Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) characterization to measure the changes between the various organic groups. It is worth noting that, as shown in Figure 2c, the tensile vibration peak at   $  1172\ cm^{-1}  $   assigned to the C–N bond in EMIm shifts to a lower wavenumber of   $  1162\ cm^{-1}  $ . This significant shift indicates that the bond interaction between the N atom in   $  EMIm^{+}  $  and   $  Pb^{2+}  $   in   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   leads to the weakening of the C–N bond.  $  ^{26}  $   At the same time,   $  3130-3181\ cm^{-1}  $   is the vibration peak of the inherent organic group N–H, and the peak is relatively broad. A new tensile vibration peak at   $  3081\ cm^{-1}  $   emerges in the IL-treated structure compared to the other two structures. We performed transient photocurrent decay (TPC) and transient photovoltage decay (TPV) characterization to study the charge transfer characteristics of the device. As shown in Figure 4c, the longer decay time of TPV indicates that the device with the  $ (\mathrm{EMIm})_x\mathrm{MA}_{1 - x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_4)_x\mathrm{I}_{1 - x}]_3 $  interlayer has a slower surface charge recombination, which is attributed to the improved film quality and fewer nonradiative recombination sites. At the same time, a longer decay time provides more opportunities to extract carriers from the perovskite to their respective electrodes, thereby greatly reducing the carrier recombination. As shown in Figure 4d, the photocurrent decay is a little faster in the IL-treated device, indicating the more efficient charge transport of the PSCs with the  $ (\mathrm{EMIm})_x\mathrm{MA}_{1 - x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_4)_x\mathrm{I}_{1 - x}]_3 $  interlayer. Further the improved film quality and increased crystallinity after [EMIm]- $ \mathrm{BF}_4 $  treatment can reduce the resistance to charge transfer and increase the efficiency of charge collection.

It can be seen from the analysis that the reduction of grain boundaries and the passivation of defect states both lead to the reduction of trap states. For a deeper understanding of the carrier dynamics of the   $  (\mathrm{EMIm})_{x}\mathrm{MA}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_{4})_{x}\mathrm{I}_{1-x}]_{3}  $   intermediate layer, we performed steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy and time-resolved PL (TRPL) to examine the trap states in PVK. Figure 3a shows that the IL-treated film exhibits a stronger absorption peak at 778 nm than the control sample, under the same energy of light excitation. It

indicates a reduced trap state in an IL-treated sample. At the same time, we also observe that the peak position of the IL-treated sample has a slight blue shift compared to the control sample, which again indicates a decrease in its defect state density.  $  ^{29}  $   Figure 3b shows the TRPL decay of the thin film with the same device structure. It can be seen that the carrier lifetime of the IL-treated sample is longer than that of the control device, also consistent with the result of the steady-state PL. The modification of the perovskite surface by IL is indeed beneficial to reduce the defect state density on the surface. However, in the case of high-concentration IL modification, the steady-state PL and TRPL indicate the opposite effect of the modification (Figures S5 and S6), which is because of high complexity of the surface of the perovskite to trap the photogenerated carriers.

The UV absorption is shown in Figure 3c. Compared with the control device, the light absorption capacity of IL-treated sample is slightly enhanced, mainly due to the better crystalline quality. But the absorption capacity is reduced after modification at high concentration, which indicates the low film quality of high IL concentration-treated sample. Although it is mentioned in previous reports that when pseudohalogen   $  BF_{4}^{-}  $  is substituted for   $  I^{-}  $ , the band gap will decrease; when the   $  MA^{+}  $  site is replaced by a large organic cation, the band gap increases.  $  ^{30,31}  $   However, in this work, under the premise that the intermediate layer cannot hinder charge transfer or cause energy level mismatch, a thinner intermediate layer is used so that component substitution cannot be the determinant for the band gap change. Therefore, the band gap has not changed and the absorption edge has not shifted. To intuitively prove that the nonradiative recombination of the IL-treated perovskite is reduced due to the reduction of the defect state density, it is necessary to explore the trap density   $  N_{trap}  $   in the prepared perovskite film. Therefore, we conduct space–charge limiting current (SCLC) characterization. The performance of PSCs with   $  (\mathrm{EMIm})_{x}\mathrm{MA}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_{4})_{x}\mathrm{I}_{1-x}]_{3}  $   interlayer reconstruction is examined. The detailed performance is summarized in Figure 4a. For controlled equipment, the maximum PCE is 17.5%, with   $  J_{sc} = 23.3 \, mA/cm^{2}  $  ,   $  V_{oc} = 1.01 \, V  $  , and FF = 74%. The maximum PCE of the IL-treated PSC with a concentration of 0.03 mg/mL EMImBF  $  _{4}  $   is 19.0%, with   $  J_{sc} = 23.4 \, mA/cm^{2}  $  ,   $  V_{oc} = 1.04 \, V  $  , and FF = 78%. We then tested the performance of 30 sets of devices (Figure 4b) and found that the performance of the IL-treated solar cells was generally better, indicating better repeatability. Unfortunately, under high-concentration modification conditions, the performance of PSCs has a significant drop compared to the control device (Figure S7). This may be due to the excessively thick composite layer hindering charge transport.

Further the improved film quality and increased crystallinity after [EMIm]- $ \mathrm{BF}_4 $  treatment can reduce the resistance to charge transfer and increase the efficiency of charge collection.

Impedance spectroscopy (EIS) further characterizes the interface properties and the carrier transport process of the device. The Nyquist plot of PSCs was measured in the dark at a bias voltage of 0.9 V, and is shown in Figure 4e. Based on the simplified equivalent circuit model of carrier transmission, a simple semicircle is fitted to the data. It can be seen that the semicircle radius of the IL-treated device is larger than that of the unmodified device, indicating a larger   $  R_{rec}  $  . It indicates that the carrier recombination behavior has been effectively

suppressed by the   $  (\mathrm{EMIm})_{x}\mathrm{MA}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_{4})_{x}\mathrm{I}_{1-x}]_{3}  $   interlayer. This is also consistent with the results obtained from the photoluminescence analysis described above.

From the dark-state current characterization, as shown in Figure 4f, it can be seen that the dark-state current of the IL-treated device is two orders of magnitude smaller than that of the control device, which again indicates that the leakage current is effectively suppressed in the interface-reconstructed device, and the modified composite layer has fewer defect traps.  $  ^{34}  $

We further checked the stability of IL-treated equipment. By aging the film in high-humidity air (relative humidity (RH) > 90%), the humidity stability of the perovskite is shown in Figure 5a. After being placed in this environment for 30 min, a visible yellow color can be seen in the control film, indicating that the perovskite has been degraded into   $  PbI_{2}  $  . By contrast, the IL-treated PVK film retains the black color after 30 min. It indicates that the   $  (\mathrm{EMIm})_{x}\mathrm{MA}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_{4})_{x}\mathrm{I}_{1-x}]_{3}  $   interlayer successfully enhances the humidity stability. Because   $  CH_{2}-CH_{3}  $   is present as a side group on the imidazole, the bonding of   $  EMIm^{+}  $  pushes the   $  CH_{2}-CH_{3}  $   side group outside the perovskite surface, which is supposed to enhance the hydrophobicity of the PVK film. For clarity, we first measured the water contact angle (Figure 5b). The bare PVK film has a small contact angle of   $  11.5^{\circ}  $  , while the IL-treated film has a large contact angle of   $  42.9^{\circ}  $  . It indicates that the   $  (\mathrm{EMIm})_{x}\mathrm{MA}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_{4})_{x}\mathrm{I}_{1-x}]_{3}  $   surface is more resistant to moisture. As a result of the improved humidity stability of PVK, the device stability of PSCs with the   $  (\mathrm{EMIm})_{x}\mathrm{MA}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_{4})_{x}\mathrm{I}_{1-x}]_{3}  $   interlayer under   $  25^{\circ}C  $   and   $  \sim20\%  $   RH is largely improved compared to the control device (Figure 5c). At the same time, we have also conducted research on the thermal stability of the film. The films with and without IL modification were placed in a glovebox at   $  85^{\circ}C  $   for continuous heating for 12 h, and then compared with the unheated film for XRD characterization, as shown in Figure S8. We can see that there is a   $  PbI_{2}  $   peak at the   $  12^{\circ}  $   position. To quantitatively analyze it, XRD is used to calculate the   $  PbI_{2}  $   content: 17% for the control film and 13% for the IL-treated film. Therefore, we conclude that the thermal stability of the device is improved to a certain extent after the   $  (\mathrm{EMIm})_{x}\mathrm{MA}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_{4})_{x}\mathrm{I}_{1-x}]_{3}  $   interlayer is formed but it is too thin to be effective to prevent the thermal decomposition of   $  MA^{+}  $  . But what is interesting is that after long-term annealing, the IL-treated sample has a higher perovskite lattice orientation (110) than the control after long-term annealing, which is consistent with our previous statement. The interlayer is beneficial to the crystallinity of the perovskite.

# 3. CONCLUSIONS

In summary,  $ \mathrm{EMImBF_4} $  IL is treated on a  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  surface to induce the formation of the  $ (\mathrm{EMIm})_x\mathrm{MA}_{1 - x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_4)_x\mathrm{I}_{1 - x}]_3 $  interlayer. It is found out that the formation of the new interlayer induces secondary grain growth. This is due to the interactions of  $ \mathrm{BF_4}^- $  and  $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $  and  $ \mathrm{EMIm}^+ $  and  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2 + } $ , which causes the grain boundary migration to form bigger crystal grains. The formation of the  $ (\mathrm{EMIm})_x\mathrm{MA}_{1 - x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_4)_x\mathrm{I}_{1 - x}]_3 $  interlayer indicates that  $ \mathrm{EMIm}^+ $  and  $ \mathrm{BF_4}^- $  can fill the  $ \mathrm{V_{MA}} $  and  $ \mathrm{V_I} $  vacancies, respectively. It is also proved that N in an imidazole group passivates the undercoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2 + } $  defect. Because of the reduction of GBs and defect states, the nonradiative recombination is largely reduced. The PSCs with the  $ (\mathrm{EMIm})_x\mathrm{MA}_{1 - x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_4)_x\mathrm{I}_{1 - x}]_3 $  interlayer indicate  $ 19.0\% $  efficiency with higher photovoltage and fill factor than the control device. Owing to the hydrophobicity of the  $ (\mathrm{EMIm})_x\mathrm{MA}_{1 - x}\mathrm{Pb}[(\mathrm{BF}_4)_x\mathrm{I}_{1 - x}]_3 $  interlayer, the unencapsulated device stability is much better than the control device.

# 4. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

4.1. Device Fabrication. Our group has described the synthesis and preparation of NiO nanoparticles in detail in the previous article report.  $  ^{35}  $   PVK film deposition is a routine process, as indicated in ref 36. The EMImBF  $  _{4}  $   (MeOH) solution is spin-coated on a PVK layer, with thermal annealing at 100 °C for 5 min. Then, the PC  $  _{61}  $  BM and BCP layers were deposited on the surface of the perovskite film. Finally, the Ag is evaporated to a thickness of 80 nm to form an electrode.

4.2. Characterization. XRD patterns were collected using a Bruker instrument (D8 advance, Germany). The surface morphologies and cross-sectional structure morphology were studied by SEM (Hitachi, SU-70, Japan). The ATR-FTIR spectra were recorded by the Micro-FTIR (Agilent, Cary660). The binding energies of the films were analyzed by XPS (Shimadzu, Japan). The PL of the films on glasses was checked by a fluorescence spectrophotometer (Agilent Cary Eclipse) using a 532 nm incident light to excite, while the TRPL spectra were observed by a Horiba PL spectrometer (Japan) with a 457 nm light to excite. The optical absorbance of the films on glass was measured by a UV-IR spectrophotometer (Agilent Cary 5000). J-V characteristics were measured by the Keithley 4200 m together with a sunlight simulator (Newport Solar Simulator 3A, AM 1.5, 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) calibrated with a standard silicon reference cell.
