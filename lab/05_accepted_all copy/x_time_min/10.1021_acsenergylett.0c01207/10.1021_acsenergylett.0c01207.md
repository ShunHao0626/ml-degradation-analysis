# ABSTRACT

Ion migration represents an intrinsic instability of metal halide perovskite solar cells. Here we show that triple-cation   $  FA_{x}MA_{y}Cs_{1-x-y}PbI_{3}  $     $  [FA^{+}=(NH_{2})_{2}CH^{+}, MA^{+}=CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}]  $   active layers with mixed orthorhombic, post-perovskite (  $  \delta_{ortho}-CsPbI_{3}  $  ) and cubic perovskite (  $  \alpha  $  ) phases (i.e.   $  \alpha/\delta  $  -phase   $  FA_{x}MA_{y}Cs_{1-x-y}PbI_{3}  $  ) exhibit improved cation stability against applied bias relative to pure   $  \alpha  $  -phase perovskites (i.e.   $  FA_{0.85}Cs_{0.15}PbI_{3}  $   and   $  FA_{0.76}MA_{0.15}Cs_{0.09}PbI_{3}  $  ). Infrared photothermal heterodyne imaging and time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry are used to visualize exclusive   $  \alpha  $  -phase perovskite lateral device   $  A^{+}  $  cation accumulation (depletion) at perovskite negative (positive) electrode interfaces. Operational stability testing of solar cells reveals similar degradation behavior;   $  \alpha/\delta  $  -phase   $  FA_{x}MA_{y}Cs_{1-x-y}PbI_{3}  $   lateral devices/solar cells, by contrast, show improved stabilities. Enhanced   $  \alpha/\delta  $  -  $  FA_{x}MA_{y}Cs_{1-x-y}PbI_{3}  $   stability is rationalized by   $  \delta_{ortho}  $  -phase inclusions, acting as barriers through which   $  A^{+}  $  cations do not easily migrate. This study thus provides new insights into cation migration in   $  FA_{x}MA_{y}Cs_{1-x-y}PbI_{3}  $   perovskites and suggests a materials design strategy towards suppressing cation instabilities in hybrid perovskites.

Mixed cation and anion   $  APbX_{3}  $  -type lead halide perovskites   $  [A^{+}=Cs^{+}, CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}[MA^{+}]  $  , or   $  (\mathrm{NH}_{2})_{2}\mathrm{CH}^{+}[\mathrm{FA}^{+}];\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{Br}^{-}  $   or I  $  ^{-}  $  ] have received extensive attention for use as low cost, efficient light harvesters in solar cells.  $  ^{1-5}  $   Many issues regarding long-term active layer stability, however, remain unresolved.  $  ^{6}  $   Electric field-induced ion migration in perovskite solar cells is of particular concern because it results in current-voltage   $  (J-V)  $   hysteresis, metastable power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) and accelerated active layer degradation.  $  ^{7-16}  $   Facile ion migration within the perovskite lattice stems from relatively fragile ionic bonding between heavy, low-valence ions. This results in small activation barriers and correspondingly moderate diffusion coefficients for halide anions and A-site cations.  $  ^{9-17}  $   The mobile nature of   $  A^{+}  $   cations and   $  X^{-}  $   anions is thus an instability of all metal halide perovskites. While numerous recent studies have focused on halide migration  $  ^{9-16}  $   and segregation  $  ^{18-21}  $  , significantly less work has gone into investigating cation migration in metal halide perovskites.  $  ^{22-24}  $   This is especially true of double- and triple-cation   $  FA_{x}MA_{y}Cs_{1-x-y}PbX_{3}  $   alloys.  $  ^{25,26}  $

$ \mathrm{APbX_3} $ -type perovskites are constructed through corner-sharing  $ \mathrm{PbX_6}^{4-} $  octahedra with  $ \mathrm{A}^+ $  cations that fill their interstitial voids to act as steric stabilizers. They assume either perfect cubic

Yet, despite this, working devices are still subject to the effects of ion migration.

Schelhas et al.  $  ^{25}  $  have recently demonstrated that  $  A^{+}  $  in both  $  FA_{0.85}Cs_{0.15}PbI_{3}  $  and  $  FA_{0.76}MA_{0.15}Cs_{0.09}PbI_{3}  $  solar cells undergo perovskite  $  \alpha  $ -phase/post-perovskite  $  \delta  $ -phase segregation via in situ X-ray diffraction measurements under simulated solar illumination. This resistance to cation migration translates into improved device operational stability of  $ \alpha/\delta $ - $ \mathrm{FA_{0.33}MA_{0.33}Cs_{0.33}PbI_3} $  lateral devices and solar cells relative to complementary, triple-cation,  $ \alpha $ -phase  $ \mathrm{FA_{0.76}MA_{0.15}Cs_{0.09}PbI_3} $  devices. As such, the study points to possible new approaches for mitigating the effects of cation migration in working perovskites through compositional microstructuring of their thin films.
