# Solution-Processed   $  Cu_{9}S_{5}  $   as Hole Transport Layer for Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

# ABSTRACT

Organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells have tremendous developments in recent years. As a hole transport material (HTM), 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) is widely used in n-i-p perovskite solar cells. However, it may lead to the perovskite film degradation due to the dopant of lithium bis((trifluomethyl)sulfonyl)amide (Li-TFSI) which has strong hydrophilicity.  $ \mathrm{Cu_9S_5} $  is considered as a superior p-type transport material, which also has the favorable energy level matching with the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of Spiro-OMeTAD. Herein, solution-processed organic-inorganic integrated hole transport layer (HTL) was reported, which

# INTRODUCTION

Tremendous developments have emerged in the field of perovskite solar cells recently. $ ^{1-5} $  As a widely used material in perovskite solar cells,  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  has excellent properties. $ ^{6-8} $  In recent years, the modifications of the perovskite solar cells have been extensively studied and the PCE has been enhanced up over  $ 22\% $ . $ ^{9-12} $  However, the long-term stability of the device is still an urgent problem to be solved.

To date, Spiro-OMeTAD has been widely used as the hole-transporting material (HTM).  $  ^{13,14}  $   However, it suffers from low hole mobility and low conductivity, and therefore requires doping to perform efficiently. 4-Tert-butylpyridine (tBP) and Li-TFSI which has strong hydrophilicity, have been generally doped into Spiro-OMeTAD. Thus, the solar cell devices suffer a severe degradation because the perovskite material   $  \left(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{PbI}_{3}\right)  $   would quickly degrade to   $  PbI_{2}  $   when exposed to moisture.  $  ^{15}  $   In order to improve the stability, it is of great importance to explore a hydrophobic HTM. Up to now, some researches about dopant-free HTM have been reported. In

Cu $ _{9} $ S $ _{5} $  is a p-type transport material with suitable energy level matching. $ ^{20,21} $  We believe that it can applied as an effective HTL due to its intrinsic high mobility. In this paper, a stable n-i-p planar perovskite solar cell with undoped Spiro-OMeTAD/Cu $ _9 $ S $ _5 $  as HTL and SnO $ _2 $  implanted nitrogen ions (N:SnO $ _2 $ ) as ETL is reported. Our results showed that the double-layered Spiro-OMeTAD/Cu $ _9 $ S $ _5 $  applied as HTM can improve the stability of n-i-p perovskite solar cells. In the meantime, we also developed a method to adjust the LUMO of SnO $ _2 $  via nitrogen implantation, through which N:SnO $ _2 $  was available. As a commonly used technique, ion implantation is widely applied in doping inorganic semiconductor materials. $ ^{23-25} $  FTO with tunable work function was achieved via different fluence of zirconium ion implantation and was applied in p-i-n perovskite solar cell in our previous work. $ ^{26} $  On this basis, we believe that it is also feasible to dope the transport layer by ion implantation. # RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

$ \mathrm{Cu_9S_5} $  nanoparticles were prepared by following the previous report via using the precursor solution hot inject method. The characterization of structure, absorption and bandgap of  $ \mathrm{Cu_9S_5} $  are shown in Figure 1. The peaks in the powder XRD patterns of  $ \mathrm{Cu_9S_5} $  nanoparticles are consistent with the previous literature data, which confirms the formation of  $ \mathrm{Cu_9S_5} $ . The calculated optical band-gap of  $ \mathrm{Cu_9S_5} $  film is shown in Figure 1b. The  $ \mathrm{Cu_9S_5} $  thin film has a optical band-gap of  $ \sim 1.73 $  eV. As given in Figure 1c, the HOMO of  $ \mathrm{Cu_9S_5} $  film was calculated as  $ -5.15 $  eV, which exactly match the HOMO of Spiro-OMeTAD (-5.20 eV).

Figure 3a displays the J-V curves of the solar cells. The measurement was carried out under simulated AM 1.5G solar irradiation at  $ 100\mathrm{mW/cm^2} $ , which indicated that the device with HTL of undoped Spiro-OMeTAD /  $ \mathrm{Cu_9S_5} $  nanoparticles gives  $ V_{oc} $  of  $ 1.03\mathrm{V} $ ,  $ J_{sc} $  of  $ 22.35\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $  and PCE of  $ 17.07\% $ , which is nearly equivalent to the device with Li-TFSI doped Spiro-OMeTAD ( $ V_{oc} $  of  $ 1.04\mathrm{V} $ ,  $ J_{sc} $  of  $ 22.33\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $  and PCE of  $ 17.17\% $ ). As for the solar cell with single  $ \mathrm{Cu_9S_5} $  nanoparticles or undoped Spiro-OMeTAD as HTL, the performance was not so good. According to our experiment, although the spin-coated  $ \mathrm{Cu_9S_5} $  layer has favorable energy level and superior p-type properties, the film is relatively thin ( $ \sim 10\mathrm{nm} $ ). While the spin-coated Spiro-OMeTAD can form an approximately  $ 130\mathrm{nm} $  film, which is thick enough to cover the perovskite film. Thus, it is considered that the excellent PCE was achieved by the synergistic effect of the double layer HTM. Figure S1 presents the statistics PCE data for 24 individual Spiro-OMeTAD/ $ \mathrm{Cu_9S_5} $  based perovskite solar cells. The average PCE is  $ 16.33\% $  and the maximum PCE is  $ 17.10\% $ . We also recorded the long-term stability of devices with different HTL for  $ 1200\mathrm{h} $ , which were stored in air atmosphere without encapsulation (Temperature:  $ 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , Humidity:  $ 20 - 30\% $ ). The devices were tested every 50 hours in the first  $ 200\mathrm{h} $  and every 200 hours after  $ 200\mathrm{h} $ . The normalized evolution of PCE are shown in Figure 3b. For the device based on the traditional Li-TFSI doped Spiro-OMeTAD HTL, the PCE decrease slightly in the first  $ 200\mathrm{h} $ . Starting from  $ 200\mathrm{h} $ , the decrease in device performance was gradually accelerated. After  $ 1200\mathrm{h} $  of storage, the device could only retain about  $ 60\% $  of its initial PCE. In contrast, as for the undoped Spiro-OMeTAD/ $ \mathrm{Cu_9S_5} $  device, there had been only a slight decrease. After  $ 1200\mathrm{h} $  storage in ambient environment, it could still retain about  $ 96\% $  of its initial PCE, showing excellent stability. The ion implantation process of N:SnO $ _2 $  was displayed in Figure 4a, that is, 30 keV nitrogen ion beam with the fluence of  $ 1 \times 10^{15} $  ions/cm $ ^2 $  was implanted to SnO $ _2 $  film, followed by thermal treatment at 150 °C for 30 minutes. Finally, N:SnO $ _2 $  could be obtained. The distribution of nitrogen implanted to SnO $ _2 $  films was calculated by the software ‘the stopping and range of ions in matter’ (SRIM), which was displayed in Figure S3. The energy and fluence of the ion beam correspond to the dopant concentration and depth, respectively.

Figure 4b presents the charge transfer at ETL/perovskite film interface. To figure out the energy level of SnO $ _2 $  and N:SnO $ _2 $ , ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) was carried out to analyze the states of SnO $ _2 $  and N:SnO $ _2 $ . The value of Femi level is calculated by the equation E $ _F $  = 21.2 - E $ _B $ , where E $ _B $  is the high binding energy cutoff (Figure 4c and 4d). The valence band maximum (E $ _{VBM} $ ) can be calculated by the equation E $ _{VBM} $  = E $ _F $  + E $ _A $ , where E $ _A $  is the low binding energy onset. The conduction band minimum (E $ _{CBM} $ ) is calculated through the band-gap, which is available through absorption spectroscopy. The value of the band-gap for SnO $ _2 $  and N:SnO $ _2 $  is 3.81 eV and 3.76 eV, respectively (Figure 4e and 4f). According to Figure 4c-f, the E $ _{VBM} $  and E $ _{CBM} $  of SnO $ _2 $  is -8.14 eV and -4.33 eV, which is in good accordance with literature data for SnO $ _2 $ . $ ^{28} $  As for N:SnO $ _2 $ , the E $ _{VBM} $  and E $ _{CBM} $  is -7.67 eV and -3.91 eV. Carriers are injected into the ETL, and then transport to ITO. The results of E $ _{CBM} $  indicate that N:SnO $ _2 $  has more favorable energy level matching at N:SnO $ _2 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  interface than that of SnO $ _2 $ , which further optimized electron transport for SnO $ _2 $ . The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements of SnO $ _2 $  and N:SnO $ _2 $  based devices have been carried out at 0.94 V in the dark to verify the charge transfer at the ETL/perovskite interface. # CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we reveal a solution processed Spiro-OMeTAD/Cu $ _9 $ S $ _5 $  layer as HTM to improve the stability of perovskite solar cells. In addition, the energy level of Cu $ _9 $ S $ _5 $  also matches well with Spiro-OMeTAD. A n-i-p perovskite solar cell has been fabricated via a double structured HTL of undoped Spiro-OMeTAD/Cu $ _9 $ S $ _5 $  without additional additive to avoid severe degradation from the permeation of moisture. Since there is no hydrophilic additive, it is conductive to the long-term stability. In another site, N:SnO $ _2 $  was applied as ETL of the perovskite solar cells. The  $ J-V $  curve hysteresis of the device was reduced due to the favorable energy level matching of N:SnO $ _2 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  interface which results from a better charge transfer. Finally, it achieved a PCE of 17.10% and retained about 96 % of PCE after 1200 hours in ambient atmosphere without any encapsulation. Herein, we develop a method which could effectively reduce the hysteresis and improve the stability of perovskite solar cells by employing N:SnO $ _2 $  ETL and double HTL Spiro-OMeTAD/Cu $ _9 $ S $ _5 $  without additional doping.

Perovskite solar cells fabrication. The preparation of ITO substrates and perovskite film have been reported in our previous work. $ ^{26} $  SnO $ _2 $  (Tin (IV) oxide, 15% in H $ _2 $ O colloidal dispersion, Alfa Aesar) was spin-coated on the ITO glass at 3000 rpm for 30 seconds, followed by annealing at 150 °C for 30 minutes. Then the SnO $ _2 $  was treated via nitrogen ion implantation. The perovskite film was prepared by a low pressure assisted method. $ ^{32,33} $  Spiro-OMeTAD powder was dissolved in chlorobenzene (70 mg/mL) and then it was spin-coated on the perovskite film at 4000 rpm for 30 s. As for the Li-TFSI doped Spiro-OMeTAD, a 17.5 μL/mL Li-TFSI (520 mg/mL, in acetonitrile) and 29 μL/mL tBP were added to Spiro-OMeTAD solution. Cu $ _9 $ S $ _5 $  nanoparticles were dispersed in 1-butanol (20 mg/mL), then it was spin-coated on the undoped Spiro-OMeTAD film at 3000 rpm for 30 s (Figure S5). Au layers was deposited via high-vacuum thermal evaporation. 70 nm layer of Au was deposited as the anode at a constant speed. Finally, perovskite solar cell with the configuration of ITO/ SnO $ _2 $ / CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ / undoped Spiro-OMeTAD/ Cu $ _9 $ S $ _5 $ / Au was obtained.
