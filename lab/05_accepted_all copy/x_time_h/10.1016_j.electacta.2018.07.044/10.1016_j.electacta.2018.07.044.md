# 1. Introduction

Since organolead halide perovskite material was firstly used as light harvester in a liquid-electrolyte-based dye-sensitized solar cell in 2009 [1], it had received tremendous attention due to its unique properties such as solution-processable, efficient light absorption, bipolar transport, long exciton diffusion length (  $  >1 \mu m  $  ) and carrier lifetime, tunable bandgap as well as weak exciton binding energy (<50 meV) [2–7]. In the past few years, the PCE of perovskite solar cells has attained to 22.1% from the initial value of 3.8% [8–10]. Apart from the pristine merits of perovskite materials like methylammonium lead triiodide (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  ) itself, this great progress can be mainly ascribed to modification of materials for each functional layer, process improvement like solvent engineering and surface engineering, etc. done by all the researchers [11–15]. # ABSTRACT

Electron transport layer (ETL), functioning as the electron transportation and extraction layer as well as hole-blocking layer, plays an important role in planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Majority of the state-of-the-art PSCs contain a   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL always requiring a high temperature (500 °C) treatment, which is both energy-consuming and incompatible with flexible substrates, thus hindering large-scale application in flexible devices. Here, we demonstrated a low-temperature (70 °C) solution-processed Mg-doped rutile   $  TiO_{2}  $   as ETL in planar PSCs, while the thermal-evaporated copper phthalocyanine (CuPc) and doctor-bladed carbon were introduced as the hole transport layer (HTL) and counter electrode, respectively. The as-prepared PSC obtains a 15.73% power conversion efficiency (PCE), which is quite an excellent efficiency among carbon-based planar PSCs, getting an increase by 16% compared to the 13.56% PCE of the pristine   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based device. The prominent increment is mainly attributed to the faster charge extraction, better electrical conductivity and suppressed charge recombination of Mg-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $  . Besides, using highly stable CuPc and commercial carbon makes the as-prepared PSCs highly durable over 30 days when exposed to the ambient air with a relative humidity of 50%. Since all the processes are conducted under 100 °C, our work paves a way for developing cost-effective and highly stable PSCs compatible with flexible substrates.

Typically, the structure of PSCs can be classified into n-i-p type and p-i-n type, in both of which the perovskites are sandwiched between the ETL and HTL. As a vital functional layer in PSCs, ETL is always supposed to fulfill several requirements: (i) high transparency to ensure high incident light transmittance; (ii) suitable conduction band (CB) matched with the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level of perovskite to facilitate charge transport and deep valence band to efficiently block holes [16]. (iii) excellent charge-extraction ability; (iv) low surface roughness to facilitate the perovskite crystallization [17]. In the previous researches, several materials have been used as ETL, such as:  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  [18],  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  [19],  $ \mathrm{WO_x} $  [20] [6,6],-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) [21], etc. Among them, the most commonly used is compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  for its superior properties: low cost, easy to prepare, befitting CB and valence band (VB) level, excellent transparency as well as high physical and chemical stability. It is also worth mentioning that the most efficient PSC reported to date right uses compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  as ETL with a conventional mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  as scaffold [10]. $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL are spin coating and spray pyrolysis. But, these methods always call for a high temperature treatment, making them incompatible with flexible substrates and thus setting a limitation for industrial scale roll-to-roll fabrication. In order to eliminate the above-mentioned obstacle, many low-temperature processes have been explored to prepare  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL, such as direct spin-coating of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanoparticle dispersion [22], magnetron sputtering [17] and atomic layer deposition (ALD) [23]. Recently, a new chemical bath deposition (CBD) method for  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  growth which only requires an operation temperature as low as  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  was reported. Aswani et al. employed as-prepared  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  as ETL together with Spiro-OMeTAD as HTL and Au as cathode, and finally achieved a PCE of  $ 13.7\% $  [24]. Liang et al. obtained a PCE of  $ 12.6\% $  by using the same structure with the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL treated by ultraviolet ozone [25]. Besides, Yan, et al. achieved a PCE of  $ 11.6\% $  by using CBD processed  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  with multilayer graphene as counter electrode [26]. To further improve the conductivity and decrease the numerous trap states of the pristine  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , additive-doping method has been widely used. For instance, graphene nanoflakes [27] and graphene quantum dots [28] have been introduced as additives to increase the conductivity and carriers extraction of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL. Moreover,  $ \mathrm{Y}^{3+} $  [29],  $ \mathrm{Mg}^{2+} $  [30,31],  $ \mathrm{Li}^{+} $  [32] as effective ion dopants were also introduced to  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL to tailor the energy band structure, increase the conductivity and decrease the trap density. Nevertheless, these ion doping methods always need a high temperature treatment or a complex sol-gel process, which will indeed increase the cost and process complexity.

As for another crucial component of PSCs, HTL plays a significant part in facilitating hole transportation. Up to date, highly efficient PSCs generally employ a p-type organic small-molecule or polymeric hole conductor, such as 2, 2', 7, -7'-tetrakis (N, N'-di-p-methoy-phenylamine)-9, 9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) [19,24,33], Poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA) [34], poly-3-hexylthiophene (P3HT) [35], poly (3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene): polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) [36], as hole transporting material (HTM). However, these organic HTMs always suffer from disadvantages of high cost, low conductivity as well as low stability against thermal and moisture in their pristine form. Besides, the corresponding doping techniques to improve their carrier density and conductivity further increase the synthetic-cost and instability of the HTMs [37,38]. Thus, seeking for new HTMs with high moisture and thermal stability, low cost as well as high hole mobility for PSCs is still going on [39].

In this study, a low-temperature CBD method is employed to prepare   $  TiO_{2}  $   (rutile) ETL for PSCs, in which the Titanium tetrachloride (  $  TiCl_{4}  $  ) is used as the only titanium source. In the meantime, magnesium as an effective ion dopant is introduced to modify the physical and optoelectronic properties of   $  TiO_{2}  $ . It is found that Mg doping can effectively facilitate the charge transport and suppress charge carrier recombination between the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL and perovskite light absorber. Therefore, there is a prominent increment in the short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) as well as the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of Mg-doped devices. The optimized device obtained a PCE of 15.73%, which is comparable to or even better than the device with a structure of FTO/rutile   $  TiO_{2}  $ /perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD/Au. Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS), steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TR-PL) measurements are taken to shed light on the underlying mechanisms for the enhanced performance of PSCs.

N, N-Dimethylformamide (DMF, ≥99.8%), Dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO, ≥99.8%),   $  TiCl_{4}  $   (≥98%) and anhydrous   $  MgCl_{2}  $   (99.9%) were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. Chlorobenzene (≥99.8%) was from Alfar Aesar. CuPc was bought from Aladdin and the commercial carbon paste (solvent is DBE, a mixture of Dimethyl succinate, Dimethyl glutarate and Dimethyl adipate) was from Shenzhen Dongdalai Chemical Co., Ltd. All the chemicals and reagents were directly used without any further purification.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

The conductive fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass substrates (NSG-10) were chemically etched with 2 M hydrochloric acid and zinc powder. Then, the FTO substrates were sequentially cleaned with acetone, anhydrous alcohol and deionized water in an ultrasonic bath for 15 min, followed by an O $  _{3}  $ /ultraviolet treatment for 30 min to remove the organic residues. To prepare the precursor solution for TiO $  _{2}  $  to grow, an aqueous stock solution of TiCl $  _{4}  $  (98%, stored in the freezer at 0 °C) was diluted to 200 mM with different MgCl $  _{2}  $  concentration (0, 0.01, 0.025, 0.04, 0.055 M) being added. The dissolution process lasted for a few minutes in ice bath with stirring until a homogeneous solution was formed. The cleaned FTO substrates were then placed vertically in a glass container filled with precursor solution and kept at 70 °C for 3 h in a thermostat water bath. Afterwards, the treated substrates were rinsed with deionized water in a sonication bath for 2 min to remove any loosely bound materials, and then dried at 100 °C in air for 1 h. The perovskite layer was deposited on the TiO $  _{2}  $  photoanode layer by a spin-coating method as reported previously [40]. In detail, the mixed-cation perovskite solution was prepared from a precursor solution containing FAI (1 M), PbI $  _{2}  $  (1.1 M), MABr (0.2 M) and PbBr $  _{2}  $  (0.2 M) in a 4:1 (volume ratio) mixture of anhydrous DMF: DMSO. The pre-dissolved 1.5 M stock solution of CsI in DMSO was added to the above perovskite precursor in 5:95 vol ratio to achieve the desired composition. Then the as-prepared solution was heated at 60 °C for half an hour with stirring. This triple cation perovskite solution was spin coated on the substrates through a consecutive two-step process at 1000 and 5000 rpm for 10 and 22 s respectively, with 130 μL chlorobenzene as anti-solvent being quickly dripped on the substrates 5 s prior to the end of the second spinning process. The spin-coated substrates were then annealed at 100 °C for 45 min to obtain high crystalline perovskite film in a nitrogen-filled glove box. In order to obtain large perovskite grains, a solvent assisted annealing procedure was then conducted as follows: the film was covered by a petri dish during the whole annealing process, followed by inserting 3 μL DMF in the edge of the petri dish. To prepare the HTL 35 nm CuPc was thermally evaporated on the Glass/FTO/TiO $  _{2}  $ /perovskite substrate under a base pressure of  $  3 \times 10^{-8}  $  Torr at a speed of ~1 Å s $  ^{-1}  $  determined by a quartz crystal monitor. Finally, the commercial carbon paste was deposited on the prepared film by doctor-blading technique and then dried at 85 °C for 15 min to form the counter electrode of the PSC.

# 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we demonstrated a low temperature processed and easily prepared homogeneous Mg-doped rutile   $  TiO_{2}  $   as excellent ETL for efficient carbon-based planar PSCs. The optimized Mg (0.04)-  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL-based device demonstrated a champion PCE of as high as 15.73%, which is quite an excellent efficiency among carbon-based planar PSCs, with a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.26 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.025 V and a FF of 0.689, showing an increase of 16% in PCE compared to the 13.56% PCE of the none-doped device. This salient enhancement stems from the improved surface morphology, higher electron conductivity, and suppressed charge carrier recombination as well as enhanced charge extraction. In addition, the introduction of cost-effective and highly stable CuPc as HTL and commercial carbon as counter electrode can not only bring down the cost, but also enhance the device stability, making the as-prepared PSCs retain over 91% of their initial PCE after being exposed to ambient air for 30 days. With the merits of low cost, low-temperature processing, ease-of-fabrication as well as high efficiency and excellent stability, our device shows great potential in achieving its commercial application in the near future.
