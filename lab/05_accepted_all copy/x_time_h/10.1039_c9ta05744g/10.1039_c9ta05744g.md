# Abstract

Methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI $ _3 $ ) perovskite commonly used in hole-transport material (HTM)-free carbon-based perovskite solar cells (C-PSCs) is ambient-unstable, and its glove box preparation process is essential but incompatible with the low-cost mass-production of C-PSCs. Although mixed cation/halide type perovskites are more durable against exposure to heat and moisture compared with single cation MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite, the air-processed fabrication of stable mixed perovskite films out of glove box has yet to be achieved in C-PSCs. We herein fabricate a multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs)-incorporated FA $ _x $ MA $ _{1-x} $ PbI $ _y $ Br $ _{3-y} $  perovskite film in ambient air, in which hydrophobic MWCNTs are firstly introduced into the mixed perovskite matrix to mediate the crystallization of FA $ _x $ MA $ _{1-x} $ PbI $ _y $ Br $ _{3-y} $  and resist the erosion of moisture into perovskite layer. Based on the as-prepared MWCNTs-incorporated FA $ _x $ MA $ _{1-x} $ PbI $ _y $ Br $ _{3-y} $ , the whole C-PSCs with the structure of ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /perovskite/C are constructed in actual ambient air condition with low annealing temperature <150 °C, achieving outstanding power conversion efficiencies of 16.25% (0.08 cm $ ^2 $ ) and 12.34% (1 cm $ ^2 $ ). Particularly, the all-air-processed mixed cation C-PSCs maintains the long-term stability of 93% under ambient air conditions during 22 weeks, exhibiting the best ambient-air stability of PSCs fabricated in air so far. This study provides a feasible route for the commercialization development of low-cost, stable and efficient PSCs.

# Introduction

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted intensive attention with their impressive power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to 24.2%,  $  ^{1-6}  $  but the problems concerning stability and cost still hinder its commercialization development.  $  ^{7-10}  $  Hole transport material (HTM)-free carbon-based perovskite solar cells (C-PSCs) are arguably the most promising for commercialization applications because of their low cost, outstanding photovoltaic performance, excellent stability and simple manufacturing process.  $  ^{11-13}  $  Since Han and colleagues firstly fabricated the C-PSCs by infiltrating methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ ) into high-temperature processing device with configuration of TiO  $  _{2}  $ /ZrO  $  _{2}  $ /C, the PCEs of the C-PSCs have climbed from the initial 6.6% to the optimized 15.6% by adding ammonium chloride additive into the perovskite precursor solution to assist the crystallization of perovskite.  $  ^{11, 14, 15}  $  Further, to reduce the energy consumption cost of C-PSCs, a low-temperature carbon electrode based PSCs with the structure of FTO/TiO  $  _{2}  $ /MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ /C was developed by Yang and coworkers, yielding the highest reported PCE of 14.38% for C-PSCs up to then.  $  ^{16}  $  Recently, our group constructed a series of low-temperature processed C-PSC based on the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskite, achieving a remarkable PCE of 16.03%.  $  ^{17-19}  $  Despite these achievements, it should be pointed out that the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  absorber layer in C-PSCs is commonly fabricated in the glove box, which is incompatible with the low-cost mass-production of C-PSCs.

In comparison with single cation   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite, mixed cation/halide type perovskites have been demonstrated to be more durable against exposure to heat and moisture.  $  ^{20}  $   Accordingly, traditional PSCs with organic HTM based on the air-processed mixed perovskites out of glove box have been successfully fabricated.  $  ^{20-27}  $   For instance, Lan et al. prepared the mixed   $  MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   perovskite films in air by partly replacing MAI with MACl, whose devices with the structure of   $  FTO/TiO_{2}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}/spiro-OMeTAD/Au  $   could only maintain the stability of 70% for 96 h under air condition.  $  ^{21}  $   Under same device structure, the improved air-processed mixed   $  MAPbI_{3-x}(SCN)_{x}  $   perovskite device prepared by Yan et al. maintained the stability of 86.7% for 500 h under air condition.  $  ^{22}  $   Especially, Singh and Miyasaka

Besides, incorporation of a small amount of carbon materials (e. g. carbon nanotubes (CNTs), graphdiyne and graphene quantum dots) into perovskites has been reported to be valid to improve the morphology and crystallinity of perovskite in the glove box in traditional PSCs with organic HTM, such as the CNTs-incorporated single cation  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite, the graphdiyne-incorporated  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite or mixed  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.85}\mathrm{MA}_{0.15}\mathrm{Pb(I}_{0.85}\mathrm{Br}_{0.15})_3 $  perovskites.[28-30] This prompted us to hypothesize that carbon materials may be introduced into mixed perovskite to assist the crystallization of perovskite out of glove box for C-PSCs. Particularly, the fabricated mixed cation C-PSCs maintains the long-term stability of  $ 93\% $  during 22 weeks, exhibiting the best ambient-air-stable performance among PSCs fabricated in air so far.[20-27]

# Results and Discussion

The mixed perovskite films were fabricated via a two-step deposition (Fig. S1†).  $  ^{31, 32}  $   First, a yellow transparent dense PbI  $  _{2}  $   film was deposited from a PbI  $  _{2}  $  /DMSO/DMF solution onto the ITO/SnO  $  _{2}  $   substrate. Then the FAI:MABr precursor in IPA solvent was spin-coated on the pre-deposited PbI  $  _{2}  $   film via the inter-diffusion process to form the mixed FA  $  _{x}  $  MA  $  _{1-x}  $  PbI  $  _{y}  $  Br  $  _{3-y}  $   perovskite films. The single cation MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite was also prepared in parallel using the above same method. Scanning electron microscope (SEM) and X-ray diffraction (XRD) of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   and FA  $  _{x}  $  MA  $  _{1-x}  $  PbI  $  _{y}  $  Br  $  _{3-y}  $   films are presented in Fig. S2†. By comparison, the FA  $  _{x}  $  MA  $  _{1-x}  $  PbI  $  _{y}  $  Br  $  _{3-y}  $   perovskite film shows larger grain size with good crystallinity (Fig. S2a, b†). XRD patterns (Fig. S2c†) also show intensified peaks for the FA  $  _{x}  $  MA  $  _{1-x}  $  PbI  $  _{y}  $  Br  $  _{3-y}  $   perovskite compared to the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ , indicating a higher crystallinity formation of perovskite with the mixed cations. Besides, a diffraction peak (12.5°) of PbI  $  _{2}  $   can be detected in the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite film, indicating the degradation of pure MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite in the air fabrication process. This verifies the better stability of FA  $  _{x}  $  MA  $  _{1-x}  $  PbI  $  _{y}  $  Br  $  _{3-y}  $   against moisture compared to MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite.  $  ^{20}  $   To obtain more stable mixed cationic perovskite in ambient air condition, a small amount of MWCNTs dispersion liquid (0.5% and 1.0% in volume fraction) was added into the FAI:MABr precursor solution to further improve the perovskite morphology and crystallinity. SEM images of the mixed FA  $  _{x}  $  MA  $  _{1-x}  $  PbI  $  _{y}  $  Br  $  _{3-y}  $   perovskite films with different MWCNTs (W/O, 0.5% and 1.0%) are presented in Fig. 1a-c. From Fig. 1a, one can see that pristine perovskite particles are 100-600 nm in size with a great number of grain boundaries. Compared to the pristine perovskite without MWCNTs, the perovskite with 0.5% MWCNTs shows visibly larger crystallite dimension (Fig. 1b). As reported in the literature,  $  ^{33}  $   MWCNTs filled in the boundary between perovskite grains as templates may retard the transformation kinetics of perovskite beneficial to more defect-free crystal growth of perovskite, hence resulting in increased perovskite grain size and improved perovskite crystallinity. Such high-quality perovskite films obtained by incorporating MWCNTs into the perovskite layer have also been observed in the conventional PSCs.  $  ^{29, 34, 35}  $   It is

well-known that moisture can easily penetrate the grain boundaries to degrade the perovskite film.  $  ^{36}  $   Thus, enlarging the perovskite particle size is effective solution to improve the stability of air-processed perovskite. Additionally, a small amount of MWCNTs is observed out of the surface of the perovskite, which can bridge the perovskite layer with carbon electrode to facilitate the charge transfer. To intuitively compare the flatness of perovskite films, atomic force microscope (AFM) images of perovskite films with different MWCNTs on ITO/glass substrates and the corresponding root mean square (RMS) roughness were measured and shown in Fig. 1d-f.  $  ^{37}  $   The RMS roughness of the perovskite film with 0.5% MWCNTs is determined to be 15.9 nm, which is slightly larger than that of the pristine perovskite (11.4 nm). The slightly rough surface of the perovskite film with 0.5% MWCNTs is ascribed to a small amount of exposed MWCNTs on the perovskite surface. Oppositely, excess MWCNTs (1.0%) make the RMS roughness of the perovskite film increase to 28.6 nm, which could be confirmed by the SEM morphology of the involved perovskite film. The wetting capability of water on perovskite films plays an important role in keeping the stability of perovskite films and devices.  $  ^{38}  $   By comparing the contact angles of water on perovskite films with different MWCNTs (Fig. 1g-i), it can be found that the contact angles of water on perovskite film with 0.5% MWCNTs is 90.80°, which is obviously higher than that of pristine perovskite (29.54°), indicating that perovskite film with 0.5% MWCNTs can resist the erosion of moisture effectively.

XRD patterns (Fig. 2a) show that all perovskite films are tetragonal phase with diffraction peaks at 14.2°, 24.6°, 28.5°, 32.0°, 40.5°, and 43.3°, in line with the reflections at (110), (202), (220), (310), (224), and (314), respectively.  $  ^{39-41}  $   Note that the peak intensity at 14.2° and 28.5° becomes stronger after the introduction of MWCNTs, which indicates enhanced crystallinity in perovskite film with 0.5% MWCNTs.  $  ^{42,43}  $   Nevertheless, when the molar ratio of MWCNTs increases to 1.0%, the perovskite film arises a slight signal of PbI  $  _{2}  $   at 12.5° and the crystallinity of the

The measurements of steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra were investigated for the   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite films with different MWCNTs on ITO substrates to study the effect of MWCNTs on the optical properties and charge transfer and recombination (Fig. 2c, d).  $  ^{39}  $   As can be seen from steady-state PL spectra in Fig. 2c, the PL emission peak centered at 794 nm does not change even when the amount of MWCNTs in perovskite films increases, indicating no change of energy bandgap of perovskite. However, the peak intensity is getting weaker in   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite films with 0.5% MWCNTs, indicating that the PL quenching process is enhanced. This indicates reduced defect states of perovskite films with 0.5% MWCNTs, which can lead to the enhanced FF and PCE of the device by decreasing the nonradiative recombination.  $  ^{29}  $   Excess MWCNTs (1.0%) weakens the PL quenching process. At the same time, TRPL spectra are also used to evaluate the photogenerated carrier lifetime (Fig. 2d). The spectra are fitted by biexponential decay function, and the obtained results suggest the existence of two quenching processes including the carrier extraction occurred at the interfaces of   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite with different MWCNTs as well as the carrier trap recombination.  $  ^{28}  $   Detailed fitting parameters are presented in Table S1†. As shown in Fig. 2d, the average carrier lifetime of   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite films with different MWCNTs (W/O, 0.5% and 1.0%) are 16.42, 13.71 and 24.96 ns, respectively. Such results indicate a faster photogenerated carrier extraction for   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite films with 0.5% MWCNTs, which is beneficial for the enhancement of   $  J_{SC}  $   and PCE values.

# Conclusion

In conclusion, we have successfully prepared a stable mixed perovskite film by incorporating MWCNTs into the  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}PbI_yBr_{3 - y}} $  to mediate the crystallization of perovskite and facilitate formation of compact and large-size perovskite film. Besides, hydrophobic MWCNTs exposed on the surface of the perovskite films can also resist the erosion of moisture into perovskite films, and enhance the connection between perovskite layer and carbon electrode. SEM and XRD patterns for 60 days demonstrate the excellent ambient-stability of the  $ 0.5\% $  MWCNTs-incorporated perovskite film. Such an air-processed perovskite is implemented into a stable HTM-free C-PSC with the structure of ITO/SnO2/perovskite/C, achieving a high PCE of  $ 16.25\% $  and superior thermal, photo- and ambient stability. Notably, the MWCNTs-incorporated C-PSCs device exhibits the best ambient-air-stable performance of PSCs fabricated in air so far. # Characterizations

The contact angles of water on the perovskite films were measured using a contact angle measurement (OCA 15pro).
