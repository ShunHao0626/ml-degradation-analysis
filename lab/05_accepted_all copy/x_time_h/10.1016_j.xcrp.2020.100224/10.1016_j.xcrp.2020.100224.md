# SUMMARY

Dissociation of organic molecules is one critical factor leading to degradation of perovskite solar cells. The much-improved thermal stability of formamidinium (FA) can significantly reduce molecular dissociation; however, FA-based perovskite suffers from high defect density, which affects efficiency and long-term stability. In this work, based on a precise ion-exchange reaction at the grain surface, we fabricate a   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}/Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $   heterostructure with a Cs-rich   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $   quasi-shell structure. The Cs-rich inorganic structure on the perovskite surface increases defect formation energy and reduces defect density, leading to a power conversion efficiency of 20.7%. The encapsulated device maintains 95% of its initial efficiency after 1,000 h of continuous operation, corresponding to a calculated lifetime approaching 2 years. The device can operate at 60°C for 250 h and at 85°C for over 3,000 min, which is one of the best operational stabilities for   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $  -based devices.

# INTRODUCTION

Hybrid organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) attract great interest because of their high power conversion efficiency (PCE), easy fabrication, and low cost. Although nowadays the efficiency of PSCs is over 25%, stability needs to be much enhanced to meet application standards.  $  ^{1}  $   Because of its weak ionic bond character, the organic cations easily dissociate, which is a great challenge to overcome, limiting the stability of PSCs.

Most high-efficiency PSCs rely on the use of methylammonium (MA) in the A-site.  $  ^{2,3}  $   However, methyl amine has a low boiling temperature and large vapor pressure, which makes it easy to dissociate and decompose.  $  ^{4,5}  $   Without strict encapsulation, MA could evaporate and cause irreversible changes in the perovskite structure.  $  ^{6}  $   Hence, development of alternative molecules with better thermal stability than MA is important to improve the stability of PSCs.

Formamidinium (FA) lead iodide-based perovskite   $  \left(\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3}\right)  $   attracts much attention because of its much better thermal stability compared with MA.  $  ^{6,7}  $   Significant efforts have been made to develop high-performance   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   PSCs.  $  ^{8}  $   Addition of small monovalent cations, such as   $  Cs^{2,9,10}  $   and Rb,  $  ^{11}  $   stabilizes the perovskite structure and effectively increases device performance. To increase the grain size and reduce the grain boundary at the interface, thiocyanate  $  ^{12,13}  $   or thiol  $  ^{14}  $   was added to the precursor. Inclusion of large organic molecules reduced defect density on the perovskite surface and improved the efficiency and stability of the device.  $  ^{15-17}  $   Use of

Despite significant improvement of device performance, in comparison with widely used components, including MA, MA-free and FA-based perovskites show much lower efficiency; the highest efficiency for inverted and normal structural devices is 20.5%  $  ^{19}  $  and 21.6%,  $  ^{20}  $  respectively. In this work, we fabricate a heterostructural perovskite (HSP) film consisting of a Cs-rich   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $   quasi-shell structure on   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $   grains, using cesium thiocyanate (CsSCN) as a reactant to exchange the surface FA cations with Cs. The inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $  -dominated surface stabilizes the surface structure, leading to increased defect formation energy and reduced defect density. We then fabricate PSCs based on this heterostructure using an inverted device structure. The device exhibits an efficiency of 20.7%, the highest efficiency reported so far for an inverted structural device based on MA-free perovskites. Moreover, the device has much improved stability; it maintains over 80% efficiency after storing under atmospheric conditions for 1,000 hours and over 95% of efficiency after over 1,000 h of continuous operation biased near maximum power point (MPP), which is the best value for   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $  -based inverted structural solar cells. The device also has excellent operational stability at high temperatures of 60°C and 85°C.

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

# Perovskite Film Structure

CsSCN was synthesized using ammonium thiocyanate (NH $ _4 $ SCN) and cesium carbonate (Cs $ _2 $ CO $ _3 $ ) as reactants, and the structure of the product was characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD), infrared spectrum, and element analysis (Figure S1; Table S1). Cs $ _{0.15} $ FA $ _{0.85} $ PbI $ _3 $  perovskite films with and without CsSCN additive were fabricated by a typical one-step spin coating process on NiO $ _x $  films; the details can be found under Experimental Procedures. We used scanning electron microscopy (SEM) to characterize the morphology of the perovskite films (Figure S2). Top-view SEM images of the perovskite films show that the grain size of the HSP film (with the CsSCN additive) is larger than that of the control film (without CsSCN). This can be attributed to SCN ions, which suppress nucleation and increase grain size.

To examine the crystal structure, we conducted thin-film XRD measurements for the different perovskite films (Figures 1A and 1B). The characteristic diffraction peaks of (110) and (220) planes of the HSP film move to larger angles of   $  14.06^{\circ}  $   and   $  28.25^{\circ}  $   from   $  14.03^{\circ}  $   and   $  28.22^{\circ}  $  . The shift of angles indicates a reduced facet distance, which is generally attributed to inclusion of smaller ions in the lattice.  $  ^{23}  $   Because Cs and SCN have a smaller radius than FA and I, respectively,  $  ^{24}  $   to clarify the specific ions causing the shift of diffraction angles, we fabricated two control films by adding an equal amount of NaSCN or cesium iodide (CsI) as an extra additive in precursors individually (Figure S3). Only CsI causes a similar shift of the diffraction peak, indicating that the Cs ion is responsible for the shift of the diffraction peak instead of SCN.

# Device Efficiency

To evaluate the photovoltaic performance of the control and HSP films, we constructed PSCs using an inverted p-i-n structure of ITO/NiO $ _{x} $ /CsFAPbI $ _{3} $ /PCBM/BCP/Cu (Figure 4A) because the inorganic hole transporting layer (HTL) has better thermal stability than the normal structure using organic HTL. Typical current density-voltage (J-V) curves are plotted in Figure 4B. The champion HSP PSC delivers a PCE of 20.70% with a short-circuit current density ( $ J_{SC} $ ) of 23.54 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{OC} $ ) of 1.10 V, and fill factor (FF) of 0.80. The control device has a champion PCE of 19.04%, mainly because of reduced  $ V_{OC} $  (1.04 V) and  $ J_{SC} $  (22.59 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ). The enhanced current density and voltage of the HSP film can be ascribed to the improved carrier transportation and reduced defect density of the perovskite film, as discussed above. We also prepared a sample with CsSCN on NiO $ _{x} $  (Figure S10) and found that the higher  $ V_{OC} $  is not attributable to accumulation of SCN around NiO $ _{x} $  (Figure 1D). To confirm the function of the Cs-rich shell structure, we prepared another device by adding Pb(SCN) $ _{2} $  to the precursor (Figure S11). Despite the film showing a larger crystal size than HSP, the efficiency of the device is only 18%, and the open voltage is only 0.98 V. This confirms that formation of HSP is critical to reduce defect density and enhance efficiency.

When keeping the devices under atmospheric conditions (RH 30%), the HSP device maintains over 80% of efficiency after 1,000 hours, whereas the control device loses over 50% of efficiency. We then studied the photostability of the device by tracking the morphology change of the film after light soaking for 3 days. # Theoretic Simulation

We then studied the mechanism responsible for the reduced defect density and enhanced stability of the HSP structure by theoretic simulation (Experimental Procedures; Note S2). In comparison with organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite, the inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   structure has better thermal stability.  $  ^{45}  $   We calculated the formation energy of   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $   (x = 0, 0.078, 0.156, 0.234, 0.313). As shown in Figure S17, the formation energies decrease with the increase in Cs, indicating increased stability by adding Cs. The stable Cs-rich shell could act as a protection layer to decrease ion migration, which can reduce formation of Schottky vacancies.  $  ^{46}  $   Shockley defects correspond to diffusion of ions to the surface, whereas the interstitial defects correspond to the internal movement of ions in the crystal. In summary, we fabricated a   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}/Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $   heterostructure based on a delicate surface ion exchange reaction. # Device Operational Stability Tests

Complete PSCs were encapsulated by a cover glass with a polyolefin-laminated film (PIB) by hot-pressing in a laminator. Long-term stability measurements of encapsulated perovskite devices were obtained in a constant temperature and humidity incubator under one-sun equivalent illumination provided by a white light-emitting diode (LED) light source (420–780 nm). The J-V curves were recorded automatically with a reverse scan rate of   $  0.1 V \cdot s^{-1}  $   every 10 min, which was used to extract the parameters for the aging data.

# Computational Methods

All DFT-based first-principle calculations were performed using the Vienna Ab-initio Simulation Package (VASP)  $  ^{51,52}  $  with the projector-augmented wave (PAW)  $  ^{53}  $  method. Generalized gradient approximation (GGA),  $  ^{54}  $  formulated by Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof (PBE), was used as the exchange correlation functional. The plane-wave cutoff energy was set to 520 eV. The gamma-centered k-point mesh with grid spacing of  $  2\pi \times 0.03\AA^{-1}  $  was used for electronic Brillouin zone integration. To properly take into account the long-range van der Waals interaction that plays an important role in hybrid perovskites, the vdWs-optB86b  $  ^{55}  $  functional was adopted. A  $  4*4*4  $  supercell was used in the formation energy and defect formation energy calculations of mixed perovskites.
