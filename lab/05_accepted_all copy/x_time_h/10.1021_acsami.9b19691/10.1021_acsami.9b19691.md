# Abstract

High power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term stability are inevitable issues faced in practical device applications of perovskite solar cells. In this paper, significant enhancements in the device efficiency and stability are achieved by using a surface-active lead acetate   $  (\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{OAc})_{2})  $   at the top or bottom of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (  $  MAPbI_{3}  $  )-based perovskite. When a saturated   $  Pb(OAc)_{2}  $   solution is introduced on the top of the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite precursor, the   $  OAc^{-}  $  in   $  Pb(OAc)_{2}  $   participates in lattice restructuring of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   to form   $  MAPbI_{3-x}(OAc)_{x}  $ , thereby producing a high-quality perovskite film with high crystallinity, large grain sizes, and uniform and pinhole-free morphology. Moreover, when   $  Pb(OAc)_{2}  $   solution is mixed in the poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) solution in the bottom way, the   $  OAc^{-}  $  in   $  Pb(OAc)_{2}  $   improves the water resistance of PEDOT:PSS. As the   $  OAc^{-}  $  easily bonds with the   $  Pb^{2+}  $ , the deposition of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   precursor onto the   $  Pb(OAc)_{2}  $   mixed with PEDOT:PSS results in a reduction of the uncoordinated Pb, leading to strong stabilization of perovskite layer. Both the top- and bottom-treated devices exhibit enhanced PCE values of 18.93% and 18.28%, respectively, compared to the conventional device with a PCE of 16.47%, which originates from decreased trap sites and reduced energy barriers. In particular, the bottom-treated device exhibits long-term stability, with more than 84% of its initial PCE over 800 h under an ambient environment.

Key words:   $  Pb(OAc)_{2}  $  ; top and bottom ways; high efficiency; long-term stability; perovskite solar cells.

# 1. Introduction

Inverted planar perovskite solar cells (IPSCs) have been rapidly evolving as promising candidates for future renewable green energy sources, owing to their numerous advantages including high absorbance, small exciton binding energies (approximately  $ \sim 20 $  meV), long electron-hole diffusion lengths (100 to  $ 1000\mathrm{nm} $ ), long lifetimes (approximately 100 ns), and simple solution processing  $ ^{1-5} $ . Although the performance of IPSCs has steadily improved, with a power-conversion efficiency (PCE) of over  $ 25\% $ , further improvements in efficiency and stability are required to enable their commercialization  $ ^{6-9} $ .

It is well known that the satisfactory performance of IPSCs is closely related to the high quality of the perovskite layer, in the form of large crystal grains, high uniformity, smooth and pinhole–free morphology, and full coverage   $  {}^{10-12}  $  . Non-uniform perovskite film results in a high leakage current and short charge transport distances, leading to severe electron–hole recombination and material degradation   $  {}^{13-14}  $  . Furthermore, inferior surface coverage causes incident photons to pass straight through the uncovered areas, consequently resulting in a reduced photocurrent   $  {}^{15}  $  . Therefore, obtaining a high-quality active layer of perovskite is a critical factor for achieving high-performance and stable IPSCs.

To date, various effective strategies have been explored and applied to obtain high-quality perovskite films. In recent years, the   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   additive has been used as a lead source to fabricate high-quality films, because it can act as a crosslinking agent to stabilize the perovskite intermediate phase by generating strong hydrogen bonding. This stable intermediate phase may induce a tardy crystallization process and low nucleation rate, resulting in fine morphology and large grains in perovskite films   $  {}^{16}  $  . Zhang et al.   $  {}^{17}  $   adopted   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   as a lead source instead of lead halide to prepare a perovskite film for deferred crystal growth, and obtained ultra-smooth and

Although devices constructed using   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   exhibit higher efficiency than conventional devices fabricated using   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  PbCl_{2}  $ , the mismatched energy level between the charge–selective layers (hole transport layer (HTL) and electron transport layer (ETL)) and the perovskite active layer causes severe energy losses during charge migration, consequently decreasing the device efficiency   $  {}^{18}  $ . Moreover, the application of   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   is strictly limited to the perovskite active layer.

In addition to the PCE, the long-term stability of IPSC is a critical issue to be resolved   $  {}^{19-21}  $  . It has been reported that PEDOT:PSS used as an HTL in IPSC possesses highly hygroscopic characteristics, which can destroy perovskite film in humid air, causing poor stability of the active layers and devices   $  {}^{22-23}  $  . Several attempts have been made to modify the PEDOT:PSS features to achieve efficient and stable solar cells   $  {}^{24-29}  $  . Ma et al.   $  {}^{30}  $   added hydrophobic Nafion into PEDOT:PSS. Nafion accelerates the phase separation between the PEDOT and PSS, and enhances its conductivity. Meanwhile, the work function of a Nafion-decorated PEDOT:PSS film strongly matches with the valence band of perovskite, resulting in an improved open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) and PCE. Moreover, hydrophobic Nafion can enrich the HTL surface and protect the upper perovskite layer against water and impurities from the PEDOT:PSS and indium tin oxide (ITO). However, despite these advantages, Nafion-treated devices have exhibited relatively low efficiency, with the highest PCE being 16.72%, and even encapsulated devices have exhibited only 80% of the initial PCE after 500 h under ambient conditions.

In this work, we successfully utilized   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   on the top and bottom of the perovskite active layer, referred to as the top and bottom ways, respectively, to fabricate efficient and stable   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  -based IPSCs. In the top way, a saturated   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   solution was introduced on the top of the perovskite precursor film, while the   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   mixed with PEDOT:PSS was introduced prior

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1 Materials preparation

All solvents were bought from Sigma–Aldrich, which could be utilized without any further purification. MAI and   $  PbI_{2}  $   were purchased from Tokyo Chemical Industry (TCI).   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   was bought from Sigma–Aldrich. A 45 wt% perovskite precursor solution with excess MAI was prepared by dissolving MAI and   $  PbI_{2}  $   (molar ratio 1.2:1.1) in a mix solvent (DMF:DMSO = 10:1). Saturated   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   solution was obtained by adding 5 mg of   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   into 1 ml of chlorobenzene then stirred and heated at 60 °C overnight. Because of some undissolved   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   in chlorobenzene, the solution was filtered by 0.45 μm nylon filters before device fabrication.   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   modified PEDOT:PSS was prepared via directly dissolving 5 mg of   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   into 1 mL of PEDOT:PSS aqueous solution (Baytron PH) and stirred overnight. The ITO–glass substrate was sequentially washed by water, acetone, ethanol and isopropanol, then dried at 85 °C overnight.

# 2.2 Device fabrication

# 2.2.1 Pb(OAc)  $  _{2}  $   activated top way

The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) images of the film without and with the treatment of   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   were analyzed to demonstrate the generation of   $  \mathrm{MAPbI_{3-x}(OAc)_{x}}  $  . In contrast to the pristine   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   film with only one peak, which could be attributed to adsorbed   $  H_{2}O  $ , the   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $ -treated film exhibited a broad spectrum of O 1s with several peaks, as illustrated in Figure 2(d). The broad spectrum could be divided into four peaks, with binding energies of 530.2, 531.5, 532.6, and 533.8 eV, ascribed to the Pb–O bond, C=O, C–O, and adsorbed   $  H_{2}O  $ , respectively   $  {}^{35}  $  . Therefore, this confirmed the existence of   $  OAc^{-}  $  in the modified perovskite film and generation of   $  \mathrm{MAPbI_{3-x}(OAc)_{x}}  $   by the doping of   $  OAc^{-}  $  . The high-resolution XPS spectra of Pb 4f and I 3d in Figure 2(e) and (f) displayed obvious peak shifts towards a lower binding energy in the   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $ -treated film. A reduced binding energy indicates an enhanced electron screening effect owing to improved electron density, while an enhanced binding energy implies a weakened electron density   $  {}^{36}  $  . Because the electronegativity of oxygen is stronger than that of iodine and lead atoms, the oxygen anion in   $  OAc^{-}  $  would bond with the Pb cation when the   $  OAc^{-}  $  replaced   $  I^{-}  $ , resulting in decreased binding energy of the   $  \mathrm{MAPbI_{3-x}(OAc)_{x}}  $  .

# 2.2.2 Pb(OAc)  $  _{2}  $   activated bottom way

As for  $ \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_2} $  activated bottom way, the fabrication process was the same with normal way except replacing pristine PEDOT:PSS with  $ \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_2} $  modified PEDOT:PSS (Figure 1c).

Figure 1 depicts the film fabrication routes developed in this work. The conventional approach in Figure 1(a) is implemented according to our previous work  $ ^{31} $ . Compared to the conventional way, the top way included an additional coating process for the  $ \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_2} $  prior to deposition of the PCBM (Figure 1(b)). Because the excess MAI in the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  precursor mainly existed in the surfaces and grain boundaries in the film before deposition of the  $ \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_2} $ , the deposited  $ \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_2} $  reacted with the excess MAI to form  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ , and consequently produced a high-quality perovskite film with enlarged grains, and smooth and pinhole-free morphology. # 3.3.3 Stabilities of IPSCs fabricated using bottom way

Another outstanding advantage of the bottom way is the provision of significant enhancements in device stability, which is a critical issue for practical applications. Figure 7(a) presents the efficiency variations of the Al electrode based devices fabricated using the conventional and bottom ways with an increasing exposure time in an  $ \mathrm{N}_2 $ -filled glove box without any encapsulation. As indicated in Figure 7(a), it is clear that the bottom-treated device exhibited improved stability compared to the conventional device. The PCE of the conventional device was degraded to approximately  $ 50\% $  of the initial PCE, while the bottom-treated device maintained approximately  $ 90\% $  of its initial PCE after an exposure time of  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $ . Our earlier work demonstrated that the Al electrode is easily corroded and oxidized under moist environments, especially when contacting the PCBM [50]. Figure S11(a) confirmed the expectation. Therefore, we replaced the Al electrode with a stable Au electrode, as illustrated in Figure S11(b), and monitored the device stability. Although the PCEs of the devices with the Au electrode were slightly lower than that of the device with the Al electrode (Figure 7(b)), long-term device stability was achieved. Figure 7(c) and (d) illustrate the efficiency variations of the devices with an increasing exposure time in air without encapsulation. As indicated in Figure 7(c), the IPSC with  $ \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_2} $ -treated PEDOT:PSS exhibited relatively high stability, maintaining more than  $ 84\% $  of its initial PCE over  $ 800\mathrm{h} $  under an ambient environment with a RH of  $ 30\% $  to  $ 40\% $ . In contrast, the conventional device underwent rapid degradation, losing its initial efficiency of approximately  $ 60\% $  under the same environment. Even under higher humidity ( $ 65\% $  to  $ 70\% $ ), the bottom-treated device exhibited excellent moisture stability, and the PCE only decreased to  $ 82\% $  of the initial value after  $ 100\mathrm{h} $  (Figure 7(d)). However, the conventional device exhibited a significant loss of the initial PCE of approximately  $ 99\% $  after only  $ 25\mathrm{h} $ .
