# Understanding the Effects of Fluorine Substitution in Lithium Salt on Photovoltaic Properties and Stability of Perovskite Solar Cells

Tai Wu, Rongshan Zhuang, Rongjun Zhao, Rongmei Zhao, Lizhong Zhu, Gaoyuan Liu, Runtao Wang, Kanghong Zhao, and Yong Hua*

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) additive is widely employed to improve the hole mobility of the hole-transporting layer (HTL) in perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, the hygroscopic nature of Li-TFSI is not beneficial to the long-term stability of PSCs. Herein, a new more water-resistant Li-PFSI is used to replace Li-TFSI. As a result, the best power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 22.14% is achieved for Li-PFSI-treated PSCs, exceeding that of the control cell with Li-TFSI (20.25%). Importantly, the Li-PFSI-based cell shows impressive environmental and thermal stability. Moreover, we first comparatively investigate the effect of the amount of fluorine substitution in lithium salt (2F for Li-FSI, 6F for Li-TFSI, and 10F for Li-PFSI) on the HTL's physical properties and their photovoltaic performance in PSCs. We found that more fluorine substitution can improve the HTL charge-carrier transfer and photovoltaic performance in PSCs. Our findings provide key missing information for designing new additives toward efficient and stable PSCs.

Significant progress has been made in the field of lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) over the past decade owing to a combination of the excellent optoelectronic properties of perovskite active materials, including strong and tunable optical absorption, ambipolar charge transport, long charge-carrier diffusion length, small exciton binding energy, and cost-effective solution processability.  $  ^{1-7}  $   Adopting the device structure of dye-sensitized solar cells, the first PSCs with the utilization of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   as the light absorber reported by Miyasaka et al. in 2009 was found to be unstable, because of the quick fade upon dissolution of the perovskite active materials in the triiodide/iodine liquid-state electrolyte.  $  ^{8}  $   In order to address this shortcoming, solid-state hole-transporting material (HTM) 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) was employed as an alternative to liquid electrolyte to successfully construct the first all solid-state mesoscopic heterojunction PSCs featuring a FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /m-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite/HTM/Au cell configuration that was stable for 500 h, developed by Park and co-workers in 2012,  $  ^{9}  $   in which the HTM is the key component for extracting/transporting the photogenerated hole from the perovskite to the back contact metal electrode, effectively

The hygroscopic nature of Li-TFSI tends to lead to absorption of water molecules upon air exposure and perhaps aggregation from the HTM films, leading to some pinholes in those HTM films, which inevitably facilitate the degradation of perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{31-33}  $   Although a variety of alternative additives, such as copper salts,  $  ^{34}  $   ionic liquids,  $  ^{35}  $   and Lewis-acids,  $  ^{36}  $   have been employed in PSCs, only a few of them were found to be more promising than Li-TFSI.  $  ^{37,38}  $

It is well-known that the hydrophobicity of organic molecules can be increased by replacing hydrogen atom with fluorine atom, which may help enhance the stability of PSCs in air.  $  ^{39}  $   Furthermore, fluorination of organic functional groups has been reported as passivation agent for effectively reducing the trap states in the perovskite surface and grain boundaries.  $  ^{40,41}  $   However, chemical tuning of different fluorine substitution in lithium salt and its influence on the related device performance is so far virtually unknown, to the best of our knowledge. Thus, it is significantly crucial to develop novel water-resistant lithium salt additives as alternatives to Li-TFSI for improving PSC stability and to intensively investigate the influence of varying amounts of fluorine groups in lithium salt on physical properties of HTMs as well as their photovoltaic performance in PSCs.

In this contribution, three structurally similar lithium salts differing in F-substitutions, termed Li-FSI, Li-TFSI and Li-PFSI, have been explored as additives for Spiro-OMeTAD, as shown in Figure 1. For the first time, we then systematically investigated the influence of the number of fluorine substitutions (2F for Li-TFSI, 6F for Li-TFSI, and 10F for Li-PFSI) in lithium salts on the physical properties of Spiro-OMeTAD, as well as their photovoltaic performances in PSCs. It is found that the introduction of more F-substitutions in lithium salt can result in better hole extraction/transfer capability of Spiro-OMeTAD, better HTM surface morphology, and lower charge-recombination rate as well as trap defects in perovskite material. As a result, Li-PFSI-based solar cells show photovoltaic performance better than that of Li-FSI and Li-TFSI counterparts. In addition, increasing the number of fluorine substitutions from 6 to 10 leads to a noticeable increase in the hydrophobic nature of the Spiro-OMeTAD film, which is beneficial for enhancing the long-term stability of PSCs.

shown in Figure 3f. carrier can be transferred quickly by the film based on Li-PFSI in comparison to the ones using Li-FSI and Li-TFSI and thus effectively reduces the probability of carrier nonradiative recombination.  $  ^{59,60}  $   Therefore, we can conclude that the incorporation of more F atoms into lithium salts can significantly enhance the carrier transfer process. The more efficient charge-carrier transport in Li-PFSI-based perovsite/Spiro-OMeTAD film as compared with that of the Li-FSI and Li-TFSI counterparts is mainly attributed to the lower trap density in the surface of perovskite  $  ^{26,61}  $   and also the more homogeneous Spiro-OMeTAD thin film,  $  ^{62}  $   as discussed later.

To investigate the long-term stability of PSCs based on different Spiro-OMeTAD additives, aging tests were measured for 1000 h under 40% RH and continuous full sun illumination at room temperature. As shown in Figure 5a, the unencapsulated PSCs devices based on Li-PFSI showed good stability, maintaining about 90% of the original efficiency after storage in ambient air (RH ≈ 40%) within 600 h, whereas the PCE of the devices incorporating Li-TFSI and Li-FSI degraded to about 81% and 65%, respectively. Subsequently, these devices were stored under the maximum power point (MPP) tracking with continuous one sun illumination; the Li-PFSI-treated cell maintained 82% of the initial PCE value after 400 h, whereas Li-TFSI- and Li-FSI-based devices showed a fast efficiency drop to 67% and 46%, respectively. Moreover, the stability of devices was also investigated at an extremely high humidity over 70% (Figure 5b). The Li-PFSI-treated device retained about 66% of its initial PCE after 360 h, showing better moisture stability than that of Li-TFSI (50%) and Li-FSI (32%). The decrease in moisture stability for these devices can be mainly assigned to the hydrophilic character of Li salt additives, which easily induce moisture uptake and lead to the efficiency drop.  $  ^{23,33}  $   Therefore, Li-PFSI-based devices show better moisture stability compared to the other ones owing to the improved hydrophobicity of the Spiro-OMeTAD/Li-PFSI layer, as demonstrated in Figure S15. The thin-film surface morphology of the HTM layer is highly important for achieving good stability and photovoltaic performance of PSCs. Top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was employed to examine the surface morphology of the Spiro-OMeTAD layers coated with different lithium salt additives. As shown in Figure 6a, the perovskite shows a very homogeneous capping layer on the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  substrate. When the Li-FSI-based Spiro-OMeTAD is deposited onto the perovskite surface (Figure 6b), a high density of "macro-sized islands" can be detected on the film. As for the spin-coated Li-TFSI-based Spiro-OMeTAD film, appropriate "nano-sized islands" appeared, as shown in Figure 6c. Noticeably, the Li-PFSI-based Spiro-OMeTAD produces extraordinarily dense and uniform HTM capping layers on the perovskite surface without any observable "islands" (Figure 6d), a quite uniform surface morphology conducive to the good PSCs device performance and stability we have achieved. To understand the above-mentioned phenomenon, the hygroscopicity of the additives was evaluated by exposing them to ambient air ( $ \mathrm{RH} \approx 40\% $ ). It is apparent that Li-FSI absorbs water and aggregates within 30 min, whereas Li-PFSI remains solid-state under the same conditions (Figure S14). Clearly, this result shows that the Li-FSI is the most hygroscopic additive while the Li-PFSI is the least. Moreover, contact angles of the water droplet on perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD film were also measured to characterize the hygroscopicity. As shown in Figure S15, Li-FSI- and Li-TFSI-based Spiro-OMeTAD films exhibit water contact angle of  $ 56^{\circ} $  and  $ 72^{\circ} $ , respectively, whereas the angle increases to approximately  $ 85^{\circ} $  for Li-PFSI. These results indicate that Li-PFSI is more hydrophobic than Li-FSI and Li

TFSI because of the water-repellent character of the fluorine atom and is likely to slow the rate at which water molecules are able to intercalate into the perovskite layer; this is in good agreement with our observation that replacing the Li-TFSI with the Li-PFSI additive can effectively improve the PSCs devices' long-term stability. On the basis of these above research results, we propose a mechanism for how the Li-PFSI yields a quite uniform surface morphology of Spiro-OMeTAD, as illustrated in Figure 7. The fast absorption of water from the air leads to severe aggregation of Li-FSI toward the Spiro-OMeTAD film, and then the superhygroscopic aggregated Li-FSI is rapidly hydrated by moisture to create "macro-sized islands" in the Spiro-OMeTAD film, which eventually promotes the perovskite layer decomposition. In comparison with Li-FSI, the greater hydrophobicity of the Li-PFSI not only effectively prevents the aggregation of Li-PFSI but also allows the subsequent Spiro-OMeTAD film to be more strongly moisture-resistant with a homogeneous HTM surface morphology, which ultimately improves the long-term stability of PSCs devices.

In this work, for the first time, we systematically investigated the influence of varying amounts of fluorine groups in lithium salt (2F for Li-TFSI, 6F for Li-TFSI, and 10F for Li-PFSI) on physical properties of Spiro-OMeTAD as well as their photovoltaic performance in PSCs. It has been found that the introduction of more fluorine substituents to lithium salt can not only noticeably improve hole extraction/transfer and film-forming capability of Spiro-OMeTAD but also lower the charge-recombination rate and trap density in the perovskite film. The PSCs based on Li-FSI containing two fluorine groups as Spiro-OMeTAD additive showed a PCE of 19.64%. The control device based on Li-TFSI with six fluorine groups provided a slightly higher PCE of 20.25%. When Li-TFSI is repalced with Li-PFSI with 10 fluorine substitutions, obvious enhancements of   $  J_{so}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $  , and FF are observed. As a result, the Li-PFSI-based cell yielded a champion PCE of 22.14%. The 1 cm² device based on Li-PFSI shows a very impressive PCE of 19.17%, which is higher than that of devices based on Li-TFSI (18.09%) under the same working conditions. Furthermore, the Li-PFSI-based PSCs without encapsulation exhibit higher environmental and thermal PSCs stability in comparison with those based on Li-TFSI because of the greater ability to effectively resist humidity.
