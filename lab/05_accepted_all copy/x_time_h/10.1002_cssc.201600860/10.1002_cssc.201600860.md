# Introduction

Third-generation photovoltaic devices that generate electricity from sunlight have been surging for several decades. Aiming at the realization of efficient and cost-effective photovoltaics, some candidates of next generation solar cells, such as dye-sensitized solar cells (DSCs),  $  ^{[1,2]}  $  quantum dot solar cells (QDSCs),  $  ^{[3]}  $  and organic solar cells (OSCs),  $  ^{[4,5]}  $  have been developed and studied extensively all over the world. Originally, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) were based on the concept of DSCs with low power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 3.8% in 2009.  $  ^{[6]}  $  Since then, the PCE of the highest performing PSCs has greatly progressed and has reached the current world record of 22.1% through revolutionary improvements in the materials and device engineering.  $  ^{[7-9]}  $  The high PCE of PSCs is attributed to exceptional material properties, such as remarkably high absorption over the visible spectrum, low exciton binding energy, charge carrier diffusion length in the micrometer scale,  $  ^{[10]}  $  sharp

Effective selective contacts are equally important as perovskite material in PSCs.  $  ^{[12]}  $   Although perovskite materials are capable of transporting electrons and holes simultaneously,  $  ^{[13,14]}  $   the presence of an efficient electron-selective contact layer (ESL) can prevent any resultant charge recombination.  $  ^{[13,15]}  $   In fact, many researchers have proven that a good ESL are crucial for determining not only the PCE, but also the device stability of PSCs.  $  ^{[16-21]}  $   Thus, the exploration of ESL materials for PSCs stimulates extensive research interests and remains one major topic in this field.

Currently, only a limited number of materials have been checked as an alternative ESL for PSCs. Initially, PSCs were fabricated with a mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   scaffold as the ESL. Later, some metal oxides, such as ZnO,   $  SnO_{2}  $ , and   $  WO_{x}  $ , have also been investigated in planar PSCs owing to their easy processing and good optical and charge-transporting properties.  $  ^{[22-24]}  $   Among these metal oxide ESLs, ZnO is particularly promising owing to its high transparency, suitable work function, high electron mobility, and structural tailorability at a nanoscale level.  $  ^{[25]}  $   In addition, low-content doping/modification of metal oxides has been considered as a way of improving the selectivity of ESLs.  $  ^{[26-29]}  $   However, the study of ZnO-based ternary oxides as ESLs for PSCs holds a rare record. There has been an instability of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   on ZnO that originated from hydroxyl groups and/or residual acetate ligands on the ZnO surface that accelerate the decomposition of the perovskite absorber.  $  ^{[30,31]}  $   Ternary oxides might be effective in solving this instability issue by low-content doping/modification.

# Results and Discussion

Solution-processed ZMO films were coated on indium tin oxide (ITO) substrates by a facile sol-gel method. The undoped and 5, 10, 20, and  $ 30\mathrm{mol}\% $  Mg-doped ZnO are denoted as 0, 5, 10, 20,  $ 30\% $  ZMO, respectively. Figure 1 shows the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) of the ZMO ESLs. As shown in Figure 1, the binding energy (BE) peaks related to  $ \mathrm{Mg}2\mathrm{p} $   $ (50.9\mathrm{eV}) $ , as well as  $ \mathrm{Zn}2\mathrm{p} $  and O 1s, are observed for ZMO samples with  $ \mathrm{Mg} $  dopant. Increasing  $ \mathrm{Mg} $  content results in the increase of the intensity and area of the peak of  $ \mathrm{Mg}2\mathrm{p} $ , indicating  $ \mathrm{Mg} $  was well implanted to  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $ .[34]  $ \mathrm{Mg} $  KLL peak  $ (306.7\mathrm{eV}) $  is also supportive of the presence of  $ \mathrm{Mg} $  in the typical broad-scan XPS spectra of representative  $ 10\% $  ZMO owing to the characteristic binding energy peak in Figure S1 (in the Supporting Information).[33] The BE peaks of  $ \mathrm{Zn}2\mathrm{p}_{3/2} $   $ (1022.4\mathrm{eV}) $  and  $ 2\mathrm{p}_{1/2} $   $ (1044.6\mathrm{eV}) $  for all ZMO ESLs are similar to that of binary  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $ , suggesting the replacement of  $ \mathrm{Zn}^{2+} $  by  $ \mathrm{Mg}^{2+} $ .[26] The XPS results demonstrate the successful doping of  $ \mathrm{Mg} $  elements and the formation of ternary oxide films of ZMO.

As discussed above, the difference of the thermal stability for perovskite films implies that PSCs may exhibit a similar trend in device durability during storage, because the impact of ESLs on perovskite can gradually emerge owing to the difference of surface and interface properties. Figure 11a shows the stability result of PSCs based on 0 and 10% ZMO as ESLs stored in a 30% relatively humidity ambient air environment at room temperature. Despite small variations, the stabilities of the different devices are similar for the initial 200 h of storage time; however, further increasing the storage time reduced the PCE of 0% PSC more significantly than that of 10% PSC. This observation partly supports the conjecture that the hydroxyl-induced degradation is a main mechanism to cause reduction of PCEs during the storage. Figure 11b shows the photostability results of the devices under simulated sunlight illumination. Compared to the PSC device based on 0% ZMO, the 10% PSC device decreased more gradually under illumination within 8 h, indicating the incorporation of Mg into ZnO is beneficial for improved stability. To further detect the effect of Mg doping on ZnO and understand the origin of stability improvement, the contact angles of the ZMO films with deionized water are measured (shown in Figure S11). The results show that the 10 and 20% ZMO films are more hydrophobic than the ZnO film, as evidenced by the contact angle increase from 68.9° for 0% ZMO to 71.4° and 73.5° for 10% ZMO and 20% ZMO, respectively. # Conclusions

We have successfully developed   $  Zn_{1-x}Mg_{x}O  $   (ZMO) as electron selective contact layer for   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  -based perovskite solar cells. A champion device with the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 16.5% was achieved based on the optimized 10% ZMO (0.4 M). This high efficiency originated from improved optical properties, suitable energy levels, excellent charge extraction efficiency, and suppressed charge recombination between the interface of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   and 10 mol% Mg-doped ZMO (10% ZMO), as revealed by transmittance spectra, electron mobility, time-resolved photoluminescence spectroscopy, and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy measurements. More importantly,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   deposited on 10% ZMO thin films exhibit better thermal stability and better durability as well as photo-stability of the resultant PSC device, as compared to pure ZnO. This improvement could be ascribed to the improved chemical/photochemical stability of the wider bandgap 10% ZMO. This work helps to establish a method to improve not only the photovoltaic performance, but also stability/durability of perovskite solar cells for potential real-world applications.

# Experimental Section

# Materials

Zinc acetate dihydrate (99.9%), magnesium acetate tetrahydrate (99.5%), 2-methoxyethanol (99.8%), and ethanolamine (99.5%) were purchased from Macklin, Inc. The lead iodide (beads, -10 mesh, 99.999% trace metals basis) and lithium salts (>99.0%) were purchased from Aldrich. The   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (>99.0%) was purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Co. (PLT). The Spiro-OMeTAD [2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-4-methoxyphenylamino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene, >99.0%] was purchased from Lumtec. Ultradry solvent of N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, >99.9%) and isopropanol (>99.9%) were obtained from J&K and Acros, respectively. All the chemicals and solvents were kept in the glove-box before starting our experiment.

# Device fabrication

The pre-patterned ITO substrates were cleaned with detergent, deionized water, acetone, and 2-propanol in sequence. The ZMO precursor solutions were prepared by mixing zinc acetate dihydrate with magnesium acetate tetrahydrate (0, 5, 10, 20, or  $ 30\mathrm{mol}\% $  ) and dissolving in a mixture of 2-methoxyethanol as the solvent and ethanolamine as the stabilizer under sufficient stirring. The ratio of ethanolamine to total metal acetate was 1.0. The sol-gel-derived ZMO films (with  $ x\mathrm{mol}\% $  Mg in the precursor solids) were prepared by spin-coating on ITO-glass substrates. Then the films

were heated on a hot plate at 150°C for 10 min, and transferred into an oven for annealing at 290°C for 30 min in air. After cooling to room temperature, the ZMO films were rinsed with deionized water and ethanol, followed by drying for 30 min at 200°C. A 460 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  precursor solution of PbI  $  _{2}  $  in DMF (with 120 μL TBP in 1 mL PbI  $  _{2}  $  precursor solution) was then spin-coated on the top of the ZMO layer at 4000 rpm for 30 s in the glove-box and dried at 70°C for 10 min. After cooling to room temperature, the films were dipped into a CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  I 2-propanol solution (10 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $ ) for 50 s, followed by annealing at 70°C for 20 min to give a desired crystallite formation. As hole-transporting material (HTM), a Spiro-OMeTAD solution [80 mg of Spiro-OMeTAD, 10.5 μL of TBP, and 46.5 μL of a lithium-bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) solution (170 mg Li-TFSI/1 mL acetonitrile) in 1 mL chlorobenzene] was spin-coated at 4000 rpm for 30 s on the CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $ . The substrates with HTM were left overnight in the dry air in dark at room temperature. Finally, 60 nm thick Ag electrode was thermally evaporated on the top of HTM to produce a completed PSC device.

# Device characterization

The  $ J-V $  characteristics of solar cells were measured by a computer-controlled Keithley 2400 source meter measurement system with an AM 1.5G filter at calibrated  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $  illumination, as determined by a standard silicon reference cell (91150V Oriel Instruments). The effective area of the cell was defined to be  $ 0.1\mathrm{cm}^2 $  using a non-reflective metal mask. IPCE spectra were measured in air under short-circuit conditions using a commercial IPCE setup (Crowntech QTest Station 1000AD), which was equipped with a 100 W Xe arc lamp, filter wheel, and monochromator. Monochromated light was chopped at a frequency of  $ 80\mathrm{Hz} $  and photocurrents were measured using a lock-in amplifier. The setup was calibrated against a certified silicon reference diode. Electron-transporting property was investigated by using the space-charge limited current (SCLC) model based on electron-only devices with a configuration of ITO/ZMO/Ca/Al.  $ J-V $  characteristics were measured in the dark by a Keithley 2400 source measurement unit. The electron mobility values were calculated according to the Mott-Gurney law by the equation:  $ J = 9\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r\mu (V - V_{\mathrm{bi}} - V_r)^2 /8L^3 $ , where  $ \varepsilon_0 $  is the permittivity of free space,  $ \varepsilon_r $  is the dielectric constant,  $ \mu $  is the electron carrier mobility,  $ V $  is the voltage drop across the device  $ (V = V_{\mathrm{app}} - V_{\mathrm{bi}} - V_r) $  where  $ V_{\mathrm{app}} $  is the applied voltage to the device, and  $ V_{\mathrm{bi}} $  is the built-in voltage owing to the difference in WF of two electrodes),  $ V_r $  is the voltage drop owing to the contact resistance and series resistance across the electrodes and  $ L $  is the film thickness.[26] The impedance spectroscopy measurement was carried out by a VSP multichannel potentiostatic-galvanostatic system (Biologic, France), under dark conditions by applying a  $ 10\mathrm{mV} $  voltage perturbation with the frequency ranging between  $ 1\mathrm{MHz} $  and  $ 0.1\mathrm{Hz} $ . Z-View Analyst software was used to model the Nyquist plots obtained from the impedance measurements.
