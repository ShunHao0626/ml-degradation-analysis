# ABSTRACT

Operational stability remains the foremost concern delaying the commercialization of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Ions diffusion from iodine-rich perovskite layer to metal electrode is one main reason for the irreversible devices degradation. Here we introduce chemically crosslinked TMTA (trimethylolpropane triacrylate) at both bulk perovskite layer and perovskite/PCBM interface to suppress the ions diffusion toward electrode. The TMTA in perovskite layer suppresses ions migration along grain boundaries and TMTA at perovskite/PCBM interface blocks ions diffusion toward electrode, owing to its continuous network structure and chemically inert nature. Diffusion experiment, permeation experiment and resistive random-access memory (RRAM) investigation confirm the effectively blocked ions diffusion in PSCs with TMTA whether under heat, light or electric field conditions. The resulting PSCs exhibit 7-fold improvement in operational stability at elevated temperature of  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , retaining  $ \sim 80\% $  of initial efficiency after maximum power point tracking for 1000 hours under continuous illumination. The PSCs with TMTA also exhibit good thermal stability and retain over  $ 90\% $  of the initial efficiency after aging at  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 1000 hours.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are regarded as one of the most promising photovoltaic technologies due to their low cost and high efficiency.[1-8] Long-term stability remains a major bottleneck delaying the commercialization of PSCs[9-13] since the power conversion efficiency (PCE) has reached over 23%[14], which is on par with that of established photovoltaic modules. Among stability issues, operational stability is of foremost concern as it simulates the real power-generation process of solar cells under continuous illumination and external load.[15, 16] Apart from illumination and external load, temperature is another crucial factor determining the operational stability of PSCs that cannot be avoided by encapsulation.[17] Previous studies have demonstrated that even if almost no degradation is observed in operational stability at 20 °C after maximum power point (MPP) tracking for 600 hours, PSCs can still rapidly degrade over 20% merely within 100 hours when increasing the operational temperature to 50 °C or 65 °C.[18, 19] Recently, the operational stability at room temperature has been greatly improved through adopting FA-based materials (CsMAFA, FA-free system)[20-23], introducing Eu $  ^{3+}  $ -Eu $  ^{2+}  $  ion redox shuttle[24] or fluoride ions strategy[25]. PSCs even show good operational stability under concentrator conditions (10 suns) if controlling the temperature at ~30 °C through air stream flowing.[26] However, up to now, most operational stability measurements are conducted at room temperature (~30 °C), and rare reports are done at elevated temperature. If basing on the consensus stability strategy proposed by international summit on organic photovoltaic (ISOS-L-2, taken standard absence for operational stability in PSCs),[27] long-term operational stability at elevated temperature (60 °C or even higher) has become imperative for the next development of PSCs.

To improve the operational stability at elevated temperature, ions diffusion in PSCs should be well suppressed since the ions diffusion rate rapidly increases with temperature according to Nernst-Einstein equation.[28] In previous study, we demonstrated in-situ crosslinking strategy to fabricate operationally stable PSCs through introducing crosslinkable organic molecule TMTA (trimethylolpropane triacrylate)

into bulk perovskite layer.[29] The TMTA coordinates to grain boundaries (GBs) and then crosslinks with each other, thus blocking the ions migration along GBs in perovskite layer. The resulting PSCs exhibit good operational stability at room temperature, retaining  $ 78.5\% $  of initial efficiency after MPP tracking for 400 hours. To further improve the operational stability, apart from ions migration in perovskite layer, ions diffusion from iodine-rich perovskite layer to metal electrode should also be considered. Previous studies have demonstrated that due to the ions diffusion from perovskite to electrode, stability issues of PSCs appear not only in perovskite itself, but also in the interfaces between the various layers of device.[30, 31]

In PSCs, there are two major interfaces, perovskite/CTL (charge transport layer) interface and CTL/metal electrode interface. During devices operation, ions can penetrate through these interfaces, leading to interfacial degradation or even reaction with metal electrode, thus accelerating device degradation.[32, 33] Studies have demonstrated that the introduction of ions-blocking layer (IBL) is an effective method to suppress ions diffusion in PSCs. For example, Grätzel et al. introduced reduced grapheme oxide (rGO) as IBL at CuSCN CTL/Au interface to avoid electrode erosion, extending the operational stability to 1000 hours.[34] In inverted PSCs, Han et al. introduced carbon quantum dots (CQD) or even Bi as IBL at PCBM CTL/Ag interface to block ions diffusion toward electrode, achieving good operational stability for 500 hours.[31, 35] However, above-mentioned studies all aim at blocking ions diffusion at CTL/metal electrode interface to suppress the electrode erosion, but rare reports focus on the interfacial degradation at perovskite/CTL interface during MPP tracking. In fact, previously reported ions-induced electrode erosion will happen only when the ions have already penetrated through CTL and induced perovskite/CTL interfacial degradation first. Studies have confirmed that ions penetration into organic CTL (spiro-OMeTAD or PCBM) will induce deep-level defects in CTL and deteriorate their charge transport ability.[36, 37] Therefore, study at perovskite/CTL interface is necessary since both the degradation at perovskite/CTL and CTL/electrode interfaces will be

Recalling our previous work where crosslinked TMTA possesses good thermal and ions-blocking properties[29], here we introduce chemically crosslinked TMTA not only at bulk perovskite layer (cross-sectional element mapping shown in Figure S1) but also at perovskite/PCBM interface to effectively suppress the ions-induced degradation in PSCs. TMTA (Figure 1a) is spin-coated on top of perovskite film and then annealed at  $ 140^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  to promote its crosslinking, thus forming chemically crosslinked TMTA IBL.  $ (\mathrm{FAPbI}_3)_{0.95}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_3)_{0.05} $  perovskite (abbreviated as FAMA, Figure 1b) is used in this work since it can endure high temperature ( $ 140^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) annealing without efficiency loss in comparison with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ . Therefore, the high temperature crosslinking of TMTA will not deteriorate device efficiency and over  $ 20\% $  efficiency is obtained in PSCs. More importantly, different from previous reports where using interlayers at perovskite/CTL interface to passivate defects[38, 39], the TMTA layer in our work is used as an IBL to block ions diffusion toward electrode during device operation (Figure 1c), not only

protecting PCBM CTL and suppressing interfacial degradation, but also resisting the possible ions reaction with Cu electrode. # 2.1.Materials

TMTA is obtained from Aladdin (China).  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{PbI_2} $ , DMF and DMSO are obtained from Alfa Aesar. MACl, FAI and MABr are obtained from Xi'an p-OLED (China). CB solvent is obtained from Aldrich.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

The glass/ITO substrate (2 cm*2 cm) is cleaned with ultrasonication in detergent, distilled water, acetone and isopropanol. The substrate is further treated with   $  O_{2}  $   plasma for 5min. P3CT-N solution (1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in methanol) is spin-coated on ITO substrate in air at 4000 r.p.m and then annealed at 100 °C for 10min. Perovskite precursor solution is obtained by mixing 15 mg MACl, 721.6mg FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   and 28.7mg MAPbBr  $  _{3}  $   in DMF/DMSO solvent (4:1, volume ratio). Perovskite layer is deposited by a typical anti-solvent method in glovebox filled with N  $  _{2}  $  . The perovskite precursor solution is spin-coated on ITO/P3CT-N at 2000 r.p.m. for 10s and 4000 r.p.m. for 20s. During spin-coating, 300 μL chlorobenzene (CB) is dropped on the substrate 10s prior to the end of the second programme. Then the substrate is annealed at 140 °C for 20min to obtain perovskite films. For PSCs with IBL, 0.5mg TMTA is added into the perovskite solution before spin-coating. And TMTA solution of 1mg/mL in CB is further spin-coated on perovskite films and then the film is annealed at 140 °C for 20min to promote the crystalline of perovskite and TMTA crosslinking simultaneously. After cooling down to room temperature, 10mg/mL PCBM solution in CB is spin-coated on top of perovskite at 2000 r.p.m. for 45s. Finally, the substrate is transferred into vacuum chamber; 40 nm C60, 8 nm TPBi and 100 nm Cu are thermally evaporated

# 2.3. Characterization

The  $ J - V $  characteristics are recorded using Keithley 2400 sourcemeter under the solar simulator (Newport Oriel Sol3A) with simulated AM 1.5G illumination  $ (100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2}) $ . # 2.4. Cross-section scanning electron microscope (SEM)

SEM images are recorded using field emission scanning electron microscope (Hitachi, S-4800) with an accelerating voltage of 8 kV.

Cross-section element mapping is conducted using SEM (FEI Quanta FEG 250) with accelerating voltage of  $ 20\mathrm{kV} $ .

# 2.5. Diffusion experiment

ITO substrate with perovskite (8*8mm) is immersed into toluene solvent (2 mL) in sealed bottle under white LED illumination. After 15 hours, the solution is used to measure its UV-Vis absorbance.

# 2.6. Permeation experiment

Devices with ITO/Cu/Perovskite (with or without IBL)/PCBM are annealed at   $  60\ °C  $   in a sealed box with   $  I_{2}  $   powder around. At thermal conditons,   $  I_{2}  $   powder will sublimate to form Iodine vapour, which may permeate through PCBM and perovskite layer, reacting with Cu film beneath to yield CuI. The substrates are further analysed using a powder X-ray diffractometer (Bruker AXS D8 Advance, Germany).

# 2.7. RRAM investigation

Perovskite layer and IBL layer are deposited similar to PSCs. Then a thin PCBM layer (2mg/mL in CB) is spin-coated on top. Finally Au (60 nm) is deposited to form top electrode with diameter of 100  $  \mu  $  m. RRAM devices are measured in dark using semiconductor characterization system (Keithley 4200-SCS).

Light, heat and electric field are three major factors determining the ions diffusion in PSCs under operating conditions. Therefore, we design three independent experiments to evaluate the ions-blocking property of TMTA IBL. First, light-induce ions diffusion is investigated with diffusion experiment by immersing perovskite films with IBL (named as IBL) and without IBL (named as FAMA) into toluene. Under illumination, Iodine will escape from perovskite layer due to ions diffusion and then transform into  $ \mathrm{I}_2 $ , which will be captured by toluene solvent as shown in Figure 2a.[42] We can compare the Iodine diffusion rate through measuring the absorbance of  $ \mathrm{I}_2 $  in toluene. According to Lambert-Beer law, we calculate the Iodine amount extracted from perovskite films and then the average Iodine diffusion rate (details in Figure S2). For control FAMA, the diffusion rate is  $ 5.7*10^{-8} $  mol  $ \mathrm{m}^{-2}\mathrm{s}^{-1} $ , which is nearly ten time higher that of films with IBL ( $ 7.6*10^{-9} $  mol  $ \mathrm{m}^{-2}\mathrm{s}^{-1} $ ), indicating the excellent ions blocking property of chemically crosslinked TMTA under illumination. Second, heat-induced ions diffusion is studied with permeation experiment by putting devices with structure of ITO/Cu/Perovskite/IBL (or without)/PCBM at  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  in Iodine vapour atmosphere (Figure 2b, details in Experimental Section). PCBM layer is deposited on perovskite films to simulate the real situation in PSCs. If Iodine permeates through perovskite/PCBM, it will react with the Cu film beneath to yield CuI, which can be detected by X-ray diffraction (XRD).[42] In FAMA films, CuI appears merely within 1 hour, while no obvious CuI peak appears in perovskite with TMTA IBL even after 10 hours. This result indicates that PCBM layer is unable to block Iodine diffusion in PSCs and that the using of TMTA IBL can effectively suppress this diffusion even at thermal conditions.

Third, the electric field induced ions diffusion is also studied. Ions diffusion is unwanted in PSCs but widely applied in resistive random-access memory (RRAM).[43] Due to ions diffusion, two states of high-resistance-state (HRS) and low-resistance-state (LRS) exist in typical perovskite-based RRAM devices, which can be used as “0” and “1” to store information.[44] Perovskite-based RRAM is at HRS initially. When subjecting to positive bias, I⁻ ions in perovskite films start to migrate toward anode, thus reacting with metal anode and accumulating around. As a result, many I⁻ vacancies are left in perovskite layer and form I⁻ vacancy filaments for electric conduction, transiting the RRAM from initial HRS to LRS. The switching voltage from HRS to LRS represents how easily ions migrate in devices.[44] Motivated by this, we design RRAM devices using control FAMA or IBL-based perovskite films to examine the ions diffusion under electric field. Note that a thin PCBM layer is deposited between perovskite and Au electrode to simulate real situation in PSCs (Figure 2c), which is slightly different to typical RRAM devices. In such devices, the switching voltage represents the driving force required for ions diffusion and penetration through PCBM. The FAMA devices exhibit a switching voltage of 1.9 V with abrupt increase in current, which is comparable with the built-in electric field of PSCs (~1.5 V). This result indicates that in control FAMA PSCs, ions are entirely possible to migrate and penetrate PCBM layer under built-in field. Note that once RRAM transits from HRS to LRS during scanning forward from 0 V to positive voltage, the HRS will be maintained since the current is still very high when scanning reverse from positive voltage to 0 V again, indicating the irreversible ions diffusion. In real RRAM devices, the recover from LRS to HRS requires negative voltage, which is beyond the scope of this work. The irreversible ions diffusion will lead to irreversible degradation in PSCs, which will be discussed in stability section below. However, in devices with TMTA IBL, ions diffusion can be blocked by chemically crosslinked TMTA layer, thus suppressing the formation of conductive vacancy filaments (Figure 2d). The switching voltage from HRS to LRS is 4.1 V, over 2 times higher than that in FAMA devices. Such a high switching voltage ensures the entirely blocked ions diffusion under built-in electric field in real PSCs. Combining with the results from diffusion and permeation experiments, we can

# 3.4. Operational Stability

Excellent thermal stability is one precondition for operational stability since ions diffusion rapidly increases with temperature according to the ionic conductive theory of Nernst-Einstein relation. As shown in Figure 5a, thermal stability is obviously increased in PSCs with TMTA IBL and 95% of initial efficiency is retained even after annealing at 80 °C. Despite slightly rapid degradation at 100 °C, the PSCs with TMTA IBL still retain over 70% of the initial efficiency. However, FAMA devices start to degrade at 60 °C and obvious efficiency loss is observed at 80 °C as only 75.6% of the initial efficiency is retained. When further increasing to 100 °C, FAMA PSCs sharply degrade, merely retaining 15.6% of

the initial efficiency. Next, we prolong the aging time to 1000 hours to investigate the long-term stability of PSCs (Figure 5b). PSCs with TMTA IBL retain over  $ 90\% $  of the initial efficiency after aging at  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 1000 hours. While only  $ 62.1\% $  of the initial efficiency is retained in control FAMA devices under the same conditions. XRD of the aged PSCs is recorded (Figure S7) and the peaks ascribed to perovskite are still clearly observed. This result indicates that the possible phase segregation induced degradation in mixed perovskite layer can be excluded, which agrees well with previous report that  $ < 10\% $  Br content in mixed perovskite ( $ 5\% $  in our work) can avoid phase segregation and lead to stable perovskite alloys[47]. Note that in the XRD patterns of FAMA PSCs (Figure S7), an extra peak ascribed to CuI appears around  $ 42.6^{\circ} $ , indicating the possible ions migration/diffusion and reaction with Cu electrode during thermal aging. # 4. Conclusion

In summary, we demonstrate an ions-blocking strategy to fabricate operationally stable PSCs at elevated temperature through the introduction of chemically crosslinked TMTA as IBL. Diffusion experiment, permeation experiment and RRAM investigation confirm that TMTA IBL can efficiently block ions diffusion in PSCs whether under heat, light or electric field conditions, thus suppressing the irreversible device degradation. The PSCs with crosslinked TMTA IBL exhibit much improved operational stability even at elevated temperature of 60 °C, retaining approximately 80% of initial efficiency after MPP tracking for 1000 hours under continuous illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). To the best of our knowledge, this is the first report of inverted PSCs with long-term operational stability (>500 hours) at elevated temperature. While in control PSCs, less than 60% of the initial efficiency is retained merely within 300 hours under the same conditions. In addition, thermal stability is also greatly improved in PSCs with TMTA IBL. The resulting PSCs can endure high temperature of 100 °C without severe efficiency loss and over 90% of the initial efficiency can be retained after aging at 60 °C for 1000 hours.

Our work highlights and demonstrates the role of interfacial protection at perovskite/CTL interface on operationally stable PSCs, especially at elevated temperature. Different to previous reports about electrode protection at CTL/metal electrode interface to suppress the ions-induced electrode erosion,[31, 34, 35] we propose the strategy of blocking ions diffusion at perovskite/CTL interface by using chemically crosslinked TMTA IBL. We also confirms that the ions penetration into PCBM from perovskite will deteriorate the electrons mobility of PCBM, damaging the electrons transmit and transport in PSCs and thus inducing irreversible device degradation. The insertion of crosslinked TMTA at perovskite/PCBM interface will block the ions diffusion from perovskite to PCBM due to its

As a result, both the ions penetration into PCBM and possible ions-induced reaction with metal electrode are suppressed, thus improving the operational stability of PSCs. We firmly believe that our ions-blocking strategy at perovskite/CTL interface will be a promising method to fabricate operationally stable PSCs, which may provide a new direction for the further development of PSCs.

# Highlights

1. Ions blocking strategy is proposed to fabricate operational stable perovskite solar cells (PSCs) through the incorporation of chemically crosslinked TMTA.

2. TMTA in perovskite layer suppresses ions migration along grain boundaries and TMTA at perovskite/PCBM interface blocks ions diffusion toward electrode.
