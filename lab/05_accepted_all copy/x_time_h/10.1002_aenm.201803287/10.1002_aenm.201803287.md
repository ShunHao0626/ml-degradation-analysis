# Molecular Engineering of Copper Phthalocyanines: A Strategy in Developing Dopant-Free Hole-Transporting Materials for Efficient and Ambient-Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Xiaoqing Jiang, Dongping Wang, Ze Yu,  $  ^{*}  $   Wanying Ma, Hai-Bei Li, Xichuan Yang, Feng Liu,  $  ^{*}  $   Anders Hagfeldt, and Licheng Sun  $  ^{*}  $

Copper (II) phthalocyanines (CuPcs) have attracted growing interest as promising hole-transporting materials (HTMs) in perovskite solar cells (PSCs) due to their low-cost and excellent stability. However, the most efficient PSCs using CuPc-based HTMs reported thus far still rely on hygroscopic p-type dopants, which notoriously deteriorate device stability. Herein, two new CuPc derivatives are designed, namely CuPc-Bu and CuPc-OBu, by molecular engineering of the non-peripheral substituents of the Pc rings, and applied as dopant-free HTMs in PSCs. Remarkably, a small structural change from butyl groups to butoxy groups in the substituents of the Pc rings significantly influences the molecular ordering and effectively improves the hole mobility and solar cell performance. As a consequence, PSCs based on dopant-free CuPc-OBu as HTMs deliver an impressive power conversion efficiency (PCE) of up to 17.6% under one sun illumination, which is considerably higher than that of devices with CuPc-Bu (14.3%). Moreover, PSCs containing dopant-free CuPc-OBu HTMs show a markedly improved ambient stability when stored without encapsulation under ambient conditions with a relative humidity of 85% compared to devices containing doped Spiro-OMeTAD. This work thus provides a fundamental strategy for the future design of cost-effective and stable HTMs for PSCs and other optoelectronic devices.

In state-of-the-art PSCs, charge-selective contacts including hole-transporting materials (HTMs) and electron-transporting materials (ETMs) are indispensable components for achieving high PCE and high stability.  $  ^{[12-14]}  $   They play essential roles in extracting and collecting the photo-generated charge from the perovskite to the respective electrode, thus

effectively reducing undesirable recombination losses at the interface and improving solar cell performance. To date, the most efficient PSCs typically utilize 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD)

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted widespread research interest due to their low

production cost, simple processability, and remarkably high power conversion efficiency (PCE).  $  ^{[1-4]}  $   A great deal of research effort has been dedicated to the development of PSCs, including perovskite compositional engineering, advanced deposition techniques for the fabrication of high-quality perovskite films, device architecture design, and charge-selective layer optimization, as well as stability improvement etc.  $  ^{[5-9]}  $   In just a few years, the PCE of PSCs has been dramatically increased from the initial 3.8% to a certified value of over 23%, making them one of the most promising choices among low cost next-generation solar cell technologies.  $  ^{[10,11]}  $

Additionally, Spiro-OMeTAD and PTAA are subject to low hole mobility and conductivity in their pristine form due to their amorphous nature.  $  ^{[15]}  $   Therefore, p-type dopants or additives, such as lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI), are ubiquitously required to increase the conductivity, and thus to enhance the overall efficiency.  $  ^{[16-18]}  $   Discouragingly, lithium salts are hygroscopic, which could accelerate device degradation.  $  ^{[18-21]}  $   Consequently, the exploration of cost-effective and dopant-free alternative HTMs with high stability and moisture resistance is an important endeavor for the large-scale application of PSCs. In this context, a variety of dopant-free HTMs have been developed in PSCs, including small molecules, conductive polymers, and inorganic hole conductors.  $  ^{[22-29]}  $

Another interesting class of HTMs is metal phthalocyanines, which have been intensely used as p-type semiconductors in thin film transistors and organic solar cells, owing to some of their unique properties, such as ease of synthesis related to low production cost, relatively high hole mobility, and high hydrophobicity, as well as excellent chemical, thermal, and photo stability.  $  ^{[30]}  $   The optical and electronic properties of these macrocycles can be easily adjusted by modifying the peripheral and non-peripheral substituents and the metal center.  $  ^{[31]}  $   In this sense, metal phthalocyanines, such as copper (II) phthalocyanines (CuPcs) and zinc (II) phthalocyanines (ZnPcs), have attracted considerable attention as HTMs in PSCs.  $  ^{[31-40]}  $   Torres and Nazeeruddin et al. developed molecularly engineered ZnPcs as solution-processable HTMs in PSCs, showing an impressive PCE of 17.5%.  $  ^{[31]}  $   Recently, a CuPc derivative has also been explored as a HTM in PSCs, affording a high PCE of 18.8%.  $  ^{[41]}  $   It was also disclosed that the devices with CuPc-based HTMs displayed an outstanding thermal stability in comparison to Spiro-OMeTAD and PTAA. Nevertheless, the most efficient PSCs employing ZnPc- and CuPc-based HTMs reported thus far still need to be p-doped to achieve high PCE. Until now, dopant-free CuPc and ZnPc HTMs in PSCs, in particular the high-performing ones, have been rarely reported.  $  ^{[30,35,42,43]}  $

Here, two new CuPc derivatives, coded as CuPc-Bu and CuPc-OBu (Figure 1), are produced by molecular engineering of the non-peripheral substituents in the Pc rings, and applying them as dopant-free HTMs in PSCs, in conjunction with three dimensional (3D)/two dimensional (2D) graded perovskite as the absorbing layer. The motivation was to study the influence of the structural change in the substituents of the Pc rings on the molecular arrangement of CuPcs in thin films, which regulates the charge transport property and the solar cell performance. Notably, there is only a small difference in the substituents of these two CuPcs, one with butyl groups (CuPc-Bu) and the other with butoxy groups (CuPc-OBu). However, it was found that the incorporation of the butoxy groups in the substituents of CuPc-OBu induced a significant influence on directing the molecular ordering in thin films and the hole transport ability. The optimized PSCs employing CuPc-OBu as dopant-free HTMs afforded a best PCE of  $ 17.6\% $  under full sun irradiation  $ (100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $ , AM 1.5G), which was substantially higher than that of the devices based on CuPc-Bu  $ (14.3\%) $ . The much-improved performance for CuPc-OBu based devices was mainly attributed to more effective charge transport and collection, strongly associated with its superior hole mobility. Additionally, the stability of un-encapsulated PSCs based on dopant-free CuPc-OBu HTMs was further studied in ambient conditions with a relative humidity (RH) of  $ 85\% $  at room temperature, in comparison with doped Spiro-OMeTAD. The devices incorporating CuPc-OBu exhibited a much-improved ambient stability, maintaining  $ 84\% $  of the initial efficiency after  $ 120\mathrm{h} $ .

The two new CuPc derivatives were further examined as dopant-free HTMs in PSCs. The energy level diagram and device architecture of PSCs are shown in Figure 4a and Figure 4b. The PSCs were fabricated with a configuration of FTO glass/compact TiO $ _2 $ /mesoporous TiO $ _2 $ /perovskite/HTM/Au. It is worth noting that 3D/2D graded perovskite was used as the absorbing layer in this study; the reasons for this will be discussed in the following section. The mixed-ion 3D perovskite (FAPbI $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.85} $ (MAPbBr $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.15} $  was prepared using a solvent-engineering technique according to a previous method. $ ^{[54,55]} $  An in-situ grown 2D perovskite PEA $ _2 $ PbI $ _4 $  (PEA $ ^+ $  = C $ _6 $ H $ _5 $ C $ _2 $ H $ _4 $ NH $ _3^+ $ ) thin capping layer was formed on top of the 3D perovskite film by spin-coating a phenethylammonium iodide solution, which reacted with excess PbI $ _2 $ . $ ^{[56,57]} $  The top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM) image of the 3D/2D perovskite, as shown in Figure S11 in the Supporting Information, clearly demonstrated a morphology change with some smaller size crystals in contrast to the 3D perovskite. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern of the 3D/2D perovskite (Figure S12, Supporting Information) confirmed that the newly formed perovskite was

consistent with the PEA $ _2 $ PbI $ _4 $  phase. $ ^{[57]} $  Figure 4c displays the cross-sectional SEM image of fabricated PSCs. The perovskite crystals were infiltrated into the mesoporous TiO $ _2 $  scaffold (~150–200 nm) and formed a uniform capping layer with a thickness of ~700 nm. The HTM layer (~40 nm) was then deposited on top of the perovskite film, followed by thermal evaporation of a layer of 100 nm gold as the back contact. More detailed information about solar cell fabrication is described in the Supporting Information.

As shown in Figure 4d and Table 2, it is noticeable that all the photovoltaic parameters of the best device with CuP-Bu were lower than the corresponding values of the CuPc-OBu-based devices, in particular for the  $  J_{sc}  $  (21.0 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ) and FF (0.66), resulting in an inferior PCE of 14.3%. The  $  J-V  $  hysteresis effect of devices with CuPc-Bu and CuPc-OBu was examined, and the results are summarized in Figure 4d and Table 2. The PSCs with CuPc-OBu exhibited a reduced hysteresis relative to that of CuPc-Bu-based devices. The hysteresis indexes were calculated to be 0.10 (CuPc-Bu) and 0.03 (CuPc-OBu), respectively, according to previous literature. $ ^{[59]} $  It has been reported that the  $  J-V  $  hysteresis is strongly associated with the nature of the electronic contact between the perovskite and the HTM layer. $ ^{[60,61]} $  The superior hole transport property of CuPc-OBu is expected to facilitate hole extraction and minimize the charge accumulation at the perovskite/HTM interface, thus resulting in a small hysteresis.

The incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) measurements were performed for PSCs with pristine CuPc-Bu and CuPc-OBu as shown in Figure 4e. The CuPc-OBu based devices exhibited higher IPCE values than those of CuPc-Bu based devices over the whole measured region. The integrated current densities from the IPCE spectra for devices based on CuPc-Bu and CuPc-OBu were estimated to be 20.5 and 21.8 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , respectively, which were basically in line with the values from the J-V measurements. The higher   $  J_{sc}  $   obtained for CuPc-OBu based devices should be mainly due to more efficient hole transport and collection, originating from its superior hole transport characteristic.

To verify the efficiencies obtained from the  $ J - V $  characterizations, the steady-state photocurrent output of PSCs based on CuPc-Bu and CuPc-OBu as HTMs at the maximum power point for  $ 180~\mathrm{s} $  under full sun irradiation was studied, as shown in Figure S15 in the Supporting Information. The corresponding stabilized PCEs are displayed in Figure 4f. The PSCs based on CuPc-OBu displayed a steady-state efficiency of  $ 16.3\% $ , which

was substantially higher than that of the devices containing CuPc-Bu (13.3%). This result is in good agreement with the PCE values obtained from J-V curves. The reproducibility of the PSCs employing CuPc-Bu and CuPc-OBu as HTMs were also investigated based on 30 identical devices as presented in Figure S16 in the Supporting Information. The statistical data showed small standard deviations, indicative of good device reproducibility. The average PCEs for PSCs based on CuPc-Bu and CuPc-OBu were   $  13.3 \pm 0.9\%  $   and   $  16.0 \pm 1.0\%  $  , respectively.

To gain more insights into the charge transfer properties of the PSCs based on CuPc-Bu and CuPc-OBu as HTMs, EIS measurements were carried out under one sun illumination with varied bias voltages in the frequency range from   $  10^{6}  $  Hz to 0.1 Hz. The Nyquist plots are given in Figure 5a and 5b. The corresponding results are plotted in Figure 5c and 5d according to a previously reported equivalent circuit model given in the insets of Figure 5a and 5b.  $  ^{[37]}  $  According to this model, the arc in the high frequency region represents the hole transport characteristic between the HTM and the cathode (  $  R_{HTM}  $  ). The main arc in the low frequency region is related to the charge recombination characteristics (  $  R_{rec}  $  ). At a given potential, the devices using CuPc-OBu showed a lower  $  R_{HTM}  $  than the corresponding value for the devices with CuPc-Bu (Figure 5c). This indicated more effective hole transport for the CuPc-OBu-based PSCs, which accounted for the higher FF achieved for such devices. The larger  $  R_{rec}  $  observed for the CuPc-OBu-based devices (Figure 5d) explained the slightly higher  $  V_{oc}  $  obtained from the J-V characterizations.

Finally, the stability of the PSCs (ten cells) based on dopant-free CuPc-OBu was further evaluated for 120 h in ambient conditions with 85% RH at a temperature of 20 °C, stored in the dark without any encapsulation. For comparison purposes, the stability of PSCs (PCE = 18.8%) with the most commonly used HTM Spiro-OMeTAD containing Li-TFSI and 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP) was also investigated under identical conditions. Figure 6a and 6b present the evolution of the photovoltaic parameters for the representative PSCs based on these two kinds of HTMs. The devices with CuPc-OBu exhibited good long-term stability, retaining 84% of their initial efficiency after 120 h (Figure 6b). Note that the humidity condition of 85% RH used in this work is the standard testing procedure for commercialized silicon solar cells. The excellent ambient stability achieved for CuPc-OBu-based devices under such a stringent testing condition is very remarkable. By contrast, the devices containing doped Spiro-OMeTAD presented a considerably faster degradation rate, with only 21% of the original efficiency remaining during the testing period (Figure 6b). Representative photovoltaic parameters for PSCs containing CuPc-OBu and doped Spiro-OMeTAD at different stages during the stability test are listed in Table S1 and Table S2 in the Supporting Information. After 120 h, a distinct color change of the perovskite film from dark brown to yellow was detected for Spiro-OMeTAD-based PSCs, as shown in Figure 6c, whereas no significant color change was observed for the CuPc-OBu-based devices during the testing period. The yellow color was an indication of the formation of PbI  $  _{2}  $  . This comparison indicated that the HTM layer plays an important role in determining the ambient stability of PSCs. # 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, two new CuPc derivatives as dopant-free HTMs in PSCs have been designed by tuning the non-peripheral substituents of the Pc rings. The effects of the structural change in the substituents of the Pc rings on the molecular arrangement of CuPcs in thin films and the hole transport property, as well as on the overall solar cell performance, were systematically studied. It was determined that the structural change in the substituents of the Pc rings had a pronounced influence on the molecular orientation, and thus the hole mobility. The champion PSCs incorporating CuPc-OBu exhibited a much improved PCE of 17.6% compared to that of CuPc-Bu based-device (14.3%), which was largely ascribed to more efficient charge transport and collection as a result of the superior hole transport capability of CuPc-OBu. The PCE achieved by CuPc-OBu is among the top efficiencies reported to date for PSCs based on CuPc HTMs. In addition, PSC devices containing dopant-free CuPc-OBu HTMs also exhibited excellent stability when stored without encapsulation in ambient conditions with a RH of 85% relative to doped Spiro-OMeTAD, mainly due to the high moisture resistivity of the CuPc-OBu interlayer. The present study offers a new design strategy for further development of highly efficient and stable HTMs for PSCs and other optoelectronic devices.
