# Excellent Moisture Stability and Efficiency of Inverted All-inorganic  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr}_2 $  Perovskite Solar Cells through Molecule Interface Engineering

Shuzhang Yang  $  ^{1}  $  , Liang Wang  $  ^{1*}  $  , Liguo Gao  $  ^{2}  $  , Junmei Cao  $  ^{2}  $  , Qianji Han  $  ^{1}  $  , Fengyang Yu  $  ^{1}  $  , Yusuke Kamata  $  ^{1}  $  , Chu Zhang  $  ^{3}  $  , Meiqiang Fan  $  ^{3}  $  , Guoying Wei  $  ^{3}  $  , Tingli Ma  $  ^{1,3*}  $

$  ^{1}  $  Graduate School of Life Science and Systems Engineering, Kyushu Institute of Technology, 2-4 Hibikino, Wakamatsu-ku, Kitakyushu, Fukuoka, Japan.

$  ^{2}  $  State Key Laboratory of Fine Chemicals, School of petroleum and chemical engineering, Dalian University of Technology, Panjin, 124221, P. R. China.

# Abstract

All-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have drawn widespread interest owing to its excellent thermal stability comparing with its organic-inorganic hybrid counterpart. Poor phase stability caused by moisture, however, has thus far limited their commercial application. Herein, by modifying the interface between the hole transport layer (HTL) and the perovskite light absorption layer, and by optimizing the HTL for better energy alignment, we controlled growth of perovskite, reduced carrier recombination, facilitated carrier injection and transport, and improved PSC's power conversion efficiency (PCE) and moisture stability. When tested using positive bias scan, a significant improvement PCE of  $ 9.49\% $  is obtained which is the champion efficiency of  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $ -based inverted PSC at present. The stability measurement shows that the passivated  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $ -based inverted PSCs can retain  $ 86\% $  of its initial efficiency after  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  preserved in ambient air with  $ 65\% $  relative humidity (RH). This study paves a new way for enhancing the moisture stability and power conversion efficiency of  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $ -based PSCs.

Keywords: All-inorganic inverted perovskite solar cells,  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $ , Interface engineering, N749, Moisture stability.

# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have received widely interest since the first demonstration of the use in solar cells  $  ^{1}  $  and further improving its power conversion efficiency (PCE) in solid-state PSCs.  $  ^{2-5}  $  Recently, the PCE of inorganic/organic PSCs has reached 25.2%.  $  ^{6}  $  PSCs main advantages include a high absorption coefficient, appropriate band gap, easy fabrication process and low cost  $  ^{7-9}  $ ; as a result, it is widely regarded as a promising photovoltaic technology for commercialization. However, conditions of humidity and heat expose the fragility of the organic component of the perovskite absorption layer, resulting in subpar device operational lifetime, which precludes commercialization of PSCs  $  ^{10-12}  $ . More and more studies have thus focused on all-inorganic metal halide perovskite (ABX  $  _{3}  $ , A represents cation, B metal and X represents halide) solar cells. All-inorganic Br rich perovskite with same cations are mainly containing CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   $  ^{13,14}  $ , CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br  $  ^{15,16}  $ , CsPbIBr  $  _{2}  $   $  ^{17,18}  $ , and CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $   $  ^{19-20}  $ . Among these CsPbIBr  $  _{2}  $  has a narrower band gap (around

At present, the PCE of normal structure (FTO/ electron transport layer (ETL) /PVK/HTL/Au)   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs has reached more than 10 %  $  ^{22}  $  . Yet, their operational stability remains a key problem to be solved. Previous studies have shown that   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs exhibit much better thermal stability than inorganic/organic hybrid halide PSCs; they can maintain 90 % of the maximum PCE after exposing the device to 200 °C for 1 h  $  ^{23}  $  . However, the most serious drawback of all-inorganic perovskite with mixed halide is their poor moisture stability. Several researchers have pointed out that moisture can lead to reversible and irreversible degradation of perovskite films  $  ^{24}  $  . It is easy for water molecules to penetrate the perovskite structure and form an intermediate monohydrate and dihydrate perovskite. Water molecules with grain boundaries will form hydrogen bonds with the cations at the A site, which weakening the bond between the cation and the octahedron, facilitating deprotonation of the A site cations. Accelerated deprotonation also takes place in the presence of external stressors such as heat or electric fields  $  ^{25, 26}  $  . Many approaches have tried to overcome this significant drawback. For example, Huang et al. showed a new method to stabilize the perovskite layer by introducing water-insoluble PbO layer   $  {}^{27}  $  . Zhao and co-workers retarded the formation of cracks and pinholes in   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   by using choline iodide. The resulting PSCs are highly reproducible and stable, reaching the highest PCE in ambient conditions of   $  45 \pm 5  $   °C  $  ^{28}  $  . Finally, our group employed   $  Nb^{5+}  $   ions into the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite, stabilizing the all-inorganic PSCs by tuning the composition of perovskite  $  ^{29}  $  .

In recent years,   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based PSCs demonstrated some obvious advantages in inverted device structures, e.g. moisture stability and a suppressed hysteresis effect. The PCE of   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based inverted PSCs with hybrid perovskite has reached 20 %  $  ^{30}  $  . However, few studies considered all-inorganic PSCs with   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based inverted device structure (especially   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $  -based PSCs), the excellent moisture stability, good photovoltaic performance and simple processing procedure of   $  NiO_{x}  $   make it a prime candidate for improving the moisture stability of all-inorganic inverted PSCs  $  ^{31, 32}  $  .

In this study, we employed   $  NiO_{x}  $   as HTL in inverted   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $  -based PSCs. To match the energy level between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $ , the Fermi level of   $  NiO_{x}  $   was modulated by incorporating cesium ion. Furthermore, we propose a simple interface modification strategy for   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based inverted all-inorganic   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs, with Tris(N,N,N-tributyl-1-butanaminium)[[2,2''6',2''-terpyridine]-4,4',4''-tricarboxylato(3-)-N1,N1',N1'']tris(thiocyanato-N)hydrogen ruthenate(4-) (N749) being inserted into the interface of   $  Cs-NiO_{x}/CsPbIBr_{2}  $ . Our research proposes that N749 plays a crucial part in tuning energy level, improving open circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) from 0.86 to 1.22 eV and yielding a PCE of 9.49 %. This is the highest record PCE of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $  -based inverted PSCs until now. In addition, the device exhibits extremely high moisture stability, retaining 86 % of its initial PCE after 1000 hours in ambient air with 65% relative humidity. Our results provide an important way to prepare high performance all-inorganic perovskite devices.

# Experiment section

# Materials

Materials and Reagents: FTO conductive substrate (Ying Kou OPV Tech Co. Ltd), Nickel acetate (Wako, Japan). Lead bromide (PbBr $ _2 $ , 99.99 %, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.), Cesium iodide (CsI, 99.9 %, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.), Tris(N,N,N-tributyl-1-butanaminium)[[2,2''6',2''-terpyridine]-4,4',4''-tricarboxylato(3-)-N1,N1',N1'']tris(thiocyanato-N)hydrogen ruthenate(4-) (N749, Wako, Japan), [6,6]-phenylC $ _{61} $  butyric acid methyl ester (PC $ _{61} $ BM, >99.5 %, Wako, Japan) and bathocuproine (BCP, 99 %, Wako, Japan). All liquid reagents were purchased from Wako.

# Device fabrication and Characterization

Preparation of the cesium salts doped   $  NiO_{x}  $   substrate: The patterned FTO substrate was washed ultrasonically with acetone, water, and ethanol for 0.5 h. The   $  NiO_{x}  $   precursor doped with different cesium salts (CsI, CsBr, CsCl, and CsAc) was prepared by mixing 1 % (molar ratios) cesium salts with 0.2 M   $  \mathrm{Ni(Ac)_{2}\cdot4H_{2}O}  $   IPA solution and stirred overnight. Afterwards, the HTL was deposited using the spin coating method at 3000 rpm for 30 s, and then the samples were annealed at 400 °C for 1 h. For the passivation layer, a N749 IPA solution with a concentration range between 0.01 and 0.03 M was deposited on the   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $   surface at 5000 rpm for 30 s. Then baked it at 100 °C for 10 min. The detail processing procedure of perovskite,   $  PC_{61}BM  $  , BCP and Ag electrode can be found in previous works [30]. For the long-time stability test, the device was sealed with a glass coated with UV glue that was solidified by exposing it to UV light at 150 W for 10 s. About characterization methods and equipment information, please refer to our pervious works [22].

# Results and Discussion

The disadvantages of undoped   $  NiO_{x}  $   are its low conductivity and unmatched Fermi level, which can increase the risk of recombination and thus reduce efficient charge collection. It is reported that metal elements doped   $  NiO_{x}  $   can obtain better conductivity and realize a higher work function  $  ^{33-35}  $  . Herein, we selected different cesium salts as dopant, adding them into the   $  NiO_{x}  $   precursor (marked as   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $  ). This is shown in Figure S1a. It was found that the precursor color changed from green to blue after doping with cesium salt. The absorption (Figure S1b) and ultraviolet photoemission spectroscopy (UPS) of   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $   samples were also tested (Figure S2). The results show significant improvement of work function—which is agreement with the findings reported in the literature  $  ^{36}  $  . We further found that the PSCs with CsI doped   $  NiO_{x}  $   has the best efficiency, primarily due to better energy level matching (Figure S3). The   $  V_{OC}  $   of the devices after optimization is still relatively low, however, which is probably a result of a larger offset of   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $ /perovskite.

Therefore, the holes generated by the perovskite will transfer to the N749 first and then be collected by the HTL (Cs-NiO  $  _{x}  $  ). Finally, all the holes are collected by the FTO substrate. The concentration of the N749 significantly impacts the overall performance of PSCs. Therefore, N749 with different concentration levels (0.01-0.05 M) were deposited on the Cs-NiO  $  _{x}  $   surface. The characteristic J-V curves of CsPbIBr  $  _{2}  $  -based PSCs with different concentrations of N749 are shown in Figure S5a. In addition, the relevant detailed photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table S1. The highest PCE, 9.49 %, is obtained with a N749 concentration of 0.03 M. To understand why this configuration resulted in the highest PCE, an electrochemical impedance measurement (EIS) was made (see Figure. S5b). The values of the equivalent circuit are shown in Table S2. Apparently, the suppression of recombination process and decrease of series resistance of PSC passivation with 0.03 M N749 are responsible for improving the PCE. Consequently, the PSCs passivation with 0.03 M N749 was selected for further study.

The   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $   and   $  Cs-NiO_{x}/N749  $   films images of scanning electron microscope (SEM), the scanning Kelvin probe microscopy (SKPM) and the atomic force microscope (AFM) are plotted in Figure 2. Evidently, applying N749 on the surface of   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $   significantly alters the film surface morphology. In Figure 2a and b, some agglomerated black regions on the   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $   surface can be observed easily, which might affect the crystallization process of perovskite and may result in severe charge recombination between HTL and perovskite. In contrast, following the introduction of N749, there are no observable pinholes and agglomerations, with the surface being much smoother. According to the 3D AFM images (Figure 2c and d), the presence of N749 lead to a reduction of the surface roughness from 9.16 nm to 8.17 nm (as compared to unpassivated   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $   layers). Similarly, surface potential variations measured using SKPM demonstrate a lower surface potential profile when   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $   film was passivated by N749 (Figure 2e and f). N749 passivation thus can improve the work function of the   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $ , facilitate charge carrier transfer and collection between the   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $   and the perovskite. In addition, passivated   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $   films have similar transmittance properties in the visible light spectrum as the ordinary   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $   films (Figure S6). Photon absorption by the perovskite layer is thus undiminished.

At present, a modified hysteresis index (HI) is widely used to describe the difference in the performance under different scanning direction:

$$
\mathbf {H I} = \frac {J _ {R S} (0 . 8 V _ {O C}) - J _ {F S} (0 . 8 V _ {O C})}{J _ {R S}}

$  J_{\mathrm{RS}}  $  (0.8  $  V_{OC}  $ ) and  $  \mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{FS}}  $  (0.8  $  V_{OC}  $ ) are corresponding to the  $  \mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{SC}}  $  at 80 % of the  $  V_{OC}  $  of the RS and FS measurements  $  ^{38,39}  $ . The steady photoluminescence (PL) measurements reveal no impurity phase, which is agreement with the UV-visible absorption spectra (Figure 4e). Next, PL and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) can give insights into the photogenerated carrier properties of the structures of the FTO/  $  Cs-NiO_{x}/CsPbIBr_{2}  $   and FTO/  $  Cs-NiO_{x}/N749/CsPbIBr_{2}  $  . The PL intensity of the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   film with a N749 layer has lower peak intensity comparing with its counterpart without a N749 layer, demonstrating that carriers can be efficiently extracted by   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $ , resulting in fast fluorescence quenching. Figure 4f shows the typical TRPL spectra. The carrier decay lifetime is obtained by fitting curves using a biexponential decay the parameters are listed in Table S4. The results show that the device without a N749 layer delivers PL decay time of   $  \tau_{1} = 1.30  $   ns and   $  \tau_{2} = 6.24  $   ns, with an average lifetime of 1.45 ns. On the other hand, the device with a N749 are   $  \tau_{1} = 0.75  $   ns and   $  \tau_{2} = 3.36  $   ns, with an average lifetime of 0.75 ns. The 50 % decrease of the average lifetime suggests that the N749 enhances charge transfer from perovskite to   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $  . In addition, the passivation effect of N749 effectively accelerates the hole injection to HTM making possible the improved   $  V_{OC}  $   of the N749-treated devices. The above-mentioned results point to an excellent photovoltaic property of the   $  Cs-NiO_{x}/N749  $   PSCs, which can be attributed to the optimized interface connection, reduced work function, as well as suppressed recombination.

Most importantly, we also tested the durability of the PSCs. It is clearly seen that the characteristic absorption edge of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $  film totally disappeared after  $ 24\mathrm{h} $ . Because of larger perovskite grains, the perovskite films have lower surface area/volume ratios, as well as fewer defects (usually exist in the grain boundaries). Thus, the perovskite films with smaller perovskite grains is more easily degradation induced by water. Furthermore, perovskite films with larger grain size are beneficial for better solar cell performance resulting from the longer nonradiative lifetimes and longer carrier diffusion distance. Larger perovskite grain size is thus desirable for both the efficiency and the stability of devices  $  ^{54-56}  $  . Figure 6a and 6b shows the SEM images of samples without and with N749. The N749 passivation thus has a significant effect on the crystallization process of the perovskite film.

# Conclusions

In conclusion, we study the feasibility of a simple method for passivating   $  Cs-NiO_{x}  $   surface by N749 to improve the device performance and moisture stability of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $  -based inverted PSCs. This method markedly improved the PCE of the PSCs, from 6.28 % to 9.49 % and drastically improved moisture stability. The improvements are mainly due to N749's role in assisting perovskite crystal growth, inhibiting interfacial charge recombination and enhancing the hole injection to HTL. The application of N749 could effectively suppress the phase segregation and prevent the moisture infiltration. The stability measurements confirm that the PSCs passivated by N749 can keep 86 % of initial efficiency over 1000 h under ambient air with 65 % RH. This work thus paves a new way to prepare all-inorganic halide perovskite devices with outstandingly stable and efficient performance.
