# Abstract

Inverted perovskite solar cells with  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  hole transport layer (HTL) have attracted abundant attention due to low-cost fabrication and high device-efficiency. However, the further development of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ -based inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs) would be restricted by the unsatisfactory electrical properties in pristine  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  and the non-ideal charge transfer between perovskite and  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ . In this work, a facile and effective method was designed to regulate the electronic property of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  HTL and make an alteration to the interface between the perovskite and  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ .  $ \mathrm{NH_4F} $  was used as an interfacial modifier to enhance the charge transfer between the perovskite layer and NiOx. The  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ -F layer was generated with high transmittance and low trap density. Besides, the power conversion efficiency of the device was improved from  $ 17.17\% $  to  $ 18.94\% $  with the improvement of short-circuit current ( $ \mathrm{J_{sc}} $ ) by using the structure of ITO/ $ \mathrm{NiO_x/NH_4F/MAPbI_3/PCBM/BCP/Ag} $ . Finally, the device with  $ \mathrm{NH_4F} $  exhibited better stored stability in the dry condition compared with the control device.

# Keywords:   $  NiO_{x}  $  ; Inverted PSCs; Trap-state density; interface

# 1. Introduction

As one of the next generation photovoltaic technologies, organometal halide hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved tremendous development because of the superb optoelectronic properties and the potential for low-cost production[1, 2]. In the past ten years, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has been increased from  $ 3.8\% $  [3] to over  $ 24\% $  [4], which was even higher than that of multicrystalline silicon based solar cells [5] and CIGS solar cells [6]. Due to the low-deposition temperature (typically  $ < 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ), negligible hysteresis, and high efficiency, the inverted PSCs were becoming more attractive among the various structures of PSCs[7]. Recently, the charge transfer between the perovskite layer and the hole transport layer (HTL) was observed to be a major factor, which would deteriorate the performance of the device [8-10].

Typically, the electrical and optical requirements of the HTL and the ideal interfacial environment should be simultaneously satisfied by the high-effective charge transfer in PSCs. Several p-type materials such as poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene): poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) [11, 12], poly(triaryl amine) (PTAA) [13],  $ \mathrm{Cu_2O} $  [14, 15] and  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  [16-18] have been used as the HTL in the inverted PSCs.  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  was regarded as one of the most suitable p-type semiconductor in high-efficiency inverted PSCs, the reported champion PCE for the  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ -based inverted PSCs has reached  $ 21\% $  [9], which was similar with that of the PTAA-based device [13]. However, the low intrinsic conductivity of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  was a major drawback, which would lead to the hole accumulation and charge recombination, various strategies such as the metal-ions doping [7, 19-23] and the surface modification [8-10] have

been proposed to solve this problem, Metal-ions doping was a very efficient way to enhance electrical conductivity and charge carrier mobility of   $  NiO_{x}  $  , Liyuan Han's group doped Mg-Li ions into the   $  NiO_{x}  $   HTL to achieve very rapid carrier extraction and a certified PCE>15% was obtained with an aperture area above   $  1\ cm^{2}  $   [24]. Jae Woong Jung et al. employed Co-doped NiOx to reduce the trap-sites and suppressed charge recombination in the interface [23]. Zhu-Bing He's team used the Cu doping strategy to further improve the conductivity, charge transfer and extraction of   $  NiO_{x}  $   HTL, a high PCE of 20.26% was obtained for small-area devices by using Cu:  $  NiO_{x}  $   HTL [18]. Furthermore, some other metal ions such as Li [8], Zn [20], Fe [22], Ag [21] and Rb [19] were also doped into   $  NiO_{x}  $  .

A novel path to improve the conductivity of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  and the interface was provided by the surface modification. For example, 2,2'-(perfluoronaphthalene-2,6-diylidene) dimalononitrile (F6TCNNQ) was introduced by Zhu-Bing He and his co-worker to improve the conductivity and mobility of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ , the PCE of the device with  $ \mathrm{NiO_x / F6TCNNQ} $  reached  $ 20.86\% $  [9]. Moreover, alkali chloride-modified  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  was simultaneously used by Zhu-Bing He' group to suppress the interfacial recombination in inverted PSCs,  $ 20.96\% $  PCE was obtained with a high open-circuit voltage (Voc) (1.15V) [10]. Furthermore, Sai-Wing Tsang et al. treated the  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  surface with n-Butylamine to reduce the surface dipole in NiOx film and the defect density in the perovskite layer [25]. The electronic properties of NiOx played an important role in the performance of inverted-planer perovskite solar cells.

In this paper, a facile strategy to tune the  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  thin films by improving the electronic properties and the charge transfer simultaneously was proposed. First, ammonium fluoride  $ (\mathrm{NH_4F}) $  was used as an interfacial modifier to suppress the interfacial recombination and improve the charge transfer;  $ \mathrm{NH_4F} $  was spin coated onto the  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  film, and  $ \mathrm{NiO_x - F} $  was obtained by low-temperature annealing, F element could be well detected in  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  films. The  $ \mathrm{NiO_x - F} $  layer was generated with high transmittance and the perovskite on the  $ \mathrm{NiO_x - F} $  layer was obtained with good contact and low trap-state density. The power conversion efficiency of the device was improved from  $ 17.17\% $  to  $ 18.94\% $  with the improvement of short-circuit current  $ (\mathrm{J_{sc}}) $  by using the structure of ITO/ $ \mathrm{NiO_x / NH_4F / Perovskite / PCBM / BCP / Ag} $ . Finally, the device with  $ \mathrm{NH_4F} $  exhibited better stored stability in the dry condition compared with the control device.

# 2. Experimental Section

# 2.1 Materials and fabrication process

$ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , MAI,  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  and BCP were all obtained from Xi'an Polymer Light.  $ \mathrm{NH_4F} $ , Chlorobenzene (CB), IPA, DMF and DMSO were all obtained from Aladdin. All solvents were used without any further purification.

The cleaned ITO substrates were treated in UV-ozone for 10 min. The NiOx nanoparticles were synthesized according to our previous work [26], the NiOx ink was spin-coated onto the ITO substrates at 3000 rpm for 30 s, then the NiOx films were obtained by annealing at 120☐ for 10min. For interfacial modification of   $  NiO_{x}  $   thin films, the   $  NH_{4}F  $   solution (1, 2, 5 wt% in DI water) was dropped onto the   $  NiO_{x}  $   films and kept for 60 s, and the treated film were spin-coated at 5000 rpm for 30 s, and then annealed at 80☐ for 5min. The 1M MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   precursor solution in DMF: DMSO (10:1, v/v) was stirred at 60☐ overnight. Then the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film was fabricated at 3000 rpm for 30 s and 120 μL CB was quickly dripped onto the surface of the films at 10s after the beginning of spin-coating, then the films were annealed at 105 °C for 5 min. PC  $  _{61}  $  BM (20 mg/mL in CB) was prepared onto the perovskite films at 4000 rpm for 20 s. BCP (0.5 mg/mL in IPA) was deposited onto the PCBM layer at 2000rpm for 30s. Finally, 100 nm Ag electrode was thermal evaporated to finish the device fabrication. The active areas (0.1 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) were defined by using metal masks.

# 2.2 Characterizations

The morphology of the samples was measured by field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM, ZEISS SUPRA55). The image of nanoparticles was obtained by TEM (JEM 1200EX). The phase of the perovskite was collected by X-ray diffraction (XRD, SmartLab from Rigaku). The valence state of elements was measured by using

The I-V curves of the device with the structure of ITO/NiOx (or NiOx-F)/Au were shown in Fig.3 (b) in order to analysis the effect of  $ \mathrm{NH_4F} $  on the properties of NiOx film. Compared with the NiOx device, a higher voltage of the NiOx-F device was observed under the same current density. The direct conductivity ( $ \sigma $ ) can be calculated according to the relation  $ I = \sigma \mathrm{Ad}^{-1}\mathrm{V} $ , d corresponds to the thickness of the films and A is the active area of the films. The thickness of the NiOx film was  $ 21~\mathrm{nm} $  and the active area of the films was  $ 0.1~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ . The results showed that the conductivity of the NiOx-F film exhibited an increase from  $ 6.3\times 10^{-5}\mathrm{S}\cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-1} $  to  $ 1.09\times 10^{-4}\mathrm{S}\cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ , therefore, the conductivity of NiOx film could be well enhanced with the treatment of  $ \mathrm{NH_4F} $  solution [29].

Meanwhile, the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) of the device was measured to analysis the enhancement mechanism of conductivity with the purpose of further assessing the behavior of  $ \mathrm{NH_4F} $  on the trap-state density of NiOx film. As shown in Fig. 3 (c) and (d), the current-voltage curves were tested by fabricating the device with the structure of ITO/NiOx (or NiOx-F)/perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD/Au, the I-V curves was linear at the low bias voltage and a steeply rising current was observed as the bias voltage increase. The device was prepared with the structure of ITO/NiOx/NH $ _4 $ F/Perovskite/PCBM/BCP/Ag to verify the enhancement behavior of NH $ _4 $ F on the performance of the perovskite device, as shown in Fig. 4 (a). The agreement between the Jsc and the integrated current density from the measured EQE spectrum should be verified. The EQE spectrum was shown in Fig. 4 (c). # Conclusion

To sum up, a facile and effective method was found to regulate the electronic property of NiOx HTL; the interface between the perovskite and NiOx was altered. Besides,  $ \mathrm{NH_4F} $  was acted as an interfacial modifier to improve the trap-state density and the charge transfer between the perovskite layer and NiOx. The NiOx-F layer was generated with high transmittance and low trap density. The power conversion efficiency of the device was improved from  $ 17.17\% $  to  $ 18.94\% $  with the improvement of short-circuit current  $ (\mathrm{J_{sc}}) $  by using the structure of ITO/ $ \mathrm{NiO}_x / \mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{F}/ $  Perovskite/PCBM/BCP/Ag. Finally, the NiOx-F device exhibited better stored stability in the dry condition, compared with the control device.
