# Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid halide perovskites represented by   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbX_{3}  $   (MAPbX  $  _{3}  $  , X=I, Br, Cl) possess unique optoelectric properties such as tunable optical bandgaps, large absorption coefficients, long carrier diffusion lengths, and high carrier mobilities [1–7]. Utilizing the organic-inorganic hybrid halide perovskites as photoactive layer, perovskite solar cells (PVKSCs) have been attracting ever-increasing interests during the past decade, and versatile strategies have been developed to fulfill high efficiency of PVKSCs, including tuning the composition, crystallinity, phase and morphology of the perovskite absorber layer through optimizing

Bulk heterojunction (BHJ) perovskite solar cells (PVKSCs) are advantageous in passivating defects and facilitating electron extraction/transport. Most of the reported fullerene derivatives involved in BHJ-PVKSCs render single-site interactions, limiting their passivation effect. Herein, we developed a perfluoroalkyl and pyridine-cofunctionalized fullerene derivative   $  \left(\mathrm{C}_{60}\text{-PyF15}\right)  $  , and applied it as an additive of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   layer to construct inverted (p-i-n) BHJ-PVKSCs, affording double-site defect passivation of perovskite film and a champion power conversion efficiency of 20.10%, which is among the highest values for fullerene-incorporated inverted BHJ-PVKSCs. To elucidate the crucial role of the perfluoroalkyl group in efficiency enhancement, another analogous novel non-fluoro-substituted pyridine-functionalized fullerene derivative with the same chain length of alkyl group   $  \left(\mathrm{C}_{60}\text{-PyH15}\right)  $   was also synthesized, which afforded a champion PCE of 19.22% and is lower than that of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15. The double-site defect passivation ability of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 is resulted from the coordination interaction of the pyridine moiety with   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions and hydrogen bonding between the fluorine atom within the perfluoroalkyl group and the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}  $  cations. These enable ordered orientation of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}  $  and suppressed ion migration, leading to efficiency enhancement and improved ambient and thermal stabilities.

In particular, additive engineering accomplished by incorporating small amounts of additives like small organic molecules   $  [2,16–19]  $  , polymers   $  [20,21]  $  , fullerene derivatives   $  [22–28]  $   into perovskite layer has been demonstrated to effectively improve the film quality of perovskite through regulating its crystallization process and passivating the defects   $  [2,29–31]  $  .

Additive engineering has been extensively employed to enhance the efficiency and stability of inverted PVKSCs. incorporating fullerene derivatives as additives into perovskite layer, bulk heterojunction (BHJ) PVKSCs are constructed, which mimic the commonly used architecture of polymer solar cells and show boosted efficiency and stability relative to the single-component PVKSCs [36,38,39]. For instance, as the first fullerene-based BHJ-PVKSC [37], [6,6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) was incorporated into  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  precursor solution with a concentration of below  $ 0.1\mathrm{wt}\% $  and able to fill the pinholes and vacancies at perovskite grain boundaries, resulting in effective passivation of the Pb-I antisite defects [26,36-42]. Later on, Jen et al. synthesized a fluoroalkyl-substituted fullerene derivative  $ (\mathrm{DF - C_{60}}) $  and incorporated it into  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  solution to construct inverted BHJ-PVKSC, achieving an obvious PCE enhancement from  $ 15.67\% $  to  $ 18.11\% $  resulted from defect passivation. In addition, the hydrophobic nature of  $ \mathrm{DF - C_{60}} $  contributed to improved ambient stability of the devices [43]. Furthermore, they incorporated  $ \mathrm{DF - C_{60}} $  into the low-bandgap Pb-Sn binary perovskite  $ (\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{Pb}_{0.5}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.5}\mathrm{I}_3) $  via an anti-solvent approach to construct graded heterojunction PVKSCs. The graded distribution of  $ \mathrm{DF - C_{60}} $  within the perovskite film effectively decreased the number of trap sites, improved the absorber quality with lower Urbach energy and higher surface/bulk recombination resistance, leading to an outstanding PCE of  $ 15.61\% $  [44]. More recently, Li et al. applied a hydrophilic fullerene derivative [6,6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid-(3,4,5-tris(2-(2-(2-methoxyethoxy)ethoxy) ethoxy) phenyl) methanol ester (PCBB-OEG) as an addition into  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  precursor solution, which acted as a soft-template to assist the growth of high-quality  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  crystals, leading to reduced trap-states of perovskite grain boundaries and eliminated hysteresis. As a result, PCE of the inverted BHJ-PVKSC device reached  $ 20.2\% $ , which represents the highest value for fullerene-incorporated inverted BHJ-PVKSC device [22]. It is worth noting that most of these reported fullerene derivatives involved in inverted BHJ-PVKSCs bear only single functional groups such as fluoroalkyl, ester, polyethylene glycol, which render single-site interactions with perovskite, thus limiting their passivation effect. Therefore, an open questions arises: whether can involving additional functional groups onto fullerene derivative additive render multiple-site and reinforced interactions with perovskite?

Herein, we designed and synthesized a novel perfluoroalkyl and pyridine-cofunctionalized fullerene derivative (  $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15), and applied it as an additive of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   layer. The resultant inverted BHJ-PVKSCs deliver a champion PCE of 20.10%, which is dramatically higher than that of the control device without additive (17.09%) and among the highest values for fullerene-incorporated inverted BHJ-PVKSCs. Another analogous novel non-fluoro-substituted pyridine-functionalized fullerene derivative with the same chain length of alkyl group (  $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15) was also synthesized and employed as an additive of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   layer so as to elucidate the role of the perfluoroalkyl group. A series of characterizations were carried out, revealing that the coordination interaction between the pyridine moiety within   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 with   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite and the formation of hydrogen bonds between the fluorine atom within the perfluoroalkyl group with the   $  MA^{+}  $  cations result in double-site defect passivation of perovskite film, leading to the dramatic efficiency enhancement as well as improved ambient and thermal stabilities of BHJ-PVKSC devices.

# Device fabrication

The FTO-coated glass substrates were sequentially washed with detergent, deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol for 15 min, then dried under vacuum in an oven overnight. Followed by treated in an ultraviolet-ozone chamber for 15 min. The as-prepared NiO $ _{x} $  precursor solution was spin-coated onto the FTO at 4000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at 280 °C for 1 h to obtain NiO $ _{x} $  HTL. Then, the HTL-coated FTO substrates were transferred into a nitrogen-filled glovebox to deposit MAPbI $ _{3} $  perovskite films. For the fabrication of the perovskite layer, the control perovskite film, a PbI $ _{2} $ /CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ I mixture solution with 1.1 M PbI $ _{2} $  and 1 M CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ I in mixed solvents (DMF/DMSO, 7:3, v/v) was prepared and stirred at 60 °C overnight. For the preparation of C $ _{60} $ -PyF15/C $ _{60} $ -Py15-doped perovskite films, a solution of C $ _{60} $ -PyF15/C $ _{60} $ -Py15 in chlorobenzene (CB) was added into the perovskite solution according to different weight ratio of 0.005 wt%, 0.01 wt%, 0.05 wt% and 0.1 wt% to prepare the doping precursor solutions. Afterwards, all the solutions were spin-coated onto the NiO $ _{x} $  substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s. Followed by dropping 100 μL chlorobenzene during the last 20 s of the spinning process. The film was then heated at 100 °C for 10 min. PCBM (20 mg/mL in chlorobenzene) was then spin-coated on perovskite films at 2000 rpm for 30 s. Then a cathode buffer layer bathocuproine (BCP, 0.5 mg/mL) was spin-coated on PCBM ETL with a solution of isopropanol (IPA). Finally, Ag electrode (70 nm) was deposited on BCP in a vacuum chamber under a pressure of about 10 $  ^{-5}  $

Torr. The effect active area of the device was   $  0.1 \, cm^{2}  $   which was defined by a shadow mask.

# Results and discussion

# Syntheses and characterizations of  $ C_{60} $ -PyF15 and  $ C_{60} $ -PyH15

The novel perfluoroalkyl and pyridine-cofunctionalized fullerene derivative  $ (\mathrm{C}_{60} $  -PyF15) was synthesized facilely by a two-step reaction, including a Bingel-Hirsch reaction in the second step as illustrated in Fig. 1a (see Supporting Information S1 for details). For comparison, another analogous novel non-fluoro-substituted pyridine-functionalized fullerene derivative with the same chain length of alkyl group  $ (\mathrm{C}_{60} $  -PyH15) was also synthesized.

The molecular structures of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 and   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 were characterized by   $  {}^{1}H  $  ,   $  {}^{13}C  $   and   $  {}^{19}F  $   NMR spectroscopy, matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight (MALDI-TOF) mass spectrometry and Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy. According to the   $  {}^{1}H  $   NMR spectrum of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15, the proton signals of two different types of hydrogens within the pyridine group and one methylene of perfluoroheptyl chain are clearly observed at 8.10, 8.91 and 4.89 ppm, respectively (see Supporting Information Fig. S2a). The   $  {}^{19}F  $   NMR spectrum of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 shows a signal at -80.66 ppm assigned to the terminal trifluoromethyl and other signals in the range of -118.98 to -126.09 ppm attributed to the other fluorine atoms within the perfluoroheptyl group (see Supporting Information Fig. S2c). Likewise,   $  {}^{1}H  $   and   $  {}^{13}C  $   NMR spectra

of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 indicate the characteristic proton and carbon signals (see Supporting Information Fig. S4). MALDI-TOF mass spectra of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 and   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 show the dominant ionic peaks at m/z = 1237 and 967, respectively, confirming their proposed chemical structures (see Supporting Information Fig. S5). Besides, FT-IR spectroscopic study was further carried out to verify the chemical structures of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 and   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 (see Supporting Information Fig. S6). Both   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 and   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 exhibit an intense vibrational peak at 526 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , which is ascribed to the   $  C_{60}  $   cage [45,46]. On the other hand, characteristic vibrational peaks of the pyridine group within   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 and   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 are found at 1592 and 1591 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , while those of the stretching vibrations of the C=O bond are detected at 1764 and 1740 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , respectively. Apparently, the stretching vibrational signals of the heptyl chain observed at 2922 and 2850 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 disappear in the FTIR spectrum of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15.

# Effect of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 additive on the photovoltaic performance of inverted BHJ-PVKSC devices

In order to evaluate the effect of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 additive on the photovoltaic performance of device, we fabricated inverted BHJ-PVKSC devices with the structure of FTO/  $  NiO_{x}/MAPbI_{3} + C_{60}  $  -PyF15/PCBM/BCP/Ag (Fig. 1b).   $  NiO_{x}  $   film (~20 nm thick) prepared by a sol-gel process is incorporated as the hole transport layer (HTL), and bathocuproine (BCP) with a thickness of ~6 nm is spin-coated onto the PCBM electron transport layer (ETL) as a cathode buffer layer.   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 was dissolved in chlorobenzene and doped into the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite precursor solution with different doping ratio (0.005, 0.01, 0.05 and 0.1 wt%), and the optimized doping concentration of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 additive is determined to be 0.05 wt% according to the photovoltaic performance (see Supporting Information S8). For comparison, the control devices without any additive, with the same amount of chlorobenzene solvent additive, and with   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 additive were also fabricated through the same fabrication process. Fig. 2a compares the current density-voltage (J-V) curves of the BHJ-PVKSC devices with and without 0.05 wt%   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15/  $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 additive obtained under 1 sun illumination, from which the corresponding photovoltaic parameters including open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ), short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ), fill factor (FF),

PCE, series resistance (  $  R_{s}  $  ), and shunt resistance (  $  R_{sh}  $  ) are obtained and summarized in Table 1. The control device without any additive exhibits the best PCE of 17.09%, with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.013 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.67 mA/cm², a FF of 74.38%. Incorporating chlorobenzene solvent additive results in negligible influence on the device performance (see Supporting Information Table S3). After incorporating   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15, the best PCE increases dramatically to 20.10% derived from a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.073 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 23.14 mA/cm², and a FF of 80.96%. Noteworthy, such a PCE of 20.10% is dramatically higher than that of the control device without any additive (17.09%) and among the highest values for fullerene-incorporated inverted BHJ-PVKSCs (see Supporting Information S9) [22]. Upon using   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 as additive, both of the average and best PCEs increase relative to the control device without any additive as well, which are however lower than those of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 device. These results reveal that involvement of the pyridine group in fullerene derivative benefits device efficiency enhancement, while the perfluoroheptyl group additionally contributes to further efficiency enhancement. Hence, perfluoroheptyl and pyridine cofunctionalization appears to be crucial for realizing double-site interaction with perovskite (Fig. 1c) as discussed in details below.

The PCE enhancement induced by   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15/  $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 additive is mainly due to the simultaneous increases of   $  V_{oc}  $  ,   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF (see Supporting Information Fig. S13). This tendency is verified by comparing the statistical photovoltaic parameters based on 50 devices fabricated independently (Fig. 2b, see also Supporting Information Fig. S14 for the box plots). On the other hand, like the reported planar inverted PVKSCs, all of our BHJ-PVKSCs devices exhibit negligible hysteresis under reverse and forward scans (Fig. 2c, see also Supporting Information Table S6 for detailed photovoltaic parameters). Moreover, we measured the steady-state photocurrent outputs at the maximum power point, and find that PCEs of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 and   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 devices can be stabilized at ~19.95% and ~18.92% respectively during 300 s illumination, which are both higher than that of the control device (~17.02%) (see Supporting Information Fig. S15). We further confirmed the increase of   $  J_{sc}  $   by comparing the external quantum efficiency (EQE) values of the devices with and without   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15/  $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 additive, which indicate that both   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 and   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 devices have higher EQE responses in the region of 380–750 nm than that of the control device, resulting in higher integrated   $  J_{sc}  $   (Fig. 2d).

# Effects of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 and   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 on the built-in potential and charge carrier dynamics of the BHJ-PVKSC device

To investigate the effect of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15/  $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 on the built-in potential of the BHJ-PVKSC device, we measured the Mott-Schottky (M-S) plots of devices with and without   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15/  $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 additive (Fig. 5a), from which the obtained built-in potentials (  $  V_{bi}  $  ) of the control device and devices with   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 and   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 are 0.81, 0.84 and 0.88 V (see Supporting Information S22 for detailed analyses). The largest increase of the   $  V_{bi}  $   value after incorporating   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 additive suggests that   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 may provide the strongest driving force to promote the charge separation and to suppress the electron-hole recombination by the dipole effect of perfluoroalkyl group [20,59,60]. In addition, carrier concentration (  $  N_{A}  $  ) can be calculated from the linear region of M-S plots [61]. After incorporating   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 additive, the   $  N_{A}  $   value increases from   $  1.09 \times 10^{16}  $   to   $  1.24 \times 10^{16}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $   (see Supporting Information Table S8). This indicates that   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 incorporation leads to reduced defect density of perovskite layer. This contributes to the largest   $  V_{oc}  $   value of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 device, due to the increased   $  V_{bi}  $   value resulted from the dipole effect of perfluoroalkyl group.

To unveil the correlation between the incorporated   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 within perovskite film and the enhanced   $  V_{oc}  $  , we further detected visually the surface potential (  $  V_{sp}  $  ) of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite film by Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) to verify the change of work function of the perovskite film, and found that after incorporating   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15/  $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 additive the surface potential of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite film becomes more positive than that of the pristine MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite film (see Supporting Information Fig. S25). This suggests that the work function of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite is lowered after incorporating   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15/  $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 additive [22,62], which is beneficial for the improved   $  V_{oc}  $   and charge transfer [63,64]. By considering the similar energy levels between   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 and pristine perovskite, the decreased work function (WF) of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15-based perovskite film compared to that of the pristine perovskite film should be attributed to the self-assembly of perfluoroalkyl side-chain within   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 on top of the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite layer producing an additional interfacial dipole moments, which can further enhance the built-in electric field of device, resulting in an further enhancement of   $  V_{oc}  $   and benefits electron transport at the perovskite/PCBM interface [20,22,54]. The decreased   $  \varepsilon  $   value suggests a suppressed monomolecular Shockley-Read-Hall (SRH) recombination process, which is beneficial for improving device performance [24]. The slope of   $  C_{60}  $ -PyF15 device is calculated to be 1.35 kT/q, which is smaller than those of control device (1.85 kT/q) and the   $  C_{60}  $ -PyH15 device (1.54 kT/q), indicating that   $  C_{60}  $ -PyF15 incorporation leads to the most effective suppression of the trap-assisted carrier recombination within the perovskite layer, consequently contributing to the highest photovoltaic performance. The smallest   $  J_{0}  $   value of the   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 device suggests the lowest recombination loss and corresponding enhanced FF [67].

# Effects of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 and   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 on the stability of the BHJ-PVKSC device

The effect of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15/  $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 additive on the ambient stability of the BHJ-PVKSC devices was investigated by storing the devices without any encapsulation in ambient and dark conditions (temperature:   $  25 \pm 5^{\circ}C  $  , relative humidity: 35%). As shown in Fig. 6a, PCE of the   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 device maintains approximately 85% of its initial value after exposed in ambient condition for 1400 h, whereas the   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 and the control devices retain only ~69% and ~56% of their initial PCEs respectively under the same condition. The degradation of device performance is mainly originated from the decreases of   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF (see Supporting Information Fig. S29 for detailed analyses). The improved ambient stability of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 devices were further verified by comparing the colors of the perovskite films with and without   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 which were exposed in air with 40–70% relative humidity for more than three months (Fig. 6b). Obviously, the pristine perovskite changes from black to yellow after 90 days aging, whereas   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15-incorporated perovskite film still retains its initial black color after 90 days aging. Moreover, the XRD peaks of the pristine perovskite film show obviously decomposition along with the dramatic increase of   $  PbI_{2}  $   phase, whereas   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15-incorporated perovskite exhibits negligible change of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   phase (Fig. 6c), suggesting that incorporation of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 effectively protects the perovskite film from the erosion of moisture due to the hydrophobicity of   $  C_{60}  $   cage and the perfluoroheptyl chain. This assumption is supported by comparing the static water contact angles of perovskite films with and without   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15/  $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15. The water contact angles of the pristine   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  ,   $  MAPbI_{3} + C_{60}  $  -PyH15 and   $  MAPbI_{3} + C_{60}  $  -PyF15 films are   $  41.5^{\circ}  $  ,   $  47.8^{\circ}  $   and   $  56.6^{\circ}  $  , respectively (see Supporting Information Fig. S30). The enhanced hydrophobicity of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15-incorporated perovskite film is attributed to the hydrophobic perfluoroheptyl chain within   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15, which effectively block the penetration of moisture into perovskite film through the grain boundaries, leading to the enhanced device stability.

Thermal stability of PVKSCs with and without   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15/  $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 additive were further investigated by monitoring the PCEs of devices heated to 85 °C for 660 h in a nitrogen-filled glovebox (Fig. 6d), and the corresponding changes of   $  V_{oc}, J_{sc}  $   and FF parameters are listed in Supporting Information Fig. S31. Obviously, the   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 device exhibits the highest thermal stability, retaining exceeding 81% of its initial PCE. For comparison, the control and   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15-incorporated devices retain only 43% and 55% of their initial PCEs, respectively. # Conclusions

In summary, we synthesized a novel perfluoroalkyl and pyridine-cofunctionalized fullerene derivative (  $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15) as well as another analogous novel non-fluoro-substituted pyridine-functionalized fullerene derivative with the same chain length of heptyl group (  $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15) via a two-step reaction, which were employed as additives of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   layer to construct inverted BHJ-PVKSCs.   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15-incorporated BHJ-PVKSC device achieve a champion PCE of 20.10%, which is dramatically higher than that of the control device without additive (17.09%) and among the highest values for fullerene-incorporated inverted BHJ-PVKSCs. The pyridine moiety within   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 and   $  C_{60}  $  -PyH15 renders coordination interaction with   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite layer, and the fluorine atom within the perfluoroheptyl group of   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 provides an additional robust site to form N-H···F hydrogen bonds with the   $  MA^{+}  $  cations, resulting in ordered orientation of   $  MA^{+}  $  cations and suppressed ion migration. As a result,   $  C_{60}  $  -PyF15 additive enables double-site defect passivation of perovskite film, leading to the dramatic efficiency enhancement as well as significantly improved ambient and thermal stabilities of BHJ-PVKSC devices, which can maintain 85% of its initial efficiency after exposing under the ambient condition (~35% relative humidity) for 1400 h and under thermal treatment (85 °C) in nitrogen atmosphere without encapsulation for 660 h. With the effectiveness of double-site defect passivation of perovskite film in efficiency and stability enhancements, our proof-of-concept study sheds light on

designing multifunctional additives for high-efficiency and stable perovskite solar cells.
