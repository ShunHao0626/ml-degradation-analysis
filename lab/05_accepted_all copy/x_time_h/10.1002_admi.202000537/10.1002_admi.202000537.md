# PEG Modified CsPbIBr  $  _{2}  $  Perovskite Film for Efficient and Stable Solar Cells

Yibo You, Wei Tian,* Min Wang, Fengren Cao, Haoxuan Sun, and Liang Li*

Cesium lead mixed-halide perovskite (CsPbIBr $ _2 $ ), as one of the all-inorganic perovskites, has attracted great attention owing to its great ambient stability and suitable bandgap. Unfortunately, due to its low film coverage, high density of defects and unfavorable band energy level, the CsPbIBr $ _2 $  based solar cells suffer from low efficiency. In this work, the Lewis base poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG) is adopted as additive to modify the pure CsPbIBr $ _2 $ . By optimizing the molecular weight and dosage of PEG, the resultant PEG:CsPbIBr $ _2 $  film possesses suppressed non-radiative electron-hole recombination, a favorable energy band structure and a weaker sensitive to the moisture. As a result, the device based on the PEG:CsPbIBr $ _2 $  yields a champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 11.10%, with a open-circuit voltage of 1.21 V, a short-circuit current of 12.25 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and a fill factor of 74.82%, which is 44.3% higher than its counterpart without PEG. Moreover, the PEG modified device shows excellent long-term stability, retaining over 90% of the initial efficiency after 600 h storage in ambient condition without encapsulation. In comparison, the device without PEG shows an inferior stability with PCE sharply dropping to 0% within 50 h.

The surface modification provides an effective strategy to improve the prop-

erties of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   film, such as surface morphology, band energy, work function, etc. Liu et al. deposited   $  SmBr_{3}  $   at the interface of   $  TiO_{2}  $   and   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite layer, which can effectively change the crystallization and morphology of the perovskite film and create a gradient energy band structure at the interface.  $  ^{[20]}  $   Finally, the   $  SmBr_{3}  $   modified devices attained a champion PCE of 10.88%. Zhang et al. employed the intermolecular exchange strategy to modify the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   film by spin-coating CsI solution on the surface of the perovskite, which can decrease the pinholes and increase the grain size.  $  ^{[21]}  $   As a consequence, a champion PCE of 9.16% was realized. Meanwhile, the solution additive is also a kind of strategy in modifying the inorganic perovskite film. Hayase et al. reported the incorporation of cesium xanthate (CsXth) into   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   precursor. When the perovskite film was annealed at a high temperature, CsXth decomposed into cesium sulfides, and then the   $  S^{2-}  $   entered into the perovskite lattices and interacted with   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $ , which can strongly promote the stability of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   and boost the PCE.  $  ^{[22]}  $

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cell (PSC) has made spectacular progress in power conversion efficiency (PCE), which has increased from 3.8% to 25.2% in the past decade.  $  ^{[1,2]}  $   However, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite suffers from poor long-term stability, partly due to the volatile nature of organic components.  $  ^{[3-10]}  $   Thus, replacing the organic component with inorganic counterpart is a reasonable strategy to mitigate this problem.  $  ^{[11,12]}  $   After substituting the organic cations with inorganic cesium ion (Cs  $  ^{+}  $  ), the all-inorganic perovskites have an identified higher intrinsic stability under heat, oxygen, and even illuminated conditions.

The enhancement mechanism of polymer additive is generally ascribed to the facts that polymer improves the morphology of perovskite films and reduces the defect concentration. Nevertheless, the effects of polymer additive on energy level tuning, morphology control and defect passivation, especially the stability issue, have not been clearly unraveled. Meanwhile, the reported efficiency and stability of  $  \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2}  $  based PSC are still far from desired.

Herein, to address the aforementioned problems, PEG is selected as a model polymer additive to be introduced into   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   film. On the basis of systematic characterization, the PEG molecule has a strong interaction with the Lewis acid   $  PbBr_{2}  $ , which can control the nucleation and crystal growth process of perovskite, yielding a pinhole-free perovskite film with favorable band energy structure and remarkably reduced defect states. The C–O–C bond strongly coordinates with   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   to stabilize the   $  \alpha  $ -phase. In addition, PEG physically covers the perovskite, which also plays the role as the moisture protection barrier. By optimizing the molecular weight and dosage of PEG, we realize a champion PCE of 11.10% for devices based on PEG modified   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $ , which is among the highest reported values for   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   based PSCs. Moreover, the PEG modified device shows a satisfied stability, which can keep a PCE of over 90% after 600 h storage without encapsulation.

# 2. Results and Discussion

PEG, as one kind of Lewis bases, was reported to have a strong interaction with the Lewis acid such as   $  PbI_{2}  $   or   $  PbBr_{2}  $  . Besides, previous studies reported that the dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) as the solvent can interact with   $  PbBr_{2}  $   and   $  PbI_{2}  $   to form into intermediate adduct to slow down the crystallization process of perovskite.  $  ^{[27]}  $   To investigate how PEG interacts with   $  PbBr_{2}  $   at the presence of solvent DMSO, the Fourier transform infrared spectrometer (FTIR) spectra of DMSO,   $  PbBr_{2}  $  -DMSO, and PEG:  $  PbBr_{2}  $  -DMSO are shown in Figure 1a. The latter two samples were prepared by spin coating the solution on the glass, and followed by annealing at 70 °C for several hours to evaporate the extra DMSO. The residual powders were collected for FTIR measurement. As shown in the enlarged image, the S=O stretching vibration frequency has a red-shift from 1054 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for DMSO to 1008 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for   $  PbBr_{2}  $  -DMSO and then further shifts to 1005 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for PEG:  $  PbBr_{2}  $  -DMSO, illustrating that the introduced PEG can strengthen the interaction between   $  PbBr_{2}  $   and DMSO, which would greatly slow down the crystallization rate.  $  ^{[28]}  $   The time-dependent photographs of the perovskite film during crystallization process in Figure S1, Supporting Information reveal a slow color change for PEG:CsPbIBr  $  _{2}  $  , demonstrating that the introduction of PEG can obviously decelerate the film formation process, in accordance with the FTIR result. Besides, compared to the sample without PEG, PEG:CsPbIBr  $  _{2}  $   shows a synchronous change of color. As shown in Figure 1b, after annealing at 200 °C, the DMSO volatilizes completely, and the S=O frequency disappears. The C–O–C stretching vibration frequency emerges at 1103 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for pure PEG.  $  ^{[29]}  $   While

for PEG:PbBr $ _2 $  and PEG:CsPbIBr $ _2 $ , the C–O–C frequency values are shifted to 1080 and 1094 cm $ ^{-1} $ , respectively, due to the reduced force constant of C–O–C stretch. This manifests the strong interaction between PEG and Pb $ ^{2+} $ . The XPS spectra of pure CsPbIBr $ _2 $  and PEG:CsPbIBr $ _2 $  are shown in Figure 1c. For pure CsPbIBr $ _2 $ , the peaks located at 142.8 and 137.9 eV are attributed to Pb $ ^{2+} $ . While for the PEG modified sample, the Pb $ ^{2+} $  peaks shift toward low binding energy, attributing to the strong interaction between PEG and Pb $ ^{2+} $ . On the basis of the above results, the PEG additive can assist the formation and stabilization of CsPbIBr $ _2 $  by strongly interacting with Pb $ ^{2+} $ .

X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern of pure   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   and PEG:  $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   were collected to illustrate the crystallization properties of the perovskite, as shown in Figure 1d. The strong diffraction peaks at   $  14.97^{\circ}  $  ,   $  22.56^{\circ}  $  , and   $  30.17^{\circ}  $   can be assigned to the (100), (110), and (200) planes of   $  \alpha-CsPbIBr_{2}  $  , respectively.  $  ^{[30]}  $   However, PEG modified perovskite shows different XRD pattern, with increased intensity for the (110) plane and reduced intensity for the (100) and (200) planes, indicating a preferential growth of perovskite crystals with the presence of PEG. The transmission electron microscope (TEM) image of the PEG:  $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   is shown in Figure 1e. As a shell polymer surrounding the perovskite crystal, PEG shows no crystal lattice. The covered perovskite crystal obtains an obvious lattice fringes, meaning that the PEG additive does not affect its crystallinity. The inset image shows the perovskite crystal is fully covered by the PEG molecule. The energy dispersive X-ray (EDX) line scanning of the O, Cs, Pb, I, and Br signals are shown in Figure S2, Supporting Information. The scanning position is labeled in TEM image by the orange line. An increased intensity at the position of about 60 nm is observed for O element, which originates from the PEG molecules. While for other elements Cs, Pb, I, and Br, the corresponding signals increase at around 65 nm, implying that the PEG molecules cover the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   crystal. The schematic illustration of the formation process for PEG:  $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   is shown in Figure 1f. The   $  PbBr_{2}  $   interacts with the PEG molecules and DMSO through C–O–C and S=O groups, resulting in an intermediate PEG-  $  PbBr_{2}  $ -DMSO adduct. The adduct is expected to slow down the crystal growth rate and improve the quality of perovskite. Then the CsI molecules combine with   $  PbBr_{2}  $   to maintain a metastable state, and form into the cubic   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   after annealing.

The scanning electron microscope (SEM), atomic force microscope (AFM) and Kelvin probing force microscopy (KPFM) images of the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite without (Figure 2a–c) and with PEG (Figure 2d–f) were recorded. For the pristine   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   film, there are a large number of pinholes on the surface, which would induce amounts of shunting paths and unfavorable interfacial charge transfer, resulting in a low fill factor (FF), open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ), and short-circuit current (  $  J_{sc}  $  ). On the contrary, the PEG modified perovskite layer shows a full and uniform surface coverage with many large grains. Similarly, the AFM images in Figure 2b,e also show that the perovskite film without PEG possesses several pinholes on the surface, whereas the PEG modified perovskite shows a better coverage.

The  $ J_{sc} $  shows a linear relationship versus light intensity with slope close to one, implying negligible bimolecular recombination in the devices. $ ^{[33]} $  The  $ V_{oc} $  has a linear relationship with natural logarithmic light intensity. Previous works have reported that the deviation between the slope of  $ V_{oc} $  and the value of kT/q reflects the trap-assisted recombination. Compared with the slope of 4.27 for pure CsPbIBr $ _2 $  device, the PEG:CsPbIBr $ _2 $  device has a smaller slope of 2.38, illustrating that the trap-assisted recombination in the device have been significantly reduced with the introduction of PEG. As shown in Figure 4c, the leakage current is measured under dark to reveal the charge recombination in the devices. $ ^{[38]} $  The current density of the PEG:CsPbIBr $ _2 $  device is about two orders of magnitude lower than that of the device based on pure CsPbIBr $ _2 $ , which is attributed to the compact morphology and higher quality of the PEG modified perovskite.

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, we modify the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite with PEG by optimizing its molecular weight and concentration. The PEG modified perovskite gives rise to a high surface coverage without pinholes, resulting in greatly reduced defects concentration. During the fabrication process, the introduced PEG obviously slows down the crystallization rate and strongly interacts with the   $  Pb^{2+}  $ , which can decrease the non-radiative recombination sites. In addition, the PEG modified   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite obtains a favorable energy band and a better photogenerated carriers separation efficiency at the interface of the perovskite and   $  TiO_{2}  $ , which lead to an increased   $  V_{oc}  $ . As a result, we attain a champion PCE of 11.10% for the PEG-modified   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs. Moreover, the PEG-modified device obtains a great resistance against the heat and moisture, resulting in a great stability.
