# Trifluoromethylphenylacetic Acid as In Situ Accelerant of Ostwald Ripening for Stable and Efficient Perovskite Solar Cells

Hepeng Zhu, Chenghao Duan, Minchao Qin, Zidan Liu, Jiong Li, Ligang Yuan, Qiaoyun Wen, Xinhui Lu, Lei Shi,* Jiangsheng Xie,* and Keyou Yan*

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite thin film quality, including large grain size and low defect, is one of the fundamental factors to improve the performance and stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, trifluoromethylphenylacetic acid (TFPA) as the accelerant of Ostwald ripening is introduced and an in situ crystal growth control (ICGC) strategy is developed to obtain high-quality thin films. With adding a proper amount of TFPA into the FAI/MAX (X is Br and Cl) precursor in the two-step method, the larger grain size and reduced defect density perovskite are achieved due to Ostwald ripening. The ICGC process makes the average particle size from 476 to 625 nm. The defect density is reduced by a factor of 3.18. The high-quality thin film endows the champion PSC (SnO  $  _{2}  $  /perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD) with a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of champion PSC higher than 22.19%. Furthermore, TFPA is insoluble in the water and thus TFPA-modified film is resistive to moisture. The device can be stored in an argon glove box for 2000 h without efficiency change and when exposed to 65–75% relative humidity for more than 1500 h, it retains more than 85% of the initial PCE.

There are two types of factors resulting in instability of PSCs. One is the extrinsic factor, such as the stress of oxygen, light, moisture, and temperature-induced degradation. The other is the intrinsic factor, such as ionic properties, defects, unstable components, and interface materials.  $  ^{[23-26]}  $   As well known, solubility of the precursor and weak formation energy of perovskite facilitate the solution process to fabricate photovoltaic device and thus these bring the rapid development of PSCs with low entry level.  $  ^{[27-29]}  $   However, it is the double-edged sword.  $  ^{[30]}  $   First of all, due to weak formation energy, organic cations (methylamine [MA] and formamidine [FA]) and halogen anions (I, Br, and Cl) will tend to volatilize during annealing and illumination, which brings about a lot of vacancy defects,  $  ^{[10,31,32]}  $   mostly belonging to shallow defects.  $  ^{[31,33]}  $   With halide anions and organic cations evaporating from the perovskite surface, the inorganic   $  Pb^{2+}  $   has a tendency to be reduced to   $  Pb^{0}  $   elementary substance distributing on the perovskite film surface and grain boundaries, which is deep level trap resulting in nonradiative recombination to impact final device performance.  $  ^{[34-38]}  $   According to previous reports, all the defects are believed to play an important role in the chemical degradation of the perovskite

Organic–inorganic perovskite materials have been receiving widespread attention since the beginning of application in the photovoltaic field, due to their splendid photoelectric properties, such as efficient light absorption, outstanding charge carrier mobility, and longer intrinsic carrier diffusion length.  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   After approximately one decade, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been increased rapidly from the initial 3.8% to 25.5%.  $  ^{[4-12]}  $   PSCs have possibility to surpass the certified 26.7% PCE of monocrystalline silicon solar cells.  $  ^{[12]}  $   However, on the road to commercialization, there are many challenges to be solved, such as sufficient stability to commercial standard and lead leakage to the environment. Thus, it can be utilized to control the film morphology.  $  ^{[50-52]}  $

In this work, we demonstrated an in situ crystal growth control (ICGC) method to engineer the Ostwald ripening using a variety of amounts of the trifluoromethylphenylacetic acid (TFPA) additive and attained larger and more uniform grain size perovskite thin film with lower defects. Different amounts of TFPA were added into 1 mL FAI/MAX (X is Br and Cl) precursor solution and reacted with PbI  $  _{2}  $  -dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) to optimize the film morphologies. The increasing amount of TFPA could tune Ostwald ripening to increase the grain size and the proper amount (1 mg TFPA) leads to the most uniform and best-distributed crystalloid. From the grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) and X-ray diffraction (XRD) results, the optimized perovskite film had slightly better crystallinity. The trap state density had lowered by a factor of 3.18. Due to more outstanding crystallization quality and fewer defects, the PCE of the champion device exceeded 22.19%, with an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.130 V, short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 24.69 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and a fill factor (FF) of 79.55%. Simultaneously, trifluoromethyl group existing in TFPA brought hydrophobicity for the optimized perovskite film, which made PSCs resist high humidity for long-term stability. It still maintained more than 85% of the initial PCE when exposed to 65–75% relative humidity for more than 1500 h. Therefore, we precisely control the process of Ostwald ripening through ICGC to gain efficient and stable PSCs.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The FA-based   $  \left(\mathrm{FA}=\mathrm{CH}\left(\mathrm{NH}_{2}\right)_{2}^{+}\right)  $   perovskite film was produced through the two-step sequential deposition method. The traditional post-treatment was achieved by spin-coating TFPA on perovskite surface and the ICGC process through Ostwald ripening was adding TFPA into the organic precursor. Obtaining perovskite films with more uniform and larger grains is always a significant topic for the preparation of PSCs.  $  ^{[53,54]}  $   We first investigated the effect of TFPA on the perovskite grain size.

We further performed X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) tests on the perovskite film without and with TFPA (Figure 3d,e). For the XPS spectra of pure perovskite film, the peaks located at 143.4 and 138.5 eV were attributed to   $  Pb^{2+}  $  , and the peaks located at 141.6 and 136.7 eV represented metallic   $  Pb^{0}  $   that were verified as nonrecombination centers to deteriorate the photovoltaic performance of PSCs. The   $  Pb^{2+}  $   peaks shifted toward a low energy level in ICGC perovskite film with TFPA (Figure 3d), which was caused by the interaction between carboxyl group of TFPA and Pb. In addition, when carboxyl group partially occupied the iodine (I) vacancy and coordinated with Pb, the peaks of I as electron donor emerged transformation toward low energy level (Figure 3e). These results were consistent with the FTIR results. So we were convinced that the carboxylate ion (  $  COO^{-}  $  ), being coordinated with the lead element, would act as a passivation effect. Generally, due to low binding energy of perovskite, the elements, including halide anions (I, Br, and Cl) and organic cations (MA and FA), will inevitably run away from the perovskite layer during thermal annealing and illumination, which may result in   $  Pb^{2+}  $   losing coordinated anions and cations and being reduced to metallic   $  Pb^{0}  $  . [31,34,37] The carboxyl group in TFPA can passivate noncoordinated   $  Pb^{0}  $   to present the formation of deep level trap, thus improving the performance of the device in the following. [57]

Whether passivation is effective directly depends on the trap states density reduction of perovskite films. Defects would result in the loss of luminescence due to nonradiation recombination, so we used steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra to study the defective property. First of all, we tested PL of perovskite films containing different concentrations of TFPA deposited on the glass, and found that when the concentration of TFPA was appropriate, the PL intensity was significantly increased (Figure 4a). The strongest PL intensity was identified from the film treated with 1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  TFPA, indicating the lowest density of trap state. Simultaneously, we also observed that the PL peaks of the ICGC perovskite films had obviously blue shift compared with the reference sample. According to previous reports, the blue shift of PL peak was attributed to the fewer defect states.  $  ^{[58]}  $  In addition, when there were excess amounts of TFPA loading,

the PL intensity of the corresponding perovskite film decreased. We further conducted TRPL tests to explore the crystalline quality and defects density of the films (Figure 4b). The TRPL curves were double-exponentially fitted using computational formula   $  f(t) = A_{1}\exp(-t/\tau_{1}) + A_{2}\exp(-t/\tau_{2})  $  , where t represented the time,   $  A_{1}  $   and   $  A_{2}  $   were the decay amplitude, decay time   $  \tau_{1}  $   could be described by fluorescence quenching, and decay time   $  \tau_{2}  $   could be attributed to trap-assisted recombination. We noted that the values of   $  A_{1}  $   and   $  A_{2}  $   and   $  \tau_{1}  $   and   $  \tau_{2}  $   of ICGC film were equal, which was a form of single-exponent (Table 1). This meant that the fluorescence quenching of the ICGC film was hardly affected by the defect state. And the value of   $  \tau_{1}  $   of the ICGC of film was much greater than the reference. So we concluded that ICGC process could effectively diminish density of trap state and improve the photogenerated carrier lifetime.

In addition, we also performed the space charge limited current (SCLC) measurements with a structure of ITO/PEDOT: PSS/perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD/Ag (ITO: indium tin oxide; PEDOT:PSS: poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):polystyrenesulfonate; spiro-OMeTAD: 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spirobifluorene) to estimate the trap density of the reference film and the ICGC film. Figure 4c showed that as the applied voltage increased, the devices first showed an obvious Ohmic effect, and then when the voltage increased to the critical point of defect filling, the current rose sharply. The defect density was calculated based on the equation:  $  N_{\text{defects}} = 2\varepsilon \varepsilon_0 V_{\text{TFL}} / eL^2  $ , where  $  \varepsilon  $  was the relative dielectric constant of perovskite,  $  \varepsilon_0  $  was the vacuum permittivity,  $  V_{\text{TFL}}  $  was the trap-filled limit voltage,  $  e  $  was the electron charge, and  $  L  $  was the thickness of the perovskite film.  $  V_{\text{TFL}}  $  acquired from fitting curves was 0.59 and 0.24 V, respectively, belonging to the reference film and the optimal film. Furthermore,  $  N_{\text{defects}}  $  calculated was  $  7.54 \times 10^{15}  $  and  $  2.37 \times 10^{15}  $ , respectively, with reduction by a factor of 3.2. The consequence from SCLC was agreed with PL and TRPL. To clarify the improvement of ICGC technology on photovoltaic devices, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) characterization was conducted to understand the charge transport properties in PSCs. Under a bias voltage of 1.03 V and AM1.5G illumination, the Nyquist plots of cells from conventional method and ICGC technology were obtained, respectively (Figure 4d). # 3. Conclusion

In summary, we introduced an ICGC strategy to control Ostwald ripening and fabricated high-performance and stable perovskite solar cells.
