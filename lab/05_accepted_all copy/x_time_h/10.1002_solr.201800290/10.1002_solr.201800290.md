# Synergy Effect of Both 2,2,2-Trifluoroethylamine Hydrochloride and SnF $ _{2} $  for Highly Stable FASnI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _{x} $  Perovskite Solar Cells

Bin-Bin Yu, Leiming Xu, Min Liao, Yinghui Wu, Fangzhou Liu, Zhenfei He, Jie Ding, Wei Chen, Bao Tu, Yi Lin, Yudong Zhu, Xusheng Zhang, Weitang Yao, Aleksandra B. Djurišić, Jin-Song Hu, and Zhubing He*

The environmentally friendly additive 2,2,2-trifluoroethylamine hydrochloride (TFEACl) is used in synergy with SnF $ _{2} $  to enhance the efficiency and stability of FASnI $ _{3} $ -based solar cells. Both TFEA $ ^{+} $  and Cl $ ^{-} $  are present in the films, but only Cl $ ^{-} $  is incorporated into the crystal lattice of the perovskite. The addition of TFEACl suppresses the segregation of SnF $ _{2} $ , resulting in improvements in film morphology, in addition to a more favorable energy band alignment, and improved suppression of the formation of Sn $ ^{4+} $ . Consequently, reduced charge recombination and improved charge collection result in an efficiency enhancement from 3.63 to 5.30%. The stability of the devices is also significantly enhanced, with devices with TFEACl retaining over 60% of initial PCE after 350 h of light soaking in ambient, while devices without TFEACl experience failure in 120 h under the same testing condition.

Organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved an impressive power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 23.3%,  $  ^{[1]}  $  and consequently they are attracting increasing attention from both academic community and industry. Despite impressive improvements in the efficiency, the toxicity of lead remains a significant concern for the future large scale utilization of PSCs.  $  ^{[2]}  $  Therefore, there have been intensive research efforts to develop lead-free perovskites which would yield high efficiency PSCs.  $  ^{[2]}  $  Among different lead-free perovskites reported to date, the

highest efficiencies obtained were reported for tin-based perovskites.  $  ^{[2]}  $   Tin-based perovskites are the most promising candidates to date for lead replacement due to similar ionic radii of tin and lead.  $  ^{[2]}  $   In addition, tin-based perovskites possess small electron and hole effective masses similar to lead-based perovskites,  $  ^{[3,4]}  $   while they exhibit narrower bandgaps and higher charge carrier mobilities.  $  ^{[5]}  $   However, despite favorable intrinsic material properties, tin-based PSCs commonly exhibit lower efficiency and inferior stability compared to lead-based devices.  $  ^{[2]}  $   This is mainly due to easy oxidation of divalent   $  Sn^{2+}  $   to a more stable   $  Sn^{4+}  $ , which affects the charge neutrality of the perovskite and leads to the degradation, p-type self-doping and/or the formation of impurity phases.  $  ^{[2,5]}  $   In addition, tin-based perovskites rapidly crystallize at room temperature, which results in difficulties in achieving reproducible formation of high quality films with good uniformity, crystallinity, and substrate coverage.  $  ^{[5]}  $

Due to attractive intrinsic material properties of tin-based perovskites, there has been significant interest in the investigation of tin-based perovskites,  $  ^{[3-39]}  $   and different approaches have been adopted to address the problems of preventing tin oxidation and improving film quality for photovoltaic applications. Common approach is to use different additives to suppress the formation of   $  Sn^{4+}  $   and thus reduce the hole density in the films, as well as improve the film uniformity and coverage. The work function shift is expected to result in the improvement of the energy band alignment at perovskite interfaces with both PEDOT:PSS and PCBM (Figure 2f). This would in turn result in improved charge collection, and consequently improved device performance. $ ^{[44-46]} $

In addition to the effect of TFEACl on the device efficiency, we also examined the effect on stability. Figure 6 shows the morphology evolution of the control and the optimal perovskite films exposed to ambient atmosphere at 26 °C with the humidity of 80%. Obvious change in the morphology of the control films is observed even in the early stages (1 and 2 h exposure). After 24 h, the control film is obviously degraded, while in the optimal films the only change is the appearance of some pinholes. The absorption spectra (Figure 6) of the optimal films do not exhibit a significant change within 90 min, while the absorption of the control films is significantly reduced within 90 min. This indicates improved stability of the films with TFEACl upon exposure to humid atmosphere. Based on this encouraging result for film stability, we also evaluated device stability. Figure 7 shows the degradation of the control and optimal devices under light soaking (continuous simulated solar AM 1.5G illumination, resulting in a temperature of 50 °C). attributed to   $  Cl^{-}  $   (increase in grain size, improved crystallinity) and   $  TFEA^{+}  $   (hydrophobicity).

# 3. Conclusion

We demonstrated significant improvement in the performance (higher PCE, lower hysteresis) and stability of   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -based devices using TFEACl additive in addition to commonly used   $  SnF_{2}  $  . The addition of TFEACl results in the inhibition of   $  SnF_{2}  $   segregation, improving the film morphology and device performance. Film crystallinity and grain size are also improved due to the incorporation of   $  Cl^{-}  $  , and the shift in the energy level alignment results in improved charge collection and reduced recombination. Furthermore, the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   is additionally suppressed, and the device stability is improved, likely through the combination of factors such as reduced tin oxidation, slower diffusion of oxygen and moisture into the film due to improved crystallinity, and increased hydrophobicity of the film due to   $  TFEA^{+}  $  incorporation in grain boundaries.
