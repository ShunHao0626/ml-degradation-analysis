# Abstract

Extending photoelectric response to near-infrared region (NIR) has become an urgent subject for the research of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, it is still a challenge due to the shortage of matching NIR photovoltaic materials and device structure. The rapid development of NIR organic photovoltaic materials and devices (OPVs) in recent years offers new opportunity for developing such PSCs. Herein, to broaden the photoresponse of PSCs, a novel PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl bulk-heterojunction (BHJ) organic layer was successfully integrated on the PSCs, which extended the photoresponse of the device to  $ 950~\mathrm{nm} $ . And more, to boost the utilization of NIR light, the Au nanorods (Au NRs) were introduced into the organic BHJ layer through localized surface plasmon effect (LSPR). To further improve the device stability and satisfy solution competition, the  $ \mathrm{MoO_3} $  was fabricated as hole transport layer substituting traditional Spiro-OMeTAD. After adopting such a device, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) was increased significantly from  $ 16.67\% $  to  $ 21.55\% $ , which was the optimum among the reported organic/perovskite hybrid solar cells and the devices employing  $ \mathrm{MoO_3} $  as the hole transport layer. The illumination and humidity stability of the perovskite/organic/noble metal integrated solar cells were also significantly improved. The devices under standard sunlight irradiation for 1000 h maintained more than  $ 70\% $  of initial PCE. This work demonstrates that the perovskite/organic/noble metal integrated structure is a novel and powerful approach to obtain efficient and stable NIR-harvesting PSCs.

Keywords: Perovskite solar cells, Photoresponse, Plasmon, Au nanorods, Stability.

# Introduction

Halide PSCs have attracted extensive attention due to low cost, simple preparation process and superior photovoltaic performance   $  [1-4]  $  . The certified PCE of PSCs has achieved 25.2% to date, which is comparable to that of commercial silicon solar cells   $  [5]  $  . However, current efficient halide perovskite materials only absorb a small fraction of ultraviolet (UV) and visible light (400–800 nm) due to their optical bandgap of approximately 1.60 eV, which only occupy 45–50% of entire solar spectrum (280–2500 nm), and thus restricts the effective light harvesting and PCE improvement of PSCs   $  [6-7]  $  . Therefore, broadening the light absorption and photovoltaic response of PSCs to the NIR region become an efficient method to further improve the PCE of PSCs, which is possible to further approach the Shockley-Queisser limit   $  [8]  $  . To utilize the NIR of solar spectrum, Sn-based perovskite materials with low band gap have been used as light harvesting active layer for solar cell applications   $  [9]  $  . Although the light response up to 1060 nm can be achieved by applying a mixed Sn-based perovskite materials, the device response was restricted by poor Sn (II) stability and lower open circuit voltage (Voc)   $  [10]  $  . In addition to reduce the band gap of perovskite absorber layer, the use of tandem device structure with other thin film with NIR absorption, such as crystalline silicon and copper indium gallium selenide, can also broaden the photoresponse of device   $  [11-15]  $  . However, fabrication process of tandem device is highly complicated which increases significantly the cost and is not suitable for large scale application   $  [16]  $  . Therefore, it is crucial to find a simpler and more feasible strategy to effectively broaden the NIR spectral absorption of device for the development of PSCs.

In recent few years, the research on OPVs has achieved significant progress, and the OPVs have obtained high PCE or high Voc   $  [17-24]  $  . And some novel narrow-band organic photovoltaic acceptor materials, such as BTP-4Cl are fabricated by Hou et al., which demonstrates excellent NIR absorption until to 1100 nm and high electron mobility   $  [25]  $  . This type of narrow-band organic materials have been applied to the OPVs devices by forming BHJ with PBDB-TF polymer, and have achieved record PCE of 16.5% for single-junction OPVs   $  [25]  $  . It is expected that this kind of narrow-band organic acceptor materials be integrated with PSCs, which may broaden NIR spectral response of PSCs and further improve the PCE of the PSCs to a brand level. Because the charge carrier diffusion length for the organic materials is usually

about 20 nm, which is much thinner than the thickness of the PSC layer, therefore, the direct integration of the organic acceptor with PSCs layer should be detrimental to the collection of charge carries. Herein, on solving this problem, the PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl BHJ was first introduced to the PSCs as a whole. It should be highlighted that up to now, only a few work have attempted to fabricate integrated solar cell of organic BHJ and perovskite, but the device performances are much lower than those of typical PSCs due to the energy level mismatching of the organic with PSCs and the insufficient harvesting of NIR light   $  [26]  $  .

Differing from the previous literature, we further introduced the Au NRs layer to boost the harvesting of NIR as well as visible light, which could efficiently and extraordinarily scatter and reflect NIR light through LSPR effect. In addition, the common hole transport layer of Spiro-OMeTAD in PSCs is expensive and has poor stability upon exposure to the environment and relatively low hole mobility, which causes the loss of Voc and the instability of perovskites   $  [27]  $  . Metal oxides will be a better replacement to overcome these shortages. Inorganic hole transport material of   $  MoO_{3}  $   is one of the potential materials as a replacement of the organic Spiro-OMeTAD materials, due to its higher stability and lower cost   $  [28]  $  . Therefore, after integrating the PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl BHJ/Au NRs layer into PSCs, we further explored   $  MoO_{3}  $   as an alternative hole transport layer. After gathering the above material and structure advantages, we finally obtained the novel, spectral extended, efficient and environment-stable perovskite/organic/Au NRs hybrid PSCs. The photoelectric response of the hybrid PSCs was extended to 950 nm, while the PCE was highly increased from 16.67% to 21.55%. It should be highlighted that 21.55% is the highest PCE among the various reported perovskite/organic hybrid solar cells and also the PSCs based on   $  MoO_{3}  $   hole transport layer   $  [29, 30]  $  .

# Results and Discussion

Figure 1a displays the schematic preparation process of the integrated perovskite/BHJ film for efficient NIR-light harvesting. The fabrication process was optimized and included mainly five steps. Firstly, the   $  SnO_{2}  $   electron transport layer was synthesized by spin-coating on FTO substrate and then annealed at   $  150\ ^{\circ}C  $   for 30 min. Secondly, the perovskite solution was spinned coating on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer. Thirdly, PBDB-TF was introduced into chlorobenzene anti-solvent and slowly dropped into the perovskite film at appropriate time to assist growth of the perovskite film   $  [29]  $  .

Then the perovskite film was annealed at 150 °C for 15 min to promote the crystallization. Fourthly, the BHJ layer of PBDB-TF and BTP-4Cl with Au NRs were directly spinned coating on the perovskite film. Finally, the PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl BHJ layer was annealed at 100 °C for 5 min to synthesize compacted film. The device structure is displayed in Figure 1b, which consists of FTO anode, SnO₂ electron transport layer, perovskite absorption layer, BHJ/Au NRs NIR light harvesting layer, MoO₃ hole transport layer and Au cathode. For this device, the Cs₀.₀₅FA₀.₈₃MA₀.₁₇PbI₀.₈₃Br₀.₁₇ perovskite film absorbs high energy photons (300-800 nm, PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl BHJ film absorbs low energy photons (800-950 nm) and high energy photons that have not been completely absorbed by the perovskite. The hybrid PSCs can obtain current from both perovskite and BHJ layers. The inset in Figure 1b displays the molecular structures of PBDB-TF and BTP-4Cl. PBDB-TF consists of S, F, and O atoms with lone pairs of electrons which can effectively passivate the trap states in the perovskite film by Pb²⁺ and improve charge transport and collection [29]. The charge generation and transport mechanism of the hybrid PSCs is displayed in Figure 1c, which is designed for the following reasons: (1) the perovskite material is a direct bandgap semiconductor and the exciton is easily separated because of weak exciton binding energy [31]; (2) The perovskite film has ambipolar transporting characteristics and high mobility, showing minimal charge recombination probability [32]; (3) The perovskite and BHJ film can be both prepared by orthogonal solvents, allowing the BHJ be well deposited on the perovskite film [33]; (4) The PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl layer easily extracts holes from the perovskite film due to the suitable energy level matching and then transport holes to the Au electrode by MoO₃ film, which can block transport of electrons to the Au electrode; (5) The lower-energy photons transmitted by the perovskite film can be absorbed by PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl film to generate electron–hole pairs. The generated electrons are easily transported by BTP-4Cl to the perovskite layer and finally collected by FTO electrode.

Besides the light harvesting, the PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl film also accelerate charge carrier generation and transport function for the hybrid PSCs, thus, the mobility of perovskite and PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl films is critical for the charge extraction and transport. It has been reported that perovskite films have high charge mobility and long electron and hole diffuse length [37], so mobility of PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl layer has a significant influence on the performance of the hybrid PSCs. To further obtain the electron and hole mobility of BHJ layer, PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl film were characterized by space charge limited current method. The cyclic voltammetry was performed to measure the HOMO and LUMO energy levels of BTP-4Cl, as shown in Figure S15. The HOMO and LUMO energy levels of BTP-4Cl are deduced to be at -5.68 eV and -4.12 eV, respectively. Therefore, the electrochemical band gap (EgEC) of BTP-4Cl are calculated to be about 1.56 eV. Because the absorption onset for BTP-4Cl is located at 950 nm, the optical band gap (Egopt) is calculated to 1.31 eV. However, the EgEC is substantially larger than the Egopt, owing to that Egopt does not take into account the exciton binding energy [40, 41]. # Conclusions

In conclusion, we presented a unique photovoltaic response broadening and boosting strategy for NIR-harvesting PSCs, which was mainly based on the integration of a novel PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl BHJ organic layer. Several new contributions in this work should be highlighted. Firstly, the photo-response of the hybrid PSCs was successfully extended to over  $ 950~\mathrm{nm} $  due to the additional light harvesting of low bandgap organic PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl BHJ film. In addition, it also enhanced the charge extraction ability due to the exactly matching band gap with the

Journal Pre-proof

perovskite layer. Secondly, the Au NRs with corresponding LSPR in NIR region were introduced into the BHJ layer, considerably boosting the NIR light harvesting of BHJ. After loading Au NRs in the BHJ film, the IPCE of the PSCs in the NIR surpassed  $ 50\% $  and the integrated Jsc values obtained from the IPCE ranging of 800-950 nm reached  $ 3\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ . Thirdly, the  $ \mathrm{MoO_3} $  was fabricated as hole transport layer substituting traditional Spiro-OMeTAD, which improved the device stability and satisfy solution competition. After adopting such a device, the PCE was increased significantly from  $ 16.67\% $  to  $ 21.55\% $ , which is the optimum among the reported organic/perovskite hybrid solar cells and the devices employing  $ \mathrm{MoO_3} $  as the hole transport layer. The illumination and humidity stability of the perovskite/organic/noble metal integrated solar cells were also significantly improved. This work demonstrates that the perovskite/organic/noble metal integrated structure is a novel and powerful approach to obtain efficient and stable NIR-harvesting PSCs.

# Highlights

- A novel PBDB-TF: BTP-4Cl bulk-heterojunction organic layer was firstly successfully integrated on the PSCs, which extended the photoresponse of the device to  $ 950~\mathrm{nm} $ , and the integrated Jsc values reached  $ 3\mathrm{mA/cm}^2 $ , which is the most efficient hybrid device with NIR response reported up to now.

- We further introduced the Au NR layer to boost the harvesting of NIR as well as visible light, which could efficiently and extraordinarily scatter and reflect NIR light through localized surface plasmon resonance effect.

- We finally obtained the novel, spectral extended, efficient and environment-stable perovskite/organic/Au NRs hybrid PSCs.

- The PCE was increased significantly from  $ 16.67\% $  to  $ 21.55\% $ , which was the optimum among the reported organic/perovskite hybrid solar cells and the devices employing  $ \mathrm{MoO}_3 $  as the hole transport layer.

- More importantly, the illumination and humidity stability of the perovskite/organic/noble metal integrated solar cells were also significantly improved. The devices under standard sunlight irradiation for  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  maintained more than  $ 70\% $  of initial PCE.
