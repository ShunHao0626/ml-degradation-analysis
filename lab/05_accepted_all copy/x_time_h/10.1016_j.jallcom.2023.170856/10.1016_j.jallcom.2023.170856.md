# 1. Introduction

Recently,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  , as a kind of cesium-based all-inorganic perovskite materials, has drawn considerable attention owing to its fantastic thermal stability and suitable bandgap in tandem solar cells. The solar cell based on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   has been reported efficiency of 17.5% so far, demonstrating great potential commercial application [1-4]. Fabricating high-efficiency   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite solar cell (PSC), a reliable way is to deposit high-quality perovskite film, that is, dense, uniform, and stable perovskite film. Various methods have been tried to deposit high-quality perovskite films, such as solvent engineering [5], compositional engineering [6,7], interface engineering [8,9], additive engineering [10-14], etc. Among them, additive engineering is widely used as a simple and effective method. Based on this method, various kinds of additives have been incorporated into the corresponding perovskite precursor to control the grain crystallization process, so as to fabricate high-quality perovskite films.

# ABSTRACT

Cesium-based all-inorganic perovskite material   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   has attracted much attention due to its excellent thermal stability and suitable bandgap in tandem solar cells. However, the poor performance of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based perovskite solar cell (PSC) hinders its development towards commercial application. In this study, excess CsBr is incorporated into   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursors for high quality perovskite films. Meanwhile, an ultrathin   $  MgF_{2}  $   layer is introduced as the buffer layer to improve the thermal and humidity stability of photovoltaic device. With the optimal thickness of   $  MgF_{2}  $   (1 nm) and 3 % excess CsBr, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based PSC reaches 15.6 %, which has a great improvement than that of PSC without buffer layer (13.1 %). More importantly, the extremely stable   $  MgF_{2}  $   buffer layer can effectively isolate the perovskite light absorber and the metal electrode, even under the stress of high temperature, thus leading to excellent long-term stability of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs.

Motivated by the above works, we incorporated CsBr, one of the components for CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite, into CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite precursor and evaluated their effects on CsPbI $ _2 $ Br thin films. The results demonstrate that smooth and uniform perovskite films can be obtained when CsBr is in excess, thus leading to high-efficiency CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs.

In the meantime, in an inverted planar PSC, a buffer layer is usually inserted between the electron transport layer (ETL) and the metal electrode, and the most commonly used buffer layer material is BCP [17,18]. Our previous studies have confirmed that a 5-nm-thickness of BCP layer could make a fine electronic contact, with no charge accumulation emerging at the interface between ETL and the metal electrode, thus leading to photovoltaic devices with a passable PCE [19]. However, the BCP buffer layer, as an organic material, is unstable at high temperature. Therefore, researchers try to introduce a stable inorganic material to replace BCP. For example, in June 2014, Seok team fabricated PSC with inorganic material LiF as a buffer layer based on the inverted planar structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/MAPbI $ _3 $ /PCBM/LiF (0.5 nm)/Ag, and achieved a PCE of 14.1 %, which was the highest PCE of the MAPbI $ _3 $ /PCBM based solar cells at that time [20]. In February 2018, Chen Wei's team used N-type inorganic oxide CeOx insteading of BCP as the buffer layer (it can also be used as an ETL alone), and achieved a high PCE of 18.7 % as well as enhanced long-term stability [21]. # 2.2. Characterization of the device

The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images were collected at 5 kV with the field emission SEM (Hitachi, S-4800). The X-ray diffraction (XRD) characterization was performed on the Bruker AXS D8 Advance (Germany) with Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda = 0.154  $   nm). The UV-Vis spectra were measured on a Lambda 950 spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer Co., USA). The current density-voltage (J-V) curve was measured with a Keithley 2400 source meter with scan rate of 0.1 V S  $  ^{-1}  $  , and the scan voltage is in the range of -0.2 V to 1.4 V. The corresponding simulated sunlight is provided by a solar simulator with a 450 W xenon lamp equipped with a UV filter, and the light intensity is limited to 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $   by a standard c-Si solar cell. The capacitance-voltage (C-V) characteristic curves under dark state or light (60 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) conditions were obtained using electrochemical workstation (Zahner Zennium), the test frequency was set to 1 KHz, and the bias voltage range was -0.2 V to 1.4 V.

# 3.2. Optoelectronic performance of the device

Based on above   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   absorbers with excess 3 % CsBr, photovoltaic devices with the structure of "FTO/NiOx/CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br/PCBM/MgF  $  _{2}  $  (x nm)/Ag" were prepared (Fig. 1a). The light current density-voltage (J-V) curves of devices with different   $  MgF_{2}  $   thicknesses are shown in Fig. 5a, and the specific performance parameters are listed in Table 1. It can be seen that when the thickness of   $  MgF_{2}  $   is 1 nm, the device performance is optimal and little hysteresis emerges under forward and reverse scans (Fig. 5b). At this time, the champion PCE reaches 15.6 %, with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.27 V, a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 15.5 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and a FF of 0.79. Compared with the solar cell without buffer layer (that is, the thickness of   $  MgF_{2}  $   is 0), the performance parameters of 1-nm-  $  MgF_{2}  $   device have been all improved to a certain extent. When the thickness of   $  MgF_{2}  $   is further increased from 1 nm to 4 nm, the device performance decays rapidly, causing the PCE to almost drop to 0. The reason should be mainly attributed to the fact that the   $  MgF_{2}  $   thickness is too large, which makes the photo-generated carriers unable to tunnel, resulting in the current-density drops sharply, which is obviously different from BCP buffer layer. BCP as a buffer layer has greater tolerance to thickness just as our previous study [19].

In addition, we also give the statistical distribution of photovoltaic device performance (each   $  MgF_{2}  $   thickness has 8 cells). The results are shown in Figs. 5c-5f. The statistical distribution diagram can also clearly indicate that the PCE increases first and then decreases sharply with the increase of   $  MgF_{2}  $   thickness and achieves the best value of 15.6 % when   $  MgF_{2}  $   thickness is 1 nm. A similar trend is demonstrated for   $  V_{OC}  $   and FF, as shown in Fig. 5c and e. The results demonstrate that the performance of photovoltaic devices is very sensitive to the   $  MgF_{2}  $   thickness, which is consistent with the optimal device performance trend shown in Fig. 5a, and also confirms the reliability of the experimental results.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, we have demonstrated an effective method to fabricate high performance inorganic PSCs. The dense and uniform  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  perovskite films can be obtained by the incorporation of excess CsBr ( $ \sim 3\% $ ) into  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  precursors, and a champion PCE of  $ 15.6\% $  is achieved with the insertion of an ultra thin ( $ \sim 1\mathrm{nm} $ )  $ \mathrm{MgF}_2 $  buffer layer. Notably, the various performance parameters of the device, including  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}, J_{SC}, FF} $ , all have been improved, which leads to a significant enhancement of PCE. The internal reason should be the introduction of wide-bandgap  $ \mathrm{MgF}_2 $  material, which causes the PCBM/Ag interface of the device to form an ohmic contact, thereby eliminating the accumulation of charge at the interface, reducing the energy loss inside the device, and improving the photoelectric conversion of the photovoltaic device effectively. Furthermore, due to the excellent stability of the  $ \mathrm{MgF}_2 $  material itself, after being used as a buffer layer, it can better isolate the ETL and the silver electrode, which makes the perovskite absorber well encapsulated and protected, thus leading to enhanced long-term stability of the device.
