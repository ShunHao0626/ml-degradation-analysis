Two-dimensional (2D) perovskites, which have excellent stability compared with three-dimensional (3D) perovskites owing to the effective protection of the hydrophobic organic ligands, have become a research hotspot and have made great developmental progress in recent years. Herein, an n-butylammonium iodide (BAI)-post-treatment process was developed to fabricate 2D-3D hybrid perovskite with a thin layer of 2D perovskite covered on the surface of 3D  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite. The growth process of 2D perovskite is formed through the chemical reaction between BAI and the residual  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , which improves stability and reduces the number of crystal defects of 3D perovskite by optimizing stoichiometry. Compared with the 3D counterpart, the 2D-3D hybrid perovskite shows outstanding light and air stability when exposed to external environments. Moreover, structure conversion from 3D to 2D-3D can induce the passivation of defects in the 3D films. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the 2D-3D solar cell exceeds  $ 18\% $  and retains  $ 80\% $  of the initial value after more than  $ 2000\mathrm{h} $  of storage without encapsulation.

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have developed rapidly in the last ten years due to their excellent photoelectron properties, such as a suitable optical band edge, weak exciton binding energy, and high absorption coefficients   $  [1-4]  $  . The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has increased rapidly from 3.8% to over 23%   $  [5-11]  $  . Although high efficiency has been achieved, the long-term stability of PSCs is the most challenging issue for commercial applications in the future   $  [12, 13]  $  .

Three-dimensional (3D) perovskite materials, especially the materials based on methylammonium (MA) cations, are sensitive to oxygen, moisture, and ultraviolet light, among other things. The instabilities may arise from crystal structure conversion, chemical decomposition, or halide segregation demixing   $  [14–16]  $  . Therefore, long-term exposure of PSCs to these external conditions easily leads to rapid degradation of perovskite components, which reduces the materials' light-absorbing ability and performance. Through composition engineering, which uses cation intermixing including methylammonium (MA), formamidinium (FA), cesium (Cs), or rubidium (Rb), the thermal or structural instabilities can be controlled to a certain extent   $  [17–22]  $  . Interface modification is another method to improve the stability of the PSCs. For example, the functionalization of perovskite films with hydrophobic organic molecules or moisture-tolerant inorganic materials can improve moisture resistance for PSCs   $  [23–27]  $  . Recently, two-dimensional (2D) perovskites have attracted much attention due to their large, hydrophobic organic cations blocking moisture from infiltrating   $  [28, 29]  $  . However, pure 2D perovskite solar cells have shown rather low PCE due to their narrower absorption range, low carrier mobility, and short carrier diffusion length   $  [30–33]  $  . Therefore, 2D-3D hybrid perovskite has received more and more attention, in which a 2D perovskite layer acts as a protective coating against moisture for preventing the 3D perovskite from degrading   $  [34–39]  $  .

In this work, a method is reported for fabricating a 2D-3D perovskite by post-treatment with n-butylamine iodide (BAI) on a one-step deposited MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  film. By adding an ultra-thin layer of 2D perovskite via interface modification at the grain boundaries and on the surface of pristine 3D perovskite, the obtained PSCs maintained the high performance of the 3D perovskite films and the stability of the 2D perovskite layer. The novel device fabricated using this approach gives a PCE of 18.3%, with an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.09 V, a short-circuit current (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 22.55 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  and a fill factor (FF) of 0.74. After exposure to the air with a humidity of 30–40% for three months without any encapsulation, the device can still maintain 80% of its initial efficiency.

# 2.2. Fabrication of Perovskite Solar Cells

Fluorine-doped tin-oxide-coated (FTO) glass substrates of  $ 1.5\mathrm{cm}\times 1.5\mathrm{cm} $  were patterned by laser etching. The FTO substrates were washed by ultrasonic cleaning and rinsing in a detergent aqueous solution, deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol; dried by clean air flow; and finally treated via oxygen plasma for 6 min. The diluted  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  colloidal solution (by five times deionized water) was spin-coated onto the cleaned FTO substrates at  $ 4000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 30~\mathrm{s} $ , then annealed in ambient air at  $ 170^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $ . After being cooled down to the room temperature (RT), the substrates were treated by oxygen plasma for 6 min again for further deposition.

The  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite solution was prepared by dissolving  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  (1.2 M) and MAI (1.2 M) into mixed solvents of DMF:DMSO ( $ V:V = 7:3 $ ). The precursor solution was stirred for  $ 2\mathrm{h} $  prior to use. The solution was spin-coated on  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2/\mathrm{FTO} $  substrates at  $ 3500~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 30~\mathrm{s} $  in a nitrogen-filled glove box. During the spin-coating,  $ 0.2~\mathrm{mL} $  of chlorobenzene (CB) was dropped onto the substrate after  $ 10~\mathrm{s} $ . After drying at RT for  $ 10~\mathrm{min} $ , the film was annealed at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 60~\mathrm{min} $ . To prepare the 2D-3D perovskite

# 3. Results and discussion

The structure of 2D-3D perovskite solar cells, composed of FTO/SnO $ _2 $ /3D perovskite/2D perovskite/Spiro-OMETAD/Au, is shown in figure 1(a), in which a 2D perovskite layer is formed on the surface of 3D perovskite. The fabrication procedure of the 2D-3D perovskite is described in detail in figure 1(b). MAPbI $ _3 $  precursor solution was dropped on the SnO $ _2 $ -coated FTO substrate, and the substrate was spun at fixed rotation speed. CB, as anti-solvent, was added during spin-coating. Then, 2D-3D perovskite was fabricated by spin-coating BAI solutions of differing concentrations on MAPbI $ _3 $  film. The color of the perovskite film changed from brown to cyan when low BAI concentrations (0.5–2 mg/mL) were spin-coated (figure S1). The color changed into a dark brown with BAI concentrations of more than 5 mg/mL, and the color remained unchanged after annealing.

XRD characterization (figure S2) was used to analyze the structures of the perovskite films treated with different BAI concentrations, and the patterns match well with the reported results of 3D perovskites [40]. The peaks around  $ 14.9^{\circ} $ ,  $ 28.8^{\circ} $ , and  $ 31.9^{\circ} $  were indexed with the (110), (220), and (310) planes of perovskite, while the small peak at  $ 12.8^{\circ} $  was related to unreacted  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . The presence of a small amount of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  may passivate the defect states and improve the performance of the perovskite [41, 42]. Contact angle (CA) measurements were used as a tool to demonstrate the hydrophilicity of the materials. Figure S4 shows CAs of glycerol and tricresyl phosphate on the 3D, 2D-3D, and 2D perovskite films. According to the principle of minimum energy, if the surface energy of the solid is less than that of the liquid, the solid surface adsorption is weakened, and the liquid will shrink and reduce the surface energy of the system. The greater the difference becomes between the solid and liquid surface energy, the worse the wettability of the liquid. The significant reduction in surface energy means that 2D perovskite can hinder the contact between 3D perovskite and water, represented as the enhanced hydrophobicity of the 3D perovskite film surface.

The peak intensity of the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  phase, as indicated by the asterisks, clearly increased in the 3D perovskite film sample during the 33 days of storage, while the 2D-3D perovskite film exhibited a weak  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  peak after the same storage time. # 4. Conclusion

In summary, BAI interface modification, in which a thin layer of 2D perovskite was covered on the surface of 3D perovskite through the chemical reaction between BAI and residual  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , was successfully used to fabricate 2D-3D hybrid perovskite. The transition from a 3D to a 2D-3D structure was shown to both reduce crystal defects by compensating for the presence of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and improve light and air stability significantly without sacrificing efficiency. The 2D-3D perovskite solar cells retained  $ 80\% $  of their initial PCE after more than  $ 2000\mathrm{h} $  of storage. Our work provides insight for improving perovskite solar cell performance and stability.
