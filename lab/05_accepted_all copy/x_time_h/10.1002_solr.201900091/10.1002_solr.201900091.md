# 1. Introduction

Hybrid organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PeSCs) have attracted great interest over the past years due to their high power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) exceeding 20%.  $  ^{[1-4]}  $   However, their low stability under sun illumination and thermal aging conditions hamper their further application.  $  ^{[5,6]}  $   As an alternative, cesium-based all-inorganic perovskites show much better light and thermal stabilities, providing a possible path to stable PeSCs.  $  ^{[7-13]}  $   Recently, a cesium lead mixed-halide perovskite,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  , has been successfully applied in photovoltaic devices due of its better structural stability in the cubic phase than   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   counterparts and the reasonable broad bandgap of

studies, PC $ _{61} $ BM has nice thermal and chemical stabilities, which allow its use under standard thermal or sun illuminating test. $ ^{[20,29]} $  Nevertheless, the inherent drawbacks of PC $ _{61} $ BM have been reported as follows: 1) PC $ _{61} $ BM cannot efficiently block the hole transport due to its shallow highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) with an energy level of  $ \approx $  -6.0 eV. $ ^{[30-32]} $  2) PC $ _{61} $ BM as a weak Lewis acid has an insufficient passivation effect. $ ^{[33,34]} $  3) PC $ _{61} $ BM alone cannot efficiently block the iodine ions diffusion toward the cathode (i.e., Ag), $ ^{[35]} $  resulting in fast cathode corrosion. In recent works, various   $  Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   films, either amorphous or crystalline (a-  $  Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   or c-  $  Nb_{2}O_{5}  $  ), have been thoroughly studied as ETL to fabricate n-i-p-type PeSC,  $  ^{[36-38]}  $   achieving high PCEs over 19%, when using inorganic/organic hybrid perovskite (i.e.,   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  ,   $  MA_{2/6}FA_{4/6}Pb(Br_{1/6}I_{5/6})_{3}  $  ) as an absorber layer. From this point of view, solution-processing at room temperature is highly desired.

Very recently, the layered   $  Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   crystal has been prepared and introduced to catalysis,  $  ^{[43]}  $   but not yet to photovoltaics. In this work, 2D c-  $  Nb_{2}O_{5}(001)  $   NS with a uniform thickness of   $  \approx1.5  $   nm and lateral size over 100 nm is prepared through a physical exfoliation of the layered   $  Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   crystal. These solution-processable c-  $  Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   NS is introduced as ETL for inverted   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PeSCs in combination with   $  PC_{61}BM  $ . Importantly, the devices with the bilayer c-  $  Nb_{2}O_{5}/PC_{61}BM  $   ETL exhibit better photovoltaic performance and higher (or comparable) thermal stability compared with those with only   $  PC_{61}BM  $   or   $  ZnO/C_{60}  $   ETL. The introduced c-  $  Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   NS is found to efficiently facilitate the electron transport and block hole transport at the perovskite/ETL interface, reduce the defect density, and suppress the corrosion of the Ag electrode by retarding iodine ions diffusion.

The surface composition of   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}(001)  $   NS was probed using X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) (see Figure S4, Supporting Information). To investigate the mechanism behind the improved device performance upon insertion of c-Nb $ _2 $ O $ _5 $ , more studies have been performed. Figure S12, Supporting Information, shows the UV–vis absorption spectra of the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film with different ETLs. It is clearly seen that the absorption of the Perovskite (PVK) layer is slightly enhanced in the spectral range of 330–500 nm upon the introduction of the c-Nb $ _2 $ O $ _5 $  layer. As the c-Nb $ _2 $ O $ _5 $  layer is highly transparent (Figure S13, Supporting

Information), such an absorption enhancement might be attributed to the scattering effect of the c-Nb $ _2 $ O $ _5 $  layer, which contributes to the increase in  $ J_{\text{SC}} $  as suggested by aforementioned  $ J-V $  tests. Figure 4a,b shows steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) spectra, respectively. It is clearly seen that the pristine CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film displayed strong PL at 664 nm, fully consistent with the previous report. $ ^{[46]} $  When ETL was covered on the perovskite film, the PL was significantly quenched. Particularly, the bilayer ETL of c-Nb $ _2 $ O $ _5 $ /PC $ _{61} $ BM produced more significant quenching effect than the monolayer ETL of c-Nb $ _2 $ O $ _5 $  or PC $ _{61} $ BM. The average PL decay time ( $ \tau_{\text{AVE}} $ ) was calculated by fitting the time-resolved PL with a single-exponential function. The pristine CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film exhibited a long  $ \tau_{\text{AVE}} $  up to 5.56 ns. Covering the PVK film with the PC $ _{61} $ BM monolayer significantly reduced  $ \tau_{\text{AVE}} $  to 1.12 ns. In comparison, covering with the c-Nb $ _2 $ O $ _5 $ /PC $ _{61} $ BM bilayer further reduced  $ \tau_{\text{AVE}} $  to 0.80 ns. All these results suggest that the c-Nb $ _2 $ O $ _5 $ /PC $ _{61} $ BM bilayer can more efficiently extract the photo-generated electron from the PVK layer than the PC $ _{61} $ BM monolayer.

Furthermore, the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) model was used to evaluate the passivation effect of the   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   layer, for which capacitor-like devices with the configuration of ITO/  $  PC_{61}BM/CsPbI_{2}Br/ETL/Ag  $   were fabricated. Their dark J-V curves are shown in Figure 4d and feature a sharp rise at the trap-filled limit (TFL) voltage, where all the trap defects are filled by electrons.  $  ^{[47,48]}  $   The trap density (  $  N_{trap}  $  ) is calculated using the equation   $  N_{trap}=2\varepsilon_{0}V_{TFL}/ed^{2}  $  , where   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   and   $  \varepsilon  $   are the vacuum permittivity and the dielectric constant of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, respectively,  $  ^{[49]}  $   d is the thickness of the prepared   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, and e is the elementary charge. The value of   $  N_{trap}  $   is calculated to be   $  3.64\times10^{15}  $   and   $  1.94\times10^{16}cm^{-3}  $   for the devices with and without the   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   layer, respectively. Thus, the use of the   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}/PC_{61}BM  $   bilayer ETL obviously reduces the defect density of the PVK film, highlighting the role of the   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   layer in passivating the traps or defects of the perovskite film. The passivation effect of   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   NS might be attributed to the presence of abundant Brønsted acid sites (i.e., surface OH groups) and Lewis acid sites (i.e.,   $  NbO_{4}  $   tetrahedra) on the (001) surface of   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   NS,  $  ^{[43,50]}  $   which can protonate or accept an electron from the typical defects of   $  PbX_{3}^{-}  $  or uncoordinated X⁻ (X=halide), and thus passivate these deep traps.  $  ^{[33,51]}  $

In addition, the vertical electron mobility (  $  \mu_{e}  $  ) in the devices with or without c-Nb  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $   was determined using the Mott–Gurney equation:   $  J_{\mathrm{SCLC}} = 9\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{r}\mu_{e}(V - V_{\mathrm{BI}})^{2}/(8L^{3})  $  . [52] The definitions for   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $  ,   $  \varepsilon_{r}  $  , V, V  $  _{BI}  $  , and L, and the calculation details

for   $  \mu_{e}  $   are described in the SI. Accordingly, the   $  \mu_{e}  $   is calculated to be   $  4.97 \times 10^{-4} cm^{2} V^{-1} s^{-1}  $   for the device with c-  $  Nb_{2}O_{5}  $  , almost one-order higher than that (  $  4.20 \times 10^{-5} cm^{2} V^{-1} s^{-1}  $  ) of the device without c-  $  Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   (see Figure S14, Supporting Information). Thus, the reduced defect density combining with the higher electron mobility in the devices with c-  $  Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   may well account for their increased   $  J_{SC}  $   and FF, as suggested by J-V tests.

The long-term thermal stability of the inverted   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PeSCs with different ETLs was monitored under a standard heat condition of   $  85^{\circ}C  $   in the   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glove box. Figure 5a shows their normalized PCE over the thermal aging time. Obviously, the lifetime performance of the devices with the   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}/PC_{61}BM  $   bilayer ETL is significantly better than that seen for the devices with the   $  PC_{61}BM  $   monolayer. The devices with   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   retained 80% of their original PCE after 400 h of heating at   $  85^{\circ}C  $ , which is very comparable with that reported for the inverted devices with inorganic   $  ZnO/C_{60}ETL  $  . In contrast, the devices without   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   suffered almost 50% PCE loss after the same aging test. It seems that the   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   layer plays an important role in enhancing the thermal stability of inverted   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PeSCs.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we have successfully prepared 2D c-  $  Nb_{2}O_{5}(001)  $  NS with a uniform thickness of  $  \approx1.5  $  nm and lateral size over 100 nm through a simple physical exfoliating process. Importantly, the as-prepared c-  $  Nb_{2}O_{5}(001)  $  NS are solution-processable, which allows their application as ETL for inverted  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  PeSCs. The PeSCs with the c-  $  Nb_{2}O_{5}/PC_{61}BM  $  bilayer ETL achieved a champion PCE of 11.74% with an average SPO of 10.22% for 1000 s, which is higher than those obtained from the devices with only  $  PC_{61}BM  $  (9.14% and 7.38%) and the state-of-the-art  $  ZnO/C_{60}  $  ETL (10.65%) prepared under similar

conditions. More importantly, the devices with   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   exhibited a high thermal stability with only 20% PCE loss after 400 h thermal aging at 85 °C, even comparable with that with   $  ZnO/C_{60}  $   ETL. The impressive device performance has been attributed to the introduction of the   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}(001)  $   layer with favorable band levels, strong acidic nature, and small lattice fringe spacing along the surface (001) plane, which not only facilitate the electron transport, block the hole transport but also efficiently enhance the defect passivation and retard the iodine ions diffusion toward the Ag electrode. This work thus provides not only a new solution-processable ETL material but also a deeper insight to design ETL for inverted inorganic PeSCs.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: Lead iodide (99.98%) was purchased from TCI; cesium bromide (99.99%), 1,2-dichlorobenzene (99.9%), dimethyl sulfoxide (anhydrous, ≥99.8%), and dimethylformamide (DMF) were purchased from Alfa Aesar. Ni(OCOCH₃)₂·4H₂O (98%) and diethanol amine (99%) were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical. The ZnO nanoparticle solution (2.5 wt% in isopropanol), C₆₀, PC₆₁BM and Bphen were from Avantam, Solarmer Materials Inc., and Luminescence Tec. Co., respectively. All chemicals were applied to the experiments without further purification.

Preparation of   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   NS:   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   was prepared using a previously reported hydrothermal method.  $  ^{[44,54]}  $   Typically, 3 mmol ammonium niobium oxalate   $  [NH_{4}(NbOC_{2}O_{4})_{2}(H_{2}O)_{2}\cdot nH_{2}O]  $   was dissolved in 40 mL of deionized water and then sealed in a 60 mL Teflon-lined stainless-steel autoclave. A hydrothermal reaction was carried out at 175 °C for 3 days. The obtained solid was filtered, washed thoroughly with deionized water, and dried at 80 °C overnight. After calcination of the dry powder at 673 K for 4 h,   $  c-Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   was obtained with the yield of 95%. Then, 530 mg   $  Nb_{2}O_{5}  $   with 25 mL ethanol was milled for 24 h using a ball-milling machine with the rotation speed of 400 rpm. The obtained sample was dispersed in ethanol with a concentration 2 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   and used after filtration.

Preparation of PeSCs: The ITO-coated glass (  $  \approx15\Omega sq^{-1}  $  , Huanan Xiangcheng Tec. Cor.) was cleaned using the previously reported method and further treated under UVO for 15 min. The   $  NiO_{x}  $   HTL was prepared using the previously reported method.  $  ^{[16]}  $   Briefly, an EtOH solution of   $  \mathrm{Ni(OCOCH_{3})_{2}\cdot4H_{2}O}  $   (0.1 mol mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) containing 0.6 vol% diethanol amine was spin-coated on an ITO substrate. The as-prepared layer was heated at 325 °C for 1 h to obtain   $  NiO_{x}  $   HTL.

To prepare the perovskite precursor solution, a 1.1 M solution of CsBr and   $  PbI_{2}  $   in a mixture of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO):DMF (9/1; v/v) was stirred at 60 °C overnight. The precursor solution (80 μL) was dropped onto the   $  NiO_{x}  $   substrate and spin-coated via a two-step process: 800 rpm for 6 s
