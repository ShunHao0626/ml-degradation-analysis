# A molecular engineering of the hole-transporting material spiro-OMeTAD via manipulation of alkyl groups

D. Tomkute-Luksiene  $  ^{a}  $  , M. Daskeviciene  $  ^{a}  $  , T. Malinauskas  $  ^{a}  $  , V. Jankauskas  $  ^{b}  $  , R. Degutyte  $  ^{c}  $  , R. Send  $  ^{d}  $  , H. Wonneberger  $  ^{d}  $  , I. Bruder  $  ^{d*}  $   and V. Getautis  $  ^{a*}  $

Aliphatic substituent effects on the HOMO energy levels and the ability to transport charge and form stable molecular glasses of systematically modified spiro-OMeTAD analogous were investigated. It was determined that thermal properties, energy levels and hole mobility values are dependent on the number of alkyl substituents and their position in the investigated spirobifluorene-based hole transporting materials (HTMs). The charge mobility of HTM3 possessing seemingly insignificant m-methyl group in the diphenylamino moieties is the highest with the value of   $  2.8 \cdot 10^{-3}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $  /V·s at the   $  6.4 \times 10^{5}  $   V cm  $  ^{-1}  $   field strength. It was found that moving one methoxy group into m-position in the diphenylamino fragment ensured stable amorphous phase of HTM1. Moreover, the long-term stability of the solid state dye-sensitized solar cells (ssDSSC) device comprising HTM1 was significantly enhanced over the cell with spiro-OMeTAD in lifetime tests. Findings described in this publication could be applicable to the hybrid solar cell research as a number of well-performing architectures rely heavily upon doped spiro-OMeTAD as HTM.

Keywords: charge transport, ionization potential, morphological stability, hybrid solar cells, spirobifluorenes

# Introduction

Continual growth of human population and improving living standards result in increasing energy consumption, while combustion of fossil fuels, which currently is the main way to satisfy global energetic needs, causes environmental pollution and climate change  $  ^{1,2}  $  . Thus development of sustainable energy solutions is urgently needed to manage these challenges. Among various renewable sources solar energy has several advantages and is believed to have the largest potential of satisfying the future global energetic needs  $  ^{3,4}  $  . Solar energy can reduce the dependence on energy import as it is widely available throughout the world, and it enhances the security of supply because no fuel price risk or constraints are involved  $  ^{5}  $  . Besides, solar power could eventually contribute to stabilize the costs of electricity generation by increasing energy variety. Solar photovoltaics (PV) do not generate greenhouse gas and other pollutants (such as sulphur and nitrogen oxides) emissions during operation; in addition, little

or no water is consumed  $  ^{6}  $  . These benefits of solar cells (SC) are especially significant in dry and hot areas where large-scale use of fresh water for cooling of thermal power plants and local air pollution become severe problems  $  ^{5}  $  . About 90 percent of photovoltaic market is currently occupied by crystalline silicon solar cells  $  ^{5}  $  ; however, relatively high production cost resulting in long financial payback time  $  ^{1}  $   encourages a search of cheaper alternatives. Due to their potential low cost, easy processing and good performance dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs) based on nanocrystalline semiconductors are expected to become a promising alternative photovoltaic technology. Among various hole conductors employed in solar cells, low molecular weight organic compounds have advantages of plentiful sources, low cost, easy film formation and simple tuning of properties by structure modifications  $  ^{7}  $  . On the other hand, such p-type semiconductors, forming stable amorphous phases, are also attractive materials to be used in organic light-emitting diodes, photorefractive applications or electrophotography  $  ^{8}  $  .

Recently, organometal halide perovskites (e.g., methylammonium lead halides   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbX_{3}  $  , where X = halogen) have emerged as promising light harvesters for high-efficiency nanostructured photovoltaic devices  $  ^{9-12}  $  . In these cells, the hole transporting material is the key component. HTMs used for perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are expected to have good hole-drift mobility and high morphological stability as well as to meet certain HOMO and LUMO energy requirements. From the class of small molecule HTMs, 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(4,4'-dimethoxydiphenyl-amino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) (Fig. 1a) is the most often used as hole-transporting material in solid-state dye-sensitized solar cells (ssDSSCs)  $  ^{15,16}  $   as well as in PSCs  $  ^{9,17-20}  $  .

Alternative low molecular weight p-type semiconductors such as 3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene- $ ^{21} $ , pyrene- $ ^{22} $ , linear  $ \pi $ -conjugated- $ ^{23} $ , azomethine- $ ^{24} $ , butadiene- $ ^{25} $ , methoxydiphenylamine-substituted carbazole- $ ^{26} $ , and triphenylamine-based $ ^{27-28} $  structures pave the way towards low-cost, environmentally friendly, and efficient HTMs. However, the majority of these compounds fail to show performance similar to that of spiro-OMeTAD. Unfortunately, spiro-OMeTAD is found to exist in semicrystalline state and has a tendency to crystallize in the device, thus impairing cell's performance $ ^{15} $ . Therefore, the strategy for modification of the HTM in order to obtain truly non-crystallizable molecular glasses with retained outstanding processability and tuneable electronic properties could be a great achievement in improving the performance and stability of the respective photovoltaic devices.

Until now only a few concrete recommendations have been made with regard to molecular structure modification. Jeon et al. reported that PSCs performance was dependent on the

The PSCs based on modified spiro-OMeTAD, i.e. possessing 2,4'-dimethoxydiphenylamino fragments showed highly improved performance, which resulted in an overall power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 16.7 %, compared to PCE (  $  \sim  $  15%) of conventional p-OMe positions. Recently, we have demonstrated that the introduction of local asymmetry into the spiro-OMeTAD molecule suppresses crystal growth in the HTM layer and significantly increases solar cell resistance to thermal stress and overall lifetime of the ssDSSC device  $  ^{30}  $  .

Current our research was inspired by the study of Nukada, Sato and Akasaki  $  ^{31}  $  who reported how seemingly insignificant methyl groups at different positions of N,N,N',N'-tetraarylbenzidine (TPD derivative) molecule (Fig. 1b) can influence the hole-drift mobility. They have found that the introduction of methyl groups into the side phenyl moieties increases hole drift mobility from o- to p- positions. It was also revealed that the presence of two methyl groups is superior to that of a single one. Taking into consideration that spiro-OMeTAD (Fig. 1a) is conceptually derived mostly from the respective TPD compounds  $  ^{32}  $ , we have applied these findings for our research.

In this study, we systematically modified spiro-OMeTAD by either inserting additional methyl group(s) or by moving methoxy group(s) to metha-position and investigated their structure-property relationship. Methyl groups were selected because of the commercial availability of the starting materials; moreover there is a very slight difference between methyl and ethyl groups in this case  $  ^{31}  $  . Methoxy group was replaced by ethoxy in some cases in order to increase the local asymmetry of the molecule. Thus, we report the synthesis and characterization of seven spirobifluorene derivatives HTM1-HTM7 (Fig. 2) as well as their photoelectric and morphological properties.

# Results and discussion

# Synthesis and morphological properties of spiro-OMeTAD analogues.

Variously substituted diphenylamines 1–7 were synthesized from the respective aniline and appropriate aryl halide employing Buchwald-Hartwig reaction as precursors for the synthesis of corresponding spirobifluorenes HTM1–7. A water-mediated catalyst preactivation was necessary for the success of this reaction. It was performed by mixing palladium (II) acetate with the ligand in the mixture of 1,4-dioxane and water at 80°C for about 1.5 min  $  ^{33}  $  . Reaction was over in 15-30 minutes and the products were isolated with 85-96 % yield (Scheme 1). Cross-coupling of 2,2',7,7'-tetrabromo-9,9'-spirobifluorene with synthesized diphenylamines 1–7 in the presence of Pd(II) acetate, tri-tert-butylphosphonium tetra-fluoroborate and sodium tert-butoxide in dry toluene at 110°C afforded target compounds HTM1–7 with 70-87 % yields (Scheme 2).

The results of NMR spectroscopy, MS and elemental analysis confirmed the structures of compounds HTM1–7. All these compounds are soluble in common organic solvents (e.g. tetrahydrofuran, chloroform, dichloromethane etc.) which make them suitable for solution processing. The detailed description of the synthesis procedures and spectroscopic data is presented in the Supporting Information.

The thermal properties were studied by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) measurements. It was found that moving one of methoxy groups into m-position, additional methyl(s) or m-methoxy groups in the diphenylamino fragments did not significantly change the thermal stability of hole transporting material compared to spiro-OMeTAD. The materials HTM1-5 start to decompose at temperatures in the range between 400 and 451°C (Fig. 3, Fig.S1, Table 1), confirming their good thermal stability and stating practical utilization.

Apparently introduction of one additional methyl group into m-position in HTM3 was not enough to inhibit the tendency to crystallize, and both glass transition (120°C) and melting of crystals (237°C) were observed during DSC experiment as reported elsewhere  $  ^{30}  $  . The melting of the crystals was detected at 271°C for compound HTM6, while polymorphism was observed in the case of HTM7 (i.e. melting was registered at 220°C, 233°C and 237°C).

# Optical and photoelectrical properties.

The UV-vis absorption bands of the spirobifluorene-based HTM1-7 and the parent spiro-OMeTAD were measured in THF and are presented in Fig. 6. All investigated HTMs are based on the similar principal structure as spiro-OMeTAD, and therefore, their UV-vis spectra are quite similar as well.

Two main photon absorption maxima at ca. 303 nm and 387 nm are assigned to   $  \pi-\pi^{*}  $   transitions of the conjugate system in analyzed compounds. The comparison of UV/vis spectra of new HTMs with spiro-OMeTAD shows that the movement of methoxy group to m-position (HTM1) or additional m-methoxy fragment (HTM2) resulted in a slight hypsochromic (4 nm) shift. Most likely, due to the presence of methoxy groups at m-positions in the one of benzene rings in the diphenylamine moieties, the methoxydiphenylamine-substituted fluorene fragments are slightly more twisted out-of-plane compared with spiro-OMeTAD. Moreover, the molar absorptivity of all synthesized compounds is slightly larger compared to spiro-OMeTAD (69670 M  $  ^{-1}  $  cm  $  ^{-1}  $   at 387 nm), varying from 76492 (HTM3) to 95833 M  $  ^{-1}  $  cm  $  ^{-1}  $   (HTM2), which is typical to compounds of this class  $  ^{32, 35-36}  $  . The largest hyperchromic shift is observed for HTM2 possessing two m-methoxy fragments in one of benzene rings in the diphenylamine moieties.

When considering the use of an organic material for hole-transport applications it is important to have an understanding of its solid state ionization energies  $ (I_{p}) $ . This understanding can help in identifying suitable partner organic transport and inorganic electrode materials. The ionization potential was measured by the measured by photoelectron spectroscopy in air (PESA) method (Fig. S2) and results are presented in Table 1. The  $ I_{p} $  values of all investigated materials were quite similar and close to that of spiro-OMeTAD (5.00 eV). Movement of methoxy group to  $ m $ -position or introduction of additional  $ m $ -methoxy group in compounds HTM1 and HTM2 resulted in the increase of  $ I_{p} $  by 0.12 and 0.08 eV correspondingly compared to spiro-OMeTAD. Additional methyl group(s) in  $ m $ -position of diphenylamino fragment(s) of the HTM3 and HTM4 decreased ionization potential to 4.95 eV and 4.91 eV accordingly. However, compound HTM5 possessing two  $ m $ -methyl groups in one of benzene ring showed ionization potential very similar to that of spiro-OMeTAD. Replacement of one methoxy group with ethoxy in transporting materials HTM6 and HTM7 also did not have significant influence on  $ I_{p} $ .

The corresponding layers of spiro-OMeTAD were also prepared in order to compare the charge-transporting ability of new HTMs.

# Photovoltaic performance.

Synthesized compounds were also used as hole-transporting materials in ssDSSCs. Photovoltaic properties of the devices constructed with the solid hole transporters were measured using a perylene monoimide dye ID504 as a sensitizer  $  ^{42}  $  . The J-V characteristics of cells with spirobifluorene derivatives HTM1–7 under AM 1.5G illumination are shown in Fig. 9 and Table 2.

It is noteworthy that all devices with synthesized transporting materials demonstrated higher open-circuit voltage (800-900 mV) compared to spiro-OMeTAD (760 mV). However, structure modifications resulted in lower short-circuit photocurrent density   $  U_{sc}  $   of new coumpounds in comparison to spiro-OMeTAD. As expected from charge mobility measurements, introduction of two m-methoxy groups (HTM2) in one of benzene rings had particularly negative influence on short-circuit current density (1.84 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   in comparison to 9.76 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   of solar cell comprising spiro-OMeTAD) and overall conversion efficiency (0.6 % compared to 4.8 % in the device with spiro-OMeTAD). Compound HTM3 with additional m-methyl group has shown similar   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF values and the increase of   $  V_{OC}  $   by 60 mV with respect to spiro-OMeTAD. Finally, the overall conversion efficiency of solar cell with HTM3 was equal to that of device with spiro-OMeTAD. A small difference in   $  J_{sc}  $   and   $  V_{OC}  $ , and a large difference in FF is observed comparing HTM6 with HTM3. It can be explained by stronger tendency of HTM6 to crystallize as was revealed by DSC analysis. Most probably the crystallization of the HTM inside the pores or on the interface affects the contact between the light absorber and the hole transporter  $  ^{30}  $  .

Device comprising HTM7 demonstrated the best open-circuit voltage value (900 mV) of all investigated samples. Decrease in the short-circuit current density in the case of HTM4-7 most likely could be attributed to the larger charge recombination at HTM/dye interface, which arises from the alterations in the shape of these molecules and subsequent changes in packing and charge transport properties.

Previously we reported that the introduction of the local asymmetry into the spiro-OMeTAD molecule had suppressed crystal growth in the HTM layer and had significantly increased solar cell resistance to thermal stress and overall lifetime of the ssDSSC device  $  ^{30}  $  . This inspired us to investigate the stability of the device comprising HTM1 as this hole transporting material is fully amorphous and shows only glass transition temperature. Devices based on HTM1 and spiro-OMeTAD were investigated to determine their stability (Fig. 10). Both solar cells were sealed and constantly kept at 60°C and 30 % humidity after fabrication. It was revealed that despite more than twice lower initial overall conversion efficiency, sample with HTM1 was more stable compared to DSSC based on spiro-OMeTAD. A sharp decrease of the conversion efficiency of the device with spiro-OMeTAD was registered at the beginning of experiment and was equal to ca. 50% after 120 hours. Only 40 % and 30 % of initial efficiency were observed after 170 hours and at the end of experiment, accordingly. In the case of compound HTM1, the efficiency of the device remained almost unchanged and was equal to 85 % of initial cell's performance at the end of investigation after 300 hours.

# Conclusions

In summary, seven systematically modified spiro-OMeTAD analogous were synthesized and investigated as HTMs. The  $ I_{p} $  values of all investigated materials were quite similar and close to that of spiro-OMeTAD. The charge mobility of HTM3 possessing additional  $ m $ -methyl group in the diphenylamino moieties is the highest with the value of  $ 2.8 \cdot 10^{-3} \mathrm{~cm}^2/\mathrm{V} \cdot \mathrm{s} $  at the  $ 6.4 \times 10^{5} \mathrm{~V} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1} $  field strength. This illustrates the statement that photoelectric properties can be improved by introducing seemingly insignificant methyl groups. Moreover, it was found that moving one of methoxy group into  $ m $ -position in the diphenylamino fragment ensured stable amorphous phase of spiromolecule HTM1. For this reason the long-term stability of the ssDSSC device comprising HTM1 was significantly enhanced over the cell with spiro-OMeTAD in lifetime tests.
