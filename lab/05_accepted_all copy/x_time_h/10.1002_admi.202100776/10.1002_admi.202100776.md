# Quantum Dots Encapsulated by   $  ZrO_{2}  $   Enhance the Stability of Perovskite Solar Cells

Kai Wang, Ruohe Yao, Pei Shen, Yi Fang, Ling Chen, and Hong Wang*

In the last several years, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are faced with big problems in the stability issues, which also occur in the Interface modification materials. Herein,   $  CsPbBr_{1.5}I_{1.5}  $   quantum dots (QDs) encapsulated in   $  ZrO_{2}  $   sol is introduced to improve device stability to some extent. The encapsulated QDs can improve the morphology of the perovskite layer and effectively passivate the perovskite film. The device fabricated by   $  CsPbBr_{1.5}I_{1.5}  $   QDs encapsulated achieves a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 21.23%, corresponding to 18.86% for the unmodified control devices. The performance of the device based on   $  CsPbBr_{1.5}I_{1.5}/ZrO_{2}  $   nanocomposites modified remains 81.8% of initial PCE after 500 h.

# 1. Introduction

In recent years, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) as a kind of green energy, attract much attention and become a hotspot in the energy field.  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs astonishingly has upgraded to 25.5%  $  ^{[4]}  $   from the initial 3.8%.  $  ^{[5]}  $   Due to the contribution of wide optical absorption and high charge mobility, perovskite materials have been spotlighted on the solar cell materials. Although the PCE of PSCs has achieved tremendous advances, stability and efficiency have been key factors restricting the development of PSCs.  $  ^{[6-11]}  $   For solution-processed perovskite films, the defects on the surface of the perovskite crystals are the challenge of device instability. Perovskite films produce amounts of grain boundaries which lead to the formation of defect states during the preparation process.  $  ^{[12-14]}  $   The surface defects may cause great charge car-

sivating the defects through regulating the grain size as the passivation agents. Recently, quantum dots (QDs) passivation has been applied to PSCs to reduce interface defects by filling holes.  $  ^{[30-32]}  $   QDs were introduced into the PSCs interface engineering as a significant passivation method that can reduce the non-radiative recombination. But the stability of QDs is still an important factor that plagues modification strategies. They are easily decomposed in harsh environments such as humid air. Commonly, polymers, shells, and semiconductors were introduced into the QDs structure to increase the stability and optical properties. Later, the structure of composite QDs compounds was developed. Composite   $  CsPbBr_{3}/MoS_{2}  $    $  ^{[33]}  $   has been fabricated, corresponding to similar compounds which have also been reported such as   $  CsPbBr_{3}/TiO_{2}  $    $  ^{[34,35]}  $   and   $  CsPbBr_{3}/SiO_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[36,37]}  $   Zheng et al.  $  ^{[38]}  $   construed the   $  CsPbI_{3}/Mg_{x}Zn_{1-x}Te  $   the high-quality, functional nanocomposites. This strategy was proved to improve charge diffusion and separation causing better optical properties. Recently,   $  ZrO_{2}  $   has been introduced to composite QDs due to its chemical stability and easy solution processing. Thereby the combination of QDs and   $  ZrO_{2}  $   attracts attention ascribed to boosting the performance of the device of composite QDs/  $  ZrO_{2}  $   structure.  $  ^{[39]}  $

In this study, we use   $  ZrO_{2}  $  , as the interface modification layer, to encapsulate the QDs. We obtain the   $  ZrO_{2}  $   sol by hydrolysis reaction.  $  ^{[40]}  $   Zirconium n-propoxide is hydrolyzed by trace water in n-propanol and toluene to get ZrOH and   $  ZrOC_{3}H_{7}  $   which interact to form   $  ZrO_{2}  $   sol. Acetic acid was added to prevent precipitation. The   $  ZrO_{2}  $   sol formed mesh interpenetration structure to encapsulate QDs. The   $  CsPbBr_{1.5}I_{1.5}/ZrO_{2}  $   nanostructure improved the performance and stability of PSCs device with the functions of surface passivation and withstanding harsh environments. The QDs can also modify the morphology and passivating the surface defect. The champion performance of the device based on   $  CsPbBr_{1.5}I_{1.5}/ZrO_{2}  $   achieves a significantly PCE of 21.23%, in contrast to the control device with a PCE

Therefore, passivation surface defects are one of the methods to improve the stability and device performance of PSCs simply and efficiently.  $  ^{[21-24]}  $

# 2. Results and Discussion

Figure 1a shows the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of   $  CsPbBr_{1.5}I_{1.5}  $   nanocomposites and Cubic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $ , respectively. Compared to cubic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $ , the (100) and (200) peaks shift increased for the   $  CsPbBr_{1.5}I_{1.5}  $ . Since the diameter of iodine atom is larger than the bromine atom, as the content of the Br in the QDs increases, the lattice contraction appears, causing the XRD peak to shift to a higher angle.  $  ^{[41]}  $   In fact, the (100) and (200) peaks of the   $  CsPbBr_{1.5}I_{1.5}  $   move to a higher angle in line with theory.  $  ^{[42,43]}  $

Figure 1b,c shows the HRTEM lattice image, the average size of QDs was about  $ 12\mathrm{nm} $  uniformly distributed in the solution. It can be clearly found that lattice spacing is  $ 0.6\mathrm{nm} $  from the HRTEM. Similarly, we can see the QDs are evenly wrapped in  $ \mathrm{ZrO}_2 $  colloid from Figure 1d, which can greatly improve stability.

To investigate the property of the film of PSC device, the XRD pattern of perovskite  $ +\mathrm{CsPbBr}_{1.5}\mathrm{I}_{1.5} / \mathrm{ZrO}_2 $  and perovskite films has been compared in Figure 2a. There is no noticeable change in the peeks positions of perovskite  $ +\mathrm{CsPbBr}_{1.5}\mathrm{I}_{1.5} / \mathrm{ZrO}_2 $  films. It is worth mentioning that the intensity of the diffraction peak is

enhanced by taking the   $  CsPbBr_{1.5}I_{1.5}/ZrO_{2}  $   as the interface layer. That indicated that   $  CsPbBr_{1.5}I_{1.5}/ZrO_{2}  $   enhances the crystallinity of the perovskite instead of changing the crystal structure.

To further investigate the optical properties of   $  CsPbBr_{1.5}I_{1.5}  $   QDs encapsulation and pure   $  CsPbBr_{1.5}I_{1.5}  $   QDs, the PL emission spectra for film state and solution state were measured. The slight redshift of the PL peak may attribute to   $  ZrO_{2}  $   encapsulation caused by quantum confinement effect which leads to the change of QDs size. The UV spectrum of QDs and QDs/  $  ZrO_{2}  $   nanocomposites were plotted in Figure S2, Supporting Information. It can be clearly seen from the figure that the QDs/  $  ZrO_{2}  $   almost has a slight red shift compares to the UV spectrum of pure QDs. # 4. Experimental Section

Materials: All reagents were directly purchased from commercial sources without further purification. Cesium carbonate, 1-Octadecene, Oleic acid, and Oleylamine were purchased from the Adamas. Methylammonium iodide (MAI), lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ ), lead Bromide (PbBr $ _2 $ ), acetonitrile (99.8%), chlorobenzene (99.9%), and dimethylformamide (DMF) (99%) were purchased from the Sigma-Aldrich. Zirconium propoxide (ca. 70% in 1-Propanol), Li-bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI), and 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP) were purchased from the TCI. 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) (99.0%) was purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Co., Ltd.

Device Fabrication: ITO glass substrates were cleaned with detergent, de-ionized water, acetone, and iso-propanol, in sequence, then dried in an oven at 120 °C for 30 min and treated with ultraviolet ozone for 15 min. A diluted SnO₂ nanoparticle solution (2.67 wt.%) was deposited on ITO substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s, and then annealed at 150 °C for 30 min. To prepare MAPbI₃ precursor solution, the molar ratio for PbI₂: MAl is 1:1 and the concentration of perovskite solution is 400 mg mL⁻¹ For the MAPbI₃ perovskite, the prepared perovskite precursor solution was deposited on the SnO₂ substrate at 4000 rpm for 20 s at a constant temperature under ambient conditions. After annealing for 5 min, Spiro-OMeTAD in CB solution (72.6 mg mL⁻¹) with 17.5 μL of LiTFSI (520 mg mL⁻¹ in Acetonitrile) and 29 μL of tbp was deposited the perovskite layer by spin-coating at 4000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, MoO₃ (5 nm) and Au (100 nm) were sequentially deposited on the hole transport layer under a high vacuum (vacuum is 1 × 10⁻⁴ Pa) at a rate of 0.01 and 0.1 nm s⁻¹, respectively.

Device Characterization: XRD patterns were measured by Bruker D8 Advance X-ray Diffractometer at 40 kV and 40 mA using Cu Kα radiation. The cross-sectional images of QDs/ZrO₂ PSC were taken by SEM (ZEISS Merlin). PL spectra were obtained using a spectrofluorometer (HORIBA/Fluorolog-3). The QDs PSC patterns were measured under AM1.5G solar irradiation with an intensity of 100 mW cm⁻² through Enlitech SS-F5-3A solar simulator. J-V properties were measured by the method of Enlitech Ltd. and Keithley 2400 source meter under dark conditions. UV-vis absorption spectra were measured on a PERSEE TU-1901 spectrophotometer. EQE measurements were conducted on devices through a lock-in amplifier under illumination with a monochromatic light from a xenon arc lamp. The time-resolved PL measurements were performed with an Edinburgh Instruments spectrometer (FLS980).
