# Abstract:

Tin perovskite solar cells (TPSCs) are rising as the most promising candidates for lead-free PSCs. However, the randomly crystallized tin halide perovskite with high concentration of defects is still the bottleneck for obtaining high efficiency. Here, we report the pretreatment of   $  FASnI_{3}  $   (formamidinium, FA) perovskite film by spin-coating a solution of organic halide salt n-propylammonium iodide (PAI) before annealing. It is found that PAI can induce the templated growth of   $  FASnI_{3}  $   crystals by reconstruction of the intermediate phase, resulting in a highly crystallized   $  FASnI_{3}  $   film with preferential orientation along (100) plane and suppressed trap density. Finally, a stabilized efficiency of 11.22% was certified at an accredited test center and the cell is stable, maintaining over 95% of its initial efficiency after 1000 hours of operation at the maximum power point.

# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells that use organic-inorganic lead halide semiconductors as light absorbers have achieved high efficiencies as well as improved stabilities after ten years of development.  $  ^{1-8}  $   However, the risk for lead leakage could impede their commercialization. One way to solve this problem is to develop effective encapsulation technique to sequestrate lead inside the devices.  $  ^{9,10}  $   The other is to investigate lead-free alternatives.  $  ^{11-15}  $   Among them, tin halide perovskites are the most promising candidates due to their narrow band gap, low exciton binding energy and high carrier mobility,  $  ^{16-19}  $   whereas they often show fast crystallization process and are easy to be oxidized, leaving behind rough morphology and high-level concentration of defects, which limit the improvement in the performance of tin perovskite solar cells (TPSCs).  $  ^{20,21}  $

To address these disadvantages, a lot of efforts have been devoted to optimize the crystallization process. For example,   $  \pi  $  -conjugated Lewis base molecules, dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and pseudohalogen were applied to form intermediate adducts with   $  SnI_{2}  $   to slow down the crystal growth.  $  ^{[22-24]}  $   In addition, the long-chain ammonium cations such as ethylammonium, butylammonium and phenylethylammonium were used to induce the oriented crystallization of tin perovskites or to form 2D-3D structures to passivate the trap states and prevent the moisture.  $  ^{[25-27]}  $   Recently, a few of reports have published efficiencies over 12%.  $  ^{[28,29]}  $   However, it is noticed that these cells were unstable under light soaking, which is difficult for them to obtain stabilized results under strict standards as are stipulated by accredited test centers. The reason can be attributed to the high defect density in TPSCs.  $  ^{[11,16-20,29]}  $   Therefore, new methods are required for further improving the crystallization quality of tin halide perovskite film.

# Results and Discussion

Fig. 1a shows the schematic fabrication process of the TG-FASnI $ _3 $ . From Fig. 1b(3), a smoother morphology of an intermediate phase with bright boundaries can be observed. To further confirm the location of PAI, we amplified the signals of (100) peak in Fig. 1c and no change is found for PAI-treated samples in the peak positions compared to the pristine  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  (Fig. S7, ESI†). This result means that PA cation will not enter the crystal lattice of  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  and might locate at the grain boundaries, which is consistent with the SEM images of Fig.1b(3). According to a lot of previous works, the long-chain ammonium cations such as butylammonium and phenylethylammonium that are applied as additives tend to form 2D structure on the surface of 3D  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $ . To testify whether 2D structure also formed in our method, we fabricated the pure 2D  $ \mathrm{PA}_2\mathrm{SnI}_6 $  film. As a result, the XRD peaks of 2D  $ \mathrm{PA}_2\mathrm{SnI}_6 $  (Fig. S8, ESI†) don't match any peaks in TG-FASnI₃ films. Considering that the signals of 2D structure may be too weak to be detected by XRD, we then performed the 2D grazing-incidence wide-angle x-ray Scattering (GIWAXS) with the incident angle of  $ 0.1^{\circ} $  and  $ 1^{\circ} $  to check the structure of  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  and TG-FASnI₃ from top to the bulk (Fig. S9, ESI†). Similarly, no Debye-Scherre rings or spots at low q value between  $ 0.4 - 0.8\AA^{-1} $  that can be assigned to the large lattice distance of 2D structures are observed for TG-FASnI₃ sample (Fig.S9c and d, ESI†),[25,27,36] which means 2D structure doesn't exist in TG-FASnI₃. Moreover, the  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  sample displays diffraction Debye-Scherre rings with an isotropical intensity distribution (Fig. S9a and b, ESI†), indicating considerable randomness in the orientation of the crystal domains. In contrast, sharp and discrete Bragg spots can be observed for TG-FASnI₃, which means the crystal domains are highly oriented with the (100) plane vertical to the substrate surface (Fig.S 9c and d, ESI†). The current-voltage ( $ I-V $ ) curves of the two devices are shown in Fig. 3c. The detailed devices parameters under different scanning directions can be found in Table S1. # Conclusion

In summary, we successfully fabricate templated-growth   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films with high orientation and less defects and increase thereby the efficiency and stability of TPSCs. A stabilized efficiency of 11.22% was certified by an accredited test center. In this work, we notice that although the defects of TPSC are suppressed by using our pretreatment method with PAI, the defect density in TPSC is still higher than that of lead-based PSCs (  $  10^{13}-10^{14}cm^{-3}  $  ).  $  ^{42,43}  $   This should be the main reason for the comparatively low efficiency of TPSCs. Further advances in the suppression of defects in TPSCs should enable the efficiency exceeding 15% with high working stability.
