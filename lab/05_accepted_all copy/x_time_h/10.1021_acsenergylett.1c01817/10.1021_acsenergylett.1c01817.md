# Humidity-Assisted Chlorination with Solid Protection Strategy for Efficient Air-Fabricated Inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   Perovskite Solar Cells

Sheng Fu, Wenxiao Zhang, Xiaodong Li, Jianming Guan, Weijie Song, and Junfeng Fang*

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Humidity-assisted chlorination with solid protection via 1,2-bis(chlorodimethylsilyl)ethane (Si–Cl) incorporation is demonstrated as an effective strategy to address water erosion for efficient air-fabricated inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Si–Cl molecules can rapidly react with water to reduce moisture erosion during deposition in air. In addition, the hydrolysis production of hydrogen chloride (HCl) can chlorinate   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   while the spontaneous polymerization of 1,2-bis(hydroxydimethylsilyl)ethane (Si–OH) builds a solid protection, which would improve the crystallization and phase stability of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $  . Therefore, a champion efficiency of 18.93% with high open-circuit voltage of 1.176 V is achieved, which is the highest efficiency among the inverted inorganic PSCs. Furthermore, the moisture tolerance of both the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films and air-fabricated PSCs gets significant improvement. In addition, the nonencapsulated device shows good operational stability without efficiency drop after 1 sun illumination at 65 °C for 900 h.

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been widely accepted as next-generation photovoltaics and reached certified efficiency over 25% in 10 years of development with the organic-inorganic perovskite as active-layer materials.  $  ^{1-4}  $   However, light and thermal decomposition of the hybrid perovskite seriously limit their long-term stability because of the volatile organic composition.  $  ^{5-8}  $   All-inorganic iodide lead cesium   $  (\mathrm{CsPbI}_{3})  $   perovskite with nonvolatile nature and ideal photoelectric properties is one of the most promising alternatives to inorganic-organic perovskite in photovoltaics,  $  ^{9-12}  $   especially as the top light-harvesting layer in tandem cells.  $  ^{13}  $   As   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs have made great progress in terms of efficiency, their air fabrication has gradually gained importance for future low-cost production. Water erosion in air is one of the culprits impeding their air fabrication. As is well-known, the thermodynamically unstable photovoltaic phases (  $  \beta  $   and   $  \gamma  $  ) of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite would spontaneously transit into nonphotovoltaic phase (  $  \delta  $  ) with the distortion of lead iodine octahedral   $  [PbI_{6}]^{4-}  $  .  $  ^{10,14,15}  $   The undesired phase transition could be significantly accelerated under water erosion. On the one hand, water molecules could enlarge the halide vacancy density in the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   lattice for the large solvation enthalpy and low vacancy-forming energy of halide ions during deposition in ambient conditions.  $  ^{16-18}  $   The halide vacancies would reduce the ionic binding between the neighboring   $  [PbI_{6}]^{4-}  $   and catalyze the phase transition. Here, focused on addressing water erosion for air-fabricated inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs, we propose a novel strategy named humidity-assisted chlorination with solid protection via the 1,2-bis(chlorodimethylsilyl)ethane (Si–Cl) incorporation. Moisture-sensitive Si–Cl molecules can rapidly react with water in air and form a humidity-less condition during coating in air. In addition, the hydrogen products of chloride (HCl) and 1,2-bis(hydroxydimethylsilyl)ethane (Si–OH) also act as the facilitators for crystallization and phase stability. HCl could chlorinate   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   and control grain growth for better crystallization.  $  ^{20,34-36}  $   Si–OH molecules could spontaneously polymerize to form the polysiloxane (P-SiO) filling the grain boundaries (GBs) for solid protection. As a result, high-quality and stable   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films are obtained at RH 40% ambient conditions (Figure S1). Significantly, a champion efficiency of 18.93% with high   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.176 V is realized, which is the highest efficiency among inverted inorganic PSCs (Table S1).

The optimized devices feature good duration against various aging tests without any encapsulation. The device retains 93.1% of its initial efficiency after aging at RH 10% condition for 1000 h, and the untreated (Ref) devices lost half within 432 h. In addition, the device maintains 92.5% after maximum power point (MPP) tracking for 1700 min in RH 30% ambient conditions. In a glovebox, the target device presents no drop in efficiency after maximum power point (MPP) tracking at 65 °C for over 900 h, whereas the Ref suffered from serious decrease within 300 h.

It is known that the inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films always suffer from poor crystallization with inferior morphology because of their high nuclear energy.  $  ^{20}  $   The chlorine ions have been proven to reduce the nuclear energy of inorganic perovskite, and the Lewis-acid siloxane can also control the crystallization for large grains, which infers the crystallization promotion of Si–Cl incorporation. X-ray diffraction (XRD) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) measurements are employed to estimate crystalline influence. As shown in Figure 2a, all the films show the   $  \beta  $   phase XRD patterns with the same band gap of 1.68 eV (Figure S6), where the peaks located at 14.4° and 28.9° correspond to the (110) and (220) crystal plane.  $  ^{40,41}  $   Consistent with the poor moisture tolerance, the Ref has already suffered from phase transition with a nonphotovoltaic phase (  $  \delta  $  ) at 10° in XRD patterns during annealing. In comparison, benefiting from the less-humidity condition and enhanced moisture tolerance, the moisture-induced   $  \delta  $   phase is effectively removed with Si–Cl incorporation. Additionally, in comparison with the diffraction intensity of (110) and (220) peaks, the films fabricated with the Si–Cl incorporated precursors are stronger than the Ref, which reveals the crystalline promotion of Si–Cl, and 1 mg/mL is the optimum concentration. Figure 2b shows the top-view SEM images of various   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films, and the cross-sectional SEM images are presented in Figure S7. The Ref film displays the poor morphology similar to that reported in previous literature contributions,  $  ^{16-18}  $   with many pinholes and small grains. Air-fabricated   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films always contain massive defects due to the humidity-induced halide vacancies and poor crystallization. Powerful evidence can be found in Figure 1d. The loss of halide ions under water erosion would leave the lead ions uncoordinated and form the metal lead (  $  Pb^{0}  $  ) as the deep-energy-level traps in Ref films.  $  ^{42,43}  $   The pinholes and unpackaged GBs also distribute numerous traps, which would result in the poor photoelectric properties with serious nonradiative recombination for   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films. In comparison, the Si–Cl incorporation could reduce the water erosion during grain growth and produce high-quality films with effective passivation, which thus might improve the photoelectric properties of the target films. The trap densities of various films are simulated by the trap-filled limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ) in the space charge limited current (SCLC) curves with the hole-only devices (Figure 2c). In good agreement, the trap density is reduced from   $  5.36 \times 10^{15}  $   to   $  3.23 \times 10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $   after Si–Cl incorporation. In addition, the doped   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film also shows better conductivity than the Ref films (Figure S8), demonstrating the fast carrier transport for fewer traps. Panels d and e of Figure 2 display the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL spectra of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films coated on clear glass, respectively. The Si–Cl doped films show two-fold PL intensity and prolonged carrier lifetime (Table S2) in comparison with the Ref, which directly reveal the improved photoelectric properties with less nonradiative recombination

owing to the reduced defects and effective passivation with Si–Cl incorporation.

The inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs with the structure of ITO/P3CT-N/CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  /PCBM/C60/BCP/Ag are fabricated in air (RH 40%) (Figure 3a and the cross-sectional SEM image in Figure S9). The statistical photovoltaic parameters from 30 individual devices for each dopant concentration are listed in Table S3, and the J-V curves of the champion device are presented in Figure 3b. The Ref devices show a champion efficiency of 16.51% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.115 V, a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 19.89 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , a fill factor (FF) of 74.42%, and a stabilized power output (SPO) of 15.91% (Figure 3c). In good agreement with the photoelectrical property improvement, the devices fabricated from the Si–Cl doped films exhibit the higher efficiency with improved   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF (Figure 3b inset and Figure S10). A champion efficiency of 18.93% is realized based on the optimum devices (1 mg/mL) with a high   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.176 V,   $  J_{sc}  $   of 20.10 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , FF of 80.04%, and SPO of 18.36%, which is the highest efficiency among inverted inorganic PSCs (Table S1). In addition, the optimized device also features minimized hysteresis (Figure S11 and Table S4), which might profile from the reduction of fewer traps.  $  ^{38}  $   The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra (Figure 3d) reveal the well-matched current density to the   $  J_{sc}  $   from J-V curves with the integrated current density of 18.89 and 19.61 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   for the Ref and Si–Cl doped devices. Moreover, the chlorinated PSCs feature the higher absorption at the long-wavelength region. Thus, the humidity-assisted chlorination with solid protection strategy can significantly improve photovoltaic performance of air-fabricated inverted CsPbI $ _3 $  PSCs because of the higher  $ V_{\mathrm{bi}} $  and less nonradiative recombination.

Along with the efficiency, stability is another key parameter for the future application of perovskite photovoltaics. The hydrophobic P-SiO at GBs could insulate the external erosion, and the water contact angle is increased from the initial   $  35.7^{\circ}  $   to   $  63.2^{\circ}  $   with Si–Cl doping (Figure S15), which would prolong the moisture tolerance of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films. We evaluate the phase stability of the nonencapsulated   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films via accelerated aging at RH 40% condition for 5 h. As presented in Figure 5a, the Ref film has rapidly transited into a nonphotovoltaic phase with the absorption spectrum gradually changing to the   $  \delta  $   phase (Figure S16a) within 3 h. In sharp contrast, the chlorinated film exhibits black phase and unchanged absorption during the 5 h test (Figure S16b). After aging, the Ref film has totally transmitted into the   $  \delta  $   phase while the treated film maintains the   $  \beta  $   phase (Figure 5b), which proves that Si–Cl incorporation can improve phase stability and moisture tolerance for air fabrication. Consistent with the moisture stability of the films (Figure 5a,b), the Ref device has suffered from rapid decrease (Figure S17a) within 72 h in comparison to no phase transition of the optimized device after aged for 144 h in RH 25%. Previous literature contributions report absorption water can also induce the aggregation of the bathocuproine (BCP) layer and degradation of [6,6]-phenyl-C-61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) layers.  $  ^{50-52}  $   A preheated treatment before J-V measurement is applied to remove the absorption water and restore the efficiency (see Figure S17 for details). We further estimate the moisture stability of the devices at RH 10% condition. As shown in Figure 5c, the Ref device reveals an inferior duration of humidity and has dropped after aging for 432 h (Figure 5c, and the detailed parameters are listed in Table S5). In comparison, the optimized device maintains 93% of the initial

efficiency (Table S6) after the same aging for 1000 h, corresponding to the enhanced moisture tolerance of chlorination and solid protections by P-SiO in   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films.

The operational stability of target devices without any encapsulation is estimated by means of MPP tracking under a   $  100 \, mw/cm^{2}  $   LED array illuminated at   $  65 \, ^{\circ}C  $   in glovebox conditions (Figure S18). As shown in Figure 5d (other parameters are presented in Figure S18a–c), the Ref device lost its photovoltaic performance within 300 h, which might result from the phase instability for the massive traps. Inspiringly, the Si–Cl doped device maintains 99.6% of the initial efficiency after 900 h of aging. The J–V curves of the optimized device before and after MPP (Figure S19) are well consistent with measurements, and the efficiency is revealed to be unchanged around 17.80%, which further proves the superior stability. Considering the good moisture and operational stability after chlorination, MPP measurements at RH 30% are performed for the nonencapsulated devices (Figure S20). The optimized device retains 92.5% of the initial efficiency after tracking for 1700 min in comparison with 61.5% for the Ref after 890 min of tracking. Thus, combining superior photovoltaic performance with good stability for inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs, humidity-assisted chlorination with solid protection strategy has been proven feasible for air-fabricated high-performance inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs in future applications.

In summary, we have proposed an effective strategy named humidity-assisted chlorination with solid protection to settle the water erosion for efficient air-fabricated inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs. The Si–Cl molecules can rapidly react with water and form a humidity-less condition during deposition. The HCl produced by hydrolysis can chlorinate the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   for improved crystallization and humidity tolerance. Another hydrolysis production of Lewis-acid Si–OH can control the grain growth and spontaneously polymerize into P-SiO to fill the GBs, which provide solid protection and effective passivation to the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films. A champion efficiency of 18.93% with high   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.176 V is realized for the air-fabricated inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs. In addition, the optimized devices without any encapsulation also present good duration against moisture aging and ambient MPP measurements. In addition, the optimized devices feature superior operational stability and retain unchanged efficiency after MPP tracking at 65 °C for 900 h. These findings provided a new strategy to simplify fabricating conditions and address the fatal point of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   for future application of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs.
