# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have become one of the most promising photovoltaic technologies due to their high efficiency and low cost. The power conversion efficiencies (PCE) have rocked from 3.8% to over 20% in past few years [1–8]. Despite the rapid progress in efficiency, device stability remains of foremost concern for the next development of PSCs [9]. Due to their fragile nature, perovskite films degrade rapidly when exposed to moisture, oxygen, high temperature or light conditions [10,11]. To improve device stability, various methods are employed to moderate the surface or bulk properties of perovskite films through additive engineering [12]. These methods mainly follow the ideas of morphology control [13–18], surface passivation [19–24] or reduced-dimensional perovskite introduction [25–28]. For example, Wu et al. achieved large-area uniform perovskite films through using dual additives of methylammonium acetate (MAAc) and thio-semicarbazide (TSC), greatly improving the thermal stability of PSCs [29]. Besides, Zheng et al. passivated the surface defects in perovskite films using additive of quaternary ammonium halide anions and cations, thus enhancing the devices stability in moisture air [30]. In addition to the surface or bulk properties, the microstructure of perovskite films also plays an important role in device degradation [31–33]. # ABSTRACT

Spontaneous grain polymerization strategy is proposed to fabricate efficient and stable perovskite solar cells (PSCs) through the incorporation of polymerizable additive ethyl 2-cyanoacrylate (E2CA). E2CA lies in and chemically anchors to grain boundaries (GBs) owing to -CN and -C=O groups' coordination with  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , thus passivating the defects at GBs and leading to high devices efficiency of  $ 21.03\% $ . Importantly, E2CA in perovskite films will spontaneously polymerize to a hydrophobic polymer at GBs when exposed in moisture air, thus blocking GBs channel for moisture penetration and enhancing the moisture-resisting properties of perovskite films. As a result, PSCs with E2CA exhibit superior stability in moisture air (relative humidity:  $ 40 - 60\% $ ), retaining  $ \sim 90\% $  of the maximum efficiency after aging over  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $ . Even under high temperature  $ (85^{\circ}\mathrm{C}) $  in moisture air, non-encapsulated  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -E2CA devices still show good stability despite the burn-in degradation, retaining over  $ 90\% $  of the post burn-in efficiency after aging  $ 200\mathrm{h} $ .

With this respect, GBs issues should be well considered for the fabrication of stable PSCs. Generally, solution-processed perovskite films contain many GBs due to their polycrystalline nature. Increasing the grain size can reduce but not eliminate GBs as it is still a challenge to fabricate single-crystal perovskite films at present. As a result, this method can only improve the device stability to some extent. On the other hand, Li et al. reported a crystal crosslinking method to improve device stability by using butylphosphonic acid 4-ammonium chloride (4-ABPACl) additives [38]. 4-ABPACl acts as a crosslinker at GBs through  $ \mathrm{-PO(OH)_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{NH_3^+} $  terminal groups' hydrogen bonding with neighbouring grains. The resulted PSCs exhibited greatly improved stability, retaining  $ \sim 70\% $  of its initial efficiency after aging  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  in air. However, 4-ABPACl is unable to block moisture penetration through GBs due to its hydrophilic nature, thus limiting the further improvement in device stability. Recently, we proposed an in-situ crosslinking strategy to improve device stability by introducing a crosslinkable and hydrophobic additive of trimethylolpropane triacrylate (TMTA) into perovskite films [39]. The  $ \mathrm{C = O} $  groups in TMTA facilitate its chemically anchoring to GBs in perovskite films and the  $ \mathrm{C = C} $  groups in TMTA promote its crosslinking polymerization after thermal post-treatment. Here, we introduce a new additive of ethyl 2-cyanoacrylate (E2CA, Fig. 1a) to overcome the two problems mentioned above. First, to decrease the polymerization temperature of additive, a strong electron-withdrawing group of -CN is incorporated to the C=C groups in E2CA, facilitating its polymerization at low temperature without damaging perovskite films. E2CA can spontaneously polymerize to a hydrophobic polymer even at room temperature in moisture air (Fig. 1a), thus linking the neighbouring perovskite grains and enhancing their water-resisting property. Second, -CN groups provide another linking point, besides-C=O groups, for E2CA to chemically anchor to GBs in perovskite films (Fig. 1b) and thus enhance the interaction between E2CA and perovskite grains. This enhanced interaction strengthens E2CA's anchoring ability and avoids its shedding from GBs at high temperature, thus improving their heat-resisting property. As a result, efficient and stable PSCs are achieved with efficiency over 21%. The resulted PSCs retain ~90% of the maximum efficiency after aging over 1000 h in ambient air (relative humidity: 40–60%). Furthermore, PSCs with E2CA also show good thermal stability (85°C) even in moisture air without encapsulation, retaining over 90% of the “post burn-in” efficiency after 200 h.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

MAI and P3CT-N are synthesized in our lab according to previous report [40]. PbI $ _2 $ , E2CA and PCBM are purchased from Alfa Aesar, Sigma-Aldrich and American Dye Source respectively. The solvent DMF and DMSO are obtained from Sigma-Aldrich and Alfa Aesar, respectively.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

The ITO substrate (20 mm * 20 mm) is sequentially cleaned through ultrasonication in detergent, deionized water, acetone and isopropanol for 15 min respectively. The cleaned substrates are dried under N2 flow and then treated in O₂ plasma for 2 min. The P3CT-N solution (1 mg/mL in methanol) is deposited on ITO substrate at 4000 r.p.m for 30 s, and then annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. The perovskite precursor solution is prepared by mixing PbI₂ (668.5 mg) and MAI (230.6 mg) in 1 mL DMF:DMSO (4:1 vol ratio). For solution containing E2CA, different amount E2CA is added into the clear solution of PbI₂/MAI solution before fabrication. Perovskite layer is deposited using typical anti-solvent method in glovebox filled with N₂. The perovskite precursor solution is spin-coated on ITO/P3CT-N at 4800 r.p.m. for 20 s. During spin-coating, 300 μL chlorobenzene (Sigma-Aldrich) is dropped on the center of the substrate 12 s prior to the end of the program. The substrate is then annealed on hotplate at 80 °C for 5 min to promote perovskite crystallization. For MAPbI₃-E2CA, the perovskite films are further transferred to moisture air (relative humidity: 40–60%) for 10 min to promote E2CA's polymerization. PCBM solution (10 mg/mL in chlorobenzene) is spin-coated on perovskite layer at 2000 r.p.m. for 45 s to form electron transporting layer. Finally, the substrate is transferred into vacuum chamber; 20 nm C60, 8 nm BCP and 100 nm Cu are thermally evaporated under high vacuum (1 * 10⁻⁴ Pa). The active area, as defined by the overlap of Cu and ITO, is 0.06 and 0.09 cm².

# 2.3. Device characterization

The J-V curves are recorded using Keithley 2400 sourcemeter under the solar simulator (Newport Oriel Sol3A) with simulated AM 1.5 G illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). The light source is a 450 W xenon lamp calibrated by a standard Si reference solar cell (Newport, 91,150 V). Unless otherwise stated, The J-V curves are all measured in glovebox under forward scan from 1.2 V to -0.2 V. The EQE measurement is conducted in air using Newport quantum efficiency measurement system (ORIEL IQE 200TM) combined with a lock-in amplifier and 150 W xenon lamp. The light intensity at each wavelength is calibrated by one standard Si/Ge solar cell.

# 2.4. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR)

FTIR samples are prepared by scraping off perovskite layer from substrate and then mixed with pre-dried KBr powder. FTIR spectra are recorded in transmittance mode using IR spectrometer instrument (Thermo, Nicolet 6700).

# 2.5. Scanning electron microscope (SEM)

SEM images are recorded using field emission scanning electron microscope (Hitachi, S-4800) with an accelerating voltage of 8 kV. Cross-section SEM images are recorded with an accelerating voltage of 4–8 kV according to the sample states.

# 2.6. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

TEM images are recorded using JEOL2100, Japan. TEM samples are prepared by dropping perovskite solution (0.3 M) and then rapidly blowing dry on TEM grids. The samples are annealed at 100 °C for 1 h and then collected in glass Petri dish to avoid the possible damage of grids.

# 2.7. Photoluminescence (PL) spectra

PL spectra are recorded with a Fluorolog-Horiba fluorometer with excitation wavelength at  $ 450\mathrm{nm} $ . Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) decays are conducted with excitation wavelength at  $ 450\mathrm{nm} $  and emission wavelength at  $ 770\mathrm{nm} $ .

# 2.8. Air stability test

Air stability is recorded by storing the non-encapsulated devices in air with controlled humidity (40–60%) at room temperature (  $  \sim  $  25   $  {}^{\circ}  $  C). Before J-V measurement, the PSCs are put into a vacuum chamber for

~10 min to remove the moisture absorbed on surface.

# 2.9. Thermal stability test

Thermal stability is recorded by storing the non-encapsulated devices on a hotplate setting at   $  85\ °C  $   in air (relative humidity: 40–60%). The J-V curves are measured regularly by transferring PSCs into glovebox. Note that the PSCs are put into vacuum chamber for 5–10 min and cooled to room temperature before measurement.

# 2.10. Light stability test

Light stability is recorded by storing non-encapsulated devices under 1 sun illumination (white LED lamp) in air (relative humidity: 40–60%). To calibrate the LED lamp illumination intensity, a perovskite solar cell is first measured under the solar simulator (Newport Oriel Sol3A) with simulated AM 1.5G illumination (450 W xenon lamp) to obtained a   $  J_{sc}  $  . Then the perovskite solar cell is measured again under white LED lamp to reach the same   $  J_{sc}  $   through regulate the intensity of LED lamp.

# 3. Results and discussion

# 3.1. Spontaneous grain polymerization

Due to the existence of  $ -\mathrm{CN} $  groups, the  $ C = C $  bond in E2CA becomes more active so that just  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  in moisture air can initiate its polymerization even at room temperature (Fig. 1a), changing E2CA from viscous liquid to a compact solid. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) is conducted in  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -E2CA films before and after exposure in moisture air to verify their spontaneous polymerization (Fig. 1c). Pure E2CA exhibit two characteristic peaks of  $ -C = O $  ( $ \sim 1746~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ ) and  $ -C = C $  groups ( $ 1636~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ ). In  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -E2CA films, the peak from  $ -C = C $  groups gradually weakens until disappears with

# 3.2. Morphology and microstructure characterization

Morphology and microstructure depend on the nucleation and crystal growth process during perovskite formation. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) of perovskite films before and after annealing is conducted to examine the nucleation and growth process respectively. In MAPbI $ _3 $  films, nucleation only occurs at the interface of anti-solvent/perovskite layer, inducing non-uniform nucleation between surface and bulk. While in MAPbI $ _3 $ -E2CA, E2CA serves as a template and enables uniform heterogeneous nucleation in the whole perovskite film, just as previously reported PMMA-templated nucleation [41]. As shown in Fig. 2a–c, perovskite films before annealing gradually change from randomly mixed nanoparticles and nanorods to homogeneous nanorods with the incorporation of E2CA, indicating the gradually improved nucleation process [41]. Fig. 2d–i exhibits the top-view and cross-sectional SEM images of perovskite after annealing. MAPbI $ _3 $  films show inhomogeneous grain size ranging from 70 nm to 680 nm (Fig. 2j) with clearly observed GBs. When introducing E2CA, perovskite grains become much more homogeneous, ranging from 50 nm to 150 nm (Fig. 2l).

# 3.4. Photovoltaic device performance

To check the effectiveness of E2CA, we fabricate inverted PSCs with device architecture of ITO/P3CT-N [40,48] /Perovskite/PCBM/C60/BCP/Cu, as shown Fig. 5a. MAPbI $ _3 $  devices exhibit an efficiency of  $ 19.43\% $  with an open-circuit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{oc}}) $  of  $ 1.10\mathrm{V} $ , short-circuit current density  $ (J_{\mathrm{sc}}) $  of  $ 22.66\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  and fill factor (FF) of  $ 78.2\% $  (Fig. 5b). When incorporating E2CA, the devices efficiency increases first and then rapidly decreases with the increasing concentration of E2CA (Fig. S2). The best devices performance appears at E2CA concentration of  $ 5\mathrm{mg/mL} $ . MAPbI $ _3 $ -E2CA  $ (5\mathrm{mg/mL}) $  devices exhibit highest efficiency of  $ 21.03\% $  with a  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  of  $ 1.12\mathrm{V}, J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  of  $ 23.02\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  and FF of  $ 81.4\% $  (Fig. 5c). In addition to efficiency, the  $ J-V $  hysteresis is also studied. MAPbI $ _3 $ -E2CA devices show negligible hysteresis (Fig. 5c) with maximum power output of  $ 20.71\% $  (Fig. 5c inset). While MAPbI $ _3 $  devices show discernable hysteresis (Fig. 5b) with maximum power output of  $ 18.39\% $  (Fig. 5b inset), which should be caused by the GB defects in perovskite films as confirmed in Fig. 4. Specific photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table S1. Ideality factor  $ (n) $  is estimated to further understand the recombination mechanism in PSCs. Both MAPbI $ _3 $  and MAPbI $ _3 $ -E2CA devices exhibit  $ n $  value around 1.2 (Fig. S3), indicating that Shockley-Read-Hall recombination via bulk defects is dominate in our PSCs [49]. Therefore, space charge-limited current (SCLC) measurement is conducted to quantify the trap density in MAPbI $ _3 $ -E2CA and MAPbI $ _3 $  films. As shown in Fig. 5d, the dark current-voltage response of electron-only devices exhibits three regions: Ohmic region (olive) at low bias, trap-filled limited region (orange) at intermediate bias and SCLC region (red) at high bias [50]. In trap-filled limited region, the current density rapidly increases nonlinearly, indicating the transition to trap-filled limit (TFL), a region where all trap

states are filled by injected electrons [50]. The onset voltage   $  V_{TFL}  $   is related to the trap state density (  $  n_{t}  $  ) according to formula of   $  V_{\mathrm{TFL}} = (e n_{\mathrm{L}} L^{2}) / (2 e \varepsilon_{0})  $  , where e is elementary charge, L is perovskite film thickness,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the dielectric constants of perovskite (  $  \varepsilon = 28.8  $  ),   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity. Trap density (  $  n_{t}  $  ) can be calculated through the measurement of   $  V_{TFL}  $  . The   $  V_{TFL}  $   in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films is 0.675 V, which is obviously larger than that in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -E2CA (  $  V_{TFL}  $   of 0.492 V), indicating the more trap states in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films. The trap density in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   is 1.062 * 10  $  ^{16}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $   and the trap density in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -E2CA is 0.774 * 10  $  ^{16}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  . The lower trap density agrees well with the better devices performance in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -E2CA. Fig. 5e shows the external quantum efficiency (EQE) of our PSCs. All the devices exhibit high EQE value especially in the visible region from 420 nm to 740 nm (>80% EQE), agreeing with their high   $  J_{sc}  $   extracted from J-V curves. Statistical analysis of device efficiency is summarized in Fig. 5f among 50 separated PSCs. High reproducibility is observed in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -E2CA devices with an average efficiency of 19.51 ± 0.62%, which is 7% higher than that of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   devices (average efficiency of 18.23 ± 0.52%).

# 3.5. Devices stability

Apart from efficiency, devices stability is another important issue for PSCs. For MAPbI $ _3 $ -E2CA, E2CA will rapidly polymerize to a compact polymer when exposed in moisture air, thus crosslinking neighbouring perovskite grains just like but better than previously reported 4-ABPACl [38] due to the hydrophobic nature of E2CA itself. Contact angle measurement further confirms this point as MAPbI $ _3 $ -E2CA film shows much larger contact angle with H $ _2 $ O than MAPbI $ _3 $  (Fig. S4). As shown in Fig. 6a, MAPbI $ _3 $ -E2CA devices exhibit excellent stability in moisture air (relative humidity: 40–60%), retaining 88.3% of the maximum efficiency after over 1000 h (non-normalized data and specific photovoltaic parameters in Fig. S5). While the stability is much inferior in control MAPbI $ _3 $  devices and < 20% efficiency can be retained merely after 500 h. Thermal condition will accelerate the degradation of PSCs, especially in moisture air. For example, control MAPbI $ _3 $  devices lose 95% of their efficiency with 100 h (Fig. 6b) when exposed at 85 °C in air (relative humidity 40–60%). In comparison, MAPbI $ _3 $ -E2CA devices exhibit much better stability under the same condition. Despite rapid burn-in degradation at first 30 h, almost no degradation is observed in the next 170 h in MAPbI $ _3 $ -E2CA PSCs, retaining 92% of the post burn-in efficiency (68% of initial efficiency) after aging 200 h (non-normalized data and specific photovoltaic parameters shown in Fig. S6). Apart from moisture and thermal stability, the light stability of PSCs is also improved in MAPbI $ _3 $ -E2CA devices. Under 1 sun illumination in moisture air (relative humidity: 40–60%), non-encapsulated MAPbI $ _3 $ -E2CA devices still retains over 60% of initial efficiency after 60 h, while the MAPbI $ _3 $  devices degrade completely (Fig. S7a, images of devices degradation shown in Fig. 7b).

In perovskite films, GBs serve as penetration channels for moisture, oxygen or other ambient atmosphere and the degradation usually starts from GBs and then accelerates to the whole films [31]. This is the reason why MAPbI $ _3 $  single crystal (no GBs existence) can be stored in moisture air as long as 2–3 years without notable degradation [16]. However, for polycrystalline perovskite films, GBs is unavoidable and always exist. Therefore, to achieve stable PSCs, GBs must be well engineered. In our spontaneous grain polymerization method, E2CA lies at GBs (HR-TEM in Fig. 3) and chemically anchors to GBs owing to its coordination effect with PbI $ _2 $  (FTIR in Fig. 4). When exposed in air, E2CA will spontaneously polymerize to a

compact polymer, protecting energetically unstable GBs and blocking the penetration channels of moisture due to its hydrophobic nature (Fig. 7a). SEM is used to track the evolution of perovskite films (Fig. 7b) when exposed in air (relative humidity: 40–60%, room temperature). For MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ -E2CA, no obvious change is observed in SEM images and the perovskite grains are still clearly distinguished even after aging 400 h. In addition, the color change from dark to yellow (Fig. 7 inset) and the appearance of PbI  $  _{2}  $  peak under X-ray diffraction (XRD, Fig. S8) both indicate the rapid degradation of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  films. While these phenomenon are not observed in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ -E2CA films, confirming the good film stability and agreeing with their improved device stability.

# 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, we introduce a spontaneous grain polymerization method to fabricate efficient and stable PSCs through the incorporation of polymerizable additive E2CA. FTIR and HR-TEM results indicate that E2CA lies in and chemically anchors to GBs in perovskite films owing to its coordination effect with   $  PbI_{2}  $  , thus effectively passivating the defects at GBs. The PSCs with E2CA reach over 21% efficiency (stabilized power output of 20.71%) without obvious hysteresis.
