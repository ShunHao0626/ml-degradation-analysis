# 1. INTRODUCTION

Within the past decade, PSCs have developed at an unprecedented rate and achieved an astounding power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.2% comparable to commercial silicon solar cells.  $  ^{1,2}  $   The high efficiency derives from many desirable optoelectronic properties of perovskite, such as the high absorption coefficient,  $  ^{3}  $   long intrinsic carrier recombination lifetime,  $  ^{4}  $   and high carrier mobility.  $  ^{5}  $   Most certificated PCEs are achieved by the unique combination of mesostructured architecture and mixed-cation and halide perovskite composition, integrating the advantage of efficient electron extraction, enhanced defect tolerance, reduced recombination and improved stability.  $  ^{6,7,8,9}  $   Nevertheless, devices based on these state-of-the-art structures still have

In general, charge accumulation is likely to occur within the perovskite and at the adjacent interfaces.  $  ^{12}  $   Since the high defect tolerance of perovskite materials  $  ^{13}  $   and the inefficient charge extraction of the n-type collection layer,  $  ^{14,15}  $   charge accumulation mainly generates at the ETL/perovskite interface.  $  ^{16,12,17}  $     $  TiO_{2}  $   based ETL is still the most common choice for high-efficiency devices. Theoretical calculations indicate that electrons are easily trapped by oxygen vacancies and Ti(IV) ions.  $  ^{18,19}  $   The accumulated charge at the   $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface is known to create a potential barrier and hence weaken electron transport, which might increase the risk of non-irradiative charge recombination and lead to hysteresis behavior, deteriorating the device performance.  $  ^{12}  $   Most importantly, the light soaking causes severe device degradation in ambient environment when charges are accumulated at the interface. Many researches are recently focusing on the enhancement of device performance and stability by eliminating the charge accumulation at ETL/perovskite interface.

Several approaches have been reported to promote charge separation and mitigate charge accumulation at   $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface. Employing an interfacial modification layer was firstly considered to accelerate electron extraction. For example, fullerene (  $  C_{60}  $  ),  $  ^{12,14}  $   graphene,  $  ^{20}  $   and ionic salts,  $  ^{21,22}  $   have demonstrated effective electron extraction capability in PSCs compared with single   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer. Another effective approach is to introduce electric dipole layer at the interface. Previous studies suggest that the photogenerated carriers can be effectively extracted to avoid charge accumulation and recombination at trapping sites by applying an external reverse bias.  $  ^{29}  $   Therefore, introducing interfacial dipoles can accelerate charge transfer while simultaneously avoid damage to the real device, which has been a promising solution for overcoming the charge trapping issue. Specifically designed interlayers of tris(2,4,6-trimethyl-3-(pyridin-3-yl)phenyl) borane,  $  ^{30}  $   ionic liquids,  $  ^{31,32}  $   and 3-(1-pyridinio)-1-propanesulfonate  $  ^{33}  $   have been reported to create strong interfacial dipole moments to facilitate charge transfer and improve stability. However, most of these modification molecules are applied into a planar PSC, the large number of micro interfaces existing in the mesoporous structured PSC should also be taken into consideration. In addition to the charge extraction, the defect passivation of dipole molecules and their effects on crystal growth in the mesoporous structure are expected to be enhanced for further eliminating the interfacial charge accumulation.

Herein, a zwitterion of TMAO was employed as the interfacial dipole to anchor both mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   and perovskite. The created driving force at the interface significantly mitigates the charge accumulation not only by promoting electron extraction and transfer, but also by reducing defects at   $  TiO_{2}  $   and perovskite surfaces. This work suggests that the use of dipole layer is a promising strategy to overcome the interfacial charge accumulation for achieving high-performance, hysteresis-free and stable PSCs.

Based on the modified interface, PSCs with different TMAO concentrations were fabricated to explore the effects of interface dipoles on the device performance.   $  FA_{0.85}MA_{0.15}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $  -based devices were prepared employing a regular mesoporous structure (Figure 1a). The typical current density-voltage (J-V) curves of devices with different TMAO concentrations (0 mg/mL, 1 mg/mL, 2 mg/ml and 3 mg/ml) are shown in Figure 3a, and the corresponding photovoltaic properties are summarized in Table 1. To confirm the reduced charge accumulation contributing to the improvement of the photovoltaic performance, capacitance-voltage (C-V) measurement was conducted. # 3. CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, we have demonstrated that inserting an interface dipolar layer is an effective and facile strategy to eliminate charge accumulation at the ETL/perovskite interface and unravel the importance of eliminated charge accumulation on the enhancement of device efficiency and stability. The dipole molecule, TMAO, employed as a connecting bridge between mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and perovskite induces the improved energy level alignment, better perovskite growth in the mesoporous and reduced energetic barriers of electron transport, thereby improving charge transfer and diminishing charge densities at the interface. As a result, the interfacial ionic defects and carrier recombination are considerably suppressed. Through optimizing the concentration of zwitterion, the device yields a PCE value of  $ 21.77\% $  with almost no hysteresis and shows a longevity in ambient air under continuous illumination. The efficient electron transfer and eliminated interfacial charges account for the remarkable enhancement of device stability and the reduction of hysteresis effect, which are more attractive for the future commercialization of PSCs.
