# Facile Surface Modification upon  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  Films Leading to Simultaneously Improved Efficiency and Stability of Inverted Perovskite Solar Cells

Hongmei Zhu, Boyuan Huang, Shaohang Wu, Zhenzhong Xiong, Jiangyu Li* and Wei Chen*

Ms. H. Zhu, Dr. S. Wu, Mr. Z. Xiong, Prof. W. Chen

Wuhan National Laboratory for Optoelectronics, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, Luoyu Road 1037, Wuhan 430074, China

Mr. B. Huang, Prof. J. Li

Shenzhen Key Laboratory of Nanobiomechanics, Shenzhen Institutes of Advanced Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shenzhen 518055, Guangdong, China

*Corresponding Authors, E-mail: jjli@uw.edu (J. Li), wnlochenwei@mail.hust.edu.cn (W. Chen)

# Abstract

The intrinsic stability issues of organo-metal halides perovskite materials induced by moisture, heat, light and electrical field constitute big obstacles preventing perovskite solar cells (PSCs) from industrialized application. Herein, we have demonstrated that when a hydrophobic organic acid molecule, 4-Dimethylaminobenzoic acid, is introduced to modify the top surface of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  thin film, it can realize a facile way to simultaneously enhance stability and efficiency of the corresponding PSCs. This surface modification can not only improve the perovskite film's electronic quality but also provide it with certain water-proof capability. Accordingly, the efficiency of the inverted PSC is improved from  $ 17.43\% $  to  $ 19.87\% $ . More encouragingly, after 1 sun light soaking,  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  thermal aging, and high humidity ( $ 70\% $ ) aging for 1000 hours, the modified  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  based PSCs can retain  $ 91\% $ ,  $ 81\% $  and  $ 98\% $  efficiency, respectively.

# Introduction

The newly emerged photovoltaic technology, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) using organo-metal halides perovskites   $  (\mathrm{AMX}_{3}, \mathrm{A} = \mathrm{Cs}^{+}, \mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}^{+} (\mathrm{MA}^{+})  $   or   $  H_{2}NCH=NH_{2}^{+} (FA^{+})  $  ;   $  M = Sn^{2+}  $  ,   $  Pb^{2+}  $  ,   $  Ge^{2+}  $  ;   $  X = Cl^{-}  $  ,   $  Br^{-}  $  ,   $  I^{-}  $  ) as light absorbers, have attracted widespread attention owing to the high efficiency, low-cost and ease of manufacture features. Beginning with 3.8% in 2009,  $  ^{1}  $   the state-of-the-art power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has rapidly increased to 22.7%.  $  ^{2}  $   The fast PCE evolution of PSCs is unprecedented in the long developing history of photovoltaics, which should be largely attributed to excellent photoelectric properties of the solution processed perovskite films, such as high optical absorption coefficient, small exciton binding energy, and long charge carriers diffusion lengths.  $  ^{3-6}  $   However, it has been widely recognized that the low-temperature (typically in the range 80–150 °C) deposited perovskite films normally lack chemical and structural stability, which will rapidly degrade under the stresses caused by moisture, heat, light and electrical field.  $  ^{7}  $   For example,   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   has been proved to be intrinsically unstable at 85 °C in the ambient or   $  N_{2}  $   atmospheres.  $  ^{8}  $   While an alternative light harvester,   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  , which possesses broader absorption spectrum to enable high PCE of over 20%,  $  ^{9}  $   has been proved to be lack of structural stability.  $  ^{10}  $

Through composition engineering, long-chain amines, such as 5–aminolamidic acid,  $  ^{11}  $  polyetherimide,  $  ^{12}  $   $  CF_{3}CH_{2}NH_{3}^{+}  $ ,  $  ^{13}  $   $  C_{6}H_{5}(CH_{2})_{2}NH_{3}^{+}  $ ,  $  ^{14}  $   $  C_{4}H_{9}NH_{3}^{+}  $ ,  $  ^{15}  $  were introduced to partially replace short-chain  $  MA^{+}/FA^{+}  $  cations, converting the conventional perovskites with three-dimensional crystal structure to lower

But unfortunately, such stability enhancement was normally achieved by sacrificing efficiency of the corresponding PSCs, which was due to the degraded photoelectric properties of the low-dimensional perovskite films. $ ^{16} $  To date, incorporation of inorganic  $ \mathrm{Cs}^{+17} $  and/or  $ \mathrm{Rb}^+ $  cations $ ^{18} $  into  $ \mathrm{MA}^+ / \mathrm{FA}^+ $  based mixed perovskites, have been reported with improved structural or thermal stability without sacrificing efficiency very much.

Beyond tuning the bulk perovskite films' compositions, it is known that appropriate surface modification can also largely improve the film's and the corresponding device's stability. Various kinds of surface modification layers have been implemented in PSCs, such as polystyrene, $ ^{19} $  phenylalkylamine, $ ^{20} $  amino-functionalized graphene, $ ^{21} $  quaternary ammonium halide $ ^{22} $  etc. Typically, these surface modification strategies can just change the surface property of the films without largely tuning the bulk film's photoelectric properties. Therefore, the improved stability can be achieved without efficiency sacrificing. Even in some cases, appropriate surface modification can passivate the surface traps of perovskite films and/or help to block the interfacial shunt-paths, which can facilitate interfacial charge transfer and prevent interfacial recombination, and hence improve the device's efficiency. $ ^{23} $

With the dual aims of simultaneously enhancing efficiency and stability in mind, we introduced a hydrophobic organic acid molecule, 4-Dimethylaminobenzoic Acid (4-DMABA), to modify the top surface of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film. # Results and discussion

Spin coating of 4-DMABA molecules on the perovskite film can protect it from ruining by the environmental aspects. A schematic diagram shown in Figure 1a illustrates the interaction mode of 4-DMABA molecules with perovskite. 4-DMABA is chosen because of its molecular structure (Figure S1, Supporting Information). It is thought that the carboxyl group of 4-DMABA can strongly coordinate with the positively charged ions (  $  Pb^{2+}  $   sites) on the film surface, providing a possible passivation effect of surface traps.  $  ^{25}  $   Meanwhile, the hydrophobic dimethylamino group can provide the modified film with certain water-proof capability.

To confirm the formation of coordination bonds between 4-DMABA and perovskite film, we conducted the Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy measurement on the powder samples of 4-DMABA,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ , and 4-DMABA/MAPbI₃ mixture. The powder samples were prepared by scrapping the pristine and surface modified  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films from the FTO glass substrates. The results are shown in Figure 1b. SEM images shown in Figure 1c and 1d reflect that after surface modification, the morphology of the top surface of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film has largely changed. Photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy is a simple but effective way to ascertain the surface passivation effect. Generally, radiative recombination between charge carriers through intra-bandgap trap states will lead to a red-shift of the PL emission peak in comparison to that from the band edge transition. Surface passivation could fill the trap states which would enable recovery of the bandgap and lead to a blue shift of the PL peak.  $  ^{27}  $   Figure 1e shows the steady-state PL spectra for the perovskite films with and without surface modification by 4-DMABA. It can be observed that the PL intensity of the 4-DMABA modified MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film is 43% higher than that of the pristine MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film. Peak position is blue shifted from 771 nm for the pristine MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film to 767 nm for the modified one. PL intensity enhancement and peak position blue shift collectively indicate that the electronic quality of the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film is enhanced due to surface modification.  $  ^{28}  $   It is proposed that the deep trap states (the positively charged under-coordinated Pb  $  ^{2+}  $  ) on the perovskite film surface have been

In order to further clarify the electronic quality improvement and interfacial charge transfer properties, we resorted to time-resolved PL (TRPL) spectroscopy measurement. The carrier-recombination lifetime measured by TRPL is commonly taken as a hallmark of semiconductor film quality, with longer decay lifetimes as indicators of better material quality.  $  ^{30}  $   As shown in Figure 1f, it can be found that the fitted PL lifetimes for the pristine and modified MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films are 102 ns and 166 ns, respectively. Longer carrier lifetime is likely due to the elimination of trap-mediated non-radiative recombination in the modified MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film. For the pristine and modified MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films after covered by a thin layer of PCBM, the PL lifetimes decreased to 6.29 ns and 10 ns, respectively. The PL quenching is very efficient in both samples, corresponding to the interfacial charge transfer efficiencies as high as 90.2% and 96.2% for the perovskite films before and after surface modification.  $  ^{31}  $   This is in consistent with the steady-state PL spectra.

# Conclusion

In summary, we have developed a facile surface modification way by using a hydrophobic organic acid molecule (4-DMABA) to simultaneously realize high efficiency and stability of the inverted PSCs. The efficiency improvement from originally  $ 17.43\% $  to  $ 19.87\% $  is demonstrated, which could be ascribed to surface traps passivation and recombination suppression associated with the surface

Page 15 of 29

Journal of Materials Chemistry A

modification; while the stability improvement is demonstrated to be attributed to the hydrophobic surface capping layer with certain water-proof capability, which has been directly proved by an in-situ pc-AFM characterization along with aging time under controlled light and humidity. Encouragingly, after 1 sun light soaking, 85 °C thermal aging, and high humidity (70%) aging with/without encapsulation for 1000 hours, the modified MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  based PSCs retained 91%, 81% and 98%/61% of their initial efficiency, respectively. These data indicate that through the surface modification on perovskite, even the pure MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  based PSCs could be largely approaching to the standard stability test for commercialized solar cells. Furthermore, we believe this facile surface modification could be applied in the upgraded perovskite materials system, such as FA  $  ^{+}  $ /Cs  $  ^{+}  $  based perovskites; by doing so, a more stable PSC could be expected, which may finally pass the accelerated aging tests.

# Experimental Section

Materials: All the chemical reagents were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used as received without further purification.

Device Fabrication: Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) coated glass (Pilkington TEC 15,  $ 15\Omega $  / square) was etched using  $ 0.1\mathrm{M} $  HCl solution diluted in milliQ water without Zn powder. The etched substrates were cleaned with  $ 2\% $  hellmanex diluted in the milliQ water, rinsed with milliQ water/ethanol, and then dried with clean air. A p-type NiMgLiO compact layer ( $ \sim 20~\mathrm{nm} $  thick) was then deposited on the FTO glass by spray pyrolysis according to our previous work.[47] The anti-solvent method was used for the perovskite layer deposition:  $ 80\mu \mathrm{L} $  DMF/DMSO (4: 1 by volume) solution of

$ \mathrm{PbI}_2 / \mathrm{MAI} $  (1.05: 1 by molar ratio, with the concentration of  $ 1.5\mathrm{M} $ ) was spin-coated at a rotation speed of 5000 rpm for 30 seconds, followed by quickly drop-casting of  $ 200\mu \mathrm{L} $  diethyl ether as anti-solvent within 10 seconds. The pristine  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film was accordingly formed by annealing at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 10\mathrm{min} $ . For the modified  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film preparation,  $ 80\mu \mathrm{L} $  isopropanol solution of 4-DMABA with the concentration of  $ 2\mathrm{mg}\mathrm{mL}^{-1} $  was spin-coated onto the pristine  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film at a rotation speed of 3000 rpm for 30s, which was then annealed at  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 10\mathrm{min} $ . After the perovskite films were prepared, a chlorobenzene solution of PCBM ( $ 20\mathrm{mg~mL}^{-1} $ ) was spin-coated on top of them at the rotation speed of  $ 1500\mathrm{rpm} $  for 30 seconds. Subsequently, the saturated methanol solution of BCP was spin-coated on top of the PCBM layer at  $ 6000\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 30\mathrm{s} $ . At last, one batch of films were transferred to the evaporator chamber,  $ 100\mathrm{nm} $  thick Ag electrodes were deposited under high vacuum ( $ < 3\times 10^{-4}\mathrm{Pa} $ ).

Characterizations: SEM images were obtained using a Nova Nano 450 scanning electron microscope (FEI Co., Netherlands). XRD characterization was performed on an Empyrean X-ray diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (PANalytical B.V. Co., Netherlands). The infrared spectra were obtained on a VERTEX 70 Infrared Fourier transform microscope (Bruker Co., Germany). The PL spectra were recorded on an Edinburgh FLS920 fluorescence spectrometer (Edinburgh Co., UK). The local photocurrent-measurements (pcAFM) were performed using a commercial AFM system (Asylum Research MFP-3D Bio) with Nanosensors PPP-EFM conductive probes, the spring constant of which are about 2.8 N m  $  ^{-1}  $  . A solar simulator (Oriel, model 9119) with AM 1.5G filter (Oriel, model 91192) was used to give an irradiance

The long-term stability was measured with a CHI1000c multi-channel electrochemistry workstation (Chenhua, Co., China).

# Table of Contents Entry

A facile surface modification strategy based on a hydrophobic modifier (4-DMABA) allows to simultaneously enhance stability and efficiency of the inverted perovskite solar cells. The efficiency improvement is ascribed to surface traps passivation and recombination suppression, while the stability improvement is attributed to the hydrophobic surface capping layer with certain water-proof capability.
