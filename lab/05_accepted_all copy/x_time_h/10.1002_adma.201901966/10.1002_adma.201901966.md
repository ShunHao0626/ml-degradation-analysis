# Low-Dimensional Perovskites with Diammonium and Monoammonium Alternant Cations for High-Performance Photovoltaics

Pengwei Li, Chao Liang, Xiao-Long Liu, Fengyu Li, Yiqiang Zhang, Xiao-Tao Liu, Hao Gu, Xiaotian Hu, Guichuan Xing,* Xutang Tao, and Yanlin Song*

Low-dimensional Ruddlesden–Popper (LDRP) perovskites are a current theme in solar energy research as researchers attempt to fabricate stable photovoltaic devices from them. However, poor exciton dissociation and insufficiently fast charge transfer slows the charge extraction in these devices, resulting in inferior performance. 1,4-Butanediamine (BEA)-based low-dimensional perovskites are designed to improve the carrier extraction efficiency in such devices. Structural characterization using single-crystal X-ray diffraction reveals that these layered perovskites are formed by the alternating ordering of diammonium (BEA $  ^{2+}  $ ) and monoammonium (MA $  ^{+}  $ ) cations in the interlayer space (B-ACI) with the formula (BEA) $  _{0.5}  $ MA $  _{n}  $ Pb $  _{n}  $ I $  _{3n+1}  $ . Compared to the typical LDRP counterparts, these B-ACI perovskites deliver a wider light absorption window and lower exciton binding energies with a more stable layered perovskite structure. Additionally, ultrafast transient absorption indicates that B-ACI perovskites exhibit a narrow distribution of quantum well widths, leading to a barrier-free and balanced carrier transport pathway with enhanced carrier diffusion (electron and hole) length over 350 nm. A perovskite solar cell incorporating BEA ligands achieves record efficiencies of 14.86% for (BEA) $  _{0.5}  $ MA $  _{3}  $ Pb $  _{3}  $ I $  _{10}  $  and 17.39% for (BEA) $  _{0.5}  $ Cs $  _{0.15}  $ (FA $  _{0.83}  $ MA $  _{0.17}  $ ) $  _{2.85}  $ Pb $  _{3}  $ (I $  _{0.83}  $ Br $  _{0.17}  $ ) $  _{10}  $  without hysteresis. Furthermore, the triple cations B-ACI devices can retain over 90% of their initial power conversion efficiency when stored under ambient atmospheric conditions for 2400 h and show no significant degradation under constant illumination for over 500 h.

Moreover, the LDRP perovskites are generally composed of multiple quantum wells (QWs), resulting in slow charge transfer between perovskite QWs that reduced carrier extraction efficiency.  $  ^{[24-27]}  $

Recently, low-dimensional perovskites formed by alternately ordering of guanidinium and methylammonium (MA) cations in the interlayer space (ACI) has been a reasonable strategy to increasing PCEs of low-dimensional PSCs. Compared with the common LDRP perovskites, ACI perovskites show narrower bandgaps ( $ E_g $ ) that enhanced optical absorption, leading to the PCE over 14%. $ ^{[28]} $  However, the improvement of PCE is at the expense of stability due to the reduced content of hydrophobic slabs. Another approach is to incorporate diammonium cations into 3D perovskite, developing a series of Dion-Jacobson (DJ) phase low-dimensional perovskite. $ ^{[29,30]} $  This special structure with reducing interlayer distance of inorganic slabs weaken the quantum confinement effect with enhanced PCE beyond 13%. $ ^{[29-31]} $  Meanwhile, the spacer of DJ perovskite with two amino groups at both ends can form hydrogen bonds with the inorganic slabs without any gaps, showing better stability compared to ACI and LDRP perovskite (van der Waals gap). $ ^{[29,30]} $  Nevertheless, the degree of dissociation of photogenerated carriers in DJ perovskite is much lower than that in ACI perovskite. $ ^{[28,32]} $  Furthermore, a spread of Egs in these low-dimensional perovskites due to varying degree of quantum confinement will lead to carrier localization and mobility reduction. Therefore, the conflict between stability and efficiency is a fundamental challenge for low-dimensional perovskite photovoltaic devices.

Here, we propose a strategy to improve light absorption capability and carrier extraction efficiency by designing 1,4-butanediamine (BEA) as the insulating organic cation to form a new type of low-dimensional perovskite with the formula of  $ (\mathrm{BEA})_{0.5}\mathrm{MA}_n\mathrm{Pb}_n\mathrm{I}_{3n + 1} $ , which contains diammonium  $ (\mathrm{BEA}^{2+}) $  and monoammonium  $ (\mathrm{MA}^{+}) $  alternating cations in the interlayer space (B-ACI). Unlike the traditional ACI perovskites, B-ACI perovskites have hydrogen bond (between BEA and inorganic slabs), which result in more stable layered perovskite structure.[30] In addition, B-ACI perovskites adopted fewer long-chain organic ligands, which can significantly improve carrier transport performance compared with DJ perovskites.[28] Therefore, B-ACI perovskite has the advantages of both ACI and DJ perovskites. Single-crystal X-ray diffraction reveals the  $ (\mathrm{BEA})_{0.5}\mathrm{MA}_n\mathrm{Pb}_n\mathrm{I}_{3n + 1} $  have higher crystal symmetry and shorter interlayer distance that decrease the Egs compared with  $ (\mathrm{BA})_2 $   $ \mathrm{MA}_{n - 1}\mathrm{Pb}_n\mathrm{I}_{3n + 1} $  (BA:  $ n $ -butylammonium,  $ \mathrm{C_4H_9NH_3} $ ) perovskite counterparts. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) and ultraviolet-visible absorption also confirm that B-ACI perovskites have wide absorption window. Density functional theory (DFT) suggests that  $ (\mathrm{BEA})_{0.5}\mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Pb}_3\mathrm{I}_{10} $  is a direct bandgap semiconductor with lower exciton binding energy compared with  $ (\mathrm{BA})_2\mathrm{MA}_2\mathrm{Pb}_3\mathrm{I}_{10} $ . Meanwhile, B-ACI perovskite constructs a narrow distribution of quantum well widths, leading to efficient carrier transport pathway. As a result, the barriers of charge transport are efficiently reduced, making balanced carriers (electron and hole) diffusion length over  $ 350~\mathrm{nm} $  with enhanced mobility. Sequentially, PSCs prepared with  $ (\mathrm{BEA})_{0.5}\mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Pb}_3\mathrm{I}_{10} $  exhibit an improved PCE of  $ 14.86\% $ . Furthermore, the mixed cations (FA, MA, and Cs) PSCs with

BEA as the interlayer spacer delivered a record PCE of 17.39%, which is the highest efficiency for ACI perovskite devices. In addition, the B-ACI perovskite devices exhibit higher stability under humidity, heating and light soaks than that of LDRP perovskite devices due to alternating hydrogen bonding interactions between BEA  $  ^{2+}  $  cations and inorganic slabs.

As shown in Figure 1a, we synthesized the typical flake B-ACI perovskite and selected the large single crystals (Figure S1, Supporting Information) to analyze the structure of the perovskite. The structure of (BEA)  $  _{0.5}  $  MA  $  _{3}  $  Pb  $  _{3}  $  I  $  _{10}  $   was solved and refined by using single crystal X-ray crystallography and the relevant data are presented in Table S1 (Supporting Information). As shown in Figure 1b, (BEA)  $  _{0.5}  $  MA  $  _{3}  $  Pb  $  _{3}  $  I  $  _{10}  $   belongs to the triclinic space group of P-1 with lattice parameters of a = 8.7916(4) Å, b = 8.8098(3) Å, and c = 22.3335(10) Å. The alternating arrangement of BEA and MA, which act as spacers between the perovskite layers, enables (BEA)  $  _{0.5}  $  MA  $  _{3}  $  Pb  $  _{3}  $  I  $  _{10}  $   to have typical 2D B-ACI perovskite characteristics. These results are also suitable for low-n values perovskite (n = 1 and 2; Figure S2, Supporting Information). In Figure S3 (Supporting Information), the experimental X-ray diffraction (XRD) data of (BEA)  $  _{0.5}  $  MA  $  _{3}  $  Pb  $  _{3}  $  I  $  _{10}  $   power is in good agreement with the theoretical calculation determined by single-crystal XRD analysis, indicating successfully obtained B-ACI perovskite by simple combining (BEA)I  $  _{2}  $ , MAI, and PbI  $  _{2}  $   in a stoichiometric ratio (0.5:3:3). In addition, Density Functional Theory (Figure 1c) calculation manifests that (BEA)  $  _{0.5}  $  MA  $  _{3}  $  Pb  $  _{3}  $  I  $  _{10}  $   is a direct bandgap semiconductor (at Z point, E  $  _{g}  $   = 1.589 eV, close to the experimental value in the following discussion).

We then studied the morphological evolution and crystal orientation of B-ACI perovskite films. The morphology of the B-ACI perovskite was investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) as shown in Figure 1d. It exhibits a full coverage and single crystal-like film with typical 2D perovskite features with large grains. Cross-sectional SEM image of the smooth B-ACI perovskite film is shown in Figure 1d (right, down) with the thickness about 350 nm that matching carrier diffusion length (in the following discussion). As shown in Figure 1e (X-ray diffraction), the   $  (\mathrm{BEA})_{0.5}\mathrm{MA}_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{3}\mathrm{I}_{10}  $   film exhibits excellent crystallinity with three obvious diffraction peaks of (111), (202) and (313), which represent three dominant planes of B-ACI   $  (\mathrm{BEA})_{0.5}\mathrm{MA}_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{3}\mathrm{I}_{10}  $   perovskite. This script is also evident in low n value of   $  (\mathrm{BEA})_{0.5}\mathrm{MA}_{n}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{3n+1}  $   (n = 1 and 2; Figure S4, Supporting Information). To further probe the   $  (\mathrm{BEA})_{0.5}\mathrm{MA}_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{3}\mathrm{I}_{10}  $   orientation with respect to the substrate, 2D grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) measurement was carried out. As shown in Figure 1f, the   $  (\mathrm{BEA})_{0.5}\mathrm{MA}_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{3}\mathrm{I}_{10}  $   film exhibits sharp, discrete Bragg spots along the same rings, indicating a textured polycrystalline film in which the crystal domains are oriented with their (101) planes parallel to the substrate surface (Figure S5, Supporting Information). This crystal orientation is favor of efficient separation of carriers.  $  ^{[5]}  $

Considering the actual conditions in the operation of solar cells, we conducted stability tests under atmospheric humidity, continuous illumination and high temperature using   $  (\mathrm{BEA})_{0.5}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.15}(\mathrm{FA}_{0.83}\mathrm{MA}_{0.17})_{2.85}\mathrm{Pb}_{3}(\mathrm{I}_{0.83}\mathrm{Br}_{0.17})_{10}  $   and   $  (\mathrm{BA})_{2}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.1}(\mathrm{FA}_{0.83}\mathrm{MA}_{0.17})_{1.9}\mathrm{Pb}_{3}(\mathrm{I}_{0.83}\mathrm{Br}_{0.17})_{10}  $   perovskite solar cells, respectively. Figure 4d shows the evolution of normalized PCE over time for encapsulated devices stored in ambient atmospheric under dark (humidity   $  \approx35\%  $  ). B-ACI devices exhibit good environmental stability, retaining 90% of their initial PCEs after aging 2400 h. However, LDRP only retain 85% their initial PCEs. Figure 4e illustrates the continuous illumination stability of the devices. The B-ACI devices maintain 93% of their initial efficiency under continuous illumination after 500 h at room temperature. However, the LDRP devices only maintain 82% of their initial PCEs. Thermal stability of the devices is also executed under 85 °C without encapsulation as shown in Figure 4f. The B-ACI devices lost 80% of their initial efficiency after 100 h. Contrastively, LDRP devices completely lost their function after 90 h. In addition, the stability against moisture is also performed via placing the devices in a humidity chamber (relative humidity of 85%) with simple encapsulation. As shown in Figure 4g, the B-ACI devices can retain over 80% of their initial PCEs after 300 h, while LDRP devices retain only 70% of their initial PCEs. The outstanding stability performance of B-ACI devices are mainly attributed to the inhibition of the formation large n-value perovskite (  $  n\geq5  $  ), high symmetry and strong interaction between interlayers, which effectively reduces ion migration and prevent decomposition under water, oxygen, and light.  $  ^{[11]}  $

In conclusion, we have demonstrated a new type low-dimensional perovskite (B-ACI) with BEA organic ligand for wide light absorption windows and efficient carrier extraction efficiency. The B-ACI perovskite delivered an efficient carrier localization process, leading to a barrier-free and balanced carrier transport pathway, and enabling balanced carrier diffusion length over 350 nm as well as enhanced mobility. Employed B-ACI perovskite as the active layers, we achieved champion device efficiency of 17.39%, which is much higher than that of LDRP perovskite (13.96%). Moreover, B-ACI perovskite has hydrogen

bond between BEA and inorganic slabs, thus the devices show much more stable against humidity, heating and light soaks. The results demonstrate the new B-ACI perovskite is a promising material to overcoming the performance-stability compromise in low-dimensional perovskite devices.
