# Additive Engineering by Bifunctional Guanidine Sulfamate for Highly Efficient and Stable Perovskites Solar Cells

Xuping Liu, Jihuai Wu,  $  ^{*}  $  Yuqian Yang, Deng Wang, Guodong Li, Xiaobing Wang, Weihai Sun, Yuelin Wei, Yunfang Huang, Miaoliang Huang, Leqing Fan, Zhang Lan, Jianming Lin, and Kuo-Chuan Ho

High efficiency and good stability are the challenges for perovskite solar cells (PSCs) toward commercialization. However, the intrinsic high defect density and internal nonradiative recombination of perovskite (PVK) limit its development. In this work, a facile additive strategy is devised by introducing bifunctional guanidine sulfamate (GuaSM;   $  CH_{6}N_{3}^{+}  $  ,   $  Gua^{+}  $  ;   $  H_{2}N-SO_{3}^{-}  $  ,   $  SM^{-}  $  ) into PVK. The size of   $  Gua^{+}  $  ion is suitable with   $  Pb(BrI)_{2}  $   cavity relatively, so it can participate in the formation of low-dimensional PVK when mixed with   $  Pb(BrI)_{2}  $  . The O and N atoms of   $  SM^{-}  $  can coordinate with   $  Pb^{2+}  $  . The synergistic effect of the anions and cations effectively reduces the trap density and the recombination in PVK, so that it can improve the efficiency and stability of PSCs. At an optimal concentration of GuaSM (2 mol%), the PSC presents a champion power conversion efficiency of 21.66% and a remarkably improved stability and hysteresis. The results provide a novel strategy for highly efficient and stable PSCs by bifunctional additive.

# 1. Introduction

The perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on lead amine halide are particularly distinctive in various photovoltaic technologies due to its unique characteristics,  $  ^{[1-4]}  $  such as low-cost manufacturing technology,  $  ^{[5,6]}  $  adjustable band gap,  $  ^{[7]}  $  high absorption factor,  $  ^{[8,9]}  $  and low exciton binding energy.  $  ^{[10,11]}  $  The prominent performance of PSC has rapidly increased its certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 3.8%  $  ^{[12]}  $  in 2009 to 25.2% at present,  $  ^{[13]}  $  which have aroused enormous concern by industry and academia. The   $  Gua^{+}  $   can incorporate into the PVK structure and become one of the organic cations in the PVK crystals, so that it can participate in the formation of low-dimensional PVK when mixes with   $  \mathrm{Pb(BrI)}_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[25-27]}  $   The O and N atoms of   $  SM^{-}  $   containing lone electron pairs can coordinate with   $  Pb^{2+}  $  . # 2. Results and Discussion

Figure 1 shows the schematic diagram of the preparation process of PVK film and the internal structure of the PVK, and the detailed preparation procedures are described in the Supporting Information. The precursor solution of PVK is spin-coated on TiO $ _2 $ /FTO (fluorine-doped tin oxide) substrate and annealed at 100 °C. During the process, GuaSM participates in the formation of PVK, which effectively reduces grain boundaries and increases grain size, and the defects and recombination are effectively reduced. Subsequent characterization will confirm this result.

In order to investigate the variation of the crystallinity of PVK films doped with different concentration of GuaSM (0 ≤ GuaSM ≤ 20%), the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of PVK films are exhibited in Figure 2a. It can be observed that the crystallinity of tetragonal phase (14.2°) yields a gradual increase until the doped concentration reaches 2%, which can be attributed to the bifunctional GuaSM improving the quality of the PVK film. Then the diffraction peaks appreciably decrease with the increased concentration of GuaSM, it is caused by the excessive dissolution of GuaSM in the solution, resulting in the saturation and precipitation of low-solubility PbX₂ and preventing the formation of tetragonal phase PVK crystals.

In order to explore the impacts of GuaSM on PVK films, Figure 3a shows the top-sectional scanning electron microscopy (SEM) of PVK films doped with GuaSM. Compared with the pristine film with small grain size and large grain boundary, the PVK films doped with 4% and 10% GuaSM represent larger grain size but with increasing pinholes and defects. # 3. Conclusions

In summary, we demonstrate a facile additive strategy by introducing bifunctional molecules guanidine sulfamate (GuaSM) into PVK layer. The synergistic effect of dual-functions greatly ameliorates the performance and stability of PSCs. We demonstrated that   $  Gua^{+}  $  can participate in the formation of 1D PVK, and the O and N atoms of SM groups can coordinate with   $  Pb^{2+}  $ , which significantly passivate defects and reduce recombination. The optimal device doped with 2% GuaSM achieves a champion

PCE of 21.66% with excellent environmental stability and slight hysteresis. While the pristine device got an efficiency of 18.67%. Our results provide a novel additive strategy by bifunctional molecules for stable and high-performance PSCs.
