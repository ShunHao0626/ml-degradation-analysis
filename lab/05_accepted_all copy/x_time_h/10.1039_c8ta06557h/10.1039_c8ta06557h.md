# Large-area perovskite solar cells with  $ \mathrm{Cs_xFA_{1 - x}PbI_{3 - y}Br_y} $  thin film deposited by vapor-solid reaction method

Long Luo,   $  ^{†a}  $   Yulong Zhang,   $  ^{†a}  $   Nianyao Chai,   $  ^{a}  $   Xi Deng,   $  ^{a}  $   Jie Zhong   $  ^{a}  $  , Fuzhi Huang   $  ^{a}  $   Yong Peng,   $  ^{*a}  $   Zhiliang Ku,   $  ^{*ab}  $   Yi-bing Cheng   $  ^{a}  $

Large-area, highly uniform   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   mixed cation perovskite films were prepared by a facile vapor-solid reaction method. In this method, CsBr and   $  PbI_{2}  $   were firstly deposited via thermal evaporation, and then exposed to FAI/FACl vapor to form   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   perovskite. With the incorporating of CsBr, the crystallinity of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite could be significantly improved. Meanwhile, the FACl vapor could accelerate the growth rate of   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   perovskite in the vapor-solid reaction process. By optimizing the content of Cs and the ratio of FACl/FAI, we obtained high quality, pin-hole free   $  Cs_{0.24}FA_{0.76}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   films. And based on these films, the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) in small size reached a champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 17.29%. Without encapsulation, the device with   $  Cs_{0.24}FA_{0.76}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   perovskite showed much better moisture- and thermal-stability in comparison to that with the pristine   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite. Further, we also fabricated PSCs modules with the size of   $  8 \times 8 \, cm^{2}  $   and achieved a PCE of 12.24%, indicating such vapor-solid reaction method have certain superiority in the manufacture of large-scale PSCs.

# Introduction

In recent years, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials have gained wild attention in the area of solar cells, due to their attractive optoelectronic properties and easy, low-cost synthesis  $  ^{1-5}  $  . By now, the world champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has reached 22.7%, far exceeds that of the other emerging photovoltaic technologies such as dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs) and organic photovoltaics (OPV)  $  ^{6}  $  . Though been obtained in lab-scale device, the high PCE value offers PSCs the basic prerequisite for competing with traditional silicon solar cells in future photovoltaic market. The question, then, is how to magnify the size of PSCs by certain techniques which are suitable for industrial production. Since organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials could easily be deposited by solution process, several scalable printing methods including spray-coating  $  ^{7}  $  , slot-die coating  $  ^{8}  $  , doctor-blade coating  $  ^{9}  $   and soft-cover deposition  $  ^{10}  $  , have been developed to prepare large area perovksite films. All of these methods showed great potential for achieving real sense of next generation “printable

solar cells". However, printing is not yet a mature technology for optoelectronics. More importantly, to date, only pure methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) perovskite films have been reported that can be deposited in large size by these printing techniques. But, as we all know, MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite is not conductive to the long-term stability of PSCs because of its intrinsic stability issues  $  ^{11-15}  $  . So in these regards, developing new methods, which are suitable for industrial production, to prepare large-area MA-free perovskite films would be of great significance for pushing PSCs industrialization forward.

As a widely used thin-film deposition technique, thermal evaporation has been successfully adopted in organic electronics. And fortunately, this technique is also available for the deposition of organic/inorganic hybrid perovskite material  $  ^{16}  $  . Since the first report of using dual source vacuum deposition method to prepare highly uniform MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $   perovskite  $  ^{17}  $  , more and more researcher have begun to express an interest in thermal evaporation technology  $  ^{18-28}  $  . Meanwhile, various vapor-solid reaction methods in form of chemical vapour deposition (CVD) have been explored to obtain high quality perovskite films.  $  ^{29-36}  $   Obviously, in comparison with solution-processing methods, these vapour-based techniques offer extra advantages such as non-use of toxic organic solvent, good thickness controllability and ease of combining with silicon solar cells in the form of tandem devices. What's more, stable perovskites such as formamidinium lead triiodide (FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  )  $  ^{31}  $   and Cs  $  _{0.5}  $  FA  $  _{0.4}  $  MA  $  _{0.1}  $  PbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Br  $  _{x}  $   mixed-cation perovskite  $  ^{26}  $   can also be easily obtained through these scalable techniques. Up to now, by using vapor-based techniques, a high PCE exceeding 20%  $  ^{24}  $   has been achieved on Cs  $  _{0.23}  $  MA  $  _{0.77}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $  -based PSCs with active area of 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  .

Unfortunately, the PCE of large-area devices still kept at low level  $  ^{31, 36}  $  . The main reason is that, in the case of vapour-solid reaction, the growth rate of perovskite is pretty low and sensitive to a small variation in the vapor density and the reaction temperature. As a result, a little bit of uneven vapour density or temperature distribution would cause the poor crystallinity of perovskite film  $  ^{36, 37}  $  . Hence, how to realize the homogenous growth of perovskite film in vapour-solid reacting process is the critical issue for high performance, large-area PSCs.

In this work, we employed a facile vapor-solid reaction method to prepare large-area, high quality   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   mixed-cation perovskite film. By controlling the content of   $  Cs^{+}  $ , we obtained pinhole-free, uniform   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   film. The champion   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $ -based PSCs achieved a PCE of 17.29%. Meanwhile, we found that FACI vapor could speed up the vapor-solid reaction rate and at the same time, suppress the formation of oversaturated   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   perovskite. Thus, the   $  CsBr/PbI_{2}  $   composite film could rapidly and completely transform into   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   perovskite in a large-size. Eventually, we fabricated PSCs modules with the size of   $  8 \times 8 \, cm^{2}  $ , and the champion PCE reached 12.24%. Without MA ion,   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   perovskite showed remarkable thermal- and moisture-stability. To the best of our knowledge, this work is the first demonstration of fabricating high performance, large-area   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $ -based PSCs modules by using vapor-solid reaction method.

# Experimental

# Materials

Unless stated otherwise, all reagents and solvents were purchased from Aladdin or Sigma-Aldrich used as received.  $ \mathrm{SnCl}_2\cdot 2\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  were purchased from Aladdin. FAI was purchased from Lumtec, Taiwan. CsBr was purchased from Alfa Aesar. FACI was purchased from Xi'an p-OLED Corp.

# Device fabrication

FTO glasses (Universal Laser Systems, VLS2.30) were patterned by etching with femtosecond laser. Then the glass substrates were cleaned by sequential ultrasonication in a detergent solution, deionized water, and anhydrous ethanol. 5 g urea was dissolved into 400 mL deionized water, followed by the addition of 100 μL mercaptoacetic acid and 5 mL HCl (37 wt%). Finally, SnCl₂·2H₂O was dissolved in the solution at 0.012 M. The cleaned FTO glass was soaked into the SnCl₂ solution for 3h at 70 °C and then dried by gas gun.

CsBr and   $  PbI_{2}  $   were deposited on the compact   $  SnO_{2}  $   film by thermal evaporation. The CsBr films deposition rate was maintained at   $  0.3\ \AA\ s^{-1}  $  . After the crucible cooling down, the   $  PbI_{2}  $   films were deposited successively at a rate of   $  3\ \AA\ s^{-1}  $  . Then, the as-prepared composite films were transferred into a tube furnace and faced to another glass substrate with FAI/FACI film sprayed on it. The distance of the two substrates was about 2.5cm. After that, the tube furnace was cleaned by   $  N_{2}  $   and then pumped down to a pressure of about 100 Pa. Subsequently, the tube furnace was heated up to   $  160\ ^{\circ}C  $   for 30

min and  $ \mathrm{CsBr / PbI_2} $  composite film gradually transformed into  $ \mathrm{Cs_xFA_{1 - x}PbI_{3 - y}Br_y} $  perovskite. After cooling down, hole-transporting layer (73 mg spiro-MeOTAD, 9.36 mg Li-TFSI salt and  $ 30~\mu \mathrm{L} $  4-tert-butylpyridine in  $ 1\mathrm{mL} $  chlorobenzene.) was deposited on the perovskite film by spin coating. Finally,  $ 80~\mathrm{nm} $  of Au was evaporated on the hole-transporting layer as the top contact.

# Characterization

The x-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the samples were recorded using an X-ray diffractometer (XRD, D8 Advance). The UV measurements were carried out by using UV-vis (lambda 750S, PerkinElmer). The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra and the time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) were obtained by using PL microscopic spectrometer (Flex One, Zolix, China) and Delta Flex Fluorescence Lifetime System (Horiba Scientific Com., Japan), respectively. The morphology and composition were observed by a field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM, Zeiss Ultra Plus). The current density-voltage (J-V) curves were measured using a solar simulator (Oriel 94023A, 300 W) with a source meter (Keithley 2400) under  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $  illumination (AM 1.5G) with a scan rate of  $ 10\mathrm{mVs}^{-1} $ . The active area of small size PSCs and PSCs modules are  $ 0.16\mathrm{cm}^2 $  and  $ 41\mathrm{cm}^2 $ , respectively.

# Results and discussion

Fig. 1 Schematic illustration of the vapor-solid reaction method used for preparing  $ \mathrm{Cs_xFA_{1 - x}PbI_{3 - y}Br_y} $  mixed-cation perovskite.

The vapor-solid reaction method could be considered as a combination of physical vapor deposition (PVD) and low-pressure chemical vapor deposition (LP-CVD). As shown in figure 1, CsBr and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  were firstly deposited by thermal evaporation in sequence. And then, the CsBr/PbI $ _2 $  composite film reacted with FAI/FACl vapor in a tube furnace at  $ 160^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ . To ensure the evenness of the vapor-solid reaction, FAI/FACl composite film was loaded on the substrate by ultrasonic spray coating with a uniform thickness, and the distance of the two substrates was about  $ 2.5\mathrm{cm} $ .

By evaporating different content of CsBr, we obtained   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   perovskites with x = 0, 0.14, 0.24 and 0.33. X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements were performed on these films to identify their crystal structure. Without   $  Cs^{+}  $   (x = 0), the   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite exhibited an obvious diffraction peak at   $  2\theta = 11.8^{\circ}  $  , manifesting the existence of   $  \delta  $   phase   $  FAPbI_{3}^{38}  $  . And by introducing   $  Cs^{+}  $  , all of the   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   perovskites showed stable black phase at room temperature (Fig. 2a). With the increasing of   $  Cs^{+}  $  , the peak of the (110) plane at   $  2\theta = 14^{\circ}  $   shifted towards higher angles (Fig. 2b), is indicative of a decrease in the lattice parameter and the formation of   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   crystal  $  ^{39}  $  . To further confirm the incorporation of Cs and Br into   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite, X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS) was also carried out as the shrinkage of the cubo-octahedral volume could lead to a change of the chemical bonding nature between Pb and I. As shown in figure S1a, b there is no Cs 3d and Br 3d peaks detected in the pristine   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite, and these peaks showed up with the adding of CsBr. Moreover, the two dominant peaks at 136.7 ev and 141.5 ev in figure S1c, which corresponding to the Pb   $  4f_{7/2}  $   and Pb   $  4f_{5/2}  $   levels, shifted towards higher binding energies. A same shift trend could also be observed in the I 3d peaks in figure S1d, suggests that the lattice parameter is slightly decreased with the CsBr doping  $  ^{40}  $  .

The surface morphology of the   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   perovskite films were observed by scanning-electron microscopy (SEM). There were lot of pin holes (red circle) on the surface of the pristine   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite (Fig. 3a), which should be ascribe to its phase transformation form   $  \alpha  $  -phase to   $  \sigma  $  -phase at room temperature  $  ^{41}  $  . In fact, the   $  \alpha  $  -phase (black) of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite is only stable at high temperature. And as the temperature cooling down to room temperature, the   $  \alpha  $  -phase is prone to transfer into   $  \sigma  $  -phase, accompanied by a fast decomposition in air. Thus, plenty of voids or pin holes would generate and cause a poor coverage of the film. However, all of the   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   perovskite films with different amount of CsBr exhibited compact and pin hole-free surface morphologies (Fig. 3 b-c), indicating the   $  \alpha  $  -phase of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   crystal could be easily stabilized at room temperature by the introducing of CsBr. The element distribution of the perovskite film was verified by the energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS). As shown in figure S2,

both of Cs and Br elements have a homogenous distribution in the perovskite layer. And more notably, though Br was introduced into the film by evaporating CsBr layer, the content of Br in the as-prepared   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   perovskite films was significantly lower than that of the Cs (see Fig. S3). We deduced that in the vapor-solid reaction, most of the Br elements were exchanged by the Cl and released in the form of FABr. To confirm this, we also used pure FACl to react with   $  CsBr/PbI_{2}  $   composite film and the film changed its color gradually from pale yellow to pale white, indicating the totally release of Br (see Fig. S4).

Based on these uniform and pin hole-free   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   perovskite films, we fabricated PSCs with a traditional   $  SnO_{x}  $  /perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au multilayered planar structure. The current-voltage (J-V) curves of the devices were recorded under standard AM 1.5 illumination and shown in figure 4. The pure   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   device gave a PCE of 11.33%, with an open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 997 mV, a short circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 17.35 mAcm  $  ^{-2}  $   and a fill factor (FF) of 0.65. With the increasing content of CsBr, the PCE value of the device exhibited an upward trend. And when the ratio of Cs/FA came to 0.24/0.76, the device showed a champion PCE of 17.29%, with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1065 mV, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.88 mAcm  $  ^{-2}  $   and a FF of 0.71. As shown in Fig. S5, The stabilized power output of the champion device maintained a   $  J_{sc}  $   value of approximately 18.86 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and a PEC of 15.84% for 100s, respectively.

The significantly growth of   $  J_{sc}  $   was further verified by the incident-photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectrum (Fig. 4b). Without CsBr, the IPCE spectrum showed highest value of 70% at around 500 nm, and gradually decreased its value to ~60% for wavelength between 500 nm and the FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   band-edge. This downtrend of IPCE value in longer wavelength region can also be observed in the   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $  -device with x = 0.14. We attribute this feature to the high trap density of the perovskite film. Whereas, when x increased to 0.24, the   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $  -device kept high IPCE value of ~80% over the whole wavelength range 450-800 nm, indicating a better quality of the perovskite crystal. The integrated current density of the   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $  -devices with x = 0, 0.14, 0.24, 0.33 were 16.83 mAcm  $  ^{-2}  $  , 19.05 mAcm  $  ^{-2}  $  , 21.02 mAcm  $  ^{-2}  $   and 20.35 mAcm  $  ^{-2}  $  , respectively, which is in agreement with the   $  J_{sc}  $   derived from the J-V curves.

To further certify the positive effect of CsBr on the crystal quality of FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite, steady-state photo-luminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL (TRPL) measurements were carried out with the excitation wavelength of 470nm. As shown in figure 5a, the PL intensity of glass/ $ \mathrm{Cs_xFA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_y} $  samples was remarkably enhanced with the increasing of CsBr from 0 to 0.24, indicating the reducing of trap-state density in  $ \mathrm{Cs_xFA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_y} $  samples $ ^{42} $ . When the content of CsBr came to 0.33, the PL intensity had a slightly drop, due to the lattice mismatch caused by the excess  $ \mathrm{Cs^{24}} $ . TRPL measurements were also carried out on the glass/ $ \mathrm{Cs_xFA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_y} $  samples to compare their electron life time (Fig. 5b). It showed that the PL decay time of pristine FAPbI $ _3 $  sample was much shorter than that of the other samples. And  $ \mathrm{Cs_xFA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_y} $  perovskite with  $ x = 0.24 $  exhibited the longest PL decay time, which was quite in accordance with the steady-state PL results (the detail fitting parameters are shown in Table S1).

Besides the CsBr, we found that FACI vapor also played an important role in the growth of perovskite crystal. In pure FAI vapor, it would take  $ 30\mathrm{min} $  for the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film to transform into perovskite completely. And the phase transition of perovskite form  $ \alpha $ -phase to  $ \sigma $ -phase was promoted by continuous exposing the film in FAI vapor (see Fig. 5a). However, in FAI/FACI composite vapor, no distinct  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  peak can be found in the XRD pattern of the sample with  $ 20\mathrm{min} $  reaction time, indicating a faster growth rate of perovsktie crystal. And more importantly, by further extending the reaction time to  $ 40\mathrm{min} $ , no phase transition was detected. Obviously, the faster growth rate and better phase stability of perovskite crystal would in favour of obtaining highly uniform, large size films and

improving the device reproducibility. Two batches of PSCs were (each one consist of 20 devices) fabricated in FAI and FAI/FACI vapor, respectively. PCE values of these devices are compared in figure 6c. Without FACI in the vapor, the device PCE distributed in a wide range of values from 13% to 16.89%. While introducing FACI in the vapor, the as-prepared devices exhibited a much narrower distribution range of PCE values. Ultimately, we fabricated PSCs modules with an active area of 41.25 cm $  ^{2}  $  and the champion module possessed a high PCE of 12.24% (Fig. 6d). To the best of our knowledge, this is the highest module efficiency for the vapor-based techniques. The long-term stability of the modules without encapsulation was tested at ambient condition (RH = ~30%, 25 °C) and heating condition (N $  _{2}  $  atmosphere, 60 °C), respectively. As shown in figure 6e, after stored for ~200 h without encapsulation at ambient condition, the Cs $  _{x}  $ FA $  _{1-x}  $ PbI $  _{3-y}  $ Br $  _{y}  $  device maintained 83% of its initial PCE, while the pristine FAPbI $  _{3}  $  device lost most of its performance. Similarly, the Cs $  _{x}  $ FA $  _{1-x}  $ PbI $  _{3-y}  $ Br $  _{y}  $  device exhibited much better thermal stability than that of the pristine one (Fig. 6f). Such large-area, high performance and stable PSCs modules fabricated by facile vapor-solid reaction may pave the way for accelerating the industrialization of PSCs technology.

# Conclusions

In summary, we fabricated   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   mixed-cation films by a facile vapor-solid reaction method, which can easily be adaptive to the requirement of industry. By optimizing the content of CsBr, we obtained uniform and pin hole-free   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   perovskite films with low trap density. The champion PSCs based on these   $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Br_{y}  $   perovskite films achieved a high PCE up to 17.29%. Further, the slow growth rate of perovskite crystal, which is the main issue for the vapor-solid reaction technique, was addressed by introducing FACI into the FAI vapor. In comparison with pure FAI, the perovskite crystal prepared in FAI/FACI mixing vapor showed faster growth rate and better phase stability, rendering high device reproducibility. On the basis of these improvements, large size PSCs modules were fabricated with an active area of 41.25cm  $  ^{2}  $  . The PSCs modules exhibited a champion PCE of 12.24%, and showed outstanding long-term stability at ambition air conditions and heating condition (  $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere, 60 °C). These results suggest that our facile vapor-based method could provide a new approach for fabricating high-performance, large-area and stable PSCs module.
