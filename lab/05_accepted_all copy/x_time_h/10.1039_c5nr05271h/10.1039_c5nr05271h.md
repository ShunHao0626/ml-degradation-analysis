# Graphene oxide/PEDOT:PSS composite hole transport layer for efficient and stable planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells

# Abstract

We investigated graphene oxide (GO)/poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) composite as a promising candidate for practical application of 2-D carboneous hole transport layer (HTL) to planar heterojunction perovskite solar cell (PeSC) consisting of transparent electrode/HTL/perovskite/fullerene/metal electrode. Both insulating property of GO and ununiform coating of transparent electrode with GO causing poor morphology of perovskite induced low power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 6.4\% $ . On the other hand, PeSC with GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTL, exhibited higher PCE of  $ 9.7\% $  than that of device fabricated with conventional PEDOT:PSS showing PCE of  $ 8.2\% $ . Higher performance is attributed to decreased series resistance  $ (R_{S}) $  and increased shunt resistance  $ (R_{Sh}) $ . Well-matched work-function between GO  $ (4.9\mathrm{eV}) $  and PEDOT:PSS  $ (5.1\mathrm{eV}) $  probably results in more efficient charge transport and overall decrease in  $ R_{S} $ . The existence of GO with large bandgap of  $ \sim 3.6\mathrm{eV} $  might induce effective blocking of electrons, leading to increase of  $ R_{Sh} $ . Moreover, improvement in long-term stability under the atmospheric condition was observed.

Keywords: Planar heterojunction perovskite solar cell, Hole transport layer, Graphene oxide, Composite, Long-term stability

# Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials such as methylammonium lead halide (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbX $ _3 $ ) have attracted significant attention as a promising material for photovoltaic applications due to their high absorption coefficient, excellent carrier transport, chemical and structural diversity, and proper band gap. After the perovskite was used as a sensitizer in dye-sensitized solar cells by Miyasaka in 2009, PCE of solar cells including perovskite materials has been rapidly increased up to ~20 % over the last 5 years [1-8]. Although the toxicity of soluble lead (II) salts has been emerged as a critical problem, several reports on the life cycle and environmental impact analysis of lead based perovskite solar cell (PeSC) described that there is no need to dismiss lead based perovskites as considerable materials for solar cell technology [9-10]. In general, high efficiency perovskite solar cells (PeSCs) employed meso-superstructured or condensed metal oxide such as TiO $ _2 $  and Al $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ . In that case, because high-temperature process (>450 °C) is required for the preparation of high-quality metal oxide, it is not suitable for the flexible power sources [6-8, 11-14]. To overcome this issue, planar heterojunction PeSCs consisting of indium tin oxide (ITO)/HTL/CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbX $ _3 $ /1-(3-methoxycarbonyl)-propyl-1-phenyl-(6,6)C61(PCBM)/metal cathode have been suggested by several groups [15-17]. In contrast to conjugated polymers, because perovskite materials have long exciton diffusion lengths (100-1000 nm) and lifetimes (~100 ns), they can be applied to planar structured solar cells [18]. To demonstrate highly efficient planar solar cells, choice of appropriate electron acceptor layer is very important. Among various materials, fullerenes are considered as excellent candidates owing to efficient and fast charge transfer (0.40 ns) from perovskite to fullerenes [19, 20]. Moreover, the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) levels of fullerenes and the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) levels of CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbX $ _3 $  are well aligned for electron transfer [15]. These properties make fullerenes ideal electron acceptor materials and thus interest in CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbX $ _3 $ /PCBM planar PeSCs has

been dramatically increased. Most studies on planar PeSCs have been focused on the optimization of morphology of perovskite active layer by controlling crystallization process [16, 21-24]. Yang Yang et al. reported ~11 % PeSC using mixed halide (e.g.,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $  ) absorber layer fabricated by the vapor-assisted solution process [16, 21]. Alex Jen et al. and D. Y. Kim et al. studied on the effect of additives such as 1,8-diiodooctane (DIO) and N-cyclohexyl-2-pyrrolidone (CHP) on the morphology and overall PCE of PeSCs, respectively [22, 23]. In addition, Jinsong Huang et al. produced high quality perovskite layer by optimizing sequential 2-step spin coating of lead halide and methyl ammonium iodide [24].

For further improvement in the performance of planar PeSCs, efficient carrier transport to electrodes through HTL and electron acceptor layer is also a crucial requirement [25-28]. In general, poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS), which is widely used as a HTL in polymer solar cells, has been applied as HTL for generated hole transport from perovskite active layer to ITO. However, because PEDOT:PSS has latent problems such as high acidity and hygroscopic nature, inhomogeneous electrical property, and poor electron blocking ability, a few studies on the development of promising alternatives to PEDOT:PSS HTL has been reported by several groups [29-36]. Improved PeSCs with nickel oxides  $ (\mathrm{NiO_X}) $  HTL have been demonstrated as a result of improved wetting and better aligned energy level with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  [32, 33]. However, to obtain considerable efficiency, additional high temperature process or  $ \mathrm{UV / O_3} $  treatment, which are incompatible with simple and low cost manufacturing of PeSCs, is required. Application of high work function (WF) self-organized HTL composed of PEDOT:PSS and perfluorinated ionomer (PFI) also improved PCE of PeSC by increasing built-in potential [34]. Very recently, 2-D carboneous materials such as GO and reduced graphene oxide (RGO) have been used as HTLs in PeSC [35, 36]. B. Sun et al. investigated the effect of GO thickness on the performance of PeSC and J.-S. Yeo et al. compared the performance of RGO incorporated PeSCs with devices

Generally due to the insulating property of GO, performance of solar cells is highly sensitive to the thickness of HTL, thus it can be disadvantageous for realization of GO as HTLs. Furthermore, because several RGOs reduced by using chemicals have low dispersion concentration and stability, it is difficult to be applied to scalable and reproducible manufacturing of PeSCs [37].

In this study, as a promising candidate for practical application of carboneous HTLs, we investigated GO/PEDOT:PSS composite. We expect that GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTL can complement drawback of single GO and conventional PEDOT:PSS. Composite HTLs were fabricated by sequential spin-coating of commercialized GO and PEDOT:PSS. Electrical, optical, and structural properties were characterized and their effects on the performance as well as long-term stability of PeSCs were studied.

# Experimental

Cleaned ITO coated glass substrates (Samsung Corning Co.) were treated with UV/O $  _{3}  $  plasma for 20 min to make hydrophilic surface state. The commercialized GO (Graphene-Supermarket) was dispersed in DI water with a concentration of 1 mg/ml and GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTLs were fabricated by sequential spin-coating of GO dispersion and PEDOT:PSS (Heraeus, CleviosTM P VP AI 4083, a weight ratio of 1:6) at 6000 rpm for 40 s onto ITO followed by annealing at 150 °C for 5 min in atmospheric condition. For comparison, GO and PEDOT:PSS HTLs were also spin-coated and annealed at the same conditions. To fabricate CH $  _{3}  $ NH $  _{3}  $ PbI $  _{3}  $  active layer on each HTL, the 20 wt% precursor solution was prepared by dissolving methyl ammonium iodide and lead(II) iodide (PbI $  _{2}  $ , 99%, Aldrich) with a 1:1 molar ratio in 2-methoxyethanol. After stirring and filtration, 3.5 vol% CHP was added into the precursor solution as a morphology controller to achieve highly smooth film morphologies [23]. Then, the mixture solution was spin-coated at 7000 rpm for 90 sec onto each HTL followed by annealing at 140 °C for 30 sec in N2-filled glove box. After that, PCBM electron acceptor layers were formed on CH $  _{3}  $ NH $  _{3}  $ PbI $  _{3}  $  active layers by the spin-coating of PCBM solution dissolved in chlorobenzene (20 mg/ml) at 1000 rpm for 60 sec. Finally, the metal cathode of LiF (0.7 nm)/Ag (80 nm) was thermally evaporated in vacuum with a pressure of <10 $  ^{-6}  $  Torr.

Optical and structural characteristics were carried out using UV-vis absorption spectrophotometer (Varian AU/DMS-100S) and field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM, HORIBA EX-250), respectively. Surface morphologies of each HTL were observed using atomic force microscopy (AFM, NT-MDT Prima). Information on the crystallinity of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  was obtained from the X-ray diffraction (XRD) data taken using a PAN alytical Empyrean with Cu Kα ( $ \lambda = 0.1542 $  nm) radiation.

# Results and discussion

As shown in Fig. 1, bi-layered heterojunction PeSCs were fabricated with GO, PEDOT:PSS, and GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTLs. Due to its ultimate transparency and easy synthetic process, GO has been more attractive than conventional PEDOT:PSS as a charge transporting interlayer for organic optoelectronics. To achieve high performance with insulating GO HTL, uniform and full cover of ITO with very thin GO is important. In addition, post chemical or thermal treatment to improve electrical property of GO as a result of removal of oxygen function group is needed. In our previous report, ITO was successfully coated with one or two layers of GO by using synthesized GO under the critically controlled conditions and by applying moderate thermal treatment at  $ \sim 250^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for few minutes superior PCE and stability was achieved in polymer solar cells, comparing to conventional PEDOT:PSS system [38]. However, when we used mass-produced GO, formation of homogeneous dispersion of GO is difficult and thus full cover of ITO with high uniformity was not observed. In this case, poor hole transport and collection to ITO can be expected at the uncovered ITO regions due to the direct contact of perovskite active layer and ITO. Furthermore, ununiform GO on ITO induces inhomogeneous electrical characteristics owing to the insulating property of GO and which can be considered as critical problem leading to poor reliability of device performance for the market entry. To overcome this point, as a practically applicable 2-D carboneous HTLs to low temperature ( $ <150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) solution processed manufacturing of PeSCs, GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTLs were prepared by successive spin-coating of GO and PEDOT:PSS and compared with single GO and conventional PEDOT:PSS in terms of their influence on the performance as well as long-term stability.

Before the fabrication of devices, we investigated electrical, optical and structural characteristics of HTLs on ITO. Because the light is absorbed through the HTLs on ITO, optical transparency of HTL is important factor determining photocurrent. As shown in Fig. 2

(a), all of the HTLs are highly transparent in the overall range of wavelength with a transmittance values up to 85 % and do not significantly alter the transparency of ITO. Next, to find the effect of HTLs on the electrical property of intrinsic ITO that affects charge transport and collection, we measured sheet resistance of ITO substrates with and without HTLs using 4 point-probe and exhibited in Fig. 2 (b). Since very thin HTLs were coated on ITO, there is no noticeable difference in the sheet resistance. Slightly increased sheet resistance from ~9.5 Ω/cm² to 9.7-10 Ω/cm² was evaluated after incorporation of HTLs. Interestingly, ITO with GO HTLs showed relatively large variation in sheet resistance and it might be attributed to the ununiform covering of ITO with insulating GO. Fig. 2 (c) shows AFM images of each HTL on ITO. Because surface morphology of HTL has a crucial influence on the electrical properties of devices such as series resistance (R $  _{S}  $ ) and the shunt resistance (R $  _{Sh}  $ ), we observed AFM images of HTLs on ITO. The root mean square (RMS) roughness values of PEDOT:PSS and GO in an area of 5 μm × 5 μm were evaluated to 1.8 nm and 3.2 nm, respectively. In our previous work, RMS roughness of GO HTL fabricated using carefully synthesized and dispersed GO was similar to PEDOT:PSS, while high RMS roughness was obtained in this study. This indicates that mass-produced GO, which is used in this study, can't fully cover the ITO with high uniformity, as we discussed. Meanwhile, by applying PEDOT:PSS on GO, RMS roughness was decreased to 1.8 nm, indicating that sequential deposition of PEDOT:PSS results in planarization of the rough GO HTL.

As mentioned, for highly efficient PeSCs, control of crystallization of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite to minimize large morphological deviation is the most important and difficult issue. Potential of GO/PEDOT:PSS composite as HTL in PeSC was evaluated by the measurement of J-V characteristics under the standard conditions. For comparison, solar cells employing PEDOT:PSS and GO also were characterized and resultant performances of each device were shown in Fig. 5 (a) and Table 1. When the devices were fabricated without any HTLs, seriously poor performance was obtained or devices were not operated. Hence, we believed that HTL is crucial requirement for successful operation of PeSCs. PeSC with conventional PEDOT:PSS HTL showed reasonable PCE of  $ 8.23\% $  with open-circuit voltage  $ (\mathrm{V_{OC}}) $  of 0.80 V, short-circuit current  $ (\mathrm{J_{SC}}) $  of  $ 14.35\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $ , and fill-factor (FF) of  $ 71.61\% $ . In the case of device with GO, relatively poor performance  $ (\mathrm{V_{OC}} $  of 0.84 V,  $ \mathrm{J_{SC}} $  of  $ 12.75\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $ , FF of  $ 59.58\% $ , and PCE of  $ 6.42\% $ ) was exhibited. It is attributed to the ununiform formation electrically insulating GO layer on ITO and growth of inhomogeneous perovskite active layer with many pin-holes on GO HTL. In contrast, by using GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTL superior performance to single GO and conventional PEDOT:PSS based PeSCs was achieved as follows:  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $  of 0.84 V,  $ \mathrm{J_{SC}} $  of  $ 15.72\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $ , FF of  $ 73.71\% $ , and PCE of  $ 9.74\% $ . In particular, anomalous hysteresis has been raised as important issues to confirm the accurate characterization of PeSCs. Several studies on hysteresis of PeSCs reported that perovskite solar cell architectures consisting of mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite/hole transport materials (HTMs) undergo a large hysteresis with the scan direction in the J-V measurement [7, 40]. On the other hand, planar structured PeSCs of perovskite and PCBM have been demonstrated to show negligible hysteresis behavior due to more efficient electron extraction through PCBM, resulting in balanced electron and hole transport [41-43]. We also have carefully observed hysteresis properties of PeSCs for accurate characterization of J-V performance. As shown in Table 1, devices with PEDOT:PSS, GO, and GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTL showed virtually no hysteresis at forward and reverse scan.

For further confirmation on the possibility of GO/PEDOT:PSS HTL, reliability of device performances was investigated and important parameters were recorded in Fig. 5 (b). GO/PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:PSS based devices showed excellent reproducible performances. On the contrary, devices inserting GO exhibited relatively wide variations due to poor uniformity of GO and large morphological deviations of perovskite active layers. EQE and IQE of devices with different HTLs were also measured and shown in Fig.5 (c) and (d). The calculated   $  J_{SC}  $   values using EQE spectrum were well-matched with   $  J_{SC}  $   values evaluated from the J–V curves and the PeSC employing GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTL illustrated the highest and broad spectrum from 300 nm to 800 nm. In the case of GO/PEDOT:PSS contained PeSC, EQE of ~80 % was obtained at a maximum peak, whereas the PEDOT:PSS based device exhibited lower EQE of ~65 %. Moreover, the IQE spectrum of PeSC using GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTL showed higher values in overall wavelength range compared to the spectrum of PEDOT:PSS and GO based devices. Although the absorbance of perovskite active layers on each HTL is similar as discussed, the highest IQE was obtained in the case of composite HTL incorporated device. This indicates that photon-to-electron conversion occurs more efficiently in the PeSC with GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTL.

The   $  R_{S}  $   and   $  R_{Sh}  $  , strongly affecting overall performance of devices, were calculated from the J-V curves and shown in Fig. 6 (a). By applying GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTL, reduced   $  R_{S}  $   was obtained comparing to GO and PEDOT:PSS based devices. This suggests superior charge transport ability of GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTL. Finally, to examine one of the most important requirements for the market entry of PeSC, effect of different HTLs on the long-term stability was investigated. Change of PCE with exposed time in atmospheric condition (temperature of 21~24 °C and humidity of 38~55 %) was recorded without any encapsulation. As shown in Fig. 7 (a), PCE of PEDOT:PSS based device showed a sharp decline, while degradation of PCE was considerably improved by inserting GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTL or GO HTL. Improved stability in GO/PEDOT:PSS composite HTL is expected that GO prohibit direct contact of ITO with highly acidic PEDOT:PSS. Unexpected reason of the improved stability was found through the XRD measurement. As shown in Fig. 7 (b), we measured XRD patterns of perovskite layers that exposed in atmospheric condition for 2 days and 5 days. In case of XRD patterns of as-fabricated perovskite layer on different HTLs, there is no noticeable difference. After exposure to air for 2 days, new peak at 12.65 ° assigned to (001) crystal plane of PbI₂ was observed in the XRD patterns of every perovskite layer. In particular, more prominent (001) peak of PbI₂ with the decreasing of (110) peak of CH₃NH₃PbI₃ was shown in the XRD patterns of perovskite layer deposited on conventional PEDOT:PSS. This indicates that severe degradation of perovskite structure was occurred on PEDOT:PSS. After 5 days, strong (001) PbI₂ peak was observed in all perovskite layer. The color of the films was also changed from dark brown to yellow resulting from decomposition of perovskite crystalline. # Conclusion

In conclusion, GO/PEDOT:PSS composite, which is considered as a promising candidate to complement drawback of single GO and conventional PEDOT:PSS, was employed as HTL to planar heterojunction PeSCs. Conventional PEDOT:PSS based PeSC showed reasonable PCE of 8.23 %, while device with single GO HTL exhibited relatively low performance of 6.42 %. Low efficiency was caused by inhomogeneous coating of ITO with mass-produced GO, resulting in poor morphology of perovskite layer. In contrast, PeSC with GO/PEDOT:PSS composite fabricated by sequential spin-coating of GO and PEDOT:PSS, demonstrated superior performance as follows:   $  V_{OC}  $   of 0.84 V,   $  J_{SC}  $   of 15.72 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , FF of 73.71 %, and PCE of 9.74 %. Well-matched WF between GO (4.9 eV) and PEDOT:PSS (5.1 eV) probably results in more efficient hole transport and collection to ITO. In fact, decreased   $  R_{S}  $  , which is an important parameter determining carrier transport and collection, was evaluated from J-V curves. Higher   $  R_{Sh}  $  , implying more efficient suppression of carrier recombination, was also calculated. The existence of GO with large bandgap of   $  \sim  $   3.6 eV might induce effective blocking of electrons. The aforementioned reasons suggest that decreased   $  R_{S}  $   and increased   $  R_{Sh}  $   strongly affects the improvement of performance of GO/PEDOT:PSS based PeSC. Furthermore, significant enhancement in the stability under the atmospheric condition was observed, comparing to conventional PEDOT:PSS system. GO prohibiting direct contact of ITO with highly acidic PEDOT:PSS might result in improved stability. Also, we found that decomposition of perovskite to   $  PbI_{2}  $   was slowly occurred when the perovskite was prepared on GO/PEDOT:PSS HTL. This phenomenon was reproducibly observed in the XRD analysis. The study on the restricted decomposition of perovskite on GO/PEDOT:PSS was currently underway. These results demonstrate that GO/PEDOT:PSS composite can be sufficiently considered as a practically applicable 2-D carboneous HTLs to low temperature solution processed manufacturing of PeSCs.
