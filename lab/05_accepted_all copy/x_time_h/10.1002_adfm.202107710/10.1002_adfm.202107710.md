# Efficient Bulk Defect Suppression Strategy in FASnI  $  _{3}  $  Perovskite for Photovoltaic Performance Enhancement

Bohong Chang, Bo Li, Zhongxiao Wang, Hui Li, Lian Wang, Lu Pan, Zihao Li, and Longwei Yin*

Despite Sn-based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) prevailing over lead-free candidates, the Sn vacancies   $  \left(\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{Sn}}\right)  $   and   $  Sn^{4+}  $   defects seriously deteriorate device photovoltaic performance. The presently reported methods can only effectively achieve surface defect passivation, and it is of great challenge and fundamental importance to develop efficient strategy to deal with the intrinsic defects located inside the lattice. Herein, a novel bulk defect suppression strategy is proposed, introducing large organic piperazine cations   $  \left(\mathrm{PZ}^{2+}\right)  $   into the lattice of 3D FASnI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite to restrain the generation of bulk defects. The incorporation of PZ  $  ^{2+}  $   results in forming a FA  $  _{1-2y}  $  PZ  $  _{2y}  $  Sn  $  _{1-y}  $  I  $  _{3}  $   (0 ≤ y ≤ 0.25) structure with no reduction in dimensionality, which guarantees the continuity of   $  [SnI_{6}]  $   octahedral structures with unobstructed carrier transport and reduced charged defects. The potent interactions between PZ  $  ^{2+}  $   and   $  [SnI_{6}]  $   structures enhance V  $  _{Sn}  $   formation energy and effectively suppress bulk defect formation. As a result, the FASnI  $  _{3}  $  +1%PZ films exhibit optimized crystalline quality, decreased background carrier density, lower p-type self-doping, and reduced trap state density. Benefiting from the above advantages, the FASnI  $  _{3}  $  +1%PZ device achieves an optimal PCE of 9.15% and unencapsulated device maintains over 95% of initial PCE after aging for 1000 h in N  $  _{2}  $   golvebox. The bulk defect suppression strategy provides fire-new building bricks toward high-performance Sn-based PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Halide perovskites as promising candidates for the next-generation photovoltaic materials have attracted extensive attention due to excellent semiconductor properties such as adjustable bandgaps, high absorption coefficients, long carrier diffusion length, and low-cost solution production.  $  ^{[1,2]}  $   The single-junction Pb-based PSCs have achieved an optimal certified PCE of more than 25%,  $  ^{[3]}  $   but the concerns about the toxicity and

leakage of lead hinder their large-scale commercialization.  $  ^{[4]}  $   Low-toxicity Sn-based perovskites are considered as the most promising alternatives due to more suitable optical bandgaps, larger carrier mobilities and smaller exciton binding energy.  $  ^{[5]}  $   However, the low   $  V_{Sn}  $   formation energy, the   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation and the rapid and uncontrollable crystallization severely impede the device performance improvement.  $  ^{[6,7]}  $   Reductives as well as reducing atmosphere have been applied to effectively prevent   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation, suppress p-type self-doping, and enhance perovskite stability.  $  ^{[8,9]}  $   Crystallization regulation strategies have been proven impactful to reduce crystallization rate and optimize film morphology.  $  ^{[10]}  $   Most additives can easily achieve surface passivation and suppress   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation, but how to effectively cope with the intrinsic defects located inside Sn-based perovskite films remains a tough challenge.

Previous researches have demonstrated that some organic diamine cations with specific size such as ethylenediammonium (EDA $  ^{2+}  $ ) and propylenediammonium (PN $  ^{2+}  $ ) can enter into 3D perovskite structure without reducing the dimensionality. $ ^{[21,22]} $  The Kanatzidis research group first reported that EDA $  ^{2+}  $  could serve as an A-site cation to induce massive Schottky style defects and achieve the bandgap adjustment of FASnI $  _{3}  $  perovskite, which results in optimized photoelectric properties with an optimal PCE of 7.14% and enhanced air stability. $ ^{[21]} $  Jokar et al. demonstrated that adding 1% EDAI $  _{2}  $  can eliminate the pinhole in films, passivate surface defects, inhibit Sn $  ^{2+}  $  oxidation, and induce crystal structure slow relaxation to achieve the maximum PCE of 8.9%. $ ^{[23]} $  Hillebrecht et al. proved that EDA $  ^{2+}  $  could incorporate into MAPbI $  _{3}  $  by replacing MA $  ^{+}  $  in a 1:1 ratio along with a deficit of Pb $  ^{2+}  $  for charge compensation and larger space, forming a special perovskite with a structural formula of MA $  _{1-2\gamma}  $ EDA $  _{2\gamma}  $ Pb $  _{1-\gamma}  $ I $  _{3}  $  ( $  0 \leq \gamma \leq 0.25  $ ). $ ^{[24]} $  Nowadays, EDAI $  _{2}  $  as a recognized high-efficiency additive has been widely applied in the preparation of high-quality Sn-related perovskite films. $ ^{[25,26]} $

So far, only a few similar diamine ions incorporated into 3D perovskite structure have been discovered and reported and all of them are primary amine cations. According to inductive effect theory that attaching more hydrocarbon groups to nitrogen atom will increase the electron cloud density around it, the secondary amine cations will provide stronger interaction force with   $  [SnI_{6}]  $   inorganic octahedral structures than primary amine cations, which is extremely significant for suppressing the formation of bulk defects in Sn-based perovskites. Organic piperazine cation (  $  PZ^{2+}  $  ) as a typical secondary diamine cation with suitable size (272 pm) is very promising to get into the 3D perovskite lattice, but so far there is no related report. Song et al. reported that PZ molecular could suppress the undesirable p-doping in   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   films and improve film coverage.  $  ^{[27]}  $   Wang et al. considered that   $  PZI_{2}  $   acted as an interlayer between layered perovskites to form a 2D structure to stabilize black-  $  CsPbI_{3}  $   phase.  $  ^{[28]}  $   Likewise, Meng et al. reported that   $  PZI_{2}  $   additives linked colloids to form large pre-nucleation clusters and finally crosslinked the perovskite crystals at the grain boundaries, resulting in high-quality   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite films with reduced defects.  $  ^{[29]}  $

In this work, we for the first time demonstrate that the   $  PZ^{2+}  $   can be incorporated into 3D   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite to form   $  FA_{1-2\gamma}PZ_{2\gamma}Sn_{1-\gamma}I_{3}  $   (  $  0 \leq \gamma \leq 0.25  $  ) structure, to our knowledge, which is the largest organic cation that can largely enter 3D perovskite without reducing the dimensionality. The special perovskite structure insures the continuity of   $  [SnI_{6}]  $   inorganic octahedral structures with unobstructed carrier transport and the potent interactions between   $  PZ^{2+}  $   and   $  [SnI_{6}]  $   structures

enhance the   $  V_{Sn}  $   formation energy to effectively suppress bulk defect formation. The   $  FASnI_{3}+1\%PZ  $   films exhibit excellent semiconductor properties with suppressed p-type self-doping, decreased background carrier density, reduced trap state, intensified carrier lifetime. In addition, the introduction of   $  PZ^{2+}  $   can help to regulate semiconductor energy band structure, optimize crystallization orientation and enhance film antioxidant stability. Benefiting from the aforementioned advantages, the   $  FASnI_{3}+1\%PZ  $   device achieves an optimal PCE of 9.15% and the unencapsulated device maintains over 95% of initial PCE after aging for 1000 h in   $  N_{2}  $   golvebox.

# 2. Results and Discussion

In this study, we added 0%, 2%, 5%, and 10% PZ (molar fractions based on FA acetate) into concentrated hydroiodic acid solution of pristine FASnI  $  _{3}  $  and successfully grew corresponding powder crystals.  $  ^{[30]}  $  The scanning electron microscope (SEM) images are shown in Figure S1 in the Supporting Information. The shape of the crystals containing PZ  $  ^{2+}  $  exhibits a special symmetry, which is very similar to the “hollow” perovskite crystals in previous study and obviously different from LD perovskite crystals.  $  ^{[31]}  $  LD layered perovskite formed by cutting a specific 3D-perovskite plane preserves the features of this plane and only exhibits a thickness difference in the direction perpendicular to the plane.  $  ^{[32]}  $  Differently, the crystal morphology changes greatly with the increased PZ addition and the characteristics of the original 3D FASnI  $  _{3}  $  crystal disappear. To study the content of organic components in above powder crystals, we carried out H-nuclear magnetic resonance (  $  ^{1}  $  H-NMR) measurements and proved that the PZ  $  ^{2+}  $  are successfully incorporated into the powder crystals and the intake elevates with the increased PZ content in mother liquor. As shown in Figure 1a, the peaks located at 8.98 and 8.64 ppm belong to the -NH  $  _{2}  $  and =NH  $  _{2}  $   $  ^{+}  $  in FA  $  ^{+}  $ , respectively, and the chemical shift at 7.78 ppm is the -NH  $  _{2}  $   $  ^{+}  $ - peak in PZ  $  ^{2+}  $ . The peak of the -CH- in FA  $  ^{+}  $  is located at 7.86 ppm and the -CH  $  _{2}  $ - in PZ  $  ^{2+}  $  is at 3.32 ppm. Based on the peak area ratio of -CH- and -CH  $  _{2}  $ -, we calculated the ratio of PZ  $  ^{2+}  $  and FA  $  ^{+}  $  in corresponding powder crystals (Table S1, Supporting Information) and found that the proportion of PZ  $  ^{2+}  $  and FA  $  ^{+}  $  in crystals was higher than that in mother liquor due to the lower solubility of PZ  $  ^{2+}  $ .

Because the general passivating agents cannot get inside perovskite structure, how to deal with intrinsic bulk defects in Sn-based perovskite is a tricky issue. Our analysis has proven that the   $  PZ^{2+}  $   as one of the few cations can enter into   $  FASnI_{3}  $   structure with no dimensionality reduction, which shows great potential to tackle bulk defects in Sn-based perovskite. Moreover, the Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy (Figure S9, Supporting Information) indicates that there are effective hydrogen bond interactions between the   $  -NH_{2}^{+}-  $  groups and the   $  [SnI_{6}]  $   octahedral structures, making the C–N stretching vibration in   $  PZ^{2+}  $   shift from 1188 to 1163 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , which can enhance the electron cloud density around   $  [SnI_{6}]  $   structures to promote the   $  V_{Sn}  $   formation energy.  $  ^{[47]}  $   Importantly, the following characterization and analysis for corresponding perovskite films and devices well prove the viewpoint that the   $  PZ^{2+}  $   inside   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite can availably suppress bulk defect formation to optimize film quality and enhance device performance.

We adopted two different pathways to prepare perovskite precursor solutions. One was using the equal amount of FAI and   $  SnI_{2}  $   precursors with different   $  PZI_{2}  $   content (written as   $  FAI+SnI_{2}+xPZI_{2}  $  , x = 0%, 1%, 2%, and 5%). The other was using preprepared perovskite powder with different amount of PZ (written as   $  FASnI_{3}+xPZ  $  , x = 0%, 1%, 2%, and 5%). One step spin-coating method with chlorobenzene as antisolvent was adopted to prepare perovskite films. In the UV–vis–NIR absorption spectra for   $  FAI+SnI_{2}+xPZI_{2}  $   films (Figure S10, Supporting Information), the absorption edges blue shift with the increase of   $  PZI_{2}  $   content, similar to the phenomenon in perovskite powder (Figure 1d). In the XRD patterns for   $  FAI+SnI_{2}+xPZI_{2}  $   films (Figure S11a, Supporting Information) and   $  FASnI_{3}+xPZ  $   films (Figure S11b, Supporting Information), the (100) peaks

of the two films also shift to lower angle with the increase of   $  PZ^{2+}  $  . Above results indicate that the   $  PZ^{2+}  $   can also enter into the lattice of   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films to change optical bandgap and expand interplanar spacing. Comparing the XRD patterns of eight films in Figure 3a, the right amount of   $  PZ^{2+}  $   will optimize film crystalline quality, but excess   $  PZ^{2+}  $   will worsen crystallinity, probably due to massive destroyed   $  [SnI_{6}]  $   inorganic structures. Obviously, the   $  FASnI_{3}+1\%PZ  $   films possess the optimal orientation and the highest crystallinity, which is probably contributed to the reduced crystallization rate. Using grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) measurement, we further prove that the   $  FASnI_{3}+1\%PZ  $   film has preferred orientation in (100) plane than   $  FASnI_{3}  $   film. As shown in Figure S12 (Supporting Information), both patterns show continuous Debye–Scherrer rings in (100) plane, but the   $  FASnI_{3}+1\%PZ  $   film exhibits higher diffraction intensity in the   $  q_{z}  $   direction, indicating preferred vertical orientation with respect to the substrate, which is conducive to enhancing carrier transport and optimizing photovoltaic performance. Figure 3b and Figure S13 (Supporting Information) show the surface morphology of   $  FAI+SnI_{2}+xPZI_{2}  $   and   $  FASnI_{3}+xPZ  $   films deposited on ITO/PEDOT:PSS substrates, respectively. The grain size of two perovskite films obviously reduces with the increase of   $  PZ^{2+}  $   content. The   $  FAI+SnI_{2}  $   and   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films exhibit large grains with apparent pinholes at grain boundaries and the loose connections between grains, which is possibly caused by the slow nucleation and rapid crystallization. Introducing   $  PZ^{2+}  $   can significantly increase the nucleation accompanied by the reduction of grain size, eliminate the pinholes and enhance the connection between grains. Excessive   $  PZ^{2+}  $   will result in smaller grains and the   $  FASnI_{3}+5\%PZ  $   film shows deteriorative film morphology with many pinholes.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we demonstrate that large organic   $  PZ^{2+}  $   can be incorporated into 3D   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite to form   $  FA_{1-2\gamma}PZ_{2\gamma}Sn_{1-\gamma}I_{3}  $   (  $  0 \leq \gamma \leq 0.25  $  ) structure, which effectively insures the continuity of   $  [SnI_{6}]  $   inorganic structures with unobstructed carrier transport, enhances the   $  V_{Sn}  $   formation energy and suppresses the generation of bulk defects. The characterizations of semiconductor properties and defect state indicate that proper   $  PZ^{2+}  $   inside   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films helps to decrease background carrier density, reduce trap states, suppress p-type self-doping, and intensify carrier lifetime. Moreover, the introduction of   $  PZ^{2+}  $   can achieve semiconductor energy band structure regulation, crystal orientation optimization, and film antioxidant stability improvement. The   $  FASnI_{3}+1\%PZ  $   device exhibited an optimal PCE of 9.15%, mainly attributed to the enhancement of   $  V_{oc}  $   resulting from the reduction of bulk defects and the unencapsulated device kept over 95% of initial PCE after aging for 1000 h in   $  N_{2}  $   golvebox. Our work highlights the potential for achieving high performance Sn-based perovskite by introducing large organic cations to suppress 3D perovskite bulk defects.
