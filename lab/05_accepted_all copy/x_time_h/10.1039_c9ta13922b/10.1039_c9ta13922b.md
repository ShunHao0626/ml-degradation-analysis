# Low-temperature Interfacial Engineering for Flexible   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   Perovskite Solar Cells with High Performance Beyond 15%

All-inorganic cesium lead halide (CsPbX $ _3 $ ) perovskites exhibit superior thermal stability compared to the organic-inorganic hybrid counterparts. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been over 16%. However, the high-temperature annealing limits its feasibility on flexible devices. Here, low-temperature processed flexible CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs are designed by introducing Al-doped ZnO (AZO) as an electron-transport layer and tert-butyl cyanoacetate (t-BCA) as a passivation layer. The thickness-insensitive AZO significantly enhances the quality of perovskite films and the reproducibility of the PSCs. As well the t-BCA can effectively passivate the trap states and suppress charge recombination of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br films. The as-optimized flexible CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs exhibits a high PCE of 15.08% (with the active area of 0.1 cm $ ^2 $ ), which is one of the highest efficiency for flexible all-inorganic PSCs. The devices show outstanding stability, retaining 93% of the original PCE after being stored 60 days, 91% and 86% of the initial efficiency after continuously heating 360 hours at 85 °C and stored under 65% RH for 30 hours, respectively. In addition, the PSCs exhibits excellent mechanical stability, remains 85% of original value after 1000 bending cycles at a curvature radius of 3 mm.

In this work, it is proposed to fabricate high-efficiency flexible inorganic PSCs by interfacial engineering, which uses the Al-doped ZnO (AZO) as ETL and tert-butyl cyanoacetate (t-BCA) as an anodic passivation layer on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films. The thickness-insensitivity AZO not only provides an appropriate energy level alignment with perovskites to improve the   $  V_{oc}  $   but also enhances the quality of perovskite films and improve the reproducibility of the flexible   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   PSCs. Furthermore, the t-BCA can effectively passivate the surface trap states of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films under low-temperature process. Thus, the resultant short circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ),   $  V_{oc}  $  , fill factor (FF) and PCE of the optimized flexible PSCs (with the active area of 0.1 cm²) are up to 1.26 V, 15.87 mA/cm², 75.41% and 15.08%, respectively. The performance is one of the highest reported efficiencies for flexible inorganic PSCs. Besides, the flexible   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs are extremely stable, which maintain 85% and 91% of their original PCEs after 1000 bending with the curvature radius of 3 mm and continuously heating 360 hours at 85 °C, respectively.

# Results and discussion

We introduce the AZO as ETL and t-BCA on the perovskite films to passivate the defects for preparing flexible PSCs with superior photovoltaic performance and device stability. The device structure of PSCs (high-conductivity PEDOT:PSS (hc-PEDOT:PSS)/AZO/CsPbI $ _2 $ Br/t-BCA/PTAA/MoO $ _3 $ /Ag) is shown in Figure 1a. The hc-PEDOT:PSS shows excellent performance as flexible electrodes to replace the fragile indium tin oxide electrodes. [33] As illustrated in Figure 1b, the cyano group of t-BCA can chelate with undercoordinating Pb $ ^{2+} $  and Cs $ ^+ $  to passivate the defects on the surface of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite. Moreover, it has been demonstrated that AZO could serve well as ETL in organic-inorganic PSCs. [34,35] This also triggers us to explore its feasibility for all-inorganic PSCs. Figure 1c, Figure S2 and Figure S3 shown the energy level alignments between the perovskite and different ETLs, which results an enhancement of V $ _{oc} $ . [36-39] The UV-vis absorption spectra and PL spectra of ZnO and AZO are shown in Figure S4. As the ball-and-stick models of AZO and ZnO are present in Figure 1d, the intrinsic defects of ZnO could be efficient passivated by doping Al. [40]

To investigate the quality of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   layers based on different ETLs, the top-view scanning electron microscope (SEM) images of perovskite are firstly presented in Figure 2a-c, Figure S5 and Figure S6. The perovskite on   $  SnO_{2}  $   and AZO layer is compact, while that on ZnO has obvious pinholes. Figure 2d shows the X-ray diffraction (XRD) peaks of perovskite films on AZO and ZnO at 14.4° and 29.4°, which correspond to the (100) and (200) crystal planes of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   cubic structure. The XRD peaks of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   based on   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer at 14.2° and 29.1°, which is likely growth of I-rich perovskite crystals on   $  SnO_{2}  $   at low temperature. [41] According to the Scherrer formula:

$$
D _ {h k l} = \frac {K \lambda}{\beta_ {h k l} \cos \theta}
$$

where the   $  D_{hkl}  $   is the crystallite size in the direction perpendicular to the lattice planes, hkl are the Miller indices of the planes being analyzed, K is a numerical factor frequently referred to as the crystallite-shape factor,   $  \lambda  $   is the wavelength of the X-rays,   $  \beta_{hkl}  $   is the width (full-width at half-maximum) of the X-ray diffraction peak in radians and   $  \theta  $   is the Bragg angle. The   $  \beta_{hkl}  $   of perovskite upon AZO is lower than those upon ZnO and   $  SnO_{2}  $ , suggesting that the crystallinity of perovskite films on AZO is enhanced. [11, 42, 43] Figure 2e shows the UV-visible absorption spectra of the perovskite films with difference ETLs. The low temperature prepared perovskite films have an absorption edge at   $  \approx653  $   nm (1.9 eV), which is consistent with the previously reported results of   $  \alpha-CsPbI_{2}Br  $ . [44] The absorbance is enhanced when using AZO as ETLs. Furthermore, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) measurements are carried out to study the interfacial effects of difference ETLs on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films. As illustrated in Figure 2f, the perovskites upon AZO film exhibit higher efficient PL quenching than that based on ZnO and   $  SnO_{2}  $ , whatever the excitation light from air side (AS) or glass side (GS), suggesting a stronger electron extraction capability of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films based on AZO. Comparing the PL intensity of the excitation light from AS and GS of difference ETLs, the perovskite on ZnO and   $  SnO_{2}  $   films displayed a strong PL intensity at the top side, while the PL emission at the GS with a weak signal. The PL signals of perovskite on AZO from different sides are similar. It demonstrates that both the top and bottom surfaces have the similar optical property, and the perovskites upon AZO grows well. [31] And the thickness of AZO is up to 56 nm, the thickness-insensitivity AZO is more suitable for future roll-to-roll fabrication. [32] So far, the effects of the flexible PSCs performance with different ETLs are evaluated in Figure S1 and Table S1, the device based on AZO shows the highest PCE (12.74%).

As for further improving the efficiency of flexible  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  PSCs, a passivation layer is prepared on the surface of perovskite films. The XRD peaks of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  films remain unaltered after t-BCA layer passivating, which means the t-BCA does not incorporate into and perturb the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  crystal lattice (Figure S7). However, a slightly increased intensity of the main diffraction peaks is observed, suggesting enhanced texturing or crystallinity of perovskites. In addition, we evaluate the dynamics of the charge recombination via a variety of measurements including steady state PL, time-resolved PL (TRPL) decay and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). As shown in Figure 3a, the perovskite films with t-BCA layer show a blue-shifted PL peak (643 nm) compared with the perovskite without t-BCA (647 nm). The interfacial charge transfer properties in PSCs are investigated by EIS measurements. Herein, the EIS spectra are measured at an applied bias of   $  V_{oc}  $   with a frequency range from 1 MHz to 100 Hz and 30 mV AC amplitude under working condition. The Nyquist plots from the impedance spectra and the corresponding circuit model of the PSCs with and without t-BCA layer are shown in Figure 3c and Figure S8, respectively. The PSCs based on the t-BCA layer suffer larger recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) values than the control devices.  $  ^{[49]}  $   So the recombination process could be effectively suppressed by the t-BCA (Table S2), which indicates that the enhanced charge transport properties and reduced recombination rate result in the efficiency improvement of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with t-BCA layer. Figure 3d-f show that the Cs 3d and Pb 4f peaks both shift toward lower binding energy while the N 1s peak from the t-BCA shifts toward higher

In addition, this interaction reduces the content of metallic lead in perovskite films, which further suggests the t-BCA can passivate the perovskite surface trap states.  $  ^{[6, 50]}  $

The reverse current density of the device with t-BCA layer is reduced by one order of magnitude relative to the control device, which indicates the increased shunt resistance and the suppressed charge carrier recombination and leakage current. In order to further examine the passivation effect of t-BCA layer on perovskite, several characterization techniques including the trap-state density based on a space-charge limited current (SCLC) measurement model and light intensity-dependent  $ J - V $  measurement are illustrated in Figure 5. The trap state density  $ (N_{\mathrm{t}}) $  are calculated via the trap-filling limited voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{TFL}}) $  using the equation

$$
N _ {t} = \frac {2 \varepsilon_ {0} V _ {T F L} \varepsilon_ {r}}{q L ^ {2}}
$$

(2)

where  $  q  $  is the electron charge,  $  L  $  is the thickness of the perovskite layer,  $  \varepsilon  $  is the relative dielectric constant,[51, 52] and  $  \varepsilon_0  $  is the vacuum permittivity. For the electron-only device, as shown in Figure 5a, the defect-state density was  $  1.32 \times 10^{16} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}  $  for the device without t-BCA layer and  $  1.01 \times 10^{16}  $  for the device with t-BCA layer. The results of the hole-only device show that the  $  V_{\mathrm{TFL}}  $  is reduced from  $  0.82\mathrm{V}  $  to  $  0.67\mathrm{V}  $  with t-BCA passivation (Figure 5b), which verifies the effective passivation by t-BCA. The device structures are shown in Figure S13. Furthermore, the relationships between photoelectric response and Incident light intensity (I) are investigated to estimate the recombination reactions (Figure 5c-d). The recombination mechanism can be understood according to the equations

$$
J _ {s c} \propto I ^ {\alpha} (\alpha \leq 1)
$$

(3)

and

$$
V _ {o c} = \varepsilon K T \ln (I) / q
$$

(4)

where  $ \alpha $  and  $ \varepsilon $  are ideal factor related to recombination,  $ K $  is the Boltzmann constant,  $ T $  is Kelvin temperature,  $ q $  is the elementary charge. Under the open-circuit conditions, the deviation of slope from 1 KT/q indicates the occurrence of trap-assisted recombination. [53] The PSCs with t-BCA layer show a smaller slope of  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  versus the natural logarithm of light intensity (1.58 KT/q) than the control one (1.84 KT/q), revealing the reduction in trap-assisted recombination.

The value of   $  \alpha  $   for the PSCs without space charge effect will be close to 1, and the PSCs with t-BCA layer (  $  \alpha=0.932  $  ) are subjected a smaller space charge effect than the control devices (  $  \alpha=0.913  $  ), revealing the decrease of bimolecular recombination.  $  ^{[54, 55]}  $   These results prove that the efficient defect passivation of the perovskite with t-BCA layer can restrain charge recombination, thus favoring the improvement of PV performance and device stability.

To investigate the mechanical stability, the mechanical durability of flexible   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with t-BCA layer is tested by the bending measurement with different curvature radii and cycle times. Figure 6a shows the normalized PCE versus different bending curvature radii, and the inset shows the photograph of the flexible PSCs. The J-V curves of the flexible PSCs before and after bending at curvature radii of 3 mm for 1000 cycles are shown in Figure 6b. The PCE of the flexible PSCs exhibits no significant degradation after bending the curvature radius of 12 mm. In addition, the flexible PSCs show excellent mechanical flexibility. There is only about 15% attenuation of PCE value after 1000 bending cycles at a curvature radius of 3 mm.

# Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated a significant improvement in device performance and stability of flexible   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs via introducing AZO as ETL and t-BCA as an anodic passivation layer. The perovskite films deposited on AZO reveal a stronger electron extraction capability and adjustable energy level alignment. Moreover, the t-BCA can effectively passivate the perovskite surface trap states and enhance the charge transport properties. Thus, as-optimized the flexible   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs shows a high PCE of 15.08% at 0.1 cm  $  ^{2}  $  . This strategy provides a new avenue towards upscaling the flexible inorganic PSCs for further applications.
