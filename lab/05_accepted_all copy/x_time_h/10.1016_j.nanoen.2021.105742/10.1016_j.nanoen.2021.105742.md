# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are booming in view of longer carrier diffusion length, higher optical absorption coefficient, and low fabrication cost   $  [1-8]  $  . A validated photoelectric conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.2% has been recently reported   $  [9]  $  . In general, most of PSCs with more than 23% certified PCE are radically based on conventional (n-i-p) device configurations   $  [10-13]  $  . In comparison, the inverted devices (p-i-n) possess better repeatability and alleviated hysteresis   $  [4,14,15]  $  , which makes them attract widespread attention. Bai et al.   $  [16]  $   prepared the 3D–2D graded perovskite solar cells with an overall PCE of 19.89%. Luo et al.   $  [17]  $   introduced a method to prepare perovskite films by using a solution-processed secondary growth (SSG) technique, and the inverted device achieves a PCE close to 21%. Recently, Zheng et al.   $  [18]  $   presented a physical lead reduction (PLR) concept and predicted that the theoretical PCE of inverted device is still greater than 25% when attenuating the perovskite thickness to one-third.

# ABSTRACT

Numerous defects may occur during the preparation of perovskite films, and these defects obstruct the charge transfer and accelerate the decomposition of films. Herein, the 1-propionate-4-amino-1,2,4-triazolium tetrafluoroborate (PATMBF $ _{4} $ ) is employed as a multifunctional additive for caesium (Cs), methylammonium (MA) and formamidinium (FA) triple-cation-based perovskites (CsFAMA) to prepare inverted planar solar cells. The amino group (-NH $ _{2} $ ) in PATM $ ^{+} $  can donate free electron pair to form a Pb-N coordination bond with perovskite, which can not only regulate crystal growth, but also decrease the generation of charge recombination centers to make charge transfer more effective. The hydrogen bond formed between the carbonyl group (CO) contained in PATM $ ^{+} $  and FA $ ^{+} $  (O⋯H-N) restrains the migration of FA $ ^{+} $  to the film surface, thus retarding the decomposition of film. Hence, the photoelectric conversion efficiency (PCE) of the champion device is as high as 21.35%. In addition, the PCE can sustain over 85% after exposure to air for 4000 h with a relative humidity of 40 ± 5%, and only drops by 10% in the case of 85 °C more than 1000 h (N $ _{2} $ ). It can retain 90% of PCE under the full-spectrum sunlight for 800 h (N $ _{2} $ ).

However, numerous defects are likely generated on the grain boundaries (GBs) and surface, such as vacancies, gaps and substitution defects caused by ion migration, due to the low formation energy of defects in perovskite   $  [19,20]  $  . Moreover, temperature-induced perovskite decomposition accelerates the out-diffusion of organic cations (FA  $  ^{+}  $  : formamidine cation, MA  $  ^{+}  $  : methylamine cation) in the form of volatile derivatives, leading to PbI  $  _{2}  $   enrichment in the perovskite film, which could trigger trap-assisted recombination, exacerbate ions migration, and degrade perovskite   $  [21,22]  $  . To overcome the above problems, scholars have carried out considerable investigations and found that thermodynamics and growth kinetics of the perovskite films can be modulated by additive engineering   $  [23–30]  $  , such as polymers   $  [31,32]  $  , ammonium salts   $  [33,34]  $  , low-dimensional perovskite   $  [35,36]  $  , Lewis acid   $  [37,38]  $  , Lewis base   $  [39–41]  $  , and ionic liquids   $  [42,43]  $  , etc. Among, ionic liquids stand out due to their high ionic conductivity as well as high thermal stability and electrochemical stability   $  [22]  $  . # 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Synthesis of ionic liquid

The 4-amino-1,2,4-triazolium and Ethyl 2-chloropropionate were dissolved in acetonitrile with a 1:2 molar ratio. The solution was churned at 65 °C for 60 h, rotary evaporated, and then vacuum dried at 55 °C for 3 days, a yellow viscous liquid was obtained. Next, the obtained substance was dissolved in acetone with sodium fluoroborate in a molar ratio of 1:1 and whisked at 30 °C for 24 h, suction filtered, distilled off the solvent under decompressing. Then, the product was washed with dichloromethane, the precipitate was removed by suction filtration, the filtrate was subjected to vacuum distillation, obtained a light-yellow oily solution, dried at 55 °C for 3 days under vacuum. Eventually, the PATMBF  $  _{4}  $  with fluoroborate as negative ion and triazolium as positive ion was synthesized, and its relative molecular mass is 272.03. Nuclear magnetic resonance (  $  ^{1}  $  H NMR) spectrum of PATMBF  $  _{4}  $ : 1.048 (s, 3H), 1.196 (t, 3H), 2.331 (s, H), 4.196 (q, 2H), 5.687 (s, 2H), 8.412 (s, 1H), 8.580 (s, 1H).

# 3. Results and discussion

We designed and synthesized the PATMBF $ _{4} $  with amino as well as carbonyl functional groups. The synthesis routes are exhibited in Scheme 1a, and the specific synthesis details are presented in the Experimental Section. The PATMBF $ _{4} $  was blended into the caesium (Cs), methylammonium (MA) and formamidinium (FA) triple-cation-based perovskite (CsFAMA) precursor solution to fabricate light absorbing layer. Throughout this dissertation, the term CsFAMA/PATMBF $ _{4} $  and CsFAMA will be used to refer to samples with or without PATMBF $ _{4} $ , respectively. Fig. 1a and b show the surface morphology of the CsFAMA and CsFAMA/PATMBF $ _{4} $  films by scanning electron microscope (SEM). There is a rather large difference in the grain sizes, and the corresponding grain size distribution is illustrated in Fig. 1c. The CsFAMA film shows more GBs, and its grain sizes are mostly about 100–200 nm. For CsFAMA/PATMBF $ _{4} $  film, the significantly increased grain sizes and decreased GBs indicate that the PATMBF $ _{4} $  could better control the quality of perovskite film. Importantly, most of the grains in CsFAMA/PATMBF $ _{4} $  film penetrate the entire cross section, which provides favorable conditions for the transport of photogenerated carriers (Fig. 1d and e). We adopted energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) mapping to confirm the distribution of PATMBF $ _{4} $  in perovskite film. As illustrated in Fig. 1f and g, the spatial distribution of B and F elements (characteristic elements in PATMBF $ _{4} $ ) is consistent with the Pb and I elements, with relatively low content in film and dense distribution near GBs, indicating the PATMBF $ _{4} $  additives are incorporated into the entire perovskite film [52].

Moreover, we tested the X-ray diffraction (XRD) spectra to deeply study the crystallinity of perovskite. We observe the same diffraction peaks from Fig. 1h, illustrating that the addition of additives makes no difference on the phase composition of film, which means most of the PATMBF $ _4 $  molecules remain on the surface or exist near the GBs [53,54]. Whereas the intensity of the main diffraction peaks increases and half-peak breadth decreases, revealing that the crystallinity of perovskite is improved and its particle size enlarges (Fig. 1i) [26,55]. The above results are consistent with the observations from SEM and EDS. Besides, the diffraction peak near 12.7° corresponds to PbI $ _2 $  produced by incomplete conversion or decomposition in CsFAMA perovskite film [31,56]. In contrast, the characteristic peak signal disappears after incorporating PATMBF $ _4 $ , which could be due to the increased conversion rate of PbI $ _2 $  or the decomposition of perovskite film is inhibited [46]. In addition, the UV-Vis absorption ability of the CsFAMA/PATMBF $ _4 $  film is enhanced, which might be attributable to the improved grain size and better crystallinity of perovskite film (Fig. 1j). Given the above, there is a certain interaction between PATMBF $ _4 $  and perovskite, which can regulate the growth of grain, and thus forming a high-quality film with small GBs and large grains [57].

The high-resolution X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) tests were enforced to investigate the interaction between PATMBF $ _4 $  and perovskite. The broad spectra of perovskite films are listed in Fig. S1 in the Supporting Information. The Pb 4f XPS spectrum of CsFAMA/PATMBF $ _4 $  film in Fig. 2a reveals shift in the signals of valence electrons, manifesting that there is a coordination bond between the Pb element and the PATMBF $ _4 $  [15]. Moreover, the XPS spectrum of Pb 4f in CsFAMA film shows two small shoulder peaks caused by unsaturated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  [44]. After adding PATMBF $ _4 $  to the precursor solution, the shoulder peaks are disappeared, suggesting the interaction reduces the presence of unsaturated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $ , which has a favorable effect on the transmission of photogenic carriers. The stability of device is a critical factor for commercialization. Long-term exposure to air or under heating, organic cations will accelerate the migration to surface of film, thereby damaging the device. We conducted a series of studies on unpackaged devices to explore their stability. Fig. 5b compares the environmental stability of devices. After exposure in air with a relative humidity of  $ 40 \pm 5\% $  for  $ 2000\mathrm{h} $ , the PCE of CsFAMA/PATMBF $ _4 $  device exceeds the case of CsFAMA device, and still remains  $ 90\% $  of its original PCE. When the exposure time is  $ 4000\mathrm{h} $ , the PCE of the optimized device remains  $ 85\% $ , the control device is  $ 40\% $ . The CsFAMA/PATMBF $ _4 $  device was heated at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for more than  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  in a nitrogen atmosphere, its PCE is only reduced by  $ 10\% $ , while the PCE of CsFAMA device is down to  $ 50\% $  of the original value (Fig. 5c). We also heat-treated the perovskite films and monitored the changes in their UV-Vis absorption ability, as shown in the Fig. S6. With the increase of heating time, the UV-Vis absorption ability of the CsFAMA film continues to decrease, but the CsFAMA/PATMBF $ _4 $  film changes little. Fig. S7 shows thermogravimetric analysis spectra of PATMBF $ _4 $ , the initial decomposition temperature of PATMBF $ _4 $  is around  $ 220^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , and the maximum decomposition temperature is  $ 300^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , which is beneficial to the thermal stability of the device. In addition, we measured the light stability of devices under full-spectrum sunlight at room temperature, as shown in Fig. 5d. The CsFAMA/PATMBF $ _4 $  device maintains  $ 90\% $  of PCE after more than  $ 800\mathrm{h} $  of irradiation, while the CsFAMA device is only  $ 60\% $ . The main reason for better stability can be that the interaction between PATMBF $ _4 $  and perovskite can effectively stabilize the perovskite lattice, delay the decomposition of the device during long-term placement and inhibit the increase in trap density. From Fig. 5a, after photoaging treatment, the change in the tDOS value of device with PATMBF $ _4 $  is negligible, while the tDOS of control device increases significantly throughout the range. This phenomenon further confirms the reason for the excellent stability of CsFAMA/PATMBF $ _4 $  device.

# 4. Conclusions

To sum up, we have designed and synthesized a multifunctional material named PATMBF $ _4 $ , which can remarkably promote the performance of inverted planar PSCs, and the PCE of CsFAMA/PATMBF $ _4 $  device is up to 21.35%. Without any encapsulation, the CsFAMA/PATMBF $ _4 $  device exhibits promising thermal and environmental stability. Our research confirms that the interaction between -NH $ _2 $  in PATM $ ^+ $  and Pb $ ^{2+} $  restrain the generation of charge recombination centers. Simultaneously, there exists hydrogen bond interaction between PATM $ ^+ $  and FA $ ^+ $ , which is beneficial to immobilize FA $ ^+ $ , retarding the decomposition of the perovskite film. Our research highlights the potential of multifunctional ionic liquid for superior performance PSCs, and it provides a new direction for the application of ionic liquids in optoelectronic device.
