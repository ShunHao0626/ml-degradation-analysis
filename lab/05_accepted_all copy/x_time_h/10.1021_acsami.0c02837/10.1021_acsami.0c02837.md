# ABSTRACT

Inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with   $  C_{60}  $   framework are known for their common drawback of low power conversion efficiency (PCE) < 20% because of non-radiative recombination and inefficient charge transport at their perovskite interfaces. Here, we report an ultra-thin [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) as a cap layer on perovskite films to overcome this issue. Such a functional cap layer efficiently passivates trap states and establishes a gradient energy level alignment onto perovskite, facilitating the efficient charge transfer and extraction. The as-fabricated inverted PSCs capped with such ultra-thin PCBM exhibit a record PCE of 20.07%. After the storage under   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere for more than 500 hours, the PCE of PSCs retains over 85% of its initial level. Our work provides an effective method to upgrade inverted PSCs with the   $  C_{60}  $   framework with improved efficiency and stability.

Keywords: Interface Engineering, Inverted Perovskite solar cell, Cascaded energy level, Defect passivation, High efficiency, Ultra-thin PCBM.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Researchers have made rapid developments of organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) in the past decade. From the first use of perovskite by Miyasaka et al. $ ^{1} $  as the light absorber in solar cells, the certificated power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has roared up to 25.2% $  ^{2}  $ , which is extremely close to the Shockley–Queisser (S-Q) limit of single-junction solar cells. Recently, PSCs with an inverted planar p-i-n structure received extensive attentions due to their ease of fabrication $ ^{3-6} $ , hysteresis free $ ^{7,8} $ , compatibility with a roll-to-roll process $ ^{9,10} $  and the possibility fabrication of multi-junction cells $ ^{11} $ , etc. However, most of inverted planar p-i-n structured PSCs suffer from low efficiencies (< 20%), though a surge of progress has been made towards high quality perovskite films. In fact, the performance of PSCs depends not only on the quality of film of each functional layer $ ^{12} $  but it also depends on the interfaces between them $ ^{13-15} $ . For high efficiency PSCs, a carrier trap-free perovskite film, as well as a proper energy level alignment between the perovskite and charge transport layers (CTLs), is highly desired. It has been reported that the traps, dominated by under-coordinated lead centers, halide vacancies and Pb-I antisites, are mainly located at the surface of perovskite film $ ^{16} $ . Such traps, serving as the carrier recombination centers, contribute to the main thermal loss in PSCs that hinders the open-circuit voltage (V $ _{\text{OC}} $ ) toward the Shockley-Queisser limit. On the other hand, an unbalanced and insufficient charge extraction caused by mismatched energy level alignment can make the thermal loss in PSCs even more, particularly, it can induce a severe hysteresis in PSCs. Therefore, engineering the interface in order to eliminate the interfacial traps and establish a cascade energy level alignment is expected to substantially improve the performance of PSCs $ ^{21,28} $ .

It has been reported that organic/inorganic materials $ ^{6,17,18,19} $  (such as PMMA, PS, lead sulfide, etc.) can work as the passivation layer to reduce the traps in the perovskite through protonation (or de-protonation) reactions/chemical bonds. However, the passivation layers, such as PMMA, PS, and  $ \mathrm{PbSO_4} $ , are insulators in nature; they potentially inhibit the charge transport and increase the series resistance ( $ \mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{S}} $ ) in PSCs. This leads to reduced short circuit current densities ( $ \mathbb{J}_{\mathbb{SC}} $ ) and relatively low fill factors (FF) of PSCs. Optimizing the energy level alignment at the interface of perovskite/CTLs by using single-walled carbon nanotubes $ ^{20} $ , perovskite quantum dots $ ^{21} $ , etc. as the interlayer, can effectively promote the carrier transport and extraction efficiency. However, due to the weak passivation effect of these interlayers, trap-assisted carrier recombination loss yet remains $ ^{22,23} $ . Most devices use fullerene and its derivatives as the electron transport layer, and there have been many works that used PCBM as the electron transport layer $ ^{24-30} $ . However, PCBM-based PSCs are still unsatisfactory due to some issues. Firstly, the morphology of the PCBM film is subject to underlying perovskite film. The rough perovskite film will cause the formation of discontinuous PCBM film, which is inferior to devices efficiency $ ^{27,28} $ . Secondly, the thickness of the PCBM is also one of the factors that affect the performance of the device. Seok et al. adjusted the thickness of the PCBM, and finally found that the thinner PCBM can obtain better performance while maintaining uniform film coverage $ ^{30} $ . Besides, low electron mobility of PCBM may cause another issue toward high-performance PSCs $ ^{44} $ . To solve these problems, previous researchers mainly concentrated on modifying fullerene derivatives via molecular design. This method involved a complex synthesis and purification process that could hardly predict the functions of the target molecules. Therefore, facile and effective methods that can simultaneously eliminate the interfacial traps

In this work, ultra-thin [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) was adopted as a perovskite surface modifier and simultaneously passivation layer to ameliorate the interface between perovskite and  $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  layers; such an ultra-thin layer has a thickness of less than  $ 3\mathrm{nm} $  (observed by Atomic Force Microscopy, Figure S1). The ultra-thin PCBM heals the pinholes and smoothens the rough surface of the perovskite films eliminating surface traps. In addition, it optimizes the energy level alignment at the interface of perovskite/ $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  by regulating the energy levels of the top surface of perovskite films. Besides, the perovskite films, modified by the ultra-thin PCBM, give enhanced carrier lifetime compared to pristine samples as measured by time-resolved photoluminescence spectra (TRPL). Owing to such efficient surface passivation and optimized energy level alignment, a champion PCE of over  $ 20\% $  for the final PSCs achieved with an obsessive  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  of  $ 1.12\mathrm{V} $ . The constructed photovoltaic devices showed prohibited hysteresis and improved stability.

# 4. CONCLUSIONS

We demonstrated a facile strategy to achieve high performance inverted PSCs by using an ultra-thin PCBM to modify the surface of perovskite films. Such ultra-thin PCBM treatment induced an optimized surface morphology of as-formed perovskite films and fewer surface defects. In addition, ultra-thin PCBM regulated the energy levels of the top layers of perovskite. An alleviated band alignment was achieved inside the perovskite film which favored the charge transport and extraction. Finally, a champion efficiency of  $ 20.07\% $  was achieved for PSCs, while the ultra-thin PCBM treated PSCs possessed improved stability. These desirable properties pave a new way for further prospective p-i-n PSCs.
