# 1. Introduction

The materials and device engineering have thoroughly been devoted to organometallic halide perovskite semiconductors, offering perovskite solar cells (PSCs) as a compelling photovoltaic technology in this field. In recent years, the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has been achieved to  $ >25\% $  which is comparable to commercialized photovoltaic materials such as amorphous silicon or copper-indium-gallium-selenides [1-4]. The interface engineering of PSCs is a key strategy for achieving high efficiency and long-term stability because the interfaces in the device play a decisive role in determining charge transport, recombination, energy loss of PSCs [5]. Depending on the structure and electrical properties of the interfacial materials, the PSCs can be classified into three typical architectures: mesoporous structure, planar  $ n - i - p $  (conventional) structure, and  $ p - i - n $  (inverted) structure [6-9]. While the record efficiencies of the state-of-the-art PSCs are made in the well-established conventional device structure, the PCEs of the inverted devices are rather inferior mainly due to the un-optimized

The optimal electron transport layer (ETL) is a crucial component in the inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs) for achieving high photovoltaic performance and device stability. We herein address the alloy ETL strategy, which incorporates a non-fullerene small molecule, ITIC-4F, into the fullerene-based ETL to optimize the electron-selective interfacial properties of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  -based inverted PSCs. The alloy ETL exhibited tailored energy level alignment at the interface of perovskite and ETL, so the electron-extraction/recombination behavior is greatly improved. Also, the hydrophobic surface of the alloy ETL effectively impedes not only moisture invasion but also morphological deterioration under continuous operation, so the resultant  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  -based inverted device modified with the alloy ETL exhibited superior power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to  $ 19.99\% $  ,with a negligible hysteresis. The alloy ETL-based PSCs also achieves  $ 25.1\% $  efficiency under indoor lighting condition, which promises a feasible design strategy of interfacial optimization in high-performance PSCs using organic semiconductors.

Two interfaces, hole transport layer (HTL) and electron transport layer (ETL), are present in the inverted device to accommodate efficient charge transport and effective blocking of opposite carrier. The inorganic and organic materials have been utilized in HTL of the inverted PSCs, which are advantageous in achieving optimized photocurrent generation and high device stability   $  [10–14]  $  . Meanwhile, the fullerene derivatives exclusively have been used as ETL materials in inverted device owing to superb function in electron transport and facile solution processing   $  [15–19]  $  . The outstanding passivation effect of   $  [6,6]  $  -phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester   $  \left(\mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM}\right)  $   on surface or grain boundary of the perovskite absorber contributes to suppressed charge recombination and reduced energy loss in electron transport   $  [20,21]  $  . Other fullerene derivatives or n-doping strategy of fullerene ETL have also been reported for improving the electron transport and the trap passivation of the interface of PSCs   $  [22–24]  $  . Nevertheless, the constrained structural variation of fullerene may limit further improvement of electronic properties to achieve high photovoltaic performance of PSCs. For the past decade, n-type organic small molecules have been studied to work well in PSCs as an alternative to the fullerene-based ETLs   $  [25]  $  . The non-fullerene acceptors (NFAs) with a typical multifused ladder-type structure exhibit tunable energy levels and controlled optoelectronic properties which contribute to enhancing the PCE of organic solar cells. Besides, the electron-donating functional groups (e.g., cyano or carbonyl groups) onto the NFAs work as Lewis base which can passivate the under-coordinated antisites of Pb-I in perovskite   $  [26,27]  $  . However, the investigations on NFAs for the use of PSCs particularly as ETLs have not completely replaced the fullerene derivatives due to relatively inferior fill factor (FF) and open circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  )   $  [28,29]  $  . The typical low band gap (  $  E_{g}  $  ) of NFAs would not provide enough energy offset for efficient hole blocking, resulting in substantial exciton quenching and energy loss at the interface of the NFA-based PSCs. The bulky solubilizing groups of NFAs intrinsically limit densely-packed structure, so inefficient electron transport to the cathode might induce unexpected charge recombination at the interface of the device. Nevertheless, NFAs provide more chances of stabilizing the optoelectronic properties under the harsh condition with UV light and thermal stress. Thus, a possible solution to address the issues associated with NFAs to achieve highly efficient and stable PSCs is the use of alloy ETLs of   $  PC_{61}BM  $   and NFAs, where two different molecules provides a synergistic benefits of improved photovoltaic properties and enhanced operative stability in alloy ETL of PSCs.

Recently, a few reports adopting ITIC to fabricated mixed ETLs with PC $ _{61} $ BM has been reported, and the alloy ETLs yielded better PCEs by facilitating the charge transport and collection properties [30,31]. However, those reports did not thoroughly study the rationale of mixed ETLs, and their PCEs were still unsatisfactory as compared to those with the fullerene-based inverted PSCs. Taking all these factors into consideration, we herein address an organic alloy ETL based on PC $ _{61} $ BM and a NFA has been designed and fabricated in the inverted PSCs. In the alloy ETL, 3,9-bis(2-methylene-((3-(1,1-dicyanomethylene)-6,7-difluoro)-indanone))-5,5,11,11-tetrakis(4-hexylphenyl)-dithieno[2,3-d:2',3'-d']-s-indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b')dithiophene (ITIC-4F) is selected as a NFA component to modulate energy levels, optoelectronic properties, and hydrophobicity of the ETL because of the unique advantages such as intrinsic high degree of order, molecular packing, and electron mobilities as compared to ITIC. We refer to the report of Liu et al., that the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level of ITIC was shifted downward by introducing the fluorine substituent [32]. The fluorinated NFA possesses strong hydrophobicity, which will contribute to passivation of underlying perovskite layer against to humidity. Furthermore, ITIC-4F could passivate the defect sites at the surface of perovskite films where ITIC-4F can donate electron density to the Pb $ ^{2+} $  and form a coordinate or dative covalent bond, effectively neutralizing the excess positive charge in the crystals [33]. The alloy ETL of PC $ _{61} $ BM and ITIC-4F provided optimal electrical properties and tailored electronic structure of ETLs, which contribute to improved electron extraction and favorable electron transport properties. The optimized alloy ETL boosted the PCEs of the inverted PSCs up to 19.99% with an open circuit voltage (V $ _{OC} $ ) of 1.08 V, a short-circuit current (J $ _{SC} $ ) of 23.73 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and a fill factor (FF) of 0.78 under 1-sun illumination, those were superior to those of single component-based ETLs (16.71 and 12.17% for neat PC $ _{61} $ BM and ITIC-4F, respectively). The alloy ETL also delivered better photovoltaic properties under indoor lighting condition with high operative stability over the PC $ _{61} $ BM-based one. Furthermore, low surface free energies (SFEs) of the alloy ETL afforded remarkably enhanced ambient and thermal stability of the devices.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

The nickel oxide solution was prepared by dissolving 122.22 mg of Ni (OCOCH $ _3 $ ) $ _2 $ ·4H $ _2 $ O (Sigma-Aldrich, 0.49 mmol) in 2-methoxyethanol (Sigma-Aldrich, 6.3 mL), and then monoethanolamine (Sigma-Aldrich, 30 μL) was added in the mixture. The solution was stirred for 2 h at room temperature. For perovskite absorber, PbI $ _2 $  (Perovskite grade, Sigma-Aldrich), CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I (Perovskite grade, TCI chemicals), and dimethyl sulfoxide (anhydrous, Sigma-Aldrich) were mixed with 1:1:1 M ratio in dimethyl formamide (anhydrous, Sigma-Aldrich) at the concentration of 1.4 M at room temperature. The solutions were filtered with a PTFE-D filter (a pore size of 0.2 μm) before spin coating.

# 2.2. Fabrication of Thin-Film perovskite solar cells

The ITO-coated glass substrates (sheet resistance   $  \sim20\ \Omega\ sq^{-1}  $  ) were cleaned sequentially with water, acetone, and isopropanol under sonication for 20 min, and treated with UV-  $  O_{3}  $  .   $  NiO_{x}  $   layer was formed by spin-coating the precursor solution (filtered with a PTFE-D filter with a pore size of 0.2   $  \mu  $  m) on ITO-coated glass (3000 rpm, 60 s), and then the films were annealed at 340 °C for 1 h in ambient air. After cooling the substrate to room temperature, the perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated by 2-step at 500 rpm for 5 s and then, 5000 rpm for 25 s to form a compact perovskite layer with a thickness of   $  \sim400  $   nm. After 7 s, 400   $  \mu  $  L of anhydrous chlorobenzene was poured onto the substrate during spin coating and then, the film was annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. The ETLs were prepared by casting the solution of ITIC-4F and PC  $  _{61}  $  BM in chlorobenzene at a varied ratio. Bis-C60 surfactant (2 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in isopropyl alcohol) was sequentially deposited at 3000 rpm for 30 s as a cathode modifier [34–36]. (Figure S1 for chemical structure of Bis-C60) Finally, 100 nm of Ag was evaporated under high vacuum (less than   $  5\times10^{-6}  $   Torr). The device area is defined as 10 mm  $  ^{2}  $   by the shadow mask.

As compared to PC $ _{61} $ BM, ITIC-4F is a favorable  $ n $ -type semiconductor due to its high-lying energy levels, higher electron affinity, and decent electron conductivity and mobility, and thus ITIC-4F has been regarded as a promising alternative to fullerene acceptor in organic solar cells (OSCs). Nevertheless, NFAs have not completely replaced PC $ _{61} $ BM in inverted PSCs because they did not deliver comparable PCEs to the fullerene-based PSCs [37]. Alternatively, we herein designed an alloy ETL in which PC $ _{61} $ BM and ITIC-4F were mixed to combine the advantages of each component to combine their advantages (Scheme 1c). Fig. 1a-f show the surface topologic images of the ETL thin films with a varied weight ratio of PC $ _{61} $ BM to ITIC-4F. The neat PC $ _{61} $ BM (10.0:0.0) and neat ITIC-4F (0.0:10.0) films exhibited roughness ( $ \sigma_{\text{RMS}} $ ) of 10.16 and 6.96 nm, respectively. Meanwhile, the alloy ETLs exhibited roughness in the range of 6–8 nm which is smoother than the neat PC $ _{61} $ BM layer. It is noted that the rough surface of the MAPbI $ _3 $  layer possesses roughness exceeding 20 nm, as shown in Figure S2, and thus the flat and smooth surface of the alloy ETL would be beneficial for providing favorable electron transfer and extraction to cathode in the device. The charge transfer properties of the alloy ETLs were studied by monitoring photoluminescence (PL) quenching of the perovskite layer with different ETLs. As shown in Fig. 1g, the characteristic emission peak of MAPbI $ _3 $  was centered at 760 nm, and the PL intensity was significantly decreased with the presence of ETLs. In specific, the alloy ETL that contains 5 wt% of ITIC-4F exhibited the least PL emission, indicating facilitated electron transfer as compared to the neat PC $ _{61} $ BM or ITIC-4F-based ETLs. On the other hand, the alloy ETL with the higher ratio of ITIC-4F led to less quenching efficiency, thus we confirmed the most optimized electron transfer capability to be the composition of 9.5:0.5 for PC $ _{61} $ BM and ITIC-4F, respectively, in the alloy ETL. Despite the excellent photovoltaic performance, the stability in the ambient condition is the most challenging issue for the commercialization of PSCs due to the native hygroscopic property of the perovskite semiconductors. For the PSCs with an inverted architecture, the resistive properties of the ETL are critical factors for protecting the devices to degradation since the ETL layer is mainly exposed to the degradation agents such as moisture or oxygen in the air. In this regard, the influence of the alloy ETL on the shelf-stability of the photovoltaic performance for the PSCs was evaluated. Fig. 5a shows PCE decay of the devices with different ETLs under continuous light soaking (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) in the ambient atmosphere. The device with a ITIC-4F exhibited a gradual degradation in ambient condition, which results in poor performance below 30% of the initial value under irradiation for 120 min. In comparison, PC  $  _{61}  $  BM and the alloy ETL provided better ambient stability without severe PCE degradation under continuous irradiation, which retained ~72% and 87% of initial efficiencies after 120 min, respectively. Notably, the alloy ETL not only boosted the photovoltaic performance of the inverted devices but also afforded superior ambient stability under continuous irradiation. To work out the rationale behind the stability improvement, the contact angle measurement for different ETLs was compared. As displayed in Fig. 5b, the water contact angles were 75.2°, 80.5°, and 86.5° for the neat PC  $  _{61}  $  BM, the alloy ETL, and the neat ITIC-4F, respectively, which reveals the hydrophobic surface of ITIC-4F. We quantified the surface tension of three ETL films deposited on the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  layer by van Oss-Chaudhry-Good (vOCG) method as shown in Figure S5 [47]. (Table S5) The SFE for PC  $  _{61}  $  BM (48.1 mJ m  $  ^{-2}  $  ) was decreased to 46.1 mJ m  $  ^{-2}  $  as 5 wt% of ITIC-4F was employed, which indicates that the addition of ITIC-4F into PC  $  _{61}  $  BM exhibits the effect of stability on penetration of oxidizing agents. However, the PCE decline occurred more rapidly in the ITIC-4F-based device despite high surface tension. To further understand the ambient stability of the devices under continuous illumination, the morphology variation of the ETLs under 1 sun illumination was compared. (Fig. 5c) The fresh ETL films exhibited the compact surface morphology with roughness less than 10 nm regardless of the composition. However, the irradiation for 120 min yielded coarse surface morphology with much-increased roughness in the ETLs with a single component, which may be attributed significant

self-aggregation and photodimerization of the   $  PC_{61}BM  $   or ITIC-4F, while the alloy ETL exhibited a rather smooth surface with the roughness of 12.89 nm [48]. This result indicates that the alloy ETL is relatively insensitive to the light/thermal bias in ambient condition for long-time, which may arise from the low degree of self-assembly in the mixture of   $  PC_{61}BM  $   and ITIC-4F. The exceptional stability under continuous illumination can be a unique feature of the alloy ETL because the photodimerization not only strongly influences the crystallinity and morphology of films but also accelerates burn-in degradation of the operational stability of solar cells [49]. The thermal stability of the devices of the alloy ETLs was further studied by applying thermal stress in dark/  $  N_{2}  $   condition, because continuous illumination induces heat generation of the devices, as observed in Figure S6. As shown in Figure S7, the alloy ETL exhibited superior thermal stability in dark/  $  N_{2}  $   condition with continuous thermal stress at 100 °C, and it is originated from the morphological stability. Thus, we conclude that the alloy ETL not only provides a barrier to protect the underlying perovskite layer from degradation agents in the ambient atmosphere, but also stable morphology of ETL, which synergistically contribute to the excellent ambient stability in continuous illumination of the PSCs.

We finally examined the photovoltaic performance of the devices under indoor lighting condition, because the PSCs have enormous potential for indoor light harvesting due to strong absorbance of perovskite semiconductors in the visible light region (390–760 nm) [50]. The white light emitting diode (LED) (6,500 K) light was employed as a light source to simulate the indoor illumination environment. The intensity of the LED light was set to 1,000 lx, which corresponds to 311.1   $  \mu  $  W cm  $  ^{-2}  $   from its photon flux density, as shown in Figure S8. Fig. 6a compares the J-V curves of PSCs with an alloy ETL to that of the PC  $  _{61}  $  BM-based ETL under LED lighting. The device based on the alloy ETL can achieve a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 0.88 V,   $  J_{SC}  $   of 0.123 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , FF of 0.67, and a   $  P_{indoor}  $   of 71.54   $  \mu  $  W cm  $  ^{-2}  $   under LED lighting, and the PCE of the device was 23.3%. In the case of the PC  $  _{61}  $  BM ETL, the device exhibited a rather lower   $  P_{indoor}  $   of 64.00   $  \mu  $  W cm  $  ^{-2}  $   under LED lighting with a PCE of 20.58%. (Table 2) The integrated current densities of two devices obtained from the EQE spectra (Fig. 3c) and the photon flux density (Fig. 6b) were 0.133 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and 0.101 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , respectively, which were consistent with the   $  J_{SCs}  $   from J-V measurements. (Table 2) Fig. 6c compares the stabilized photocurrent generation and corresponding SPO of two devices under LED lighting. The alloy ETL-based device achieved stabilized photocurrent density of 118.3   $  \mu  $  A cm  $  ^{-2}  $   at a bias of 0.66 V under the LED lighting (SPO = 78.1   $  \mu  $  W cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) while the PC  $  _{61}  $  BM ETL-based device recorded 110   $  \mu  $  A cm  $  ^{-2}  $   at a bias of 0.64 V (SPO = 70.4   $  \mu  $  W cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ), and thus those were corresponded to stabilized PCEs of 25.1 % and 22.6%, respectively. Higher SPO of the device with an alloy ETL for 600 s demonstrates distinct contribution and potential of the alloy ETL for high-performance and reliability of indoor power source applications. [51] Additionally, the stability of the devices under continuous indoor lighting condition was conducted in ambient condition without encapsulation. As shown in Fig. 6d, the alloy ETL-based device exhibited superior stability under continuous LED lighting (1,000 Lux) in ambient condition for >400 h. All those results are particularly interesting that the alloy ETL generates much better photovoltaic performance under low-light intensity lighting condition with high operative stability.

# 4. Conclusions

The alloy strategy for designing an optimal organic ETL for high-performance PSCs with an inverted architecture is described in this work. The alloy ETL possesses joint benefits of  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  (e.g., high electron mobility) and ITIC-4F (e.g., low-lying energy level) which allowed beneficial optoelectronic properties for efficient electron transport and reduced interfacial recombination. As a result, the mixed ETL made of  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  and ITIC-4F not only modulates the energy level matching, but also electronic structure at the interface contact to  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer simultaneously.
