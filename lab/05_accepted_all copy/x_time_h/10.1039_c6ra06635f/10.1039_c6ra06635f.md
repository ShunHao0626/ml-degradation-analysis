# Modeling of Degradation and Recovery of Perovskite Solar Cells

Hamed Sohrabpoor  $  ^{a}  $  , Giovanni Puccetti  $  ^{b}  $  , Nima E. Gorji  $  ^{b,c,*}  $

$ ^{a} $ Department of Mechanical Engineering, Azad University of Dezful, Dezful, Iran  
 $ ^{b} $ Department of Electrical Engineering, University of Bologna, Bologna, Italy  
 $ ^{c} $ Department of New Technologies, University of Tabriz, Tabriz, Iran

# Abstract

The degradation and recovery in the device parameters of perovskite-based solar cells are modeled for devices aged by exposing to air humidity/moisture. Several devices are considered with a same  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  absorber layer but with different window, hole transport layer and metallic back contacts. We proposed several models to fit the experimental data, reported in the literature, for the degradation of fill factor, efficiency and short-circuit current density. The kinetic models proposed for the modeling of degradation/recovery in the fill factor is based on variation in fast and slow metastable defect states in the respective layer. The degradation/recovery of short-circuit current density is modeled by proposing a new model which accounts the density of metastable defects before/after aging. Finally, the degradation of the device efficiency is modeled by fitting the data with proper functions mostly following the Gaussian bi-exponential shape. It is shown that instability of the device parameters might be due to variation in metastable defects in any component of a perovskite solar cell. However, the fill factor, or consequently the series resistance, is the main degradation source in perovskite solar cells.

Keywords: Modeling, Degradation, Recovery, Perovskite, Solar cell, Fill Factor.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite-based solar cells with  $ >20\% $  efficiency, obtained over last 5 years, still suffer from the high degradation rate or poor stability. The main instability resource of such organic-inorganic devices is moisture and exposure to air humidity. Several research groups have been systematically studying this aspect in last three years which are briefly discussed here. In 2013, Prof. Snaith et al. identified that the light-induced desorption of surface-adsorbed oxygen causes a critical instability in mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -sensitized solar cells [1]. They proposed a rather stable  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -free device which losses the fill factor,  $ (FF) $ , by about  $ 30\% $  within 200 hours aging (under one sun illumination) and then becomes almost stable over the next 1000 hours aging. The short-circuit current density,  $ J_{sc} $ , and open-circuit voltage,  $ V_{oc} $ , of this device showed a lesser variation. Several reasons were attributed to  $ FF $  degradation and efficiency,  $ \eta $ , of such devices e.g. oxygen desorption at vacancy cites of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and changes imposed at the interface of Spiro-OMETAD/Au at the back region of the cell. In 2014, the research group of Prof. Gratzel reported a novel electron-rich molecule called  $ H101 $  as a replacement with Spiro-OMETAD layer [2]. The  $ V_{oc} $  of this device decreased slightly, whereas, the  $ J_{sc} $  and  $ FF $  were the main parameters which were affected by heating at  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 7 days. This degradation behavior was primarily attributed to the moisture induced mechanism in a very hygroscopic perovskite layer. In 2015, Chen et al, inserted a thin layer of CuI instead of PEDOT:PSS as the hole transport layer, HTL, and

used Ag instead of Al at the back contact [3]. This device resulted in 13.28% efficiency and improved the air stability of the cell as compared to devices employing PEDOT:PSS as HTL. Efficiency drop of   $  \Delta\eta\approx-10\%  $   was reported after 14 days storage in air compared to   $  \Delta\eta\approx-73\%  $   for PEDOT:PSS-based devices. This degradation behavior was again attributed mainly to FF degradation and a gradual decrease of   $  J_{sc}  $  . In 2016, Li et al. reported 11.76% efficiency with AgAl back contacted perovskite cell and PEDOT:PSS as front contact [4]. Again, the efficiency degraded mainly by loss in fill factor,   $  \Delta FF  $  , after 360 hours aging under a relative humidity of 10% in air. The   $  V_{oc}  $   of this design didn't change too much and the   $  J_{sc}  $   improved or dropped slightly by aging. A similar degradation trend was observed for regular perovskite devices with   $  TiO_{2}  $   and Spiro-OMETAD partners under continues illumination [5]. XPS measurements of such cell revealed that broken interface binding of hole transport layer and Au metallic contact is the degradation source while   $  TiO_{2}/perovskite  $   interface doesn't have an effective role in device instability. Therefore,   $  \Delta\eta  $   was mainly driven by   $  \Delta J_{sc}  $   and   $  \Delta FF  $  . The latter was also responsible for the cell recovery. Some other literature have also reported similar degradation and recovery trend for the perovskite devices with different design and aged under similar conditions [6, 7, 8]. Nevertheless, in all the above reputed publications, the device instability was mostly driven by the variation in FF and in some extent by   $  J_{sc}  $  . Therefore, we base our modeling on given data in four different references (Ref. [3, 4, 5, 6]) on experimentally realized perovskite solar cells with similar active layer but different window (CuI & PEDOT:PSS [3]), different HTL (PEDOT:PSS & Spiro:OMETAD [4]), and back contact (AgAl &

Our modeling approach, for degradation/recovery of FF, is based on a kinetic model provided by Myong and Lim in 2006 [9]. This model was primary proposed to fit the degradation/recovery trend of FF in a-Si:H solar cells aged by light and temperature. This model accounts on concentration of fast and slow metastable defect states in i-region of p-i-n structures. Since perovskite solar cells are also a sandwich of pin layers we apply their model to explain the FF variation by humidity [10]. In addition, the gradual change in   $  J_{sc}  $   of perovskite cells by air exposure is modeled by developing a new approach based on variation of drift length and defect density. # 2. Theory

The loss in device parameters,  $ \Delta \eta $ ,  $ \Delta FF $ , and  $ \Delta J_{sc} $  has been calculated for the aging period,  $ \Delta t $ , after which the respective parameter does not degrade or becomes stable or starts to recover. We first address the degradation/recovery of  $ FF $  as this parameter has shown to be responsible for degradation of efficiency of the considered devices (1,2,3). It's been reported that perovskite materials have a long carrier diffusion length,  $ L_{D} = 10\mu \mathrm{m} $ , due to planar diffusion of indirect excitons [14]. Perovskite-based solar cells are also considered as p-i-n structures as was experimentally investigated in figure 5 of Ref. [15]. Routinely, for a uniform electric field,  $ E = (V_{bi} - V_a)/d $ , in the i-region, the carrier collection is given by  $ L_{D} = \mu \tau E $ . Note that, the electric field,  $ E $ , is the total of built-in voltage,  $ V_{bi} $  and applied voltage  $ V_{a} $  over the perovskite thickness,  $ d $ . In this case, the ambipolar recombination lifetime,  $ \tau $  allows the carrier to drift to the respective electrodes with a drift mobility,  $ \mu $ . Recently, the transport mean free path,  $ l_{0} $ , in perovskite devices was connected to  $ L_{D} $  by Gevorkian et al. [14] as,

$$
L _ {D} = l _ {0}. (p. \rho / \hbar) ^ {3} \text {   where   } l _ {0} = N ^ {- 1 / 3}
$$

(1)

where  $ p, \rho $ , and  $ N $  are exciton momentum ( $ \sim 2.7 \times 10^{-25} kg.m.s^{-1} $ ), indirect exciton radius ( $ \sim 5 $  nm), and bulk concentration of defects, respectively. Due to similarity in device physics of a-Si:H and perovskite thin film solar cells, e.g. p-i-n structure, drastic change of  $ FF $ , we can use the Nguyen approach to relate  $ FF $  to  $ L_D $  for perovskite devices here,

$$
F F = C _ {0} + A. \log \frac {L _ {D}}{d} \text {   or   } F F = C _ {1} - A. \ln N
$$

(2)

where  $ N $  is the bulk defect density, and  $ C_{0,1}^{(0.39)} $  and  $ \mathrm{A} = 0.3 $  are constants. Interestingly, for perovskite devices  $ |\mathrm{A}| = 1 / 3\approx 0.33 $  which is the power of  $ n $  in Eq. (1). To connect the model to experimental data, bringing the parameter time in the model  $ (FF(t)) $  and in order to get rid of the coefficients which vary from one device to another, we get the ratio (normalized) value of fill factor as,

$$
F F _ {n} = \frac {F F (t) - F F _ {d}}{F F _ {i} - F F _ {d}} = \frac {\ln (N / N _ {d})}{\ln (N _ {0} / N _ {d})},
$$

(3)

where   $  FF_{d}  $   is degraded fill factor after aging by air moisture,   $  FF_{i}  $   is the initial fill factor before aging,   $  N_{0}  $   and   $  N_{d}  $   are defect density in perovskite layer before and after aging, respectively. Note that, this modeling is not limited only to the defect increment in perovskite layer, but it is also applied to the defect increment in   $  TiO_{2}  $   or HTL layers too. In all Refs. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8], the degradation of full device was attributed to defect increment in either sides of perovskite interfaces and HTL thickness. However, the proposed model for   $  FF(t)  $   is flexible to replace the perovskite layer with   $  TiO_{2}  $   or HTL. Just it needs assuming that FF of the full device changes by N increment in a respective layer. First-principal study by Yin et al. [16] has shown that   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   and methylammonium lead iodide (  $  MAPbI_{3}  $  ) perovskites have unusual defect physics which is mostly associated to Pb ions. Although an in-depth understanding of defects in perovskites is still lacking, a photoluminescence, PL, study by Wen et al. [17] showed that both slow and fast metastable defects are present in these materials. This PL decay was fitted to a bi-exponential function including both slow and fast trapping/detrapping lifetimes and they concluded that both type of slow,   $  N_{s}  $ , and fast,   $  N_{f}  $ , metastabilities are present in perovskite layer with comparable weights and probabilities. Therefore, here we also ascribe the possibility of presence of both slow and fast metastabilities in the i-region of the perovskite cell under study and get,

$$
N = N _ {0} + N _ {f} + N _ {s} \& N _ {d} = N _ {0} + N _ {d f} + N _ {d s}
$$

(4)

where indices  $  df  $  and  $  ds  $  stand for fast and slow defects in degraded cell. Recently, we have developed a time-dependent model for defect increment under stress conditions and show that the density of fast and slow defects simply is obtained as [18]

$$
N _ {s} = N _ {d s}. e x p (\frac {- t}{\tau_ {s}}) \& N _ {f} = N _ {d f}. e x p (\frac {- t}{\tau_ {f}})
$$

(5)

where  $ \tau_{s} $  and  $ \tau_{f} $  are time constants for the fast and slow metastable defects to return to stable state, respectively. By replacing the above equations in (3), the following kinetic model is obtained [19],

$$
F F _ {n} = \frac {\ln \left\{n \times \left[ 1 + \alpha . e x p (- t / \tau_ {f}) + \beta . e x p (- t / \tau_ {s}) \right] \right\}}{\ln n}
$$

(6)

where  $ n $  is given by,

$$
n = \frac {N _ {0}}{N _ {0} + N _ {d f} + N _ {d s}} = e x p (\frac {F F _ {d} - F F _ {i}}{A}),
$$

(7)

# 3. Modeling Results and Discussion

# 3.1. Modeling the degradation/recovery of FF

Fig. 1 provides the modeling results for degradation/recovery of  $  FF  $  as a function of aging time (hours) using Eq. (6). All device 1,2,3, fabricated by different research groups, were exposed to air with relative humidity. This similar aging test allows comparing the modeling results. In addition, we selected three different perovskite solar cells with almost similar structure but different in window, HTL or back contact layers. This

allows the reader to find the modeling applied to a variety designs of perovskite devices [15]. As shown in the Fig. 1a,b,c, we separated the recovery and degradation trend of every device and modeled the variation of FF accordingly. For this, we recorded the initial and degraded values of fill factor,   $  FF_{i}  $   and   $  FF_{d}  $ , for every degradation/recovery feature within the respective aging time of every device, and then fitted the data with model using Eq. 6 and finding the proper values of fitting parameters given in Table 1, 2 and 3. The fitting parameters are   $  \tau_{F}  $ ,   $  \tau_{S}  $ , and n for every device, respectively. The data of Fig. 1a were taken from Ref. [3] and show both recovery and degradation trend when CuI material is used as window layer. Regardless of the actual initial value, FF was increased by exposing it to air after 80 hours. According to Table 1 it was improved from 95% to 109%. The model fits with data when we get   $  \tau_{F}<\tau_{S}  $   (30 vs. 300 ns). Exposing the device longer to humidity, degraded the FF. This occurs from initial value of 107% to 95% and   $  \tau_{S}  $   is one order of magnitude smaller than the recovery case. This means that humidity is creating metastable defects with higher density which cause faster recombination (smaller lifetime). This assignment is verified by comparing n values of 0.99 to 0.7 for recovery and degradation trend, respectively. Smaller n means higher   $  N_{d}  $   according to Eq. (7) and a higher   $  N_{d}  $   causes degradation by a lower FF or increased series resistance. We have considered the effect of stress on FF and series resistance of solar cells in our previous publication [18]. The increase in parameter   $  \alpha  $   (from 0.53 to 0.8) is also consistent with decreased n as both represent the defect increment by time. Note that, in [3], the aged devices were NOT encapsulated and thus the moisture influence is more probable. We will also verify the variation in defect density by modeling the   $  J_{sc}(t)  $   of every device in the last section of this manuscript. Obviously, CuI contacted cells showed a smaller degradation rate than the one with PEDOT:PSS. The lower stability of PEDOT:PSS contacted device may be because of acidic and hygroscopic nature of PEDOT:PSS as well as the moisture-sensitive perovskite layer [3]. Our modeling cannot be applied to such degradation source only if one considers that the metastable defects may be created at the boundaries or mobile ions migrate towards the interfaces via these grain pathways. This is still possible as we included several parameters in the baseline of our modeling e.g.  $  \tau_{S}, F  $  which can be assigned to several defects at different levels of bandgap, and parameter  $  K  $  which maybe related to other device parameters that have not been considered straightly in the model. Looking at aging test results of Ref. [3, 4, 5, 6] it's obvious that  $  FF  $  variation is significant in all devices, open-circuit voltage is almost unchanged and  $  J_{sc}  $  is changing slightly less than  $  FF  $ .

# 3.3. modeling of degradation/recovery of   $  J_{sc}  $

According to Ohshima et al.   $  J_{sc}  $   degrades for shorter diffusion or drift lengths,   $  L_{diff.}  $   or   $  L_{drift}  $ , of minority carriers [32]. It is known that increased defect density reduces the diffusion/drift length via   $  J_{sc} \propto L \propto \tau \propto 1/N  $ . In ultrathin perovskite devices i.e.   $  d_{perovskite} \leq 500  $   nm, we can assume that only drift component can be very effective in a pin structure perovskite solar cell. Then we can relate   $  J_{sc}  $   to   $  L_{drift}  $   through the ratio of initial and degraded/recovered current density as,

$$
J _ {s c} (t) = \frac {J _ {s c} - J _ {s c , d}}{J _ {s c , d} - J _ {s c , i}} = \frac {L - L _ {i}}{L _ {d} - L _ {i}} = \frac {1 / N - 1 / N _ {0}}{1 / N _ {d} - 1 / N _ {i}},
$$

(16)

where   $  J_{sc,i}  $   and   $  J_{sc,d}  $   refer to initial and degraded short-circuit current density after aging, respectively. Furthermore, the relationship between   $  N_{d}  $   and   $  N_{0}  $   is exponential as we calculated in Ref. [25],

$$
N _ {d} (t) = N _ {0}. e x p (- \gamma . t)
$$

(17)

where  $ \gamma $  is defect annealing rate and  $ t $  is current injection time (in this case exposure to moisture). Therefore, Eq. (16) leads to,

$$
J _ {s c} (t) = \frac {N _ {d}}{N _ {0} - N _ {d}}. e x p (\gamma . t)
$$

(18)

The above equation well describes the degradation/recovery of  $  J_{sc}  $  over aging time. For  $  N_0 < N_d  $  the ratio of  $  \frac{N_d}{N_0 - N_d} < 0  $  and thus  $  J_{sc}(t)  $  is degrades. Oppositely,  $  \frac{N_d}{N_0 - N_d} > 0  $  for  $  N_0 > N_d  $ , the ratio of  $  \frac{N_d}{N_0 - N_d} > 0  $  and  $  J_{sc}(t)  $  increases over time or recovers. In case of the data we considered in this paper, Ref. [3, 4, 5, 6], all  $  J_{sc}  $  trends show degradation which means increment of metastable

defect density by moisture effect. The reduction in  $  J_{sc}  $  of Device 1 with PEDOT:PSS after about 350 hours was only 10% [3]. It shows a primary degradation and then is recovered after about 100 hours aging. Oppositely, the  $  J_{sc}  $  Device 1 with CuI goes under degradation from the beginning and after 350 hours aging, gradually decreases to reach 70%* $  J_{sc,i}  $ . The  $  N_0  $  and  $  N_d  $  are then obtained in the order of  $  10^{15} \text{ cm}^{-3}  $  and  $  10^{17} \text{ cm}^{-3}  $  using Eq. (18). These values are, clearly, smaller in case of Device 1 with PEDOT:PSS with the difference that at the beginning of aging we get  $  N_0 = 10^{15} \text{ cm}^{-3} > N_d = 10^{14} \text{ cm}^{-3}  $ . When  $  J_{sc}  $  drastically reduces by aging,  $  N_d  $  will be much greater and even may go beyond  $  10^{20} \text{ cm}^{-3}  $ . This is what we see in Device 2 with Al and Device 3 with Spiro-OMETAD. The other devices show incomparable -Δ $  J_{sc}  $  and -ΔFF e.g. within 10-40%. This confirms that the most of degradation arise by variation in FF. Recovery occurs when the electric field is strong such that it compensates decreased  $  L_{drift}  $  (because of increment in metastable defects). It worth noting that among all devices considered here, only Device 2 with AgAl shows recovery in  $  J_{sc}  $  which is actually Δ $  J_{sc} = +10\%  $ ; an increase from the beginning of aging. We calculated  $  N_0 = 10^{17} \text{ cm}^{-3}  $  and  $  N_0 = 10^{16} \text{ cm}^{-3}  $  for this case. Finally, we found no remarkable change in  $  V_{oc}  $  of the considered devices or the variation in  $  V_{oc}  $  was very random.

# 4. Conclusion

We theoretically investigated the degradation/recovery of fill factor, efficiency and short-circuit current density of perovskite solar cells aged at air-humidity exposure. The experimental data were taken from Refs. [3, 4, 5, 6]. Three devices were considered with same absorber layer but different window, HTL and back contact all aged in a same way (exposing to air humidity). Fill Factor was modeled by considering the variation in metastable defect density and their role in degradation/recovery of the cell. We started by connecting  $  FF  $  to carrier drift length,  $  L_{D}  $ , which is in turn related to metastabilities of both slow/fast types. This allowed us to track the variation of FF over time,  $  FF(t)  $ , and fit the data with data for every recovery/degradation trend. Alternatively, the efficiency was fitted with proper functions present in MATLAB-fitting tool. The efficiency variation trend mostly fit with bi-exponential functions indicating that both slow/fast metastable defect are responsible for change in efficiency over time. Finally, short-circuit current density was modeled by a new proposed equation based on defect creation/annihilation over aging period. The modeling results shed light on underlying physics behind the instability of perovskite-based solar cells. It is concluded that fill factor is responsible parameter in degradation/recovery of perovskite solar cells. Fill factor as a measure of series resistance degrades by defect increment and recovers by defect annihilation under aging test. The metallic contacts are crucial in having a stable device and the efficiency stability of every cell is determined by stability of fill factor rather than short-circuit current density or open-circuit voltage. Our modeling shows that a single degradation/recovery model cannot be applied to the whole device instability behavior which actually makes the modeling of such phenomena rather complicated.
