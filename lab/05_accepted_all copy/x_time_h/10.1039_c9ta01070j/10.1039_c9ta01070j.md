# Abstract

Small molecules, such as Spiro-OMeTAD are widely used in photovoltaic devices including perovskite and dye-sensitized solar cells due to excellent hole transport properties. However, this kind of small molecules is often adopted with hygroscopic additives and dopants suffering from aggregation and demixing under environmental stress thus leading to pinholes/voids in resultant films. By using the typical molecular Spiro-OMeTAD as an example, we herein introduced hydrophobic polymeric Poly(4-vinylpyridine) (P4VP) component to substantially reduce the undesired pinholes during device fabrication and operation. It leads to the significant improvement of device long-term stability, which kept 80% of their original PCE for over 6000 h in ambient air and the promotion of light stability. In addition, P4VP can saturate uncoordinated Pb sites in perovskite crystals, resulting in improved device performance (certified PCE of 20.6%) with negligible hysteresis. These results emphasize the effects of pinhole constraint in both the temporal and spatial perspectives, which provides a feasible approach to improve the stability in small-molecule-based hole transport materials for perovskite solar cells and other optoelectronic devices.

Keywords: pinhole constraint; hole transport layers; perovskite; stable; solar cell

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic halide perovskites have attracted tremendous attention in photovoltaic field due to their excellent optoelectronic properties including high absorption coefficient   $  {}^{1,2}  $  , long charge carrier lifetime   $  {}^{3,4}  $  , low trap density   $  {}^{5}  $  , low-cost fabrication   $  {}^{6}  $   and so on. With the continuous efforts from all over the world, the power conversion efficiencies (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have increased dramatically from 3.8% to certified 23.7%   $  {}^{2,7-13}  $  . Among all the architectures, particularly the regular (n-i-p) structured PSCs were extensively developed due to its simple fabrication and high efficiency   $  {}^{13-15}  $  . A great deal of effort has been devoted into the exploration of ideal hole transport layer (HTL) materials, including polymer, small molecule, and inorganic species. Inorganic HTLs, mainly represented by CuI   $  {}^{16}  $  , CuSCN   $  {}^{17}  $   and NiO   $  {}^{18}  $  , possess the advantages of both high mobility and low cost. However, it is difficult to deposit these inorganic HTLs on the top of perovskite materials without their damage and interaction, as the corresponding fabrication processes are mostly incompatible to the absorber underneath   $  {}^{19}  $  . Polymeric materials such as commercial PTAA   $  {}^{20-22}  $  , P3HT   $  {}^{23-25}  $   can be well deposited on the perovskite layer. Due to their relatively low conductivity and consequently ultra-small thickness, it requires perovskite films with great wettability, which makes the resulting solar cells rarely achieve the similar reproducibility level as that of the state-of-art device  $  {}^{26}  $  . Small molecules are another class of HTL that has been successfully used in perovskite solar cells due to their excellent photoelectric properties and the ease of processing. However, as an interlayer between perovskite and electrode, these small molecules are not only required to transport holes but also are desired to be dense enough to retard the invasion of ambient small molecules (especially H  $  _{2}  $  O and O  $  _{2}  $  ), as well as ion diffusion between perovskite components and electrode, those are crucial to the efficient and stable PSCs. Among of the small - molecule organic HTLs, spiro-OMeTAD (spiro) represents the most superb HTL because of its suitable highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels, excellent hole-extraction and transportation abilities   $  {}^{27}  $  . Most of the state-of-art PSC devices are designed by using spiro as HTL   $  {}^{11}  $  . However, as a small molecule, it is hard for spiro to form pin-hole free and uniform thin films during spin coating due

In addition, the conductivity of spiro fails to meet the requirement for high performance devices. Therefore, most of works adopted a certain amount of Li-TFSI and 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP) as effective additives / dopants  $ ^{28} $  into spiro to improve its conductivity. Unfortunately, these additives are highly hygroscopic, which can adsorb ambient moisture to segregate in the spiro film, which results in extra pinholes in the doped film. These pinholes may allow small molecular species (such as H $ _2 $ O and O $ _2 $ ) in ambient invade into the entire device, which leads to further degradation of PSCs. Meanwhile, the volatile species in the perovskite film such as CH $ _3 $ NH $ _2 $  and HI could easily diffuse out via the pinholes to interact with electrodes  $ ^{29} $ , leading to the formation of metal halide to corrode the electrodes  $ ^{30} $ . In view of the disadvantages of spiro, some reports have accordingly prepared pinhole-free spiro by changing the solvent from the commonly used chlorobenzene to chloroform  $ ^{29,31} $ , or deposited spiro films via vacuum evaporation, to improve the long-term stability of related devices  $ ^{32} $ . These methods only work with spiro film without additives, while for films with additives, a large number of pinholes are generated due to water adsorption and accumulation during device operation and inducing further degradation. Therefore, preventing pinholes formation in spiro layers under outdoor operation condition is a key challenge to achieve the long-term stable PSCs based on small - molecule HTLs.

Here, by using spiro as an example, we first proposed a simple approach to greatly improve the film quality of the small molecule HTLs and the resulting long-term stability of the device. By introducing a long chain hydrophobic polymer Poly(4-vinylpyridine) (P4VP) into spiro precursor solution, we successfully smoothed, strengthen and reduced the pinholes in the as-deposited HTL during the film deposition. Meanwhile, the pinholes formation caused by the water absorption of additives during device storage in high humidity were effectively suppressed, as is clearly revealed by in-situ optical microscopy measurement. Non-encapsulated P4VP modified PSCs exhibit better long-term stability in ambient air (about 25%-50% relative humidity), retain 80% of their original PCE for over 6000 h storage. # 2. Results and Discussion

To reduce pinholes amount during small - molecules - based HTL formation such as spiro-OMeTAD, it is preferred to apply materials with excellent film-forming capability which is hydrophobic and miscible with spiro. To this end, “soft” polymer with long carbon chain such as P4VP becomes an ideal candidate. The chemical structure of commercially available P4VP is shown in Figure S1. We use P4VP as an example to investigate the compatibility between small molecule and polymer for highly-efficient perovskite solar cells with improved stability. For convenience, we denote the reference (spiro doped with Li-TFSI and TBP) as Spiro and the target sample (Li-TFSI and TBP doped spiro modified with P4VP) as Spiro:P4VP.

As a small molecule, spiro is prone to easily form certain number of pinholes by spin-coating method during film deposition   $  {}^{29, 31}  $  . We observed the similar results by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (Figure 1a) and atomic force microscope (AFM) images (Figure 1c). Such pinholes dramatically reduce the quality of HTL film, further deteriorating the corresponding photovoltaic performance and stability of PSCs. Surprisingly, by introducing an optimal amount of P4VP (1 mg/mL), we successfully reduced the pinholes in HTL film (Spiro:P4VP) compared to the pristine spiro film, as shown in Figure 1b, d. In addition, the obvious surface roughness caused by such pinholes existing in Spiro are largely reduced by P4VP, as shown in Figure 1c, d, indicative of a dense and uniform HTL film. The root mean square roughness (RMS) for Spiro and Spiro:P4VP samples were measured to be 66 nm and 36 nm, respectively. (Figure S2) In consideration of the “soft” characteristics of P4VP that is easy to be cast into films, it is reasonable to expect that these polymers help to reduce such pinholes occurred in the Spiro layer.

developed because the Li-TFSI serves as moisture adsorption centers. Prevention of the possible moisture effects for PSCs is a key issue, which protects both perovskite layer and carrier transport layer simultaneously. The molecules with hydrophobic long alkyl group generally have lower wettability with water. Accordingly, we measured the contact angle of water on the top of the Spiro film and Spiro:P4VP film. The obtained values is   $  44^{\circ}  $   for Spiro and   $  91^{\circ}  $   for Spiro:P4VP film, respectively, as shown in Figure 1e,f. This implies that the P4VP modified HTL has superior hydrophobicity, which might better protect the perovskite and carrier transport film from water invasion. Actually the invasion process of water to Li-TFSI doped spiro films is an adhesion process that involves three phases e.g. liquid, gas, and solid. Assuming the unit area at the interface of each phase, the change of Gibbs free energy during the process is as follows:

$$
\Delta G = \gamma_ {l - s} - \gamma_ {l - g} - \gamma_ {s - g}
$$

(1)

where the   $  \gamma_{1-s}  $  ,   $  \gamma_{1-g}  $  , and   $  \gamma_{s-g}  $   are surface energies of liquid-solid, liquid-gas and solid-gas. Among all surface energies, the   $  \gamma_{1-g}  $   can be directly measured through experiment and the value of water at room temperature is   $  0.072\ N\cdot m^{-1}  $  . According to Young's wetting formula:

$$
\gamma_ {s - g} = \gamma_ {l - s} + \gamma_ {l - g} c o s \theta
$$

(2)

where the   $  \theta  $   is the contact angle that can be deduced:

$$
\Delta G = - \gamma_ {\mathrm{g-l}} (1 + \cos \theta)
$$

(3)

According to formula (3) we calculate  $ \Delta G $  for the Spiro film and Spiro:P4VP film to be  $ -0.124 $  and  $ -0.071\mathrm{J} / \mathrm{m}^2 $ , respectively. Spiro film exhibits less  $ \Delta G $  upon P4VP addition (Figure 1g), which indicates the water adhesion process is less favored from a thermodynamic point of view. It is further inferred that the P4VP modified spiro film potentially improves the long term stability of resultant devices, which is shown in the following part.

Accordingly, an in-situ morphology evolution of the Spiro film and Spiro:P4VP film were characterized to gain the further insight into the differences of the pinhole formation processes under moist circumstance (80% relative humidity). As shown in figure 2a, e, we selected the regions (0.20*0.25 mm $  ^{2}  $ ) of Spiro and P4VP modified Spiro films before testing in humidity environment. It is observed that some pinholes were witnessed in the Spiro based film, but was absent from P4VP modified film. By comparison, the introduction of P4VP in the Spiro film allows the significantly reduced number of pinholes in the case of high humidity (Figure 2e-h). The significant difference between two films indicates that P4VP is an effective polymer to prevent pinhole formation in the HTL in humidity. Therefore, through the P4VP molecules modification, it not only fills up the pinholes in small molecule films during its fabrication, but also prevents extra voids formation due to hydrophilic Li-TFSI, which complies well with the results of contact angle measurements.

Combined with the in-situ observation from optical microscopy images and hydrophilic/hydrophobic characteristics of the P4VP component, the expected functionality of Spiro:P4VP hole transport materials was shown in figure 2j. On one hand, hygroscopic Li-TFSI is much easier to adsorb surrounding water thus produces extra pinholes. On the other hand, such pinholes existed in spiro may provide more diffusion pathways for undesired ions, thereby cause further degradation of the entire solar cells. The presence of P4VP improves mechanical properties of the whole layer due to elimination of pin holes, which help to prevent the diffusion of small molecules (such as  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  and  $ \mathrm{O}_2 $  from surroundings and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_2 $  and HI from perovskite film), thus offers an efficient method to enhance the long-term stability of the spiro based PSCs.

To gain the insight into the improvement of the   $  V_{oc}  $   and the reduced hysteresis in Spiro:P4VP based device, we fabricated hole-only devices (ITO / PEDOT:PSS / PVSK / Spiro or Spiro:P4VP / Au) to measure the trap-state density and the hole mobility in the full device. Figure 3d shows the dark state I–V curves of these two devices. The linear relation indicates an “ohmic region” response of the hole-only device at low bias voltage. With the bias voltage increasing, the current quickly increases nonlinearly, demonstrating that the trap-states begin to be filled until the trap-filled limit is achieved. The trap-filled limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ) of the perovskite films covered with Spiro and Spiro:P4VP are 0.98 and 0.64 V, respectively. The enhancement of hole mobility and the reduction of defect states density in perovskite film, improve the resulting optoelectronic properties of the devices. This is also consistent with reduced photoluminescence (PL) spectral response and shorter lifetime by time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurement. (Figure S5, S6, Table S1). The increase in carrier mobility and the decrease in density of defects are likely attributed to the lone pair electrons of nitrogen may coordinate with dangling lead atoms near the perovskite/HTL interface, passivating positive under-coordinated Pb defects, and inhibiting the recombination of carriers   $  {}^{35,36}  $  . In order to provide more direct evidence regarding the Pb coordination situation, we conducted X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurement. As shown in Figure S7, the binding energy with respect to 4f  $  _{7/2}  $   of divalent Pb  $  ^{2+}  $   in the reference (bear perovsktie film) and the sample covered with P4VP were measured to be 138.9 eV and 138.6 eV, respectively. Compared with the pristine perovskite film, the binding energies of Pb valence electrons exhibit a slight shift toward lower values when treated with P4VP. It is reported species with lone pair electrons of the pyridine group in P4VP can form coordination bonds when interacting with undercoordinated Pb atoms, which decreases the binding energy of Pb 4f orbitals. It thus indicates that P4VP plays an important role in defect passivation, which is in line with previous reports   $  {}^{37}  $  .

Then we tracked the stability of the PSC devices. In order to reduce the deviation caused by the small number of samples, 10 devices were measured in each group, which is considered of statistical significance. The normalized PCE as a function of the storage time in the air environment (relative humidity: 25%-50%, temperature: 25-40 °C) is shown in figure 4a. The unencapsulated Spiro:P4VP cells retain 80% of their original PCE (the average value is 19.02%) for over 6000 h storage in ambient air, which is compared with other works (Figure S8). This means that the modification of HTL film quality and hydrophobicity by introducing P4VP play an important role in the long-term stability of the device. We further explored the illumination stability and thermal stability of these devices. Figure 4b reveals the device performance evolution upon continuous 1 sun illumination. When P4VP was employed in the Spiro, the device retained 90% of its original PCE after about 700 h (while the device-based Spiro under the same illumination condition dropped to 80% after 550 h). In addition, respect to thermal stability, several previous studies reported that small molecule spiro-OMeTAD would form pinholes under thermal conditions due to its low glass transition temperature, which increased the contact between perovskite and electrodes and further induces the device performance degradation  $  ^{41}  $  . However, by modifying the Spiro film with P4VP we obtained an impressive result that maintained 80% of original PCE under continue 85 °C heating after 300 h (the device based Spiro under the thermal condition drop to 80% after 50 h). All of these stability tests demonstrate that the decrease of pinholes by P4VP inhibit the pinholes formation, due to the hydrophobic carbon chain and its soft characteristics, which effectively suppress the film degradation and lead to a better device stability.

The spatial distribution of Ag in the aged devices, including Spiro and Spiro:P4VP are shown in Figure 5. The thickness of perovskite containing Pb and I (mainly) elements, HTL and Ag electrode was about 500, 200 and 100 nm respectively. Apparently, for the Spiro based devices (Figure 5a), substantial Ag signals were observed to penetrate through the entire device layer after 30 days aging in humid environment. However, in the sample with Spiro:P4VP, the Ag signals were only observed in the original layer without apparent diffusion (Figure 5e). This suggests that P4VP modified Spiro effectively inhibits the diffusion of Ag ions. The reason is obvious that the P4VP was used early as selective ion-exchange resins and chelate-forming resins which can achieve optimum sorption of metal ion via coordinating with pyridine groups  $ ^{42} $ . Therefore, it is reasonable to expect that P4VP can also inhibit the penetration of Ag ions through chelation and coordination since it contains pyridine nitrogen which is known as an excellent ligand for silver ions. It also explains why P4VP can prevent Ag ion from spreading. In addition to the suppression of Ag diffusion, we also found Spiro:P4VP could suppress the diffusion of iodine ions. It is reported that when compared to inert nitrogen, the ambient air easily accelerates the corrosion of Ag electrodes, factually it means that oxygen from air oxidizes HI into water and iodine and the iodine and metallic silver form AgI easily, where methyl ammonium iodide migration is facilitated by the small pinholes in the hole transport layer spiro-MeOTAD  $ ^{29} $ . The formation of silver iodide significantly increased the series resistance and J–V hysteresis of the corresponding devices. Moreover, the inter-diffusion of ions further accelerates the degradation of the device which is unfavorable to long-term stability  $ ^{43} $ . The device based on Spiro:P4VP effectively reduce the pinhole of HTL, thus inhibiting the diffusion of Ag and I ions, improving the device optoelectronic performance and long-term stability.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we have developed a general pinhole suppression method to improve the stability of small-molecule-based HTLs in perovskite solar cells. It successfully reduces the intrinsic pinholes of spiro during film fabrication, and suppresses the voids

formation during long-term exposure to humid air. The improvement of device stability is ascribed to the pinhole constraint in both temporal and spatial perspectives. P4VP modified PSCs exhibited excellent stability that endures more than 6000 h in ambient air (still keeping 80% of their original PCE) without any encapsulation. It is partially attributed to the successful suppression of iodine migration and silver diffusion. Moreover, after using P4VP, we obtained a certified PCE of 20.6% in the resulting device. These findings provide a new approach to the fabrication of pinhole free HTLs for efficient perovskite solar cells with improved stability.

All reagents were used as received without further purification, including   $  PbI_{2}  $   (99.999%, Sigma-Aldrich), CsI (99.9%, Sigma-Aldrich), P4VP (AR 98%, Aladdin Industrial Corporation), spiro-OMeTAD (Lumtec),   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloid precursor (Alfa Aesar, tin(IV) oxide, 15% in   $  H_{2}O  $   colloidal dispersion), N,N-dimethylformamide (99.99%, Sigma-Aldrich), dimethyl sulfoxide (99.5%, Sigma-Aldrich), isopropanol (99.99%, Sigma-Aldrich), chlorobenzene (99.9%, Sigma-Aldrich), acetone (AR Beijing Chemical Works), ethanol (AR Beijing Chemical Works), aminomethane (CP Beijing Chemical Works), hydrogen iodide (57%, Aladdin Industrial Corporation), 4-tert-butylpyridine (99.9%, SigmaAldrich), acetonitrile (99.9%, Sigma-Aldrich) and ITO substrate (Shanghai B-Tree Tech. Consult.). The methylamine iodide (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  ), Formamidine iodide (  $  HC(NH_{2})_{2}I  $  ), methylamine chlorine (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Cl  $  ) and methylamine bromine (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Br  $  ) were synthesized using the methods reported everywhere.

# Device Fabrication

The ITO substrate was sequentially cleaned with ultrapure water, acetone, ethanol and isopropanol. After 45 minutes of UV- $ \mathrm{O}_3 $  treatments, an  $ 80~\mathrm{nm} $ -thick layer of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  film was spin-coated on substrate with the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  nanocrystal solution at 4000 rpm for 30 s for two times, then annealed at  $ 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 30 min in air. The standard perovskite film fabricated by a two-step solution process: which the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  (1.3 M, dissolved in DMF:DMSO (9:1, v:v)) mixed with  $ 5\% $  CsI was spin coated on ITO/ $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  with 2500 rpm for 30 s and annealed at  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 1 min in nitrogen glovebox. After cooling the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  coated substrate to room temperature in nitrogen glovebox, mixed organic cation solution (MAI 0.12 M; MABr 0.05M; MACl 0.07M; FAI 0.23M, dissolved in isopropanol) was spin-coated with 2300 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at  $ 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 15 min in air. Then the HTM solution, where a spiro-OMeTAD / chlorobenzene (60 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ ) solution was employed with the addition of  $ 28~\mu\mathrm{L} $  Li-TFSI / acetonitrile (260 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ ), and  $ 24~\mu\mathrm{L} $  4-tertbutylpyridine, was deposited by spin-coating at 3500 rpm for 30 s. Finally, the device was finished by thermal evaporation of Au (100nm) under

# Characterization

The morphology of spiro-OMeTAD were measured using a scanning electron microscope (SEM) (SEM, Hitachi S-4800). Photoluminescence (PL) was measured by FLS980 (Edinburgh Instruments Ltd) with an excitation at 470 nm. Transient-state photoluminescence (TRPL) was measured by FLS980 (Edinburgh Instruments Ltd) with an excitation at 470 nm. The TPV and TPC measurements were performed on Molex 180081-4320 simulating one sun working condition, the carriers were excited by a 532 nm pulse laser. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were determined by the electrochemical workstation (Germany, Zahner Company), employing light emitting diodes driven by Export (Germany, Zahner Company). The current density-voltage characteristics of perovskite devices were obtained using a Keithley 2400 source-measure unit under AM1.5G illumination at 1000 W/m² with a Newport Thermal Oriel 91192 1000 W solar simulator. External quantum efficiencies were measured by an Enli Technology (Taiwan) EQE measurement system. The high angle annular dark field scanning transmission electron microscopy (HAADF-STEM) measurement was recorded by a FEI Titan G2 microscope equipped with an aberration corrector for probe forming lens.
