# Abstract

Material passivation is essential to enhance the quality of perovskite materials and boost the performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, most of the previous reports only paid their attentions to improve the quality of perovskite films by adopting single passivation. Here, we demonstrate a facile strategy which can carry out double passivation to improve the performance of PSCs. By using the dilute halide salt PEABr solution to treat the perovskite film,   $  PbI_{2}  $   can precipitate from the perovskite. Both PEABr and   $  PbI_{2}  $   can passivate the perovskite film and by combining PEABr and   $  PbI_{2}  $ , the double passivation improves the performance of PSCs significantly. Very high short circuit current density of 24.30 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $ , open circuit voltage of 1.10 V and fill factor of 79.75% are achieved which leads to a surprising efficiency of 21.32% for the passivated device. The improved efficiency is mainly according to available surface passivation of the perovskite material, leading to repressed non-radiative recombination and unhindered charge collection. In addition, the passivated device exhibits better PCE stability relative to the control device.

Keywords: perovskite solar cells, double passivation, PEABr, PbI  $  _{2}  $  , recombination

# Introduction

The power conversion efficiency (PCE) improved from 3.8% in 2009 to recent 24.2%  $  ^{1,2}  $  rapidly makes perovskite and its derivative appear a competitive candidate for next generation thin-film solar cells and other photoelectric devices.  $  ^{3-5}  $  Therefore, three-dimensional (MA/FA) hybrid organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite materials have been studied extensively since various outstanding properties, such as strong light harvesting capability, high light absorption coefficient, low fabrication cost, ambipolar charge transport properties, long carrier diffusion lengths and adjustable bandgap for tandem devices.  $  ^{6-12}  $  However, for the moment, the efficiency and stability of PSCs are still the main blocks towards the commercialization of this promising solar cell.  $  ^{13,14}  $

To promote the device performance, large number methods have been suggested to further improve the PCE and stability of PSCs in the past few years. Till now, the highest short circuit current density   $  (J_{sc})  $   of PSC is approaching to its limit (26 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  ), and then it forces us to pay more attention to improve the open circuit voltage   $  (V_{oc})  $   or fill factor (FF)  $  ^{15}  $   so that the PCE could be further enhanced. It is reported that non-radiative trap-assisted recombination plays an very important function in FF of PSCs.  $  ^{16-18}  $   And it should deserve special notice that compared to other photovoltaic materialin solar cells such as silicon, polycrystalline perovskite includes under-coordinated ions as defects at the surface and grain boundaries of their crystal, which are considered as the centers of Shockley-Read-Hall non-radiative recombination.  $  ^{19}  $   These unique defects on the surface and grain boundary can lead to the severe carrier recombination in polycrystalline perovskite thin film.  $  ^{20}  $   The performance of PSCs could be further improved as long as the non-radiative recombination in the perovskite layer could be effectively suppressed.  $  ^{21}  $   To achieve this goal, many methods such as compositional engineering, interface engineering, passivation engineering, encapsulation and device structure engineering have been proposed. Among those methods, passivation engineering is widely used due to its simple process. For example, Yang et al.  $  ^{22}  $   suggested a self-induced PbI  $  _{2}  $   passivation of the perovskite layer by controlled the annealing time. The result of SEM, XRD and

TRPL indicated that   $  PbI_{2}  $   in the perovskite film could reduce the boundary defects at the grain boundaries and interfaces of   $  TiO_{2}/Perovskite  $   and improve the performance of PSCs. Sun et al.  $  ^{23}  $   introduced a small molecule of SubPc to the surface of perovskite film, and the result showed that with the introduction of SubPc the perovskite film exhibited reduced surface defect density. Therefore, the charge carrier lifetime was increased and the PCE of PSCs was improved. Recently, Han et al.  $  ^{24}  $   employed an organic dye (AQ310) as the passivator to reduce the trap states of the perovskite film, their result showed that the trap states of perovskite film could be reduced with the usage of AQ310 and the average PCE of 19.43% was achieved. All of these studies have showed that the passivation engineering is an available method to enhance the performance of PSCs. Compared with traditional passivation materials, because of its remarkable exceptional chemical, electrical, physical properties and excellent environmental stability, organic halide salt (such as, PEAI, BAI, NMAI, PMAI and so on) expects to be used as an effective passivation material.  $  ^{25-29}  $   Meanwhile, the addition of organic halide salt is beneficial to the formation of two-dimensional (2-D) perovskite material with high stability and excellent passivation effect,  $  ^{30-31}  $   which could improve the performance of PSCs. However, most of the previous reports only paid their attentions to improve the quality of perovskite film by single passivation.

In this work, we demonstrate a facile strategy which can carry out the double passivation to improve the performance of PSCs. By using the dilute halide salt PEABr solution to treat the perovskite film,  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  can precipitate from the perovskite. Both PEABr and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  can passivate the perovskite film and by combining PEABr and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , this is quite different from previous reports, and the double passivation improves the performance of PSCs significantly. The result of electrical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurement shows that the non-radiative carrier recombination of perovskite film is reduced after the double passivation. The photoluminescence (PL) spectra measurement and the transient photocurrent (TPC)/transient photovoltage (TPV) measurement also indicates that with the double passivation, perovskite film exhibits surprising properties of inhibited carrier recombination and better charge

UV-Vis absorption spectra and XRD measurement are presented to investigate the influence of the PEABr introduction on the perovskite film. As shown in Fig. 3a, we can find that the perovskite film without treatment and treated with 2 mg/mL PEABr achieves almost the similar absorption spectra, which demonstrates that the introduction of PEABr has no effect on the optical absorption of the perovskite film and this corresponds to the almost similar   $  J_{sc}  $   for the control device and the device treated with 2 mg/mL PEABr. Thus, more works are required to find the reason for the improved device performance. In a recent report, You et al.  $  ^{[31]}  $   used a similar halide salt PEAI to treat the perovskite film and achieved a very high performance PSC. In their work, they claimed that there has no 2-D perovskite formation after the halide salt PEAI treatment and the enhanced performance of PSCs is attributed to the PEAI surface passivation. As is well known, PEABr has similar physical and chemical properties as PEAI. Here, we assume that PEABr could improve the performance of PSCs in the same way. To verify this, the corresponding XRD patterns of PEABr, the perovskite without treatment and the perovskite treated with 2 mg/mL PEABr are depicted in Fig. 3b. The XRD patterns of perovskite films exhibit diffraction peaks at 14.33°, 28.51° and 31.96° assigned to the (110), (220) and (310) lattice planes of the tetragonal perovskite structure respectively. Clearly, relative to the control film, the perovskite film treated with 2 mg/mL PEABr exhibits a new diffraction peak at 5.48°, which is corresponding to the XRD peak of PEABr (blue curve). This demonstrates

that the perovskite treated with 2 mg/mL PEABr exhibits similar result with the report by You et al. and the same passivation function should exist in our work. Beyond this, the XRD measurement results also reveal that with the introduced PEABr, the (110) peak intensity of perovskite is reduced slightly, and tiny PbI $ _2 $  peak (12.7°) appears in the XRD patterns for the perovskite film. This shows that the PEABr solution treatment can result in the production of PbI $ _2 $  from the perovskite film. To confirm this observation, PEABr solutions of different concentration are used to treat the perovskite film and then the XRD is used to measure the films. Fig. S5 shows the XRD patterns of the perovskite films treated with 1 mg/mL and 3 mg/mL PEABr. For all the samples, it can be observed that the peak intensity of PbI $ _2 $  appears after the treatment. Furthermore, with the increase of PEABr concentration, the peak intensity of PbI $ _2 $  increase and the peak intensity of perovskite decreases. Thus, it can be confirmed that the PEABr solution can indeed lead to the appearance of PbI $ _2 $ . This is different from You's work, where no PbI $ _2 $  is found after the treatment. The previous studies have indicated that PbI $ _2 $  grains can locally passivate the perovskite film and at the rich PbI $ _2 $  regions, the defect trapping is significantly suppressed. $ ^{32-36} $  It can be supposed that PbI $ _2 $  after the PEABr solution treatment can also passivate the perovskite and improve the device performance. Thus, both PEABr and PbI $ _2 $  can passivate the perovskite film and the double passivation should be responsible for the device performance improvement. The double passivation by PEABr and PbI $ _2 $  is a new strategy different from You's work.

It is obvious that some white columns could be observed between the grain boundaries in Fig. 3d, however, it could not be observed in Fig. 3c. These white columns have been proved to be  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , which is consistent with the XRD results. To further understand the reason for the performance improvement of PSCs, PL and TRPL for the perovskite films with/without the double passivation were measured. The perovskite films were deposited on the glass/ITO substrates directly, and during the measurement the samples were sealed by spin-coating a layer of insulating polymer (PMMA) to protect the samples. It is obvious in Fig. 5a that the peak intensity for the sample with the double passivation is increased compared to the control sample. Meanwhile, the peak position seems to be a slight blue shift after the treat of PEABr, this could be attributed to the introduction of Br element, which could increase the band gap of the perovskite. The increased peak intensity demonstrates that the radiative recombination is enhanced and then the non-radiative recombination is partially suppressed for the perovskite film with the double passivation. Fig. 5b shows the TRPL behaviors for the perovskite films with/without the double passivation. It is clear that the perovskite film without any treatment obtained a shorter lifetime of 441.82 ns compare to the film with the double passivation (722.67 ns). This is because that the density of traps in the control film is larger than the film treated with the double passivation. Therefore, the photogenerated charge carrier recombination in the film with the double passivation could be efficiently repressed. All of these results indicate that the double passivation method could efficiently passivate the perovskite film. Hence, the density of traps in the perovskite film is reduced and the non-radiative charge carrier recombination is effectively suppressed. Transient photocurrent (TPC) and transient photovoltage (TPV) of perovskite solar cells were further performed to verify these results. Fig. 5c shows the photocurrent decay of the control device and the device with the double passivation measured at short-circuit condition. It is obvious that the device with the double passivation achieves a faster decay of 313.04 ns compare to the control device with a decay time of 572.97 ns. This demonstrates that with the double passivation, the perovskite film exhibits much more efficient charge transport and charge extraction due to the reduced density of traps on the surface of perovskite film. Hence, the FF is enhanced.

# Conclusion

This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved

In summary, we demonstrate a facile strategy which can carry out the double passivation to improve the performance of PSCs. By using the dilute halide salt PEABr solution to treat the perovskite film,   $  PbI_{2}  $   can precipitate from the perovskite. Both PEABr and   $  PbI_{2}  $   can passivate the perovskite film. With the double passivation, the trap density is decreased and the charge carrier recombination is efficiently suppressed. The device with the double passivation behaves the best PCE of 21.32%, with a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 24.30 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.10 V and relative outstanding FF of 79.75%, which is greatly higher than the control device. Meanwhile, the device with the double passivation exhibits outstanding PCE stability, retaining 94.71% of its initial value even after storing it for 300 h, and 93.00% of initial value after heating it for 60 min at 85 °C and humidity of approximately 50% under atmosphere environment without any encapsulation. All the results suggest that the double passivation strategy is an efficient way to improve the perovskite material and enhance the performance of PSCs.

In this paper, we demonstrate a facile strategy which can carry out double passivation to improve the performance of PSCs. By using the dilute halide salt PEABr solution to treat the perovskite film,  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  can precipitate from the perovskite. Both PEABr and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  can passivate the perovskite film, the double passivation improves the performance of PSCs significantly.
