# Abstract

We present a simple and effective method for increasing the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) and oxidative stability of tin-lead hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs). The Ag-doped PSCs with encapsulation retain 95% of their initial PCE after exposure to ambient air for 1000 h. Ag doping increases the grain size and reduces the thickness of amorphous intergranular films, and thereby inhibits the infiltration of oxygen into the perovskite crystals. A quantum mechanical simulation with density functional theory revealed that Ag doping increases the energy barrier to the adsorption of oxygen onto the perovskite surface. Ag doping also relaxes the microstrain in the perovskite, which suppresses the chemical interactions between   $  O_{2}  $   and the perovskite. We also verify that this method is also applicable to the tin-only perovskite for highly stable lead-free PSCs. We believe that these results open up the possibility of the use of various dopants to fabricate stable lead-free PSCs.

Perovskites with an ABX $ _3 $  crystal structure (A: inorganic or organic cation, B: divalent metal cation, X: halogen ion) have superior optoelectronic properties, so are considered next-generation photovoltaic materials. They exhibit optical band gaps in the range 1.4–1.6 eV, long charge-carrier lifetimes, and superb absorption coefficients, $ ^{1-5} $  so the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of lead-based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been increased above 25%. $ ^{6} $  However, the toxicity of lead remains an obstacle to the practical use of lead-based PSCs.

Tin-based perovskites are considered the most viable replacements for lead-based perovskites because of tin's excellent carrier mobility, and the relatively high PCEs of the associated PSCs. $ ^{7,8} $  However, tin-based perovskites have severe deficiencies, including the facile oxidation of Sn $ ^{2+} $  to Sn $ ^{4+} $ , fast crystallization, uneven morphology, and high defect density. $ ^{9-12} $  The standard reduction potential of Sn $ ^{4+} $ /Sn $ ^{2+} $  is +0.15 V, whereas that of Pb $ ^{4+} $ /Pb $ ^{2+} $  is +1.67 V; therefore, tin-based perovskites are more easily oxidized than lead-based perovskites. $ ^{13} $  This vulnerability of tin-based perovskites causes self-p-doping of the crystals, which increases the background hole density and disrupts charge carrier transport. $ ^{14} $  In addition, due to their poor stability against oxygen, tin-based perovskite films degrade upon air exposure in a few minutes. $ ^{15,16} $  Thus, although the optoelectronic properties of tin-based PSCs are superior to those of lead-based PSCs, further improvements in the oxidative stability of tin-based PSCs are still required.

In the attempt to replace lead-based PSCs with tin-based PSCs, recent studies have combined tin with lead to overcome the disadvantages of tin-only PSCs. The bandgap of tin-lead mixed perovskites can be tuned (1.2–1.6 eV) by controlling the ratio of tin to lead, which means they can be used as low-bandgap absorbers in tandem solar cells.  $  ^{[17-20]}  $   The vulnerability of tin-lead hybrid perovskites to oxidation is less than that of tin-only lead-free perovskites.  $  ^{[21]}  $   Since the

However, the   $  Sn^{2+}  $   in tin-lead hybrid perovskites also oxidizes to form   $  Sn^{4+}  $   in a few hours upon exposure to ambient air,  $  ^{21}  $   so these hybrids also need further development.

Many efforts have been made to improve the oxidative stability of tin-based perovskite films. SnF $ _2 $  is commonly added to the tin-based perovskite precursor and acts as a reducing agent that minimizes Sn $ ^{2+} $  oxidation to Sn $ ^{4+} $ . $ ^{13,22} $  In addition to SnF $ _2 $ , other tin halides (i.e., SnCl $ _2 $ , SnBr $ _2 $ , SnI $ _2 $ ), $ ^{23} $  and other additives $ ^{24-26} $  have been found to have positive effects on the stabilization of tin-based perovskites. Incorporation of additional organic cations can improve the film morphology and passivate the surface defects of lead-free perovskites, and thereby improves their stability. $ ^{27,28} $  Compositional engineering can be an effective method for improving the oxidative stability of tin-based perovskites. The introduction of bromide to tin-based perovskites to form mixed halide FASnI $ _x $ Br $ _{3-x} $  suppresses photocatalytic reactions with TiO $ _2 $  that accelerate the degradation of the perovskite, and thereby improves the operational stability of the associated PSCs. $ ^{29} $  The incorporation of ultra-sensitive Ge into tin-based perovskites results in Sn-Ge hybrid perovskite alloys. $ ^{30} $  The exposure of the Sn-Ge-alloyed perovskite to air produces a thin GeO $ _2 $  layer at the grain boundary, which protects the inner crystals from further oxidation. However, compositional engineering can induce unintended changes in the physical properties of perovskites such as their energetic structures, carrier transport dynamics, and Goldschmidt tolerance factors. $ ^{31} $

As for compositional engineering, the ion doping of perovskites can be used to tune the crystals' bulk properties. Two distinctive differences between doping and compositional engineering are that the amount of dopant added to the perovskite precursor is typically much smaller, and that dopants are located at either interstitial or substitutional sites. In this study, we performed the doping of tin-lead hybrid perovskite crystals to tune their bulk material properties and demonstrated that this approach provides a simple and effective method for improving their oxidative stability, and increasing the PCEs of the associated PSCs. We doped tin-lead hybrid   $  (\mathrm{NH}_{2})_{2}\mathrm{CHPb}_{0.5}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.5}\mathrm{I}_{3}  $   (  $  FAPb_{0.5}Sn_{0.5}I_{3}  $  ) perovskite with Ag to suppress oxidative degradation of the crystal. Ag was chosen because 1) its ionic radius (1.15 Å) is similar to those of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   (1.19 Å) and   $  Sn^{2+}  $   (1.18 Å) so Ag doping does not severely distort the crystal lattice, and 2) Ag doping increases the PCEs of PSCs.  $  ^{34, 35, 38}  $   We fabricated hybrid perovskite films and used them as photoactive layers to fabricate PSCs with the structure indium tin oxide (ITO)/PEDOT:PSS/perovskite/  $  C_{60}  $  /bathocuproine (BCP)/Ag. We demonstrated that the Ag doping of the tin-lead hybrid perovskites improves the PCEs of the associated PSCs and suppresses both the self-doping and the oxygen-induced oxidation of the perovskite films. We investigated the mechanism of the improved oxidative stability of the perovskite from morphological and thermodynamic viewpoints. Our density functional theory (DFT) calculations indicate that Ag doping also improves the oxidative resistance of lead-free   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite.

Dopant concentrations up to 0.09 increased the PCEs of the associated PSCs and the PSC with [Ag] = 0.09 exhibited the highest PCE; however, further increases in the Ag concentration decreased the PCEs of the associated PSCs (Figure S1). To investigate the effects of Ag doping on the carrier concentration in the associated PSCs, we measured the thermal voltage as a function of the temperature difference of perovskite films without and with Ag doping, and then extracted the Seebeck coefficients S (Figure 1c). Undoped perovskite films had   $  S = 620 \mu V K^{-1}  $  , and Ag-doped films had   $  S = 1040 \mu V K^{-1}  $  . These positive values of S indicate that the perovskite films were p-doped; this result is consistent with the self-doping nature of tin. The increased S of the Ag-doped films indicates a decrease in carrier concentration.  $  ^{39-41}  $   These results reveal that Ag doping suppressed self-doping in the perovskite films.

To quantify the carrier concentration, we obtained the capacitance  $  ^{-2}  $  -voltage curves of the PSCs without and with Ag doping (Figure 1d), and carried out Mott-Schottky analyses by using the equation

$$

The trap-filled-limit voltages   $  V_{TFL}  $   were obtained to quantify the effects of Ag doping on the hole defects in the perovskite films.   $  V_{TFL}  $   was determined at the kink point, at which the increase in the dark current as a function of voltage on a log-log scale changes from a slope of 1 to a slope >3. The undoped device had   $  V_{TFL} = 0.81  $   V, and the Ag-doped device had   $  V_{TFL} = 0.73  $   V (Figure 1f). The carrier trap density is proportional to   $  V_{TFL}  $   so this decrease in   $  V_{TFL}  $   in the Ag-doped device reveals that Ag doping reduces the trap density; this result is consistent with the increase in   $  V_{OC}  $   of the device with [Ag] = 0.09 (Table 1).

Ag doping induced a downward shift in the perovskite (012) peak; this shift increased as   $  [Ag]  $   increased (Figure 2d). The downward shifts in the XPS spectra might be due to the increased lattice constants that resulted from the presence of interstitial Ag dopant because it changes the effective nuclear charge.  $  ^{44,45}  $

We quantified the effects of Ag doping on the oxidative stability of the tin-lead hybrid perovskite films and the associated solar cells. The tin-lead hybrid perovskite films were fabricated without and with Ag doping. Sn 3d XPS spectra of those films were obtained before and after exposure to dehumidified air for  $ 14\mathrm{h} $  (Figure S2). We deconvoluted the XPS spectra and then calculated the ratio of  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{4+} $  to  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  in each film (Figure 3a; Table S1). The as-prepared undoped film had  $ 33\% $   $ \mathrm{Sn}^{4+} $ , and the as-prepared film with  $ [\mathrm{Ag}] = 0.09 $  had  $ 30\% $   $ \mathrm{Sn}^{4+} $ ; these results are consistent with those for the reduced self-doping of the Ag-doped perovskites. After exposure to dehumidified air for  $ 14\mathrm{h} $ ,  $ \mathrm{O}_2 $ -induced degradation increased the  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{4+} $  content to  $ 69\% $  in the undoped film, but only to  $ 54\% $  in the Ag-doped film. This result indicates that Ag doping suppresses the oxidative degradation of the perovskite films.
