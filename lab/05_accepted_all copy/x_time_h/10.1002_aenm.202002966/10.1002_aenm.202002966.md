# Defect Suppression in Oriented 2D Perovskite Solar Cells with Efficiency over 18% via Rerouting Crystallization Pathway

Yi Yang, Cheng Liu, Olga A. Syzgantseva, Maria A. Syzgantseva, Shuang Ma, Yong Ding,* Molang Cai, Xuepeng Liu, Songyuan Dai,* and Mohammad Khaja Nazeeruddin*

Vertically oriented 2D perovskites exhibit promising optoelectronic properties and intrinsic stability, but their photovoltaic application is still limited by the low power conversion efficiency (PCE) compared to 3D analogs. Here, a new crystallization pathway (RCP) is reported to suppress defects in vertically oriented 2D perovskite caused by its over-rapid self-assembly behavior. By controlling the specific adsorption of an ammonium halide additive on different perovskite crystal planes, the dynamic preferred growth of (111) plane is intentionally restrained, and the minority (202) planes emerge as secondary nucleation sites to stimulate the creation of large grains. As the halogen-regulated deprotonation of ammonium proceeds, the (111) crystal plane gradually recovers its growth dominance, and a vertically oriented 2D perovskite film finally forms with high homogeneity, reduced trap density of states, and desired carrier transport/collection kinetics. Solar cells using RCP-2D films show a highly reproducible and stable PCE reaching  $ 18.5\% $  with a high fill factor of  $ 83.4\% $ . These findings provide critical missing information on simultaneously achieving highly oriented and less defective 2D perovskite films for excellent device performance.

In this contribution, we reroute the crystallization pathway (RCP) to suppress defects in vertically oriented 2D perovskite induced by its over-rapid self-assembly behavior. A joint experimental and theoretical investigation shows that incorporating iodide into the ammonium halide additive will change its preferential adsorption surface from (202) to (111). The dynamic preferred growth of (111) plane is temporarily restrained during the secondary nucleation stage, which provides opportunities for the minority (202) planes to emerge as nucleation sites to generate large grains. The subsequent deprotonation of ammonium promotes the restoration of the (111) dominated growth, and a vertically oriented 2D perovskite film finally forms with high homogeneity and reduced deep-level trap states. All three films show preferential growth of (111) crystal plane with a similar   $  I_{111/202}  $   around 1.2 at the initial stage (within the first 10 s of the crystallization) while varying the halide constituent generates a different crystallization pathway. The (111)-dominated growth was observed throughout the entire crystallization process for the film with   $  NH_{4}Cl  $ , and the thermal annealing made a limited contribution to the crystallinity, indicating an over-rapid crystallization feature of 2D perovskite. On the contrary, a retarded crystallization involving a suppressed growth of (111) crystal plane and an enhanced orientation along the (202) plane was observed for both films with   $  NH_{4}I  $   and   $  NH_{4}I_{x}Cl_{1-x}  $ , and the (111)-dominated orientation could be gradually restored during the thermal annealing. Although the three annealed films unexceptionally demonstrate an   $  I_{111/202}  $   around 1.4 regardless of the different crystallization pathways, the film with   $  NH_{4}I_{x}Cl_{1-x}  $   possesses higher crystallinity and decreased full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) for both (111) and (202) planes (Figure S2, Supporting Information). This suggests rerouting the crystallization pathway by incorporating iodide into ammonium halides is a potential approach to simultaneously improve the crystal quality and grain orientation of 2D perovskite.

The effect of the RCP strategy on final perovskite morphology was explored by cross-sectional scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Though columnar morphologies were obtained for the three films (Figure 1d–f), numerous grain boundaries, longitudinal discontinuity, and misaligned growth appeared in the film with   $  NH_{4}Cl  $  . The films with   $  NH_{4}I  $   and   $  NH_{4}I_{x}Cl_{1-x}  $   exhibit larger grains and fewer boundaries with better continuity and lower pinhole density (Figure S3, Supporting Information). This improvement in film morphology confirms that the RCP method is a suitable route to fabricate high-quality 2D perovskite films.

The detailed crystallographic orientation of 2D perovskite films was investigated by the grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) (Figure 1g–i). Both   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   and   $  NH_{4}I_{x}Cl_{1-x}  $  -contained films express strong, sharp, and discrete Bragg spots, suggesting the directional alignment.  $  ^{[23,24]}  $   The absence of diffraction spots along   $  q_{z}  $   between 0 and 1 Å  $  ^{-1}  $   together with the clear characteristic absorption peaks of the layered perovskite (Figure S4, Supporting Information) suggests the dominated out-of-plane orientation.  $  ^{[25,26]}  $   The more concentrated and intense diffraction spots for the   $  NH_{4}I_{x}Cl_{1-x}  $

film demonstrate not only better crystallinity but also a higher degree of out-of-plane orientation, possibly attributed to the enhanced crystal quality and alignment. However, the film with   $  NH_{4}I  $   exhibits obvious isotropic Debye–Scherrer arcs at   $  q_{z}=1\ \AA^{-1}  $  , indicating an additional feature of random orientation, which will restrain the out-of-plane charge transport and severely damage the device performance. The pole plots of the azimuth angle integrated around   $  q_{z}=1\ \AA^{-1}  $   (Figure S5, Supporting Information) present a stronger peak at an azimuth angle of   $  90^{\circ}\ (I_{90})  $  , with the peak intensity at   $  1^{\circ}-5^{\circ}\ (I_{0})  $   unchanged for the film with   $  NH_{4}I_{x}Cl_{1-x}  $  . The reduced   $  I_{90}/I_{0}  $   ratio from 4.53 to 1.75 provides powerful evidence that the preferred out-of-plane orientation is supported and other unfavorable orientations are avoided in the   $  NH_{4}I_{x}Cl_{1-x}  $   film, contributing to a much ordered and uniform crystallization.  $  ^{[25]}  $   Therefore,   $  NH_{4}I_{x}Cl_{1-x}  $   is the best candidate to reroute crystallization considering all the factors including morphology, crystallinity, and preferential orientation.

Decreased charge transport lifetime and increased carrier recombination lifetime demonstrate an enhanced charge transfer/collection efficiency and reduced carrier recombination for the RCP-2D device (Table 3).[42] The more significant change in carrier recombination lifetime than to transport lifetime corresponds to more enhancement of  $  V_{\mathrm{OC}}  $  rather than  $  J_{\mathrm{SC}}  $ . In summary, we have demonstrated high-efficiency 2D PSCs showing a PCE reaching 18.5% with a high FF of 83.4%, which is among the most efficient 2D PSCs. Our results indicate that rerouting the crystallization pathway is a feasible strategy to suppress defects in vertically oriented 2D perovskite films. We reveal the critical role of additive adsorption in regulating the nucleation and crystallization kinetics for achieving 2D perovskite films with low trap states and desired carrier transport/collection properties. This study provides a broad avenue to simultaneously achieve highly oriented and low-defect 2D perovskite films for advanced device performance, which can be further applied to other 2D perovskite optoelectronic devices, such as light-emitting diodes (LEDs), photoemission, and photodetectors.
