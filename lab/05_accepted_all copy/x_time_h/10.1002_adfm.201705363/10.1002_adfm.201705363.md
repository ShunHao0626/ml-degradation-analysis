# Humidity-Induced Degradation via Grain Boundaries of   $  HC(NH_{2})_{2}PbI_{3}  $   Planar Perovskite Solar Cells

Jae Sung Yun,  $  ^{*}  $  Jincheol Kim, Trevor Young, Robert J. Patterson, Dohyung Kim, Jan Seidel, Sean Lim, Martin A. Green, Shujuan Huang, and Anita Ho-Baillie  $  ^{*}  $

The sensitivity of organic-inorganic perovskites to environmental factors remains a major barrier for these materials to become commercially viable for photovoltaic applications. In this work, the degradation of formamidinium lead iodide (FAPbI $ _3 $ ) perovskite in a moist environment is systematically investigated. It is shown that the level of relative humidity (RH) is important for the onset of degradation processes. Below  $ 30\% $  RH, the black phase of the FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite shows excellent phase stability over 90 d. Once the RH reaches  $ 50\% $ , degradation of the FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite occurs rapidly. Results from a Kelvin probe force microscopy study reveal that the formation of nonperovskite phases initiates at the grain boundaries and the phase transition proceeds toward the grain interiors. Also, ion migration along the grain boundaries is greatly enhanced upon degradation. A post-thermal treatment (PTT) that removes chemical residues at the grain boundaries which effectively slows the degradation process is developed. Finally, it is demonstrated that the PTT process improves the performance and stability of the final device.

In this study, we systematically investigate the effects of humidity on  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  films and solar cells. We show that the  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  perovskite is relatively stable when stored below  $ 30\% $  RH at room temperature. Severe degradation of the perovskite is observed when the sample is stored at  $ \approx 50\% $  RH. The black perovskite starts to lose its color to pale yellow or becomes clear in some regions due to the formation of a nonperovskite yellow phase and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . When soaked into water, the black perovskite was completely decomposed into  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  without presence of the yellow phase. We performed Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) on the degraded samples. First, we found that the formation of nonperovskite phases initiates at the grain boundaries and the phase transition proceeds toward grain interiors. Second, it was observed that ion migration along the grain boundaries is greatly enhanced after degradation, possibly due to a rapid solvation process occurring at the grain boundaries. We developed a post-thermal treatment (PTT) that may remove chemical residues and trapped moisture at the grain boundaries formed during processing which not only reduced ionic defects but also effectively improved the performance and stability of the final device.

We performed bias voltage dependent KPFM measurement on fresh samples, degraded samples (pale yellow region of the sample stored  $ \approx 50\% $  RH for 3 d) and samples that received a thermal treatment which we call post-thermal treatment. KPFM is capable of detecting local surface potential caused by ions. We have shown previously that mobile ion migration can be induced by applying a bias at the AFM tip and KPFM is capable of detecting the local potential set up by individual ions.[37] After the applied bias at the AFM tip is removed, it takes several minutes for the CPD values to return to their original values. CPD changes in such a large time scale, up to several hundred seconds, can be related to the presence of ion migration due to the applied bias voltage. As described earlier, the degradation initiates at the grain boundaries when moisture is introduced. Molecular dynamics simulation of MAPbI $ _3 $  showed that a rapid solvation process occurs in which I atoms are released by the interaction of water molecules with Pb atoms within methylammonium iodide (MAI). $ ^{[41]} $  Therefore, it is likely that the amount of ionic defects and/or mobile ions is greater at the grain boundaries after degradation. This explains why a much larger variation of the CPD difference at the grain boundary is observed under voltage bias in the degraded sample compared to the fresh sample.

Wang et al.  $  ^{[42]}  $  performed cross-section transmission electron microscopy (TEM) on MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskite and identified non-stoichiometric amorphous regions at the grain boundaries. The

segregation of nonstoichiometric material such as that having excess MAI at the grain boundaries facilitates the quick permeation of moisture. Another high resolution TEM study on FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite performed by Aguiar et al. $ ^{[28]} $  showed that residual water vapor or oxygen can be trapped at the grain boundaries during the processing of the samples. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, we showed that the RH level is critical for the onset of degradation process in   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  . Surprisingly, the black phase of the   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite showed excellent phase stability when stored below 30% RH over 90 d. At 50% RH, degradation of the   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite occurs rapidly resulting in the formation of nonperovskite phase. When the film is soaked into water, complete decomposition into   $  PbI_{2}  $   occurs. We also observed morphological changes of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   upon degradation. Our KPFM studies revealed that the nonperovskite phase initiates at the grain boundaries, eventually transforms neighboring grains into the nonperovskite phase and forms a large crystal. KPFM measurement confirmed that CPD differences between the grain boundaries and grain interiors are larger when the film is degraded. Also, we compared ion migration behavior at the grain boundaries between fresh and degraded samples via bias voltage dependent KPFM measurement. Our results demonstrated that ion migration is more active at the grain boundaries when the sample is degraded which could be responsible for an increased number of mobile ions at the grain boundaries. We then performed a rather simple post-thermal treatment process that drives out chemical residues at the grain boundaries reducing ion migration at the grain boundaries. Finally, we fabricated devices with and without PTT process. The devices with the PTT process show improved performance with the best efficiency of 18.1%. We also performed air stability test. Our results demonstrated that air stability is greatly improved by the PTT process and we propose that this process can also be employed to any solution processed perovskite devices.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: All chemicals were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, Alfa Aesar or Lumtec (Luminescence Technology Corp.) and used without further purification. CH(NH $ _2 $ ) $ _2 $ I was synthesized by reacting 15 g formamidine acetate and 30 mL HI (57 wt% in water) in 100 mL ethanol at 0 °C for 2 h with stirring. The precipitate was recovered by rotary evaporator at 50 °C for 1 h. The white crystal was obtained by washing with diethyl ether followed by recrystallization from Ethanol.

Sample Fabrication: A dense   $  TiO_{2}  $   blocking layer was deposited by spray pyrolysis (  $  \approx50  $   nm) using   $  20 \times 10^{-3}  $   M titanium diisopropoxide

bis(acetylacetonate) solution at 450 °C on cleaned FTO glass (TEC8).  $  ^{[28]}  $   To prepare 1.2 M   $  \mathrm{CH(NH_{2})_{2}PbI_{3}}  $   solution, the prepared   $  \mathrm{CH(NH_{2})_{2}I}  $   is mixed with   $  PbI_{2}  $   in dimethylformamide at 1:1 mole ratio at room temperature. The solution with added HI (0.10 g mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) was spread on the   $  bl-TiO_{2}/FTO  $   and spun at 6500 rpm for 30 s using gas-assisted method.  $  ^{[7]}  $   The films were dried on a hot plate at 160 °C for 20 min. For the post-thermal treated perovskite solar cell, the deposited black perovskite films (which were cooled for 20 min after initial annealing in a   $  N_{2}  $   filled glove box) were reannealed for 1 h at 85 °C. A solution of spiro-OMeTAD containing 41.6 mg spiro-OMeTAD, 7.5 μL of a 500 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide in acetonitrile and 16.9 μL 4-tert-butylpyridine in 0.5 mL chlorobenzene was spin-coated on the FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /bl-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /FTO substrate at 3000 rpm for 30 s. A 100 nm gold electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation to complete the device.

Characterization: Relative humidity was controlled within the range from 10% to 85% using an ESPEC SMS-2 environmental chamber. The temperature and humidity data were recorded by both the device thermocouple and humidity sensors and an ONSET HOBO MX1101 humidity and temperature probe. X-ray diffraction patterns were measured using a PANalytical Xpert Materials Research diffractometer system with a Cu Kα radiation source (  $  \lambda = 0.1541  $  nm) at 45 kV and 40 mA. KPFM measurements were carried out using an AFM (AIST-NT SmartSPM 1000) in air using a gold coated Si tip with 6 nm radius of curvature (HYDRA6R-100NG-10, APPNANO) as the probe in a noncontact mode with an AC voltage of -1 to +1 V. The measurement is performed at a resonance frequency of 66 kHz and a spring constants of  $  \approx 0.284  $  N m  $  ^{-1}  $ . The PL spectrum was measured in a spectrometer using a thermoelectric cooled Si-CCD detector, with excitation at 405 nm. The J-V measurements were performed using an IV5 solar cell I-V testing system from PV measurements, Inc. (using a Keithley 2400 source meter) under illumination power of 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  by an AM1.5G solar simulator (Oriel model 94023A) with a 0.159 cm  $  ^{2}  $  aperture and a scan rate of 1.2 V s  $  ^{-1}  $ . All J-V measurements were undertaken on encapsulated perovskite devices (encapsulant: Nagase ChemTex, XNR5570) at room temperature in ambient condition.
