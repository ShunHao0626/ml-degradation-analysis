# ABSTRACT

Owing to the remarkable advantages of the perovskite solar cell (PSC), people regard the PSC as an auspicious candidate for photovoltaic devices in the near future, but light instability of the PSC, caused by  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  being a photo catalyst to decompose perovskite films under UV radiation, limits its practical applications. To improve light stability, removing  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer from PSC devices is the best solution, but the photovoltaic conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of the reported  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -layer-free PSCs are much lower than  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -layer-containing PSCs. # INTRODUCTION

Solar cells, which are capable of converting solar energies to electric energies, are widely researched given the imperious need to develop sustainable energy sources so as to reduce fossil fuel demands and hence alleviate the problems of climate change caused by carbon dioxide increases.  $  ^{1-4}  $   Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are garnering more and more attentions of researchers and the photovoltaic conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has been readily promoted from 3.8% to 20.1% in several years.  $  ^{5}  $   Owing to the various advantages of the PSCs, i.e. the large coefficient for light-absorption,  $  ^{6}  $   remarkable mobility of charge-carriers,  $  ^{7}  $   low cost and facile fabrication processes based on solution,  $  ^{8-10}  $   PSCs have good prospects for commercialisation and are most probably photovoltaic devices of the next generation.  $  ^{11-15}  $

But the poor thermal, humidity, and light stability of PSC devices limits their practical application.  $  ^{16-20}  $   The thermal and humidity stability can be strengthened readily by encapsulation of the devices, reformulation of perovskite composition, inclusion of a hole-transport material (HTM),  $  ^{21}  $   or doping of HTMs.  $  ^{22}  $   Nevertheless it is rather challenging to enhance the stability of PSC devices under long-time illumination. Snaith et al.  $  ^{24}  $   states that   $  TiO_{2}  $  , as an excellent photo-catalyst under UV radiation, causes the compositional degradation of perovskite films. The stability of PSCs is mainly determined by that of the perovskite layer. Hence though Bphen is organic and   $  TiO_{2}  $   is inorganic, Bphen-layer-containing solar cell presented a better stability compared to the   $  TiO_{2}  $  -included one.

Researchers have adopted various strategies to handle the negative influence of   $  TiO_{2}  $  , e.g. interface passivation and use of a UV filter, etc.  $  ^{24-26}  $   However, the best solution would be removing   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer from PSC devices, but keeping excellent photovoltaic performance of   $  TiO_{2}  $  -layer-containing devices.  $  ^{21,22}  $     $  TiO_{2}  $  -layer-free PSCs have advantages of low cost and facile fabrication at a low temperature. However, to date, the reported   $  TiO_{2}  $  -free PSCs usually have much low PCEs compared to   $  TiO_{2}  $  -layer-containing devices.  $  ^{21,22,27-34}  $

Measurements and Characterizations. We were puzzled that the PCEs of the PSCs with structures 3 and 4 are close to each other. It seemed that the  $ \mathrm{bl - TiO_2} $  layer is useless, removal of which causes no drop in the PCE. After careful analysis, the mechanism behind the puzzling phenomena has been clarified. In fact, in the cell of structure 4, formation of the Schottky barrier (SB) between the perovskite film and the ITO electrode provides the blocking role of the  $ \mathrm{bl - TiO_2} $  layer of the cell of structure 3, leading to both structures of cells having nearly the same PCEs. PSCs have good prospects for commercialisation, but light instability of PSC devices limits their practical application owing to  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  being a photo catalyst under UV radiation and being able to accelerate decomposition of organic groups of perovskite films. The best solution would be removing  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer from PSC devices, but to date the reported  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -layer-free
