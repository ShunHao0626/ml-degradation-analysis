# Deep Surface Passivation for Efficient and Hydrophobic Perovskite Solar Cells  $  ^{†}  $

Junmin Xia,  $  ^{\dagger a}  $   Chao Liang,  $  ^{\ddagger a}  $   Shiliang Mei,  $  ^{a}  $   Hao Gu,  $  ^{a}  $   Bingchen He,  $  ^{a}  $   Zhipeng Zhang,  $  ^{a}  $   Tanghao Liu,  $  ^{a}  $   Kaiyang Wang,  $  ^{a}  $   Sisi Wang,  $  ^{a}  $   Shi Chen,  $  ^{a}  $   Yongqing Cai  $  ^{*a}  $   and Guichuan Xing  $  ^{*a}  $

Defect passivation has developed as an attractive approach to promoting the performance of perovskite solar cells. However, robust control of the interplay between the defects and adsorbates is challenging and critical for applications. Here, the hydrophobic 3-(Trifluoromethyl) phenethylamine hydroiodide (CF₃PEAI) is introduced on the surface of perovskite films. By controlling the post-process, the amphipathic passivation agent CF₃PEAI can form 2D perovskite and deepen into the films (>30 nm), which can further passivate the internal defects and improve the long-term stability of perovskite films. Furthermore, the CF₃PEAI 2D passivated perovskite films exhibit better energy level alignment, lower surface roughness, longer carrier lifetime, lower trap state density, and higher stability than the control and without annealing ones. Our first-principles simulation reveals that the CF₃PEAI effectively renormalizes the defective states of the vacancies with benign characteristics. The defect-adsorbate complex facilitates the manipulation of versatile electronic states for a prolonged lifetime of photoexcited carriers with improved structural integrity. Perovskite solar cells' performance is enhanced from 18.87% to 21.05% along with improved stability, which can retain 98% of initial performance after 528 h in ambient with a humidity of 70-80%.

# Introduction

As the most promising photoelectric materials, organic-inorganic hybrid lead halide perovskites have attracted considerable attention in the photovoltaic field due to their superior ambipolar carrier transportability, $ ^{1} $  long charge carrier diffusion length and lifetime, $ ^{2} $  tuneable bandgap, $ ^{3} $  impressive visible light absorption coefficient, $ ^{4} $  and low-temperature solution processability. $ ^{5} $  In the short decade, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has increased rapidly from 3.8% $ ^{6} $  to 25.2%, $ ^{7} $  which is close to the efficiency of monocrystalline silicon solar cells. $ ^{8} $  Moreover, the stability that restricts their industrial development has been dramatically improved. $ ^{9-11} $

However, the solution-processed films inevitably have some defects in the crystal structure, including (1) intrinsic point defects, (2) impurities defects, and (3) two-dimensional surface defects or grain boundary defects.  $  ^{12}  $   These defects would increase the carrier nonradiative recombination,  $  ^{13}  $   reduce the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of solar cells,  $  ^{14}  $   thus affecting the PCE

of PSCs. Furthermore, the interaction between defects and mobile ions significantly induces the hysteresis  $  ^{15}  $   and decomposition of the devices.  $  ^{16}  $   As polycrystalline, these defects in perovskite films are inevitable.  $  ^{17}  $   Hence, defect passivation has become a crucial optimization strategy for stable and efficient PSCs.  $  ^{8,9,18-20}  $   To passivate the defect and optimize interfacial parameters, the researchers have designed various passivation technologies based on different mechanisms, including the excess lead iodide (PbI  $  _{2}  $  ),  $  ^{21}  $   Lewis acid/base,  $  ^{22}  $   the two-dimensional (2D) perovskite with different functional groups  $  ^{23-25}  $   and so on.  $  ^{26}  $   Among them, the 2D perovskite passivation has attracted substantial research interest due to its superior passivation effect, outstanding hydrophobicity, and tuneable photoelectrical properties, which can reduce the trap density and enhance the stability of perovskite films.  $  ^{27}  $   According to recent reports, the aromatic organic ammonia molecules exhibit an excellent passivation effect.  $  ^{28-30}  $   Although these surface passivation technologies have significantly improved the PSCs, the passivation mechanism employing different functional groups and reaction depth are still unclear, which needs to be systematically studied.  $  ^{31}  $

Here, we employed the amphipathic 3-(Trifluoromethyl)phenethylamine hydroiodide  $ \mathrm{(CF_3PEAI)} $  as the hydrophobic passivation molecule to study the passivation mechanism and the interplay between the defect and adsorbate for robust manipulation. The absorption intensity of passivated films retains almost the same, while that of the pristine has a dramatic drop after 13 days (Fig. S7a). Furthermore, Fig. 6c shows the time dependent PCE variation of unencapsulated devices stored in ambient conditions (RH, 70-80%). It is observed that the PCE of   $  CF_{3}PEAI  $   2D passivated devices sustains almost no decrease (98%) for 528 h storage, while the pristine devices retain less than 85% of their initial PCE, which is consistent with the results of the absorption test.

# Conclusion

In summary, through a combined study with first-principles calculations, we have demonstrated  $ \mathrm{CF_3PEAI} $  as a competent candidate for surface passivation whose functional group could passivate various defects and prevent the surface from reacting with the adsorbed moisture. Also, we conclude that with annealing, the passivation molecules could react more deeply with the perovskite surface. The formed  $ \mathrm{CF_3PEAI} $  2D passivation layer can remarkably increase the  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  by up to  $ 70\mathrm{mV} $ , attributed to the promoted energy level arrangement and suppressed trap states. With the  $ \mathrm{CF_3PEAI} $  2D passivated films, the PCE of the devices has increased from  $ 18.87\% $  to  $ 21.05\% $ . Beyond the significant improvement of the devices, the PCE retains about  $ 98\% $  after more than  $ 500\mathrm{h} $  aged in the ambient (RH,  $ 70 - 80\% $ ).
