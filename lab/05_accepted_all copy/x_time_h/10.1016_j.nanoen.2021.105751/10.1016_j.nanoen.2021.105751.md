Longevity has been a long-standing challenge for perovskite photovoltaics. In general, Li-TFSI/t-BP are the state-of-the-art bi-dopants for the hole-transporting layer (HTL) in perovskite solar cells (PSCs), although such dopants significantly diminish the stability of devices. Here, we reported a novel dopant of fluorinated iron(III) porphine (Fe(III)-PP) as a potential alternative to the Li-TFSI/t-BP. The optimized PSCs exhibit a champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 21.73\% $ , which outperforms the standard bi-dopants Li-TFSI/t-BP  $ (21.07\%) $ . The high migration barrier together with the hydrophobic nature of the Fe(III)-PP, which in turn suppress intrinsic ion migration and alleviate extrinsic moisture-induced degradation, resulting in prominent improvement of long-term stability of PSCs up to  $ 1200\mathrm{h} $  under air exposure without encapsulation.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted extensive attention over the past years due to their intriguing photophysical properties and high power conversion efficiency (PCE)   $  [1-3]  $  . Since the beginning of active research on PSCs, the small organic molecule 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxy-phenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) and conjugated macromolecule poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA) are widely employed and efficient hole-transporting materials in n-i-p PSCs, both of which are usually doped by standard bi-dopants system bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP) due to the low hole mobility and poor conductivity in pristine (Fig. S1)   $  [4-9]  $  . Despite the positive effects of these dopants on the PCE of solar cells, it has been proved that the state-of-the-art bi-dopants Li-TFSI/t-BP are detrimental to the long-term stability of such devices, which remain one of the most challenging obstacles to impede further development of PSCs   $  [10-14]  $  .

Li-TFSI is deliquescent and hygroscopic which tends to absorb moisture from atmosphere to accelerate the hole-transporting layer (HTL) degradation and perovskite decomposition, strongly diminishing

the stability of PSCs [15]. Meanwhile, the Li ions with small van der Waals radius easily migrate across the perovskite absorber layer and accumulate in   $  TiO_{2}  $  , correspondingly leading to stability issues and J-V hysteresis [16]. On the other hand, t-BP, as liquid component with relatively low boiling point in PSCs, which can gradually evaporate during device storage and its corrosive nature towards perovskite leads to new threat to the long-term stability of PSCs [17]. Thus, it is highly desirable and urgent to develop alternative dopants with improved efficiency and stability of PSCs. Several potential dopants have been reported, such as inorganic salt and acid [18,19], ionic liquid [20,21], metal-organic framework [22,23], fullerene derivatives [24,25] and copper-based complexes [26]. However, thus far, these dopants also can't challenge with the performances of the state-of-the-art bi-dopants Li-TFSI/t-BP.

In this work, we reported highly efficient and stable PSCs by employing a fluorinated iron(III) porphine (Fe(III)-PP, Fig. 1a) as a potential dopant for PTAA. Owing to the excellent doping performance, high migration barrier and hydrophobic nature of Fe(III)-PP, the corresponding PSCs exhibit a promising efficiency above 21% with superior long-term stability.

# 2. Results and discussion

Regarding the doping mechanism, the interaction between Fe(III)-PP and PTAA is schematically illustrated in Fig. S2. The nitrogen atoms of the host hole-transporting material PTAA can interact with the electrophilic centers of the fluorinated Fe(III)-PP by a coordination manner, accordingly leaving hole on the triphenylamine moiety, which can be delocalized on the molecule or can even hop along the percolated hole transporter in the amorphous solid HTL [27]. To understand the doping mechanism and electron transfer between the PTAA and Fe(III)-PP, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed. As shown the electrostatic potential (ESP) surface of Fe(III)-PP in Fig. 1a, the dominant positive charges are delocalized on the whole Fe(III)-PP, indicating the strong electron accepting ability and Lewis acid properties [28]. Then, we calculated the charge density difference  $ (\triangle \rho) $  of HTL donor-accept system, i.e., PTAA: Fe(III)-PP complex (Fig. 1b). In order to facilitate the calculations, we simplified the structure of PTAA, the surface in blue and red represent the electron accumulation and depletion, respectively. It can be observed that the total electron density of the PTAA segment is decreased with corresponding gain of the hole density, while electron density of Fe(III)-PP is increased around central electrophilic Fe(III), which means that partial electrons transfer from PTAA segment to Fe(III)-PP, thereby realizing the effective doping of PTAA [24]. This result is consistent with the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) electron distribution of PTAA: Fe(III)-PP complex, totally localizing on the electrophilic Fe(III)-PP cores (Fig. S3). To in-depth evaluate the electron transfer between PTAA and Fe(III)-PP, electron spin resonance (ESR) spectroscopy was measured for dopant-free PTAA and  $ 2.0\% $  Fe(III)-PP doped PTAA solutions (molar ratio with respect to the repeat unit of PTAA). As shown in Fig. 1c, no

appreciable signal is observed in dopant-free PTAA. In contrast, the Fe(III)-PP doped PTAA reveals a strong radical signal, which is obvious evidence of efficient charge transfer between PTAA and Fe(III)-PP [29, 30]. Therefore, both theoretical calculations and experimental results demonstrated the effective electrons transfer between the PTAA and Fe(III)-PP, subsequently leading to increase the free hole density and enhance the conductivity of PTAA.

Ultraviolet photoemission spectroscopy (UPS) spectra were performed to analyze the influence chemical doping on energy levels, as shown in Fig. 1d and e [31,32]. According to the work function  $ (E_{\mathrm{cut - off}}) $  and valence band maximum  $ (E_i) $ , the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels, versus vacuum were calculated to be -5.21 and -5.14 eV for PTAA films doped by  $ 2.0\% $  Fe(III)-PP and Li-TFSI/t-BP, respectively. The deeper HOMO energy level of Fe(III)-PP doped PTAA would be favorable for improving hole-injection efficiency and suppressing voltage loss in PSCs (Fig. S4) [33].

The charge transportation behaviors of PTAA doped by 2.0% Fe(III)-PP and Li-TFSI/t-BP have been microscopically determined using conductive atomic force microscopy (c-AFM), which is an established technique to study conductance of organic electronics [34,35]. As shown in Fig. 2a and b, the PTAA films doped by 2.0% Fe(III)-PP and Li-TFSI/t-BP display a similar root-mean-square (RMS) of 6.8 nm (Fig. S5). The corresponding current maps of PTAA with different dopants are compared in Fig. 2d and e. We find that the detected surface current of 2.0% Fe(III)-PP doped PTAA (396 pA) with c-AFM are substantially higher than those observed in Li-TFSI/t-BP doped PTAA (370 pA) at the same bias (Fig. 2g), indicating superior conductivity of HTL with novel dopant Fe(III)-PP which will facilitate hole transport, hence improving overall photovoltaic performances of PSCs.

As shown in Fig. 2h, a broad PL peak of pristine perovskite film is observed at  $ 790~\mathrm{nm} $ , which is effectively quenched when capping HTL. The quenching efficiency follows the order of  $ 2.0\% $  Fe(III)-PP doped PTAA > Li-TFSI/t-BP doped PTAA > dopant-free PTAA, suggesting that the perovskite/2.0% Fe(III)-PP doped PTAA bilayer exhibits the best hole extraction capability for the corresponding best device performances. In addition, the blue shift of PL peak for perovskite/PTAA + Fe(III)-PP is observed. We attribute the blue shift of PL peak for perovskite/PTAA + Fe(III)-PP to the defects passivation. Fe(III)-PP as a Lewis acid molecule, which can accept electron from the anionic Pb-I antisite defects thus passivates the halide-induced traps at the perovskite and p-type hole-conductor heterojunction, resulting in reduced charge trap density [29]. The TRPL results show that the average decay time  $ (\tau_{\mathrm{avg}}) $  reduced from 778 ns for the pristine perovskite film to 259, 38 and 28 ns when coating dopant-free, Li-TFSI/t-BP and  $ 2.0\% $  Fe(III)-PP doped PTAA layers on the top, respectively (Figs. 2i and S6, Table S1). Thus, TRPL measurements also confirm the improved hole extraction using  $ 2.0\% $  Fe(III)-PP as dopant, which introduces a reduced PL decay lifetime relative to that of the

Li-TFSI/t-BP doped PTAA layer.

We further performed femtosecond transient absorption (fs-TA) spectra to analyze the interfacial hole extraction and recombination kinetics at the perovskite/PTAA interface with ultrafast time resolution. As shown in Fig. 2j and k, the negative ground-state bleaching (GSB) signal appears at  $ \sim 770~\mathrm{nm} $  (Fig. S7) [36]. By comparing the GSB peak between the perovskite/PTAA  $ +2.0\% $  Fe(III)-PP and perovskite/PTAA+Li-TFSI and t-BP, we notice that the GSB signal gradually decreases with prolonged delay time, indicating the photoinduced holes are effectively extracted to HTL from perovskite. Meanwhile, the GSB peak intensity of perovskite/PTAA  $ +2.0\% $  Fe(III)-PP decreases more quickly, which further confirms the faster charge extraction of the photogenerated holes. Fig. 3l displays the transient absorption kinetics probed at GSB peaks, which are fitted with a bi-exponential decay model. In this model, the faster decay components on a timescale of picoseconds ( $ <1000~\mathrm{ps} $ ) are attributed to the process of photogenerated holes being extracted out from the perovskite layer [37], while the slower decay components on a nanosecond level ( $ >1000~\mathrm{ps} $ ) are assigned to trap-assisted recombination in the bulk perovskite and interfaces (Fig. S8) [38]. To evaluate the photovoltaic performances of n-i-p PSCs based on Fe (III)-PP as novel dopant for PTAA, the solar cells were fabricated with a structure of FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $ /m-TiO $ _2 $ /(FAPbI $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.95} $ (MAPbBr $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.05} $ /HTL/Au (Fig. 3a). The cross-sectional scanning electron microscopy (SEM) image of the overall device is presented in Fig. 3b, which demonstrates the high-quality perovskite crystal with large grain sizes ranging from 400 to 2000 nm (Fig. S9). Initially, the effect of doping concentration of Fe (III)-PP on the device performances was systematically investigated through the photocurrent density-voltage ( $ J-V $ ) characteristics, the statistical distribution of PCEs with different doping concentration is compared in Fig. 3c, and the corresponding champion  $ J-V $  curves and

device parameters are shown in Fig. 3d and Table S2. Without any dopant (0%), the PSC shows rather inferior efficiency (PCE = 13.50%,  $  J_{\text{SC}} = 21.37  $  mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ,  $  V_{\text{OC}} = 0.99  $  V and FF = 0.64) due to the low conductivity of pristine PTAA is not enough to transport the injected holes to the Au electrode, which causes severe charge recombination together with reductions of  $  J_{\text{SC}}  $ ,  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $  and FF. With gradually increasing doping concentration from 0.5% to 2.0%, the PCEs of PSCs are significantly improved and their variability are reduced. However, when the doping concentration is further increased to 3.0% and 5.0%, there is an abruptly drop of PCEs with simultaneously decreased  $  J_{\text{SC}}  $ ,  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $  and FF. Under the best optimal concentration of 2.0% Fe(III)-PP, the PSC exhibits a promising PCE of 21.73% ( $  J_{\text{SC}} = 24.05  $  mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ,  $  V_{\text{OC}} = 1.11  $  V and FF = 0.82), which outperforms the best control device with the state-of-the-art bi-dopants Li-TFSI/t-BP doped PTAA (PCE = 21.07%,  $  J_{\text{SC}} = 23.83  $  mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ,  $  V_{\text{OC}} = 1.10  $  V and FF = 0.80, Fig. 3e). This improvement is further verified by statistical distribution (Fig. 3f), the devices show good reproducibility and the PSCs based on the 2.0% Fe (III)-PP exhibit a higher average PCE (20.61 ± 1.12%) than those on the Li-TFSI/t-BP (19.87 ± 1.20%). The long-term stability of PSCs is crucial for practical applications, which was finally investigated under ambient atmosphere and room temperature with relative humidity of 50–85%. The PCE of the device based on 2.0% Fe(III)-PP doped PTAA retains over 93% of its initial efficiency after 1200 h (Fig. 4a), whereas it significantly drops to 41% of the original value for the Li-TFSI/t-BP based PSC. We further performed the stability of PSCs with 2.0% Fe(III)-PP and Li-TFSI/t-BP as HTL dopants under continuous 1-sun illumination in ambient air, as show in Fig. 4b. After over 250 h, the PSCs using Fe(III)-PP dopant retains 90% of its initial efficiency while the PCE of Li-TFSI/t-BP-based device drops to 29% of original PCE after 100 h. In addition, the Fe(III)-PP based device also shows superiorly thermal stability at 85 °C (Fig. S11). We believe that the excellent stability of the Fe(III)-PP based PSCs could be attributed to the both increased extrinsic hydrophobicity and suppressed

intrinsic ion migration.

To demonstrate the effective hydrophobic and moisture-blocking property of fluorinated Fe(III)-PP doped PTAA, we carried out water contact angle measurements (Fig. 4c). As expected, the 2.0% Fe(III)-PP doped PTAA exhibits a higher water contact angle (96°) than the Li-TFSI/t-BP doped PTAA (88°), which suggests a larger barrier for water penetration in the HTL and correspondingly protects the inner perovskite from extrinsic moisture. To further investigate the degradation process of perovskite crystal in complete devices and analyze the origin of superior stability of Fe(III)-PP-based PSCs, we performed synchrotron-based two dimensional grazing incidence X-ray diffraction (2D-GIXD). As shown in Fig. 4d, for the Li-TFSI/t-BP-based device after 1200 h under ambient atmosphere, the perovskite partially decomposes into PbI₂ due to the large amounts of water adsorption by the Li-TFSI and t-BP damage (Figs. S12 and S13), as indicated from the 2D-GIXD that diffraction spot corresponding to PbI₂ (001) plane is increased at q ≈ 9 nm⁻¹ and the typical perovskite diffraction rings at q ≈ 10, 20 and 22 nm⁻¹ corresponding to (110), (220) and (310) crystal planes are decreased [39]. In contrast, the PbI₂ diffraction spot is much weaker and perovskite diffraction rings are much stronger for the aged Fe (III)-PP-based PSCs (Fig. 4e), which demonstrate slower degradation rate and better moisture resistance capability.

(TOF-SIMS) measurements were carried out to determine spatial distribution of Li and Fe ions in the aged PSCs. As shown in Figs. 4f, h and S14, the results confirm that massive Li ions from the HTL have migrated to both sides and even across the perovskite film reaching to  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer, leading to strongly affect the intrinsic stability and  $ J - V $  hysteresis of PSCs (Fig. S16, Table S4) [16]. Comparatively, for Fe(III)-PP, the migration of Fe ions is significantly hindered, still remaining in HTL without obvious diffusion (Fig. 4g and h), which in turn enhance the intrinsic stability of PSCs.

The first-principles DFT computational analysis was further used to study the migration barriers of Fe and Li ions in the perovskite lattice [40]. The simulated model is based on representative  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ , the migration pathways of Fe and Li ions at its initial, transition, final states and corresponding energy barriers are shown in Fig. 5a and b. Obviously, the maximum potential energy is achieved when the Fe ion passes through the shared face of two neighboring unit cells of perovskite (Fig. 5a). The estimated energy barrier of Fe ion is  $ 0.54\mathrm{eV} $  which is significantly higher than of Li ion  $ (0.32\mathrm{eV}, $  Fig. 5b). We attribute the larger migration barrier of Fe ion to the following reasons. On the one hand, Fe ion has a larger van der Waals radius  $ (0.94\AA) $  than Li ion  $ (0.70\AA) $ , which is beneficial to inhibit its migration in the perovskite lattice and increase the migration barrier. On the other hand, we speculate that Fe(III) ion with high potential may have stronger interaction with intrinsic negative ions in the perovskite crystals (such as iodine), accordingly inhibiting their migration. To verify our viewpoint, firstly, we studied the ESP of Fe ion during the migration process. As shown in Fig. 5c-e, the results show that there is a significant interaction between Fe ion and perovskite crystals. To quantitatively analyze the interaction between Fe, Li ions and perovskite crystals, we further calculated the electron localization function (ELF) of Fe and Li ions migration in perovskite lattice. The value of ELF depends on the electron localization and ranges from 0 to 1, where the value of 1 indicates the electron totally localized, the value of 0.5 matches with the electron-gas-like pairs probability and the value of 0 corresponds to extremely low charge density [41]. As shown in Fig. S17, the contour color around the Li ion is blue, which indicates that less electrons located in these regions and there is a weak interaction between the Li ion and the surrounding iodine ions [41]. On the contrary, there is an obviously higher ELF value around the Fe ion compared with Li ion (Fig. 5f-h), indicating the Fe ion has a stronger interaction with the surrounding iodine ions, which contributes to inhibit Fe ion migration in the perovskite lattice, and correspondingly increasing the intrinsic stability of PSCs. Thus, our study not only sheds light on the PSCs intrinsic degradation mechanism by revealing the critical role of ion migration, but also suggests a unique method to further increase the stability of PSCs by increasing migration barrier of HTL dopants and alleviating ion migration within perovskite devices.

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated highly efficient and stable PSCs by employing a fluorinated Fe(III)-PP as a promising dopant for PTAA. The optimized PSCs based on the 2.0% Fe(III)-PP doped PTAA show a champion efficiency of 21.73%, versus 21.07% for the control PSCs with Li-TFSI/t-BP doped PTAA. Importantly, the PSCs exhibit dramatically enhanced long-term stability due to the extrinsically hydrophobic nature and intrinsically high migration barriers of Fe(III)-PP, maintaining above 90% of initial PCE even aging 1200 h and continuous 1-sun illumination for 250 h in ambient environment without encapsulation. Overall, the high efficiency and superior stability of PSCs unambiguously reveal tremendous potential of Fe(III)-PP to replace Li-TFSI/t-BP.
