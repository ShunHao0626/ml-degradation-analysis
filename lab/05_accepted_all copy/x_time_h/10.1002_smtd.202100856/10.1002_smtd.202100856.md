Feng Gao, Chao Luo, Xianjin Wang, and Qing Zhao*

The devices can be further divided into mesoporous or planar type depending on whether a mesoporous scaffold is involved in the ETL layer.[9] The mesoporous type where a layer of mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  is added as a scaffold, commonly shows higher efficiency than the planar type due to a better crystallization state under a relatively high temperature (typically  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ).[10-12] However, PSCs with planar structure show more prospects in terms of more compatibility with flexible device, perovskite-based tandem device applications, and simpler manufacturing compared to mesoporous type PSCs, since the rather complicated and high-temperature sintering process for mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  fabrication.[13-19]

$ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  is one of the most broadly employed electron transport materials in n-i-p structure perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Low-temperature non-hydrolyzed sol-gel method is developed to prepare  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  in order to simplify the fabrication process and match with the planar structure PSCs. Conventional low-temperature  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film using organic solvents as dispersants makes direct doping challenging due to limited solubility. Here, a newly developed water-based  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  solution is directly doped with different alkali chlorides, resulting in better conductivity, compatible energy level matching, and enhanced charge extraction in terms of electron transport layer (ETL) for PSCs. As a result, a power conversion efficiency of  $ 23.15\% $  is achieved based on NaCl-doped  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  with competitive storage stability and light stability. The water-based  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL for more general doping of various solutes opens up a new avenue for environmental-friendly manufacturing superior ETL toward high-efficiency and stable perovskite photovoltaic devices.

# 1. Introduction

The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has swiftly progressed from 3.8% to a certified value of 25.5% in the last decade,  $  ^{[1,2]}  $  a good demonstration of the outstanding photoelectric properties of perovskite materials.  $  ^{[3-5]}  $  PSCs have become strong candidates for the next-generation solar cells in the photovoltaic industry owing to high PCE, simple solution fabrication method, and potential low-cost advantages.  $  ^{[6-8]}  $  PSCs with an n-i-p configuration are usually composed of a transparent conductive electrode, an electron transport layer (ETL), a perovskite light absorption

Methods for preparing compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers at low temperatures (  $  \leq150\ ^{\circ}C  $  ) have been extensively investigated, such as hydrolyzed and nonhydrolyzed sol–gel methods, water bath methods, ball milling methods, etc.  $  ^{[13-15,20,21]}  $   In comparison, the nonhydrolyzed sol–gel method becomes the preferred method for preparing low temperature (LT)  $  \cdotTiO_{2}  $   due to its simple, controllable, and scalable reaction procedures.  $  ^{[13,20,21]}  $   Zhou et al. developed a nonhydrolytic sol–gel approach with ethanol as dispersant, and obtained a device PCE of 19.3%.  $  ^{[20]}  $   Whereas,   $  TiO_{2}  $   is easy to settle in ethanol and requires to use titanium acetylacetonate as stabilizer. In 2017, Tan et al. replaced the dispersant with methanol and chloroform, and found that chlorine-capped   $  TiO_{2}  $   colloidal nanocrystal film mitigates interfacial recombination with Cl passivation, leading to a device PCE of more than 20%.  $  ^{[21]}  $   Later, a more environmental-friendly water-based   $  TiO_{2}  $   (W-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) ETL is developed in the lab.  $  ^{[13]}  $     $  Cl^{-}  $  inherited from   $  TiCl_{4}  $   in W-  $  TiO_{2}  $   colloid is proved to inhibit the antisite defects at   $  TiO_{2}  $ /perovskite interface and suppress the deep-level state defects. In addition, the residual organic molecules on surface of   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanocrystal was further removed by a vacuum rotary evaporation process under 85 °C, leading to a much enhanced operation stability of PSCs.

Generally,   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL prepared by low-temperature methods still needs further improvement in terms of mobility, energy level alignment with perovskite, and defect density reduction, toward a mainstream level of corresponding device PCE.  $  ^{[21]}  $   Metal doping has been demonstrated to be an effective and facile solution to improve the electrical properties of ETL.  $  ^{[22-29]}  $

Especially, alkali metal salts doping has been investigated by virtue of their easy availability and incorporation.  $  ^{[23-26,32-35]}  $   For instance, the role of LiTFSI doping in mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   has been studied and found to improve the charge extraction ability,  $  ^{[24,25]}  $   yet the moisture absorption of LiTFSI has a potential impact on device stability. Sodium salts, including NaCl and   $  Na_{2}S  $ , were also doped into high-temperature   $  TiO_{2}  $ , and proven to suppress oxygen vacancies-induced nonradiative recombination.  $  ^{[26,32,33]}  $   Most of the alkali metal salt treatment work is conducted by spin coating the corresponding salt solution onto the as-prepared high-temperature   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer. Direct doping into low-temperature   $  TiO_{2}  $   has been rarely reported may be because that many salts have rather low solubility into the organic solvent used to prepare low-temperature   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer. Furthermore, there is a lack of systematic comparison research on the doping of various alkali metal cations (  $  Li^{+}  $ ,   $  Na^{+}  $ ,   $  K^{+}  $ ,   $  Rb^{+}  $ ,   $  Cs^{+}  $ ) into   $  TiO_{2}  $   for the enhancive device performance.

Based on the newly developed doping method to produce water-based  $ \mathrm{LT - TiO_2} $  ETL in the lab, such direct doping of various alkali metal cations into  $ \mathrm{LT - TiO_2} $  becomes easy and possible. Water, as the most widely used solvent, makes such study of effect of different inorganic salt on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL in planar PSCs more feasible compared to the conventionally used methanol and chloroform. In this study, we investigated the impact of different alkali chloride dopants on the water-based  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and the device performance, via adding LiCl, NaCl, KCl, RbCl, and CsCl into W-TiO $ _2 $  ETL for PSCs. On the one hand, the alkali chloride doping was found to facilitate the conductivity of W-TiO $ _2 $  and adjust the energy level arrangement, which significantly improved the electron transport capability. On the other hand, the doped chloride anions and alkali metal cations were suggested to synergistically passivate the defects at the interface and grain boundary, effectively reducing the defect state density. # 2. Results and Discussions

The typical planar PSC device structure used in this work is ITO (indium tin oxide)/TiO $ _2 $ /Perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au (Figure 1a). To prepare doped TiO $ _2 $ , we directly added different alkali metal chlorides (AMCl) to the water-based TiO $ _2 $  aqueous dispersion (labeled as TiO $ _2 $ :LiCl, TiO $ _2 $ :NaCl, TiO $ _2 $ :KCl, TiO $ _2 $ :RbCl, TiO $ _2 $ :CsCl, respectively, and collectively called as TiO $ _2 $ :AMCl). Subsequently, the doped TiO $ _2 $  solution was spin-coated onto the ITO substrate, and the TiO $ _2 $  layer was obtained after annealing (see the Experimental Section for more details, Figure 1b). The most impressive superiority of using water as solution in this work is its much higher solubility for alkali metal salts compared to other organic solvents. According to the principle of similar compatibility, polar substances (such as ionic crystals) usually have better solubility in strong polar solvents. As the most polar solvent, water is easy to dissolve ionic compounds such as alkali chlorides. Whereas alkali metal salts are estimated to be difficult to dissolve in organic solvents due to their low polarity. A designed experiment was conducted to visually verify the above conjecture. Commonly used dispersants for the preparation of TiO $ _2 $ , including water, ethanol, and methanol and chloroform, were selected for comparison. As shown in Figure S1 in the Supporting Information, after several hours of thorough stirring, NaCl still precipitated at the bottom, failing to dissolve in ethanol or methanol and chloroform. While the water solution is very clear, showing much better solubility for NaCl.

To gain further insight into the effect of doping on the electrical properties of W-TiO $ _2 $ , the current–voltage curve of the device with ITO/ETL/Au structure was measured. The vertical conductivity of TiO $ _2 $  film was calculated according to the following equation:  $ \sigma = Id(VA)^{-1} $ , where  $ A $  is the area and  $ d $  is the thickness of TiO $ _2 $  layer. $ ^{[25]} $  The experimental results show that alkali metal salt doping significantly increases the conductivity of W-TiO $ _2 $  (Figure 2d). TiO $ _2 $ :NaCl shows the best results, increasing the conductivity by 46% (Table S2, Supporting Information), followed by KCl and CsCl. Moreover, the electron mobility of W-TiO $ _2 $  and TiO $ _2 $ :NaCl film was measured by space charge-limited current (SCLC) method with the structure of ITO/Au/ETL/Au (Figure 2f). TiO $ _2 $ :NaCl film also demonstrates a larger electron mobility of  $ 5.3 \times 10^{-5} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $  compared to W-TiO $ _2 $  ( $ 3.2 \times 10^{-5} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ ). The enhanced electrical conductivity and electron mobility could facilitate the electron extraction capability of TiO $ _2 $  from perovskite, reducing the charge accumulation and recombination at the ETL/perovskite interface. $ ^{[11,35]} $

Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements were conducted to study the carrier transport dynamics at perovskite/ETL interface. Figure 3a shows the PL spectra of perovskite deposited on different TiO₂:AMCl ETLs on glass substrate. The PL intensity of the doped TiO₂/perovskite is weaker than that of the pristine W-TiO₂/perovskite, indicating that alkali metal doping brings superior carrier transport capability, visualized by the successively weakened PL peak intensity of LiCl, RbCl, CsCl, KCl, NaCl-correlated sample (Figure 3a). The lowest PL peak intensity occurs in TiO₂:NaCl/perovskite, indicating the best carrier extraction ability among these alkali metal salts. Figure 3b is the normalized TRPL of perovskite on different ETLs, showing a double exponential decay feature. The fluorescence lifetime decay curve has been well fitted, and the fitting results are shown in Table S3 in the Supporting Information. The fast decay lifetime (τ₁) is derived from the quenching of carriers at the interface, and the slow decay lifetime (τ₂) is attributed to the radiation recombination caused by traps inside the perovskite.[11,18] The pristine W-TiO₂ sample displayed the longest lifetime τ₁, while the lifetime of doped TiO₂ decreased gradually in the order of doping RbCl, LiCl, CsCl, KCl, and NaCl, and the fitted values were 100.04, 96.9, 92.7, 76.4, 75.1, 66.8 ns, respectively. The shortened carrier lifetime demonstrates that alkali

metal salt doping effectively promotes the electron transfer ability from perovskite to   $  TiO_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[49]}  $

To elucidate the effect of alkali metal salt doping on the defects of device and recombination dynamics, we characterized PSCs with   $  TiO_{2}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}:NaCl  $   as ETL by means of transient photocurrent decay (TPC) and transient photovoltage decay (TPV). TPV and TPC are important means to characterize the behavior of carriers in solar cells at device level and evaluate the transport and loss of carriers in the device.  $  ^{[36]}  $   As shown in Figure 3c, the photocurrent decay time of devices based on   $  TiO_{2}:NaCl  $   was reduced from 1852 to 544 ns, confirming strengthened charge extraction. For the decay time from TPV in Figure 3d, the PSCs with   $  TiO_{2}:NaCl  $   possessed a longer recombination lifetime of 857   $  \mu  $  s compared to that of the PSCs based on control W-  $  TiO_{2}  $   (210   $  \mu  $  s), revealing that the charge recombination within the device was suppressed.  $  ^{[15,47]}  $

Defects on the surface and interface of perovskite solar cells are the primary cause of serious nonradiative recombination.  $  ^{[5]}  $   Electron-only devices with the structure of ITO/ETL/perovskite/PCBM/Au (inset in Figure 3e) were fabricated to measure the trap density within perovskite deposited on W-TiO  $  _{2}  $   and TiO  $  _{2}  $ :NaCl ETLs. Figure 3e shows the dark I-V curve of the corresponding device, which can be divided into an Ohmic linear region and an SCLC region. When the two areas intersect, the voltage at the intersection point is called the trap-filled limit voltage (V  $  _{TFL}  $ ).  $  ^{[35,39]}  $   The V  $  _{TFL}  $   of devices based on W-TiO  $  _{2}  $   and TiO  $  _{2}  $ :NaCl are 0.546 and 0.457 V. The corresponding trap concentrations were calculated according to the equation N  $  _{L}  $   = 2V  $  _{TFL}  $  ε,ε  $  _{0}  $  /qL  $  ^{2}  $  , which are 4.54 × 10  $  ^{15}  $   and 3.80 × 10  $  ^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  , respectively (L is the thickness of perovskite film, details in Table S4, Supporting Information). This result suggests that the device based on TiO  $  _{2}  $ :NaCl has a lessened defect state density, consistent with the TPV data.

Furthermore, we carried out electrical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) to characterize the effect of alkali metal salt doping on carrier transport and recombination in the device. Figure 3f is the Nyquist plots of devices with different ETL, and Figure S6a in the Supporting Information shows the equivalent circuit used for fitting. Compared to pristine W-TiO  $  _{2}  $ , the charge transfer resistance (  $  R_{tr}  $  ) of the device using TiO  $  _{2}  $ :NaCl is reduced while the charge recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) becomes larger, indicating that NaCl doping suppresses the interface recombination (Table S5, Supporting Information). Through the relationship between capacitance and frequency, the distribution of defect states with energy can be estimated,  $  ^{[13]}  $  as shown in Figure S6b in the Supporting Information. Evidently, the defect state density in the device based on TiO  $  _{2}  $ :NaCl is greatly reduced, which may be attributed to the effective contact passivation effect of the strong chemical bonding and electronic coupling brought by the chloride anion at the interface.  $  ^{[13,18,21]}  $

The   $  Na^{+}  $   signal curve has a rather high concentration in the   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer, and drops by more than two orders of magnitude on the ITO side. It is noteworthy that it still maintains a relatively high value of distribution to a certain depth in the perovskite layer.

Among all alkali metal chlorides, NaCl-doped devices demonstrated the best PCE, followed by KCl, CsCl, RbCl, and LiCl. The control device based on pristine W-TiO  $  _{2}  $  exhibited a PCE of 21.61% with a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 24.57 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , an open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.09 V, and a fill factor (FF) of 80.80%. The primary reason for the increase of  $  V_{oc}  $  is the elevation of CBM of TiO  $  _{2}  $  by the alkali metal salt doping, more compatible with the CBM of perovskite.  $  ^{[11]}  $  Note that the order of the open circuit voltage enhancement of the PSC device with AMCl-doped TiO  $  _{2}  $  is CsCl, KCl, and NaCl from low to high, matching with the order in which they raise the energy level of TiO  $  _{2}  $ . The reduced defect density and suppressed charge recombination demonstrated by EIS and TPV are contributed to the enhanced  $  V_{oc}  $  as well.  $  ^{[35,40]}  $

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, the newly developed water-based   $  TiO_{2}  $   doped with alkali chloride achieved the best PCE of 23.15% among planar PSCs based on   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL. Due to the good solubility for inorganic salt in water, direct doping alkali chloride to water-based low-temperature   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL is simple, straightforward, and

Alkali chloride doping plays a vital role in improving the device photovoltaic performance from three aspects: first, it promotes the conductivity and mobility of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer; second, it raises the energy level of   $  TiO_{2}  $   to make it more compatible with perovskite, reducing charge accumulation and enhancing the carrier extraction capacity, leading to an increase in open circuit voltage of the photovoltaic device; finally, the interfacial chloride anions passivate interface defects as well as reduce the density of trap states, and alkali metal cations may interact with iodide anions to inhibit ion movement, leading to reduced device hysteresis. Among a series of alkali chlorides, the PSCs based on NaCl-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   demonstrated the best PCE and the lowest hysteresis. Meanwhile, the devices using alkali chloride-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   also exhibit competitive storage stability and light stability. Since water is one of the most widely used solvents, water-based   $  TiO_{2}  $   provides a brand-new manufacturing method for superior ETL deposition with more general doping toward higher performance photovoltaics. Furthermore, the practical value of water-based   $  TiO_{2}  $   in PSCs may open up a promising avenue for large-scale industrial production more compatible with commercialization in the future.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.9%), chlorobenzene (CB, 99.8%), acetonitrile (ACN, 99.9%), lead iodide (PbI  $  _{2}  $  , beads, 99.999%), cesium chloride (CsCl, beads, 99.999%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%), isopropanol (IPA, 99.7%), and titanium chloride (TiCl  $  _{4}  $  , 99.99%) were got from Alfa Aesar. 4-tert-butylpyridine (4-tBP, 96%) was obtained from TCI. Formamidinium iodide (FAI, 99.5%), methylammonium chloride (MACI, 99.5%), methylammonium iodide (MAI, 99.5%), lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI), and 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,Np-dimethoxy-phenylamino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD, 99.5%) were bought from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology in China. Potassium Chloride (KCl) was got from China National Pharmaceutical Group Corporation. Lithium Chloride (LiCl) was bought from Energy Chemical. Rubidium chloride (RbCl) was got from MACKLIN. Sodium chloride (NaCl) and Benzyl alcohol (99%) were purchased from Xilong Scientific. [6,6]-Phenyl-C61-butyric Acid Methyl Ester (PC  $  _{61}  $  BM) was got from MREDA. ITO, glass cleaning liquid, cleaning rack, and metal mask were purchased from Advanced Election Technology Co., Ltd.

ETLs Fabrication:   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticles were prepared by sol–gel method and dispersed in deionized water with a concentration of 11.3 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  . The specific process was described in the previous report.  $  ^{[13]}  $   Alkali metal chloride doping was obtained by directly adding the corresponding solid powders to the aqueous solution of   $  TiO_{2}  $   (molar ratio: 10%). The   $  TiO_{2}  $   colloid precursor was spin-coated on the cleaned glass with ITO at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Then, the substrate was transferred onto a hot plate at 150 °C for 20 min.

Device Fabrication: After depositing the ETL, the perovskite layer was prepared by a two-step spin-coating method in   $  N_{2}  $   glove box. First, 1.5 M   $  PbI_{2}  $   in DMF:DMSO (9:1) was deposited on the ETL at 1500 rpm for 30 s, then annealed at 70 °C for 30 s; second, the FAI:MACI:MAI (90 mg: 9 mg:6 mg in 1 mL IPA) was spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 30 s, then the sample was annealed at 150 °C for 15 min in ambient air. The Spiro-OMeTAD solution (90 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in CB with the addition of 35 μL mL  $  ^{-1}  $   Li-TFSI/acetonitrile (260 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) and 30 μL mL  $  ^{-1}  $   4-tBP) was coated at 4000 rpm for 30 s. At last, Au electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation at a pressure of 8 × 10  $  ^{-5}  $   Torr.

Characterization: XPS was measured using a Thermo Fisher Scientific ESCALAB Xi+, and the X-ray source was (Al Kα, 1486.6 eV). Especially the C 1s peak position at 284.8 eV was used for calibration. The UPS measurement was conducted on Thermo Scientific NEXSA with a He I

light source (21.2 eV). The vacuum of the chamber was   $  5 \times 10^{-9}  $   mbar, and the bias voltage is -5 V. The top-view SEM images were got from scanning electron microscope of GeminiSEM 300 (ZEISS) and FEI 430. The AFM images were obtained from Bruker Dimension ICON. The ToF-SIMS was performed with a ToF-SIMS 5–100 system (Münster, Germany). The voltage used for sputtering was 0.5 V (Bi  $  ^{3+}  $  ). The collected cations were all monovalent, and the anions were also monovalent except for I  $  _{2}^{-}  $  . A physical science smartlab X-ray diffraction system with Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda = 1.5406  $   Å) was conducted to collect XRD patterns. The above characterization services were provided by Ceshigo (www.ceshigo.com). PL and TRPL spectra were carried on Edinburgh Instruments Ltd. FLS980 fluorescence spectrometer system, and the excitation light was incident from the direction of the perovskite film.

The J-V characterization was performed using an Agilent B2900 source measure unit under AM 1.5G irradiation (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) with a Xeon lamp solar simulator (Solar IV-150A, Zolix). The light intensity was calibrated with a Newport calibrated KG5-filtered Si cell. The J-V curves were measured with a scanning rate of voltage interval of 10 mV and delay time of 200 ms from 1.18 to -0.01 V (reverse) or from -0.01 to 1.18 V (forward). The devices were taken out for external quantum efficiency measurement by a solar cell QE/IPCE measurement system (Solar Cell Scan100, Zolix) calibrated by a certified silicon solar cell. No bias light was used and the scan step was 5 nm. The light stability test was measured under AM 1.5G white-LED solar simulator (VeraSol-2, AAA class, Newport) without UV part in glove box. The TPC and TPV spectra were measured using a homemade system from Prof. Qingbo Meng's group in Institute of Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences. The EIS was conducted on a potentiostat/galvanostat (SP-150, Bio-Logic) at different biases.
