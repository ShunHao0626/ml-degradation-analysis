# Abstract

Interface engineering resulting in good contact, enhanced transport capability, and matched energy levels is indispensable and critical for the development of high-performance planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, we report an excellent electron-transporting layer (ETL) that can simultaneously enhance the stability and efficiency of n-i-p planar PSCs. Large  $ \pi $ -conjugated graphdiyne (GD) was introduced into cross-linkable fullerene [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric styryl dendron ester (PCBSD) to improve the film orientation. Raman spectroscopy and 2D grazing incidence X-ray diffraction (GIXRD) measurements revealed that a strong  $ \pi-\pi $  stacking interaction occurred between GD and cross-linkable PCBSD (C-PCBSD), generating a face-on stacked composite film. The orientated C-PCBSD:GD films was favorable for the growth and crystallization of the subsequent perovskite films and provided the merits of superior electron mobility, efficient charge extraction and energy-level tailoring. In addition, the thermally

The finding opens a new route to deposit the fullerene films with ordered orientation by 2D materials with large   $  \pi  $  -conjugation, and thus to control the subsequent perovskite crystallization.

Organic-inorganic metal halide based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted considerable attention owing to their rapid efficiency rising to 22% in a very short time.  $  ^{1-6}  $   Methylammonium (MA) lead halide and formamidinium (FA) lead halide, are regarded as very excellent solar light harvesting materials because of their large absorption coefficient,  $  ^{7}  $   high charge carrier mobility,  $  ^{2,8}  $   long carrier diffusion lengths,  $  ^{9}  $   feasible solution processing  $  ^{10-12}  $   and low-cost precursor materials  $  ^{13-15}  $  . Despite tremendous progress in the cell efficiency, further efforts on improving the device performance including efficiency and stability of PSCs are necessary to accelerate their commercialization process. Recently, planar structured PSCs with n-i-p or p-i-n layouts have presented comparable power conversion efficiency (PCE) with initial mesoscopic structure based devices.  $  ^{16-28}  $   Most high-efficiency PSCs have been obtained from n-i-p structures, where titanium oxide (TiO  $  _{2}  $  ) and 2,2,7,7-tetrakis(N,N'di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobi-fluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) are used as the electron-transporting layer (ETL) and hole-transporting layer (HTL), respectively. TiO  $  _{2}  $   is an excellent electron-transporting material with good optoelectronic properties and a compatible conduction band with perovskites. However, in n-i-p structured PSCs, the ETL not only determines the charge transfer characteristics but also strongly affects the morphology and the crystallization of the perovskite films as an underlayer.  $  ^{29,30}  $   The selection of an organic underlayer for perovskite films is a good choice to have a beneficial influence on interface matching and electrical stabilization. Recently, many bilayer-structured ETLs consisting of metal oxides and organic materials, such as TiO  $  _{2}  $  /PCBM ([6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester)  $  ^{31}  $  , graphene/TiO  $  _{2}  $    $  ^{32}  $  , PEIE (polyethyleneimine ethoxylated)/Y-TiO  $  _{2}  $    $  ^{33}  $  , and SnO  $  _{2}  $  /C  $  _{60}  $    $  ^{34}  $  , have been utilized successfully in perovskite solar cells attaining superior device performances. At the same time, carbon materials  $  ^{35,36}  $  , such as single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWNTs)  $  ^{37,38}  $  , graphene  $  ^{39,40}  $  , and graphene oxide  $  ^{41}  $  , are emerging

dopants in perovskite solar cells due to their unique conductivity advantages. In addition, device instability caused by interface erosion from moisture water in PSCs is a considerable issue, which seriously hampers their practical application in ambient conditions. Therefore, developing a solvent-resistant organic ETL, which could simultaneously address the issues of charge extraction efficiency, perovskite crystallization and device stability, is an efficient route to achieve high-efficiency PSCs with long-term stability.

Carbon based materials have produced a significant improvement in a variety of photovoltaic devices.  $  ^{42}  $   Among them, [6,6]-Phenyl-C61-butyric styryl dendron ester (PCBSD) is a fullerene-based cross-linkable material with two styryl groups as thermal cross-linkers.  $  ^{43}  $   To take advantage of its solvent resistance, cross-linked PCBSD (C-PCBSD) has been utilized in polymer solar cells and PSCs with remarkable long-term stabilities.  $  ^{44-46}  $   Graphdiyne (GD), as a novel large   $  \pi  $  -conjugated carbon material, has a rigid 2D network, natural band gap, and uniformly distributed pores from its   $  sp^{2-}  $   and sp-hybridized carbons configuration.  $  ^{47-51}  $   Herein, we introduce a solution-processed PCBSD:GD hybrid ETL between the   $  TiO_{2}  $   and perovskite layers in PSCs. The motivation of this work is to develop an efficient ETL with good electron transporting properties, conductivity, stability and morphology for fabricating high-efficiency and long-term PSCs. After GD modification, the C-PCBSD film exhibited a face-on orientation, which is favorable for growth and crystallization of the subsequent perovskite films in addition to bearing merits of superior electron mobility, efficient charge extraction and energy-level tailoring. Consequently, a maximum PCE of 20.19% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.11 V, short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 23.30 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , and fill factor (FF) of 78% was achieving, owing to the obvious increases of   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF.

Figure 1a shows a schematic illustration of the face-on stacked and cross-linkable PCBSD films. The molecular structures of PCBSD and GD are also shown in Figure 1a. PCBSD contains a small dendron functionalized with two thermally cross-linkable styryl groups. The two arms of the dendron provide adequate flexibility for the styryl groups to react in the solid state. Graphdiyne (GD) powder was dispersed in chlorobenzene by magnetic stirring and ultrasonic dispersion repeatedly. After two days of magnetic stirring and ultrasonic dispersion, the GD suspension was quite stable without obvious precipitation. TEM Observations revealed that the dispersed GD is uniform with the particle sizes approximately 50-80 nm (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The PCBSD: GD composite films was formed by spin-coating a PCBSD:GD chlorobenzene solution onto FTO/TiO₂ after thermal annealing at 160 °C for 30 min. The occurrence of crosslinking of PCBSD was confirmed by Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) transmittance spectra of the C-PCBSD:GD films, where the vibrational stretching band of the vinyl groups at 1622 cm⁻¹ is almost vanished (Figure S2, Supporting Information). Particularly, the strong π–π interaction between the two highly π-conjugated GD and C-PCBSD in the C-PCBSD: GD composite films caused the C-PCBSD molecules to have an ordered stacking with a face-on orientation, which is favorable for electron transport and perovskite growth.

The fabricated n-i-p planar perovskite solar cell has a device configuration of FTO/TiO $ _2 $ /PCBSD:GD/perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD/Au, as shown in Figure 1b. # Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated an excellent composite film of GD modified C-PCBSD, which simultaneously enhanced the stability and efficiency of n-i-p planar PSCs. Face-on stacked C-PCBSD molecules were formed owing to the  $ \pi-\pi $  stacking interaction between GD and C-PCBSD. The C-PCBSD:GD composite film presented the merits of superior electron mobility, efficient charge extraction and energy-level tailoring. More importantly, the ordered orientation of the C-PCBSD film modified by GD was favorable for the growth and crystallization of the subsequent perovskite film. Furthermore, the robust and adhesive C-PCBSD film provided sufficient solvent resistance to avoid the interfacial erosion. As a result, the perovskite solar cells delivered a power conversion efficiency of  $ 20.19\% $  with an obviously improved cell stability. The method we developed opens a new route to deposit the fullerene films with ordered orientation by 2D materials with large  $ \pi $ -conjugation, and thus to control the subsequent perovskite crystallization.

# Device Preparation.

The FTO substrates with a sheet resistance of  $ \sim 15\Omega/\mathrm{sq} $  were thoroughly cleaned with acetone, ethanol, and deionized water for sequence in ultrasonic bath for  $ 15\mathrm{min} $ , respectively. And then the substrates were immersed into  $ 200~\mathrm{mL} $   $ \mathrm{TiCl_4} $  solution at  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 1\mathrm{h} $ . Subsequently, the treated substrates were washed with deionized water and dried at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  in air for  $ 1\mathrm{h} $ , and then subjected to an O3/ultraviolet treatment for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $ . The C-PCBSD solution was coated onto the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer at  $ 4000~\mathrm{rpm}/40~\mathrm{s} $ . The solution was then spin-coated onto the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  or C-PCBSD layer by a consecutive two-step spin-coating process at  $ 1000 $  and  $ 4000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 20 $  and  $ 40~\mathrm{s} $ . During the second spin-coating step, the  $ 2.5~\mathrm{cm} \times 2.5~\mathrm{cm} $  substrate was treated with  $ 150~\mu\mathrm{L} $  chlorobenzene drop-casting. Then the  $ \mathrm{CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $  substrate was heated at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 10\mathrm{min} $  in nitrogen glove box. After annealing, the spiro-OMeTAD solution was coated onto the perovskite layer at 5000

# Characterization.

J-V characteristics of perovskite solar cells under 1 sun illumination were obtained using a programmable Keithley 2400 source meter under Air Mass (AM 1.5G) solar irradiation at 100 mW/cm² (Newport, Class AAA solar simulator, 94023A-U). The incident-photon-to-current efficiency (IPCE) measurement was performed using a system combining a xenon lamp, a monochromator, a chopper and a lock-in amplifier together with a calibrated silicon photodetector. The field-emission scanning electron microscope (SEM) images were obtained from a Quanta 200 FEG. AFM images were obtained using a Veeco Multimode V instrument to evaluate the surface morphology of films in the tapping mode. The absorbance and transmittance spectra were obtained with an UV–vis spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer Lambda 750). The results of solvent invasion were measured using a contact angle instrument (Data Physics OCA). X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurement was conducted using PANalytical (Empyrean) equipment. The grazing incidence X-ray diffraction (GIXRD) measurements were performed at the BL14B1 beamline of the Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF) using X-ray with a wavelength of 1.24 Å. Two-dimensional (2D) GIXRD patterns were acquired by a Mar CCD mounted vertically at a distance ~223 mm from the sample with a grazing incidence angle of 0.2° and an exposure time of 20 sec. The 2D GIXRD patterns were analyzed using the FIT2D software and displayed in scattering vector q coordinates. Raman spectra were collected on an HR 800 Raman spectroscope (J Y, France) equipped with a synapse CCD detector and a confocal Olympus microscope, and a wavelength of 514.5 nm as the excitation source. # Highlights

- Graphdiyne-modified cross-linkable fullerene was developed as an ETL in perovskite solar cells.

- The composite film exhibited a face-on orientation, which is favorable for the growth and crystallization of the subsequent perovskite films.

- A perovskite solar cell with PCE of  $ 20.19\% $  is achieved with evidently improved cell stability.

- The developed method opens a new route to deposit the fullerene films with ordered orientation by 2D materials with large  $ \pi $ -conjugation.
