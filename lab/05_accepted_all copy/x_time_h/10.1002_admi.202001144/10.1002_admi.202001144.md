# Gold Nanoparticles Functionalized with Fullerene Derivative as an Effective Interface Layer for Improving the Efficiency and Stability of Planar Perovskite Solar Cells

Rohit D. Chavan, Daniel Prochowicz, Bartłomiej Bończak, Mohammad Mahdi Tavakoli, Pankaj Yadav, Marcin Fiałkowski, and Chang Kook Hong*

Titanium dioxide (TiO $ _2 $ ) is an extensively used electron transporting layer (ETL) in n–i–p perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Although, TiO $ _2 $  ETL experiences the high surface defect together with low electron extraction ability, which causes severe energy loss and poor stability in the PSC. In this study, a new intermediate layer consisting of gold nanoparticles functionalized with fully conjugated fullerene C $ _{60} $  derivative (C $ _{60} $ -BCT@Au NPs) that enhances the interfacial contact at ETL/perovskite interface leading to a perovskite film with improved crystallinity and morphology is reported. Moreover, the studies demonstrate that the interface modification of the TiO $ _2 $  ETL with C $ _{60} $ -BCT@Au NPs substantially improves the charge extraction efficiency from the perovskite layer and suppresses charge recombination processes. Consequently, the resulting device yields a champion efficiency of 19.08% as well as devaluation in hysteresis. In addition, the unencapsulated PSCs with c-TiO $ _2 $ /C $ _{60} $ -BCT@Au NPs ETL retain 83% and 90% of their original PCEs after 500 h storage in air and exposure to continuous UV illumination for 200 h, respectively. This study provides an effective method to address the electron transporting issues between perovskite and c-TiO $ _2 $  ETL for developing stable and efficient PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

various organic  $  ^{[24-28]}  $   or inorganic modification layers.  $  ^{[29-35]}  $   In particular, fullerenes and their derivatives appeared as excellent interfacial modification layers and trap state passivators for the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL, significantly enhancing the performance and stability of PSCs.  $  ^{[36-38]}  $   Recent studies revealed that coating the   $  TiO_{2}  $   with a thin layer of pristine   $  C_{60}  $  .  $  ^{[39-42]}  $   phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM)  $  ^{[43-45]}  $   or self-assembled monolayer (SAM) of fullerene derivatives  $  ^{[46-48]}  $   promotes the electron transfer and reduces the ion accumulation at the ETL/perovskite interface.

Moreover, an appropriated functionalization of the fullerene derivatives with various anchoring groups can improve the crystallization of the perovskite film on the ETL and substantially eliminate the hysteresis effect of PSCs.  $  ^{[49,50]}  $   For example, grafting of the fullerene cage with the pyridine moiety reinforces the electron transport property by effective passivation of the trap states within the perovskite film and reduction of the nonradiative electron–hole recombination at the ETL/perovskite interface.  $  ^{[51]}  $   Further development of novel functionalized fullerenes with improved conductivity and low resistance would promote the development of efficient and stable PSCs.

Functionalization of fullerene-based materials with gold nanoparticles (Au NPs) through both covalent and noncovalent interactions has been found to effect the optical and electron transfer characteristics owing to well-known surface plasmon resonance (SPR) property of Au NPs.  $  ^{[52-54]}  $   These composite nanomaterials have attracted extensive attention for applications in sensors,  $  ^{[55]}  $   molecular electronics,  $  ^{[56]}  $   fuel cell design  $  ^{[57]}  $   and optoelectronics.  $  ^{[58,59]}  $   Conjugation of fullerene sphere with other elements of a system removes tunneling barriers for electrons and facilitates charge separation and transfer.  $  ^{[60]}  $   Efforts aimed at improving the PCE of PSCs performance and stability using the Au NPs embedded in the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL films have been widely reported.  $  ^{[61-66]}  $   However, there have been no study assessing the impact of Au NPs functionalized with fullerene derivatives on the PSCs.

Herein, we demonstrate Au NPs functionalized with a fullerene   $  C_{60}  $   derivative (  $  C_{60}-BCT@AuNPs  $  ) as an effective interface layer between compact-  $  TiO_{2}  $   (c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) ETL and perovskite absorber layer for planar PSCs. This modification not only reduces the interfacial charge recombination but also significantly enhances the charge extraction from the perovskite layer to c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   layer. Thus, the champion planar PSCs shows a PCE of 19.08%, which is superior from that of the devices fabricated on the c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   (16.96%) and   $  TiO_{2}/PCBM  $   ETLs (17.52%). Moreover, the long-term stabilities of the   $  C_{60}-BCT@Au  $   NP-based device are improved under both ambient condition (25 °C, relative humidity ≈35%) and constant UV illumination.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The fullerene-based materials employed in this work to modify the surface of c-TiO $ _2 $  ETL were the commonly used PCBM and O-butyl 4-(azahomo (C $ _{60} $ -I $ _h $ ) fullerene)benzothioate

functionalized with AuNPs (C $  _{60}  $ -BCT@Au NPs) synthesized according to the recently publish procedure. $ ^{[67]} $  The molecular structures of PCBM and C $  _{60}  $ -BCT@Au NPs are shown in Scheme 1. Importantly, in the latter case, the fullerene derivative is covalently connected to the Au surface through fully conjugated linker and such a conjugated nanostructure exhibits extraordinary ability to accept and transfer electrons. $ ^{[67]} $  The Au NPs with ≈8 nm particle size assemble with the formation of a densely packed aggregate structure (Figure S1, Supporting Information). In this study, thin layers of PCBM and C $  _{60}  $ -BCT@Au NPs were spin coated onto c-TiO $  _{2}  $  ETL under the same optimized conditions to investigate how the selected fullerene derivatives affect the transport properties and performance of PSCs (for details of thin films' formation, see the Experimental Section). The thicknesses of the PCBM and C $  _{60}  $ -BCT@Au NPs layers are estimated to be 4.9 nm using a surface profilometer.

The presence of the fullerene derivatives on the c-TiO $ _2 $  ETL was confirmed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis. Figure S2 (Supporting Information) illustrate the Ti2p, O 1s and C 1s XPS spectra of the resulting films. It is found that the C/Ti component ratio increases after deposition of PCBM and C $ _{60} $ -BCT@Au NPs layers in comparison with the pristine c-TiO $ _2 $  film (for the detailed XPS elemental analysis see Tables S1–S3, Supporting Information). TiO $ _2 $  is known to contain at the surface many oxygen vacancies acting as charge carrier traps, which can contribute to J-V hysteresis behavior. $ ^{[68]} $  PCBM has been recently applied to effectively passivate the traps on TiO $ _2 $  surface. $ ^{[43-45]} $  To clarify the impact of the used fullerene derivatives on the chemical structure of TiO $ _2 $  surface, we carried out XPS analysis of O 1s peak. The O1s XPS spectra of the investigated films shown in Figure S2b (Supporting Information) were deconvoluted into three peaks centered at ≈530, 531 and 532 eV, which can be assigned to the O–Ti $ ^{4+} $  bond, the oxygen deficiency, and the hydroxyl groups on TiO $ _2 $ , respectively. $ ^{[28]} $  The relative area of the oxygen deficiency was found to be similarly reduced in c-TiO $ _2 $ /PCBM and c-TiO $ _2 $ /C $ _{60} $ -BCT@Au NPs films in compared to pristine c-TiO $ _2 $  sample (Table S4, Supporting Information). The passivation of the oxygen deficiency on TiO $ _2 $  surface could results in reduced charge recombination at ETL/perovskite and hysteresis behavior in these devices. Moreover, the XPS measurement was made to confirm the presence of Au within the c-TiO $ _2 $ /C $ _{60} $ -BCT@Au NPs layer (Figure S2d, Supporting Information). The effect of fullerene-based interlayer on the optical and electrical properties of c-TiO $ _2 $  film was further examined by UV–vis spectroscopy and Hall effect measurements. As shown in Figure S6 (Supporting Information), the functionalization of c-TiO $ _2 $  ETL with PCBM and C $ _{60} $ -BCT@Au NPs layers has a negligible effect on the optical property in the visible wavelength range. The lack of visible typical SPR absorbance peak (around 500–600 nm) in C $ _{60} $ -BCT@Au NPs film could be attributed to the agglomeration and low concentration of Au NPs. The conductivity measured from four-point probe method is found to be highest in case of c-TiO $ _2 $ /C $ _{60} $ -BCT@Au NPs film (Table S5 in the Supporting Information and the Experimental Section). The high conductivity of the c-TiO $ _2 $ /C $ _{60} $ -BCT@Au NPs ETL could reduce the interfacial charge accumulation and series resistance of the device, leading to higher FF.

To investigate the impact of the fullerene-based interlayers on the device performance, we fabricated PSCs with an architecture of fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/ETL/perovskite/poly(triarylamine) (PTAA)/Au, where the ETL is c-  $  TiO_{2}  $ , c-  $  TiO_{2}  $ /PCBM, and c-  $  TiO_{2}  $ /C $  _{60}  $ -BCT@Au NPs. We used triple-A site cation perovskite with formulation of Cs $  _{5}  $ (MA $  _{0.17}  $ FA $  _{0.83}  $ ) $  _{95}  $ Pb(I $  _{0.83}  $ Br $  _{0.17}  $ ) $  _{3}  $  as an absorber layer. $ ^{[70]} $  The detailed device fabrication process is illustrated in the Experimental Section. As shown in Figure S7 (Supporting Information), the designed PSCs illustrated that the perovskite film on different ETLs display analogous morphology and thickness of 400 nm. Figure 1a illustrate the typical current density–voltage ( $  J-V  $ ) curves of the optimized PSCs with c-  $  TiO_{2}  $ , c-  $  TiO_{2}  $ /PCBM, and c-  $  TiO_{2}  $ /C $  _{60}  $ -BCT@Au NPs as ETLs, measured under air mass 1.5 global (AM 1.5 G) conditions at reverse scan. As shown in Figure S8 and Table 1, these devices exhibit lower PV performance than the C $ _{60} $ -BCT@Au NPs based PSCs, indicating that the PCE improvement is solely related to the Au NPs (for the  $ J-V $  curve and EQE spectrum of the best performing C $ _{60} $ -BTC ETL based device shown in Figure S10, Supporting Information).

We also studied the hysteresis effect of the investigated devices at different scans and found that the hysteresis index (HI) is obviously reduced in   $  TiO_{2}/C_{60}  $  -BCT@Au NPs devices (Figures S11–S13 and Tables S6–S8, Supporting Information). The suppression of the hysteresis effect in   $  C_{60}  $  -BCT@Au NPs functionalized device could be ascribed to the better electron transport driven by the suppressed recombination at the ETL/perovskite interface. To explore the recombination dynamics in the investigated PSCs, we measured the light intensity dependence of   $  V_{oc}  $   using a linear expression of   $  V_{oc} = n(k_{\mathrm{B}}T/q)\ln(I) + \text{constant}  $  , where n,   $  k_{B}  $  , q, T, and I are the ideality factor, the Boltzmann constant, the charge, the absolute temperature, and the light intensity, respectively. The smaller slope of   $  k_{B}T/q  $   for the   $  C_{60}  $  -BCT@Au NP based device (n = 1.3) indicates the reduction of trap-assisted charge recombination process at ETL/perovskite interface in compared to PCBM (n = 1.6) and c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   (n = 1.8) based devices (Figure 1c).

The representative SEM and AFM images of the perovskite films deposited on the c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  , c-  $  TiO_{2}/PCBM  $  , and c-  $  TiO_{2}/C_{60}\cdot BCT@Au  $   NP ETLs are shown in Figure 2a and Figure S14 (Supporting Information), respectively. It is found that the perovskite film deposited on

$  C_{60}  $  -BCT@Au NPs ETL exhibits larger average grain size and smoother surface than that of deposited on c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   and c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /PCBM films. Recent studies demonstrated that the perovskite with increased crystal size shows a much lower defect recombination centers, which is beneficial for the improved device performance of PSCs.  $  ^{[18,61-63]}  $   The better quality of the perovskite layer coated on c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /  $  C_{60}  $  -BCT@Au NPs ETL is also confirmed by the X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements. As seen in Figure 2b, all samples display similar XRD patterns with main diffraction peaks centered at 14.06, 28.1 and 31.5 degrees indicating the formation of a perovskite crystal structure. However, the diffraction peaks for the perovskite film deposited on c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /  $  C_{60}  $  -BCT@Au NPs ETL exhibit higher intensity than those of the c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   and c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /PCBM films, indicating its improved crystallinity. The optical absorption spectra of the perovskite film coated on the corresponding ETLs can be found in Figure S15 (Supporting Information). As seen, there is no obvious change in the absorption onset of c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   and fullerene-based samples, indicating that the higher   $  J_{sc}  $   in the case of the c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /  $  C_{60}  $  -BCT@Au NPs device is not supported by a larger absorption enhancement and some other effect is responsible for enhanced EQE. Thus, the increase in   $  J_{sc}  $   could be due to enhanced charge transport and collection (vide infra).  $  ^{[61]}  $

This could be correlated with the lower  $  V_{oc}  $  and huge hysteresis index in the devices with pure c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ETL.

Evaluation of the device stability in ambient condition and under light is crucial for commercialization purpose.  $  ^{[75]}  $   Finally, we study the role of fullerene-based interlayers on the long-term stability of the unencapsulated devices for 500 h (Figure 3a). The cell based on c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL exhibits a significant PCE decrement to   $  \approx60\%  $   of its initial PCE, whereas PCBM and the   $  C_{60}  $  -BCT@Au NPs modified devices maintain 72% and 83% of their original PCEs after 500 h storage in air. In addition, we studied the stability of resulting PSCs under continuous UV light irradiation. The c-  $  TiO_{2}/C_{60}  $  -BCT@Au NPs based device retains 90% of its initial PCE after exposure to UV light continuously for 200 h, which is more stable than the c-  $  TiO_{2}/PCBM  $   and c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   based PSCs, retaining only 78% and 70% of their initial PCE values, respectively (Figure 3b). # 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, we have improved both stability and efficiency of c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  -based planar n-i-p PSCs by applying Au NPs functionalized with fully conjugated fullerene   $  C_{60}  $   derivative as a novel interlayer between c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   and perovskite layers. The modified c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   layer improves the charge conductivity and interfacial energy level alignment, which facilitate the electron transfer at the ETL/perovskite interface and reduces the energy loss at the contact. Moreover,   $  C_{60}  $  -BCT@Au NPs layer improves the interfacial contact at the ETL/perovskite interface and the morphology of the perovskite film. Together with the improved perovskite film quality, the electron extraction and transfer were greatly enhanced from the   $  C_{60}  $  -BCT@Au NPs layer at the ETL/perovskite interface. Consequently, the proposed interface engineering improves all PV parameters leading to the devices with best PCE of 19.08% and negligible hysteresis. In addition, the long-term stability data imply that the applied interface engineering is an interesting strategy to avoid the fast device degradation of   $  TiO_{2}  $   based PSCs.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: FTO (8   $  \Omega  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $  ), chlorobenzene (95%), anhydrous ethanol (99.9%), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.9%), anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%), acetonitrile (99.8%), anhydrous toluene (99.9%), titanium(IV) isopropoxide (99.9%), hydrochloric acid (HCI, AR, 35–37%), 4-tertbutylpyridine (TBP, 96%), lithium bis-(trifluoromethylsulphonyl) imide (Li-TFSI, 95%), lead iodide (PbI  $  _{2}  $  , 99.9%), lead bromide (PbBr  $  _{2}  $  ), cesium iodide (CsI, 99.9%), PCBM (99.9%), and PTAA were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Formamidinium iodide (FAI-99.9%) and methylammonium bromide (MABr-99.9%) were purchased from Great-Cell. C  $  _{60}  $  -BCT@Au NPs was synthesized according to the recently published procedure.  $  ^{[67]}  $

Device Fabrication: Patterned FTO substrates were sequentially washed by ultrasonic cleaner using soap solution, deionized water, acetone, and isopropyl alcohol, followed by the treatment of UV light for 15 min. The c-TiO $ _2 $  precursor solution was spin-coated on FTO substrate at 6000 rpm for 30 s as previously reported in the literature and subsequently annealed on a hot plate at 450 °C for 30 min. $ ^{[76]} $  PCBM (10 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  chlorobenzene) and C $ _{60} $ -BCT@Au NPs (10 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  toluene) solutions were deposited on c-TiO $ _2 $  by spin-coating at 2000 rpm for 30 s and then sintered on a hotplate at 100 °C for 5 min. The one-step and “antisolvent” methods were utilized to obtain triple-A cation perovskite films. The perovskite solution was prepared by dissolving PbBr $ _2 $  (0.2 M), PbI $ _2 $  (1.1 M), MABr (0.22 M), FAI (1 M), and CsI (0.05 M) in 1 mL solvent mixture {DMF:DMSO = 8:2 (volume ratio)}. Next, the solution was spin-coated at 1000 for 10 s and 6000 rpm for 30 s. During spin-coating, chlorobenzene (100 μL) as an antisolvent was immediately added to the substrate and film was annealed at 110 °C for 90 min. To prepare PTAA solution, 15 mg of PTAA was dissolved in in 1.5 mL of toluene, followed by adding 15 μL of Li-TFSI solution (170 mg dissolved in 1 mL acetonitrile) and 7.5 μL TBP. $ ^{[77]} $  Finally, 80 nm of Au top electrode was thermally evaporated onto the PTAA.

Thin Film Characterization and Device Measurements: XRD pattern data for 2θ values were collected with Rigaku, Japan equipped with a Cu k line of 1.5410 Å. The field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM; S-4700, Hitachi) coupled with X-ray energy-dispersive spectrometer equipped with an EDAX detector. The AFM (XE-100 Advanced Scanning Probe Microscope, Park System) was conducted to the verify the RMS roughness of films. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (MultiLab2000) was utilized to measure elements and percentage compositions. The transmittance or absorbance of the films was analyzed by UV-visible measurement (Cary 300 UV-Vis). The resistivity, conductivity, and charge carrier density of the ETLs were investigated using Hall measurements (Ecopia HMS-3000 Hall Measurement system). The Hall coefficient was measured for all thin films having an optimized thickness at a temperature of 300 K (glass substrate size = 1 cm × 1 cm). The PL was measured using Acton Research Co., USA and TRPL spectroscopy was measured using MicroTime-200, Picoquant, Germany. The UPS (Model Name: Axis-Supra) was utilized to measure the band alignment of the films. The active area of shadow mask was fixed at 0.09 cm $  ^{2}  $ . A calibrated solar simulator (AM 1.5G) adjusted to an intensity of 100 mW cm $  ^{-2}  $  was used to analyze device performance. A DK240 monochromator was used to measure the EQE spectra.
