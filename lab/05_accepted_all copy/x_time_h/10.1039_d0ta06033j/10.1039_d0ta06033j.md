# Synergistic engineering of bromine and ctyltrimethylammonium chloride molecule enabling efficient and stable flexible perovskite solar cells

Hao Mei,  $  ^{†a,b}  $   Yuliang Wu,  $  ^{†c}  $   Changlei Wang,  $  ^{*a,b}  $   Shengqiang Ren,  $  ^{d}  $   Mingdi Zhang,  $  ^{e}  $   Haitao Dai,  $  ^{e}  $   Dewei Zhao,  $  ^{d}  $   Zhengying Li,  $  ^{c}  $   Qingguo Du,  $  ^{*c}  $   and Xiaofeng Li,  $  ^{*a,b}  $

Flexible perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) hold promising applications in terrestrial power plant systems irrespective to the curved surfaces, but still suffer from low efficiencies and poor stabilities. Here, we report on the synergetic strategies of combining bromine (Br) and cetyltrimethylammonium chloride (CTAC) additives to passivate both bulk and surface defects, leading to improved performance and stability of flexible PVSCs. Incorporation of Br suppresses the formation of halide vacancies in the perovskite bulk, while CTAC further modifies the film surface with reduced defects and enhanced hydrophobicity features. As a result, flexible PVSCs with efficiencies over  $ 18\% $  are achieved with high reproducibility, compared to the control devices with efficiencies about  $ 17\% $ . Our flexible PVSCs present excellent ambient stability against moisture, keeping over  $ 90\% $  of the initial efficiency after exposure to  $ 90\% $  humidity for about 170 hours. Our work offers a facile and universal strategy for the fabrication of high performance and stable flexible PVSCs.

# Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials have gained tremendous progress in the past decade due to their unique optoelectronic properties.  $  ^{1, 2}  $   Accordingly, single-junction perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) have rapidly obtained the highest certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.2%.  $  ^{3}  $   Low-temperature processibility of perovskites enables the fabrication of flexible PVSCs with high PCEs over 21%.  $  ^{4}  $   Since flexible PVSCs have promising applications in terrestrial power plants, buildings, and portable electronics due to the light weight and low cost, further enhancement of the performance and stability of flexible PVSC is highly desired.  $  ^{5-7}  $   Engineering the compositional elements of perovskite materials is an efficient route to enhance the performance of PVSCs, e.g., B-site metal elements and X-site halides in the ABX  $  _{3}  $   structure.  $  ^{8-11}  $   Theoretical calculation implies that substituting iodine (I) with bromine (Br) or chlorine (Cl) in perovskites could reduce the

defect density due to the elimination of I vacancies.  $  ^{12}  $   However, the high concentration of Br and Cl in pure lead-based perovskite materials increases the optical bandgap, away from the ideal bandgap (i.e., 1.34 eV) for single-junction solar cells, leading to low photocurrent and efficiency.  $  ^{13, 14}  $   Moreover, phase segregation is also detrimental for improving the performance of PVSCs if over 30% Br is used.  $  ^{15, 16}  $   Thus, the trade-off between the reduction of iodine vacancies and the increase in bandgap can lead to the best mixed halides PVSCs. Recently, McGehee and coworkers reported that triple halide (Cl, Br, and I) wide-bandgap perovskite light absorber has the excellent optoelectronic properties with less defects and suppressed phase segregation, enabling the high-performance single-junction and tandem PVSCs.  $  ^{17}  $

Instability of perovskites hinders their commercialization.  $  ^{18}  $   The high energy photons, high temperatures, super-oxygen, water molecules, electrical bias, and so on cause the degradation of perovskite materials in ambient.  $  ^{19-21}  $   Numerous approaches have been proposed to improve the perovskite film and device stability, for example, proper encapsulating,  $  ^{21}  $   the surface/grain boundaries passivation and the low-dimensional perovskite doping.  $  ^{22, 23}  $   Among them, the passivation via a hydrophobic polymer is considered as an effective route. We have reported that the amphiphilic block-copolymer F127 simultaneously enhances the performance and stability of planar PVSCs.  $  ^{24}  $   It is anticipated to find out an alternative molecule for highly efficient flexible PVSCs.  $  ^{25}  $   The cetyltrimethylammonium chloride (CTAC) with amphiphilic properties is a good candidate for the performance enhancement of PVSCs, that CTAC could be introduced on to the perovskite surface via a mediator-antisolvent strategy.  $  ^{26}  $   The long chain CTA  $  ^{+}  $   cation would be anchor on the surface

# Conclusions

We have demonstrated efficient and stable flexible PVSCs through the incorporation of Br and CTAC molecules. Br could reduce charge trap defects caused by halide vacancies, suppressing carrier recombination, and improving the flexible device performance. CTAC is a bi-functional molecule that both modulates the film through surface passivation and improves the device stability due to the more hydrophobic feature. Thus, we obtain flexible over 18%-efficiency PVSCs with high reproducibility and good stability by keeping over 90% of the initial efficiency for 168 hours' exposure to 90% high humidity.
