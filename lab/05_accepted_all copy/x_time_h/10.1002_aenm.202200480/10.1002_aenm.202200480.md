# Simultaneously Suppressing Charge Recombination and Decomposition of Perovskite Solar Cells by Conjugated Covalent Organic Frameworks

Riming Nie, Weicun Chu, Zhongping Li,  $  ^{*}  $   He Li, Shanshan Chen, Yaqing Chen, Zhuhua Zhang, Xiaoming Liu, Wanlin Guo,  $  ^{*}  $   and Sang Il Seok  $  ^{*}  $

# 1. Introduction

The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has rapidly increased over the past 10 years. However, along with further efficiency improvements, it is necessary to improve the long-term stability of perovskite materials, which limits the commercialization of PSCs. Therefore, it is urgent to find ways to simultaneously suppress charge recombination and degradation of perovskite materials. Here, two covalent organic frameworks (COFs) are synthesized by reacting thieno[3,2-b]thiophene-2,5-dicarbaldehyde (TTDA) with 1,3,5-tris(4-aminophenyl)benzene (TAPB) or 2,4,6-tris(4-aminophenyl)-1,3,5-triazine (TTA). The addition of these two COFs to the perovskite layer allows for more efficient charge separation through spatially separated frontier orbitals, and can also inhibit the degradation of the  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  layer and the formation of  $ \delta $ - $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $ . The PSCs with TTDA-TTA-COF exhibit higher efficiency and open-circuit voltage than those with TTDA-TAPB-COF. This is attributed to the better crystallization of perovskites induced by stronger well-conjugated properties and  $ \pi-\pi $  interactions in TTDA-TTA-COF. The champion PSC with TTDA-TTA-COF exhibits a PCE of  $ 23.35\% $  and excellent long-term stability. To the best of one's knowledge, this is the highest efficiency among PSCs fabricated using crystalline organic frameworks as additives. This work provides a new route to fabricate efficient and stable PSCs by incorporating proper COFs.

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have become one of the hottest topics in renewable energy research due to their remarkable progress in power conversion efficiency (PCE).  $  ^{[1-10]}  $   The rapidly increasing PCE can be attributed to its high absorption coefficient, tunable bandgap, high carrier mobility, long carrier diffusion lengths, and low exciton binding energy. Although the PCE of the PSC reached close to 26%, it is still well below the theoretical Shockley–Queisser limit efficiency (30.5%).  $  ^{[11]}  $   As is well known, PCE is determined by short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ), fill factor (FF), and open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ). Today,   $  J_{SC}  $   and FF of PSC are approaching theoretical limits, but   $  V_{OC}  $   still has room to increase, which is a key point to further improve efficiency by suppressing charge recombination. In addition, the commercialization of PSCs has been delayed due to the inherent poor long-term stability caused by the weak chemical stability of perovskite materials. Many strategies have been used as methods to inhibit charge recombination and increase a long-term stability of PSCs. Seo et al. reported that the deposition of n-hexyl trimethyl ammonium bromide between the perovskite surface and the hole-transporting layer (HTL) passivated the defects on the perovskite surface and promoted charge transfer, resulting in a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 22.7%  $  ^{[9]}  $  and PCE of 25.2% was achieved with the optimized electron transport layer.  $  ^{[12]}  $  We recently reported a certified PCE of 25.5% by combining Cl-bonded  $  SnO_{2}  $  with Cl-containing perovskite precursors to reduce interfacial defects between perovskite and electron-transporting layer (ETL) through the formation of a coherent interlayer.  $  ^{[10]}  $  Kim et al. suppressed anion-vacancy defects located at grain boundaries and at the surface of the perovskite films and augmented the crystallinity of the films via an anion engineering concept achieved by using the pseudohalide anion formate (HCOO  $  ^{-}  $ ). The resulting solar cell devices obtained a certified PCE of 25.2% with long-term operational stability (450 h).  $  ^{[13]}  $  Snaith et al. incorporated ionic liquids into the perovskite film and thence into positive-intrinsic-negative photovoltaic devices, which increased the device efficiency and the long-term device stability. Under continuous simulated sunlight over 1800 h at 70 to 75 °C, the encapsulated solar cells retained about 95% of the initial PCE.  $  ^{[14]}  $  Two fluorinated isomeric analogues of 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) were synthesized for HTL in PSCs. The PSCs fabricated with these HTLs showed a high efficiency of 24.8% and retained 87% of the initial PCE even after storage for 500 h in humid conditions without encapsulation.  $  ^{[15]}  $  Although much work has been attempted to improve PCE and stability, new avenues should be developed to accelerate the commercialization of PSCs.

Covalent organic frameworks (COFs) are a new class of crystalline porous polymers with high-order   $  \pi  $   structures, periodic skeletons, and discrete pores.  $  ^{[16-20]}  $   COFs are constructed with various building blocks through pre-designed strategies to achieve multi-functionality such as gas uptake, catalysts, chemical sensors, and energy fields.  $  ^{[21-35]}  $   Therefore, the visible light responsibility and tunable bandgap are easily fused into the framework of the COF.  $  ^{[22]}  $   Furthermore, the high   $  \pi  $   conjugation provides an efficient way to facilitate charge separation and transport.  $  ^{[36,37]}  $   In addition, COFs can also act as a framework to prevent the decomposition of perovskite materials and enhance the stability of perovskite materials. So far, only a few COFs have been used as doping materials for PSCs. Zhao et al.  $  ^{[38]}  $   synthesized conjugated 3D covalent organic frameworks (3D COFs) based on spirobifluorene cores linked via imine bonds (SP-3D-COFs) with novel interlacing conjugation systems. The SP-3D-COFs were incorporated into the PSCs, and the cells with SP-3D-COF 1 and SP-3D-COF 2 increased by 15.9% and 18.0% as compared to the reference undoped PSC with excellent leakage prevention. Zhang et al.  $  ^{[39]}  $   incorporated the   $  (\mathrm{Me}_{2}\mathrm{NH}_{2})^{+}  $  -encapsulated indium-based anionic metal-organic framework (FJU-17) as a “capsule” into HTL to construct a dual-functional layer HTL-FJU-17. Incorporation of FJU-17 improved hole mobility by passivating organic cation vacancies and stabilizing positively charged oxidized HTL. Thus, the PCE

increased from 18.32% to 20.34%, maintaining 90% of the initial PCE after 1000 h at ambient conditions. Kuo et al.  $  ^{[40]}  $  synthesized two types of highly crystalline 2D COFs and applied them to the fabrication of PSCs. These two COFs were revealed to aid in the crystal growth of the perovskite film and to facilitate the charge transfer through the associated interfaces. Adding 2D COF to the perovskite thin film increased the PCE from 17.40% to 19.80%. Nevertheless, the fabrication of PSCs with good stability and high PCE is still a difficult problem to solve.

In this study, we synthesized two COFs, TTDA-TAPB-COF and TTDA-TTA-COF by reacting thieno[3,2-b]thiophene-2,5-dicarbaldehyde (TTDA) with 1,3,5-tris(4-aminophenyl)benzene (TAPB) or 2,4,6-tris(4-aminophenyl)-1,3,5-triazine (TTA). The COFs were incorporated into the perovskite layer, and the corresponding PSCs were fabricated. Time-resolved photoluminescence spectroscopy (TRPL) and grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) technique were used to characterize carrier lifetime and crystallographic orientation, respectively. The energy levels of traps in PSCs were analyzed by using temperature dependent current-voltage curves. The solar cells incorporating TTDA-TTA-COF exhibited the highest PCE of  $ 23.35\% $  with excellent humidity and thermal stability.

# 2. Results and Discussion

As shown in Scheme 1, the TTDA-TAPB-COF and TTDA-TTA-COF were designed and synthesized by the Schiff base reaction. TTDA-TAPB-COF and TTDA-TTA-COF were synthesized from thieno[3,2-bjthiophene-2,5-dicarbaldehyde (TTDA), 1,3,5-tris(4-aminophenyl)benzene (TAPB) or 2,4,6-tris(4-aminophenyl)-1,3,5-triazine (TTA) in a mixture solvents of o-dichlorobenzene, 1-butanol and acetic acid (HOAc, 6 M) as catalyst at 120 °C for 72 h. After treatment, the high yields of TTDA-TAPB-COF and TTDA-TTA-COF are 89% and 88%, respectively. The final structure was first investigated by Fourier transform infrared (FT IR) spectroscopy (Figure S1, Supporting Information). TTDA-TAPB-COF and TTDA-TTA-COF showed strong new peaks at 1571 and 1567 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , which were assigned to imine linkage. Comparing the monomers including TTDA, TAPB and TTA, the obvious red shift was observed in both COFs via the solid-state electronic absorption spectra (Figure S2, Supporting Information). Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) indicated that TTDA-TAPB-COF and TTDA-TTA-COF did not show significant decomposition before 450 °C under nitrogen atmosphere (Figure S4, Supporting Information). Elemental analysis of the TTDA-TAPB-COF and TTDA-TTA-COF showed that the observed values of C, H, N, and S contents were close to the theoretical values (Table S1, Supporting Information).

To understand charge recombination kinetics, we measured incident light intensity (I)-dependent   $  J_{SC}  $  . Bimolecular recombination can be reflected by a log-log plot of   $  J_{SC}  $   as a function of I, and the relationship is given by the equation  $  ^{[45,46]}  $

$$
J _ {S C} \propto I ^ {\alpha}
$$

(2)

Under short-circuit conditions, when  $ \alpha $  is equal to 1 (the ideal condition), the bimolecular recombination of free carriers is negligible. As shown in Figure 3i, the addition of TTDA-TAPB-COF and TTDA-TTA-COF increased the  $ \alpha $  value, indicating the reduced loss of bimolecular recombination by adding COF.

The temperature-dependent current–voltage curves of PSCs without and with TTDA-TAPB-COF and TTDA-TTA-COF were measured to analyze defect states that directly influenced the charge transfer and recombination (Figure S14a–c, Supporting Information). From these current–voltage curves, the average activation energy of trapped electrons can be calculated using the Richardson–Dushman equation  $  ^{[47,48]}  $

$$
J \propto e ^ {- \Delta E / k T}
$$

(3)

where   $  \Delta E  $   is the electron activation energy. K is Boltzmann constant. T is the absolute temperature.

The dependence of the dark currents on temperature for PSCs without and with TTDA-TAPB-COF and TTDA-TTA-COF is shown in Figure S14d–f, Supporting Information. With the addition of COF, the activation energy of the traps decreased from 0.331 eV (Ref) to 0.252 eV (TTDA-TAPB-COF) and 0.295 eV (TTDA-TTA-COF), indicating that defect states shifted to shallower levels. According to previous report, the traps in the perovskite layer are mainly hole-like traps. The incorporation of TTDA-TAPB-COF and TTDA-TTA-COF shallows the energy levels of the traps, bringing these trap closer to the valence band. Therefore, holes trapped in defects have more opportunities to transfer to the valence band and to the anode. These results can be considered to be related to the inhibition of the degradation of the FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  layer and the phase transition  $  \alpha  $ -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  to  $  \delta  $ -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  through the incorporation of COF.

To explore the relationship between device performance and crystal structure, we performed grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) measurements. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, TTDA-TAPB-COF and TTDA-TTA-COF were synthesized and incorporated into perovskite layers. The incorporation of COFs prevented charge recombination by improving the crystallization and reducing the defect density, and suppressed the decomposition of the FAPbI $ _3 $  layer and the phase transition to  $ \delta $ -FAPbI $ _3 $ . PSCs with TTDA-TTA-COF have shown higher PCE and open-circuit voltage than PSCs with TTDA-TAPB-COF due to the better crystallization of perovskites induced by stronger well-conjugated properties and  $ \pi-\pi $  interactions in TTDA-TTA-COF. The champion PSC with adding TTDA-TTA-COF exhibited the highest PCE of 23.35% with excellent humidity and thermal stability. This work paves the way for the fabrication of efficient and stable PSCs using covalent organic frameworks.
