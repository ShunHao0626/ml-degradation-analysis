# Abstract

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have remarkable photovoltaic performance with the power conversion efficiency (PCE) over 22%, but they endure instability in moist environment and high temperature. Compared to the 3D perovskites, two-dimensional (2D) layered perovskites display excellent environmental stability but relatively poor photovoltaic performance. Here, we combined with 2D/3D perovskites and simultaneously introduced cesium cation (Cs  $  ^{+}  $  ) to fabricate benzylammonium-caesium-formamidinium mixed-dimensional (MD) perovskites (BE/FA/Cs MD perovskites) solar cells. The BE/FA/Cs MD perovskite device with an optimal benzylammonium content exhibits a PCE

as high as 19.24%. The improved PCE of 19.24% (BE/FA/Cs MD x=0.05) is attributed to great crystal orientation, outstanding surface quality, superior optical properties and enhanced charge transfer. More importantly, the BE/FA/Cs MD perovskite devices display superior humidity and heat stability. When subjected to 50% relative humidity (RH) for 1600 h and 85 °C for 240 h in the dark, the BE/FA/Cs MD (x=0.05) devices without encapsulation sustain 85% and 83% of their initial PCE, respectively. The results provide us an important method to gain highly efficient MD PSCs with long-term stability as a next-generation photovoltaic energy source.

# Introduction

Recently, rapidly increasing power conversion efficiency (PCE) has made organic–inorganic metal halide perovskites become most promising photoelectric materials due to the excellent properties, $ ^{1-3} $  including suitable band gap, $ ^{4} $  prominent charge diffusion lengths, $ ^{5} $  low preparation costs, $ ^{6} $  low-temperature processability $ ^{7} $  and high absorption coefficient. $ ^{8} $  At present, tremendous efforts have propelled the small-laboratory-based cell PCE of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) to reach a certified 22.7%. $ ^{9} $

Despite PSCs have high PCE, these perovskite materials undergo intrinsic instability upon exposure to moisture, light, oxygen and heat which has impeded their large-scale commercial application.  $  ^{10-16}  $   Especially, the perovskites based on the methylammonium (MA) cation displays poor humidity stability due to the pronounced hydroscopic nature and the low formation energy of

Page 3 of 20

Journal of Materials Chemistry A

MAPbI $ _3 $ .  $ ^{17,18} $  The MA-based PSCs also suffer from a fast degradation upon exposure to high temperature which is ascribed to the volatilizing of MA cation from the film during heating.  $ ^{19,20} $  Formamidinium (FA)-based perovskites which have been previously reported exhibit higher intrinsic heat stability than MA-based perovskites and can gain more highly efficient PSCs with high humidity, heat and structure stability by the introducing cesium (Cs) cation.  $ ^{21-24} $

To enhance the stability of PSCs, various efforts have been triggered,  $  ^{25-30}  $  except for developing stable cesium doping FA-based perovskite materials, two-dimensional (2D) and mixed-dimensional (MD) perovskites are the most concerned due to their more tunable optoelectronic properties and higher long-term stability.  $  ^{31-36}  $  However, these 2D and MD PSCs undergo poor PCE due to their big exciton binding energy, poor charge transport and low absorption coefficient.  $  ^{32}  $  Until 2016, Tsai et al. reported out-of-plane orientated 2D Ruddlesden–Popper PSCs with high PCE of 12.5% and high stability by hot-casting technique.  $  ^{37}  $  Subsequently, via cesium doping, Zhang et al. acquired 2D (BA)  $  _{2}  $  (MA)  $  _{3}  $  Pb  $  _{4}  $  I  $  _{13}  $  perovskite devices with a 13.7% PCE and enhanced heat and moisture stability.  $  ^{38}  $  More recently, Wang et al. found that 2D–3D heterostructured butylammonium-cesium-formamidinium lead halide PSCs exhibit the highest PCE over 18% among the reported 2D perovskite devices, and outstanding moisture resistance.  $  ^{39}  $  Although the photovoltaic performance and humidity stability of 2D and MD PSCs are significantly improved, obtaining higher PCE and higher stability against humidity, light and heat are still an urgent need.

In this study, we introduced benzylammonium into the caesium-formamidinium lead halide perovskite to fabricate MD perovskite   $  [(BE_{2}PbI_{4})_{x}(FA_{0.9}Cs_{0.1}PbI_{3})_{1-x}]_{0.85}(FAPbBr_{3})_{0.15}  $   (Simplified as BE/FA/Cs MD perovskites) solar cells. We explored the impact of different benzylammonium contents (x=0.03, 0.05, 0.10) on the properties of BE/FA/Cs MD perovskites. The results show that

the crystallinity, surface morphology, optical properties and charge transfer of the BE/FA/Cs MD perovskites all remarkably improved. When x=0.05, the high-performance MD PSCs are obtained which have an optimal PCE as high as 19.24%. Additionally, we observed that the BE/FA/Cs MD perovskite devices greatly improved superior humidity and heat stability in contrast to the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskite devices. # Results and Discussion

We tuned the content of BE $ _2 $ PbI $ _4 $  2D component with x ranging from 0.03 to 0.05 and 0.10. # Conclusion

In summary, we have demonstrated MD perovskite compositions displaying significantly improved photovoltaic performance and superior long-term stability by combining with 2D/3D perovskites and introducing  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $ . By adjusting the BE content, we obtained high-quality BE/FA/Cs MD perovskite films with high crystal orientation, superior surface morphology, outstanding optical properties and improved charge transfer, then resulting in significantly enhanced PCE and operational stability. The BE/FA/Cs MD perovskite device with an optimal benzylammonium content displays an improved PCE from  $ 16.53\% $  (for  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ ) to  $ 19.24\% $  (BE/FA/Cs MD, x=0.05). In addition, the BE/FA/Cs MD perovskite devices display superior humidity and heat stability. The unsealed BE/FA/Cs MD (x=0.05) devices retain  $ 85\% $  of their initial PCE (under  $ 50\% $  RH for 1600 h) and show greatly enhanced tolerance to high temperature (at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 240 h). The findings provide the possibility to develop more highly efficient and stable MD perovskite materials which may become a meaningful direction for further developments in PSCs and optoelectronic industry.
