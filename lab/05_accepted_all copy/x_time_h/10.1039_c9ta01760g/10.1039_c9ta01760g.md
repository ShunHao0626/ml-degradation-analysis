# Enhanced efficacy of defect passivation and charge extraction for efficient perovskite photovoltaics with small open circuit voltage loss

Jin-Feng Liao, $ ^{a} $  Wu-Qiang Wu, $ ^{*ab} $  Jun-Xing Zhong, $ ^{a} $  Yong Jiang, $ ^{a} $  Lianzhou Wang, $ ^{*b} $  and Dai-Bin Kuang $ ^{*ab} $

Suppressed charge recombination and facilitated charge collection are critical for achieving perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with improved efficiency and stability. Herein, a multifunctional 2D polymeric semiconductor is incorporated into perovskite ink to assist the crystal growth of perovskite film with large grains and less defects. Anchoring heterogeneous polymeric molecules on perovskite not only enhances the efficacy of defect passivation at the grain boundaries and/or surface but also facilitates the charge extraction and collection, as evident by thermal admittance spectroscopy and femtosecond transient absorption spectroscopy. Accordingly, the PSCs incorporated with polymeric semiconductor achieve power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) up to 21.1% (aperture 0.16 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) and 19.5% (aperture 1.0 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ), featuring an illustrious open-circuit voltage of 1.16 V (corresponding to a small voltage loss of 0.39 V) and an improved operational device stability (  $  \sim  $  90% initial efficiency retained after constant one sun illumination for 500 h). This work highlights the effectiveness of using multifunctional passivation agents capable of providing surprisingly robust efficacy in defect passivation of per molecule, which are also advantageous to crystal growth modulation and carrier dynamic management for efficient and stable PSCs.

# Introduction

Recent years have witnessed the rising trajectory of the organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite (OIP) materials by virtue of its remarkable optoelectronic properties, such as high absorption coefficient, high carrier mobility and long carrier diffusion length, etc.  $  ^{1-6}  $   Much effort has been devoted optimizing the film deposition technique, perovskite composition and device architecture which has boosted the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) to 23.7%.  $  ^{7}  $

High-quality perovskite films are crucial to realize highly efficient and stable PSCs.  $  ^{8-12}  $   However, fast reaction of   $  PbI_{2}  $   and methylammonium iodide (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  ) would often result in perovskite films with uncontrolled crystal sizes and incomplete surface coverage during the film preparation.  $  ^{13-15}  $   Previous studies have demonstrated strategies such as lewis-base

adduct approach (slow-down crystallization rate)  $  ^{16-20}  $   or template-assisted heterogeneous nucleation route (facilitate the nuclei formation)  $  ^{21, 22}  $   to enlarge grain sizes and improve surface coverage of perovskite films. For instance, poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA),  $  ^{23}  $   fullerene  $  ^{24, 25}  $   or fullerene derivative  $  ^{26-29}  $   have been widely employed as heterogeneous nucleation template to modulate the perovskite film formation with large grains and improved surface coverage. However, there is a dilemma in the utilization of such additives to improve the quality of perovskite films, because the heterogeneous additives were either insulated or light absorbent (especially in visible light region),  $  ^{26, 30}  $   which would impair charge extraction or exert a light absorption competition with active perovskite materials. Moreover, in some case, the additives themselves could act as the recombination centers depending on their intrinsic electronic properties. On the other hand, the as-prepared polycrystalline perovskite films inevitably contain a high density of uncoordinated ions defects at surfaces and grain boundaries (GBs), which can lead to severe charge recombination, and thus impair device performance.  $  ^{17, 30-32}  $   Hence, it is highly desirable to explore a new type of multifunctional additive that is capable of simultaneously modulating the crystal growth, passivating the defects, facilitating the charge transfer and collection, while without engaging in light absorption competition and charge recombination.

# Results and discussion

The methylammonium lead iodide (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ ) film was fabricated via a gas-assisted spin-coating process, in which the perovskite precursor was prepared by dissolving equal molar of PbI $ _2 $  and CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I with or without a small amount of g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  added (denoted as with or w/o g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $ , see Experimental section for details). As can be seen in Fig. 1a, the g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  incorporation is beneficial to improve the crystallinity of the resultant perovskite film, as is evident by the increased intensity of all CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  characteristic peaks. $ ^8 $  In particular, the main (110) and (112) peaks have witnessed the most significant enhancement, suggesting that the g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  can affect the crystal growth to a preferred orientation. $ ^{17} $  To investigate how the g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  interacts with the perovskite, the Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) spectra was analyzed. As displayed in Fig. S1, the pristine g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  exhibited stretching vibration at 807 and 1313 cm $ ^{-1} $ , which can be assigned to the typical C-N and C=N vibration. $ ^{33, 34} $  Similar stretching vibration at these two positions were observed in the g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  incorporated CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  film, accompanying a shift to a higher value in comparison to parental g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $ . These results reveal that the g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  additive was remained and chemically interacted with the perovskite film. This notion is further supported by the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Fig. 1b and Fig. S2). It is revealed that the Pb 4f signal shifted to a lower binding energy after g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  incorporation (Fig. 1b), indicating the undercoordinated Pb $ ^{2+} $  ions or clustersin CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  can be effectively passivated by the lone pair electrons from N atom in g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  and thus increasing the electronic cloud density. $ ^{35, 36} $  The conductive atomic force microscopy (c-AFM) were used to investigate the conductivity of the corresponding perovskite films. As displayed in Fig. 1c and 1d, it turned out that the g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  modified perovskite film presented a significantly enhanced current signal compared with the control sample, sugesting the improved conductivity of perovskite films upon g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  incorporation for improved carrier transport capability. The functions of 2D polymeric g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  additive are multifaceted, as schematically illustrated in Fig. 1e. Firtly, during the film deposition process, g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $ , that was well-dispersed in perovskite precursor, served as the heterogeneous sites to facilitate nucleation and formation of the g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $ /perovskite intermediate composite. The perovskite

intermediates merged together and evolved into large grains upon Ostwald ripening occurred in annealing process, which spontaneously extruded the heterogeneous component and thus keeping the g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  condense at GBs and/or film surface owing to the polymeric characteristics of g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $ . This is evidenced by the unifrom anchoring of heterogeneous g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  at the GBs or surface of the perovskite grains, as is shown in the transmission electron microscopy (TEM) image (Fig. S3). Secondly, the N atom in g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  enabled coordination bonding with dangling Pb $  ^{2+}  $  in CH $  _{3}  $ NH $  _{3}  $ PbI $  _{3}  $ , which slowed down the crystallization rate to support the growth of large grains with preferred orientations and more importantly exerted an add-on defect passivation functions for improved device performance. $ ^{15, 30, 38} $  Last but not least, the conductive g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  network condensed at the GBs and passivated the defects, which could function as an efficient carrier shuttle to facilitate the charge separation & transfer and also suppress charge recombination (discussed in a later section). The beneficial effect of g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  assisted heterogeneous nucleation on crystal growth modulation of perovsktie films is investigated.

As presented in Fig. 2a, the control   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film showcased a small grain size (around 200 nm) with noticeable pinholes and rougher surface (a root-mean-square value of 15 nm, Fig. S4a). The grains were disorderly oriented with numerous tortuous GBs (Fig. 2b). For the   $  g-C_{3}N_{4}  $   modified   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film, less pinholes, large grains (  $  \sim500  $   nm in size, Fig. 2c) and smoother surface (a root-mean-square value of 9.6 nm, Fig. S4b) were observed. The   $  g-C_{3}N_{4}  $   modified   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film showcased less GBs and possessed an optimized film thickness (  $  \sim450  $   nm) that is comparable to the size of the grains (Fig. 2d), which is beneficial to facilitate the charge transfer and charge collection.  $  ^{35,39-40}  $

We then investigated the impact of g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  incorporation on optical and electronic properties of the perovskite film. The light harvesting capability has been largely improved for g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  incorporated  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  film, especially in the wavelength region of 350-550 nm (Fig. S5), as a result of the improved quality of perovskite films with enlarged grains, increased thickness and enhanced crystallinity.  $  ^{42}  $  The improved quality of

the g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   incorporated perovskite film can be confirmed by the significantly enhanced photoluminescence (PE) intensity as compared to the control analogue (Fig. S6), which implies the suppressed nonradiative recombination that are related to the reduced trap states by g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   passivation, which will be discussed in detailed later. Overall, it can be concluded that the g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   was beneficial to improve the light harvesting and suppress charge recombination of the perovskite films, which is favourable to fabricate high-performance PSC devices.

We evaluated the effect of g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  incorporation on the photovoltaic performance of PSCs with a device architecture of FTO/compact  $  TiO_{2}  $ /perovskite/spiro-MeOTAD/Au, as is illustrated in Fig. 3a. The photocurrent density-photovoltage (J-V) curves of the control or g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  based PSCs were displayed in Fig. 3b, and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters were listed in the Table 1. The g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  incorporated PSCs attained a high PCE of 21.1%, along with a photocurrent density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 23.0 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , a photovoltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.16 V and a fill factor (FF) of 0.79, which represented a 17% enhancement in PCE as compared to control PSC (a PCE of 18.0% along with a  $  J_{sc}  $  of 21.7 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , a  $  V_{oc}  $  of 1.08 V and an FF of 0.77). The hysteresis index (HI) values for the PSCs with or without g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  additive have been included in Table 1. Specifically, the PSC incorporated with g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  showed a smaller HI value of 0.43%, as compared to that of the control device without g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  (5.8%). This can be ascribed to the facilitated charge extraction and collection, as well as effective defect passivation upon g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  additive incorporation, thus leading to significantly suppressed J-V hysteresis.  $  ^{[43]}  $  Fig. 3c showed the incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) for the PSCs in a wavelength region from 400 to 800 nm. A noticeable IPCE enhancement was characterized for g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  incorporated PSC, especially in the wavelength region from 350 nm to 550 nm, which is in good accordance with the observed enhanced light harvesting (Fig. S5) in this region and thus contributing to a higher  $  J_{sc}  $ . Fig. 3d depicts the stabilized photocurrent density of the champion g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  based PSC as measured at 0.95 V, resulting in a stabilized PCE of 20.8% under AM 1.5G illumination. The PCE histogram of g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  based PSCs was plotted from data collected from a batch of 30 devices (Fig. S7), and shows that over 80% of the cells had PCEs above 20% under 1 sun illumination. This result highlights the excellent reproducibility of this g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  additive engineering method. The stability of encapsulated PSCs fabricated with or without g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  under realistic operating conditions was measured. We fabricated the PSCs incorporated with different amounts of g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $ , and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table S1. Clearly, the optimal content for g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  ingredient within perovskite ink is 0.1 wt%, and a champion PCE up to 21.1% has been achieved. Herein, the content of g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  within the perovskite ink should be carefully adjusted. In one case, too less g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  (0.05 wt%) will result in insufficient defect passivation function. In another case, too much g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  (0.2 wt%) will significantly impair the device performance, which can be ascribed to the formation of perovskite film with poor morphology, i.e. observable pinholes and small grain sizes (Fig. S8). Photoelectrochemical characterizations can offer a useful perspective to understand the charge recombination and carrier lifetime within devices.  $  ^{48}  $   Accordingly, the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) analysis was carried out under varied applied bias from -0.8 to -1.0 V in the dark and the Nyquist plots were depicted in Fig. 5a-b.  $  ^{49-52}  $   Accordingly, the recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) and carrier lifetime (  $  \tau_{e}  $  ) of the PSCs were calculated by fitting the EIS spectra with an equivalent circuit model (Fig. S10) and the corresponding values are summarized in Table S2.  $  ^{48-50}  $   As a result, PSC incorporated g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   showcased larger recombination resistance and longer carrier lifetime (Fig. 5c, 5d and Table S2) as compared to control counterpart. In addition, the intensity-modulated photovoltage spectroscopy (IMVS) measurement was also performed to characterize the charge recombination within the PSCs based on different   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films (Fig. 5e).  $  ^{53,54}  $   Obviously, the carrier lifetime is longer in the device with g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   incorporation when compared with the control   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $ -based counterpart, suggesting the retarded charge recombination for the former device. The suppressed charge recombination can be attributed to the facilitated charge extraction at both the ETL/perovskite and perovskite/HTL interfaces and effectively reduced trap states densities owing to the g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   induced GBs functionalization and defect passivation. We measured the thermal admittance spectroscopy (TAS) to quantify the trap density of states (tDOS) for the devices fabricated with or without g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   additive. As is shown in Fig. 5f, one can observe the significantly reduced tDOS for the g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   device in both the deeper trap region (0.40-0.55 eV) which corresponded to defects at the film surface, and shallower trap region (0.30-0.40 eV) which is assigned to traps at GBs.  $  ^{55}  $   The reasons for the remarkable reduction of trap state densities upon g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   incorporation are multifaceted. Firstly, the g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   incorporation helps to enlarge the grain sizes and increased crystallinity, thus reducing the bulk trap densities of resultant perovskite films. Secondly, the g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   featuring soft and mechanically-flexible framework can effectively passivate the defects, such as the under-coordinated ions located at GBs and/or surface of perovskite film. More importantly, the g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   provides a surprisingly robust efficacy in defect passivation effect per molecule owing to its polymeric nature with numerous exposed N atom (lone pair electrons). Overall, it is worth noting that the defect passivation can be attributed to the beneficial effects of g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   incorporation, which not only functions as efficient “carrier shuttle” for facilitated charge extraction and collection, but also suppresses the charge recombination, thus greatly contributing to a higher   $  V_{oc}  $   and PCF.

# Conclusions

In summary, the perovskite ink incorporated with a small amount of g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  was demonstrated for large-scale fabrication of efficient and stable PSCs. PSCs incorporated g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  showed impressive PCEs up to 21.1% with an aperture area of 0.16 cm  $  ^{2}  $ .

and 19.5% with an aperture area of   $  1 \, cm^2  $  . The g-  $  CaN_4  $   (View Article Online Semiconductor) exhibited multifunctionalities, including modulation of crystal growth and film formation of perovskite via heterojunction nucleation, effective passivation of undercoordinated Pb clusters, as well as facilitated charge extraction and collection via embedded carrier shuttle path. The PSCs with g-  $  C_3N_4  $   incorporation exhibited negligible J-V hysteresis and improved operational stability with nearly 90% of the initial PCE retained under continuous 1 sun illumination for 500h. This work provides a new insight to use simple and low-cost multifunctional semiconductor materials towards the fabrication of large-scale perovskite photovoltaics with improved efficiency and stability.

# Experimental Section

Preparation of perovskite thin films. PbI $ _2 $  and CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I (MAI) with a molar ratio of 1:1 were dissolved in a mixed DMF and DMSO (4:1 by volume ratio) with 0.1 wt% g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  additive added. The g-C $ _3 $ N $ _4 $  was prepared by a traditional thermal polycondensation method of dicyandiamide at 500 °C. $ ^{34} $  The perovskite precursor was then spin-coated onto the cleaned and UV-ozone treated FTO substrate via a gas-assisted method at 6,500 rpm for 30 s. The as-spun films were annealed at 115 °C for 10 min.

Device fabrication. The hole-transporting precursor solution was prepared by dissolving spiro-MeOTAD and additives of lithium bis-(trifluoromethylsulphonyl)imide (in acetonitrile) and 4-tert-butylpyridine dissolved in chlorobenzene and then spin-coated on top of the perovskite layer to serve as HTL. The devices were completed by thermal evaporation of Au as the counter layer.

Materials and device characterization. Morphological observation was carried out with a field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM, Quanta 200F FEI). The crystallinity of the prepared perovskite films was measured using X-ray diffraction 1050). Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM) was performed with a Bruker (dimension fastscan bio). X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (UPS) was performed with a photoelectron spectrometer (ESCALAB 250, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The  $ J-V $  performances of the solar cells were illuminated using an Oriel Sol3A solar simulator (Class AAA), and the light intensity was calibrated to AM 1.5 G one sun condition  $ (100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2}) $  by using a calibrated KG5 filtered silicon reference cell (Peccell Technologies). The  $ J-V $  curves of the PSCs were recorded with a Keithley 2400 sourcemeter. The device was masked with a metal aperture to define the active area of  $ 0.16~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ . The IPCE spectra were measured as a function of wavelength from 330 to  $ 800\mathrm{nm} $  using a Keithley 2400 source meter under the irradiation of a  $ 300\mathrm{W} $  xenon lamp fitted with an Oriel CornerstoneTM 260  $ 1/4\mathrm{m} $  monochromator.
