# Abstract

While perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have emerged as promising low-cost solar power generators, most reported high-performance PSCs employ electron transport layers (ETLs, mainly   $  TiO_{2}  $  ) treated at high temperatures (  $  \geq450\ ^{\circ}C  $  ), which may eventually hinder the development of flexible PSCs. Meanwhile, the development of low-temperature processed PSCs (L-PSCs) possessing performance levels comparable to that of high-temperature processed PSCs has actively been reported. In this study, L-PSCs with improved long-term stability and negligible hysteresis were developed through the effective passivation of shallow and deep traps in organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite (OIHP) crystals and at the ETL/OIHP interface. L-PSCs with alkaline chloride modification achieved state-of-the-art performance among reported L-PSCs (power conversion efficiency (PCE) = 22.6%) with a long-term shelf life. The origin of long-term stability and the efficient passivation of deep traps was revealed by monitoring the trap-state distribution. Moreover, the high PCE of a large-area device (21.3%, 1.12 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) were also demonstrated, confirming the uniformity of the modification.

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite (OIHP) based solar cells (PSCs) have recently attracted considerable attention as a low-cost power source due to their high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and solution processability.  $  ^{[1,2]}  $   The PCEs of PSCs have rapidly improved from 3.8%  $  ^{[3]}  $   to 25.2% (refer to the national renewable energy laboratory (NREL) chart)  $  ^{[2]}  $   in less than a decade through the development in OIHP photoactive materials and charge selection layers such as the electron transport layer (ETL) and hole transport layer (HTL).  $  ^{[1,4-6]}  $   The state-of-the-art PSC devices have employed high-temperature (  $  \geq  $   450 °C) sintered   $  TiO_{2}  $   as the ETL,  $  ^{[7]}  $   which has determined the device processing temperature.  $  ^{[1,6]}  $   The demand for low-temperature processed ETLs that can be employed for flexible PSCs has continuously been raised; however, their performance (charge extraction property, stability, etc.) has been inferior to that of high-temperature processed   $  TiO_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[4,8-10]}  $   Recently, low-temperature processed   $  TiO_{2}  $   has developed and demonstrated a PCE of 21.5%.  $  ^{[8]}  $   Not only   $  TiO_{2}  $ , but also   $  SnO_{2}  $   based low-temperature ETLs achieved a PCE of 23.3%.  $  ^{[9]}  $

ZnO is a promising low-temperature ETL candidate for PSCs due to its solution-processability, high electron mobility, and suitable energy levels.  $  ^{[11,12]}  $   Although solution-processed ZnO has been successfully employed in organic photovoltaic devices and quantum dot solar cells,  $  ^{[11-13]}  $   it has been problematic for use in PSCs due to interfacial reverse decomposition reactions.  $  ^{[9,14]}  $   The basic nature of the ZnO surface leads to proton-transfer reactions at the ZnO/OIHP interface, degrading the PCE and stability of PSCs due to the decomposition of OIHP to   $  PbI_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[9,14]}  $   Interfacial decomposition can even be accelerated by the presence of surface hydroxyl groups and/or residual organic acetate ligands.  $  ^{[14]}  $   Intensive studies with regard to minimizing reverse decomposition through surface passivation have been carried out.  $  ^{[9,10,15]}  $   Nevertheless, the reported ZnO based PSCs have demonstrated considerably reduced performance compared to   $  TiO_{2}  $   based PSCs. Meanwhile, defect trap states in the ETL/OIHP interface and OIHP active layer are important factors with regard to determining the performance of PSCs, including the PCE, hysteresis, long-term stability, and effective passivation, which are major concerns in the field.  $  ^{[8,16-18]}  $   Alkaline metals have effectively suppressed ETL/OIHP interfacial defects,  $  ^{[15,18]}  $   and internal trap sites in OIHP crystals, as reported by several research groups.  $  ^{[6,17,19-22]}  $   In those studies, alkali metals were introduced into the OIHP precursor solutions to achieve the interstitial doping of alkali metals, which could increase the energy barrier to ion migration. As a result, the PCE and hysteresis of the PSCs were substantially improved.  $  ^{[17,19-21]}  $

The passivation of anionic defects in the OIHP crystals was achieved by doping halide atoms.  $  ^{[1,6,21-23]}  $   Particularly, the incorporation of Cl in the OIHP using   $  PbCl_{2}  $   or alkyl-ammonium chloride (  $  RNH_{3}Cl  $  ) successfully passivated the internal trap sites. As a result, the PCE and stability of devices were significantly improved.  $  ^{[7,23,24]}  $   Strong bonding between Pb and Cl compared to Pb and I was beneficial to passivate defect sites of the OIHP crystals.  $  ^{[8,24]}  $   Simultaneous doping of alkali metals with Cl is expected to be an effective defect passivation strategy for OIHP crystals and ZnO/OIHP interface but has not been achieved due to the poor solubility of alkaline chlorides in the OIHP precursor solutions using polar solvents such as isopropanol (IPA), dimethylformamide (DMF), and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO).  $  ^{[16,18]}  $

In this study, high-efficiency ZnO-based L-PSCs with improved long-term stability and negligible hysteresis were achieved through the simultaneous doping of alkaline metals and Cl in OIHP crystals and at the ZnO/OIHP interface. The pristine ZnO-ETLs with a thickness of  $ \sim 40 $  nm were prepared on indium-doped tin oxide (ITO) coated glass substrates via conventional in situ sol-gel conversion at  $ 130^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ .[13,25] We first modified the ZnO layers by immersing them in aqueous alkaline chloride (LiCl, NaCl, KCl, CsCl) solutions for 1 min followed by washing with deionized water. Uniform modification throughout the entire ZnO surface was confirmed via electron dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) mapping of the scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images (Figure 1a) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis (Figure S1). A shift of the Zn 2p XPS peaks toward higher binding energies indicated that Cl was bound directly to Zn (Figure S1h).[26] The incorporation of alkaline metals ( $ 14.5 \pm 0.2 $  at. %, Figure S1g) and Cl ( $ 19.6 \pm 0.4 $  at. %, Figure S1f) in the ZnO-ETLs was similar in all the modified samples.

Oxygen-deficient ZnO defects could be identified in the O 1s XPS spectra (Figure S2).  $  ^{[11,25]}  $   The higher binding-energy peak at   $  \sim532.2  $   eV could be attributed to oxygen-deficient defects such as ZnO oxygen vacancies and surface hydroxyl groups,  $  ^{[11,12]}  $   while the lower binding-energy peak at   $  \sim530.7  $   eV was from ZnO crystal lattices. The considerably reduced intensity of the higher binding-energy peak in the modified ZnO films indicated effective passivation of the oxygen-deficient defects. ZnO defect passivation was also confirmed via photoluminescence (PL) emission spectra (Figure S2g). Modification effects on the long-term stability of the L-PSCs were investigated. First, the suppression of reverse decomposition reactions was monitored by additionally annealing the OIHP layers at 100 °C for 60 min under a relative humidity of 55%. In the unmodified samples, yellow-colored PbI₂ formed after 30 min of annealing (Figure S17a). On the contrary, the KCl modified sample exhibited a negligible reverse decomposition after 60 min of thermal stress. The formation of yellow PbI₂ was also confirmed via UV-vis spectra and XRD analysis of the samples (Figures S17c – S17f). The long-term shelf-life of unencapsulated L-PSCs was tested following the ISOS-D-1 protocol (in the dark, ≈25 °C, 40% humidity, in air). [34] The L-PSC-KCl maintained 90% of its initial PCE after 1400 h, whereas unmodified L-PSC yielded less than 10% retention (Figure 4a). Long-term stability under thermal and light-soaking stress conditions were also measured. The thermal stability test was performed by placing the devices on a hotplate at 70 °C in an N₂-filled glovebox. The L-PSC-KCl achieved significantly higher stability (>75% retention after more than 200 h, Figure 4b) than the unmodified L-PSC (<13% retention). Long-term photostability under light-soaking conditions was evaluated by placing the L-PSCs under white LED light (100 mW cm⁻²) at continuous MPP conditions within an N₂-filled glovebox. After 150 h, L-PSC-KCl retained 76% of its initial PCE, whereas the unmodified L-PSC completely lost its performance (Figure 4c).

In summary, we developed low-temperature processed PSCs with state-of-the-art performance using a new modification strategy. The modification of L-PSCs using the “deposition and diffusion method” with alkaline chlorides effectively passivated both shallow and deep trap states, which were present in OIHP crystal layers and interfaces. Dual trap passivation dramatically improved the PCE, photocurrent hysteresis, and long-term stability of L-PSCs.
