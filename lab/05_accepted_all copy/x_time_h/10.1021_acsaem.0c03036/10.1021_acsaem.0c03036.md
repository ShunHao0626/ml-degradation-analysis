# Commercial Carbon-Based all-Inorganic Perovskite Solar Cells with a High Efficiency of 13.81%: Interface Engineering and Photovoltaic Performance

Ke Wang,  $  ^{\perp}  $   Zhaobin Zhang,  $  ^{\perp}  $   Ling Wang,  $  ^{\perp}  $   Koucheng Chen, Lu Tao, Yinxi Zhang, and Xingfu Zhou  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: The past few years have witnessed the rapid development of all-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs). The interface of the electron transport layer (ETL)/perovskite is the vital charge separation site in all-inorganic PSCs. In this study,   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanorod arrays (NAs) were employed as electron transport layers in   $  CsPbI_{2.25}Br_{0.75}  $   perovskite solar cells for enhancing the photovoltaic performance. Commercially available carbon was also used instead of the expensive hole transport materials and noble metals. The interface of   $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite was further modified with cesium acetate (CsAc) to reduce the interface defect and the carrier recombination. The surface morphology, crystal structure,

photoelectric property, and photovoltaic performance of the solar device were systematically investigated. More importantly, the device based on the CsAc-modified ETL/perovskite interface produced an optimization efficiency of 13.81%, which represents a record-breaking power conversion efficiency for carbon-based all-inorganic PSCs to date. This study emphasizes the critical importance of interface engineering of the ETL/perovskite and opens the door to the fabrication of carbon-based all-inorganic PSCs with high efficiency.

KEYWORDS: inorganic perovskite solar cell, commercial carbon, cesium acetate, interface engineering, efficiency

# INTRODUCTION

In the past 10 years, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have progressed rapidly from initial 3.8% to certified 25.5%.  $  ^{1,2}  $   In addition, PSCs have also made substantial progress in low-cost, large-area applications, which are comparable to silicon-based solar cells.  $  ^{3-11}  $   This is due to the advantages of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials, such as a high absorption coefficient, large carrier mobility, and adjustable band gap.  $  ^{5,12,13}  $   However, the volatility of organic molecules in organic-inorganic mixed perovskites will lead to poor thermal stability, which limits its application in PSCs to a certain extent.

Replacing the organic components of perovskite with inorganic components has been shown to be an advisable strategy to improve the thermal stability of PSCs.  $  ^{14,15}  $   In recent years, all-inorganic perovskites have drawn much attention, owing to their exceptional thermal stability and potential thermal discoloration characteristics.  $  ^{16,17}  $   Among various all-inorganic PSCs, the PCE of lead halide perovskite (CsPbX  $  _{3}  $  , X = Cl, Br, and I) as the light-absorbing material device has exceeded 19%.  $  ^{18}  $   In 2015, Kulbak et al. reported that CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $   has the same excellent photovoltaic characteristics as MAPbBr  $  _{3}  $  , especially the PSCs based on CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $   have better thermal stability and humidity stability, and have higher open

Based on the above considerations, researchers have reduced the carrier recombination within the device by preparing high-quality perovskite films or passivating surface defects of the perovskite layer. For the first time, Liu et al. doped   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   into the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite precursor solution to obtain a high-quality inorganic perovskite film with low defect density and long carrier life, as well as achieved a PCE of 15.25% and fill factor (FF) of 79%.  $  ^{37}  $   Furthermore, Liu and co-workers have demonstrated that the   $  Ac^{-}  $  functional group as a dopant plays a significant role in improving morphology, electronic properties, and band structure of perovskite and gives the enhanced photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{38,39}  $   Hong et al. incorporated   $  Ba^{2+}  $   into   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite to prepare a high-quality inorganic perovskite film (  $  BaI_{2}:CsPbI_{2}Br  $  ) under the condition of dynamic hot air-assisted crystallization, and the hot-air-processed devices retain >92% of the initial efficiency after 300 h.  $  ^{40}  $   Zeng et al. used polythiophene and a simple annealing process to passivate the perovskite interface; this strategy can reduce the electron–hole recombination inside the perovskite and minimize the internal energy loss of the device to 0.5 eV,

while it sacrificed short circuit current (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) (13.13 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) and FF (70.4%) of corresponding PSCs.  $  ^{33}  $   Moreover, Liu et al. used Cs-doped nanorod arrays as electron-transfer layers, which effectively enhanced the electron extraction and reduced the charge recombination. Based on the incorporation of Cs elements, the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   devices demonstrated a far improved PCE of 9.5%.  $  ^{41}  $   As we all know, the electron transport layer (ETL)/perovskite interface is the only effective method to inhibit charge recombination in PSCs,  $  ^{42,43}  $   but few researchers have reduced the carrier recombination in devices by passivating or modifying the ETL/perovskite interface. Therefore, optimizing the interface of the ETL/perovskite heterojunction plays a crucial role in promoting effective interface extraction, injection of charge, inhibiting carrier recombination, and enhancing the PCE of all-inorganic PSCs based on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  .

Herein, we use  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NRs as ETLs to accelerate charge transfer, as well as modify the ETL/perovskite interface with cesium acetate (CsAc) to reduce interface defects and carrier recombination. In addition, to reduce the cost of PSCs, we also replaced expensive and unstable organic hole transport layers and precious metal electrodes with commercial carbon electrodes. As a result, based on the configuration of FTO/CsAc@TiO $ _2 $  NRs/CsPbI $ _{2.25} $ Br $ _{0.75} $ /C, the best performance of the device  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ ,  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ , FF, and PCE are  $ 16.29\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ ,  $ 1.145\mathrm{V} $ ,  $ 74.10\% $ , and  $ 13.81\% $ , respectively, which represents a record-breaking PCE to date for the carbon-based all-inorganic PSCs.

To comprehensively investigate the effect of CsAc concentration on the photovoltaic performance of the solar devices, we used the FTO/TiO $ _2 $  NRs/CsPbI $ _{2.25} $ Br $ _{0.75} $ /C multilayer structure to prepare carbon-based all-inorganic PSCs. The interface of TiO $ _2 $  NRs was modified with CsAc at various concentrations of 2.5, 5.0, and 10.0 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ . Figure 3a–d exhibits photovoltaic characteristics ( $ J_{\text{SO}} $ ,  $ V_{\text{OC}} $ , FF, and PCE) of the as-prepared cells, respectively. In general, the devices manufactured with CsAc@TiO $ _2 $  NRs provide good photovoltaic performance, and the carbon-based all-inorganic PSCs employing 2.50 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  CsAc exhibit a record-high PCE of 13.81%, which yields ~21% enhancement when compared with the counterpart cells without CsAc modification (PCE of 11.38%). The statistical result is consistent with our experiment of reducing the interface defect and the carrier recombination via the interface engineering of ETL/perovskite by using CsAc modification.

The improvement of PCE mainly ascribe to the increase in  $ V_{\text{OC}} $  and FF of the solar device after the modification of the ETL/perovskite interface using CsAc. When the concentration of CsAc is 2.5 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ , the device shows the best performance in all aspects. In other words, the strategy of modifying the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NR/perovskite interface with CsAc can effectively inhibit the reverse recombination of carriers, reduce the current loss, and increase the   $  V_{OC}  $   and FF of the device. The trap-filled limit voltage ( $  V_{TFL}  $ ) corresponds to the turning point in the curves (Figure S3). Therefore, the trap density ( $  N_{trap}  $ ) can be calculated according to the equation.

$$
N _ {\mathrm{trap}} = \frac {2 \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0} V _ {\mathrm{TFL}}}{e L ^ {2}}

# CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, we introduced CsAc to modify the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NRs/perovskite interface and improve the photovoltaic performance of the commercial carbon-based all-inorganic perovskite solar cells. Benefitting from the reduction of defect state density and carrier recombination at the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NR/perovskite interface, the FF and PCE of the device with CsAc modification reached 0.74 and 13.81%, respectively, significantly superior to the control devices. Meanwhile, interface modification facilitates the separation of electrons and holes from the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NR/perovskite interface. This study emphasizes the critical importance of interface engineering of the ETL/perovskite and opens the door to the fabrication of carbon-based all-inorganic PSCs with high efficiency.

Device Fabrication. Materials. All the chemicals were used as received without further purification. Lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ ) (99.5%), cesium iodide (CsI) ( $ \geq $ 99.5%), and lead bromide (PbBr $ _2 $ ) (99.5%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.  $ N $ ,  $ N $ -Dimethylformamide (DMF anhydrous, 99.8%) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO anhydrous, 99.8 + %) and CsAc (99.9%) were obtained from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co. Ltd. Commercial conductive carbon paste was purchased from New Seaside Science and Trade Co., Ltd.

Synthesis of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NR Arrays. The FTO conducting glass (1.5 × 1.5 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) was cleaned by ultrasonic treatment with detergent, deionized water, acetone, and ethyl alcohol for 30 min and dried with nitrogen. FTO conductive glass is cleaned by UV ozone for 30 min before use.   $  TiO_{2}  $   NRs were synthesized by a hydrothermal route. In a typical synthesis, the FTO coated glass substrates were placed within a sealed Teflon reactor (50 mL), containing 20 mL of   $  H_{2}O  $  , 0.75 mL of tetrabutyl titanate, and 20 mL of hydrochloric acid (37 wt %). Then, an autoclave kettle filled with the mixture was kept at 150°C for 150 min and naturally cooled to room temperature. The FTO conductive glass with   $  TiO_{2}  $   NR arrays was taken out and washed with deionized water and ethanol. After drying with a hair dryer, the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NR arrays on the photoanode were scraped off with a double-sided blade. Finally, it was calcined at 500°C for 30 min in a muffle furnace. For CsAc modification, 100 mL CsAc (2.5/5.0/10.0 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) solution

using deionized water and isopropanol with a volume ratio of 1:1 as solvents was spin-coated on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NR array (3000 rpm for 60 s) and thermally annealed at 90 °C for 30 min in air.

Perovskite Film Deposition and Device Fabrication. CsI (0.2598 g), 0.2881 g PbI $ _{2} $ , and 0.1376 g PbBr $ _{2} $  were added to 1 mL mixed solvent (DMSO: DMF = 9: 1, v/v). The mixture was stirred slowly at room temperature for 12 h and filtered using a 0.45  $ \mu $ m organic filter for later use. The FTO/TiO $ _{2} $  NR/ perovskite substrate (with or without CsAc modification) was obtained by spin-coating 50  $ \mu $ L perovskite precursor solution (1000 rpm for 10 s and then 2500 rpm for 30 s) on the TiO $ _{2} $  nanowire array layer. The substrate was annealed at 90°C for 2 min and then at 260°C for 5 min to form a crystalline CsPbI $ _{2.25} $ Br $ _{0.75} $  film. Finally, a layer of commercial conductive carbon paste was fabricated on the top of the perovskite infiltrated scaffold by doctor blading followed by annealing at 100°C for 30 min. The fabrication of the device was finished.

Device Characterizations. The morphology of perovskite thin films and titanium dioxide nanorods was studied by field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM, Hitachi S-4800). The absorption spectra of perovskite films were collected with a UV–vis spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer Lambda 950). The XRD (Bruker D8 Advance) spectra of inorganic perovskite films were recorded on a Rigaku Ultima IV (Cu Kα radiation, λ = 1.5418 Å) at 40 KV and 40 mA. UPS spectra were recorded on an ESCALAB 250Xi XPS system. The current-density (J)-voltage(V) curves (J-V curves) were obtained by using Keithley 2400 (Newport USA, Oriel Class A, 9119SA) under a standard solar simulator with an intensity of 100 mWcm  $  ^{-2}  $  (AM 1.5 G), which was calibrated by a standard silicon solar cell (91150 V, Oriel). Incident-photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) was recorded from 300 to 800 nm wavelength by a power source (Newport 300 W xenon lamp) with a monochromatic instrument (Newport Cornerstone 260). Steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) decay spectra were recorded using an FLS 980 E fluorometer (Edinburgh Photonics) with a 670 nm wavelength excitation source. EIS was used to study the mechanism of charge migration and recombination inside the device at a voltage of 1 V in the frequency range of 1 MHz to 0.1 Hz. The space-charge-limited current (SCLC) was investigated by using an electrochemical station (CHI660C).
