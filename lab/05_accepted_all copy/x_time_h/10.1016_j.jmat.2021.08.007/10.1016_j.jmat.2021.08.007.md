Yanzhou Wang, Caidong Xie, Xincheng Yao, Qiulu Chen, Weining Liu, Yujun Fu, Qiming Liu*, Junshuai Li*, Yali Li*, and Deyan He

Key Laboratory of Special Function Materials & Structure Design of the Ministry of Education, and School of Materials & Energy, Lanzhou University, 222 South Tianshui Road, Lanzhou 730000, China

Abstract: Introducing additives into perovskite layers is an effective method to enhance the power conversion efficiency (PCE) and operation stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, we report addition of carbon nanodots (CNDs) into the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   photoactive layer to boost performance of the related PSCs. It is found that the trap density can be notably suppressed, and the crystallinity can be enhanced after introducing CNDs with an optimal quantity. The PSC with 1.0 wt% addition of CNDs delivers a notable improved average PCE of 13.77% (the highest PCE: 14.69%) from that of 12.14% for the control device without CND addition. Moreover, the CND-added   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs exhibit superior stability, i.e., ~ 86% retention of the initial PCE after 160 h aging in air with the humidity of 20-30%, to the control device.

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted tremendous attention owing to the excellent comprehensive optoelectronic properties, such as high absorption coefficients, long electron-hole diffusion lengths and tunable bandgaps for the related photoactive materials [1-6]. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs has risen unprecedentedly to  $ 25.5\% $  from  $ 3.8\% $  since the first report in 2009 [7, 8]. Moreover, facile fabrication compatible with the solution processes enables notable reduction of the fabrication cost as compared to other photovoltaic technologies such as crystalline silicon solar cells. However, due to the volatile and hygroscopic organic cations of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3^+ $  and  $ \mathrm{CH(NH_2)_2^+} $  in the organic-inorganic perovskites, the inherent structural instability would seriously influence the operation stability for this promising solar cell technology [9].

As a unique alternative, all-inorganic perovskites, substituting the organic cations by the Cs cation, show better light, moisture and especially thermal stability [10-13]. Among Cs-based all-inorganic perovskites,  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_3} $  in the cubic crystal structure exhibits the sufficient absorption of visible light because of the optical bandgap of  $ 1.73\mathrm{eV} $  [14, 15]. Nevertheless,  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_3} $  PSCs suffer from the instable performance due to the severe octahedral rotation distortion and spontaneous transformation from  $ \alpha $ -phase to the undesired  $ \delta $ -phase at room temperature [16]. Mixed-halide  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  with a suitable bandgap of  $ \sim 1.9\mathrm{eV} $  may balance light harvesting and phase stability [17]. For  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $ -based PSCs, the primary issue needing to be resolved is the inferior PCE to that of the typical hybrid perovskite counterparts [18]. Many efforts

have thus been paid on the issues including energy level matching and defect passivation for high-performance   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with the minimized energy loss [19-23]. Among them, passivating defects is critical for achieving a high carrier mobility, long carrier lifetime and diffusion length [24, 25]. Additive engineering has proven to be an effective method for passivating the grain boundaries, improving the crystallinity, and thus promoting optoelectronic properties of the perovskite layers [26, 27]. To date, additives such as salts, molecules and polymers have been applied for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs [28-34].

Carbon nanodots (CNDs), as an important member of the carbon material family, have drawn a lot of attention because of the unique physical and chemical properties [35, 36]. Recently, an investigation regarding performance enhancement for inverted  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  hybrid PSCs by grain boundary passivation using CNDs has been reported [37]. Nevertheless, report on modification of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  layers with CNDs to improve performance of the related PSC2s has been rarely found. In this report, addition of CNDs into the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  layers to modify the physical and chemical properties and thus to improve the performance of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs is discussed. The results indicate that the CND addition with an appropriate quantity can enhance the crystal quality, reduce the defect density, and improve the electrical properties and structural stability of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  layers. As a result, the PSCs with  $ 1.0\mathrm{wt}\% $  addition of CNDs deliver the averaged PCE of  $ 13.77\% $  with an evident increase from  $ 12.14\% $  for the control device without CND addition. Moreover, the enhanced operation stability is observed for the CND-added devices.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Synthesis of CNDs

CNDs were fabricated by a microwave-assisted method [38]. 1 g citric acid and 2 g urea were first dispersed in 20 mL deionized (DI) water. Then, the mixture solution was placed in a microwave oven for 5 min until obtaining a dark cluster that was then dried at 60 °C for 1 h. Following that, the product was dissolved in ethanol and centrifugated at 8000 rpm/min. After collecting the solid product, it was dried at 60 °C to obtain CNDs.

# 2.2. Fabrication of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs

Etched ITO glass substrates were first ultrasonically cleaned in the sequence of ethanol, acetone and isopropanol each for 15 min. After drying, the ITO surface was treated by air plasma for 2.5 min to make a hydrophilic surface. Then, the SnO $ _2 $  colloid precursor (15 wt% in H $ _2 $ O colloidal dispersion) was diluted in DI water with a volume ratio of 1:4, followed by spin-coating onto the ITO substrates at 3000 rpm for 40 s and annealing on a hot plate at 150 °C for 30 min in air to form the SnO $ _2 $  electron transport layer. For preparation of the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br layers, CsI, PbI $ _2 $  and PbBr were stoichiometrically dissolved in a mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO with a volume ratio of 4:1 to form a 1 M solution. Then CNDs were added into the 1 M CsPbI $ _2 $ Br precursor solution and stirred at 90 °C for 1 h. The precursor solution was then spin-coated onto the SnO $ _2 $ -coated ITO glass substrates at 3000 rpm for 60 s inside an argon-filled glovebox, which was followed by in situ drying firstly at 50 °C for 1 min and then at 160 °C for 10 min to form the CND-embedded CsPbI $ _2 $ Br layers.

# 2.3. Characterization

The morphology of the samples was characterized by a scanning electron microscope (SEM, MIRA3, TESCAN) and atomic force microscope (AFM, NT-MDT). The structural and compositional information was checked by an X-ray diffractometer (XRD, Malvern Panalytical Empyrean with Cu  $ K_{\alpha} $  radiation), transmission electron microscope (TEM, FEI Tecnai G $ ^2 $  F30), and micro-Raman spectrometer (Jobin-Yvon Horiba HR800 with the excitation wavelength of 532 nm). Optical absorption spectra were measured using a UV-VIS-NIR spectrophotometer (Shimadzu UV-3600). Photoluminescence (PL) spectra were recorded by a RF-5301PC spectrometer (Shimadzu) with the excitation wavelength of 525 nm. The current density-voltage ( $ J-V $ ) characteristic curves were recorded using a Keithley 2400 source meter at air mass (AM) 1.5G illumination (100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $ ). The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) test was performed in a frequency range of 1- $ 10^{6} $  Hz at an AC voltage with an amplitude of 10 mV using the CHI660E electrochemical workstation.

To understand the mechanisms responsible for the improved device performance because of the CND addition, more investigations were carried out. Fig. 5(a) compares the steady-state PL spectra of the pristine and 1.0 wt% CND-added   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   layers on   $  SnO_{2}  $  -coated ITO glass. It is evident that the intensity of the peak located around 650 nm, corresponding to the bandgap energy of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , remarkably reduces for the CND-added sample. According to the previous reports [43], it can be ascribed to the transfer of the photoelectrons from   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   to the embedded CNDs benefiting from the suitable energy level alignment (see Fig. 4(c)), leading to the unmatched numbers for the electrons in the conduction band and the holes in the valence band, and thus to the reduced PL emission. Accordingly, the enhanced transfer of the photoelectrons across the photoactive layer through the CND “bridges” can reduce the carrier loss because of various recombination mechanisms and thus increase   $  J_{sc}  $   of the related devices [44, 45].

distinct regions can be observed, i.e., the linear  $ I - V $  relation at the low bias and nonlinear increase of  $ I $  with  $ V $  at the relatively high voltage, which correspond to the ohmic response and conducting mechanism with all the available trap states filled by the injected carriers, respectively. The voltage corresponding to the intersect point of the tangent lines of the  $ I - V $  curves in these two regions is called the trap-filled limited voltage,  $ V_{\mathrm{TFL}} $ , which can be employed to estimate the trap density  $ (n_{\mathrm{t}}) $  following the equation of  $ n_{\mathrm{t}} = 2\varepsilon V_{\mathrm{TFL}} / (eL^2) $ , where  $ \varepsilon $  and  $ L $  are the dielectric constant and the thickness of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  layer, and  $ e $  is the elementary charge [46]. The calculated  $ n_{\mathrm{t}} $  for the pristine and  $ 1.0\mathrm{wt}\% $  CND-added  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  layers is about  $ 1.02\times 10^{16} $  and  $ 7.90\times 10^{15}\mathrm{cm}^{-3} $ , indicating that CND addition can reduce the trap density in the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  layer.

# 4. Conclusions

In this paper, addition of CNDs in the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  layer to improve the performance of the related PSCs is investigated. Thanks to the reduced defect density and surface roughness, as well as the improved crystallinity and moisture resistance for the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  layers with appropriate CND addition, the PSCs deliver the enhanced comprehensive performance including PCE and stability, as compared to the control device without CND addition. Given the facile experimental scheme and green process, it is believed that this study provides valuable exploration for developing the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $ -based perovskite solar cells with improved comprehensive performance.
