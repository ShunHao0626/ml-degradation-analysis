# Abstract

The low-cost inorganic materials,   $  KFeO_{2}/ZnO  $  , have been investigated as hole transport layer (HTL)/electron transport layer (ETL) and show better stability and high-power conversion efficiency for perovskite solar cell. Perovskite solar cells with two planar structures n-i-p and p-i-n have been fabricated using   $  KFeO_{2}  $   and ZnO showing 12.81% and 10.83% power conversion efficiency, respectively. The stability for the perovskite solar cell has been tested at 30 °C with 40–45% relative humidity for 240 h. Time-resolved photoluminescence spectra revealed that decay time for n-i-p is higher than p-i-n. The mechanism for high stability and efficiency is discussed.

# 1 Introduction

In recent years, the perovskite solar cells have drawn a lot of attention, because of the rapid increase in their power conversion efficiency   $  [1-3]  $  . These solar cells have emerged as the cleanest source of energy, because of the unique properties viz., large diffusion length, high absorption power, high extinction coefficient, ambipolar diffusion, low cost of fabrication   $  [1]  $  .

In the last decade, rapid increase of power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the organometallic perovskite solar cell form 3.8% to above 24.2% making it the next generation source of energy for wide application   $  [2, 3]  $  . Kojima et al. (2009) reported the idea of organic–inorganic-based perovskite solar cell with 4% PCE   $  [2]  $  . Afterward, numerous alterations in the structure, design, choice and type of material have done so far for the enhancement of PCE, and in short interval of time this reaches to 15%   $  [4]  $  . The basis morphology of the perovskite solar cell consists of stacking of the various thin films on substrates such as the charge transport layers—electron transport layer (ETL) and hole transport layer (HTL); perovskite layer sandwich between these ETL and HTL layers are responsible for the generation and

extraction of the electrons while the perovskite layer converts light energy into electrical energy. In the planar structure, with transparent electrodes (HTL/ETL), the process of charge separation and charge transportation has eliminated the requirement of the shunt resistance and this improves the PCE [5, 6]. The main purpose of the charge transport layers is to maintain the unidirectional flow of charge carriers in the solar cell.

The commonly used perovskite material is methylammonium lead iodide (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ ) that was used for the first time as a sensitizer for the TiO $ _2 $ -based solar with 2.2% PCE. Afterward, 22.1% PCE is achieved as it has high absorption coefficient, low bandgap (~1.5 eV), long diffusion length, balanced electron–hole mobility, high carrier mobility, high dielectric constant, and small exciton binding energies [2].

Different types of organic HTL and ETL are used so far viz., 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-pdimethoxyphenylamino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD), poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrene sulphonate (PEDOT:PSS), poly(3-hexylthiophene-2,5-diyl) (P3HT), poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA) and [6,6]-phenyl C61 butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM),  $ C_{60} $  and their derivatives, and polymer poly(triarylamine) (PTAA), 9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) and 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,Ndi-pmethoxyphenylamine)-9, respectively. These organic HTL/ETL have high PCE in the mesoporous and thin film-based structures [7]. But the cost of the organic HTL/ETL is very high and also these have low carrier mobility, low stability, gets dripped out with time, fabrication involves large steps and cause pollution. # 2 Materials and methods

# 2.1 Materials

The chemicals used for the fabrication of solar cell and thin films are of research grade and purchased from Loba-Chemie Laboratory. Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass is used as substrate. The chemical used are potassium acetate  $ (\mathrm{KCH}_2\mathrm{COOH}) $ , ferric nitrate  $ (\mathrm{Fe(NO_3)_3\cdot 9H_2O}) $ , zinc acetate dehydrate  $ \mathrm{Zn(CH_3CO_2)_22H_2O)} $ , sodium hydroxide (NaOH), citric acid monohydrate  $ (\mathrm{C_6H_8O_7}) $ , methylammonium iodide  $ (\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I}) $ , lead(II) iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2) $ , ethylene glycol  $ (\mathrm{C_2H_6O_2}) $ , isopropanol  $ (\mathrm{CH}_3)_2\mathrm{CHOH} $ , acetone  $ (\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COCH}_3) $ , deionized water.

# 2.2 Preparation of KFeO  $  _{2}  $  precursor solution

The equimolar potassium acetate (KCH $ _2 $ COOH) and ferric nitrate (Fe(NO $ _3 $ ) $ _3 $  9H $ _2 $ O) of 0.1 M are dissolved in the ethylene glycol separately, stirred at 40 °C for 30 min till these become clear solutions. Then the potassium acetate solution is added drop-wise in the ferric nitrate solution. The color of the mixture becomes light brownish. Afterward, citric acid of 0.2 M is added in this mixture and again stirred for 2 h at 40 °C. The sol of KFeO $ _2 $  is formed after 2 h. The color of the sol is brown. This sol is further used for the fabrication of thin film of KFeO $ _2 $  on FTO, which will act as a hole transport layers.

# 2.3 Preparation of ZnO precursor solution

The equimolar solutions of zinc acetate dehydrate and sodium hydroxide are prepared in ethylene glycol. The NaOH solution is added drop-wise in zinc acetate dehydrate solution and stirred vigorously for 2 h at 40 °C. The appearance of milky color sol after 2 h indicates the formation of ZnO sol and this will be used as an electron transport layers.

# 2.5 Fabrication of thin films of ZnO and KFeO  $  _{2}  $

For the investigation of structural and optical properties of transport layers, thin films of  $ \mathrm{KFeO}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  have been fabricated using spin-coating techniques on glass substrate.

The as-prepared sol of  $ \mathrm{KFeO_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  is used for the fabrication of thin films. The spin-coating of the sol of  $ \mathrm{KFeO_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  is done at  $ 8000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 60~s $  and subsequently annealed at  $ 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ . The substrate is cooled down to room temperature naturally. The fabricated films are calcined at  $ 400^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 4\mathrm{h} $  to get desired phase of  $ \mathrm{KFeO_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $ .

# 2.6 Instrumentation

PANalytical X'Pert PRO MRD, X-ray diffractometer (with Cu-Kα, λ = 1.54060 Å) is used for analyzing the crystal structure. The optical bandgap and absorption spectra is measured using the UV-1800 Shimadzu spectrometer. The current density–voltage characteristics of the solar cell device are measured using a Keithley 2400 source meter with simulated AM 1.5G solar irradiation of 100 mW/cm² (Newport, AAA solar simulator, 94023A-U). Incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra are recorded with Keithley 2400 source meter attached with 1/4 monochromator under irradiation of a 150 W tungsten lamp. Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) is measured using system that is attached with a pulse laser CO₂.

# 3.5 Stability of perovskite solar cell

The investigation of the stability parameters of the solar cell devices is very important for their terrestrial application. For this purpose, the stability of the device has been investigated with relative to time for 10 days at ambient environment at   $  30\ °C  $   with 40–45% relative humidity. # 4 Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated operation of perovskite solar cells with two different polarity configurations of solution processed inorganic charge transport layers, i.e., FTO/ZnO/CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /KFeO $ _2 $ /Pt (ZCK) and FTO/KFeO $ _2 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /ZnO/Pt (KCZ). XRD confirmed the orthorhombic crystal structure for KFeO $ _2 $  with the Pbca space group and hexagonal crystal structure for ZnO with the P63mc space group. The optical properties of the KFeO $ _2 $  and ZnO revealed the bandgap 1.93 eV and 3.56 eV, respectively. The energy band diagram for the ZnO, CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ , KFeO $ _2 $  confirmed the value of  $ E_{\text{CB}} - 3.9 $  eV,  $ -3.41 $  eV,  $ -2.86 $  eV and for  $ E_{\text{VB}} $  is  $ -0.34 $  eV,  $ -1.48 $  eV,  $ -1.48 $  eV, respectively. The employed inorganic charge transport layers show significance PCE of 12.81% for ZCK and 10.83% for KCZ configuration. IPCE spectra show the spectral response 67.58% for ZCK and 70.31% for KCZ at 550–650 nm, approximately. The integrated photocurrent for ZCK and KCZ is found to be 18.01 and 19.22 mA/cm $ ^2 $ . TRPL reveals that the decay times  $ \tau_1 $  and  $ \tau_2 $  for the ZCK and KCZ solar cell has been found to be 0.93 ns, 6.90 ns and 0.53 ns, 2.51 ns which show the recombination rate of the photons. Both the samples also retain their stability for a longer time with less gradual decrease for the case of KCZ from ZCK.

KCZ retains 75% of its initial value, while ZCK retains 60% of its initial value after 250 h. Therefore, the study suggests that the metal oxide thin films (  $  KFeO_{2}  $   as HTL and ZnO as ETL) as charge transport layers can provide the high efficiency and stability and hence is a proper choice for future solar cells.
