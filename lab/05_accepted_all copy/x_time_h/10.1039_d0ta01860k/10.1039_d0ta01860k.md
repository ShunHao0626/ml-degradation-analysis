# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are new paradigm in the photovoltaic field because their unparalleled efficiency has increased from 3.8%  $  ^{1}  $  to 25.2%  $  ^{2}  $  in the last decade. The high efficiency of PSCs benefit from their excellent optoelectronic properties including high absorption coefficient values,  $  ^{3}  $  ambipolar charge transport properties,  $  ^{4}  $  tunable energy levels,  $  ^{5}  $  and high charge-carrier diffusion lengths  $  ^{6}  $ . In spite of the rapid growth of photovoltaic performance, the large-scale deployment of perovskite materials is restricted by their stability issues. Perovskite materials are sensible to humidity,  $  ^{7}  $  O  $  _{2}  $ ,  $  ^{8}  $  heat  $  ^{9}  $  and UV light  $  ^{10}  $  which could accelerate material decomposition. Recently, researchers has inclined focus towards scale production of devices under practical application condition.

# Reducing Energy Loss and Stabling the Perovskite/poly(3-hexylthiophene) Interface through a Polyelectrolyte Interlayer

Wenxiao Zhang  $  ^{a,b}  $ , Li Wan  $  ^{a}  $ , Sheng Fu  $  ^{a,b}  $ , Xiaodong Li  $  ^{b,c}  $  and Junfeng Fang  $  ^{a,b,c*}  $

The efficient hole transport materials in n-i-p structured perovskite solar cells (PSCs) were confined to 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) and poly(triarylamine) (PTAA). However, the need for hygroscopic dopants limited the improvement of devices stability. Here, we successfully fabricated stable PSCs using dopant-free poly (3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT) HTL. Through introducing a polyelectrolyte buffer layer (P3CT-BN), the P3HT film morphology, perovskite built-in electric field, surficial defects and the holes transfer speed were all optimized, along with significantly suppressed interfacial recombination. Hence, the efficiency was improved from 13.13% to 19.23%, with a substantially improved  $  V_{OC}  $  from 0.90 V to 1.10 V and FF from 63.1% to 74.2%. Simultaneously, the un-encapsulated devices also exhibited improved stability, retaining 80% of the peak PCE in atmosphere (50% relative humidity) for 2300 h or heating at 85 °C in  $  N_2  $  for 400 h and 79% of the original PCE under simultaneous damp heat (60 °C/ 60 ~ 70% humidity) in air, respectively.

The perovskite active layer is typically sandwiched between electron transport layer (ETL) and hole transport layer (HTL) in both normal (n-i-p) and inverted (p-i-n) structured devices. In addition to charges transport function, these interfacial layers also play an important role in the devices stability. $ ^{11-13} $  For instance, the hygroscopic and acidic poly(3,4 ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonic acid) (PEDOT:PSS) may accelerate degradation of the perovskite layer and corrode the FTO or ITO glass substrate; $ ^{14-15} $  TiO $ _2 $  ETL may influence the stability of PSCs due to its UV instability; $ ^{16} $  In normal structured PSCs, the doped 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) and poly(triarylamine) (PTAA) are two routinely used HTLs to achieve the state-of-the-art performance. $ ^{17-18} $  However, the use of dopants to increase mobility is a double-edged sword. On one hand, the hygroscopic bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI) makes the HTL highly hydrophilic and lithium ion is apt to migrate toward the perovskite layer, showing a tendency towards chemical degradation, negatively influencing the stability of the entire device. $ ^{19} $  The gradual evaporation of tert-butylpyridine (tBP) dopants in HTL and its corrosive reaction with perovskite led to a new threat to the long-term stability of PSCs. $ ^{20-21} $  On the other hand, as a small molecule, the spin-coated Spiro-OMeTAD film usually forms many pin-holes with uneven surface due to its amorphous

Different categories of HTMs such as small molecular,   $  \pi  $  -conjugated polymers, organometallic complexes and inorganic salts, have been developed and employed in normal structured PSCs.  $  ^{25-27}  $

Among them, commercialized poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT) is a potential alternative for its excellent photovoltaic properties,  $  ^{28}  $  low cost,  $  ^{29}  $  ease of fabrication  $  ^{30}  $  and high hydrophobicity. In addition to morphology and energy level, it has been recognized that the polymer molecule of P3HT could not completely infiltrate into the pin-hole traps at perovskite surface, and charges tended to recombine before finding a suitable site for charge transfer.  $  ^{33}  $   Therefore a buffer layer that could interact with perovskite and act as a bridge for holes transfer from perovskite to HTL was necessary for P3HT based PSCs. Here, we conducted the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements combined with the low-energy Ar  $  ^{+}  $   cluster ion sputter depth profiling to probe the interactions on the P3CT-BN/perovskite interface. The ITO/perovskite (A), ITO/perovskite/P3CT-BN (10 nm) (B) and ITO/perovskite/P3HT (10 nm) (C) samples were all etched layer-by-layer with 10 nm thick each step (Figure 2). The “surface” curve indicated the XPS signal before etching for sample A, while indicated the XPS signal after one-step etching (10 nm/step) for sample B and C. It was apparent that even bare perovskite sample showed a shift to high binding energy along with etching owing to the Pb-rich surface of perovskite film synthesized by a two-step method. However, the peak position of sample B was always higher than sample A at “surface” and “-10 nm depth”, and was consistent with sample A at “-20 nm depth”. Meanwhile, the peak position of sample C was higher than sample A at “surface” indicating the interaction between Pb and thiophene

unit in P3HT,  $  ^{48}  $  and was consist with sample A at “-10 nm and -20 nm depth”. The shift of Pb 4f and I 3d XPS signals to higher core-level binding energies of sample B indicated the presence of interaction between P3CT-BN and Pb atom, which still existed under this interface of ~10 nm.  $  ^{44}  $  The peak positions with corresponding values in Figure 2 were also shown in Table S2 and Table S3. These results were in accordance with our previous work that the C=O have strong interaction with bare Pb at perovskite surface (Figure 2e). As a result, we concluded that P3CT-BN could passivate perovskite surface along with dozens of nanometers under it. This sufficient contact and passivation facilitated charges transfer at perovskite/HTL interface, consequently reducing interfacial trap states and recobination rate.

Stability of HTMs in regular structured PSCs is a hot issue due to the necessity of dopants to Spiro-OMeTAD HTL. To detect the effect of dopant-free and hydrophobic P3HT on the device stability, we monitored the long-term stability of devices based P3CT-BN/P3HT HTL under 50% relative humidity at room temperature (Figure S11), which maintained 80% of its original efficiency after 2300 h. The improvement of humidity stability profited from the proof function of P3HT layer, in accordance with the static contact angles (  $  \alpha  $  ) of a deionized water droplet on P3HT (109.63°) (Figure S11b). In addition to humidity stability, thermal stability was also a tricky problem concerning solar cells will typically have to operate at temperatures up to 85 °C. We tested thermal stability of the two devices heating at 85 °C in N  $  _{2}  $  . The bilayer HTL devices retained 80% of its origin efficiency after heating 400 h, whereas the control devices broke down only within 2 days (Figure 5a). It has been reported that small-molecule Spiro-OMeTAD would crystallize and low-boiling tBP additive would volatilize under thermal stress and create pathways that allow for an interaction of the perovskite and the metal electrode (Figure S12). The polymeric P3HT film with high uniformity could avoid this degradation pathway. This P3CT-BN/P3HT based device also showed improved stability under simultaneous damp heat (60 °C/ 60 ~ 70% relative humidity) in

air, remaining 79% of its original performance after 386 h (Figure 5b). Furthermore, the P3CT-BN Incorporated full devices could maintain 96% of the original stable PCE tracked at maximum power point (MPP) for nearly 34 hours, whereas the devices with only P3HT HTL degraded completely (Figure 5c). This phenomenon indicated that the better contact and reduced trap states at perovskite/HTL interface facilitated the device stability against bias and light.

# Conclusions

We inserted a polyelectrolyte buffer layer (P3CT-BN) at perovskite/P3HT interface and used them as dopant-free HTL in regular structured PSCs. The P3CT-BN buffer layer improved P3HT film quality on perovskite and showed a suitable energy level with perovskite. The interaction between P3CT-BN and perovskite reduced perovskite/HTL interfacial trap states and bettered hole transfer at this interface. As a result, the performance of P3CT-BN/P3HT based devices reached 19.23%, with   $  V_{OC}  $   up to 1.10 eV. Furthermore, the dopant-free P3CT-BN/P3HT bilayer also improved the stability of the PSC, especially with 80% of original PCE retained after heating at 85 °C for 400 h in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere, and 79% of the original stable PCE after heating at 60 °C for 386 h in atmosphere with 60 ~70 % humidity, respectively.
