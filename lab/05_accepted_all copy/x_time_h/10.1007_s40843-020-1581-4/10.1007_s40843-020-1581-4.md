# Stable tin perovskite solar cells enabled by widening the time window for crystallization

Zhensheng Dai  $  ^{1,2}  $  , Wentao Tang  $  ^{1,2}  $  , Tao Wang  $  ^{1,2}  $  , Taoyuze Lv  $  ^{1,2,3}  $  , Xinhui Luo  $  ^{1,2}  $  , Danyu Cui  $  ^{1,2}  $  , Ruitian Sun  $  ^{1,2}  $  , Liang Qiao  $  ^{1,2}  $  , Han Chen  $  ^{1,2}  $  , Rongkun Zheng  $  ^{3}  $  , Xudong Yang  $  ^{1,2*}  $   and Liyuan Han  $  ^{1,2}  $

ABSTRACT Tin perovskite solar cells (TPSCs) are the most promising candidates for lead-free perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, the poor crystallization and chemical stability of Sn perovskites are the two challenging issues for further application of TPSCs. Here, we present a strategy to stabilize  $ \mathrm{CH(NH_2)_2SnI_3} $   $ (\mathrm{FASnI}_3) $  perovskite enabled by an amine complex,  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I}\cdot 3\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_2 $  , which can hinder the major degradation issue caused by the oxidation of  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2 + } $  to  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{4 + } $  . The resulting Sn perovskite films exhibit enhanced crystallinity and stability in comparison with those made with conventional inorganic  $ \mathrm{SnF_2} $  additives. Finally, the device achieved a higher external quantum efficiency for charge extraction and a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 9.53\% $  which maintained more than  $ 90\% $  of the initial efficiency after  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  of light soaking under the standard AM 1.5 G solar illumination.

Keywords: lead-free perovskite,   $  FASnI_{3}  $  , perovskite solar cells, crystallization, stability

# INTRODUCTION

In recent years, lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have developed rapidly as a promising photovoltaic technology [1-7]. At present, the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has exceeded  $ 25\% $ , which is close to that of the commercial monocrystalline silicon solar cells [8]. However, the toxicity of lead (Pb) inevitably arouses extensive attention to the promotion of PSCs [9,10]. In this regard, lead-free perovskites have attracted great attention [11,12]. One of the most feasible strategies is to replace Pb with less toxic tin (Sn) for their similar  $ ns^2np^2 $  electronic configuration and coordination

geometry [13,14]. The Sn-based perovskite should have essentially similar semiconductor properties as the Pb-based counterpart [15,16]. In addition, full or partial replacement of Pb with Sn can adjust the band gap in the infrared region, which holds the potential for the application of tandem solar cells [17–21]. However, the poor crystallization and the chemical instability of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   due to the oxidation to   $  Sn^{4+}  $   hindered the development of efficient Sn-based PSCs [16,22].

The stability and crystallinity of the Sn-based perovskite have been successfully improved by various approaches   $  [22–36]  $  . Inorganic compounds like   $  SnF_{2}  $   and   $  SnCl_{2}  $   are the most popular additives for the compensation of oxidized   $  Sn^{2+}  $     $  [23–26]  $  . Metallic tin has been reported for reducing the   $  Sn^{4+}  $   to   $  Sn^{2+}  $   via a comproportionation reaction   $  [27,28]  $  . Application of organic halide to form two-dimensional (2D)-3D hierarchy structure has also been widely reported to effectively protect Sn-based perovskites from oxidation and improve the crystallization   $  [29–35]  $  . Besides, it has been reported that the addition of a certain amount of small organic molecules or polymers, such as trimethylamine (TMA)   $  [22]  $  , potassium salt of hydroquinone sulfonic acid (KHQSA)   $  [36]  $  , 4-fluorobenzohydrazide (FBH)   $  [37]  $  , liquid formic acid (LFA)   $  [38]  $  , 8-hydroxyquinoline (8-HQ)   $  [39]  $  , pentafluorophenoxyethylammonium iodide (FOEI)   $  [40]  $   and poly(vinyl alcohol) (PVA)   $  [41]  $  , can enhance the stability by improving crystallization and reducing defects caused by oxidation. Among them,   $  SnF_{2}  $   is the most commonly used additive in Sn PSCs. In this work, we report a strategy to stabilize   $  \mathrm{CH(NH_{2})_{2}SnI_{3}(FASnI_{3})}  $   perovskite with an amine complex,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I\cdot3CH_{3}NH_{2}  $   (MAI·3MA). Moreover, the device maintained over 90% of its initial PCE after light soaking under AM 1.5 G (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) for 1000 h. This work provides a new simple strategy for fabricating efficient and stable Sn-based PSCs.

# EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

# Materials

The following chemicals were used as obtained from commercial sources, including   $  SnI_{2}  $   (99.99%, Sigma-Aldrich),   $  \mathrm{CH(NH_{2})_{2}I}  $   (FAI; >98%, Tokyo Chemical Industry Co., Japan),   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (MAI, >98%, Tokyo Chemical Industry Co., Japan),   $  SnF_{2}  $   (>99%, Sigma-Aldrich), C60 (99.5%, Sigma-Aldrich), bathocuproine (>99%, Wako), and methylamine (MA, 30%, water, Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd.). All solutions were filtered with a 0.2 μm polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) filter before use. The amine complex of MAI·3MA was synthesized according to our previous work [42].

# Solar cell fabrication

Patterned indium tin oxide (ITO) glass substrates were cleaned in the order of detergent, deionized water, acetone and isopropanol for 20 min by ultra-sonicated. Then, the cleaned ITO substrates were treated by ultraviolet-ozone for 30 min before the deposition of the PEDOT:PSS layer. The perovskite precursor solution composed of 1 mol L  $  ^{-1}  $  SnI  $  _{2}  $  and 1 mol L  $  ^{-1}  $  FAI in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was stirred at room temperature for 2 h. The PSCs were fabricated according to our previous work [43]. In short, the precursor solution was spin-coated on the poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) layer at 1000 r min  $  ^{-1}  $  for 12 s and 5000 r min  $  ^{-1}  $  for 48 s in the glove box. Chlorobenzene (150  $  \mu  $  L) was in-situ dripped onto the perovskite film after 30 s during the second step. Then the perovskite films were annealed at

60°C for 10 s and 100°C for 12 min. Three Sn perovskite films were fabricated and denoted as Sn-control-1 (FASnI  $  _{3}  $  with 5% SnF  $  _{2}  $  in molar ratio), Sn-control-2 (FASnI  $  _{3}  $  with 5% SnF  $  _{2}  $  and MAI in molar ratio), and Sn-target (FASnI  $  _{3}  $  with 5% SnF  $  _{2}  $  and 2% MAI·3MA in molar ratio), respectively.

Finally, 50 nm C60, 8 nm bathocuproine (BCP) and 70 nm Ag electrode were evaporated under high vacuum (  $  <2 \times 10^{-7}  $  Torr). The device areas were defined by a mask with an aperture area of 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  .

# Characterization

The light absorbance spectra were obtained by a Shimadzu ultraviolet–visible (UV-Vis) 3600 spectrophotometer. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) was measured by using a Rigaku Ultima IV diffractometer and Cu Kα radiation. The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images were measured by a Zeiss ultra plus field emission scanning electron microscope. The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra were measured by a Kratos Axis UltraDLD with X-ray source of Al Kα (mono), with the incident angle and take-off angle at 90° and 45°, respectively. The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolution PL (TRPL) spectra were measured by using an FLS1000 photoluminescence spectrometer with excitation at a wavelength of 550 nm. The space charge limited current (SCLC) and Mott-Schottky analysis were performed by using a multifunctional electrochemical analysis instrument (Zahner, Germany) under dark at room temperature. The current-voltage (I-V) curves were measured under forward scan (-0.1 to 0.8 V) or reverse scan (0.8 to -0.1 V) by a fixed step voltage of 20 mV and delay time of 50 ms by a solar simulator with standard AM 1.5 G sunlight (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , WXS-155S-10, Wa-com Denso). The aperture area was defined by a mask of 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  . Monochromatic incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra were measured by a monochromatic incident light of   $  1 \times 10^{16}  $   photons cm  $  ^{-2}  $   in director current mode (CEP2000BX, Bunko-Keiki). The light intensity of the solar simulator was calibrated by a standard silicon solar cell. The solar cells were firstly encapsulated in a nitrogen-filled glovebox by cavity glass and UV-curable glue for the stability test.

Finally, we investigated the operational stability of the three sets of cells in the air (humidity  $ \sim 20\% -25\% $ , temperature  $ \sim 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ). The encapsulated Sn-target film-based cells maintained over  $ 90\% $  of its initial efficiency under simulated illumination of AM  $ 1.5\mathrm{G} $  ( $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $ ) after  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  of light soaking (the normalized efficiency attenuation was depicted in Fig. 4d red line). In contrast, the Sn-control-1 and Sn-control-2 films-based devices both lost more than  $ 60\% $  of their initial efficiencies within  $ 500\mathrm{h} $  (Fig. 4d, black and blue lines, respectively). The variations of  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ ,  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  and FF are also presented in Fig. S10. The enhanced stability of the target-device may be explained by a lower defect concentration and an improved crystallinity [40]. The target solar cells with fewer defects demonstrated excellent operational stability under light soaking, which is consistent with previous findings [54-56].

# CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we presented a strategy to stabilize  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  perovskite with an amine complex. This solution provides

very encouraging results, alleviating several problematic aspects of the Sn-based thin film perovskites. The additive allows for an enhanced crystallinity, hindered oxidation rate and lower defect concentration, thereby enhancing the efficiency and stability of tin perovskite solar cells (TPSCs). Despite those remarkable improvements, we still draw the reader's attention to the fact that the defect density in our optimal TPSC  $ (2.63\times 10^{16}\mathrm{cm}^{-3}) $  is still higher than that of lead-based PSCs  $ (10^{13} - 10^{14}\mathrm{cm}^{-3}) $  [57,58]. This is most likely one of the main reasons why the efficiency of TPSCs still lags behind that of their Pb-based counterpart to date. With promising results from the additive strategy to improve stability and morphology, we suggest that the next major step to project TPSCs forward is to further reduce the defect density within TPSCs. With that goal in mind, we should aim to enhance the efficiency up to  $ 15\% $  with high operational stability, in order to provide a viable alternative to lead-based PSCs.

# 拓宽溶液到结晶的时间窗口以获得稳定的锡基钙钛矿太阳能电池

戴臻盛  $  ^{1,2}  $  ，唐文涛  $  ^{1,2}  $  ，王涛  $  ^{1,2}  $  ，吕陶玉赜  $  ^{1,2,3}  $  ，罗新惠  $  ^{1,2}  $  ，崔丹钰  $  ^{1,2}  $  ，孙瑞田  $  ^{1,2}  $  ，乔亮  $  ^{1,2}  $  ，陈汉  $  ^{1,2}  $  ，郑荣坤  $  ^{3}  $  ，杨旭东  $  ^{1,2*}  $  ，韩礼元  $  ^{1,2}  $

摘要 锡基钙钛矿太阳能电池(TPSC)是最具有应用前景的一类无铅钙钛矿太阳能电池(PSC). 然而, 锡基钙钛矿结晶性差和化学不稳定性是制约其进一步应用的两个挑战性问题. 本文中, 我们提出了一种策略, 通过胺络合物  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}\cdot1\cdot3CH_{3}NH_{2}  $  有效抑制了由  $  Sn^{2+}  $  被氧化为  $  Sn^{4+}  $  引起的钙钛矿降解问题, 来获得更稳定的  $  \mathrm{CH(NH_{2})_{2}SnI_{3}(FASnI_{3})钙钛矿薄膜}  $ . 与仅使用常规的  $  SnF_{2}  $  添加剂制备的锡基钙钛矿薄膜相比, 添加  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}\cdot1\cdot3CH_{3}NH_{2}  $  后制备的薄膜的结晶性和稳定性得到增强. 最终, 所制备的器件获得了更高的外量子效率和9.53%的光电转换效率, 在标准的AM 1.5G模拟太阳光下老化1000 h后, 其效率仍然保持了初始效率的90%以上.
