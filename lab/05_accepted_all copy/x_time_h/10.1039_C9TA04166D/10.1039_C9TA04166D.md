# Prompting power conversion efficiency of perovskite solar cells to  $ 17.7\% $  with Indolo[3,2-b]carbazole dopant-free hole transporting material by improving its spatial configuration

Bin Cai  $  ^{a}  $  , Xichuan Yang  $  ^{*a}  $  , Xiaoqing Jiang  $  ^{a}  $  , Ze Yu  $  ^{*a}  $  , Anders Hagfeldt  $  ^{b}  $   and Licheng Sun  $  ^{ac}  $

The development of facile synthesized, dopant-free hole-transporting materials (HTMs) with high efficiency is of great significance for the potential application of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, we report two novel indolo[3,2-b]carbazole (ICZ) based small molecules via three-steps reactions in high yield without using expensive catalysts, namely C201 and C202, and further apply them as dopant-free HTMs in PSCs. Compared with C201, C202 contains two additional biphenylamino groups to improve its spatial configuration. It is found that the interplay between the molecular geometry and the aggregation behavior can exert a great influence on the film formation property and thus on the device performance. Strikingly, the champion devices employing C202 as HTMs deliver a much higher PCE of up to  $ 17.7\% $ , which is substantially higher than that of devices containing C201  $ (8.7\%) $  under  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $  illumination (AM 1.5G). It is revealed that the C202 capping layer exhibits more homogeneous and uniform surface morphology as compared to that of C201, which effectively reduces the charge recombination losses and facilitates the charge extraction, leading to a much-enhanced photovoltaic performance. This has been the first example of ICZ core-based small molecules as dopant-free HTMs in PSCs. Moreover, the PSCs containing C202 as HTMs also exhibited good long-term stability in ambient conditions  $ (40\% $  RH) as compared to devices with doped Spiro-OMeTAD, due largely to the hydrophobic nature of C202 that prevented moisture from destroying the perovskite film. This work offers a new avenue for developing cost-effective and stable HTMs for PSCs and other optoelectronic devices.

# Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs), as one of the most promising alternative energy sources, have attracted tremendous research attention due to their swift raising power conversion efficiencies (PCEs).  $  ^{1,2}  $   Normally, the light-absorbing perovskite layer is sandwiched between an electron transporting layer (ETL) and a hole-transporting layer (HTL). HTL plays an essential role in extracting and transporting holes generated by the excited perovskite under light. Moreover, it can also prevent the electron recombination processes at the interface, which deteriorate the solar cell performance.

Hole-transporting materials (HTMs) can be generally categorized into small organic molecules, inorganic materials, and polymers. Inorganic hole transport materials usually possess high hole mobility, however, it is difficult to deposit them on the top of the perovskite layer due to the limited choice of fabrication methods and solvents.  $  ^{3}  $   As a result, they

are typically utilized in the "p-i-n structure" PSCs, where the HTLs are underneath the perovskite layer.  $  ^{4,5}  $   Polymers that own excellent film-forming abilities, suffer from a relatively poor batch-to-batch synthesis reproducibility. Taken together, small organic molecule HTMs are easy to be modified, purified and solution processable.  $  ^{6,7}  $   To date, 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) is the most used small organic molecule HTM. However, the synthesis procedure of spiro-OMeTAD is rather tedious, correlated with high production cost. Moreover, the hole mobility of pristine spiro-OMeTAD is quite low (  $  10^{-5}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $   cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ).  $  ^{8}  $   Dopants like bis(trifluoromethane) sulfonimide lithium (LiTFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP) are needed in order to improve its hole mobility. LiTFSI acts as a count ion during the oxidation reaction between spiro-OMeTAD and O  $  _{2}  $  .  $  ^{9}  $   Nevertheless, LiTFSI is rather moisture-absorbing and Li  $  ^{+}  $   can easily migrate into the perovskite layer,  $  ^{10}  $   which are detrimental to the long-term device stability. In addition, t-BP must be added to improve the solubility of LiTFSI. But, it has been reported that t-BP can also accelerate the deterioration of the perovskite.  $  ^{11}  $   Although tremendous efforts have been devoted to replacing spiro-OMeTAD, most of them are still either needed to be doped to improve their hole mobilities,  $  ^{12,13}  $   or only giving a comparatively low PCEs.  $  ^{14-18}  $   Thus, the development of facile synthesized and high-performing dopant-free HTMs is highly demanding in view of future application of PSCs.

Introducing “donor-acceptor” (D-A) structure and rigid conjugated core are two of the most effective strategies to improve the hole mobility, and thus developing the dopant-free HTMs.  $  ^{19-24}  $   Indolo[3,2-b]carbazole (ICZ) core is a kind of rigid conjugated electron-rich aromatic ring, which has been successfully applied in organic light-emitting diodes (OLED),  $  ^{25}  $   organic thin-film transistors (OTFT)  $  ^{26,27}  $   and dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs)  $  ^{28}  $  , due to its outstanding photo-electric characteristics. With this regard, ICZ-based HTMs have been attempted in PSCs with dopants, the performance of which however are not satisfied.  $  ^{29}  $   Considering its facile synthesis, low cost and flexible modification, the potential of ICZ-based dopant-free HTMs in PSCs are still unexplored so far. Here, we designed and synthesized two ICZ core-based small molecule materials, coded as C201 and C202, through only three-steps reactions with a high yield (Scheme 1). Compared with C201, C202 contains two additional biphenylamino groups to improve its spatial configuration. These two materials were further deployed as dopant-free HTMs in PSCs. First, a substituted indole and a substituted aldehyde produced an ICZ frame catalyzed by Lewis acid as well as a step of oxidation.  $  ^{30}  $   It is worth noting that we can easily modify the ICZ ring precisely by introducing various functional groups from the starting materials. Then, the final HTMs can be obtained through an Ullmann reaction. Both steps can guarantee a high yield above 80% without using any expensive catalysts. UV-vis absorption spectra of the synthesized HTMs in dichloromethane solutions and in thin films are given in Fig. 2a. The UV-vis absorptions of C201 and C202 in thin films show bathochromic-shifts at the maximum absorption wavelength (  $  \lambda_{max}  $  ) of 8 and 3 nm, respectively, as compared to those in dilute solutions (the perpendicular configuration spiro-OMeTAD shows no bathochromic shift, shown in Fig. S1). The results indicate that C201 holds a stronger intermolecular   $  \pi-\pi  $   stacking, which is in accordance with the DFT results. From the formula:   $  E_{g} = 1240/\lambda_{edge}  $   (  $  E_{g}  $   stands for bandgap,   $  \lambda_{edge}  $   is the UV-vis spectra absorption edge), the bandgap of C201 and C202 are calculated to be 2.77 and 2.74 eV, respectively. The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels of C201 and C202 are -5.27 and -5.23 eV, respectively, calculated from the first oxidation potential of the cyclic voltammetry (CV) diagram, which are performed in dichloromethane solutions with ferrocene as internal reference (Fig. 2b). Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) was also carried out to get a further understanding of the HOMO level in the film. (Fig. S2, ESI†) The HOMO levels of C201 and C202 in the film are calculated to be -5.38 and -5.12 eV, respectively, with the equation   $  \varphi = 21.21 \, eV - (E_{\text{cutoff}} - E_{\text{fermi}})  $  . Benefited from its stronger intermolecular π-π stacking, C201 exhibits a slightly higher hole mobility.

The dark J-V characteristics of PSCs with C201 and C202 as HTMs were further studied. Dark current is the parasitic current, whose direction is opposite to the photo-generated current, while the swift change of its intensity can reflect the strength of built-in voltage   $  (V_{\mathrm{bi}})  $  . As presented in the Fig. 5a, C201-based devices show approximately 10 times larger dark current than that of C202-based devices in the range of   $  -0.5 \sim 0.8  $   V. Estimated from the voltage that begins to depart from the diffusion current, C201-based devices show a   $  V_{bi}  $   around 0.85 V, while C202-based devices exhibit a   $  V_{bi}  $   around 1.0 V. This can be attributed to the improved electron blocking capability of the more homogeneous C202 capping layer, which can effectively prevent direct contact between the Au counter electrode and the perovskite. This in turn will reduce the charge recombination losses, which accounts for higher   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF obtained for C202-based devices.

The stability of PSC devices based on C202 were further examined in ambient environment with a relative humidity around 40% at temperature of ~22 °C, stored in the dark without encapsulation. For comparison, the stability of PSCs containing spiro-OMeTAD doped with Li-TFSI and t-BP was also tested under the identical conditions (J-V characteristics shown in Fig. S7). As presented in Fig. 6, devices with C202 as dopant-free HTMs exhibited relatively good stability, remaining ~72% of its initial PCE after 288 h. In contrast, the devices using doped spiro-OMeTAD showed a much faster degradation rate, remaining only 23% of its original efficiency during the testing period. Water contact angle measurements were used to evaluate the moisture-resisting ability of the HTMs as shown in the inset of Fig. 6. The water contact angle of C202 (89.5°) is larger than that of doped spiro-OMeTAD (72.8°), suggesting the high moisture resistivity of C202. The hydrophobic characteristic of C202 can prevent the moisture attacking the perovskite film, which led to an improved ambient stability.

# Conclusions

In summary, we for the first time developed two new ICZ core-based small molecule materials as dopant-free HTMs in PSCs. They can be easily synthesized though only three steps reactions without using any expensive catalysts. Impressively, the best devices constructed with C202 showed a much higher PCE of up to 17.7%, which is considerably higher than that of devices based on C201 (8.7%) under one sun illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , AM 1.5G). This has been the highest efficiency for ICZ core-based HTMs in PSCs to date. The much-enhanced performance of C202-based devices should be attributed to the reduced charge recombination loss and improved charge extraction capability, strongly correlated to its more uniform film formation on top of the perovskite layer due to its less inclination to aggregation. Moreover, the PSCs containing C202 as HTMs also exhibited good long-term stability in ambient conditions (40% RH) as compared to devices with doped spiro-OMeTAD, due largely to the hydrophobic nature of C202 that prevented moisture from destroying the perovskite film. The present work demonstrates a new design strategy for developing cost-effective and stable HTMs for PSCs in the future.
