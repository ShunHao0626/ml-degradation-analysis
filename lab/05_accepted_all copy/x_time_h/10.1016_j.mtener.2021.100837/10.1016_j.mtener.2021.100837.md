# 1. Introduction

In recent years, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted extensive attention in view of their low production cost and high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and are considered as the most promising technology of the new-generation photovoltaic   $  [1-5]  $  . The prominent performance of PSCs is mainly ascribed to the remarkable physical properties of halide lead perovskites, such as weak exciton binding energy, wide light absorption range, and long charge carrier lifetime   $  [6-9]  $  . Currently, PSCs have achieved a certified efficiency of 25.5%, which is comparable with commercial photovoltaic technologies such as crystalline silicon   $  [10]  $  . Nevertheless, the long-term stability still hinders the commercialization process because of the sensitivity of perovskite materials to light, heat, and moisture   $  [11,12]  $  . Therefore, how to prolong the operational life of PSCs has become an urgent problem to be solved at present   $  [13,14]  $  .

# ABSTRACT

Recently, mixed cation-halide perovskites have become particularly interesting for high-performance perovskite solar cells (PSCs) owing to their near-ideal bandgap. Nevertheless, mixed cation-halide perovskites prepared via the existing methods are often subject to high defect density and poor stability, especially the sequential deposition methods in view of the difficulty of incorporating cesium cations (stabilizer). Herein, a simple and effective approach of   $  \delta  $  -CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br seed—assisting sequential deposition is developed. It is revealed that introducing   $  \delta  $  -CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br seeds into PbI  $  _{2}  $   film could not only effectively incorporate Cs  $  ^{+}  $   into the mixed cation-halide perovskite film but also afford   $  \delta  $   phase crystal lattices to promote the growth of   $  \alpha  $   phase perovskites during annealing. Such a process can prepare high-quality perovskite film with larger grain size, lower defect density, and longer photoluminescence carrier lifetime (1658 ns). In addition, the thermal stability and humidity stability of the perovskite film are significantly boosted. Consequently, the optimized device delivers a champion efficiency of 21.62%, with an average efficiency of 1.5% higher than that of the control device. In addition, the unencapsulated device exhibits remarkable operational stability, retaining ~92% of the initial efficiency after aging for 1000 h in ambient air. This work provides insight for further preparing the high-quality perovskite film and improving the performance of PSCs.

Although there are tremendous reports that demonstrate that both device encapsulation and interface engineering could improve long-term stability of PSCs   $  [15,16]  $  , the intrinsic unstable property of perovskite has not been alleviated   $  [17,18]  $  . As a polycrystalline film, the intrinsic instability of perovskite mainly derives from the two aspects: (i) the abnormal defect density at grain boundaries   $  [19,20]  $   and (ii) the intrinsic volatility of organic cations   $  [11,12]  $  . The defects at grain boundaries could enhance nonradiative recombination of charges, shorten the carrier life, and stimulate device degradation pathways   $  [21,22]  $  . The organic cations of perovskite could be prone to irreversible degradation under moisture, thermal stress, and UV irradiation stress, thereby accelerating degradation of the device performances   $  [23–25]  $  . To address these issues, solvent engineering   $  [26,27]  $   and additive engineering   $  [28,29]  $   have been devoted to fabricating perovskite film with minimal grain boundaries by controlling crystal nucleation and grain growth. In addition, incorporating   $  Cs^{+}  $  into mixed cation-halide perovskite is also an effective strategy to realize long-term stability of PSCs because of the much stronger Cs–I ionic bond than the H–I bond   $  [30,31]  $  . So far, Cs-doped mixed cation-halide perovskites usually are prepared by the antisolvent dripping technology, and their composition is modulated by introducing CsI into the precursor solution. However, the process of dripping antisolvent is hard to control, which is not conducive to the preparation of the large-area device [33]. In contrast, the sequential deposition method has great advantage for the preparation of the large-area device because it could much easily control the crystallization of perovskite. The disadvantage is that  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $  is difficult to integrate into perovskite film because of low solubility of inorganic cesium salt in isopropanol (IPA) [34]. Hence, there is an urgent need to develop a facile and effective strategy to not only incorporate  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $  into the mixed cation-halide perovskites via sequential deposition but also achieve grain growth.

In this work, we successfully introduced a  $ \delta $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br seed-assisted sequential deposition (SAS) approach to prepare a micron-sized grain and stable Cs-doped mixed cation-halide perovskite film. The introduction of  $ \delta $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br seeds in PbI $ _2 $  solution is beneficial to modulate the property of PbI $ _2 $  film and further control the crystal growth of perovskite film. The PSCs based on the high electronic quality Cs-doped mixed cation-halide perovskite film show reduced defect-assisted recombination rate, enhanced charge transport kinetics, and improved build-in potential in accordance with a series of independent experimental characterizations. As a result, the optimized PSCs deliver a champion PCE of 21.62%, with an average efficiency of 1.5% higher than that of the control device in accordance with statistic PCE distributions of 60 devices of each type. Moreover, these devices could sustain 92% of their initial PCE after aging for 1000 h in ambient air.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Solution preparation

The   $  SnO_{2}  $   precursor solution was prepared by adding 1 mL of the 15 wt%   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloidal dispersion into 5 mL of deionized water. To prepare   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution, 390 mg of CsI, 346 mg of   $  PbI_{2}  $ , and 275 mg of   $  PbBr_{2}  $   were dissolved in 1 mL of dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO); the obtained solution was stirred at 60 °C for 6 h and aged for 48 h. For preparing   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution, 691.5 mg of   $  PbI_{2}  $   was dissolved in 1 mL of mixed solvent (  $  V_{DMF}: V_{DMSO} = 9:1  $  ) and stirred for 2 h. The mixed organic amine salt solution was prepared by adding 360 mg of formamidine iodide (FAI), 25.4 mg of methylammonium iodide (MAI), and 36 mg of methylammonium chloride (MACl) into 4 mL of IPA. For the preparation of Spiro-OMeTAD mixture solution, 72.3 mg of Spiro-OMeTAD powder was dissolved in 1 mL of chlorobenzene (CB) with the addition of 17.5 μL of Li-TFSI stock solution (520 mg/mL in acetonitrile) and 28.8 μL of 4-tertbutylpyridine. For preparing   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution containing the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor (the molar ratio of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br/PbI_{2} = x\%  $  ), 691.5 mg of   $  PbI_{2}  $   powder and 10x μL of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution were added into mixed solvent (900 μL of N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) and 100-10x μL of DMSO). The molar ratio of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor in   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution was controlled at 0%, 4%, 6%, 8%, and 10% for preparing perovskite samples.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

The indium tin oxide (ITO) glass substrates  $ (15\Omega \mathrm{sq}^{-1}) $  were ultrasonically washed with isopropyl alcohol for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $ . The  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  precursor solution was spin-coated on the UV-treated ITO glass substrate at  $ 4000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 20~s $  and then dried at  $ 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $ .

The perovskite absorber was fabricated by the sequential deposition method in the   $  N_{2}  $   glove box. First, as-prepared   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution or   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution containing the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor was spin-coated onto   $  SnO_{2}  $   film at a rate of 1500 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 80 °C for 1 min. After that, the mixed organic amine salt solution was dropped onto the   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer and held for 5 s before being spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 30 s. The substrate was then annealed at 150 °C for 15 min in ambient air with a controlled humidity (30–40%). Spiro-OMeTAD solution was spin-coated on perovskite film at a rate of 4000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, an 80-nm thick gold layer was deposited by thermal evaporation. When measuring, a 0.1 cm  $  ^{2}  $   non-reflective mask was used to define the accurate active cell area.

# 2.3. Characterizations

X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were recorded with a Regaku D/Max-2500 diffractometer equipped using a Cu Kα1 radiation (  $  \lambda = 1.54056  $   Å). The X-ray photoelectron spectrum (XPS) measurements were performed on an ESCALab220i-XL electron spectrometer (VG Scientific) using 300W Al Kα radiation. Top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images were obtained using Hitachi S-4800. The surface roughness was measured by atomic force microscopy (AFM) (Nanoscope V, Vecco) in tapping mode. The ultraviolet–visible (UV–vis) absorption spectra were measured on a UV–vis spectrophotometer (UH4150, Hitachi). The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL (TRPL) spectra were measured on the Edinburgh Instrument FLS 980. J-V characteristics were measured with a solar simulator (450 W Model 94023A, Newport) with an AM 1.5 solar spectrum filter and a Keithley 2420 source meter. Light intensity was adjusted using an National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL)-certified Si solar cell. For the external quantum efficiency (EQE) measurement, the ORIEL IQE-200 measurement system was used. The confocal laser scanning microscope (CLSM) images were taken by confocal laser scanning microscopy (Leica TCS SP8 CARS), the light source was a 405-nm laser diode, and the detection range of PL emission was 410–450 nm. For humidity stability tests, the perovskite films were stored in ambient air atmosphere (25 °C, 60–70% R.H., and dark). For thermal stability tests, the perovskite films were kept at 120 °C in N₂ condition. For air stability tests, the unencapsulated devices were stored in ambient air atmosphere (25 °C, 25–30% R.H., and dark).

# 3. Results and discussion

First, the prepared  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  solution was spin-coated onto  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  film and annealed at  $ 80^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 1\mathrm{min} $  to obtain  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film with weak crystallization. To expound the effect of the SAS approach on the growth of final perovskite crystals, SEM was used to investigate their microstructure evolution. Fig. S3 shows the top-view SEM images of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   films containing different amounts of   $  \delta-CsPbI_{2}Br  $   seeds. The grain size of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   films with   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   is slightly larger than that of without   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , which contributes to the diffusion of the organic compounds into the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film. Fig. 2a–e and Fig. S4 display morphology of final perovskite films. The perovskite film w/o   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   appears as irregular surface with small grain size and many voids. After introduction of   $  \delta-CsPbI_{2}Br  $   seeds in the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film, the grain size of the perovskite film is significantly enlarged, and the surface is more regular without any voids. The increased grain size leads to larger surface roughness (41.9 vs. 34.3 nm) indicated

by AFM measurements (Fig. S5), which could accelerate the charge transfer between the perovskite and hole transport layers [41]. Moreover, it can be noted that on increasing the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  concentration, the average grain size enlarges gradually (Fig. 2f), demonstrating that  $ \delta $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br seeds in the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film are beneficial to the growth of perovskite grains. Considering that grain boundaries are a dominant source of defects in perovskite film and can enhance non-radiative recombination of photogenerated charges [42], the enlarged grain size is expected to boost the performance of PSCs. In addition, the mechanism of grain growth induced by the SAS approach is also expounded briefly. It is known that the crystallization process of perovskite is divided into three stages: (i) precursor solution, (ii) hexagonal  $ \delta $  phase, and (iii) complex phases (intermediate phases and  $ \alpha $  phase) [43]. Hence, we suggest that the  $ \delta $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br phase existing in the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film could partly remove the first stage of perovskite crystallization ( $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 \rightarrow \delta $  phase) and thereby allow  $ \alpha $ -phase perovskites to have enough time for crystal growth and eventually to form larger grains.

To investigate the effect of the introduction of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  on the surface composition and electronic states of the perovskite film, we took XPS of the two thin films for comparison. The I 3d peaks of the two films have almost no shift (Fig. 3b). Fig. 3c displays the high-resolution spectra of the Pb 4f. For the film w/o  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $ , except the Pb  $ 4\mathrm{f}_{7/2} $  and Pb  $ 4\mathrm{f}_{5/2} $  peaks located at 138.2 and  $ 143.1\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively, there are two tiny  $ \mathrm{Pb}^0 $  peaks located at 136.5 and  $ 141.4\mathrm{eV} $ , which would serve as nonradiative recombination centers [45]. When  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  is introduced into the perovskite film, the Pb 4f peaks are almost unchanged, whereas the signal peaks of metallic Pb disappear, confirming that the SAS approach could effectively reduce non-radiative recombination centers of the perovskite film.

To confirm that the SAS approach could improve the stability of the perovskite film, we carry out two aging tests: (i) the thermal test at  $ 120^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 3\mathrm{h} $  in the  $ \mathrm{N}_2 $  glove box and (ii) the humidity test at  $ 60\% $  RH for  $ 24\mathrm{h} $ . Fig. 5a and b shows the XRD patterns of the two perovskite films before and after aging tests. In the thermal test, for the film with  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $ , the peak intensities of the perovskite phase and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  phase are nearly the same before and after aging, indicating that the decomposition could be ignored. By

contrast, the stronger peak of   $  PbI_{2}  $   and the weaker peak of the perovskite demonstrate that the decomposition of the film w/o   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   is very serious. In the humidity test, the decomposition of the film w/o   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   is also more terrible than that of the film with   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   by comparing the peak intensity of the perovskite phase and   $  PbI_{2}  $   phase. Figs. S12–13 present photographs of the perovskite films as a function of aging time under different conditions. It is clearly seen that the humidity and thermal stability of the perovskite film could be significantly improved with the incorporation of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ . These clearly indicate that the SAS approach could greatly improve the stability of the perovskite film, which is attributed to the decreased grain boundaries and defect densities, as well as the incorporation of   $  Cs^{+}  $ . Fig. 5c and Fig. S14 display the air stability test of the unencapsulated devices at 25 °C and 25–30% R.H. The device with   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   exhibits splendid stability, preserving 92% of its initial PCE after aging for 1000 h. The device w/o   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   only retains 64% of its initial PCE.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, we demonstrated a simple but effective approach of  $ \delta $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br SAS for improving the efficiency and stability of PSCs. The presence of  $ \delta $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br seeds could inhibit the formation of Pb $ ^0 $  defects and facilitate the growth of perovskite. The final obtained perovskite film possessed the micron-sized grains, the microsecond-range carrier lifetimes, and lower trap-state density. Moreover, the thermal stability and humidity stability of the perovskite film were significantly boosted. Consequently, the fabricated PSCs delivered a champion PCE of 21.62%. The unencapsulated device retained 92% of the initial PCE after aging for 1000 h in ambient air. This work provides an encouraging
