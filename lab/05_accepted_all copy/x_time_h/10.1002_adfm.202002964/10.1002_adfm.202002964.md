# FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -Based Perovskite Solar Cells Employing Hexyl-Based Ionic Liquid with an Efficiency Over 20% and Excellent Long-Term Stability

Seckin Akin,  $  ^{*}  $   Erdi Akman, and Savas Sonmezoglu

Formamidinium lead triiodide (FAPbI $ _3 $ )-based perovskite materials are of interest for photovoltaics in view of their close-to-ideal bandgap, allowing absorption of photons over a broad solar spectrum. However, FAPbI $ _3 $ -based materials suffer from a notorious phase transition from the photoactive black phase ( $ \alpha $ -FAPbI $ _3 $ ) to nonperovskite yellow phase ( $ \delta $ -FAPbI $ _3 $ ) under ambient conditions. This transition dramatically reduces light absorption, thus, degrading the photovoltaic performance and stability of ensuring solar cells. In this study, 1-hexyl-3-methylimidazolium iodide (HMII) ionic liquid (IL) is employed as an additive for the first time in FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite to overcome the above-mentioned issues. HMII incorporation facilitates the grain coarsening of FAPbI $ _3 $  crystal owing to its high-polarity and high-boiling point, which yields liquid domains between neighboring grains to reduce the activation energy of the grain-boundary migration. As a result, the FAPbI $ _3 $  active layer exhibits micron-sized grains with substantially suppressed parasitic traps with an Urbach energy reduced by 2 meV. Hence, the resulting perovskite solar cell achieves an efficiency of 20.6% with notable increase in open circuit voltage ( $ V_{OC} $ ) of 80 mV compared with HMII-free cells (17.1%). More importantly, the HMII-doped FAPbI $ _3 $ -based cells show a striking enhancement in shelf-stability under high humidity and thermal stress, retaining >80% of their initial efficiencies at 60 ± 10% relative humidity and ≈95% at 65 °C.

The stabilization of   $  \alpha  $  -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   at relatively low temperature is one of the main challenges that PSC community is facing currently. Either a partial substitution of FA  $  ^{+}  $   with different cations such as MA  $  ^{+}  $   and/or Cs  $  ^{+}  $   or by introducing Br  $  ^{-}  $   or Cl  $  ^{-}  $   into the FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   lattice was reported as efficient attempts to stabilize the   $  \alpha  $  -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   phase under ambient conditions. However, the incorporation of such cations/anions affects the spectral response and/or the operational stability of corresponding PSCs.  $  ^{[16,21]}  $   In an interesting study, Seok and co-workers reported the intramolecular exchange process by starting with a film of lead iodide and dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) and then exchanging the DMSO with formamidinium iodide. This approach led to the formation of coarse-grained microstructures and flat surfaces without residual PbI  $  _{2}  $ , thereby achieving an efficiency exceeding 20%. However, there was no information about the stability of corresponding devices.  $  ^{[16]}  $

# 2. Results and Discussion

The  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $ -based perovskite films at a concentration of  $ 1.35\mathrm{M} $  were prepared by dissolving equimolar amount of FAI and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  precursors in DMSO/DMF (1:4 v/v) solvent. The solar cells were fabricated using the following architecture (Figure 1a): glass/FTO/c- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2/\mathrm{mp-TiO}_2/ $ perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD/Au. To the perovskite precursor solution, HMII IL precursor with different molar ratios ranging from 0.1 to  $ 2.0\mathrm{mol}\% $  were added to evaluate the effects of HMII on the optoelectronic properties of  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  perovskite (Figure 1b,c).

X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis was employed to figure out the influence of HMII on the crystallization kinetics of the perovskite film. As seen in Figure 2a, peak positions remain unaltered with the addition of HMII due to its larger molecular size. However, residual  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  peak at  $ 12.72^{\circ} $  appears in the HMII-free control film while no  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  peak is observed in the presence of HMII, indicating the complete consumption of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . Moreover, the substantial increase in peak intensity suggests an improved perovskite crystallinity, which agrees well with the severely enlarged-grains shown in scanning electron microscopy (SEM) micrographs in Figure 2b,c. SEM micrographs clearly showed a uniform film morphology with micron sized-grains compared to that of the reference, indicating that HMII interacts with  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  film to facilitate an efficient crystal growth. The interaction of the HMII IL within the FAPbI $ _3 $  film was confirmed by the Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy analysis. FTIR spectra of annealed films reveal that HMII molecules are adsorbed on the surface of the FAPbI $ _3 $  nanoparticles as indicated by the transmission bands in the region of 3200 to 2800 cm $ ^{-1} $  (Figure 2d,e). This can be assigned to C–H stretching of the imidazolium ring. Additionally, it is noteworthy that the stretching vibration peak at 1164 cm $ ^{-1} $  assigning to C–N bond in HMII shifts to lower wavenumber of 1156 cm $ ^{-1} $  in FAPbI $ _3 $ +HMII structure. This significant shift indicates a weakened C–N bond caused by the bonding interaction between N atoms of HMII and uncoordinated Pb $ ^{2+} $ . $ ^{[32]} $  Overall, FTIR results assure the successful inclusion of

IL and suggest a passivation effect of HMII toward the uncoordinated cations in perovskite, which is beneficial for inhibiting the nonradiative recombination and facilitating the charge transport process.  $  ^{[33,34]}  $

In order to evaluate the effects of HMII additive on the optoelectronic properties of FAPbI $ _3 $  film, we conducted a series of optical characterizations in the presence/absence of HMII. A mesoscopic architecture consisting of FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $ /mp-TiO $ _2 $ /FAPbI $ _3 $ /spiro-OMeTAD/Au was employed for the fabrication of PSCs in this work. The space-charge limited current (SCLC) method is also employed to quantitatively assess the trap density ( $ n_{\text{trap}} $ ) in the corresponding films.  $ J-V $  characteristics of FTO/TiO $ _2 $ /perovskite/PCBM/Au based electron-only devices were collected in

dark conditions and   $  n_{trap}  $   values were analyzed by using the equation:   $  n_{trap} = 2\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon V_{TFL}/eL^{2}  $  , where   $  V_{TFL}  $   is the trap filled limit voltage,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the permittivity of vacuum,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative dielectric constant of the FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  , e is the charge of electron, and L is the thickness of corresponding perovskite layer. As shown in Figure 4f, the   $  V_{TFL}  $   values were found to be 1.29 and 0.34 V for the control and IL-doped films, respectively, indicating that the calculated   $  n_{trap}  $   of the optimized pervoskite film is   $  4.82 \times 10^{14}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  , which is only a fourth of the trap density of the HMII-free FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   with a value of   $  1.83 \times 10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  . This result indicates a lower trap-assisted Shockley–Read–Hall monomolecular recombination.  $  ^{[46,47]}  $   The obtained lower trap density in the IL-modified FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   can be associated from the improved film quality and reduced nonradiative recombination in the presence of HMII additive and is consistent with the previous PL spectra, which is a major contributor to higher   $  V_{OC}  $   values. The lower density of traps is likely one of the causes why the J-V hysteresis is mostly eliminated in the PSCs. These result trends agreed well with XRD patterns, absorption spectra, and PL curves discussed above.

Although   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite exhibits dramatic advances in photovoltaics, overcoming moisture instability still remains a challenge for the large-scale deployment of perovskite devices. The stability in time of the perovskite layers was monitored by optical and structural methods under ambient conditions to evaluate the potential of HMII additive in device stability. XRD patterns of aged control film reveal the presence of the non-perovskite δ-phase diffraction peaks at 11.7° and 16.3°, while these peaks are absent in the HMII-based perovskite films after 10 days of storage in ambient air of relative humidity (RH) of about 60 ± 10% (Figure S8, Supporting Information). This correlates with the SEM measurements of the corresponding films after same aging process (Figure S9, Supporting Information). The phase stability of the HMII-containing   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   films was further verified by the absorbance spectroscopy. As clearly shown in Figure S10 (Supporting Information), pristine   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   decomposes totally into yellow phase while the IL-treated film indicates much better ambient stability. The digital pictures of aged films are presented in Figure S11 (Supporting Information). The yellow color in the control film represents the phase degradation, which is due to a fractional decomposition to   $  PbI_{2}  $  ,  $  ^{[49]}  $   as we infer from XRD measurements. Such a substantial improvement in film stability can be ascribed to the high hydrophobicity of long alkyl chain employing HMII IL,  $  ^{[50]}  $   which prevents the perovskite film from moisture penetration (Figure S12, Supporting Information). All these results show that HMII IL exhibits a significant potential not only on photovoltaic performance of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  -based PSCs but also on long-term stability of ensuing devices.

In the light of these observations, the stability of the modified devices under different aging conditions was investigated by comparing with the control cells. First, shelf stability test was conducted at   $  60 \pm 10\%  $   RH without encapsulation. 4 devices were monitored during the aging process. As shown in Figure 5a, the control cells degraded to 70% of their initial efficiencies just after 30 h, whereas the devices with HMII additive maintained more than 80% of their initial efficiencies after 250 h. We speculate that because the HMII can bind multiple interfaces, the enhanced shelf stability can be possibly ascribed to cross-linking of the grains and preventing role of HMII against to water penetration (Figure 1c). As a result of the significant hydrophobicity, HMII-based devices exhibited an outstanding stability against the high humidity conditions.

HMII can reduce the trap density and prevent the notorious ion migration from the perovskite lattice by anchoring the migrating ions with nonvolatile   $  [HMI]^{+}  $  cations through ionic interactions.  $  ^{[54]}  $   On the other hand, the presence of defects (especially grain boundaries) may provide a favorable pathway for the migration of such ions due to reduced steric hindrance.  $  ^{[55]}  $   In this respect, HMII can also play a subsidiary role in suppressing possible ion diffusion as a result of the formation of high-quality FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films with large grain size and reduced grain boundaries where the volatile ions can readily escape.

In addition to shelf-stability, we also investigated the operational stability of the unencapsulated cells under MPP tracking with a continuous one-sun illumination in nitrogen atmosphere (as described in the ISOS-L-1I protocol).  $  ^{[56]}  $   The pristine FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   devices showed a rapid initial decay followed by a slower decay profile, which is in agreement with the previous reports,  $  ^{[35,57]}  $   whereas the modified devices exhibited a considerable stability, maintaining   $  >85\%  $   of their initial performance after   $  \approx250  $   h of irradiation (Figure 5c). This result can be attributed to the interaction between HMII and perovskite resulting in suppression of ion migration induced by light and electrical field, and stabilization of   $  \alpha  $  -phase FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  .  $  ^{[58]}  $

# 3. Conclusions

This study demonstrates the multifaceted functions of HMII IL in boosting the photovoltaic performance and long-term stability of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  -based perovskite cells. HMII provides a liquid domain between neighboring grains to decrease the activation energy of grain-boundary mitigation and can serve as a growth-controlling reagent to obtain micron-sized   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   grains with reduced defect states. The IL with its admissible hydrophobicity forms a moisture barrier to effectively improve the air-stability of perovskite cells. With achieved high-quality and stable   $  \alpha  $  -phase   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   film, not only structural properties but also optoelectronic properties were enhanced. As a result, we achieved 20.6% cell performance. This outstanding efficiency is higher than not only IL-free but also BMII-incorporated   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  -based cells. More importantly, these improvements lead to a much better device stability under harsh conditions, i.e., thermal stress, high humidity, or light soaking. We believe that our findings suggest the significance of the HMII additive in reducing the electronic disorder and this will help the researchers to achieve high performance of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  -based PSCs by using multi-functional ionic liquids.
