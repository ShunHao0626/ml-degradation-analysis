# Abstract

Single crystal anatase  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanospindles (NSs) with highly exposed {101} facets were synthesized and employed as electron transport materials (ETM) in perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra revealed that the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NSs are more effective than  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanoparticles in accepting electrons from perovskite. Moreover, the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanospindles further endowed the PSCs with good reproducibility and suppressed hysteresis. The best device with  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NSs as ETM yielded power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 19.6\% $ , demonstrating that the home-made  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NSs is a good ETM for PSCs.

Key words: Electron transport material, Perovskite solar cell,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanospindles, Ultrathin mesoporous layer

# 1. Introduction

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

Since Miyasaka et al. employed organolead halide perovskite as a sensitizer in a liquid sensitized electrolyte based solar cell   $  [1]  $   and the subsequent breakthrough into solid-state architecture,   $  [2-8]  $   perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have drawn tremendous attentions due to their outstanding merits such as low manufacture costs, high power conversion efficiency, and tunable optoelectronic characteristics. At present, the certified device efficiency of PSCs has exceeded 22%,   $  [7]  $   showing a promising foreground for large-scale territorial application.

A large number of studies have been conducted on improving the quality of perovskite films and their fabrication process. [3-4, 7, 9-10] Selection and engineering of the electron transport materials (ETM) pose an important impact on device performance of PSCs as well. [11] In PSCs,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  is the most widely used ETM, [1-7, 9-10] although the use of other oxide materials, such as  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ , [12-13] ZnO, [14-15]  $ \mathrm{Zn_2SnO_4} $  [16] and  $ \mathrm{BaSnO_3} $ , [17-18] has also been reported. A variety of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanostructures [19-21] have been demonstrated to be superior scaffolds of the perovskites. These  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanostructures play a key role in extracting photoinduced electrons from perovskite and subsequently transferring them to an electrode. It was found that photovoltaic performance of PSCs was greatly influenced by the crystal phase, morphologies, wettability and the surface states of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanostructures.

Moreover, it has been reported that the order of the average surface energies of anatase   $  TiO_{2}  $   is   $  \{001\} (=0.90\ J/m^{2}) > \{100\} (=0.53\ J/m^{2}) > \{101\} (=0.44\ J/m^{2})  $  . [22] The facets with higher surface energies usually exhibit higher catalytic activity in photocatalysis. As a result, single-crystalline anatase   $  TiO_{2}  $   sheets with a high percentage of   $  \{001\}  $   facets have been widely deployed as photocatalysts. [22-24] On the contrary, in order to protect the light absorbers supported on   $  TiO_{2}  $   from photo-decomposing in solar cells (perovskite in this case),   $  TiO_{2}  $   crystals dominated by the facets with lower surface energies and lower catalytic activity are preferred, i.e., the facets   $  \{101\}  $   for anatase   $  TiO_{2}  $  . However, up to date, it yet lacks experimental evidences to demonstrate the applicability of the   $  \{101\}  $   facet-dominated   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanostructures in PSCs.

Herein, anatase   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanospindles (NSs) with highly exposed   $  \{101\}  $   facets (  $  \sim85\%  $  ) were synthesized by a facile solvothermal method, and was further used as ETMs in PSCs for the first time. The resulting PSCs yield PCEs as high as 19.6% with good reproducibility and stability, indicative of the applicable promise in developing high-efficiency PSCs.

The anatase   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanospindles with highly exposed   $  \{101\}  $   facets was synthesized in tetra-n-butyl titanate (TBT)-acetic acid solution at   $  200\ ^{\circ}C  $   for 24 h (the experimental section of the supplementary materials). To reveal the crystal structure of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NSs, the X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern was measured, as shown in Fig. 1a. All diffraction peaks were assigned to anatase   $  TiO_{2}  $   (JCPDS # 21–1272). Fig. 1b displays the Raman spectrum of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NSs with bands at 144, 397, 515 and   $  640\ cm^{-1}  $  , respectively.   $  E_{g}  $   peaks at 144 and   $  640\ cm^{-1}  $   represent the symmetric stretching vibration of O–Ti–O; while the   $  B_{1g}  $   peak at   $  397\ cm^{-1}  $   and   $  A_{1g}  $   peak at   $  515\ cm^{-1}  $   arise from the symmetric and anti-symmetric vibration, further confirming the anatase phase of the

The stronger PL intensity for  $ \mathrm{bl - TiO_2} $ /perovskite samples indicated the relative poor electron extraction ability, which means that an electron transfer barrier exists at the  $ \mathrm{bl - TiO_2} $ /perovskite interface, as previously reported in planar heterojunction PSCs.[26] This inefficient electron injection from perovskite to  $ \mathrm{bl - TiO_2} $  may be the main reason for the hysteresis effect. More efficient PL quenching was observed for the Dyesol  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  films and the home-made  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NS-based films, especially for the later one, which was mainly related to the enhanced charge extraction across the interface between  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanostructures and perovskite layer. spectra of the perovskite films on different ETM. The PL decay time and amplitudes are modelled using a bi-exponential equation (1):

$$
f (t) = \sum_ {i} A _ {i} e x p ^ {(- t / \tau_ {i})} + B
$$

(1)

Where  $  A_{i}  $  is the decay amplitude,  $  \tau_{i}  $  is the decay time and  $  B  $  is a constant for the base-line offset. Table S2 lists all above mentioned parameters obtained from the analysis. The average PL decay times  $  (\tau_{ave})  $  were estimated using the  $  \tau_{i}  $  and  $  A_{i}  $  values from the fitted curve data (Table S2) according to equation (2):

$$
\tau_ {a v e} = \frac {\sum A _ {i} \tau_ {i} ^ {2}}{\sum A _ {i} \tau_ {i}}
$$

(2)

The  $ \tau_{ave} $  of FTO/perovskite is 419.10 ns. When the  $ \mathrm{bl - TiO_2} $  was used, the average PL decay time is significantly reduced to 234.17 ns, and further decreased to 114.22 ns for Dyesol  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  samples and to 69.30 ns for home-made  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NSs samples, indicating that much more effective electrons transfer and collection can be achieved when our home-made  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NS was employed. This enhancement in electron transfer could prevent the accumulation of excess capacitive charge that leads to hysteresis, which was consistent with the of  $ J - V $  curves in Fig. 2b, 2d and 3b.

To further study the electron transport process, we measured the electron trap-state density in different ETM by conducting the dark current–voltage (I-V) analysis using the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) model, as shown in Fig. 4c. For each ETM measured, the linear relation at low bias voltage indicated an ohmic response, and when the bias voltage exceeded a kink point, the current quickly increased nonlinearly, demonstrating that the trap-states were completely filled. As a result, we can determine the trap-state density by the kink point, i. e., the trap-filled limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ) according to equation (3)[27]:

$$
V _ {\mathrm{TFL}} = \frac {e n _ {\mathrm{t}} L ^ {2}}{2 \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0}}
$$

(3)

Where e is the elementary charge of the electron, L is the perovskite film thickness,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative dielectric constant of   $  TiO_{2}  $   (  $  \varepsilon=48[28-29]  $  ),   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity, and   $  n_{t}  $   is the trap-state density. The   $  V_{TFL}  $   of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NS and Dyesol   $  TiO_{2}  $   films were 2.21 and 2.44 V, respectively. Therefore, the electron trap-state densities of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NS and Dyesol   $  TiO_{2}  $   films were calculated respectively to be   $  5.99\times10^{16}  $   and   $  6.60\times10^{16}cm^{-3}  $  . The lower trap state density in   $  TiO_{2}  $   NS films could help to suppress the trapping and detrapping process in PSCs, leading to a reduced J-V hysteresis and an enhanced efficiency with ultrathin mesoporous layer. [30-31]

In conclusion, solvothermal synthesized anatase   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanospindles with 85% {101} facets exposed were used as the ultrathin mesoporous layer in the PSCs. The PSCs made of them were characterized by J-V properties, stability, photoluminescence, and defect state. It was demonstrated that our   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanospindles are superior to the commercially available   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs as the mesoporous scaffolds in PSCs. Our findings may pave a new way to rational design of effective ETMs for PSCs.
