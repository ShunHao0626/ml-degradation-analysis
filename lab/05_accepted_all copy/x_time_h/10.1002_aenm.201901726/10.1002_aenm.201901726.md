# Incorporating CsF into the PbI  $  _{2}  $  Film for Stable Mixed Cation-Halide Perovskite Solar Cells

Xiaohui Yi, Zhiming Zhang, Ailing Chang, Yichen Mao, Yigang Luan, Tao Lin, Yuanzhi Wei, Yanyan Zhang, Fuyi Wang, Shaokui Cao, Cheng Li,* and Jizheng Wang*

Adding a small amount of CsI into mixed cation-halide perovskite film via a one-step method has been demonstrated as an excellent strategy for high-performance perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, the one-step method generally relies on an antisolvent washing process, which is hard to control and not suitable for fabricating large-area devices. Here, CsF is employed and Cs is incorporated into perovskite film via a two-step method. It is revealed that CsF can effectively diffuse into the   $  PbI_{2}  $   seed film, and drastically enhances perovskite crystallization, leading to high-quality Cs-doped perovskite film with a very long photoluminescence carrier lifetime (1413 ns), remarkable light stability, thermal stability, and humidity stability. The fabricated PSCs show power conversion efficiency (PCE) of over 21%, and they are highly thermally stable: in the aging test at 60 °C for 300 h, 96% of the original PCE remains. The CsF incorporation process provides a new avenue for stable high-performance PSCs.

In the past ten years, many efficient methods such as solvent engineering,  $  ^{[1-4]}  $   interfacial engineering,  $  ^{[5-7]}  $   composition engineering  $  ^{[8-12]}  $   have been developed to achieve high-quality organic–inorganic lead halide perovskite film, which is critical for realizing high-performance perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Now, Cs-doped mixed cation-halide perovskite films are extensively studied due to their relatively high stability, and they are

Directly adding these Cs salts into   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution also does not work because complex branch-like structure would appear (it might be a nonperovskite   $  \delta  $  -phase), unfavorable in forming good perovskite film.  $  ^{[19]}  $   Recently, a complex perovskite seed growth method and further a CsCl-enhanced perovskite seed method were developed by Zhao and coworkers, they successfully incorporated Cs into perovskite film and realized PSCs with high power conversion efficiency (PCE).  $  ^{[19,20]}  $

In this paper, we adopted cesium fluorine (CsF) to make Cs-doped perovskite film via the two-step method. The fabricated film is highly crystallized and exhibits very long photoluminescence (PL) carrier lifetime of 1413 ns. Meanwhile, the film exhibits high light stability, thermal stability and humidity stability. As a result, the planar PSCs (indium tin oxide(ITO)/ $ \mathrm{SnO}_2/ $ perovskite/2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-p-methoxy-phenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene(Spiro-OMeTAD)/Au) based on the Cs-doped film possess very good stability, and display PCEs of over  $ 21\% $ .

X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the control film and the Cs-doped film are shown in Figure 3c,d. It is found the characteristic peak of (001) crystallographic plane of the Cs-doped

film moves to a higher angle of 14.20° compared to 14.17° of the control film. This is a proof that Cs is incorporated in the perovskite crystal lattice, leading to the decrease of the lattice constant.  $  ^{[25,26]}  $   The peak is also much stronger and narrower (the full width at half-maximum (FWHM): 0.109° vs 0.120°), indicating greatly enhanced crystallinity of the perovskite film. It should be noted that both the control and Cs-doped films exhibit very strong PbI  $  _{2}  $   peak at 12.85°. Many groups reported that the excess PbI  $  _{2}  $   in the perovskite film is advantageous for the device performance because the moderate residual of PbI  $  _{2}  $   could passivate defects,  $  ^{[27-30]}  $   though too much residual PbI  $  _{2}  $   would be harmful. By comparing the XRD patterns of the films with various annealing times (Figure S10, Supporting Information), we can find that the PbI  $  _{2}  $   in the perovskite film is mainly derived from the decomposition of the perovskite film during the annealing processes. We controlled the amount of PbI  $  _{2}  $   in the perovskite film by adjusting the annealing time at an annealing temperature of 140 °C in air with 40% humidity. The optimal annealing time is 25 and 30 min for the control and Cs-doped films, respectively (Figure S11, Supporting Information). Figure 4a,b gives the top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM). It is seen that the Cs-doped film shows more monolithic surface morphology, consistent with its smaller surface roughness (25.5 vs 32.9 nm) indicated by atomic force microscopy measurements (Figure S2, Supporting Information).

Planar PSCs (ITO/SnO  $  _{2}  $  /perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au) were then fabricated. Figure 5a gives the current density-voltage (J-V) curves of a typical control device and Cs-doped device. The control device displays an open-circuit voltage (V  $  _{oc}  $  ) of 1.100 V, short-circuit current density (J  $  _{sc}  $  ) of 23.60 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , fill factor (FF) of 74.12%, and PCE of 19.24%. The Cs-doped device shows a V  $  _{oc}  $   of 1.134 V, J  $  _{sc}  $   of 23.99 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , FF of 74.59%, and PCE of 20.29%. It can be found that the V  $  _{oc}  $   is improved quite a lot, which can be attributed to the reduced defects in the perovskite film. External quantum efficiency (EQE) results are given in Figure 5b, the integrated J  $  _{sc}  $   for the control device and the Cs-doped device are 23.31 and 23.50 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , respectively. This is consistent with the values obtained from the J-V curves. The champion Cs-doped device has negligible hysteresis with a PCE of 21.07% (Figure 5c). We summarized the parameters (V  $  _{oc}  $  , J  $  _{sc}  $  , FF, PCE) of 53 control devices and 53 Cs-doped devices, the statistical results are given in Figure S12 (Supporting Information). It is seen that the standard deviations for all the parameters are reduced for the Cs-doped devices, indicating good reproducibility of the Cs-doped device.

It is known that in planar PSCs, the   $  V_{oc}  $   can be expressed by  $  ^{[31,32]}  $

$$
V _ {\mathrm{oc}} = \frac {n k _ {\mathrm{B}} T}{q} \ln \left(\frac {J _ {\mathrm{ph}}}{J _ {\mathrm{sc}}} + 1\right)
$$

(1)

where   $  k_{B}  $   is the Boltzmann constant, T is temperature, q is the elementary charge,   $  J_{ph}  $   is photogenerated current density and n is the ideality factor. n is a parameter that characterizes carrier recombination inside the device, and its value usually is between 1 and 2: the smaller n, the less carrier recombination inside the device, the better the device performance. Suppressing nonradiative recombination can get very high   $  V_{oc}  $   at very low light intensity.  $  ^{[33-35]}  $   Figure 5d gives the curves of   $  V_{oc}  $   versus light intensity, from which we can extract n (the slope of the curve is   $  nk_{B}T/q  $  ). The extracted n for the control device and the Cs-doped device are 1.86 and 1.34, respectively. The smaller n indicates that in the Cs-doped device, carrier recombination is greatly suppressed, which is consistent with the longer carrier PL lifetime of the Cs-doped perovskite film. Figure 4c,d gives the cross-sectional SEM images of the two devices, it can be found that the Cs-doped perovskite film is more compact, and hence should have fewer defects in the film.

Figure 6a,b, c shows the XRD patterns of the control film and the Cs-doped film before and after three different aging tests in air: a): light test under AM 1.5G illumination for 3 h; b): thermal test at 120 °C for 1 h; c): humidity test at humidity of 80% for 1 h. In the light test, both films show good stability. In the thermal and humidity tests, the decomposition of the Cs-doped film is negligible, while that of the control film is very serious (indicated by the enhancement of the peak of PbI₂). It should be noted that in the humidity test, a new diffraction peak at 11.60° appears for the control film, indicating the formation of the inactive δ-phase. In addition, ultraviolet (UV) irradiation of 200 mW cm⁻² (at λ=365 nm) was also conducted in air with 60% humidity. It should be noted that the UV light intensity within the AM 1.5G spectrum is only 4.6 mW cm⁻².[38] The SEM images of the perovskite films with/without the UV irradiation are given in Figure S22 (Supporting Information). It can be observed that there are some cracks emerging in the control film after UV light aging for 10 min (but not in the Cs-doped film), indicating the UV-induced damages for the control film.[39,40] The faster decomposition of the control film was also confirmed by XRD results (Figure S23, Supporting Information). We also performed the UV aging tests on the devices, as shown in Figure S24 (Supporting Information). After 10 min of UV irradiation, the control device drops more than 50% of its initial PCE, while the Cs-doped device remains 82% of its initial PCE. All these clearly indicate that the Cs-doped film exhibits much higher stability. The reasons for stability boosting could be explained as follows: firstly, CsF modification could enhance

perovskite crystallization, resulting in a more monolithic and compact morphology. Secondly, Cs incorporation can suppress the inactive   $  \delta  $  -phase and might inhibit the light-induced ions movement. Thirdly, the residual   $  F^{-}  $  could be very effective in passivating vacancies by forming strong hydrogen bonds with   $  FA^{+}/MA^{+}  $  and strong ionic bonds with lead in the perovskite films.

We conducted the device stability test at maximum power point (MMP) under AM 1.5G illumination of 300 s, shown in Figure 6d. The control device exhibits an obvious PCE drop of 10%, while the Cs-doped device remains very stable output. We further examined the thermal stability of the devices by aging them at 60 °C for 300 h, shown in Figure 6e. It can be found that the control device only keeps 73% of its initial PCE, while the Cs-doped device remains 96% of its initial PCE. The long-term air stability of the two devices are also investigated by storing them in air for 180 days without encapsulation (Figure 6f). It is observed that the Cs-doped device retains 95% of its initial PCE, while the control device only retains its 65% of its initial PCE.

In summary, we incorporate CsF into the   $  PbI_{2}  $   seed film to make Cs-doped perovskite film via the two-step method. The perovskite crystallization is greatly enhanced, and the film is very smooth and compact. As a result, the film PL carrier lifetime is largely extended (from 686 to 1413 ns), and its thermal stability, light stability and humidity stability are dramatically boosted. The fabricated PSCs achieve PCEs of over 21%. More importantly, without encapsulation they only show 4% PCE degradation in the aging test at 60 °C for 300 h, and they keep 95% of the initial PCE after being stored in air for 180 days. The CsF incorporation offers an encouraging way to realizing high-quality and stable perovskite films for optoelectronic applications.

# Experimental Section

Material:   $  SnO_{2}  $   (15% in water colloidal dispersion), CsI, CsBr, CsCl, CsF, DMF, DMSO, acetonitrile and IPA were purchased from Alfa Aesar. PEDOT:PSS, PCBM, and chlorobenzene was purchased from Sigma Aldrich. Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI), 4-tert-butylpyridine(4-TBP), Spiro-OMeTAD, FAI, MABr, and MACl

were bought from Xi'an Polymer Light. All the chemicals were used as received without further purification.

Device Fabrication: The PSCs were made with a planar structure of ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au. The patterned ITO glass substrate was ultrasonically washed in detergent, deionized water, acetone, and IPA for 30 min in sequence, then were dried and cleaned by O $ _2 $  plasma for 15 min. The perovskite films were deposited by the two-step method. The temperature of the glove box was kept at 25–30 °C. In detail, firstly the 1.42 M PbI₂ solution (DMF:DMSO=6.25:1, v:v) was spin coated onto SnO₂ at 1600 rpm for 45 s, and then annealed at 72 °C for 2 min. For the Cs-doped perovskite film, the 120 μL CsF (2–10 mg in 1.5 mL IPA) was dipped on the PbI₂ film during the substrate spinning with a speed of 2000 rpm, resulting in a CsF-modified PbI₂ seed. The spinning time was 20 s. And then a 120 μL mixed solution of FAI/MABr/MACl (220 mg: 22 mg: 23 mg in 3 mL IPA) was dipped onto the PbI₂ seed during the substrate spinning with a speed of 2000 rpm. The samples were quickly delivered into a homemade air box with a controlled humidity of 40% and then annealed at 140 °C. After the samples were naturally cooled down to 25 °C, they were taken into the glove box and washed by drop-casting IPA. The Spiro-OMeTAD was spin coated on the perovskite film by spin coating a mixed solution, which contains 72.3 mg Spiro-OMeTAD, 28.8 μL 4-TBP, 17.5 μL Li-TFSI solution (520 mg in 1 mL acetonitrile) and 1 mL chlorobenzene. Finally, 90 nm Au was thermal evaporated as top electrode using a shadow mask. The device size was 0.044 cm².

Characterization: J-V curves of the PSCs were measured in the   $  N_{2}  $   glove box by using a Keithley 2400 source meter. An AM 1.5G solar simulator (IQE-200, Newport) and a white light LED (S2000, HECHO) calibrated by a standard silicon solar cell were used as light source. The devices were measured both in reverse scan (1.2–0 V) and forward scan (0–1.2 V) with a scanning rate of 100 mV s  $  ^{-1}  $  . Other scanning rates were also adopted for comparison. The EQE spectra were measured using an Oriel IQE-200 multimeter. The spectral intensity was calibrated using a silicon photodetector. Optical absorption results were obtained from a Lambda 950 UV/Vis spectrometer. The SEM images were characterized by a Hitachi S-4800 microscope. Steady-state PL spectra and time-resolved PL spectra were carried out with Edinburgh Instruments Ltd. FLS980 fluorescence spectrometer system. The XPS and UPS results were recorded by a KRATOS ULTRA AXIS DLD photoelectron spectroscopy system. The XRD patterns were measured by a Rifaku D/MAX-2004 XRD with Cu K α radiation. The plasma-sputtering XPS measurements were carried out with Kratos Ultra Spectrometer equipped with monochromatized Al Kα X-ray photons discharge lamp. The UV light aging was performed by a UV light LED (MLED4-1, λ = 365 nm, Zolix). The impedance spectrum was measured using an electrochemical workstation at a bias of 1 V. The surface roughness of perovskite film was measured by an AFM (Nanoscope V, Vecco) in tapping mode. TOF-SIMS measurements were performed with a time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometer (TOF. SIMS 5, ION-TOF GmbH). Bi ion was used as analysis source. Both positive mode and negative mode were adopted.
