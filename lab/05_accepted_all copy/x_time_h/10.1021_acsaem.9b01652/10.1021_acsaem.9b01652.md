KEYWORDS: low-temperature processing, high-efficiency, all-inorganic carbon based perovskite solar cell,  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $ , alkali hydroxides

ABSTRACT: All inorganic carbon based perovskite solar cells (C-PSCs) have attracted massive attention owing to their low-cost fabrication and superior stability. However, the low power conversion efficiency (PCE) (< 10%), serious hysteresis and relatively high-temperature fabrication process (> 250 °C) largely hinder their great potential for commercialization. Herein, we demonstrate a simple and efficient interfacial engineering strategy with alkali hydroxides (KOH, NaOH) to enable the low-temperature (~ 150 °C) fabrication of high-quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films and C-PSC device (ITO/  $  SnO_{2}  $  /MOH/  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  /carbon), in which a thin layer of alkali hydroxide interfacial modifier leads to reduced work function and surface tension force of   $  SnO_{2}  $ , full   $  SnO_{2}  $ /  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   interface contact and facile electron transfer from   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   to ETL. Importantly,   $  K^{+}  $  cations can effectively suppress the halide ion migration and the vacancy defect formation by forming KBr-like compounds, thus leading to significant improvement in current-voltage hysteresis and stability together with stabilized PCE of 11.22% under contious illumination (the champion PCE in the reverse scan direction up to 11.78% (0.09 cm²) and 9.34% (1.00 cm²)), which represent the highest level for the low-temperature-processing all-inorganic C-PSCs. Our work represents advance in construction of high-efficient inorganic perovskite/ETL interface that can significantly improve the photovoltaic performance and stability of PSCs.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with mesoporous structure, planar heterojunction or inverted planar heterojunction structures, wherein a crystalline perovskite film is sandwiched between an electron and hole-transporting layer (ETL and HTL), have made unprecedented achievement in recent ten years and attained a high certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) over 25%.  $  ^{1-4}  $   However, the serious constraint on long-term air- and thermal-stability of PSC devices due to the organic components from perovskite (e.g. methylammonium (MA  $  ^{+}  $  ), formamidinium (FA  $  ^{+}  $  )) and Spiro-OMeTAD-based HTLs (e.g. 4-text-butyl pyridine (TBP) and lithium salts), hinders their widespread commercial application of new-generation photovoltaic cells.  $  ^{5-7}  $   Substitution of the fragile perovskites' organic cations and Spiro-OMeTAD-based HTL with Cs  $  ^{+}  $   and cheap carbon back-electrode can effectively settle those issues on the instability of PSCs.  $  ^{8-10}  $   The all-inorganic carbon-based PSCs (C-PSCs) with the architecture of FTO/metal oxide ETL/inorganic perovskite/carbon possess a series of spectacular merits including superb thermal stability (> 300 °C), excellent ambient stability, cost-effectiveness, and simple fabrication, are promised to be an alternative candidate for various optoelectronic applications.  $  ^{11-16}  $

Inorganic perovskites such as   $  CsPbI_{3}  $  ,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  ,   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $  ,   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   showing optical bandgaps (  $  E_{g}  $  ) ranging from   $  \sim1.73  $   to   $  \sim2.3  $   eV, are the most studied photoabsorbing materials employed in PSCs. Among them,   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   in cubic crystal structure (  $  \alpha  $  -phase) with the narrowest bandgap (  $  \sim1.73  $   eV) can achieve a high PCE over 17% when adopted in PSCs.  $  ^{17}  $   Despite high PCE, its photoactive   $  \alpha  $  -phase stability

# 2. EXPERIMENTAL

# 2.1 Fabrication of perovskite solar cells

The ITO/glass substrate was cleaned carefully with deionized water, ethanol, acetone, and isopropanol respectively and eventually handled under oxygen plasma for 20 min to eliminate the excess trace of organic residues after isopropanol evaporation. The SnO $ _2 $  colloidal dispersion solution (15 wt.%) was diluted by deionized water to 2.67 wt.%. The above solution was spin-coated on ITO/glass substrates at 3000 rpm for 30 s, with annealing at 150 °C for 30 min in air to remove residual and form a layer of uniform and dense thin film. Then KOH (5 mM, 10 mM, 20 mM, 50 mM) aqueous solution were spin-coated on SnO $ _2 $  ETL at 3000 rpm for 30 s with annealing at 100 °C for 10 min. NaOH and KNO $ _3 $  aqueous solution were operated as a same step as KOH. 0.5 M PbI $ _2 $  and 0.5 M CsBr were dissolved in 0.5 mL DMSO in N $ _2 $  atmosphere and

# 2.2 Characterization

The current-voltage curves of PSCs were recorded under AM 1.5G sunlight at 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  by a solar light simulator (Newport Oriel Sol 3A class, America, calibrated by a standard silicon solar cell) with a Keithley 2400 source-measure meter. The active area of solar cells was controlled as 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  and 1.00 cm  $  ^{2}  $  by punching the 3M tape. XRD patterns of perovskite films were examined via X-ray diffraction (Rigaku, D/max 2500 VB25/PC) with Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda = 0.15406  $  nm). The JEOL JSM-6701F scanning electron microscope (SEM) was used to characterize the morphology of perovskite films. Atom force microscopy (AFM) topography images were obtained on a atomic force microscope (Bruker Metrology Nanoscope III-D). The surface potential images of samples were acquired by Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) with a atomic force microscope (Bruker Metrology Nanoscope VIII). The typical scan frequency was 0.3 Hz and each image involved 512 × 512 pixels.

# 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Figure 1a&b and Figure S1 show the schematic diagram and cross-sectional scanning electron microscopy (SEM) image of the all-inorganic C-PSCs with structure of ITO/SnO₂/MOH/CsPbI₂Br/carbon, in which alkali hydroxide such as KOH, NaOH is deposited on the SnO₂ film to modify the SnO₂/CsPbI₂Br interface. CsPbI₂Br thin film is obtained by a simple one-step solution spin-coating process followed by subsequent thermal annealing at 150 °C, in which a series of KOH solutions (0, 5, 10, 20, and 50 mM) are spin-coated on the SnO₂ films to modify the interface between SnO₂ ETL and CsPbI₂Br layer. A champion photovoltaic performance is achieved by the KOH concentration of 20 mM (Figure S2 and Table S1). From Figure 1c&e as well as Table 1, one can observe that KOH or NaOH modification contributes to obvious improvement on fill factor (FF), open-circuit potential ( $ V_{oc} $ ) and photovoltaic performance of CsPbI₂Br C-PSCs as consequence.
