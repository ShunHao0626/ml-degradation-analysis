# Enhancing Photo-stability of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  Perovskite Solar Cells by Incorporating Black Phosphorus

Keywords: Perovskite solar cells; black phosphorus; photo-stability;  $ \mathrm{Pb}^0 $  defect; hot carrier recombination

The photo-stability is one of the most vital challenges for organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs). With the incorporation of black phosphorus (BP), well known for self-healing and its superior property to regulate charge recombination, into  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite (MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP), the associated PSCs exhibit significant enhancement in photo-stability besides the photovoltaic (PV) performance. The MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP based PSCs can retain ~94% of initial efficiency after 1000 h continuous white light LED illumination in a dry N $ _2 $  glovebox while their counterparts without BP incorporation decreased to ~30%. Although BP has very small influence on the morphology and structure of the perovskite crystals, Pb $ ^0 $  defect is effectively inhibited and hot carrier recombination is found to be retarded as confirmed by femtosecond optical spectroscopy. The utilization of the material to simultaneously realize Pb $ ^0 $  inhibition and charge recombination retard such as BP would be a promising strategy to enhance the photo-stability of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite based PSCs and their siblings.

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs), with their unprecedented power conversation efficiency (PCE) up to a certified efficiency of 24.2%, have become one of the most promising next-generation photovoltaic (PV) techniques.  $  ^{[1-7]}  $   Some commercialization trials of large size PSC module PCEs have also exceeded 15%.  $  ^{[8]}  $   Other than the cost and large-scale fabrication, the main obstacle for the commercialization of PSCs are their stability, significantly affected by the intrinsic thermodynamic properties of perovskite materials, the sensitivity of materials or devices to moisture as well as illumination, and so on. Among those factors, the photo-stability of PSCs might be one of the most important issues. Some previous studies have suggested that the photo-stability of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite based devices might be influenced by the photo-stability of perovskite materials, electron/hole transfer materials and electrical contacts.  $  ^{[6,9-11]}  $   Other than the intrinsic thermodynamic photo-stability of hybrid perovskite crystal,  $  ^{[12-14]}  $   the fast hot carrier recombination in perovskite absorber layer would not only lower the PV performance but also induce larger photo-thermal effect to deteriorate the stability of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   and lead to the following irreversible photo-degeneration. One straightforward strategy to release the photothermal effect is to block or delay the hot carrier recombination in the perovskite layers. Except the photothermal effect inducing irreversible degradation, Pb  $  ^{2+}  $   in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   is prone to be reduced to metallic Pb  $  ^{0}  $   during continuous illumination process. Pb  $  ^{0}  $   is a primary deep defects state in which severally degrades the performance of PSCs devices, as well as their long-term stability.

Black phosphorous (BP), one new type of 2D materials with high hole mobility, as a hole injection layer has been used in perovskite-based light-emitting diode (PeLED) and PSCs devices to improve the hole extraction efficiency.  $  ^{[15-16]}  $   Moreover, the recent theoretical studies had indicated that BP has an ideal function to retard charge recombination due to its unique self-repairing properties.  $  ^{[17-18]}  $   BP has also been utilized to inhibit charge recombination in some electrical application.  $  ^{[16, 19-20]}  $   In this report, we demonstrate that the incorporation of 2D

BP into  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  (MA=  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3 $ ) not only retards the hot carrier recombination but also inhibits  $ \mathrm{Pb}^0 $  defect formation. Eventually, the BP incorporation improves photo-stability compared with their  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  counterparts although both have a similar dark stability for 1000 hrs.

As shown in Figure S1 and S2, the average lateral size and thickness of the applied BP flakes are  $ \sim 0.5 - 2\mu \mathrm{m} $  and  $ \sim 8 $  nm, respectively. The BP crystal plane spacing presented is  $ \sim 0.25 $  nm corresponding to the (002) crystal plane of orthorhombic phosphorus.[21] The BP flakes are well-dispersed in the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  precursor solution as shown in Figure S3 and then the perovskite film was fabricated via a typical solvent engineering method. The influence to the absorption spectrum and XRD patterns of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite films is negligible by the incorporation of BP (Figure 1a and 1b) as well as its dosage (Figure S4). The absorption edges of both  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{BP} $  films are located at  $ \sim 780~\mathrm{nm} $  as a typical single phase pure  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite (Figure 1a).[22-25] The XRD pattern and intensity of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{BP} $  film are similar to those of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  (Figure 1b). The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) in Figure S5 indicates that the BP incorporation does not impact the chemical properties of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  either.

However, the BP incorporation has some obvious effects on the microstructures of as-deposited  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite film. Figure 1c and 1d show that both  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{BP} $  films are compact, pinhole-free and with full coverage, which present the typical morphology quality of films prepared via solvent-engineering.[26-27] However, the average grain size of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film ( $ < 400\mathrm{nm} $ , Figure 1c) is apparently smaller than that of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{BP} $  film ( $ >500 $  nm, Figure 1d), which is more or less independent on the BP concentration (Figure S4). In order to explore the distribution and integration of BP in the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite, we deposited the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{BP} $  precursor onto the TEM grids directly. In addition, the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{BP} $ -based PSCs also show an important advantage of both enhanced reproducibility with a narrow PCE distribution (Figure S9 and Table S1) and less hysteresis in J-V curves (Figure S10 and Table S2), which finally result in a stable  $ 20.23\% $  PCE output (Figure S11). The PV performance of other  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{BP} $  devices with different BP contents have been listed in Figure S12 and all show better PV performances than the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  based devices. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{BP} $ -based PSC in Figure S13 is a little higher than the corresponding pure  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  one as well. Based on the PV parameters, we found that the BP embedding into  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  can enhance all the PV parameters including the  $ J_{sc}, V_{oc} $  and  $ FF $ .

The time-resolved PL (TRPL) decay in Figure S14 reveals that the average photoluminescence (PL) lifetime ( $  \tau_{\text{av}} = 41.88  $  ns) for the MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP film is shorter than one half of that for MAPbI $ _3 $  ( $  \tau_{\text{av}} = 92.42  $  ns). This suggests that the BP in the MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite layer does not function as a passivation layer as other reported additives used for MAPbI $ _3 $  such as graphene oxide. $ ^{[28]} $  Such decrease of PL lifetime in MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP is actually similar to the case of perovskite films covered with charge transport layers to accelerate charge diffusion, $ ^{[9,29]} $  which agrees with the enhanced Jsc and FF. The transient photocurrent decay (TPC) of MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP-based devices further show the faster photocurrent decay response than that in the MAPbI $ _3 $ -based PSC (Figure S15). Meanwhile, the transient photovoltage (TPV) decay curve of the MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP-based PSC illustrates a longer lifetime than in the MAPbI $ _3 $ -based PSC, indicating significant decrease of charge-carrier recombination. $ ^{[30-32]} $  We prefer to attribute the reduced recombination to the improvement of extraction and transport of charge carriers by the BP addition in the MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite absorbers. $ ^{[33]} $

The above results indicate that BP addition can significantly improve the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  based device PV performance, we further examine the effect of BP addition on the stability. When

stored in a dark box with  $ < 20\% $  relative humidity (RH) at room temperature, the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/\mathrm{BP} $ -based PSCs without any encapsulation exhibited similar dark stability as the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  based PSCs, and specifically both maintained  $ \sim 95 - 100\% $  of the initial PCE value after  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  storage (Figure 2b). However, compared to  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  based device, the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/\mathrm{BP} $  based devices exhibited significant enhancement in photo-stability when tested in a  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $  glovebox under continuous white LEDs illumination ( $ 100\mathrm{mW/cm}^2 $ ). The PV performance of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based devices declined rapidly and finally dropped to  $ \sim 30\% $  of its initial PCE value after continuous  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  illumination, which is similar to some previous results reported.[34-38] Surprisingly, the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/\mathrm{BP} $  based device could maintain  $ 94\% $  of its original PCE after 1000 h continuous illumination. Moreover, we also tested the photostability of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/\mathrm{BP} $  and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  based devices at the maximum power point (Figure 2d). Similar to the above load-free results, the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/\mathrm{BP} $  based device also behaved with significantly improved photostability.

The XRD patterns of the MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP and MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite films after 1000 h continuous illumination are comparatively presented in Figure 3a. The PbI $ _2 $  impurity peak is distinct for the aged MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite film, which may be attributed to the thermal decomposition of MAPbI $ _3 $  because the perovskite film is slowly warmed up to  $ \sim $ 55  $ ^{\circ} $ C under continuous illumination. Although, the well encapsulation MAPbI $ _3 $  should be stable under  $ \sim $ 55  $ ^{\circ} $ C annealing, it might still release the volatile MA component slowly due to photo-thermal effects. Interestingly, the aged MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP sample showed a much weaker PbI $ _2 $  residue peak. These results suggest that the incorporation of BP can evidently enhance the resistance to photo-induced decomposition of MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite even if the MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP film temperature also increased to  $ \sim $  55  $ ^{\circ} $ C during the illumination process. We also investigated the stability of MAPbI $ _3 $  and MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP on the 55  $ ^{\circ} $ C hot-plate in N $ _2 $  glovebox (Fig.3b). The PbI $ _2 $  impurity peak for MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP is similarly to that of MAPbI $ _3 $  samples, indicating that BP incorporation

We further comparatively investigated the charge carrier dynamics of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{BP} $  and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  using femtosecond time-resolved reflectivity technique and found that the BP addition can significantly reduce the photoexcited electron and hole recombination.  $ 395~\mathrm{nm} $  and  $ 790~\mathrm{nm} $  40 fs laser pulses were used to excite the hot carriers (pump) and monitor their density  $ n_t $  in picosecond ranges (probe), respectively. The details of the time-resolved reflectivity measurement are presented in Figures S16 and S17.  $ [n_0 / n_t] - 1 $  was calculated for both  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  with and without BP (Figure 4a), and both exhibit a linear dependence on delay time under all pump fluences used in the experiments. This indicates that the hot carriers decay principally via a second-order mechanism detailed in Figure S19. We attribute this second-order process to the recombination of free electron and hole populations[39]. Here we define the characteristic lifetime  $ \tau_0 $  of this second-order recombination process as the period when the photogenerated carrier density decayed to the half of the initial value, i.e.  $ n_t = \frac{1}{2} n_0 $  as calculated in Figure S19.  $ \tau_0 $  for both materials exhibit a sharp decrease with the increase of the initial carrier density (Figure 4b), which agrees with their second-order recombination nature. However,  $ \tau_0 $  for  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{BP} $  is 1.4 to 2 times longer than that for  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  under all pump fluences investigated (Figure 4b).

This retarding effect of BP in MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP perovskite film may due to its self-repairing function. $ ^{[17-18]} $  Instead of forming new defects, the unsaturated suspension bonds formed by the double vacancy of phosphorus atoms may repair the potential defects above the surface of MAPbI $ _3 $  grains or among them, therefore delaying charge recombination. $ ^{[18]} $  This elongated carrier recombination time for MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP is considered to be beneficial for the higher PCE and enhanced photo-stability properties presented above. As for the higher PV performance, the longer carrier recombination process in MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP improves the extraction possibility of charge carriers and a corresponding higher PCE. Moreover, the recombination energy is majorly released as heat. This thermal energy needs to be transported away through phonon-phonon interaction as soon as possible, otherwise the organic component of perovskite could be damaged by the overloaded heat deposition, “heat shock”. The longer carrier recombination time in MAPbI $ _3 $ /BP extends the heat deposition time, reducing the heat flux, alleviating the thermal burden to the local MAPbI $ _3 $  crystal and therefore may lead to a higher photo-stability.

In summary, the incorporation of BP into  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite can elongate the picosecond charge recombination process. The  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{BP} $  perovskite with  $ \mathrm{Pb}^0 $  inhibited and delayed charge recombination process not only exhibited a higher PV performance but also showed a significantly enhanced photo-stability under continuous illumination. It will be a promising strategy to reduce the photo-induced damage or decomposition of hybrid perovskite based optoelectronic devices by using such material to simultaneously realize  $ \mathrm{Pb}^0 $  inhibit and charge recombination retard.

# Experimental Section

Materials: MAI was synthesized according to our previously reported technique.  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  was purchased from Alfa Aesar. Black phosphorus (BP) was purchased from by Nanjing XFNANO Materials Tech. Co., Ltd. Anhydrous dimethylformamide (DMF) and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. The  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  colloid precursor was obtained from Alfa Aesar (tin(IV) oxide,  $ 20\% $  in  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  colloidal dispersion).

Solar cell fabrication: The solar cells were fabricated on FTO substrates (TEC 7). The substrates were washed sequentially with detergent (2% Hellmanex in water), deionized water, ethyl alcohol and isopropanol, and finally cleaned under UV-ozone treatment for 10 min. The patterned TEC-7 FTO substrate was coated with a compact TiO₂ layer by spray pyrolysis of 0.2M Ti(IV) bis (ethyl acetoacetate)-diisopropoxide in 1-butanol solution at 450 °C, followed by annealing at 450 °C for one hour. The SnO₂ colloid precursor (1.5% in H₂O colloidal dispersion) was then spin-coated onto c-TiO₂/FTO substrates at 3000 rpm for 30 s, followed by baking on a hot plate in ambient air at 180 °C for 30 min.

The precursor solution was prepared by dissolving MAI (1.4 M),   $  PbI_{2}  $   (1.4 M) in DMF: DMSO with 4:1 (v/v) ratio to make a clear   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   solution. In order to incorporate different amounts of BP in the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   precursor solution, 0.02, 0.05, and 0.10 mg·mL  $  ^{-1}  $   of BP were dispersed in the solvent (anhydrous DMF: DMSO 4:1 (v/v)) using 2-h sonication prior to making the perovskite solution (i.e.,   $  MAPbI_{3}/0.02BP  $  ,   $  MAPbI_{3}/BP  $  ,   $  MAPbI_{3}/0.1BP  $  ), respectively. The perovskite layers were deposited in a dry glovebox by spin-coating the as-prepared precursor solutions onto the   $  SnO_{2}/c-TiO_{2}/FTO  $   substrates at 5000 rpm for 20 s, with an accelerating rate of 5000 rpm·s  $  ^{-1}  $  . 500 uL chlorobenzene was dripped onto the spinning substrate 15 s before the end of the spin-coating program. The perovskite film acquired was then annealed at 100 °C for 20 min.

A layer of hole transport material (HTM) was spin-coated on top of the annealed films at 4000 rpm for 25 s, which consisted of 0.1 M spiro-MeOTAD, 0.035 M bis(trifluoromethane) sulfonamide lithium salt (Li-TFSi), and 0.12 M 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) in chlorobenzene/acetonitrile (10:1, v/v) solution. Finally, a 100-nm thick Ag layer was deposited by thermal evaporation.

Characterization: The crystal structures of the formed perovskite films were characterized using XRD (Shimadzu XRD-6100 diffractometer with Cu Kαradiation). The absorption spectra of the thin films were measured with a Cary-60 UV-vis spectrophotometer. The perovskite films were characterized using a SEM (JEOL JSM-7800F Prime). The TRPL was recorded on a PTI QM/TM/IM fluorescence spectrofluorometer. XPS spectra were acquired with a Kratos Axis UltraDLD spectrometer (Kratos Analytical, A Shimadzu Group Company) using a monochromatic Al K source (1,486.6 eV). A Time-of-Flight Secondary-Ion Mass Spectrometry (TOF-SIMS) (GAIA3 GMU Model 2016/*GAIA3) was used to elemental mapping the perovskite films. The BP flakes were characterized using an AFM (Dimension Icon & FastScan Bio).

The  $ J-V $  curves were measured with a Keithley 2401 source meter with a scan rate of 0.05  $ \mathrm{V}\cdot \mathrm{s}^{-1} $  under the following setup: a nonreflective metal mask with an aperture area of  $ 0.12~\mathrm{cm}^2 $  and a  $ 5\mathrm{cm}^{*}5\mathrm{cm} $  simulated AM 1.5G illumination  $ (100\mathrm{mW}\cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-2}) $  with an Enlitech's 3A light source. The light intensity was calibrated by a stand Si cell before test. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra were measured on a QE-3011 system (Enlitech).

Stability test: For the devices, ex-situ tests were conducted by storing the devices in desiccator (relative humidity, RH  $ < 30\% $  ) under dark condition. The devices were taken out and measured in ambient condition in every 7 days. The photo-stability for the unencapsulated PSCs were tested by the following setup: a white LEDs lamp  $ (100\mathrm{mW}\cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-2}) $  continuously
