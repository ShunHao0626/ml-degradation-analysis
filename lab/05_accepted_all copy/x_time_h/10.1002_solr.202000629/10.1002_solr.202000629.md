Cesium-based all-inorganic perovskites (PVKs) are prized for their high thermal stability and wide bandgap suitable for the top layer of tandem solar cells. To further boost the photovoltaic performance of inorganic PVK solar cells (PeSCs), a variety of strategies aiming to either passivate defects or enhance the electric field are developed. Nevertheless, a double-aim strategy is less explored. Herein, a facile strategy of gradient doping is adopted for optimizing the inverted   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PeSCs. Particularly, a bicationic iodine salt, namely 2,2'-bis(trifluoromethyl)-[1,1'-biphenyl]-4,4'-diamine iodine (BFBAI  $  _{2}  $  ), is used to realize gradient doping in PVK and ZnO layers, respectively. As a result, the inverted PeSCs with the doped PVK/ZnO bilayer deliver improves power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to 14.38% along with enhanced device stability under ambient or thermal aging conditions, greatly surpassing the pristine devices. The improvements are attributed principally to the low-defect PVK layer as well as enhanced electric field across the inverted PeSCs upon gradient doping. This work thus demonstrates an efficient bifunctional strategy toward highly efficient and stable   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PeSCs with inverted configuration.

# 1. Introduction

It has been well recognized that solution-processed inorganic PVK films, especially those prepared at a low temperature, are highly defective,  $  ^{[19]}  $  which usually result in large energy loss in PeSCs. For example, the highest reported open-circuit voltages (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) are 1.40 and 1.27 V for normal and inverted  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  PeSCs,  $  ^{[15,16]}  $  respectively, both much lower than the  $  E_{g}  $  (  $  \approx  $  1.9 eV) of PVK. Such large energy loss might be attributed to the severe defect-induced nonradiative recombination.  $  ^{[20,21]}  $  In addition, high-density interfacial defects also lead to poor contact between the PVK and electrode, which increases device series resistance and reduces short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) and fill factor (FF).  $  ^{[21]}  $  To minimize the bulk and interfacial defects associated with inorganic PVKs, a variety of passivation strategies have been used,  $  ^{[21-28]}  $  including additive engineering and post-treatment. Nevertheless, most strategies focus on the passivation of either bulk or interfacial defects rather than both. On the other hand, considerable efforts have been devoted to maximizing the built-in electric field (BEF) of PeSCs, which facilitates carrier separation/transport and hence contributes to the  $  V_{OC}  $  of the device. In this work, a gradient-doping strategy has been developed for inverted   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PeSCs, in which a bication salt, namely 2,2'-bis(trifluoromethyl)-[1,1'-biphenyl]-4,4'-diamine iodine (BFBAI  $  _{2}  $  , see Scheme S1, Supporting Information), is doped in both PVK and ZnO layers. After optimization, inverted PeSCs with a champion PCE of 14.38% as well as considerable humidity stability can be obtained, much superior to the pristine devices. A combined study of time-of-flight secondary-ion mass spectroscopy (ToF-SIMS) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) reveals the gradient doping of BFBAI  $  _{2}  $   within the PVK and ZnO layers, respectively, which leads to the efficient passivation of both bulk and interfacial defects. It is also verified that additional electric field is introduced upon the gradient doping of BFBAI  $  _{2}  $   within the PVK film, which overlies with the intrinsic BEF of inverted PeSCs and hence well accounts for the improved device performance with enhanced   $  V_{OC}  $  .

# 2. Results and Discussion

CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PVK films without and with 0.8 and 1.6 mol% BFBAI $ _2 $  were prepared at  $ \approx $ 140  $ ^{\circ} $ C and characterized by means of X-ray diffraction (XRD). As shown in Figure 1a, the pristine PVK film and that with 0.8 mol% BFBAI $ _2 $  exhibit similar XRD patterns, including three diffractions at around 2 $ \theta $ =14.7 $ ^\circ $ , 20.8 $ ^\circ $ , and 29.6 $ ^\circ $ , which correspond to (100), (110), and (200) planes of the pseudocubic  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br. It indicates that the pseudocubic structure remains regardless of the trace BFBAI $ _2 $  doping. Nevertheless, the doped PVK film shows more intensive XRD peaks, indicative of improved crystallinity. In comparison, the PVK film with 1.6 mol% BFBAI $ _2 $  displays splitting XRD peaks with lower intensities, indicative of the formation of mixed phases of  $ \alpha $  and  $ \beta $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br with lower crystallinities. $ ^{[22,30,31]} $

Next, the morphologies of PVK films were investigated using top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Such a morphological evolution with increasing doping content is in line with the XRD analysis results, confirming the positive effect of trace BFBAI $ _2 $  doping on PVK growth and the negative effect of extra doping. The elemental mapping images (Figure S2, Supporting Information) reveal that the N and F elements are homogenously distributed on the surface of the doped PVK film (0.8 mol%), confirming the incorporation of BFBAI $ _2 $ . In addition, Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectrum (Figure S3, Supporting Information) shows characteristic absorption peaks (i.e., 1390, 2970, and 630 cm $ ^{-1} $ ) associated with C–NH $ _2 $ , C–H, and C–F species for the doped PVK film, $ ^{[32,33]} $  again confirming the incorporation of BFBAI $ _2 $ . Moreover, the surface composition of the doped PVK film was probed using XPS. Cs 3d, Pb 4f, I 3d core-level spectra of pristine and doped PVK films are shown in Figure S4, Supporting Information. Obviously, the Cs 3d, Pb 4f, and I 3d peaks do not shift upon the trace doping of BFBAI $ _2 $ . It indicates that trace doping does not evidently alter the chemical state of the PVK on the film surface. The cation alloying can also be excluded, which is reasonable by considering the large conformal size ( $ \approx $ 10 Å) of BFBA $ ^{2+} $  bication. $ ^{[34]} $  In addition, the Pb:I ratio can be estimated to be 1:1.73 and 1:2.26 for the pristine and doped PVK films (see Figure S5, Supporting Information), respectively, indicative of less iodine vacancy defects on the film surface of doped PVK. This might be attributed to the improved PVK crystallinity upon BFBAI $ _2 $  doping as well as the possible passivation effect of BFBAI $ _2 $  (vide infra).

The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra are shown in Figure 2c. Obviously, the EQE value is gradually improved upon BFBAI $ _2 $  doping in the PVK monolayer and then in the PVK/ZnO bilayer in the spectral region of 350–650 nm. The integrated current densities of 14.24, 14.77, and 15.56 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  are fully consistent with the  $ J_{SC} $  (15.07, 15.46, and 16.12 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ) derived from  $ J $ - $ V $  tests with errors within 5% (see Table 1). Moreover, to estimate the operational stability of PeSCs, long-term efficiency was monitored under the maximum power-point condition for pristine and doped devices. Particularly, the device with the doped PVK/ZnO bilayer yields a highly stabilized efficiency of 13.06% over a 3 h test, whereas a pristine device presents a slightly decayed efficiency (i.e., from 10.65% to 10.24%) within the same period (see Figure 2d). This result thus indicates the positive effect of BFBAI $ _2 $  doping on device operational stability. In addition, the hysteresis index is calculated to be 0.33 and 0.23 for pristine and doped devices (Figure S11 and Table S5, Supporting Information). Such a large hysteresis effect remains to be further reduced and might account for the difference between the stabilized efficiency and the PCE derived from  $ J $ - $ V $  characteristics. $ ^{[7]} $

To understand the mechanism behind the improved device performance, steady-state and transient photoluminescence (PL) spectra were recorded for pristine and doped PVK films. As shown in Figure 3a, pristine  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  displays a pronounced PL peak at  $ 656~\mathrm{nm} $ , fully consistent with the literature report.[35] In comparison, the  $ 0.8\mathrm{mol}\% $   $ \mathrm{BFBAI}_2 $ -doped PVK film exhibits a stronger PL, indicative of reduced nonradiative charge recombination within the PVK film.[36] The PVK/ZnO composite film otherwise displays a significantly quenched PL, indicative of an efficient electron transfer between them. Moreover, to verify the role of  $ \mathrm{BFBAI}_2 $  dopants in modulating carrier kinetics, the average lifetimes ( $ \tau_{\mathrm{ave}} $ ) of PL were calculated to be 7.61 and 8.17 ns for pristine and doped PVK films, respectively, by fitting the transient PL spectra with a biexponential function (Figure 3b). The larger  $ \tau_{\mathrm{ave}} $  value indicates again the reduced nonradiative recombination in the doped PVK film. On the other hand, the significantly shortened lifetime ( $ \tau_{\mathrm{ave}} = 1.67~\mathrm{ns} $ ) measured for the doped PVK/ZnO bilayer indicates a fast and efficient electron transfer between the doped PVK and ZnO layers.

To probe the role of BFBAI $ _2 $  doping in reducing defect density, electron-only devices with the configuration of ITO/PC $ _{61} $ BM/PVK/ETL/Ag were fabricated. Their dark  $ J-V $  curves are plotted in a logarithm representation (Figure 3c). Obviously, the current density sharply increases at a kinkpoint between the ohmic and space-charge-limited regions. The corresponding voltage is defined as the trap-filled limit (TFL) voltage ( $ V_{\text{TFL}} $ ), whereby all trap defects are filled by electrons. The trap densities ( $ N_{\text{trap}} $ ) can be calculated to be  $ 3.41 \times 10^{16} $ ,  $ 2.45 \times 10^{16} $ , and  $ 1.19 \times 10^{16} \text{ cm}^{-3} $  for the pristine device as well as those with the doped PVK monolayer and PVK/ZnO bilayer, respectively, using the equation  $ N_{\text{trap}} = 2\varepsilon\varepsilon_0 V_{\text{TFL}} / ed^{2} $ .[37] On the basis of the earlier analysis, it is likely that trace BFBAI $ _2 $  doping in the PVK monolayer leads to a reduced trap density due to the improved crystallinity of PVK and boundary passivation, in line with improved  $ V_{\text{OC}} $ . Additional doping in the ZnO layer further

reduces trap density via efficient passivation of interfacial defects, which may account for the improved   $  J_{SC}  $  . Moreover, the   $  V_{OC}  $   response with varied incident light intensity was recorded and is shown in Figure 3d. The   $  V_{OC}  $   of the device with doped the PVK/ZnO bilayer is almost linearly correlated with the logarithmic light intensity with a slope of   $  1.88 k_{B}T/q  $  .  $  ^{[38]}  $   lower than that   $  (2.24 k_{\mathrm{B}}T/q)  $   of the pristine device. It again indicates that the defect-assisted charge recombination is significantly reduced upon BFBAI  $  _{2}  $   doping in the PVK/ZnO bilayer.  $  ^{[38-40]}  $

To clarify the origin of the enhanced BEF in the BFBAI $ _2 $ -doped device, ToF-SIMS was utilized to explore the distribution of dopants. The obtained depth profiles along with the accordingly constructed 3D models are shown in Figure 4a and Figure S12, Supporting Information. Both profiles illustrate a regular inverted structure, as mentioned earlier. Additional  $ \mathrm{Cs_2F^+} $  signals associated with dopants can be detected for the doped device, confirming again BFBAI $ _2 $  incorporation in the ZnO and PVK layers, respectively. It is noted that the  $ \mathrm{Cs_2F^+} $  signals peak at 200 and  $ 350~s $ , respectively, much later than those of  $ \mathrm{ZnO^+} $  and  $ \mathrm{PbI^+} $  signals. As demonstrated by 3D models, the dopants are preferentially accumulated near the  $ \mathrm{NiO_x/PVK} $  and PVK/ZnO interfaces, respectively. To confirm the gradient doping of BFBAI $ _2 $  in the PVK layer, XPS depth profiling is conducted on a ITO/ $ \mathrm{NiO_x/PVK:BFBAI_2} $  film that is accessible for  $ \mathrm{Ar^+} $ -ion etching. As shown in Figure S13, Supporting Information, the F/Cs ratio remains constant (i.e.,  $ \approx 0.004 $ ) with sputtering time up to  $ 100~s $ , and then sharply increases to 0.015 with a longer sputtering time of  $ 150~s $ . This result again indicates the accumulation of BFBAI $ _2 $  dopants near the bottom of the PVK layer, in line with the ToF-SIMS analysis results.

To rationalize the gradient doping as well as passivation effect of BFBAI $ _2 $ , density functional theory (DFT) computations were conducted. The results reveal that BFBAI $ _2 $  monomers are tending to dimmerize via strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding interactions (i.e., C–F...H–N) (see Figure S14, Supporting Information). Based on literature results, $ ^{[42,43]} $  hydrogen-bonded assemblies usually induce increased surface energy relative to monomers, which may account for the accumulation of BFBAI $ _2 $  near the bottom of the PVK or ZnO film. The thermal stability of   $  BFBAI_{2}  $  -doped PeSCs was also examined at   $  85^{\circ}C  $   in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere and compared with the control devices. As shown in Figure S20, Supporting Information, the doped PeSCs exhibited a higher thermal stability, with 90% PCE remaining after 280 h, whereas the control devices retained   $  \approx 80\%  $   PCE during the same period. The above results thus prove that   $  BFBAI_{2}  $   doping is beneficial for improving humidity and the thermal stability of PVK   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , which might be assignable to the less defective PVK/ZnO layer with improved hydrophobicity.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we have developed a facile doping strategy that combines the construction of the low-defect PVK film and enhancement of BEF to improve the photovoltaic performance of inverted   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PeSCs. The incorporation of bicationic iodine dopant, namely   $  BFBAI_{2}  $ , in the PVK/ZnO bilayer leads to not only optimal crystallization of PVK but also efficient passivation for the bulk and interfacial defects. In addition, experimental and computational studies verify the gradient doping of   $  BFBAI_{2}  $   in the PVK layer as well as the generation of an additional electric field that is fully consistent with the intrinsic BEF of inverted PeSCs. As a result, the PeSCs with doped PVK/ZnO bilayer exhibit a significantly improved photovoltaic performance with the average/best PCE of 13.30/14.38%, much superior to the pristine devices with a PCE of 11.24/12.23%. Moreover, the doped PeSCs also display improved stability under ambient or thermal aging conditions. This work thus provides a simple but efficient bifunctional strategy toward highly efficient and stable   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PeSCs with an inverted configuration.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: PbI $ _2 $  (99.998%), CsBr (99.999%), and various solvents including dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO; anhydrous,  $ \geq $ 99%) and dimethylformamide (DMF; anhydrous,  $ \geq $ 99%) were purchased from Alfa Aesar. C $ _{60} $  and ZnO nanoparticle solution (2.5 wt% in ethanol) were from Suzhou Dade and Avantam, Solarmer Materials Inc., respectively. Ni(OCOCH $ _3 $ ) $ _2 $  $ \cdot $ 4H $ _2 $ O (98%) and diethanol amine (99%) were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical. 2,2'-Bis(trifluoromethyl)-4,4'-biphenyldiamine (BFBA) and hydriodic acid (57 wt% in water) were purchased from Ark Pharm Inc.

Preparation of BFBAI  $  _{2}  $  : About 300 mg BFBA (1.45 mmol) was fully dissolved in a 5 mL THF solution at 0 °C in Ar atmosphere. Then, 1 mL HI aqueous solution (57%, 7.7 mmol) was drop wise added and the mixture was stirred at 0 °C for 1 h. After the reaction, the solvent was removed under reduced pressure and the brown solid was washed with diethyl ether three times and vacuum dried to yield BFBAI  $  _{2}  $  as yellow crystalline powder (648 mg).

Preparation of PeSCs: ITO-coated glass (1.5 × 1.5 cm² with ≈15 Ω sq⁻¹) was cleaned using an established method.  $  ^{[46]}  $   The NiOₓ HTL was deposited on the ITO sheet using a solution method.  $  ^{[25]}  $   The PVK precursor solution was prepared by adding CsBr (213 mg) and PbI₂ (461 mg) in a mixed solution of DMSO:DMF (1 mL; 9/1; v/v). To prepare the PVK film, the precursor solution (80 μL) was spin coated onto the ITO/NiOₓ substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s, followed by a two-step thermal annealing process (i.e., 45 °C for 30 s and 140 °C for 2 min).  $  ^{[25]}  $   To prepare the doped PVK film with 0.8 or 1.6 mol% BFBAI₂, BFBAI₂ (5 or 10 mg) was added to PVK precursor solution, followed by similar process as earlier mentioned. Next, the ZnO and C₆₀ layers were deposited on the PVK layer using the previously reported method. To deposit the BFBAI₂-doped ZnO layer, BFBAI₂ (2 mg) was added to the ZnO nanoparticle solution (1 mL; 2.5 wt% in isopropanol), followed by a similar process as earlier mentioned. After that, Ag electrodes with 100 nm thickness were vacuum deposited. The device area was defined to be 4 mm² using a shadow mask.

Physical Characterizations: SEM images were obtained on a Hitachi SU8010 instrument. XRD patterns were recorded with  $ \lambda = 0.15406 $  nm (CuKα source) using a Bruker D8 Advance instrument. UV–vis absorption and steady-state PL spectra were recorded on Shimadzu UV2600 and Edinburgh FLS980 spectrophotometers, respectively. Transient PL spectra were measured with an Edinburgh Lifespec II instrument, using a pump light wavelength of  $ 477\mathrm{nm} $  and probe wavelength of  $ 760\mathrm{nm} $ . ToF-SIMS depth profiling was recorded using an IONTOF instrument with a  $ 30\mathrm{keV} $   $ \mathrm{Bi}^{3+} $  analysis beam. XPS and UPS measurements were carried out on a Thermo Fisher spectrometer (Escalab 250Xi) with a monochromated AlKα source. A photon irradiation with a fixed energy of  $ 2000\mathrm{eV} $  was for XPS and a He-I line with an energy of  $ 21.22\mathrm{eV} $  was for UPS.

Computational Method: The BFBAl  $  _{2}  $   dimmer was optimized using DFT with the M06-2X functional and 3-21G basis set (the M06-2X/3-21G level) using the Gaussian 09 package.  $  ^{[47]}  $   The dimerization energy was calculated at the level of M06-2X/3-21G and M06-2X/Def2QZVP, respectively, with basis set superpositon error (BSSE) correction.
