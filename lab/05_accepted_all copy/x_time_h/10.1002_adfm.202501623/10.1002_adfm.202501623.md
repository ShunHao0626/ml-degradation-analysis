# Enhancing the Stability of Perovskite Solar Cells Through an lodine Confinement Strategy in Covalent Organic Frameworks

Xiaoting Ma, Junyi Huang, Xiongjie Li, Haixuan Yu, Zhirong Liu, Yong Hu, Tianyu Sun, Yan Shen,* and Mingkui Wang*

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have shown high power conversion efficiency in solar-to-electricity conversion applications. However, the commercialization of this emerging technology is limited due to its low stability under realistic operating conditions. A major reason for this degradation is the formation and escape of iodine species upon exposure to light, polarization and thermal stress. Here, a new method is proposed to confine iodine species in the perovskite layer using a multifunctional nitrogen-rich covalent organic framework (TAPT-BP-COF). The ordered pore structure and surface binding groups of TAPT-BP-COF not only facilitate the formation of charge transfer complexes with iodine molecules, but also enable multisite chelation with undercoordinated lead ions, thereby improving the quality of the perovskite film. This iodine confinement strategy enables the resulting   $  Cs_{0.05}MA_{0.05}FA_{0.9}PbI_{3}  $   PSC devices (n-i-p) to achieve an outstanding power conversion efficiency of 25.58% (0.06 cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) under one sun illumination. The unencapsulated devices maintained over 96% of their initial efficiency after continuous maximum power point tracking for 1200 h in an   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere. Importantly, the PSCs with TAPT-BP-COF demonstrated excellent stability even under the high temperature of the ISOS-L-2 protocol, maintaining over 90% of peak efficiency at 85 °C for 800 h.

Of all mobile ions, ionic halides are the most difficult to immobilize in lead halide hybrid perovskites due to their weak binding with metal cations (such as lead ions).  $  ^{[7]}  $   Under light excitation, the generation of holes naturally promotes the oxidation of

I  $  ^{-}  $  to I  $  ^{0}  $  and finally to I  $  _{2}  $ .  $  ^{[8]}  $  I  $  _{2}  $  can easily volatilize at elevated temperature, resulting in undercoordinated Pb  $  ^{2+}  $  and metallic Pb  $  ^{0}  $  defects in the perovskite film.  $  ^{[9]}  $  Despite reports of good operational stability under continuous illumination (one sunlight intensity) at room temperature (  $  \approx  $  25 °C), it is likely possible for solar cells to reach 85 °C in real-world conditions.  $  ^{[10]}  $  In addition, the reaction between I  $  _{2}  $  and metal electrodes (such as Au or Ag) as well as the migration of I  $  _{2}  $  through charge-transporting layers have a critical impact on the photovoltaic performance and long-term stability of the device.  $  ^{[11]}  $  Even worse, too much iodine loss triggers stress-induced structural collapse and accelerates the decomposition of the perovskite into PbI  $  _{2}  $ . This is an infamous killer of perovskite integrity as it gradually replenishes the host matrix and finally leads to an irreversible degradation of the PSCs.  $  ^{[12]}  $  Therefore, preventing iodine loss is essential for the operational stability of PSCs at elevated temperatures.  $  ^{[13]}  $

Porous sorbents are most commonly used for volatile iodine treatment due to their high adsorption capacity and removal efficiency, including activated carbon, ion exchange resin, metal-organic framework, porous organic polymer, and covalent organic framework (COF).  $  ^{[19]}  $   Among these adsorbents, COFs have recently attracted great attention because of their large surface areas, tunable molecular structures, high thermal stability, exceptional physicochemical stability, and adjustable porosity.  $  ^{[20]}  $   Various COFs have been developed as efficient adsorbents for iodine capture, using nitrogen-rich functional groups,   $  \pi  $  -conjugated moieties, and ionic sites as the binding centers.  $  ^{[21]}  $   COFs with various building units constructed according to predesigned strategies have been used as channels for ions exchange,  $  ^{[22]}  $   scaffolds for perovskite growth,  $  ^{[23]}  $   modifiers for passivating the interface defects,  $  ^{[24]}  $   or inhibitors for halogens segregation.  $  ^{[25]}  $   The donor-acceptor system in COF promotes a robust charge transfer pathway, effectively suppressing charge recombination and enhancing charge separation.  $  ^{[26]}  $   He et al. exploited the ordered pore structure and abundant binding groups of COFs to cyclically chelate under-coordinated lead ions at multiple sites. This approach not only reduces defects in both bulk and grain boundaries, but also enhances energy alignment through n-type doping at the surface. These synergistic effects lead to an improved PCE of 25.64% in 0.0748 cm  $  ^{2}  $   p-i-n devices.  $  ^{[27]}  $   Yu et al. synthesized thiol-functionalized COFs to suppress ion migration at the interface, ultimately retaining 90.7% of their initial PCEs after 1000 h of maximum power point (MPP) tracking at 65 °C.  $  ^{[28]}  $   Although early research has revealed the promise of COFs to improve the efficiency of PSCs, there is no detailed study on the contribution of COFs to enhancing the stability of PSCs.

Here, we propose a versatile nitrogen-rich COF to form a crystalline "cage" that traps iodine species in the perovskite layer, thereby protecting the perovskite from irreversible iodine loss. By incorporating 2,4,6-tris(4-aminophenyl)-1,3,5-triazine (TAPT) and 2,2'-bipyridine-5,5'-dicarbaldehyde (BPDCA) into the precursor solution, imine-linked 2D COFs (TAPT-BP-COF) can be formed at the perovskite grain boundaries.[29] By integrating theoretical and experimental findings, the nitrogen-rich functional groups of TAPT-BP-COF were shown to form stable

coordination interactions with lead iodide   $  (\mathrm{PbI}_{2})  $   and under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $  . These processes are directly related to structural homogeneity, defect passivation, and electronic modifications in perovskite films. Finally, both high efficiency and excellent stability are achieved in PSCs with TAPT-BP-COF. In the   $  Cs_{0.05}MA_{0.05}FA_{0.9}PbI_{3}  $   PSCs, the target device with an n-i-p architecture achieves an efficiency of 25.58% (0.06 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ). The unencapsulated target device maintains   $  \approx96.3\%  $   of peak efficiency after MPP tracking for 1200 h under continuous 1-sun illumination at 50 °C in a nitrogen glove box. Importantly, PSCs with TAPT-BP-COF exhibit excellent stability even under high-temperature conditions. Their efficiency remains above 90% under one-sun illumination at 85 °C for 800 h.

# 2. Results and Discussion

COFs, as an emerging class of porous materials, are reported to provide an ideal platform for the development of powerful adsorbents, especially the volatile radioactive molecule iodine.  $  ^{[30]}  $   Here, TAPT-BP-COF was used to form a grain “cage” that combines with iodine substances in the perovskite layer at multiple sites (Figure 1a). TAPT-BP-COF effectively inhibits the volatilization of iodine through its interaction with iodine, thereby significantly enhancing the photothermal stability of perovskite thin films.

Experimentally, a triazine TAPT-BP-COF linked by imine bonds was successfully synthesized via a Schiff base reaction using   $  PbI_{2}  $   as a catalyst (Figure 1b).  $  ^{[22]}  $   Specifically, when the reaction solution was kept at 100 °C for 12 h with the addition of a small amount of   $  PbI_{2}  $   (0.01 M), the transparent yellow solution of TAPT-BP-COF precursor (comprising TAPT and BPDCA in N, N-dimethylformamide, DMF) transformed into a turbid gel-like substance (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The crystalline nature of the products was first examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis (Figure S2, Supporting Information). An intense diffraction peak at 2.38° was observed for the TAPT-BP-COF sample along with minor peaks at 4.11°, 4.72°, 6.29°, and 24.93° corresponding to (100), (110), (200), (210), and (001) reflection plane.  $  ^{[31]}  $   The Schiff base reaction involving TAPT and BPDCA can be confirmed by the disappearance of characteristic vibrations of N-H (in amino groups at 3207, 3323, and 3460 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ) and C=O (in aldehyde groups at 1701 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ) and the simultaneous emergence of the characteristic —C=N— stretching vibration at ≈1626 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   in the measurements of Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) (Figure S3, Supporting Information). To further investigate the formability of the TAPT-BP-COF under perovskite processing conditions (100 °C, 1 h), the reaction time was shortened from 12 to 1 h. Although the diffraction peak of the (100) plane became weaker with decreasing the polymerization time in DMF solvent, it was still present (Figure S4, Supporting Information). These results suggest the rapid polymerization of the TAPT-BP-COF with ordered structure during the perovskite crystallization process catalyzed by   $  PbI_{2}  $  .

High-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) was employed to investigate the crystal structure of TAPT-BP-COF and perovskites as well as the distribution of TAPT-BP-COF within the perovskite matrix. As shown in Figure S5a (Supporting Information), the crystalline regions of TAPT-BP-COF formed by fast polymerization have a hexagonal pore structure oriented along the c-axis of the crystal, with a lattice spacing of   $  \approx3.52  $   nm.

In the TAPT-BP-COF-doped perovskite films, HRTEM showed that TAPT-BP-COF nanosheets are uniformly distributed around the crystals of the perovskite film (Figure S5b, Supporting Information). The doped perovskite films exhibited a lattice spacing of 3.16 Å, which closely aligns with the cubic perovskite plane (001) (Figure S5c, Supporting Information). This observation indicates that TAPT-BP-COF nanosheets cannot embed into the perovskite lattice, but instead reside at the grain boundaries, effectively passivating crystal boundary defects and trapping I₂ molecules during the operating PSCs. Further evidence, as shown in Figure S6a (Supporting Information), demonstrates that COF serves as a growth scaffold for perovskites. Corresponding element mapping characterizations (Figure S6b–e, Supporting Information) clearly show the distribution of key elements in the relevant regions. Additionally, Figure S7 (Supporting Information) elucidates the self-formation mechanism of TAPT-BP-COF during the perovskite crystallization process. Specifically, PbI₂ acts as a catalyst to trigger the Schiff base reaction. During thermal annealing of the wet film, the crosslinked TAPT-BP-COF is expelled to the surface and grain boundaries.

To investigate the iodine release behavior in perovskites and the adsorption ability of TAPT-BP-COF, an iodine loss experiment was conducted. As illustrated in the inset of Figure 1c, perovskite films with and without TAPT-BP-COF were immersed in toluene and placed in sealed bottles (toluene can dissolve iodine but not TAPT-BP-COF). The sealed bottles were then exposed to continuous one-sun illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) for 100 h, and the UV–vis absorption spectra of the solutions were analyzed to evaluate the effect of TAPT-BP-COF in inhibiting halide release from perovskite films under illumination. The UV–vis spectrum

of the control perovskite solution showed a pronounced iodine absorption peak at   $  \approx500  $   nm (Figure 1c).  $  ^{[15]}  $   In contrast, the solution containing perovskite films treated with TAPT-BP-COF displayed a significantly reduced iodine absorption peak. This result indicates that TAPT-BP-COF can effectively suppress iodine release and trap it in the perovskite structure. Further experiments demonstrated that adding TAPT-BP-COF powder to the aged solution of control perovskite films and allowing it to stand for 50 min resulted in a significant decrease in the absorption peak at   $  \approx500  $   nm (Figure 1d). This finding further confirms the excellent iodine adsorption ability of TAPT-BP-COF.

This observation indicates the presence of notable intermolecular interactions between TAPT-BP-COF and iodine molecules. The calculated electrostatic potential map of TAPT-BP-COF (Figure S11a, Supporting Information) reveals that the nitrogen atoms in the triazine and bipyridine groups exhibit high electron density (red areas), indicating electrostatic attractions with iodine molecules. Further analysis shows that the binding energies between iodine and the bipyridine and triazine groups are  $ 0.01\mathrm{eV} $   $ (\mathrm{TAPT / I_2}) $  and  $ 0.05\mathrm{eV} $   $ (\mathrm{BP / I_2}) $ , respectively (Figure S11b, Supporting Information). The thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was conducted under a nitrogen atmosphere ranging from 30 to

800 °C (Figure 1f; Figure S12, Supporting Information). The TGA and DTG (derivative thermogravimetry) curves indicate that the I₂@TAPT-BP-COF composite has greater thermal stability than pure iodine. The DTG data show three distinct stages of weight-loss during thermal decomposition of I₂@TAPT-BP-COF powder. The first stage occurs at ≈153 °C with a weight loss of 4.5 wt.%. This corresponds to the release of I₂, which is physically adsorbed by TAPT-BP-COF. The second stage occurs at ≈300 °C with a weight loss of 26.6 wt.%. This corresponds to the release of I₂ via electrostatic interaction between the nitrogen-rich functional groups of TAPT-BP-COF. The third stage occurs at ≈532 °C with a weight loss of 39.5 wt.%, which corresponds to the decomposition temperature of TAPT-BP-COF. The pure iodine starts to evaporate at 70 °C and reach its maximum rate at ≈145 °C, until it completely disappears at 148 °C. We noticed that the iodine release temperature in I₂@TAPT-BP-COF is significantly higher than that of other iodine capture agents currently reported in the literatures for PSCs (Tables S1, Supporting Information).[18] This result could be caused by a strong binding force between TAPT-BP-COF and iodine molecules, which consequently impedes the escape of iodine in the operational PSCs.

Perovskite films of the formula   $  Cs_{0.05}MA_{0.05}FA_{0.9}PbI_{3}  $   were prepared without and with TAPT-BP-COF, and are referred to as control and target films, respectively. The crystallinity of the control and target films was characterized using XRD and grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) (Figure 2c,d; Figures S14 and S15, Supporting Information). In the XRD patterns, the diffraction intensity of the TAPT-BP-COF-modified film is significantly enhanced compared to the unmodified film, with the full width at half maximum (FWHM) reduced from   $  0.189^{\circ}  $   to   $  0.131^{\circ}  $  . This change indicates that the introduction of TAPT-BP-COF significantly improves the crystallinity of the film. Furthermore, the GIWAXS technique was used to examine the crystallographic orientation of perovskite films with and without TAPT-BP-COF. In the GIWAXS patterns, an intense and similar Debye-Scherrer ring at   $  q\approx10~nm^{-1}  $   can be assigned to the   $  \alpha  $   (100) plane of   $  \alpha  $  -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (Figure 2c,d). The peak at   $  q\approx9.1~nm^{-1}  $   corresponds to PbI  $  _{2}  $  . The films incorporating TAPT-BP-COF exhibited a highly crystalline   $  \alpha  $   phase with a vertically aligned orientation, whereas the control films displayed a less crystalline   $  \alpha  $   phase with a random arrangement.

To examine the impacts of TAPT-BP-COF on the optical properties and carrier recombination dynamics of the prepared films, steady-state photoluminescence (PL), UV–vis absorption, and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) intensity decay measurements were conducted on films with and without TAPT-BP-COF (Figures S16–S18, Supporting Information). The TAPT-BP-COF modified film showed a slight increase in UV–vis absorption intensity, enhancing light-harvesting efficiency (Figure S16, Supporting Information). The significantly increased PL intensities of TAPT-BP-COF modified films in Figure S17 (Supporting Information) showed an efficient suppression of nonradiative recombination in the perovskite layers. Compared to the control film, the emission bands of the TAPT-BP-COF-passivated film showed a slight blue shift of 2.6 nm (from 801.2 to 798.6 nm) and a narrowing of the linewidth from 42.1 to 41.8 nm, which indicates a reduction in defect density. Accordingly, carrier lifetimes were analyzed using a biexponential decay model function in TRPL measurements (Figure S18 and Table S1, Supporting Information). Compared to the carrier lifetime (171 ns) of the control film, the lifetime of the film was extended to 311 ns after TAPT-BP-COF modulation. The charge recombination kinetics of the TAPT-BP-COF-modified perovskite films were further assessed by femtosecond transient absorption spectroscopy (TAS) triggered by the 360 nm pump laser. The 2D pseudo-color plots in Figure 2e,f exhibited strong ground state photo-bleaching (PB)

signal (blue color) caused by carrier filling at the bandage.  $  ^{[36]}  $   The bleaching signal (green) is also broadened due to photophysical phenomena, including many-body interaction and band edge renormalization.  $  ^{[37]}  $   It is obvious that the TAPT-BP-COF-modified film shows a stronger PB intensity at 770 nm than the control, indicating an increased number of photoexcited excitons and less recombination of the photoinduced carriers in a certain time. The FWHM of the PB peak in TAS spectra at different times can be used to indicate a reduced number of background carriers due to weakened bandgap oscillations, leading to lower current leakage in the solar cell devices.  $  ^{[38]}  $   As shown in Figure S19a,b (Supporting Information), the FWHM value of the TAPT-BP-COF modified film (54 nm) is lower than that of the control film (57 nm), suggesting that there are fewer defect states and background carrier concentrations in the target sample.  $  ^{[39]}  $   The measurement of bleaching kinetics at the PB peak (770 nm) was performed to further study the carrier recombination kinetics of the perovskite films, as shown in Figure S20 (Supporting Information). The TAS decay lifetimes of 1.7 and 2.5 ns can be obtained for the control and TAPT-BP-COF-modified perovskite films, respectively, by fitting the kinetic decay traces with a bi-exponential function. The extension of exciton lifetime is due to the reduction of defect states and the suppression of nonradiative recombination, suggesting that the introduction of TAPT-BP-COF could effectively passivate the defects and enhance carrier extraction.

We optimized the device performance by varying the TAPT-BP-COF concentration. Figure S21 (Supporting Information) shows the current density-voltage (J-V) data of devices, indicating that an addition of 0.10 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  TAPT-BP-COF affords the best performance. For the following discussion, we denoted the PSC prepared without and with 0.10 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  TAPT-BP-COF as the control and target devices, respectively. Figure 3b presents the J-V characteristics and associated photovoltaic parameters (active area 0.06 cm $ ^2 $ ) under standard AM1.5G illumination of 100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $  in reverse scan (RS) and forward scan (FS). # 3. Conclusion

In summary, we proposed an iodine capture strategy to produce PSCs that work stably under photothermal conditions by introducing the iodine capture agent TAPT-BP-COF. TAPT-BP-COF can capture the light-induced iodine from the aged perovskite film in its porous network structure and form charge transfer complexes through various N species, thereby increasing the

volatilization temperature of iodine from 70 to 160 °C and further suppresses the decomposition reaction of the device during the aging process. The COF-modified devices demonstrated impressive operational stability, maintaining 96.3% of the original PCE after continuously tracking the MPP in an   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere under continuous 1-sun illumination at 50 °C for 1200 h, while the control device deteriorated to only ≈59.9% of its initial PCE within 800 h. Importantly, the PSCs with TAPT-BP-COF can maintain more than 90% of maximum efficiency even after 800 h at 85 °C.
