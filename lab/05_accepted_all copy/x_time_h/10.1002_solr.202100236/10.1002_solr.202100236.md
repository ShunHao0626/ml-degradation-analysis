# Dual-Functional-Polymer Dopant-Passivant Boosted Electron Transport Layer for High-Performance Inverted Perovskite Solar Cells

Rongguo Xu, Zhenfeng Wang, Wenzhan Xu, Xiuwen Xu, Jian Wang, Zhicheng Hu, Yu Li, Gaopeng Wang, Wanqing Cai, Shizhao Zheng, Guodan Wei, Fei Huang,* and Shihe Yang*

Herein, the use of the polymer poly([N,N'-bis(2-octyldodecyl)-naphthalene-1,4,5,8-bis(dicarboximide)-2,6-diyl]-alt-5,5'-(2,2'-bithiophene)) (PNDI-2T) as both a dopant in the [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) electron transport layer (ETL) of inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs) and a surface passivant on the perovskite layer is reported. The PNDI-2T doping is found to be crucial for improving the homogeneity of the ETL film with complete surface coverage, enhancing the electron mobility of the ETL, and promoting the energy level match between the perovskite and ETL, thereby significantly facilitating electron transport in the PSC devices. Furthermore, as a passivant, the PNDI-2T in the ETL tends to pin on the top surface of the perovskite layer via Pb–S coordination. The surface passivation is also beneficial for the suppression of ion migration and diffusion of metal species from the electrodes. In consequence, PSCs with the PCBM:PNDI-2T ETL reached an efficiency of 21.13% and retain 90% of their original performance after 860 h light soaking. This work will inspire new efforts to take advantage of multifunctional ETLs as a simple and effective method to enhance the performance and long-term stability of PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are now widely considered the most promising photovoltaic technology, and their power conversion efficiency (PCE) has skyrocketed from 3.8% to 25.5% with merely 12 years of development.  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   Such unprecedented progress is a result of extensive research on device engineering, such as component modulation,  $  ^{[4,5]}  $   solvent/antisolvent treatment,  $  ^{[6,7]}  $   and interface engineering.  $  ^{[8,9]}  $   The most extensively studied PSCs are of either the conventional n-i-p or inverted p-i-n planar-heterojunction structure, in both of which the perovskite layer is sandwiched between two opposite charge transport layers. Currently, the conventional n-i-p-structure PSCs enjoy higher PCEs, but their electron transport layers (ETLs) such as   $  TiO_{2}  $  , ZnO, and   $  SnO_{2}  $     $  ^{[10-13]}  $   require high-temperature calcination and

There is yet another simple way to modify the fullerene ETL, which is doping. For example, Yang et al. demonstrated a homogeneous ETL by simply mixing PCBM and a conjugated n-type polymer poly[(9,9-dioctyluorene)-2,7-diyl-alt-(4,7-bis(3-hexylthien-5-yl)-2,1,3-benzothiadiazole)-2',2''-diyl] (F8TBT).  $  ^{[35]}  $   The hybridization resulted in shallower energy levels, higher electron mobility, and stronger hydrophobicity, and the resulting PSCs achieved a higher PCE of 20.6% as well as better stability. Given that a passivant with a Lewis acid/base group coating on a perovskite layer has been proven effective in reducing the trap state in PSCs,  $  ^{[38,39]}  $   it appears attractive to blend the PCBM with a type of bifunctional molecule possessing both charge transfer ability and passivating groups. Here the molecules are bifunctional in the sense that they play the two roles of doping PCBM and passivating the perovskite.

Among the various passivants used for perovskites, polymers are advantageous because they can serve as a template to induce perovskite crystallization by interaction through their functional groups.  $  ^{[38,40]}  $   The long-chain structure of polymers affords multidentate interactions to optimize physical and electrical contacts of the interface, and to enable effective passivation of the surfaces or grain boundaries of perovskites,  $  ^{[39,41,42]}  $   where defects are most easily formed, often markedly improving the performance of PSCs. In this work, we introduce an n-type polymer poly  $ \left([N,N^{\prime}\text{-bis}(2\text{-octyldodecyl})\text{-naphthalene-1,4,5,8-bis(dicarboximide)-2,6-diyl}]\text{-alt-5,5'}-(2,2'\text{-bithiophene})\right) $  (PNDI-2T, Figure 1a) as a dual-functional dopant into a PCBM ETL and realize high-performance and stable PSCs. The naphthalene diimide (NDI)-based main chain endows PNDI-2T with good electron mobility. The incorporation of PNDI-2T is expected to improve the mobility of the PCBM ETL in terms of the doping function, and to improve the energy level matching between the perovskite and PCBM such that electron extraction is facilitated. In contrast, the thiophene units in the backbone of the PNDI-2T molecule can interact with the adjoining perovskite layer via Pb-S coordination, resulting in great passivation of defects in the perovskite layer. As a consequence, a perovskite device with a PCBM:PNDI-2T ETL achieves a PCE of  $ 21.13\% $  with negligible  $ J-V $  hysteresis, whereas the control device without PNDI-2T doping shows a lower PCE of  $ 19.00\% $ . Note also that the surface passivation of perovskite also ironed out the notorious ion migration problem, thus improving the stability of PSCs. This work paves a promising way for developing novel ETL materials for high-performance and long-term stable PSCs.

# 2.3. Probing the Passivating Role of PNDI-2T

Given the direct contact of the ETL containing various functional groups and the perovskite top surface with enriched defects, the possible defect passivation effects cannot be overlooked. Therefore, we further studied the PL spectra of the perovskite, perovskite/PCBM, and PCBM:PNDI-2T and found that when the samples are excited from the bottom (ITO) side, as shown in Figure S5a, Supporting Information, they all show PL emission peaks at 769 nm. However, when the samples are excited from the top (air) side, the PL peak of the perovskite/PCBM: PNDI-2T is obviously blueshifted to 766 nm, whereas the PL peaks of the perovskite and perovskite/PCBM maintain at 769 nm (Figure S5b, Supporting Information). It suggests that with PNDI-2T doping, some trap states lying in the forbidden band of perovskite can be possibly eliminated via the interaction between the PNDI-2T and the perovskite top surfaces. # 2.4. Device Stability

In addition to the enhanced PCE, the introduction of PNDI-2T in the ETL also improves the device stability. As shown in Figure 6a, the unencapsulated PSC with PCBM:PNDI-2T as the ETL shows negligible loss in PCE when stored in   $  N_{2}  $   at room temperature for 180 days. Furthermore, the long-term operational stability of the unencapsulated PSCs with and without PNDI-2T doping was also examined via maximum power point (MPP) tracking under continuous LED light illumination at 1 sun (Figure 6b). Encouragingly, after continuous operation for 860 h, the PCBM:PNDI-2T-based PSC maintains 90% of its original performance, which is much better than that of the PSCs with pristine PCBM (67%). Such a remarkable enhancement in device stability could be possibly explained by the surface passivation of the perovskite and the even and complete coverage of the PCBM: PNDI-2T, both of which could mitigate the ion migration.  $  ^{[47]}  $   Finally, we sought to verify if the ion migration is indeed suppressed in the PSCs with PNDI-2T doping. Using time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (TOF-SIMS) on the devices that had undergone 1 month aging in nitrogen, the   $  Ag^{-}  $  ,   $  I^{-}  $  , and   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  distribution depth profiles were detected. As shown in Figure 6c, the   $  Ag^{-}  $  signal can be clearly detected in the PCBM ETL, meaning that the pristine PCBM is incapable of protecting the perovskite layer from Ag penetration. In striking contrast, after doping PNDI-2T into the PCBM ETL, almost no   $  Ag^{-}  $  is observed in the ETL, and the   $  I^{-}  $  intensity is also decreased in the ETL. These results suggest that the introduction of PNDI-2T endows PCBM with a better ability to suppress ion migration in the PSCs.

Furthermore, we performed transient ionic current (TIC) measurement to estimate the mobile ion concentration (n) in the PSCs (Figure S9, Supporting Information). # 3. Conclusion

To conclude, we have successfully demonstrated a dual-functional polymer PNDI-2T to effectively modify the PCBM ETL and passivate the interface with a perovskite film. The inverted PSCs based on an optimized doping concentration of 1.0 wt% PNDI-2T achieved a high PCE of 21.13%, along with negligible J-V hysteresis. Comprehensive characterizations justified the attribution of this excellent performance to the significant improvement of electron transport between the perovskite layer and cathode, as well as the surface passivation of the perovskite. The incorporation of PNDI-2T into the PCBM ETL was also found to be vital for the suppression of ion migration and metal diffusion. Importantly, benefiting from that, the PSC with the PCBM:PNDI-2T ETL could maintain 90% of its original PCE after continuous operation for 860 h. Together, this technique of incorporating a novel polymer with a bulk dopant and surface passivant into one ETL is simple and effective and provides a promising strategy for achieving high-efficiency and long-term-stable PSCs.

Materials: Methylammonium iodide (MAI) was purchased from Dyesol. Lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ ) was purchased from TCI. Poly(bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine) (PTAA), PCBM, and 2,9-dimethyl-4,7-diphenyl-1,10-phenanthroline (BCP) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technol. Corp. Tin oxide (SnO $ _2 $ ) was obtained from Alfa Aesar. Organic solvents, including anhydrous  $ N,N $ -dimethylformamide (DMF), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and chlorobenzene (CB), were purchased from Acros. PNDI-2T $ ^{[49]} $  and PPDIN6 $ ^{[9]} $  were synthesized according to the reported literature.

Device Fabrication: ITO glass substrates were cleaned sequentially under sonication with isopropyl alcohol, acetone, and deionized water and dried in an oven at 80°C before use. After oxygen plasma treatment for 90 s, the PTAA solution with the concentration 1.5 mg mL $  ^{-1}  $  in toluene was spin coated on the ITO substrates at 4000 rpm for 30 s, followed by thermal annealing at 110°C on a hot plate in a N $  _{2}  $  atmosphere for 10 min. To prepare the 1.2 M MAPbI $  _{3}  $  precursor solution, 190 mg of MAI and 553 mg of PbI $  _{2}  $  were dissolved in a mixture with 900 μL of DMF and 100 μL of DMSO, followed by stirring at 60°C overnight. The prepared perovskite solution was spin coated on the PTAA layer at 3000 rpm for 40 s. During the spin-coating process, 200 μL of CB was dripped on the rotating substrate quickly. The precursor layers were then transferred to a hot plate for two-step thermal annealing at 60°C for 5 min and 100°C for 10 min. The PCBM and PNDI-2T were predissolved in CB and mixed with different weight percentages of 0–3.0 wt%. Subsequently, the mixed solutions were spin coated on top of perovskite layers at 2000 rpm for 30 s, followed by thermal annealing at 100°C for 10 min. The prepared BCP solution (1 mg mL $  ^{-1}  $  in IPA) was spin coated on the ETL at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, a silver electrode (80 nm) was deposited by thermal evaporation in a vacuum chamber. The active area of PSCs was 0.06 cm $  ^{2}  $ . Two types of electron-only devices based on ITO/SnO $  _{2}  $  (30 nm)/PCBM:PNDI-2T (100 nm)/PPDIN6 (20 nm)/Ag and ITO/SnO $  _{2}  $  (30 nm)/MAPbI $  _{3}  $  (400 nm)/PCBM:PNDI-2T (50 nm)/Ag were fabricated under the same process as that of PSCs.
