# Organic-Salt-Assisted Crystal Growth and Orientation of Quasi-2D Ruddlesden–Popper Perovskites for Solar Cells with Efficiency over 19%

Hongtao Lai, Di Lu, Zhiyuan Xu, Nan Zheng, Zengqi Xie, and Yongsheng Liu*

Quasi-2D Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have drawn significant attention due to their appealing environmental stability compared to their 3D counterparts. However, the relatively low power conversion efficiency (PCE) greatly limits their applications. Here, high photovoltaic performance is demonstrated for quasi-2D RP PSCs using 2-thiophenemethylammonium as spacer with nominal n-value of 5, which is based on the stoichiometry of the precursors. The incorporation of formamidinium (FA) in quasi-2D RP perovskites reduces the bandgap and improves the light absorption ability, resulting in enlarged photocurrent and an increased PCE of 16.18%, which is higher than that of reported analogous methylammonium (MA)-based quasi-2D PSC (  $  \approx  $  15%). A record high PCE of 19.06% is further demonstrated by using an organic salt, namely, 4-(trifluoromethyl)benzylammonium iodide, assisted crystal growth (OACG) technique, which can induce the crystal growth and orientation, tune the surface energy levels, and suppress the charge recombination losses. More importantly, the devices based on OACG-processed quasi-2D RP perovskites show remarkable environmental stability and thermal stability, for example, the PCE retaining   $  \approx  $  96% of its initial value after storage at 80 °C for 576 h, while only   $  \approx  $  37% of the original efficiency left for FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -based 3D PSCs.

3D perovskites, replacing methylammonium (MA) with formamidinium (FA) is an effective strategy to narrow the bandgaps due to the cations size effects and hydrogen bonding interactions, leading to high photocurrent density and enhanced efficiency.  $  ^{[6,7,31-33]}  $   Consequently, the incorporation of FA in 2D RP perovskites as well as device engineering should be efficient strategies to further improve the photocurrent and the PCEs of quasi-2D PSCs, and quasi-2D PSCs with FA-incorporated mixed cations have shown improved photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{[34-38]}  $   Nevertheless, 2D RP PSCs using FA to completely replace MA as A site cation have rarely been studied and much efforts are needed to further boost the efficiency for the future commercialization.  $  ^{[39-42]}  $   Note that the 2D RP perovskites with n = 1 or 2 compositions generally exhibit large bandgaps over 2 eV and poor charge transport properties.  $  ^{[15,42]}  $   The bandgap will reduce with the increase of n-values (n = 1 to   $  \infty  $  ) and the n-values of 2D RP perovskites are typically larger than 2 for their applications in photovoltaic devices.

In this work, we successfully fabricated high quality ThMA-based quasi-2D RP perovskite films using FA as A site cation by adopting an organic salt, namely 4-(trifluoromethyl)benzylammonium iodide (tFM-PMAI), assisted crystal growth (OACG) technique. It is found that trap density and nonradiative recombination losses of the quasi-2D RP perovskites could be greatly suppressed by using the OACG method. By performing high-resolution transmission electron microscopic (HRTEM) and grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) analysis, we found that the dominant layered-perovskite phases with different n-values and a small amount of 3D or 3D-like phases coexisted and distributed randomly in the quasi-2D RP perovskite films, and the inorganic slabs preferentially orientated perpendicularly to the substrate. An increased PCE of 16.18% was achieved for FA-based devices   $  ((\mathrm{ThMA})_{2}(\mathrm{FA})_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{3n+1}, \text{nominal } n = 5)  $   compared to the reported   $  \approx15\%  $   PCEs for analogous MA-based devices.  $  ^{[24]}  $   By using the OACG technique, a record high PCE of 19.06% was further demonstrated (nominal n = 5), combined with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.075 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 23.39 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and a fill factor (FF) of 75.8%. Importantly, the device stability of quasi-2D RP perovskites was dramatically improved compared to its 3D counterpart. Our results suggest that extended light absorption spectra of quasi-2D RP perovskites could be achieved by using FA as A site cation, which should open new avenues toward highly efficient and stable PSCs.

The precursor solution of   $  (\mathrm{ThMA})_{2}(\mathrm{FA})_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{3n+1}  $   (nominal n=5) was prepared by dissolving ThMAI, FAI, and   $  PbI_{2}  $   (molar ratio of 2:4:5) in anhydrous DMF, and MACl additive was added to help improve crystallization and film quality.  $  ^{[24,43]}  $   Note that the nominal n-value of 5 in this work is based on the stoichiometry of the precursors. As illustrated in Figure 1a, the precursor solution of   $  (\mathrm{ThMA})_{2}(\mathrm{FA})_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{3n+1}  $   (nominal n=5) was first spin-coated on glass/ITO/PEDOT:PSS substrate. Here, we use a solution of tFM-PMAI in isopropyl alcohol (IPA) as antisolvent, which we named it OACG method, to induce the crystal growth and orientation. Subsequently, the quasi-2D RP perovskite was obtained by annealing the film at 100 °C for 35 min in air. The structure of OACG-processed perovskite   $  (\mathrm{ThMA})_{2}(\mathrm{FA})_{4}\mathrm{Pb}_{5}\mathrm{I}_{16}  $   was schematically illustrated in Figure 1b. The quasi-2D RP perovskite films without (control) and with IPA as antisolvent were also fabricated for comparison

(Figure 1a). The top-view scanning electron microscope (SEM) images are shown in Figure S1a–c (Supporting Information). As shown in Figure S1a (Supporting Information), the control perovskite film without any special treatment exhibited large grain size but lots of voids. For the perovskite film processing with IPA antisolvent, the voids were reduced dramatically. Interestingly, after processing with OACG method, the voids in the film almost disappeared and high-quality quasi-2D RP perovskite film was formed, which was consistent with the tapping-mode atomic force microscopy (AFM) results (Figure S1d–f, Supporting Information). We noted that the IPA-processed and tFM-PMAI-processed perovskite films turn dark brown immediately once the solution dropped during the spin-coating process, whereas the color change of control film exhibited a visibly slower rate, indicating that IPA could accelerate the crystallization of perovskite films as IPA can slightly dissolve the perovskites and regulate the crystallization.  $  ^{[44,45]}  $   The improved film quality of OACG-processed film indicate that the crystallization kinetics could be regulated by the synergistic effects of IPA and organic salt tFM-PMAI. Note that the tFM-PMA mainly located on the surface of quasi-2D RP perovskites due to the dilute solution used in the OACG method, which was further confirmed by the XPS results discussed below. As shown in Figure 1c, the XPS spectrum reveals obvious F 1s peak for OACG-processed quasi-2D RP perovskite film as a result of the incorporation of tFM-PMA. Note that the peaks, including I 3d and Pb 4f, shifted to lower binding energy as compared to that of the control perovskite film (Figure 1d,e) due to the changed chemical environment. The results suggest that the tFM-PMA have been inserted into the surface of perovskite crystal lattice, which was further confirmed by the energy-dispersive spectrometry (EDS) measurement (Figure S3, Supporting Information), where a well-distributed F element was observed. No detectable Cl signal was observed for the control and OACG-processed quasi-2D RP perovskite films (Figure S2b, Supporting Information), suggesting that MACl mainly played the role of tuning the perovskite crystal growth in the film.  $  ^{[24,46-49]}  $   Note that a small quantity of MA may still left in the FA-based quasi-2D RP perovskite films during the process of film preparation based on the   $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR spectra (Figures S4–S6, Supporting Information), while no obvious changes for the content of MA in the MA-based quasi-2D RP perovskite film,  $  ^{[24]}  $   suggesting the MA may replace a small quantity of FA in the FA-based film by releasing FACl.  $  ^{[7]}  $

The ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) of the quasi-2D RP perovskite films further demonstrate that the

electronic structures at the surface of the OACG-processed film differs from the control film. As show in Figure S10a (Supporting Information), the work function changed from -4.55 eV to -4.60 eV for the OACG-processed film compared to the control film. The valence band maximum (  $  E_{VB}  $  ) were determined to be -5.51 eV and -5.76 eV for the control and OACG-processed films, respectively. The conduction band maximum (  $  E_{CB}  $  ) was estimated by subtracting the absolute value of VBM from the optical bandgap. As shown in Figure S10b (Supporting Information), the Fermi level (  $  E_{F}  $  ) of the OACG-processed quasi-2D RP perovskite film shifted closer to the   $  E_{CB}  $   than that of the control film, indicating a more n-type nature for the OACG-processed film. The more n-type nature could result in a larger fraction of occupied versus vacant traps and reduced trap-assisted recombination,  $  ^{[56]}  $   suggesting that the surface energy levels of quasi-2D RP perovskites could be tuned by the OACG method.

We further quantitatively determined the trap density of the quasi-2D RP perovskite films with the following equation  $ N_{\mathrm{t}} = (2\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}} V_{\mathrm{TFL}}) / (qL^2) $ , where  $ \varepsilon_0 $  is the vacuum permittivity,  $ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}} $  is the relative dielectric constant of perovskite ( $ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}} = 25 $ ),[60]  $ V_{\mathrm{TFL}} $  is the onset voltage of the trap-filled limit region,  $ q $  is the elemental charge, and  $ L $  is the thickness of the perovskite film.[61] The electron and hole trap density of the OACG-processed perovskite film were calculated to be  $ 5.74 \times 10^{15} $  and  $ 3.82 \times 10^{15} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3} $ , respectively, which were smaller than that of the control film ( $ 1.01 \times 10^{16} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3} $  for electron and  $ 4.67 \times 10^{15} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3} $  for hole), indicating that the film processed with OACG method could effectively reduce the trap density in the perovskite films and thus greatly suppressed the trap-assisted nonradiative recombination losses.

The charge collection efficiency ( $ \eta_{\mathrm{c}} $ ), including the charge transport in the perovskite layer and the charge extraction at the electrode/perovskite interfaces, could be characterized by the ratio of  $ J_{\mathrm{ph}} / J_{\mathrm{sat}} $  ( $ J_{\mathrm{sat}} $  is the saturation photocurrent density) under different  $ V_{\mathrm{eff}} $ .[65-67] The  $ J_{\mathrm{ph}} / J_{\mathrm{sat}} $  ratios were 0.936 and 0.999 for the devices based on the control and OACG-processed films under short circuit condition, respectively. The water contact angle measurements were performed to study the hydrophobic properties of the 3D and quasi-2D RP perovskite films. As shown in Figure S18 (Supporting Information), the water contact angle of 3D FAPbI3 perovskite film is  $ \approx 45.9^{\circ} $ , which is lower than that of control quasi-2D RP perovskite film ( $ 65.2^{\circ} $ ). The contact angle was further increased to  $ 71.7^{\circ} $  for OACG-processed quasi-2D RP perovskite film. The enhanced hydrophobicity can be ascribed to the improved film quality and the large-size hydrophobic organic cations incorporated in the film, which could prevent the moisture invasion and enhance environmental stability of the quasi-2D RP perovskites. Effects of direct exposure of 3D FAPbI $ _{3} $  and quasi-2D RP perovskite films in ambient conditions (30–50% relative humidity (RH), natural light at room temperature (RT)) were performed by monitoring their XRD patterns. As shown in Figure 5a, a diffraction peak at  $ \approx 11.9^{\circ} $  corresponding to the  $ \delta $ -phase FAPbI $ _{3} $  appeared in 1 h and thoroughly degraded to  $ \delta $ -phase FAPbI $ _{3} $  after 112 h for 3D FAPbI $ _{3} $  perovskite film. Importantly, no obvious changes were observed for quasi-2D RP perovskite films (Figure 5b,c), suggesting the improved environmental stability. A heat test was further performed to explore the thermal stability of 3D and

quasi-2D RP perovskite films at 100 °C in ambient air with RH of 30–50%. As shown in Figure 5d, the PbI  $  _{2}  $  diffraction peak at 12.8° was observed after 40 h and the peak intensity increased after 232 h, indicating the degradation of the 3D FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskite film. Importantly, there were no apparent changes in the quasi-2D RP perovskite films (Figure 5e,f). These results suggest that the quasi-2D RP perovskites with FA as A site cation exhibited enhanced phase, moisture and thermal stability compared to their 3D counterparts.

The long-term stability of the devices based on 3D perovskite FAPbI $ _{3} $  and quasi-2D RP perovskite (ThMA) $ _{2} $ (FA) $ _{n-1} $ Pb $ _{n} $ I $ _{3n+1} $  (average  $ n = 5 $ ) were further investigated. Figure 5g shows the evolution of PCEs of unencapsulated devices stored in ambient conditions (RH, 30 ± 5%) in the dark. After 552 h, the PCEs of 3D perovskite devices and control quasi-2D RP perovskite devices dropped to 33% and 66% of their initial values, respectively. In contrast, the devices based on the OACG-processed quasi-2D RP perovskites retained average 99% of the original efficiency, suggesting the enhanced environmental stability due to the improved film quality and hydrophobicity of the OACG-processed quasi-2D RP perovskite. Moreover, as shown in Figure 5h, the thermal stability (at 80 °C) of the quasi-2D RP perovskite devices in N $ _{2} $  was also dramatically improved by retaining ≈96% of the initial efficiency after 576 h. However, the 3D perovskite devices revealed a fast degradation rate by losing more than 63% of the original values, which is consistent with the film thermal stability measurement results discussed above. The enhanced stability of the PSCs based on OACG-processed quasi-2D RP perovskites could be attributed to the superior film quality and the incorporated large-size spacers, which could prevent moisture invasion and hinder the ion migration in quasi-2D RP perovskites. $ ^{[68,69]} $

In summary, we have successfully demonstrated a high-performance FA-based quasi-2D RP perovskite,   $  (\mathrm{ThMA})_{2}(\mathrm{FA})_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{3n+1}  $  , with nominal n-value of 5. An OACG technique using tFM-PMAI as organic salts was developed to improve the film quality and tune the surface energy levels of quasi-2D RP perovskite. The results show that the OACG-processed quasi-2D RP perovskite exhibited reduced trap density, decreased nonradiative recombination losses and enhanced charge carrier mobility. The optimized device based on the OACG-processed perovskite exhibited a champion PCE of 19.06%, which is the highest efficiency so far for quasi-2D RP PSCs to the best of our knowledge (nominal n < 6). Moreover, due to the superior film quality and the incorporated large-size hydrophobic organic spacers, the OACG-processed quasi-2D RP perovskite films exhibited dramatically enhanced environmental and thermal stabilities. Importantly, the FA-based quasi-2D RP PSCs showed remarkable environmental stability and thermal stability. For example, the PCE retaining   $  \approx96\%  $   of its initial value after storage at 80 °C for 576 h, while only   $  \approx37\%  $   of the original efficiency left for FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -based 3D PSCs. These results clearly indicate that highly efficient and stable quasi-2D RP PSCs could be achieved by choosing suitable spacer cations and using this facile and effective OACG method, thus pave the way for further boosting the efficiency and stability of PSCs toward future commercialization of this technology.

# Experimental Section

Preparation of   $  (ThMA)_{2}(FA)_{4}Pb_{5}I_{16}  $   Perovskite Precursor Solution: ThMAI, FAI, and   $  PbI_{2}  $   were mixed at a stoichiometric ratio of 2:4:5 together with adequate amount of MACI (based on the MACI/FAI weight ratio of 0.4) in a DMF solution (the concentration of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   is 1.1 M). The solution was stirred at 70 °C for 12 h and filtered using a 0.22 μm Nylon filter before use.

Device Fabrication: The inverted planner perovskite solar cells were fabricated on ITO substrates. ITO glasses were sequentially cleaned with dishwashing water, distilled water, acetone, and isopropyl alcohol. As-cleaned ITO substrates were treated with UV-ozone for 20 min before using. A PEDOT:PSS solution was spin-coated onto the ITO substrates at 5000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at 130 °C for 20 min in air. The coated substrates were then transferred to a nitrogen-filled glovebox. The perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated on the top of PEDOT:PSS-coated ITO substrates at 6000 rpm for 40 s for control samples. For the IPA-processed or OACG methods, two drops of IPA or tFM-PMAI solution (3 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  in IPA) were dropped onto the perovskite films before the end of 15 s. The samples were then annealed at 100 °C for 35 min. Subsequently, the PC  $  _{61}  $  BM (20 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  in CB) solution was spin-coated onto the perovskite layers at 1500 rpm for 40 s and BCP (0.6 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  in isopropyl alcohol) solution was spin-coated

onto   $  PC_{61}BM  $   at 5000 rpm for 60 s. Finally, 90 nm silver electrode was deposited via thermal evaporation under a pressure of   $  <1 \times 10^{-4}  $   Pa. The effective area of the device was   $  10 \, mm^2  $   defined by mask.
