# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on organometal halide perovskites as the light absorbers have experienced rapid development in the past decade, with the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) now reaching 25.2%, already comparable to the commercial silicon solar cells.  $  ^{1-5}  $   Although PSCs have achieved high performance, their stability are seriously questioned.  $  ^{6-8}  $   On the one hand, it is due to the compositional and structural instability of perovskite materials under high temperature or high humidity, the organic cations can easily escape from the lattice.  $  ^{6}  $   On the other hand, the utilization of expensive and operationally unstable organic hole transport materials (HTM) and noble metal electrode (e.g. Au or Ag) also undermine the stability of PSCs, and hinder their practical applications as a new type of renewable energy conversion device.  $  ^{9}  $   Among different strategies to improve the stability,  $  ^{10-12}  $   replacing the A-site organic cations with inorganic ions (e.g. Cs  $  ^{+}  $  ) has been proved to be an effective way to solve the intrinsic instability in perovskite materials,  $  ^{13, 14}  $   while the

# Polyethyleneimine-functionalized Carbon Nanotubes as An Interlayer to Bridge Perovskite/Carbon for All inorganic Carbon-based Perovskite Solar Cells

Yinglong Yang, $ ^{a} $  Haining Chen, $ ^{b} $  Chen Hu $ ^{a} $  and Shihe Yang $ ^{*ac} $

All inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted tremendous attention because of their intrinsic structural stability, but they suffer from the uncompetitive efficiency, especially of the carbon electrode-based PSCs (C-PSCs), wherein the insufficient perovskite/carbon contact and interfacial recombination are of particular concern. To address these problems, we designed and fabricated the polyethyleneimine-functionalized carbon nanotube (PEI/CNT), and used it as a bifunctional bridge in all inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   based C-PSCs. This functionalized PEI/CNT connects the perovskite layer and the carbon electrode to improve the quality of perovskite/carbon interface, and also works as an interfacial passivation layer due to the coordination ability of the anchored PEI molecules on CNTs. Systematic investigations revealed that the interface charge transfer resistance was reduced, and surface trap states in perovskite film were passivated. This effort led to a remarkable power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 10.55% with a high fill factor (FF) of 0.71 for the all inorganic C-PSCs. By contrast, those without the PEI/CNT bridge only attained a PCE of 7.41% and FF of 0.56. Moreover, the non-encapsulated PEI/CNT based devices also showed higher stability under high humidity or high temperature conditions. Therefore, the PEI/CNTs interface bridging strategy has the potential to achieve high efficiency and high stability of the all inorganic C-PSCs.

Among the various inorganic perovskites, the cubic phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   (  $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) is very promising because it has very suitable band gap (1.73 eV) for photovoltaic applications.  $  ^{18}  $   However, the   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   is not stable at room temperature, and can spontaneously converts into a non-perovskite yellow phase (  $  \delta  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  ), thus most   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   based PSCs show poor performance, especially of C-PSCs. Current researches are mainly focusing on how to stabilize the black   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   phase to improve the PCE. For instance, Hu et al. reported a compositional engineering approach via incorporating Bi  $  ^{3+}  $   in   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   to stabilize the   $  \alpha  $  -phase at room temperature, resulted in excellent stability as well as improved light absorption and electrical conductivity, achieving a high PCE of 13.21%.  $  ^{19}  $   Recently,   $  HPbI_{3}  $   was nominated as the precursor with the stabilization effect in the deposition of   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  . Xiang et al. concluded it into the impact from the tensile lattice strain in the   $  HPbI_{3}  $  -processed film, and achieved a PCE of 9.5% in the HTM-free C-PSCs, with super long term stability.  $  ^{18}  $   However, Ke et al. argued that what appears to be all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   stabilized in its perovskite form using   $  HPbI_{3}  $   is, in fact, the hybrid perovskite cesium dimethylammonium lead iodide (  $  Cs_{1-x}DMA_{x}PbI_{3}, x = 0.2  $   to 0.5).  $  ^{20}  $

Regardless of whatever the approach was used for stabilizing the   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   phase, the PCE of CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   based C-PSCs was still far behind those of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   based ones, mostly below 10%.  $  ^{[17, 18]}  $   Besides the phase impurity of   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  , the low photovoltaic performance of C-PSCs was also attributed to the low-quality of perovskite/carbon interface. This insufficient contact leads to deficient charge extraction and transfer at perovskite/carbon interface, and typically the fill factor (FF) in those devices was lower than 0.70.  $  ^{21, 22}  $   Moreover, trap states at grain boundaries (GBs) and surfaces as well as point defects such as interstitial defects or vacancies in the polycrystalline perovskite films can lead to strong surface recombination, resulting in severe energy losses and low performance in C-PSCs.  $  ^{23-25}  $   Thus, improving the perovskite/carbon interface quality as well as lowering the carrier recombination caused by perovskite trap states are critical to achieving high-efficiency C-PSCs. In our previous works, we optimized the perovskite/carbon interface in C-PSCs by designing a carbon nanotubes (CNTs) embedment structure, with partial CNTs implanted into perovskite crystals and formed a seamless perovskite/carbon interface, achieving super high FF exceeding 0.75.  $  ^{22, 26, 27}  $   Recently, Wang et al. reported a similar CNT bridging method by adding single-walled CNTs (SWCNTs) in both the perovskite and the carbon layers to form a high-quality perovskite/carbon interface, which not only facilitated charge extraction and transport between the two layers but also promoted the electrical conductivity of the carbon electrode.  $  ^{28}  $   In the meanwhile, it was reported that trap states in perovskite films could be passivated with some organic molecule interlayers (e.g. polystyrene, phenylalkylamine, octadecylamine, etc.), which can coordinate with Pb ions and passivate the Lewis acid traps, suppressing the interface recombination and improving the photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{29-33}  $

In this work, we combined the merits of embedment structure and passivation interlayer, and designed a polyethyleneimine-functionalized carbon nanotube (PEI/CNT) to work as a bifunctional interface bridge for   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   based all inorganic C-PSCs. This functionalized PEI/CNT not only connects the perovskite layer and carbon electrode to make better perovskite/carbon interface, but also works as interfacial passivation layer on the perovskite film. Through systematic investigation, we found that PEI molecules anchor to   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   framework by amino groups through coordination effect between the lone pairs of   $  -NH_{2}  $   (-NH-) and Pb ions of perovskite or hydrogen bonding between H atoms of   $  -NH_{2}  $   (-NH-) and iodide ions, thus passivate the surface trap states in perovskite. In devices, the interface charge transfer resistance was also reduced because of the bridged high-quality perovskite/carbon interface. This effort led to a remarkable power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 10.55% with higher fill factor (FF) of 0.71 for the all inorganic C-PSCs. By contrast, those without the PEI/CNT bridge only gained a PCE of 7.41% and FF of 0.56. Moreover, this PEI/CNT based non-encapsulated device showed higher stability under high humidity (70%) or high temperature (80 °C) in air atmosphere.

# 2. Results and discussion

The schematic device architecture of the C-PSC from a low-cost carbon paste with the PEI/CNT bridge at perovskite/carbon interface is shown in Figure 1a. A compact TiO₂ (c-TiO₂) and a mesoscopic TiO₂ (m-TiO₂) scaffold layer were deposited on the FTO glass prior to the perovskite film to work as the hole-block layer and electron transport layer, respectively. The PEI/CNT interlayer bridge was deposited and partially embedded into perovskite film during the anti-solvent dripping process. Finally, the low-cost carbon paste was deposited as counter electrode via doctor-blading. In this structure, PEI/CNT interlayer was designed to work with two functions, one is to bridge the perovskite film and carbon electrode through CNTs to achieve better quality of perovskite/carbon interface and improve charge collection and transport efficiency, the other one is to passivate the defect states on the surface region of perovskite crystal via the interaction of PEI molecules and perovskite. (Figure 1b) The PEI/CNT composite was synthesized through the schematic route in Figure 1c. In step 1, CNTs were firstly oxidized by the mixed acids (sulphuric and nitric acid) to introduce carboxylic group onto the nanotube surface. Then, PEI molecules were attached to CNTs via the dehydration reaction between carboxylic groups and amino groups.  $  ^{34}  $   PEI molecules in different molecular weight (Mw) were used in our experiment to optimize the device performance from the effect of alkyl chain length in PEI, which gave two types of PEI/CNTs for the device.

For the device performance, the critical factor affecting the device photovoltaic performance comes from the crystal quality of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $  . For the all inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   deposition, Dimethyl formamide (DMF)·HI·PbI  $  _{2}  $   was employed to replace the PbI  $  _{2}  $   as the precursor, because the DMF·HI·PbI  $  _{2}  $   processed   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film could stabilize in the perovskite phase (  $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) much easier due to the reported tensile lattice strain.  $  ^{18}  $   The as-prepared DMF·HI·PbI  $  _{2}  $   powder in this work was shown in Figure S1a, which is light yellow powder as described in other works.  $  ^{43}  $   The XRD patterns in Figure S1b compared the different phase characteristics in PbI  $  _{2}  $  , DMF·HI·PbI  $  _{2}  $   and CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  . PbI  $  _{2}  $   has the characteristic peak at 12.5°, after the reaction with HI, it

completely converted into DMF·HI·PbI $ _2 $ , which has the different new peak with PbI $ _2 $  at approximately 11 $  ^{6}  $ O $  ^{-1}  $ . The as-deposited CsPbI $ _3 $  film with this DMF·HI·PbI $ _2 $  powder as the precursor was well stabilized in the perovskite phase (black), with a tiny peak belongs to the unconverted DMF·HI·PbI $ _2 $ . This well stabilized α-CsPbI $ _3 $  excluded the device performance affected by the perovskite phase purity and guaranteed the high device efficiency.

The cross-sectioned SEM image in Figure 5a demonstrates the device structure after PEI/CNT solution dripping. The PEI/CNT was partly embedded into the perovskite layer. A more specific top-view SEM image in one selected area clearly indicates the one-side implantation state of CNT nanotube into perovskite crystal, leaving another side outside the film. (Figure 5b) Most of the outside part of CNTs were grown upward, with the length as long as several micrometres, which could be easily integrated into carbon paste during the doctor-blading process and formed the interfacial bridges. This prior embedded CNTs would work as the highway for rapid carrier separation and transport from perovskite film to the later deposited carbon electrode.

The current-voltage (J-V) characteristics of the three types of C-PSCs are displayed on Figure 5c. Data in Table 1 are the corresponding photovoltaic parameters. Obviously, the C-PSCs without the interface PEI/CNT bridge provided the lower performance, with a  $  J_{SC}  $  of 17.18 mA/cm $  ^{2}  $ ,  $  V_{OC}  $  of 0.77 V, FF of 0.56 and a PCE of 7.41%. In contrast, the C-PSCs with the PEI/CNTs (M $  _{W}  $ ~1300) as interface bridges achieved a much better performance, with the  $  J_{SC}  $  of 18.58 mA/cm $  ^{2}  $ ,  $  V_{OC}  $  of 0.80 V, FF of 0.71 and a remarkable PCE of 10.55%, where M $  _{W}  $  1300 is the molecular weight of PEI. The efficiency was improved about 42.38% in devices with the PEI/CNT (M $  _{W}  $ ~1300) interlayer compared with those without it, and that was mainly attributed to the improved FF (0.71 versus 0.56). The PEI/CNTs connected the perovskite with carbon electrode, making the interface more integrated and better contacted, which enhanced the exciton separation and carrier transport. $ ^{44} $  The device with the PEI/CNT bridge also demonstrated smaller hysteresis than that made of the control samples without PEI/CNT due to the reduced trap states in the perovskite film. $ ^{33} $  (Figure S2) When another PEI with a much higher molecular weight (750000) was used, the processed C-PSCs exhibited a much lower performance, with the  $  J_{SC}  $  decreased to 11.47% and FF decreased to 0.61, and the final PCE was only 5.51%, which can be ascribed to insulating nature of PEI. $ ^{45} $  It was reported that PEI with higher molecular weight has much longer alkyl chain and more insulating, thus may hinder the charge transport from perovskite to carbon electrode. We also compared the photovoltaic performance of C-PSCs with and without the interlayer containing only PEI molecules, and the results are shown in Figure S3. Both the  $  J_{SC}  $  and FF showed obvious improvement for the device with PEI, and the final PCE was promoted from 7.41% to 8.66%. This improvement should be attributed to the above-discussed passivation effect on CsPbI $  _{3}  $  film of PEI molecules, which reduced the interfacial charge recombination process. $ ^{33} $  The reproducibility of devices with the PEI/CNT bridge was evaluated by characterizing about 20 cells.

(Figure 5d) Compared with C-PSCs without PEI/CNT bridges, the C-PSCs with PEI/CNT bridges show higher IPCE values in the long wavelength region and their integrated photocurrent density are 15.4 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  and 17.4 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $ , respectively. The increased  $  J_{sc}  $  should be attributed to the better charge separation ability at the high-quality perovskite/carbon interface as well as reduced defect states from the passivation effect of PEI molecules in devices with the bifunctional PEI/CNT bridge.

The interfacial charge transport processes of C-PSCs without or with the PEI/CNT bridge were investigated by the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) characterization. The measurement was performed under illumination at short-circuit condition between 2 MHz and 1 Hz. The Nyquist plots for these two kinds of devices and corresponding Bode plots are demonstrated in Figure 5e-f. Only one semicircle is obtained for both devices in the Nyquist plots, and correspondingly, in the Bode phase plots, there is only a single peak detected at the high frequency area (  $  10^{4}-10^{5}  $   Hz). An equivalent circuit which fits the EIS plots is displayed in the inset of Figure 5e. Because in this measurement condition, the photocurrent is extremely higher and EIS provides information regarding the charge transfer resistance (  $  R_{ct}  $  ) and series resistance (  $  R_{s}  $  ), without obvious charge recombination process.  $  ^{46}  $   As expected, the C-PSC with PEI/CNTs as interface bridges has a smaller   $  R_{s}  $   value (20.6 Ω) than that without this interface bridges (25.9 Ω), indicating the high conductivity of CNTs. Besides, the PEI/CNT based device also has the lower   $  R_{ct}  $   value (349.3 Ω) compared with that without PEI/CNTs (407.7 Ω). As both devices contain similar structure except the perovskite/carbon interface, the difference in   $  R_{ct}  $   is mainly attributed to the different charge transfer dynamics at perovskite/carbon interface. These results reveal that C-PSCs with PEI/CNTs as interface bridges has better interface contact, enhanced charge extraction and transfer properties, which contribute to the improved photovoltaic performance in devices.

Finally, we evaluated the stability of the as-fabricated C-PSCs with the interface PEI/CNT bridges, we firstly measured the steady-state photocurrent output at the maximum power point (MPP, 0.59 V). As shown in Figure S5, the photocurrent and the corresponding PCE rise quickly to the maximum when the light was turned on, followed by a slightly decrease in a few seconds, finally stabilized during the continuous 300-s illumination. The stabilized PCE was 10.31%, well consistent with the J-V curves. The long-term stability of C-PSCs under continuous full-sun illumination at MPP tracking in air was also

compared. As indicated in Figure 6a, there was only a 11% efficiency drop after 600 h of continuous illumination measurement for the device with the PEI/CNT bridge, much better than that without the PEI/CNT bridge. We also measured the long-term stability under high humidity and high temperature conditions in air. The two kinds of unencapsulated   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   based C-PSCs without or with interlayer PEI/CNT bridges were stored in air with the humidity around 70% at at 80 °C and were tested every a few days. After two weeks, PEI/CNT based device still maintained 80% of its initial efficiency, that without PEI/CNT experienced a faster degradation and only had 68% efficiency left. (Figure 6b) This better stability can be attributed to the coordination interaction between perovskite and PEI molecules, which may improve the device stability by suppressing the perovskite decomposition and phase segregation via the increased van der Waals interactions and steric effect.  $  ^{29,31,47}  $

# 3. Conclusions

In conclusion, we have demonstrated an effective device structure for high-stability and high-performance all inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   based C-PSCs by inserting a bifunctional PEI/CNT bridging layer into the perovskite/carbon interface. With this interlayer rich in the amine functional groups, we achieved a remarkable PCE of 10.55% and obviously improved FF of 0.71. By contrast, the average PCE of C-PSCs without the PEI/CNT bridge was only 7.41%, and as expected, the FF was only 0.56. Through systematic investigations, we found that the surface trap states of perovskite were reduced and the charge transport at perovskite/carbon interface was more efficient. This PEI/CNT based device also showed its superiority in both humidity and thermal stability due to the coordination interaction and steric effect between perovskite and PEI molecules. Therefore, the bifunctional interface bridging strategy with PEI/CNTs appears to be effective for achieving high efficiency and high stability for all inorganic C-PSCs.
