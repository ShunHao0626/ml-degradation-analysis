# ABSTRACT

Methoxydiphenylamine-substituted carbazole (MODPACz) is widely used to construct hole transporting materials (HTMs) for perovskite solar cells (PSCs), whose performances highly rely on the linking way of the MODPACz units and the simplicity of the  $ \pi $ -bridge. In this paper, we report a new HTM,  $ p $ Ph-2MODPACz, using one of the simplest  $ \pi $ -bridges  $ p $ -phenylene to link the MODPACz units. The structural feature endows the  $ p $ Ph-2MODPACz with high hole mobility and conductivity, efficient hole extraction ability, and good film-forming property.  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based PSCs using doped and undoped  $ p $ Ph-2MODPACz as the HTM offer efficiencies of  $ \sim 20\% $  and  $ 16.07\% $ , respectively; both are better than the devices with the spiro-OMeTAD as HTM. The device stability of the  $ p $ Ph-2MODPACz-based PSCs is also greatly enhanced. This work demonstrates that the simplest  $ p $ -phenylene bridge for linking the MODPACz can derive a promising HTM with high device performance, providing a distinctive pathway to develop new HTMs.

# INTRODUCTION

Hole transporting material (HTM) is an essential component in perovskite solar cells (PSCs) to improve the efficiency.  $  ^{1-9}  $   Currently, spiro-OMeTAD is a widely used HTM for state-of-the-art PSCs but suffers from high cost, thermal phase transition, and unavoidable inclusion of hydrophilic dopants. A variety of alternates are thus synthesized, mostly focusing on the substitution of the spirobifluorene core, while keeping methoxydiphenylamines as peripheral hole transporting groups, also called “arms”.  $  ^{10}  $

In fact, the arm likewise plays a significant role in manipulating the property of HTMs, in particular, the hole transporting behavior. Recently, 2,7- or 3,6-methoxydiphenylamine substituted carbazole (MODPACz), with advantages of low cost, good stability and strong hole transporting ability,  $  ^{11-14}  $  is demonstrated to be a useful arm for the design of novel HTMs. Many aromatic units (  $  \pi  $  -bridges) have been reported to link the MODPACz arms with several ways as shown in Figure 1.  $  ^{15-25}  $  Among them, the HTMs with the one-arm linking way usually show poor performances for PSCs, due to the relatively low hole mobility. Meanwhile, those with three or four arms have complicated molecular configurations and are subjected to tough purifications, only giving modestly improved device performances.  $  ^{16-17}  $

Instead, the two-arms linking way has attracted more attention in designing HTMs with better device performances.  $  ^{[17,21,26]}  $   Moreover, it is found that the MODPACz-based HTMs with a simple   $  \pi  $  -bridge usually provide a higher device efficiency than those with a largely conjugated or branched one. For examples, Nazeeruddin et al. reported V886 with two MODPACz units linked by an o-dimethyenebenzene, obtaining a PCE of 16.91%;  $  ^{[19]}  $   Getautis et al. reported V997 with 2,2'-bismethylene-1,1'-biphenyl as the core, receiving a PCE of 16.50%.  $  ^{[22]}  $   However, the non-conjugated benzyl linkage in those two HTMs makes the molecules spatially distorted; the

The  $  pPh-2MODPACz  $ -based device gives very stable photovoltaic performances within 100 s exposure under the maximum power-point condition (Figure 4c). Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra were performed to understand the high performance of the PSCs with the pPh-2MODPACz as HTM. When being covered with pPh-2MODPACz and spiro-OMeTAD, the intense fluorescence of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   with an intrinsic emission at 780 nm (Figure S7) was profoundly quenched, indicating the efficient hole transfer from perovskite to HTM. The slightly enhanced PL quenching of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film was observed for the pPh-2MODPACz compared to the spiro-OMeTAD, which suggests that holes are more efficiently extracted by the pPh-2MODPACz, coinciding well with the improved hole mobility and the full surface coverage observed for the pPh-2MODPACz. The average lifetimes derived from the TRPL spectra (Figure 4e) are 432.46 ns for MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  , 35.74 ns for MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /spiro-OMeTAD, and 25.03 ns for MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /pPh-2MODPACz samples (Table S3). The pPh-2MODPACz can reduce the lifetime of holes more significantly than the spiro-OMeTAD, implying more efficient hole extractions. Furthermore, the better hole extraction of undoped pPh-2MODPACz than undoped spiro-OMeTAD was also observed from the TRPL spectra (Figure S8), which may result in the higher device performance of dopant-free pPh-2MODPACz-based PSCs.

We further investigated the stability of the pPh-2MODPACz and spiro-OMeTAD-based PSCs. Unencapsulated devices were stored in ambient air with a relative humidity of   $  \sim40\%  $  . After 1000 h storage (Figure 4f), the pPh-2MODPACz-based device remains   $  \sim85\%  $   of its initial PCE, while the spiro-OMeTAD-based one can only keep 20%. This result clearly demonstrates that the pPh-2MODPACz-based PSCs exhibit better device stability. In the case of the spiro-OMeTAD, the obvious pinholes in film observed above benefit the infiltration of water molecules to degrade the perovskite. In contrast, absence of pinholes in the pPh-2MODPACz film and its full coverage on the perovskite can prevent the penetration of moisture into the perovskite layer. # CONCLUSION

In conclusion, we have designed and synthesized a new hole transporter by linking the methoxydiphenylamine-substituted carbazole with the simplest   $  \pi  $  -bridge p-phenylene for high-performance PSCs. The obtained pPh-2MODPACz-based device gave a PCE approaching 20%, one of the highest values among PSCs containing the carbazole HTM and much higher than that from the device based on the well-known spiro-OMeTAD. Moreover, the pPh-2MODPACz-based PSCs exhibit highly enhanced device stability. More interestingly, the devices using dopant-free pPh-2MODPACz as HTM also received a high PCE of 16.07%. These results are enabled by excellent charge transporting property, full coverage on the perovskite, and strong hole extraction ability of the pPh-2MODPACz. This work demonstrates that a simple   $  \pi  $  -bridge linkage of the hole transporting units is a promising strategy to design excellent HTMs for PSCs.
