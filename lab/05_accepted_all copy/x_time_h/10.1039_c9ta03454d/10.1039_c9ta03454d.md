# Enhanced Stability and Photovoltage for Inverted Perovskite Solar Cells via Precursor Engineering

Changwen Liu  $  ^{1}  $  , Weiguang Kong  $  ^{1}  $  , Wang Li  $  ^{1}  $  , Hong Chen  $  ^{1}  $  , Dedi Li  $  ^{1}  $  , Weijun Wang  $  ^{1}  $  , Baomin Xu  $  ^{1}  $  , Chun Cheng  $  ^{1,*}  $  , Alex K. Y. Jen  $  ^{2}  $

$  ^{1}  $  Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Southern University of Science and Technology (SUSTech), Shenzhen, 518055, China

$ ^{2} $ Department of Materials Science and Engineering, City University of Hong Kong (CityU), Kowloon 999077, Hong Kong SAR E-mail: chengc@sustech.edu.cn

Keywords: precursor engineering, inverted perovskite solar cells, planar heterojunctions, FAMA, mixed-cation

# Abstract

We report on a method of precursor engineering to prepare high quality and stable perovskite based on formamidinium/methylammonium (FAMA) mixed-cations. CsI is commonly used to inhibit photoinactive phase in FAMA perovskite. However, the hydrophilic nature of CsI would result in structural instability issue under high relative humidity (RH). Besides, in inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on organic hole transport layer, low conduction band minimum (CBM) of FAMA perovskites would lead to small open circuit voltage   $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $   (  $  \sim 1.0  $   V), thus additional surface passivation/modification layers are normally employed, indicating that it is of essential necessity to improve the basic precursors for high quality perovskite films. Herein, the proposed method is realized via ternary precursor alloying which enables the perovskite with increased grain size, enhanced crystallinity, reduced trap states, and pure photoactive phase without the assistance of CsI. Without passivation/modification layers, the device   $  V_{oc}  $   is enhanced markedly from 1.0 V to 1.1 V of average, and the champion power conversion efficiency of 20.7% with negligible hysteresis is achieved. Moreover, as free of hydrophilic CsI, both the photoactive perovskite films and devices exhibit excellent stability in ambient air. Under high RH (70%), the optimized device without encapsulation only loses 16% efficiency after 1000h storage, indicating the potential for the development of efficient and stable PSCs.

# Introduction

Organic-inorganic metal halide perovskites have been receiving worldwide attention due to their excellent optoelectronic properties, e.g., high absorption coefficients, long charge carrier diffusion lengths, adjustable bandgaps, and small exciton binding energies.  $  ^{1-5}  $   Compared with traditional silicon and thin film solar cells, perovskite solar cell (PSC) develops very fast. It was first reported by Miyasaka and co-workers in 2009 with a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 3.8%.  $  ^{6}  $   Within a few short years, the state-of-art efficiency has surpassed 23%.  $  ^{7}  $   Inverted PSCs with organic hole transport layers (HTLs), due to high performance, low temperature processable (< 150°C) and small hysteresis, have excited extensive research in the photovoltaic community.  $  ^{8-11}  $   In this type of PSCs, methylammonium (MA) based perovskite, e.g., MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   or MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $  , is normally employed as light absorber.  $  ^{8,11,12}  $   However, there are concerns with respect to structural phase transition and photo/thermal instability of these perovskites.  $  ^{2,13,14}  $   In comparison, formamidinium (FA) and MA mixed-cation based perovskites, normally a combination of FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   and MAPbBr  $  _{3}  $   with different ratios,  $  ^{9,10,15}  $   behave improved optoelectronic property and stability as the result of larger ionic radius of FA and its dual-ammonia group that can effectively inhibit ion movement in the space surrounded by PbI  $  _{6}  $   octahedra.  $  ^{16}  $   However, mixing MA with FA will pull down the conduction band minimum (CBM) of perovskites,  $  ^{10,17-19}  $   which normally leads to a lower open circuit voltage (V  $  _{oc}  $  ) (~ 1.0 V) in these devices.  $  ^{9,20}  $   For instance, a V  $  _{oc}  $   of 1.02 V was achieved via optimizing the annealing temperature of HTL in the FAMA mixed-cation perovskite based inverted PSCs.  $  ^{20}  $   By surface passivation with widely used phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester on the FAMA mixed-cation perovskite, the device V  $  _{oc}  $   can be increased to 1.03 V.  $  ^{9}  $   Although V  $  _{oc}  $   can be enhanced via surface passivation,  $  ^{9,10}  $   the additional layers introduced on perovskite films inevitably complicate the fabrication process and it is of more essential necessity to improve the quality of perovskite film itself, thus it still remains challenging to enhance the V  $  _{oc}  $   of this type of devices in a simple way. Beyond that, it is known that the FAMA mixed-cation perovskites tend to form photoinactive yellow phase during the annealing process, and CsI is normally employed as phase stabilizer to guarantee the formation of photoactive black phase,  $  ^{15,20,21}  $   whereas CsI is highly hydrophilic, which would probably cause structural instability of perovskite films under high relative humidity (RH). Therefore, it is worth exploring novel precursors to prepare FAMA mixed-cation perovskite films towards improved V  $  _{oc}  $   and stability.

Herein, we report on a precursor engineering method to prepare FAMA mixed-cation based perovskite films, which has been proved to be an effective strategy to improve the device

To characterize the effect of alloying on the quality of perovskite films, X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) were recorded, as shown in Figure 2. For control perovskite (y = 0), the dominant peak at   $  14.0^{\circ}  $   indicates the black phase of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  , and the small side peaks at   $  11.6^{\circ}  $   and   $  12.6^{\circ}  $   correspond to the photoinactive hexagonal yellow phase of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   and cubic   $  PbI_{2}  $  , respectively.  $  ^{15}  $   The appearance of yellow phase confirms the incomplete phase transition in the control film, demonstrating that only   $  MAPbBr_{3}  $   hardly restrains the growth of photoinactive phase. By adding precursor C (y = 0.1), the intensity of dominant photoactive peak is enhanced, indicating improved crystallinity of perovskite film. Besides, close inspection (Inset to Figure 2a) shows that the photoinactive phase is completely eliminated with the assistance of perovskite C, while the intensity of   $  PbI_{2}  $   peak stays the same. Absorption spectra (Inset to Figure 2b) show that alloying with perovksite C does not affect the bandgap of perovskites, suggesting that the wide bandgap MAPbCl $ _3 $  in the intermediate phase is sublimed during annealing process. $ ^{19, 22} $  However, its existence is supposed to influence the quality of final perovskite films. TRPL spectra were performed to further investigate the effect of alloying on perovskite qualities. For measurement, samples were prepared by growing perovskite films on quartz. The spectra exhibit a typical bi-exponential decay behavior, and the detailed fitting parameters are listed in Table S2 in Supporting Information. The control perovskite (y = 0) exhibits a decay lifetime of 203.9 ns. Longer lifetimes are achieved from the ternary alloyed perovskites, that is, 207.6 ns and 235.5 ns for perovskite films at y = 0.1 and 0.15, respectively. The evident increase of lifetime demonstrates that alloying with C can effectively reduce charge carrier recombination in the perovskite film, indicating that the defects within the film become less with increasing y up to 0.15. $ ^{17} $  However, further increase in y results in significantly decreased lifetime of 187.9 ns, indicating that over alloying will introduce extra recombination centers into the perovskite film which are not favorable to the charge dissociation. In order to get insight into charge transfer properties, TRPL is also conducted on the perovskite/HTL samples (Figure S2 in Supporting Information). It is found that the decay lifetime is significantly reduced from 196.7 ns to 137.5 ns by increasing y from 0 to 0.15, but further increasing y leads to increased lifetime. The reduced decay lifetime indicates that charge extraction efficiency is largely improved, $ ^{24, 25} $  and the efficiency reaches the maximum at y = 0.15.

Basically, the integrated values are in agreement with the results from J-V curves (Table 1). Both the control (y = 0) and optimized (y = 0.15) devices exhibit good stability upon light exposure (Figure S4 in Supporting Information). However, compared with the control ones, the humidity stabilities of both perovskite films and devices are improved significantly, as shown in Figure 3d. The control and optimized perovskites were employed for this test in the form of films and devices, which without encapsulation were stored in dark in ambient air under a RH of 70%. Clearly, efficiency decays much slower in device based on optimized perovskite, with only 16% efficiency loss after 1000h storage. In contrast, 60% loss was observed for the control device. The stability improvement of devices stems from more stable perovskite films. As shown in the inset, after 1000h storage, the control film exhibits evident characteristic of decomposition, whereas the alloyed film is just the same as its initial appearance after annealing. The improved stability correlates tightly with the evident decrease in trap density of perovskite films, which will be studied in the following. Moreover, stability test was also carried out on the FAMA mixed-cation perovskite with Cs⁺ doping (CsFAMA) which has been widely employed both in regular and inverted PSCs,  $  ^{15,20}  $   as shown in Figure S5 in Supporting Information. After 300h storage under 70% RH in dark, it is clear that strong PbI₂ peak was appeared from the XRD pattern of CsFAMA perovskite, indicating structural instability of the film although none of the photoinactive yellow phase was observed. To get insight into charge carrier recombination within devices, light intensity (I)-dependent   $  J_{sc}  $   and   $  V_{oc}  $   were studied (Figure 3e and 3f). The correlation between   $  J_{sc}  $   and light intensity obeys the power law of   $  J_{sc} \propto I^{\beta}  $  . The fitted   $  \beta  $   is 1.12 and 1.05 for devices with y = 0 and 0.15, respectively. All of the values are close to 1, indicating no space charge effect is in the devices.  $  ^{26}  $   It is known that the deviation of   $  \beta  $   from 1 correlates closely with the nongeminate recombination within devices, and a smaller deviation indicates less recombination, thus free charge carriers can be extracted more efficiently.  $  ^{27, 28}  $   Under open circuit conditions, all the photogenerated electrons and holes finally recombine with each other. Thus the variation of   $  V_{oc}  $

upon I can reflect recombination informations within devices. The dependence of   $  V_{oc}  $   on I is described as,

$$
\frac {d V _ {o c}}{d l g I} = 2. 3 0 3 \frac {n k _ {B} T}{q}

where n,   $  k_{B}  $  , T, and q is ideality factor, Boltzmann constant, room temperature, and elementary charge, respectively. From Figure 3f, n with the values of 1.18 and 1.06 are fitted for devices with y = 0 and 0.15, respectively, indicating the Shockley-Read-Hall trap-assisted recombination within these devices.  $  ^{29,30}  $   Therefore, the closer   $  \beta  $   value and reduced n at y = 0.15 implies less trap states and reduced recombination within the device, which is consistent with the TRPL result.

As   $  V_{oc}  $   depends closely to energy band alignment in PSCs,  $  ^{5,19,31}  $   ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) was carried out to investigate the effect of alloying on energy levels of perovskites (Figure 4a). By subtracting the cutoff energy (  $  E_{cutoff}  $  ) from the He(I) energy (21.22 eV), work function can be obtained. Accordingly, the valence band maximum (VBM) of perovskite is achieved by the summation of work function and offset energy (  $  E_{offset}  $  ) that is determined from the low binding energy region in UPS. Based on the optical bandgap, one can calculate the CBM of perovskite. The energy levels shown in Figure 4b are referenced to a common Fermi level. It is clear that CBM rises evidently with the increase in y from 0 to 0.15, whereas further increasing y only leads to a minor increase of CBM. The rise in CBM is supposed to be favorable to   $  V_{oc}  $   enhancement, as   $  V_{oc}  $   correlates with the energy level difference between the CBM of perovskite and the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of HTL and normally a larger difference leads to a higher   $  V_{oc}  $  .  $  ^{32}  $   However, the device   $  V_{oc}  $   differs from this trend. It decreases rather than increases when y increases up to 0.2. This indicates that trap states must be considered, because the recombination caused by them will affect the quasi-fermi levels of electrons and holes.  $  ^{31,33}  $   To investigate the variation of trap states in the perovskites, admittance spectra (Figure 4c) were performed. The trap density (  $  N_{T}  $  ) is derived from angular frequency (  $  \omega  $  ) dependent capacitance (C) by,

$$
N _ {T} (E _ {\omega}) = - \frac {V _ {b i} d C \omega}{q W d \omega k _ {B} T}
$$

where   $  E_{\omega}  $  ,   $  V_{bi}  $  , and W is the energetic demarcation between trap energy and VBM, built-in potential, and depletion width, respectively, and   $  V_{bi}  $   and W can be obtained from the Mott-Schottky analysis as described elsewhere.  $  ^{[21, 34]}  $   As shown in Figure 4c, both shallow (  $  E_{\omega}<0.15~eV  $  ) and deep (  $  E_{\omega}>0.15~eV  $  ) trap states are significantly reduced in ternary alloyed perovskites (y=0.1

The decrease of shallow traps probably originates from the reduction of grain boundaries as the result of enlarged grain size (Figure 1),  $  ^{9}  $  while that of deep traps is due to the passivation effect of  $  PbI_{2}  $  on the surface.  $  ^{9,23,34}  $  It is evident that more reduction of deep traps is observed at y = 0.15 in comparison with y = 0.1, indicating that passivation is more thorough from  $  PbI_{2}  $  as the slight increase of  $  PbI_{2}  $  phases (Figure 2a and S1). It has been reported that manipulating perovskite films by defect passivation/elimination is favorable to enhancing the film/device stabilities.  $  ^{24,35}  $  Particularly, reducing grain boundaries would effectively suppress the migration of moisture and ionic defects through them, where their mobilities are much faster than the bulk.  $  ^{36,37}  $  Therefore, the significantly reduced shallow and deep traps can largely enhance the film/device stabilities. Note that the order of magnitude of  $  N_{T}  $  is reported of  $  10^{16}  $  for  $  MAPbI_{3}  $  and  $  10^{17}  $  for  $  FAPbI_{3}  $  at  $  E_{\omega} = 0.15  $  eV,  $  ^{21,38}  $  while  $  10^{15}  $  is achieved for the ternary alloyed perovskite (y = 0.15), indicating better qualities of this film. Similar reduction of shallow traps is also observed at y = 0.2, but the deep traps are increased markedly. In consideration of the TRPL results, it is reasonable to conclude that over residual  $  PbI_{2}  $  mainly serves as deep recombination centers to trap charge carriers, which results in evident decrease of the device  $  V_{oc}  $ . To confirm the recombination change, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was performed (Figure S6 in Supporting Information), from which selective contact resistance (  $  R_{sc}  $  ) and recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) were fitted, as shown in Figure 4d. The  $  R_{sc}  $  reduces largely with increasing y, indicating improved charge transfer between the perovskite and selective contact layer. The  $  R_{rec}  $  first increases with y from 0 to 0.15, then it drops markedly when y is increased up to 0.2. Therefore, more recombination appears at y = 0.2.

# Conclusion

In this work, a precursor engineering method was introduced to prepare high quality FAMA mixed-cation perovskite films. It is found that appropriate amount of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  alloyed with  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{MAPbBr}_3 $  enables the formation of ternary alloyed perovskite with increased grain size, enhanced crystallinity, and reduced trap states. Most importantly, it can completely inhibit the formation of yellow phase in the FAMA mixed-cation based perovskite by replacing the commonly used hydrophilic CsI that is found to be effective to stabilize the black phase but is not favorable to the structural stability of perovskite especially under high RH. Thus, the stabilities of film and devices are improved significantly. It is found that the optimized device without encapsulation only loses  $ 16\% $  efficiency after 1000h storage under  $ 70\% $  RH in ambient air. In addition, the

After optimization, a champion PCE of 20.7% with negligible hysteresis is achieved, which is comparable to the top efficiencies of devices with extra passivation/modification layers. Our major findings are expected to provide a guide to the development of highly efficient and stable PSCs in the future.
