# Bi-functional Additive Engineering for High-performance Perovskite Solar Cells with Reduced Trap Density

# Abstract

Even though organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have rapidly emerged as one of the most promising candidates for photovoltaic applications,

1

Page 3 of 25

Journal of Materials Chemistry A

simultaneously achieving controlled microstructure and subdued trap states are proven to be challenging. In this work, ammonium benzenesulfonate (ABS) is introduced as a coordination and passivation molecule into perovskite to improve the film quality and reduce defect densities. ABS can not only form the intermediate phase with precursor materials to decrease the crystallization rate, but also passivate both cationic and anionic defects at grain boundaries due to its zwitterionic structure. The trap density is significantly reduced, as demonstrated qualitatively by the confocal photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) mapping images and quantitatively by trap density of states measurement, leading to repressed trap-induced non-radiative recombination centers and elongated carrier lifetime. The highest device efficiency of 20.62% is achieved by the introduction of ABS, mainly originating from the improvement of   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF. The moisture and oxygen tolerance of unencapsulated devices are also enhanced, with only 15% degradation of the initial efficiency after storage for 1400h in ambient of   $  \approx30\%  $   humidity. This strategy offers a simple method to fabricate high-quality and less-defect perovskite films, demonstrating the potential of these high-performance PSCs for large-scale production.

# 1. Introduction

Hybrid organic-inorganic PSCs have attracted tremendous research interest due to their outstanding photoelectric properties along with cost-effective preparation processes.  $  ^{1-3}  $   Following unremitting efforts by researchers in recent years, PSCs have achieved remarkable progress with a record power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 23.3%,  $  ^{4}  $   offering potential for scaling up to production levels in the field of photovoltaic

technology. However, the intrinsic low thermal stability and low formation energy of perovskite materials release a high density of defects at the film surface and grain boundaries of polycrystalline films that are electrically detrimental.  $  ^{5-8}  $   These defects can degrade the device performance and cause instability issues due to energy disorder, reduced carrier concentration and exposed invasion sites for moisture.  $  ^{9-11}  $   Thus, it is highly desirable to eliminate the defects both to boost the PCE and prolong the durability of PSCs.

One of the major approaches to suppress the defects is to improve the film coverage and reduce the number of grain boundaries. Several strategies have been researched to regulate the surface morphology, including the manipulation of the deposition method and adjustment of the film composition. $ ^{12-20} $  These may either inevitably increase the complexity in the fabrication process hindering its large-scale production or cause phase separation upon photoexcitation due to the mixed halide perovskite component. $ ^{21,22} $  As an easier and reproducible method, additive engineering has been demonstrated to be superior in enhancing the quality of perovskite films. Some additives such as DMSO, acetonitrile and thiourea are able to coordinate with perovskite precursor materials and form the intermediate phase, which could mediate the dynamic process of crystal growth and thus reduce grain boundaries. $ ^{12,23-25} $

Of course, the grain boundaries cannot be eliminated completely no matter how the film microstructure is improved. Hence, reducing defect activity at grain boundaries has been proposed as the second approach to heal defects in polycrystalline perovskites. Different passivation molecules have been researched to interact with the charged

defects at grain boundaries. Fullerene derivatives, as good electron transport materials, could passivate the halide-induced deep traps.  $  ^{26,27}  $   Pyridine or thiourea, as electron donors, could bind to the under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions and thus passivate positively charged defects.  $  ^{25,28,29}  $   Zwitterionic compounds, trimethylammonium chloride and choline chloride are capable of passivating both positive and negative charged defects, whereas the additionally introduced halides may migrate into the perovskite lattice and lead to light-induced phase separation as mentioned above.  $  ^{30,31}  $   It thus appears that healing multiple defects in polycrystalline perovskite is necessary but very complicated. There is a scarcity of known additives to simultaneously improve the film morphology and passivate defects at grain boundaries. It is imperative to explore a bi-functional additive for fabricating high-quality perovskite films with reduced grain boundaries and less trap densities.

Herein, we introduced a novel and effective additive of ammonium benzenesulfonate (ABS) into the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  (MAPbI₃) precursor to grow large-grain and less-defect perovskite films. The ABS could act as multiple O-donor to strongly coordinate with PbI₂ and form an intermediate phase with MAI, which effectively retarded the crystallization process and produced a high-quality perovskite film. Meanwhile, the zwitterion-structured ABS remaining in the perovskite efficiently passivated both positive and negative charged defects. The negative-charged phenylsulfonate group (phenyl-SOOO⁻) as an electron donor can neutralize the positive-charged Pb clusters and uncoordinated Pb ions. The positive-charged and electron-deficient ammonium ions (NH₄⁺) are capable of passivating negative-charged

MA vacancies and Pb-I anti-sites. Visible PL and TRPL mapping characterization were used to demonstrate the reduction of trap-induced carrier recombination and the improved uniformity of ABS assisted perovskite film. The morphology improvement and passivation effect remarkably reduced the trap density of perovskite, suppressing non-radiative recombination and extending charge carrier lifetime, which increased the PCE from 17.29% to 19.64% and a highest efficiency of 20.62% was obtained for   $  FA_{0.85}MA_{0.15}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $  -based PSC. The long-term stability of PSC was also enhanced with only 15% efficiency loss after degradation for 1400 h in ambient condition. This approach provides an easy fabrication method for less defective and highly homogeneous perovskite films, showing potential for the commercialization of efficient and stable PSCs in future.

X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurement was performed to investigate the role of ABS in perovskite crystallization (Figure 2). The crystallinity of perovskite films prepared using ABS is clearly improved. Although an excessive amount of ABS slightly reduces the crystallinity, no additional peaks appear, indicating that there is no substantive second phase (Figure 2a). We further isolated the intermediate phase and compared its XRD pattern with that of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , ABS and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -ABS to find out how ABS regulates the crystal growth in the formation of perovskite film. As shown in Figure 2b, there are two new diffraction peaks for the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -ABS sample, confirming a strong interaction between  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and ABS. The multiple O donors in ABS may coordinate with  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  to form the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -ABS complex. The isolated material from the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -ABS-MAI compound exhibits new and unreported diffraction peaks, and the low-angle peaks indicate a longer interplanar distance in the crystal structure of the intermediate phase.

Thus, we speculate the MAI-PbI $ _2 $ -ABS complex as a possible intermediate compound that is formed during the deposition of perovskite film. The existence of this intermediate phase slows down the crystallization process and leads to larger grains and better crystallinity in perovskite films, well-consistent with the SEM measurements. After annealing at 100°C, the MAI-PbI $ _2 $ -ABS phase disappears, and the MAPbI $ _3 $  phase forms. The similar XRD patterns and same characteristic peak position of (110) and (220) crystal plane were observed for films with different ABS contents (Figure S2), indicating ABS has not entered the crystal structure. It is assumed that most ABS still remains in the as-prepared film and is likely to exist at the vicinity of MAPbI $ _3 $  grain boundaries and surface of MAPbI $ _3 $  films because of its high cohesive energy. $ ^{32-34} $  To check the assumption, thermogravimetry analysis (TGA) was first conducted on the ABS powder to demonstrate that ABS could not evaporate from the film after annealing at 100°C (Figure S3). In addition, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurement was performed on the ABS-1 film to determine the location of ABS, according to previous research. $ ^{35} $  The XPS depth profiles of I and S elements are shown in Figure 2c, d. The characteristic peak intensity of S2p decreases a lot after etching 60s compared to the nearly unchanged peak of I3d, suggesting the higher ABS content on the film surface than the bulk. It is well-known that the film surface consists of large amount of grain boundaries while much internal components will be exposed after etching. Therefore, it is concluded that ABS possibly locates at grain boundaries.

As illustrated in Figure 3, MAI,  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and ABS are first dissolved in the mixed solvent, then the transparent precursor solution is spin-coated on the substrate. # 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, a novel and reproducible approach based on bi-functional additive engineering has been successfully developed to fabricate large-grain and less-defect perovskite films. The trap density is characterized qualitatively and quantitatively by PL and TRPL mapping coupled with tDOS measurement. The synergistic effect of crystal growth regulation and defect passivation by ABS efficiently reduce the number of defect density and increase the carrier lifetime. The resulting ABS-1-based device using  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  as absorber demonstrates an improved PCE of  $ 19.64\% $  over that of  $ 17.29\% $  for the reference ABS-0-based device, and a highest efficiency of  $ 20.62\% $  is achieved by the  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.85}\mathrm{MA}_{0.15}\mathrm{PbI}_{2.55}\mathrm{Br}_{0.45} $ -based device. The environmental stability of PSC is also enhanced when ABS is employed. It is expected that the bi-functional additive-engineered perovskites can further promote the development of high-performance and less-defect PSCs towards large-scale commercialization.
