# Uniform Stepped Interfacial Energy Level Structure Boosts Efficiency and Stability of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   Solar Cells

Yu-Xin Luo, Feng-Ming Xie, Jing-De Chen,  $  ^{*}  $   Hao Ren, Jing-Kun Wang, Xiao-Yi Cai, Kong-Chao Shen, Lin-Yang Lu, Yan-Qing Li,  $  ^{*}  $   Yuriy N. Luponosov, and Jian-Xin Tang  $  ^{*}  $

All-inorganic  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  perovskite has attracted great attention as an absorber for perovskite solar cells (PSCs) due to its excellent thermal and light resistance. However, its device performance is restricted by the large energy level offset between  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  and the most commonly used hole-transporting layer (HTL). Herein, multicarbazolyl-substituted benzonitrile (4t-5CzBn) is inserted into the interface between  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  and HTL to form a uniform stepped (0.24 eV) interfacial energy level structure, which reduces the energy loss and boosts the hole extraction of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs. The incorporation of 4t-5CzBn induces the increase in open-circuit voltage and fill factor from 1.256 V and  $ 74.5\% $  to 1.335 V and  $ 82.3\% $ , respectively. The optimized device achieves a power conversion efficiency of  $ 17.34\% $ , which is among the highest reported values of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs. Besides the energy level tuning effect, the tert-butyl groups in 4t-5CzBn improve the moisture-resistance of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs. The unencapsulated device maintains over  $ 75\% $  of its initial efficiency after 700 h storage in air. These results demonstrate that the rational tuned energy level step benefits the performance improvement of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

The Joule heat generated during the long-term operation of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) will lead to volatilization of the organic cations, resulting in the decomposition of the perovskite film and shortening the lifetime of the device.  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   All-inorganic perovskite materials (CsPbX  $  _{3}  $  , X = I, Br, or Cl) are thus considered promising candidates to address this issue because of their excellent thermal stability.  $  ^{[4-11]}  $   Among them, the CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br perovskites have drawn much attention due to their outstanding phase stability. Moreover, the thermodynamically stable black CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br (  $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br) that featuring a bandgap of 1.92 eV is beneficial to the fabrication of high open-circuit voltage (V  $  _{OC}  $  ) all-inorganic PSCs.  $  ^{[12-14]}  $   However, although the best reported CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br PSCs have

Introducing an interfacial layer is also a reliable method to enhance the stability of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. The invasion of moisture in PSCs will be retarded by a hydrophobic interfacial layer that is deposited on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film.  $  ^{[27,28]}  $   Most organic molecules can improve device stability due to their hydrophobic nature.  $  ^{[29-31]}  $   The device efficiency will be simultaneously boosted when the introduced molecule films possess functional groups which can passivate the defects on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   surface.  $  ^{[20]}  $   Molecules with rational introduced hydrophobic substituents are more efficient in preventing the penetration of moisture in PSCs.  $  ^{[32]}  $

Inserting an energy level structure-tuning interfacial layer is promising in improving the device performance of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. However, it has not been clarified what kind of energy level structure is more suitable for the device efficiency improvement of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. Herein, multicarbazolyl-substituted benzonitrile materials (5CzBn and 4t-5CzBn) were coated on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   to construct different interfacial energy level structures. The additional tert-butyl groups in 4t-5CzBn lift the HOMO of 5CzBn, which results in the gradient interfacial energy level structure (uniform steps of 0.24 eV) of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br/4t-5CzBn/Spiro-OMeTAD  $  . Experimental results demonstrate that the uniform stepped energy level structure can suppress energy loss and boost charge extraction. The optimized   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC achieves significantly increased   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.335 eV, FF of 82.3%, and power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 17.34%, which is nearly 20% higher than that of the reference device. Moreover, the tert-butyl groups in 4t-5CzBn increase the hydrophobicity of the interfacial layer. The unencapsulated 4t-5CzBn-based device remains more than 75% of its initial efficiency after 700 h storage in the air with relative humidity (RH) of 25% at room temperature.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The multicarbazolyl-substituted benzonitrile without tert-butyl groups (5CzBn) is taken for comparison in our experiments. The molecular structure of 5CzBn is shown in Figure 1a. We synthesized 5CzBn by using stable carbazolyl and cyano units. The interaction of five adjacent carbazole fragments on 5CzBn increases the spatial resistance and reduces the molecular vibrational relaxation. The HOMO and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels of the molecule are determined by the electron-donating and electron-withdrawing moieties, respectively. For TADF materials, the weak donor implies deep HOMO and the weak acceptor suggests shallow LUMO and vice versa.  $  ^{[33]}  $   The energy level structures of CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br perovskite and 5CzBn were measured by using an ultraviolet photoelectron spectrometer (UPS). According to the results shown in Figure S2a,b, Supporting Information, the calculated VB of CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br is -5.68 eV, while the calculated HOMO of 5CzBn is -5.54 eV. The corresponding energy level arrangement is illustrated in Figure 1b. The energy level offset between CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br and 5CzBn is 0.14 eV, which is much smaller than that between CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br and Spiro-OMeTAD. X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (XPS) was used to confirm the insertion of interlayers between the perovskite layer and HTL. Notably, Spiro-OMeTAD was spin-coated on the samples and then washed by chlorobenzene to expose the surface of perovskite film. N 1s signals of various perovskite films are shown in Figure 2a. The strong N 1s signals indicate the existence of multicarbazolyl-substituted benzonitrile on perovskite films. Therefore, we conclude that the interlayers still exist on the surface of perovskite after the deposition of Spiro-OMeTAD solution. Then, the morphologies of interlayers were characterized by using an atomic force microscope (AFM). As shown in Figure 2b, the roughness of the perovskite films was significantly reduced by coating interlayers. This may benefit the physical contact between functional layers and promote carrier transport.  $  ^{[37-39]}  $   Consistent results were also obtained from the top view scanning electron microscope (SEM) images (Figure S7, Supporting Information). X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements were also carried out to study the influence of interlayer deposition on perovskite crystals (Figure S8, Supporting Information). Strong diffraction peaks at 14.6° and 29.5° that correspond to the (100) and (200) crystal planes of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  , respectively, are slightly increased after coating interlayers. It is suggested that the deposited 5CzBn and 4t-5CzBn flatten the surface of perovskite by filling the gaps between crystals rather than whittle perovskite crystals.

The positive effect of the interlayer to charge extraction was experimental demonstrated by measuring the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) profiles (Figure 3a,b). A notable quenching in

PL spectra is observed when 5CzBn or 4t-5CzBn were inserted between   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and Spiro-OMeTAD, which is ascribed to the enhanced hole extraction.  $  ^{[40]}  $   The TRPL profiles were fitted by the bi-exponential decay. The fitted PL lifetime of the perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD, perovskite/5CzBn/Spiro-OMeTAD, and perovskite/4t-5CzBn/Spiro-OMeTAD films are 19.6 ns, 14.1 ns, and 5.2 ns, respectively. The significant decrease in PL lifetime indicates the decreased resistance of hole transport and boosted hole extraction, which is consistent with PL results.  $  ^{[41,42]}  $   Moreover, the introduction of 4t-5CzBn results in a more significant change in PL intensity and PL lifetime than 5CzBn counterpart. These results reveal that a gradient interfacial energy level structure with a step around 0.24 eV benefits the charge extraction in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ /Spiro-OMeTAD system. We also computed the molecular electrostatic potential (ESP) of 5CzBn and 4t-5CzBn through density functional theory (DFT). As the results shown in Figure 3c, the cyano group is a strong electron-withdrawing group, while the groups around the benzene ring are all electron-donating groups, thus the dipole directions of 5CzBn and 4t-5CzBn with symmetrical structures are vertically upward. Therefore, the interlayer will guide the hole to the upper Spiro-OMeTAD layer. Besides, it is found that the dipole strength of 4t-5CzBn is stronger than that of 5CzBn due to the difference in number and position of tert-butyl groups (Table S1, Supporting Information). This is also responsible for the more efficient hole extraction in 4t-5CzBn-based system. The larger arcs of the Nyquist plots of 5CzBn- and 4t-5CzBn-based devices correspond to the larger recombination resistance  $ (R_{\mathrm{rec}}) $ , which indicates charge recombination is weaker in 4t-5CzBn-based device as compared with 5CzBn counterpart.[48] The fitted Nyquist plots of bare, 5CzBn, and 4t-5CzBn-based devices present  $ R_{\mathrm{rec}} $  values of 931, 1665, and  $ 3419\Omega \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $ , respectively, indicating that the charge recombination in PSCs is suppressed by inserting an interlayer and the employment of a 4t-5CzBn interlayer is more effective than 5CzBn. The results imply the small stepped energy level structure contributes to the efficient charge extraction and then reduces the energy loss in  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs.

Besides the energy level tuning effect, the tert-butyl groups in 4t-5CzBn may enhance the hydrophobicity of the interlayer and strengthen the moisture-durability of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film. Therefore, we measured the contact angles of bare perovskite, perovskite/5CzBn, and perovskite/4t-5CzBn (Figure 6a). The contact angle of perovskite film is increased from 56.7° to 82.4° after coating 5CzBn and further increased to 95.1° with additional tert-butyl groups in 4t-5CzBn. The increased contact angle indicates that the invasion of the moisture in ambient air to   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film can be effectively retarded by interlayers. Figure S16, Supporting Information, exhibits the photographs of various films stored in the ambient environment with a relative humidity of 30% at room temperature. Slower decomposition of perovskite film was observed in modified samples. The stability of constructed PSCs was also explored by recording the aging of PCE values (Figure 6b). The PCE of the reference device decays to less than 20% of its initial efficiency after being stored in an ambient atmosphere for over 700 h. The devices with interlayers show improved stability. In particular, the 4t-5CzBn-modified device maintains more than 75% of its initial PCE. In addition, the thermal stability of various PSCs was studied. Figure S17, Supporting Information, shows the thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) of 5CzBn and 4t-5CzBn. No weight loss was observed at a temperature lower than 450 °C, indicating the multicarbazolyl-substituted benzonitrile has excellent thermal stability and will not decompose during the process of device fabrication and stability testing. As the results shown in Figure 6c, after annealing at 85 °C in a nitrogen-filled glove box for over 700 h, the PCE of the reference device has decreased to 21% of its initial value, while the 4t-5CzBn-modified device remains over 50% efficiency. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, the interfacial energy level structure between   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and Spiro-OMeTAD is changed from large step to uniform small step by inserting a rational designed multicarbazolyl-substituted benzonitrile of 4t-5CzBn. The 4t-5CzBn layer with an intermediate energy level can reduce the energy loss and boost the hole extraction of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. Due to the defect passivation effect of cyano groups in 4t-5CzBn, the modified device shows a significantly improved device performance with   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.335 V, FF of 82.3%, and PCE of 17.34%, which is over 20% higher than that of the reference device. Besides, the tert-butyl groups in the 4t-5CzBn effectively slow down the destruction of perovskite by retarding the penetration of moisture into perovskite film. The storage stability and thermal stability of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs are simultaneously enhanced by using 4t-5CzBn interlayer. Our results have demonstrated that the uniform stepped energy level structure can improve the electrical properties and promote the efficiency of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs.
