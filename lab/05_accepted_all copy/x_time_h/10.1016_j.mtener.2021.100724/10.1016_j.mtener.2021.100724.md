# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have recently become the hottest topic for the next-generation solar devises, due to the low energy of formation, long exciton diffusion length, extraordinary charge mobility, weakly exciton binding energy, and low-cost fabrication   $  [1-6]  $  . To date, most reported power conversion efficiency (PCE) surpassing 22% is based on mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   as electron transport layer (ETL) in PSCs   $  [7-9]  $  , which needs a high-temperature process (  $  >450\ ^{\circ}C  $  ) and hinders its application in flexible and large-area devices. Recently, the highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is up to 25%. Interfacial engineering strategy is an efficient route for improving carrier extraction and transport in planar heterojunction PSCs. Herein, we use the rare earth (RE)-doped WO  $  _{x}  $   nanorods/SnO  $  _{2}  $   hybrid films as the electron transport layer (ETL) to accurately modulate the interfacial charge dynamics for hysteresis-free, high-performance devices. The RE-WO  $  _{x}  $   nanorods with SnO  $  _{2}  $   can improve the perovskite crystal quality and hinder electron-hole recombination by reducing the interface traps/defects between the ETL and perovskite. Meanwhile, owing to the excellent mobility and conductivity of RE-WO  $  _{x}  $   nanorods and the well gradient band alignment, the improved electron extraction and transport at the interface of ETL and perovskite in PSCs are obtained compared with the SnO  $  _{2}  $   ETL. With these desirable properties, the optimized PSC device yields a high PCE of 21.42% with negligible hysteresis. Meanwhile, the stability of PSC devices could also be significantly boosted without any encapsulation. The introduction of RE-WO  $  _{x}  $   nanorods as the interface modifier is a significant strategy to improve the interface property in the PSCs and opens up a novel and promising avenue for application in efficient and stable PSCs.

# ABSTRACT

issue, the planar type PSCs consisting of a compact ETL have been rapidly developed through a low-temperature synthesis process, owing to the long exciton diffusion length and bipolar carrier properties of perovskites   $  [10–16]  $  . However, the best PCE (over 23%) of planar-type PSCs using   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL cannot compete with the mesoporous-type PSCs using   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL, which is mainly limited by the low conductivity, interfacial defects, and imperfect energy band alignment   $  [15,17–19]  $  . Apart from the PCE, trap-assisted nonradiative recombination also dramatically affects device hysteresis and stability   $  [20–22]  $  . It is expected that the planar-type PSC with better performance will be achieved by perfecting interfacial charge extraction and transfer properties between the ETLs and perovskite layer.

under ultraviolet (UV) irradiation [23–27], and exhibits good prospects to promote the PCE and stability of PSCs. A few groups have attempted to use the   $  WO_{x}  $   as the ETL. However, their PCEs are less than 20.5% with high hysteresis, which mainly is caused by the imbalanced charge transport at the ETL/perovskite interfaces [28–30]. Graded design of carrier transport layer also plays a vital role for balancing the charge transfer resistance and suppressing the hysteresis for   $  WO_{x}  $  -based PSCs, but only a few articles have been reported so far [26]. Ma's group prepared the amorphous   $  TiO_{x}-WO_{x}  $   film as the ETL with the enhanced light transmittance and excellent conductivity and obtained a high PCE of 14.47% [25]. Zhang's group prepared the amorphous a-  $  WO_{x}/SnO_{2}  $   layer hybrid ETL for high-performance PSCs [27]. Through the interfacial engineering to improve charge extraction and mobility and suppress the interface recombination loss, the PSCs show a high PCE of 20.5%. These results demonstrate that   $  WO_{x}  $   is a potential material for modifying the interfaces of ETL. Moreover, the previous encouraging works motivate us to dope metal oxides with the lanthanides (  $  Nd^{3+}, La^{3+}, Y^{3+}, Sm^{3+}, Eu^{3+}, Dy^{3+}, Er^{3+}, Yb^{3+}  $  ), which has been demonstrated to be an effective mean to enhance the electronic and interfacial properties and related device performance [31–34].

Herein, we used the rare earth (RE)-doped  $ \mathrm{WO_x} $  nanorods to modulate the interface of  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  ETL and perovskite layer for high-performance planar PSCs. Lanthanide ions  $ (\mathrm{La}^{3+},\mathrm{Sm}^{3+},\mathrm{Eu}^{3+},\mathrm{Dy}^{3+}, $   $ \mathrm{Er}^{3+},\mathrm{Yb}^{3+}) $  -doped  $ \mathrm{WO_x} $  were prepared by the hot solvent method. RE-  $ \mathrm{WO_x} $  nanorods could not only improve the electron mobility and conductivity of ETL but also enhance the electron extraction and injection between ETL and perovskite film through the proper energy band alignment, passivation of traps/defects, and suppressing hole-electron recombination at the interface. Finally, the  $ \mathrm{La - WO_x / } $   $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  hybrid ETL-based planar PSCs exhibit the optimized PCE of  $ 21.42\% $  , in comparison to  $ 19.08\% $  for the control PSCs. The  $ \mathrm{La - WO_x / } $   $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  based PSCs also show significantly enhanced device stability. Such hybrid ETL demonstrates superiority in achieving highly efficient and stable PSCs by simple interface engineering.

# 2. Results and discussion

First, the oleic acid (OA)-modified   $  WO_{x}  $   and RE-  $  WO_{x}  $   nanorods were prepared by the typical hot injection method (see the Supporting Information) [35]. Fig. 1(a–g) display the transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images of the   $  WO_{x}  $   nanorods and RE (  $  La^{3+}  $  ,   $  Sm^{3+}  $  ,   $  Eu^{3+}  $  ,   $  Dy^{3+}  $  ,   $  Er^{3+}  $  ,   $  Yb^{3+}  $  )-  $  WO_{x}  $   nanorods, respectively. As displayed in Fig. 1(a), the nanorod-shaped   $  WO_{x}  $   is fabricated, but their diameter and length are nonuniform. After doping RE ions into   $  WO_{x}  $  , the uniform   $  WO_{x}  $   nanorods were obtained as seen in Fig. 1(b–g), which might be attributed to the   $  Cl^{-}  $   ions adsorbing onto the surfaces of the initial nanorod nuclei with   $  RECl_{3}  $   as additives and stabilizing the rod shape [36,37]. Meanwhile, the size of RE-doped   $  WO_{x}  $   nanorods becomes small. Fig. 1(h) shows the high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) image of La-  $  WO_{x}  $   nanorods. The clear fringe spacing is determined to be ~3.83 Å, corresponding closely to the (010) diffraction plane of the monoclinic phase structure of   $  WO_{x}  $   (3.76 Å) in the inset of Fig. 1(a). The crystalline lattice constants of   $  WO_{x}  $   and RE-  $  WO_{x}  $   nanorods for the (010) crystal plane are listed in Table S1. Notably, as the atomic number of lanthanide ions (  $  La^{3+}  $   to   $  Yb^{3+}  $  ) increases, the lattice constants inversely decrease from 3.83 to 3.77 Å. The energy-dispersive X-ray analysis (EDX) mapping of La-  $  WO_{x}  $   nanorods was detected to analysis the elements, as shown in Fig. S1. It can be seen that the element of La distributes homogeneously in the sample.

Fig. 1(i) displays the X-ray diffraction (XRD) spectra of RE-WO  $  _{x}  $   nanorods. Most of the diffraction peaks agreed with the standard card JCPDS 05-0392 of the monoclinic phase WO  $  _{x}  $  . As compared to the WO  $  _{x}  $  , a slight shift of the diffraction peak at (010) toward lower

angle is observed for the RE-WO $ _{x} $ , indicating the expanding of lattice constants due to the substitution of smaller W $  ^{(+5,+6)}  $  ions (0.65 Å and 0.41 Å) by larger RE $ ^{3+} $  ions (1.061 Å–0.848 Å) with the lanthanide radius contraction property [38]. And the shift levels in RE-doped WO $ _{x} $  decrease with the increase of RE atom order. This is the reason that the ionic radius of the lanthanide elements gradually decreases with the increase of atomic number. This suggests that the RE $ ^{3+} $  ions are effectively doped into the WO $ _{x} $ . Furthermore, the optical properties of WO $ _{x} $  and RE-WO $ _{x} $  are investigated by the absorption spectra in Fig. 1(j). They exhibit strong absorption intensity in the region of 300–500 nm. Fig. S2 shows that the corresponding band gaps of RE-doped WO $ _{x} $  become larger than those of the WO $ _{x} $ . This is dominated by the quantum confinement effect, and the size of RE-doped WO $ _{x} $  clearly decreases compared with the WO $ _{x} $  in Fig. 1(a–g). The slight increase of band gaps among the RE-doped WO $ _{x} $  nanorods is due to the increase of binding energy between anions and cations caused by the decreasing lattice constants [39].

Because the long alkyl chain (OA ligand) on the surface of RE-WO $ _{x} $  nanorods could limit the carrier transport and the conductivity as they are incorporated in optoelectronic device. Thus, according to the previous report [40], the original surface capping OA ligands of the RE-WO $ _{x} $  nanorods are replaced with the methylammonium iodide (MAI), which as the perovskite precursor could provide favorable seed-like sites to boost the syntheses of perovskite crystals [40]. To determine the surface properties of the RE-WO $ _{x} $  nanorods, we measured the Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra. Fig. S3(a) displays the FTIR spectra of RE-WO $ _{x} $  nanorods and MAI-capped RE-WO $ _{x} $  nanorods. The peaks centered at 2,925 and 2,855 cm $ ^{-1} $  are attributed to the C–H stretching vibration, and those at 1,539 and 1,458 cm $ ^{-1} $  are assigned to the C=O stretching of the OA ligands, which almost disappear in the MAI-capped RE-WO $ _{x} $  nanorods, indicating that most of the OA ligands capped on the surface of RE-WO $ _{x} $  nanorods are removed after the ligand-exchange treatment [40]. Furthermore, we demonstrated the successful ligand-exchange process of the RE-WO $ _{x} $  nanorods through the XPS spectra in Fig. S3(b–d). As displayed in Fig. S3(b), the W $ ^{5+} $  and W $ ^{6+} $  ions are identified in La-WO $ _{x} $  nanorods. Fig. S3(c) illustrates that the La 3d spectra still exist in the La-WO $ _{x} $  nanorods and MAI-capped La-WO $ _{x} $  nanorods, suggesting the La $ ^{3+} $  ions doped into the WO $ _{x} $ . In addition, the I 3d spectrum after ligand exchange is observed in Fig. S3(d), but it does not show any peak of I 3d in the as-prepared sample, further confirming the ligand exchange from OA to MAI. All these results demonstrate the successful preparation of MAI-modified RE-WO $ _{x} $  nanorods.

Fig. 2(b) displays the corresponding cross-section scanning electron microscopy (SEM) image. As is well known, the electron mobility of ETLs plays a vital role for the high-performance PSCs. To further demonstrate the influence of RE-WO $ _{x} $  on the performance of PSCs, we investigated the optical properties of perovskite films. Fig. 3(c) displays the absorption spectra of SnO $ _{2} $ /perovskite films and SnO $ _{2} $ /RE-WO $ _{x} $ /perovskite films. It demonstrates that the absorption intensity in the whole visible region of 300–800 nm increases in SnO $ _{2} $ /RE-WO $ _{x} $ /perovskite films, attributed to the high-quality crystallization of the perovskite film. The photoluminescence (PL) spectra of SnO $ _{2} $ /RE-WO $ _{x} $ /perovskite films in Fig. 3(d) further demonstrate weaker PL emissions than those of the SnO $ _{2} $ /perovskite films, implying the improved charge extraction and suppressed carrier recombination loss at the ETL/perovskite interface [44]. Long-term stability is a vital factor for commercial applications in current PSCs research. The stability of the devices is all monitored under operating conditions as well as under dark storage (16–25 °C, 20–30% humidity) in Fig. 6. Fig. 6(a) presents the continuous operation at MPP of the PSCs under continuous AM1.5G (100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) irradiation. As expected, the control devices showed significant less than 40% loss of its initial performance after 500 h. On the other hand, the devices based on SnO  $  _{2}  $  /La-WO  $  _{x}  $   exhibited much better stability (87.8% of its initial PCE) after 500 h under continuous light soaking. Fig. S13 shows the XRD patterns before and after light soaking for 500 h. Notably, the PSCs based on SnO  $  _{2}  $  /La-WO  $  _{x}  $   are still intact, which demonstrates that the photostability of PSCs is improved after RE-WO  $  _{x}  $   modification. Furthermore, we investigated the durability of unencapsulated PSCs under the ambient air. The device performance of the control devices significantly degraded with a drop in efficiency after 1,200 h, whereas the PSCs based on SnO  $  _{2}  $  /La-WO  $  _{x}  $   maintained 91.1% of the initial performance within 2,000 h in Fig. 6(b). Fig. 6(c) compares J-V curves

of the PSCs based on   $  SnO_{2}/La-WO_{x}  $   after stored for 2,000 h. It can be seen that the PSCs show a small loss and hysteresis-free behavior, which suggests the high-quality crystal structures of the PSCs using the   $  SnO_{2}/La-WO_{x}  $   film. The aforementioned results prove that using   $  SnO_{2}/La-WO_{x}  $   hybrid film as the ETL could largely boost the stability of the PSCs. As characterized previously, we attribute the prolonged stability to the high quality of perovskite film with better crystallinity, full surface coverage, larger grain size, as well as reduced pinholes and grain boundaries after the   $  La-WO_{x}  $   modification, and reduced interface traps/defects to hinder the moisture permeation at grain boundaries.

# 3. Conclusions

In conclusion, we have successfully demonstrated an effective strategy to modify the ETL via incorporating RE-  $  WO_{x}  $   nanorods for achieving high device performance and stability of PSCs. The La-  $  WO_{x}  $   nanorods with   $  SnO_{2}  $   not only improve the crystal quality of perovskite with better crystallinity, full surface coverage, and larger grain size but also enhance the conductivity, effective charge extraction, and excellent charge recombination resistance by reducing the interface traps/defects. Thereby, the   $  SnO_{2}/La-WO_{x}  $  -based PSCs present significantly enhanced photovoltaic properties compared with the control device. The optimized device yields a PCE value of 21.42% with negligible hysteresis, compared to those of 19.08% in   $  SnO_{2}  $  -based devices. At the same time, the long-term ambient stability and light stability of PSC devices are significantly improved. The introduction of RE-  $  WO_{x}  $   as the interface modifier is beneficial to the interface properties of the PSCs and provides a novel and effective way to improve the performance and stability of PSCs.
