# Metal–Organic Framework-Derived N-Rich Porous Carbon as an Auxiliary Additive of Hole Transport Layers for Highly Efficient and Long-Term Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Xuesong Zhou, Lele Qiu, Ruiqing Fan,  $  ^{*}  $   Ani Wang, Haoxin Ye, Changhao Tian, Sue Hao, and Yulin Yang  $  ^{*}  $

As the standard bidopants of hole transport layers (HTLs) in perovskite solar cells (PSCs), bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine not only induce adverse influence on the quality of thin films, but also seriously impair the long-term stability of devices. Herein, a metal–organic framework-derived 2D graphitic N-rich porous carbon (NPC) is first introduced into the HTLs as an effective auxiliary additive. The introduction of NPC significantly reduces the aggregation of lithium salts and the formation of HTL defects, optimizing film quality for rapid hole extraction and migration. Furthermore, inherent porosity and hydrophobicity of NPCs are extremely beneficial to restrict the permeation of Li  $  ^{+}  $  ions and anode metals, and prevent the moisture from eroding the HTLs and perovskite layers, enhancing the stability of PSCs. As expected, the PSCs with NPC realize a satisfactory fill factor of 0.76 and power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 18.51%, apparently higher than that of pristine devices (0.70% and 16.47%). In addition, over 85% of the initial PCE for optimized PSCs is maintained after 720 h of exposure to air. Obviously, an innovative strategy for highly efficient and long-term stable PSC devices is provided.

Currently, atmospheric pollution, global warming, and shortage of nonrenewable energy have seriously threatened the survival of human beings. As the times require, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been developed and aroused wide concern for their low cost, structural diversity, and high power conversion efficiency (PCE).  $  ^{[1]}  $   However, most of researches focus on the electron transport layers (ETLs) and perovskite layers, whereas the hole transport layers (HTLs) are largely overlooked.  $  ^{[2]}  $

Recently, metal–organic framework (MOF)-derived N-rich porous carbons (NPCs), which inherit adjustable pore properties and high specific surface area from MOF precursors and achieve excellent electrical conductivity and chemical/mechanical stability of carbon species, have emerged as valuable materials for electrochemical applications, such as lithium-ion batteries (LIBs), hybrid super capacitors (HSCs), water-splitting electrolyzers, and

fuel cells.  $  ^{[4]}  $   In particular, 2D highly graphitic porous carbon-based nanomaterials with rich pyridinic-N doping are considered to be of abnormal electrochemical activities, compared with 1D nanotubes or 3D nanocages.  $  ^{[5]}  $   Nevertheless, to our knowledge, 2D NPCs derived from single MOF precursors are rarely reported, and have never been introduced into PSCs, which promotes us to introduce such materials into HTLs as the auxiliary additives.

Herein, we report a class of planar heterojunction PSCs with a graphitic NPC as the auxiliary additive in HTLs (Li-TFSI/t-BP-doped Spiro-OMeTAD), which is obtained from the direct pyrolysis of a 2D In-based MOF In-Aipa,   $  [\mathrm{In}_{2}(\mathrm{Hipa}-\mathrm{NH}_{2})_{2}(\mathrm{ipa}-\mathrm{NH}_{2})_{2}]\cdot5\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}  $   (  $  H_{2}ipa-NH_{2}=5-  $  aminoisophthalic acid), as shown in Figure 1. Such devices exhibit a fill factor (FF) of 0.76 and a satisfactory PCE of 18.51%, whereas that of conventional PSCs are 0.70% and 16.47%, respectively. In addition, the PSCs with NPC possess superior long-term stability against ambient conditions.

In contrast, bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP), which are necessary to activate the major hole transport material, usually 2,2',7,7' tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD), for the rapid hole transport, may potentially impair the stability of PSC devices because of the hygroscopicity and ion behavior.  $  ^{[3]}  $   Given this background, it is extremely meaningful to develop an auxiliary additive of HTLs for high-performance PSCs.

The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) image in Figure S4a, Supporting Information, indicates that In-Aipa crystals exhibit blocky morphology stacked by plenty of sheets, corresponding to its microscopic 2D layered structures. The wide-angle powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) measurement for bulk samples of In-Aipa provides a strong support for its high phase purity (Figure S4b, Supporting Information). In addition, the porosity of In-Aipa is measured by  $ \mathrm{N}_2 $  adsorption-desorption test, displaying a type I isotherm (Figure S4c, Supporting Information), which is related to typically microporous adsorption. More direct evidences for the microporosity of In-Aipa could be achieved from the pore size distribution curve (Figure S4d, Supporting Information). Meanwhile, the Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) surface area ( $ S_{\mathrm{BET}} $ ), Langmuir surface area ( $ S_{\mathrm{Langmuir}} $ ), total pore volume ( $ V_{\mathrm{pore}} $ ), and pore size have been evaluated to be  $ 1373.8\mathrm{m}^2\mathrm{g}^{-1} $ ,  $ 1552.4\mathrm{m}^2\mathrm{g}^{-1} $ ,  $ 0.63\mathrm{cm}^3\mathrm{g}^{-1} $ , and  $ 0.889\mathrm{nm} $ , respectively, and summarized in Table S3, Supporting Information.

Encouraged by the self-provided amino nitrogen source, microscopic 2D layered structures, and the macroscopic sheet stacked blocky morphology, it is believed that In-Aipa is an excellent single MOF precursor for the preparation of 2D N-rich carbon-based nanomaterials. In this regard, as-synthesized In-Aipa crystals are carbonized under   $  N_{2}  $   flow at 800 °C, followed by being soaked in hydrofluoric acid (HF) solution to dissolve deposited In(III) species.  $  ^{[6]}  $   As shown in Figure 2a, the resulting NPC samples show a certain degree of deformation, compared with the original In-Aipa crystals. Notably, the sheet stacked structures of In-Aipa precursor are inherited by the NPC and can be easily identified under the

high-resolution SEM. In addition, the NPC samples display an obviously smaller thickness, which should be related to the exfoliation between crystal interlayers when partial constituents of MOF precursor gasify as the temperature rises, yielding enough pressure to break the   $  \pi\cdots\pi  $   interaction in In-Aipa crystals.  $  ^{[7]}  $   Subsequent transmission electron microscopy (TEM) observation suggests distinct mesoporous structures of NPC samples, due to the gasification of partial constituents and the removal of In(III) species (Figure 2b). For further confirmation, the   $  N_{2}  $   adsorption–desorption isotherm of NPC has been investigated (Figure 2c). The sharp uptake at a low relative pressure (  $  P/P_{0}  $  ) indicates the microporosity, whereas the gentle absorption of   $  N_{2}  $   and the appearance of the hysteresis loop at a   $  P/P_{0}  $   range over 0.45 are attributed to the capillary condensation of mesopores.  $  ^{[8]}  $   The pore size distributions for NPC (Figure 2d) also confirm that micromesoporous structures have been well developed by thermally carbonizing In-Aipa crystals. The   $  S_{BET}  $ ,   $  S_{micro}  $ , and   $  V_{pore}  $   of NPC are shown in Table S4, Supporting Information. Obviously, the NPC exhibits a smaller surface area (1028.4 m  $  ^{2}  $   g  $  ^{-1}  $  ) and a bigger pore volume (1.49 cm  $  ^{3}  $   g  $  ^{-1}  $  ) than that of parent In-Aipa crystals (  $  S_{BET}=1373.8  $   m  $  ^{2}  $   g  $  ^{-11}  $   and   $  V_{pore}=0.63  $   cm  $  ^{3}  $   g  $  ^{-11}  $ , respectively) because of the collapse of partial microporous structures and the formation of mesopores.  $  ^{[9]}  $

The clear lattice fringes under high-resolution TEM with a typical distance value of 0.35 nm, related to the (002) crystal plane of graphite, reveal that such NPC possesses abundant layered graphitic structures.  $  ^{[10]}  $   Two broad peaks centered around 25° and 43° in wide-angle X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern of NPC can be, respectively, attributed to the diffraction of graphitic carbon (002) and (101) crystal planes (Figure S5a, Supporting Information).  $  ^{[11]}  $   Moreover, Raman spectrum of NPC exhibits two broad peaks at 1359 (D-band) and 1585 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   (G-band) with an intensity ratio (  $  I_{D}/I_{G}  $  ) of 0.873 (Figure S5b, Supporting Information), further confirming the high graphitization of NPC. Subsequently, the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) of NPC is implemented to determine the embedded forms of N atoms in carbon matrix (Figure S5c,d, Supporting Information). The N 1s spectrum can be theoretically decomposed into two peaks, centered around 401.0 and 398.4 eV, respectively, corresponding to the characteristic feature of graphitic-N and pyridinic-N. The NPC material has been characterized by cyclic voltammetry (CV) to evaluate its highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy level. According to the relation of   $  E_{HOMO} = -(E_{\mathrm{onset,ox}} \text{ vs } Fc/Fc+1 + 5.1)(\mathrm{eV})  $  ,   $  E_{HOMO}  $   of Spiro-MeOTAD and NPC are determined to be -5.11 and -5.36 eV, respectively (Figure S6, Supporting Information).  $  ^{[12]}  $   The more negative   $  E_{HOMO}  $   than that of Spiro-MeOTAD demonstrates that such NPC material is beneficial to the hole extraction of HTLs from perovskite layers, as shown in Figure S7, Supporting Information. In addition, the conductivities of HTLs with different NPC contents have been investigated (Figure S8, Supporting Information), showing gradually improvement over the pristine Spiro-OMeTAD film as the NPC content increases, which further proves the feasibility of introducing NPC into the HTLs.

In summary, we first introduce an MOF-derived 2D graphitic NPC as an auxiliary additive of HTLs for highly efficient and long-term stable PSCs, and have convectively demonstrated that the introduction of NPC can effectively improve the film properties of HTLs, supporting the optimized hole extraction and migration, and the passivated carrier recombination. Benefiting from the above favorable contributions, the NPC-added PSCs exhibit satisfactory PCE of 18.51% and FF of 0.76, higher than that of pristine devices (16.47% for PCE and 0.70 for FF, respectively). More notably, better long-term stability of over 720 h under exposure to air without encapsulation is observed for the improved PSCs, which can be plausibly attributed to the uniform film formation and high hydrophobicity of NPC-doped HTLs, restricting the permeation of   $  Li^{+}  $  ions and anode metals and preventing moisture from eroding the devices. We believe that this innovative strategy is of important guiding significance for developing highly efficient and long-term stable PSCs by introducing MOF-derived NPC into the HTLs.
