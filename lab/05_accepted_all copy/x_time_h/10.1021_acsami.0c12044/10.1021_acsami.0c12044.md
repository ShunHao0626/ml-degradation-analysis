# ABSTRACT

Long-term thermal stability is one limiting factor that impedes the commercialization of perovskite solar cell. Inspired by our prior results from machine learning, we discover that coating a thin layer of 4,4'-dibromotriphenylamine (DBTPA) on top of a  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  layer can improve the stability of resultant solar cells. The passivated devices kept  $ 96\% $  of the original power conversion efficiency for  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  in  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $  atmosphere without encapsulation. Near-ambient pressure x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (NAP-XPS) was employed to investigate the evolution of composition and evaluate thermal and moisture stability by in-situ studies. A comparison between pristine  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films and DBTPA-treated films shows the DBTPA treatment suppresses the escape of iodide and methylamine up to  $ 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  under 5 mbar humidity. Furthermore, we have used ATR-FTIR and XPS to probe the interactions between DBTPA and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  surface. The results prove that DBTPA coordinate with perovskite by Lewis acid-base and cation- $ \pi $  interaction. Compared with the  $ 19.9\% $  efficiency of pristine sample, the champion efficiency of passivated sample reaches  $ 20.6\% $ . Our results reveal DBTPA as a new post-treating molecule that not only leads to the improvement of photovoltaic efficiency but also thermal and moisture stability.

KEY WORDS: halide perovskite, post-treatment, ambient pressure XPS, thermal stability, solar cells

# INTRODUCTION

During the past few years, organic-inorganic halide perovskites have drawn remarkable attention in photovoltaics  $  ^{1-5}  $  . Since the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) were invented in 2009  $  ^{6,7}  $  , the certificated power conversion efficiency (PCE) was improved dramatically and exceeded 24% recently  $  ^{8}  $  . Nonetheless, the poor long-term ambient stability still holds back the commercial application of PSCs. Moisture, heat, oxygen and UV light can cause film degradation  $  ^{9,10}  $  . Especially, methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ), a prototypical halide perovskite, is not thermally stable even at 85 °C, an officially used temperature to test the stability of solar cells (IEC61646).

Various in situ techniques have been applied for understanding thermal induced degradation mechanisms  $  ^{11}  $  . Conings has used conductive AFM to prove crucial surface decomposition occurs when heating   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   at   $  85^{\circ}C  $   even in inert atmosphere  $  ^{12}  $  . Fan et al. has observed surface-initiated decomposition in in situ TEM  $  ^{13}  $  . Kim et al. has used in situ grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray diffraction (GIWAXRD) and high-resolution X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (HR-XPS) measurement, and observed the appearance of the intermediate phase with re-oriented   $  MA^{+}  $  cations before thermal decomposition started   $  80-100\;^{\circ}C^{14}  $  . Huang and co-workers have employed near-ambient-pressure XPS (NAP-XPS) for the investigation of surface composition at elevated temperature under differ vapor/gas environment and observed significant loss of surface nitrogen started at   $  100\;^{\circ}C^{15}  $  . All these studies have drawn a similar conclusion that the thermal degradation processes are initiated at surface and then progressively to its bulk, and the decomposition occurs through the evaporation of MAI and production of lead iodide. Hence, surface passivation and encapsulation of volatile MAI are efficient approaches for enhancing the thermal stability of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  .

Organic molecules with special functional groups have been reported to coordinate with perovskite surfaces to improve thermal stability. As an interesting example, caffeine was employed for boosting perovskite thermal stability through the interaction between   $  Pb^{2+}  $   and C=O bonds, and the treated device can maintain high efficiency after heating treatment at   $  85^{\circ}C  $   in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere for   $  1300\ h^{16}  $  . Wang et al. presented theophylline can passivate perovskite by interacting with antisite lead. After encapsulation, their devices retained over 90% of the original efficiency at   $  40^{\circ}C  $   and 30-40% relative humidity  $  ^{17}  $  . ZnPc was reported to post-treat the perovskite film and the grain boundary was modified. The treated perovskite devices maintained 90% of the initial efficiency over 1000 h.

Their results found ZnPc restrain migration of cation through interaction between ZnPc and perovskite  $  ^{18}  $  . Fluorinated perylenediimide was introduced on perovskite to immobilize MA  $  ^{+}  $   through hydrogen bonding, the passivated device can remain 70% of initial PCE after 100°C treatment for 24h at 50% RH condition  $  ^{19}  $  . In addition, lead sulfate and phosphate were reported to stabilize the perovskite surface through the reaction between perovskite and sulfate or phosphate ions, the encapsulated device had 96.8% of the original efficiency with irradiation for 1200 h at 65°C  $  ^{20}  $  . Furthermore, rubrene can improve the stability of perovskite by cation-  $  \pi  $   interaction which immobilizes the organic cation in PSCs  $  ^{21}  $  .

Considering amine molecules are commonly used for passivating halide perovskites, we have recently applied machine learning to predict the compatibility of amines with the perovskite films  $  ^{22}  $  . Among the compatible amines, 4,4'-dibromotriphenylamine (DBTPA) stands out for the extraordinary compatibility and stability. Herein, DBTPA is applied to surface treatment of the perovskites film MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  , for the purpose of passivating perovskite surface, and therefore improving stability and efficiency of PSCs. NAP-XPS is employed to monitor the heating and moisture-induced decomposition to demonstrate the enhanced stability and efficiency of passivation. After post-treatment, the PSCs can retain over 96% of the original efficiency after 1000 h at 85 °C in N  $  _{2}  $   atmosphere without encapsulation. The enhanced average efficiency of 19.6% is obtained for DBTPA passivated perovskite, and the best PCE reaches 20.6%. This work demonstrates the all-round surface passivation is an efficient channel for long-term stable devices with high efficiency.

To further probe the interaction between  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films and DBTPA molecules, high-resolution XPS measurements were employed for the pristine  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film, DBTPA film and DBTPA passivated  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film on Si wafer substrate. The films were first deposited on Si substrates (1 cm×1 cm), and then half of the area was coated with a layer of gold as reference using a plasma sputtering coater. Figure 5a shows Pb 4f spectra for  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/ $  DBTPA. The Pb 4f peaks for the untreated  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  sample contains separated features located at  $ 143.07\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ 138.20\mathrm{eV} $ , which are attributed to  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  species from  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+}-\mathrm{I}^{-} $ bonding in perovskite[21]. As compared with the spectra for the pristine sample, the Pb peaks from  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/ $  DBTPA shifted to lower binding energies (142.56 and  $ 137.69\mathrm{eV} $ ), indicating the interaction between Br atoms from DBTPA molecules and Pb atoms on perovskite surface[16,31]. Specifically, the Br atoms with lone electron pairs can donate electron density to the under-coordinated Pb atoms with net positive charges, leading to a decrease of binding energies from Pb 4f peaks and an increase of binding energies from carbon-bonded Br 3d peaks. A similar result was reported with the modification by caffeine[16]. DBTPA molecules presented two kinds of Br species in Figure 5b. The peaks located at  $ 70.88\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ 69.84\mathrm{eV} $  can be attributed to the carbon-bonded Br species in DBTPA molecules, and the peaks located at  $ 69.02\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ 67.98\mathrm{eV} $  can be ascribed to chemisorbed Br species, which may originate from the dissociation of C-Br bonds possibly induced by X-ray beam [32,33]. However, for the DBTPA passivated  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  sample, only Br-C species from DBTPA was observed, which probably suggests an enhanced stability of DBTPA by interacting with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ . Moreover, the peaks of Br from DBTPA shifted to  $ 71.12\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ 70.08\mathrm{eV} $ , in higher binding energy. These results further indicate electron donating effect from the lone pair of Br atom in DBTPA molecules to the under-coordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  ions on the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  surface. N 1s spectra of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ , DBTPA and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/ $  DBTPA are shown in Figure 5c. # CONCLUSION

In conclusion, effective surface passivation of perovskite was realized by coating a thin layer of DBTPA via post-treatment. Both of the under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   and organic cation induced defects are passivated. Thus, the defect was passivated and the migration of ions was suppressed. The interaction between DBTPA and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   increased the stability of solar cell. Consequently, the passivated sample maintained over 96% of the original PCE for more than 1000 h at 85 °C in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere with no encapsulation. In the meantime, the highest 20.6% PCE was achieved in the best-performing devices. This observation demonstrates the all-round surface passivation is an efficient method for long-term stable and high-efficiency PSCs.

Perovskite Film Fabrication. The fabrication method of perovskite samples is based on an anti-solvent strategy. The details can be seen in our previous work  $  ^{22}  $  . 1 mol/L PbI  $  _{2}  $   and 1 mol/L MAI were dissolved in DMF and DMSO with a volume ratio of 4:1. The resulting solution was then stirred for 2 h under nitrogen atmosphere to achieve a yellow precursor solution. The substrate (FTO or silicon wafer) was sonicated in acetone, ethyl alcohol and isopropyl alcohol for 15 min. Dried by N  $  _{2}  $  , FTO or silicon wafer were treated by UV ozone for 15min. Then the clean substrate was transferred to a spin-coater (Laurell Technologies Corporation) for film fabrication. The as-prepared PbI  $  _{2}  $   and MAI solution (50   $  \mu  $  L) was placed on the FTO or silicon wafer, then had spin-coating for 30 s at 5000 rpm. To achieve good morphology of perovskite films, CB was added as an anti-solvent 6 s after starting spinning. Finally, the mirror-like perovskite films can form after heating at 100 °C for 10 min.

Perovskite Film Passivation. 4,4'-dibromotriphenylamine was dissolved in CB to prepare a 0.3 M solution. For passivation, 100   $  \mu  $  L of as-prepared solution was added on the top of a perovskite film, then had spin-coating at 5000 rpm for 25 s. After that, the samples were transferred to a hot plate, with thermal treatment at 80 °C, for 300 second for solvent removal.

Solar Cell Fabrication. The details can be seen in our previous work  $  ^{18}  $  . A 2.0 cm×2.0 cm pattern formed on FTO by etching. The substrates were then sonicated in water for 15 min. Titanium isopropoxide (0.15 M) dissolved in isopropyl alcohol was spin-coated onto the clean FTO glass at 3000 rpm for 25 s to deposit a compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   blocking layer. Heating at 100 °C (15 min) and then at 500 °C (30 min) were applied. After the temperature decreased to around 25°C, the obtained samples were soaked in   $  TiCl_{4}  $   solution for 15 min at 70 °C. Dried by   $  N_{2}  $   flow, a   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste dispersed in isopropyl alcohol (1:7 in weight) was spin-coated on compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   film at 3000 rpm for 30s, which was subsequently annealed at 500 °C for 30min. After building   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers, a perovskite layer was fabricated on top of them (see perovskite film fabrication). For passivated film, 4,4'-dibromotriphenylamine could be applied on   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   layers (see perovskite film passivation). Then 72.3 mg Spiro-MeoTAD, 4-tert-butylpyridine (30 μL) and lithium salt solution (20 μL, from 520 mg Li-TFSI in 1 mL acetonitrile) were added in chlorobenzene (1mL). The as-prepared solution was spin-casted on   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   (5000rpm, 30 s). Gold with a thickness of 80 nm was vacuum-deposited as a final layer. All the fabrications of films were conducted in a nitrogen-filled glovebox.
