# Abstract

Despite three-dimensional (3D) perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have high power conversion efficiency (PCE) beyond 22%, they are unstable under humidity, oxygen and ultraviolet light. Two-dimensional (2D) perovskites exhibit high stability but have poor photovoltaic performance. To simultaneously enhance the PCE and stability of PSCs, we designed new mixed-dimensional (MD) PSCs by introducing HOCH $ _2 $ CH $ _2 $ NH $ _3 $ I (EAI) into (FAPbI $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.85} $ (MAPbBr $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.15} $  3D perovskite. The MD devices made with [(EA) $ _2 $ PbI $ _4 $ ] $ _x $ [(FAPbI $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.85} $ (MAPbBr $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.15} $ ] $ _{1-x} $  exhibit outstanding photovoltaic performance with an improved PCE of 18.79% due to more outstanding crystal structure, superior surface morphology, enhanced charge transport and reduced carrier

# Introduction

In recent few years, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on organic–inorganic metal halide perovskite materials have shown unprecedented progress with power conversion efficiency (PCE) more than 22%, which have become the hope of optoelectronics and next-generation thin-film solar cells.  $  ^{1-7}  $

However, PSCs face great challenge of long-term stability, which has seriously hampered their further business development.  $  ^{8-14}  $   It was reported that UV light can cause increase of oxygen vacancy on surface of   $  TiO_{2}  $   to enhance deep traps, resulting in degradation of perovskite.  $  ^{15-18}  $   When temperature was elevated, the research shows that metal counter electrode and small molecules in air may diffuse into perovskite layer and the additives in spiro-OMeTAD (such as   $  Li^{+}  $   and TBP) could also migrate in perovskite internal and react with   $  I^{-}  $ , leading to degradation of perovskite and

declining of photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{19-22}  $   In addition, when perovskites are exposed to high moisture environment, they may form hydrated perovskite phases, such as monohydrate   $  (\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{PbI}_{3}\cdot\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O})  $   and dihydrate   $  ((\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{4}\mathrm{PbI}_{6}\cdot2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O})  $  . Eventually, due to the interaction between water molecules and highly hygroscopic methylammonium cations resulting in the dissociation of bonds among crystal units, perovskites may decompose to   $  PbI_{2}  $  .  $  ^{23-26}  $

In efforts to improve stability of PSCs against UV light, heat and moisture, various methods have been proposed, including: replacing the hole transport layer (HTL) or electron transport layer (ETL), incorporating the   $  Rb^{+}  $   and   $  Cs^{+}  $  , coating photocurable fluoropolymers and introducing the additives, and so on.  $  ^{27-31}  $   Recently, two-dimensional (2D) perovskite systems have got wide attention and have displayed tremendous advances due to their high moisture resistance.  $  ^{32-35}  $   In 2014, Smith et al.  $  ^{32}  $   reported layered PSCs based on   $  (\mathrm{PhC}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{5}\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{2}(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{2}\mathrm{Pb}_{3}\mathrm{I}_{10}  $   with enhanced moisture stability. In 2015, Cao et al.  $  ^{34}  $   found 2D homologous perovskites   $  (\mathrm{CH}_{3}(\mathrm{CH}_{2})_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{2}(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{3n+1}  $   (n=1, 2, 3, and 4). Subsequently, the 2D and mixed-dimensional (MD) perovskites based on   $  (\mathrm{CH}_{3}(\mathrm{CH}_{2})_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{2}(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{3n+1}  $   became the research hotspot.  $  ^{34-40}  $   Until now, 2D and MD PSCs made with   $  (\mathrm{CH}_{3}(\mathrm{CH}_{2})_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{2}(\mathrm{MA})_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{3n+1}  $   have shown great PCE and markedly improved light, heat and humidity stability.  $  ^{35,39}  $

Although these MD PSCs exhibit high stability, the PCE is still low. Therefore, finding a new MD perovskite material which have high PCE and high outstanding stability against UV light, heat and moisture at the same time is current challenge. In this study, we develop innovative MD perovskite materials made with   $  [(EA)_{2}PbI_{4}]_{x}[(FAPbI_{3})_{0.85}(MAPbBr_{3})_{0.15}]_{1-x}  $   and use them as light-absorbing layer to fabricate solar cells. The MD perovskite devices show excellent photovoltaic performance and superior stability against humidity, heat and UV light. When the 2D

The photovoltaic performance of PSCs can be affect by the carrier lifetimes. We also measured the electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) to further understand the charge transfer and recombination behavior of in the devices. Generally, at high temperature, the performance degradation of PSCs is mainly caused by the internal diffusion of small molecules from metal electrodes, air and spiro-OMeTAD, and the escape of organic components from perovskite materials after enduring the thermodynamic process.  $  ^{19-22,44,45}  $   Here, due to the same structure of the normal and MD perovskite devices, the change of heat stability is mainly resulted from perovskite layer. The thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) curves of the normal and MD perovskite are displayed in Fig. 6b. The normal perovskite begins to lose the weight at about 275.99 °C which is belonged to the loss of FAI/MABr, whereas, the MD perovskite has a higher initial decomposition temperature at 296.69 °C, attributing to the escape of FAI/MABr/EAI. The higher initial decomposition temperature for MD perovskite means that it needs more energy accumulation before decomposition and has longer time to undergo high temperature.  $  ^{44,45}  $   That is to say, the MD perovskite is more thermally stable than the normal perovskite is. Furthermore, the XRD patterns of the normal and MD (x=3%) perovskite films before and after exposing to 85 °C were performed (Fig. S14). As for the stability under UV light, it has been reported that oxygen vacancies on   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface under continuous UV irradiation will produce deep traps. # Conclusion

In conclusion, we have fabricated novel MD PSCs made from   $  [(EA)_{2}PbI_{4}]_{x}[(FAPbI_{3})_{0.85}(MA-PbBr_{3})_{0.15}]_{1-x}  $  . By carefully adjusting the 2D perovskite content, we successfully obtained high quality film with outstanding crystal structure, superior surface morphology, enhanced charge transport and reduced carrier recombination. Then superior MD perovskite devices with an improved PCE of 18.79% were fabricated. Meanwhile, the MD perovskite devices show superior stability under humidity, heat and UV light. # Experimental Section

# Synthesis of FAI and HOCH $ _2 $ CH $ _2 $ NH $ _3 $ I

FAI: Formamidine acetate (0.1 mol, 98%, Sinopharm) and hydriodic acid (0.1 mol, 57wt% in water, sigma-aldrich) were reacted by stoichiometrically and were stirred at 0 °C for 2 h. By rotary evaporation of the solvent at 60 °C under reduced pressure, the primrose yellow powder was obtained. Then it was recrystallized twice by dissolving in ethanol, cleaned by diethyl ether and collected by filtration. Finally, the product was completely dried at 60 °C in a vacuum oven for 12 h.

$ \mathrm{HOCH}_2\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  (EAI): Hydroiodic acid (0.1 mol,  $ 57\mathrm{wt}\% $  in water, sigma-aldrich) reacted with ethanolamine (0.1 mol,  $ 99\% $ , Sinopharm) about 2h with stirring constantly, the subsequent preparation steps are the same as the above.

# Device fabrication

FTO-coated glass was sonicated using detergent and ultrapure water, and then rinsed three times with ultrapure water and ethanol. By spray pyrolysis at 430 °C, a compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer which was composed with 0.4 mL bis(acetylacetonate) and 0.6 mL titanium diisopropoxide in 8 mL isopropanol was coated on the FTO. The   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste (30 nm, dyesol) was diluted with ethanol (  $  TiO_{2}  $  :ethanol =1:5.5 by weight), and then it was spin-coated on the compact layer at 4000 rpm for 20 s.

After the spin coating, the substrate was slowly sintered from room temperature to 510 °C for 3h on flattening oven. 1.4 M (Pb $  ^{2+}  $ ) perovskite precursor solutions were obtained by dissolving the stoichiometrically corresponding perovskite powders (FAPbI $  _{3}  $ ) $  _{0.85}  $ (MAPbBr $  _{3}  $ ) $  _{0.15}  $  and [(HOCH $  _{2}  $ CH $  _{2}  $ NH $  _{3}  $ ) $  _{2}  $ PbI $  _{4}  $ ] $  _{x}  $ [(FAPbI $  _{3}  $ ) $  _{0.85}  $ -(MAPbBr $  _{3}  $ ) $  _{0.15}  $ ] $  _{1-x}  $  into DMSO and DMF mixed solvent (DMF: DMSO=4:1 by volume).
