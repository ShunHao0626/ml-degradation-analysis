# Suppressing the defect through thiadiazole derivatives modulating  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  crystal growth for highly stable perovskite solar cells under dark

Hongwei Zhu  $  ^{\ddagger ab}  $  , Fei Zhang  $  ^{\ddagger ab*^{\#}}  $  , Yin Xiao  $  ^{ab}  $  , Shirong Wang  $  ^{*ab}  $   and Xianggao Li  $  ^{*ab}  $

The stability of perovskite solar cells is still one issue to be solved, though it has achieved a remarkable success since its first report in 2009. In this work, two thiadizole derivatives (1,3,4-thiadiazoleidine-2-thione, TDZT and 1,3,4-thiadiazolidine-2,5-dithione, TDZDT) were added into perovskite precursor, to modulate  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite crystal growth. Thiadizole derivatives can improve the size of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  crystal exceeded to  $ 800~\mathrm{nm} $  and reduce grain boundaries. The defect density calculated by space charge limited current (SCLC) method of perovskite was 7 times decreased when adding certain amount of TDZDT. As a result, we obtained a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 19.04\% $  accompanied by excellent stability under humidity and heat. The unsealed devices sustained  $ 91.2\% $  of the initial PCE after  $ 1200\mathrm{h} $  in  $ 65\pm 5\% $  relative humidity at room temperature under dark and over  $ 90\% $  of the initial PCE after  $ 840\mathrm{h} $  aging in  $ 30\% $  relative humidity ambient air at  $ 60 - 75^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  under dark, respectively. The result showed that thiadizole derivatives can effectively suppress the defect, thus improving the efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells.

# Introduction

Hybrid organic/inorganic perovskites have attracted much attention in the photovoltaic community due to their excellent unique properties such as high electron/hole mobility, long diffusion lengths and wide spectral absorption range, pushing their performances to a record power conversion efficiency (PCE) beyond 22%  $  ^{1}  $  . The perovskite materials was firstly reported to be used in solar cells in 2009 and the efficiency

was only 3.8%  $  ^{2}  $  . A promising strategy to solve this problem is to use a solid-state hole-transport layer reported in 2012. The solid state perovskite solar cells (PSCs) was fabricated using 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) as the hole transporting materials (HTMs) and the PCE was 9.7% along with a stable performance over a timescale exceeding 500 hours under ambient conditions  $  ^{3}  $  . From then on, PSCs have made more and more progress in device fabrication  $  ^{4}  $  , new materials development  $  ^{5,6}  $   and theoretical research  $  ^{7}  $  , but the stability is still a main issue which limits its commercial applications. Therefore, controlling the quality of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   crystal to prepare a stable active layer and improve the stability of the PSCs have become the focus of research. One mostly used method is to add some compounds, such as solvent  $  ^{8}  $  , organic acids  $  ^{9}  $  , organic metal  $  ^{10}  $  , polymer  $  ^{11}  $  , fullerene  $  ^{12}  $  , nanoparticle  $  ^{13}  $   in perovskite precursor or anti-solvent. Xie et al  $  ^{14}  $   introduced a ternary solvent system dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO)/g-butyrolactone (GBL) / N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) to fabricate uniform MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films with less surface roughness, the efficiency was 18.6% and the corresponding stability was improved. Zhang et al  $  ^{15}  $   employed α-bis-PCBM as a template agent for the anti-solvent processing to fabricate perovskite films. The efficiency of α-bis-PCBM based PSCs was 20.8% and maintained 90.1% of its initial PCE after 44 d without encapsulation (40% RH). Hou et al  $  ^{16}  $   added terephthalic acid into the perovskite precursor solution forming sheet-shaped perovskite film, the efficiency was improved to 18.51% and the devices obtained a good thermal stability. Bi et al  $  ^{17}  $  . used poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA) as a template to control perovskite nucleation and crystal growth. PMMA can enable heterogeneous nucleation and slows down the perovskite crystal growth, resulting in smooth perovskite films with fewer defects and larger oriented grains, thus making the PCE of PSCs exceed 21%. Sun et al  $  ^{18}  $   added 2-pyridylthiourea in the precursor solution made a high efficiency PSCs (18.2%), and PCE only decrease 8% after 30 days in 55±5% condition without encapsulation.

The C=S and N-H stretch are in TDZ lies at about   $  720\ cm^{-1}\ ^{19}  $   and   $  3500\ cm^{-1}\ ^{20}  $  , respectively. We calculate the trap-state density by the trap-filled limit voltage equation (2)  $  ^{33}  $  :

$$
N _ {t r a p s} = \frac {2 \varepsilon_ {0} \varepsilon_ {r} V _ {T F L}}{e L ^ {2}}
$$

(2)

where   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity,   $  \varepsilon_{r}  $   is the relative dielectric constant,   $  V_{TFL}  $   is the intersection voltage of the trap-filled limit and ohmic regimen, e is the elemental charge, and L is the thickness of the perovskite film (about 600nm). The trap densities of the pristine perovskite film, TDZDT-containing and TDZT-containing perovskite film are estimated to be   $  4.73 \times 10^{16}  $   cm  $  ^{3}  $  ,   $  6.63 \times 10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{3}  $   and   $  9.95 \times 10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{3}  $  , respectively. From the calculated results we concluded that thiadiazole derivatives can reduce trap density in perovskite film and improve the film quality, which is consistent with the result of TRPL measurement. (3)

The hysteresis effect is commonly observed phenomenon in PSCs. From the J-V curves in Figure 7d and photovoltaic parameters in Table 1, we can see that the hysteresis can be inhibited when adding thiadiazole derivatives.
