# ARTICLE INFO

While serious stability issues impede the commercialization of perovskite solar cells (PSCs), two-dimensional (2D) perovskites based on fluorinated bulky cations have emerged as more intrinsically stable materials. However, the influence of fluorination degree of the bulky aromatic cation on the performance of resulting PSCs has not been scrutinized. Here, 2D perovskites  $ (\mathrm{F_xPEA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $  ( $ x = 1,2,3,5 $ ) are grown in situ on the surface of the three-dimensional (3D) perovskite and demonstrate effective passivation of the surface defects of 3D perovskite. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the optimized devices were boosted from  $ 20.75\% $  for the control device to  $ 21.09\% $ ,  $ 22.06\% $ ,  $ 22.74\% $  and  $ 21.86\% $  for 2D/3D devices treated with 4-fluorophenethylamine iodide, 3,5-difluorophenylethylamine iodide, 2,4,5-trifluoroethylphenylethylamine iodide, and 1,2,3,4,5-pentafluorophenylethylamine iodide, respectively. We firstly reported two unexplored RP-type layered perovskites with  $ \mathrm{F}_2\mathrm{PEAI} $  and  $ \mathrm{F}_3\mathrm{PEAI} $  as bulky cations. The combined experimental and theoretical analysis revealed the reasons behind the various morphology, device performances, dynamic behavior, and humidity stability. The best performing  $ \mathrm{F}_3\mathrm{PEAI} $ -treated device retaining  $ 95.0\% $  of its initial PCE under ambient atmosphere (with RH of  $ 60\% \pm 5\% $ ) without encapsulation for  $ 300\mathrm{h} $  storage. This work provides useful guidance for selecting fluorinated bulky cations with different molecular electronic properties, which will play an essential role in further improving the performance/stability of PSCs for the sake of further commercialization.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on organic-inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskite materials represent one of the most promising photovoltaic (PV) technologies, with power conversion efficiency (PCE) soaring from an initial 3.8% in 2009 to 25.5% recently in just one decade [1-3]. Notwithstanding the huge progress made in PSCs, the commercialization is still hampered by severe stability issues, including the internal (structural) stability of the perovskite material and long-term stability of devices toward the impacts from the external environment (such as heat, light, humidity, and oxygen) [4].

Different strategies such as compositional tuning [5-14], dimensional engineering [15-19], additive engineering [20,21], interface technology [22-31] and surface lattice engineering [32] have been developed to improve both the efficiency and the stability of PSCs. $ (\mathrm{RNH}_3^+) $  as the interfacial materials [33-37]. The thin 2D perovskite layer can be formed in situ onto the bulk 3D perovskite layer. Compared with the 3D perovskite materials, the 2D RP type layered perovskites normally have wider band-gaps and larger exciton binding energy [38], which hindered the performance of solar cells based on only the 2D perovskite. However, due to their hydrophobic nature, the large organic cation spacers endowed the 2D perovskite materials with high formation energy and superior moisture stabilities [39]. In the case of the 2D/3D type perovskite layer, the capping 2D perovskite structure functions as a passivation layer by significantly reducing the defects on the surface of the 3D perovskite films and results in improvement of the efficiency. For example, Cho et al. controlled the in situ growth of a 2D quasi-(PEA) $ _2 $ PbI $ _4 $  (PEA = phenylethylamine) passivation layer on top of the bulk 3D perovskite film. The 2D/3D perovskite-based solar cell exhibited an enhanced average PCE of  $ 20.1\% $  and could retain  $ 85\% $  of the initial PCE after stressing under one sun illumination for  $ 800\mathrm{h} $  at  $ 50^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  in an ambient environment [40].

It is believed that the introduction of the fluorinated moieties into the bulky organic cations can further enhance the hydrophobicity of the material and thus improve the stability of the 2D/3D perovskite-based solar cells [41]. Grancini et al. explored the effect of 2D perovskite materials containing fluorinated aliphatic cations in reducing the interface deterioration of PSCs and yielded a  $ \mathrm{PCE} > 20\% $  with improved stability [42], Zhou et al. first introduced 2-(4-fluorophenyl)ethyl-amine (pFPEA) cation for the formation of 2D/3D perovskite layer and achieved a champion efficiency of  $ 20.54\% $  with significantly enhanced stability than the pure 3D PSC [43]. Liu et al. deposited a pentafluorophenethylammonium  $ (\mathrm{F}_5\mathrm{PEAl}) $ -based 2D perovskite layer on top of the 3D perovskite absorber layer to reduce the surface recombination and enabled a  $ \mathrm{PCE} > 22\% $  with improved operation stability [44]. In another study, Ye et al. reported that  $ \mathrm{F}_5\mathrm{PEA}^+ $  could partially replace the  $ \mathrm{PEA}^+ $  to form a mixed-ammonium 2D perovskite passivation agent. The resulted film exhibited a longer carrier lifetime and boosted the performance of PSCs from  $ 19.58\% $  to  $ 21.10\% $ , along with improved stability [45]. Recently, Hope et al. revealed the nanoscale phase segregation of layered hybrid perovskites with mixed  $ \mathrm{PEA}^+ $  and  $ \mathrm{F}_5\mathrm{PEA}^+ $  cations by nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) crystallography [46]. Besides, the improvement of PSCs' performances using 2D perovskite passivation layers based on perfluorobenzylammonium  $ \mathrm{F}_5\mathrm{PBzA}^+ $  and  $ \mathrm{FPEA}^+ $  was also compared [47].

Very recently, Zhou et al. thoroughly compared three kinds of mono-fluorinated PEA after forming a 2D/3D perovskite structure through their effects on the surface dipole, photo-physical, and photovoltaic performances. Interestingly, the oFPEAI-treated device exhibited the maximum PCE of  $ 20.6\% $ , while the pFPEAI-treated device showed the best stability [48]. However, there is no systematic study on how the bulky cations with different degrees of fluorination will influence the performance and stability of the PSCs using corresponding 2D/3D type perovskite absorbing layers.

Herein, we firstly resolved single crystals of two unexplored RP-type layered perovskites with bi- and tri-fluorinated PEAI as the bulky cations. Then we fabricated a series of PSCs with 2D/3D type perovskite layers via the in-situ formation of 2D perovskite atop the 3D matrix by using (4)-FPEA $ ^{+} $ , (3,5)-F $ _{2} $ PEA $ ^{+} $ , (2,4,5)-F $ _{3} $ PEA $ ^{+} $ , and (1,2,3,4,5)-F $ _{5} $ PEA $ ^{+} $  as the bulky cations. The comprehensive investigation demonstrates that all the 2D perovskites based on F $ _{x} $ -PEA $ ^{+} $  ( $ x = 1, 2, 3, 5 $ ) successfully passivated the defects on the 3D perovskite surface, inhibiting the non-radiation recombination of carriers at the interface of perovskite/HTL (HTL, hole transport layer). As a result, PCEs of devices made from these 2D/3D type perovskite layers boosted from 20.75% (for the control device) to 21.09%, 22.06%, 22.74%, and 21.86% for FPEAI-, F $ _{2} $ PEAI-, F $ _{3} $ PEAI- and F $ _{5} $ PEAI-containing perovskites, respectively. The best cell per-

formance was achieved for the   $  F_{3}PEAI  $  -based 2D/3D perovskite with a PCE of 22.74%. Furthermore, the   $  F_{3}PEAI  $  -based planar PSC achieved a   $  V_{OC}  $   as high as 1.14 V, corresponding to a   $  V_{OC}  $   deficit of 0.43 V based on an absorption threshold of 1.57 eV (the theoretical value of   $  V_{OC}  $   being ~1.29 V according to the Shockley–Queisser limit) [49–51]. Furthermore, the modified PSCs exhibited remarkable long-term device stability. We also found that the degree of fluorination of 2D spacer cations resulted in increased dipole moments to a different extent, which is beneficial to carrier dissociation, and corresponding 2D perovskites exhibited different formation energies correlate with crystalline quality. These combined findings prove that bulky cations with an appropriate degree of fluorination will improve the performance and stability of the 2D/3D PSCs.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1.Materials

$ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  colloid precursor (tin(IV) oxide,  $ 15\% $  in  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  colloidal dispersion) was purchased from Alfa Aesar. Lead iodide  $ (99.998\%) $  and lead bromide  $ (99.999\%) $  were purchased from TCI and AlfaAesar. Methylammonium chloride (MACl)  $ (\geq 99.5\%) $  was purchased from Xian Baolaite. Formamidinium iodide (FAI) and methylammonium bromide (MABr) were synthesized by reacting formamidine acetate (Aladdin) and hydroiodic acid  $ (57\mathrm{wt}\% $  in  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $ , Aladdin), methylamine  $ (30\mathrm{wt}\% -33\mathrm{wt}\% $  in ethanol, Aladdin), and hydrobromic acid  $ (48\mathrm{wt}\% $  in  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $ , Aladdin), respectively. The particular experimental operations were referred to as previously reported methods [52]. Parafluorophenethylamine iodide (FPEAI) was synthesized by the prior method [43]. Synthesis of 3, 5-difluorophenylethylamine iodide  $ (\mathrm{F}_2\mathrm{PEAI}) $ , 2, 4, 5-trifluoroethylpeneylethylamine iodide  $ (\mathrm{F}_3\mathrm{PEAI}) $ , perfluorophenylethylamine iodide  $ (\mathrm{F}_5\mathrm{PEAI}) $  [44]: Borane tetrahydrofuran complex solution  $ (1.0\mathrm{M} $  in THF,  $ 5\mathrm{mL}) $  was added into a solution of 3,5-difluorophenylacetonitrile  $ (1.45\mathrm{mmol}, $  Aladdin), 2,4,5-trifluorophenylacetonitrile  $ (1.45\mathrm{mmol}, $  Aladdin), in THF  $ (5\mathrm{mL}) $  dropwise at  $ 0^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  under the protection of argon, and the mixed solution was allowed to reflux overnight. After reduction, the resulting amine solution was treated with hydrogen iodide aqueous solution  $ (57\%, 1\mathrm{mL}, $   $ 7.7\mathrm{mmol}) $  and stirred at  $ 0^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 1\textrm{h} $ . The resulting brownish solution was concentrated, and the solvent was removed under reduced pressure. The resulting brown solid was recrystallized three times to yield a white crystalline final product. See Supporting information (Figs. S1-S3) for the NMR characterization of the samples.

# 2.2. Fabrication of the devices

Chemically etched FTO glass substrates (Nippon Sheet Glass) were cleaned with a detergent solution, deionized water, acetone, and anhydrous ethanol for 15 min, respectively. Next, the substrates were further cleaned with plasma treatment for 10 min. The substrate was then spin-coated with a thin layer of   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanoparticle film (1:3, weight ratio) at 3000 rpm for 30 s, followed by annealing at 150 °C for 30 min in ambient air. The   $  (\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3})_{0.92}(-\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})_{0.08}  $   perovskite solution (1.53 M   $  PbI_{2}  $  , 1.4 M FAI, 0.11 M MABr, 0.11 M   $  PbBr_{2}  $  , 0.5 M MACl in DMF:DMSO = 8:1 volume ratio) [18] was spin-coated at 1000 rpm for 10 sec and 6000 rpm for 25 s onto the FTO/  $  SnO_{2}  $   substrate. 200 μL chlorobenzene was dropped in 15 s at 6000 rpm, and the FTO/  $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite sample was treated at 150 °C for 10 min. For 2D perovskite synthesis, 100 μL of FPEAI (  $  F_{2}PEAI  $  ,   $  F_{3}PEAI  $   or   $  F_{5}PEAI  $  ) solution dissolved in isopropanol with different concentrations (2, 4, and 6 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) were further

# 3. Results and discussion

# 3.1. Density functional theory calculation

We first employed the density functional theory (DFT) calculation to visualize the electrostatic surface potential (ESP) and dipole moments (Fig. 1a–d) of the four fluorinated PEAI derivatives. The ESP calculations revealed the electropositive nature of the ammonium groups in the four molecules, which is expected to passivate the negatively charged defects on the surface of 3D perovskite [53]. Depending on the number of substituted electronegative fluorotatom [54], the dipole moments of the four molecules increase significantly from 11.7 D to 12.3 D, 14.2 D, and 14.3 D for FPEAI,   $  F_{2}  $  -PEAI,   $  F_{3}  $  -PEAI,   $  F_{5}  $  -PEAI, respectively. A high dipole moment could promote the separation of photogenerated electron-hole pairs, which is favorable for photovoltaic devices [55].

Single crystals of 2D perovskite (  $  F_{2}PEA)_{2}PbI_{4}  $   and (  $  F_{3}PEA)_{2}PbI_{4}  $   were grown by orthogonal solvent diffusion method (Fig. 1e–h, Fig. S4, Table S1), and analyzed in comparison to the reported crystals of (  $  FPEA)_{2}PbI_{4}  $   and (  $  F_{5}PEA)_{2}PbI_{4}  $   [43,57]. The DFT calculated formation energies of these 2D perovskites are shown in Table S2, which are increasing with the degree of fluorination. See the Supporting information for the details of the DFT calculation methods. # 3.2. Thin film fabrication and characterizations

The device configuration is fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/ $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / 3\mathrm{D} $  perovskite/2D perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Ag. We used a one-step spin-coating antisolvent assisted deposition method to prepare 3D binary mixed-cation perovskite film  $ (\mathrm{(FAPbI_3)_0.92(-MAPbBr_3)_{0.08}}) $ . # 3.3. Device performance after treatment

We prepared complete PSC devices and tested photovoltaic performance to evaluate the impact of the degree of fluorination. The current density–voltage (J-V) curves of the devices with 3D and 2D/3D perovskite are shown in Fig. 5(a) (reverse scan). The detailed photovoltaic parameters for all the devices can be referred to in Table 1. The best PCE of 22.74% was achieved based on the F₃-T device, while F₂-T, F₅-T, F-T, and control devices showed PCEs of 22.06%, 21.86%, 21.09%, and 20.75%, respectively, a trend that is consistent with the DFT calculated formation energy. It was found that the higher formation energy of 2D perovskite is beneficial to the passivation of 2D/3D laminated PSCs [59]. However, the exceptional slightly lower PCE from the F₅-T device could be attributed to the deteriorated morphology due to the significantly larger for-

mation energy of  $ (\mathrm{F}_5\mathrm{PEA})\mathrm{Pb}_2\mathrm{I}_4 $  that undermines the passivation effect.

The hysteresis index (HI) [65,72] of the control and F-T,  $ \mathrm{F}_2 $ -T,  $ \mathrm{F}_3 $ -T, and  $ \mathrm{F}_5 $ -T devices are calculated to be 7.40, 5.19, 8.97, 5.13, and 3.94, respectively, and the corresponding forward and reverse sweeping  $ J - V $  curves are shown in Fig. S9. The well-known hysteresis effect could be related to the trapping/de-trapping and ion migration process, etc. [74], Comparing to the control device, the decreased hysteresis of 2D/3D devices can be attributed to the passivation of the defect states at the surface and grain boundaries by 2D perovskites [75]. The exception of  $ \mathrm{F}_2 $ -T-based devices might be due to the irregularly grown 2D perovskite crystals, which is consistent with the irregular morphology of  $ (\mathrm{F}_2\mathrm{PEA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $  observed by LSCM [61]. We have made box chart statistics on the photovoltaic performance of PSCs (Fig. S10) (20 devices for each condition). It can be seen that the open-circuit voltages  $ (V_{\mathrm{OC}}) $  noticeably increase after the treatment by  $ \mathrm{F_xPEAl} $ . Among them, the  $ \mathrm{F}_3 $ -T-PSC exhibits the highest  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of  $ \approx 1.14\mathrm{V} $  (Fig. 5b), which should be attributed to the excellent passivation of 3D perovskite leading to reduced non-radiation recombination center [76]. Fig. 5(c) shows the external quantum efficiency (EQE) and integrated current density, which agrees with the corresponding  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  values measured from  $ J - V $  curves. The stabilized power output at the maximum power point (MPP) of the best devices is shown in Fig. 5(d). The 2D/3D laminated PSCs show excellent operational stability, while the current of the control device gradually increases in the first  $ 50~s $ , indicating that the defect state was captured by light.

Encouraged by the high efficiency and high stability of the devices after   $  F_{x}PEAI  $   treatment, we prepared large-area devices (aperture,   $  1\ cm^{2}  $  ) to investigate the potential of this strategy from laboratory to factory scale. The J-V curves of all conditions are shown in Fig. S9(f) (reverse scan, inset: the photo image of the large area device), and the photovoltaic parameters are listed in Table S4. The PCEs of large area F-T,   $  F_{2}-T  $  ,   $  F_{3}-T  $  , and   $  F_{5}-T  $   PSCs are 19.01%, 19.27%, 19.30%, 18.62%, respectively, in contrast to the control device (17.76%).

It can be seen from Fig. 5(e) that the PL intensity of 2D/3D perovskite films decays more slowly than the control film. # 3.5. Stability assessment of the device

The long-term stability of the 3D and 2D/3D perovskite films and corresponding PSCs were also assessed. Fig. S14 shows the contact angle (CA) of 3D and 2D/3D perovskite films. The CAs of all 2D/3D perovskite films are larger than that of the 3D perovskite and increase with the number of fluorine substitution:   $  F_{5}-T(85.12^{\circ}) > F_{3}-T(84.96^{\circ}) > F_{2}-T(83.23^{\circ}) > F-T(80.98^{\circ}) > control(70.55^{\circ})  $  . This result manifests that the fluorine-containing groups can improve the water resistance of perovskite film, and the more fluorine atoms out there, the better.

We also conducted stability tests by tracking the PCE changes every other week of the unencapsulated devices with Ag electrodes stored in the glove box (  $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere) for 105 days (Fig. 6b). By the end of the aging period, the control, F-T,   $  F_{2}  $  -T,   $  F_{3}  $  -T, and   $  F_{5}  $  -T PSCs retained 89.3%, 95.0%, 96.0%, 96.1%, and 96.2% of their initial PCE, respectively. Given that the Ag electrode is prone to oxidation and reaction with halide ions [89], affecting the accurate evaluation of the stability of the devices, we prepared PSCs with the gold electrode. We placed the unencapsulated devices under the ambient environment with 60% RH at 25 °C. Fig. S15(a and b) shows the J-V curves of the PSCs based on the Au electrode before and after aging, of which the PV parameters are listed in Table S7. The maximum PCE of the   $  F_{3}  $  PEAl-treated device further increased to 23.04% with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.12 V. As shown in Fig. 6(c), F5-T PSC exhibits the best humidity stability, retaining 95.0% of its initial PCE after 300 h, followed by   $  F_{3}  $  -T,   $  F_{2}  $  -T, and F-T PSCs, retaining 93.4%, 92.7%, 89.0% of their initial PCEs. This value of the pristine 3D PSC is only 79.2%. This trend is again consistent with the prediction of the stability from the DFT calculated formation energies:   $  F_{5}  $  -T >   $  F_{3}  $  -T >   $  F_{2}  $  -T > F-T > control.

Moreover, we traced the variation of the XRD patterns of the 3D and 2D/3D perovskite films exposed to the ambient environment for 15 days (RH = 60% ± 5%, 25 °C). Fig. 6d shows the XRD patterns of all the perovskite films before aging. After 15 days, the XRD peaks of the control film are significantly weakened, and two new peaks at ~11.7° and 12.7° appeared (Fig. 6e), which belong to the δ-phase FAPbI₃ and PbI₂ [90]. In stark contrast, the XRD patterns of all the modified perovskite films are almost unchanged, except that the intensity ratio of the (1 1 0) lattice plane of 3D perovskite to (0 0 1) lattice plane of PbI₂ became larger. In the meantime, the diffraction intensity of the 2D perovskite kept almost unchanged, indicating that the protection of aromatic fluorinated cations (Fig. S16a and b). Fig. S16(c and d) shows the magnified XRD data of the control film and the best performing F₅-T perovskite film in the diffraction angle region from 5° to 20° after 15 days of aging. All stability tests show that the in-situ grown 2D perovskite passivation layer based on fluorinated cations gives doubled advantages in further promoting the stability of PSCs devices.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we systematically investigated the impact of fluorination degree on the resulting 2D/3D perovskites. Based on the single crystallography results, DFT calculations show that the increase of fluorination significantly influences the dipole moments and formation energy. The properly turned dipole moments and formation energy can lead to beneficially separated photogenic carriers and improved device performances. The 2D perovskite layer generated in situ on the surface of 3D perovskite was demonstrated to reduce the defect density of the pristine perovskite effectively, inhibited the non-radiation recombination, and promote the charge transfer at the perovskite-contact interface. As the dipole moment increases, the PCE of the highly fluorinated PSC device increased. Due to the dipole moments of  $ \mathrm{F}_3\mathrm{PEAI} $  and  $ \mathrm{F}_5\mathrm{PEAI} $  are similar, and based on the DFT calculations of formation energy, the PCE of the  $ \mathrm{F}_5 $ -T PSC device showed a downward trend. Under the same conditions, the best cell performance was achieved for the  $ \mathrm{F}_3\mathrm{PEAI} $ -based 2D/3D perovskite with a PCE of  $ 22.74\% $ . In contrast to the control 3D PSC, the 2D/3D PSCs showed superb humidity stability, and the moisture stability increased with the increase of the degree of fluorination of the bulky aromatic cations. This work provides useful guidance for chemical modification of bulky cations, discloses the structure-properties relationships between crystal structure, film morphology, and device performances. Such relationships emphasized the importance of fluorination on moving further towards the commercialization of perovskite solar cells.
