# 1. Introduction

Among all renewable energy sources, converting solar light directly to electrical power using PVs is the most promising approach to replace exhausted conventional fossil fuels, which is urgent for the sustainable growth of human community   $  [1-3]  $  . Recently, organohalide metal perovskites (PVKs) have emerged as new materials for PV devices, which have attracted enormous awareness from PV research community   $  [4-6]  $  . The perovskite solar cells (PSCs) show a fabulous progress in the field of PV applications due to their outstanding optoelectrical merits such as large absorption coefficient, tunable band gap, high carrier diffusion length, and high charge mobility   $  [7]  $  . By using PVKs based materials as light harvester, the PSC has yielded a PCE of 25 %, which can be a competition to well-established commercialized silicon solar cell technology   $  [8]  $  . However, for successful commercialization of PSCs, simple solutions in terms of long-term stability and economic feasibility are still needed. With regard to time and cost, planar PSC architecture over traditional mesoporous PSC is inexpensive and ready, which is a significant merit for the commercialization of PSCs   $  [9]  $  .

A typical PSC configuration based on titanium dioxide  $ (\mathrm{TiO}_2) $  is the most common photoelectrode because of its perfect energy band alignment. Nevertheless, it suffers from low electron mobility that causes deficient carrier extraction at the  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 / \mathrm{PVK} $  interface [10]. In addition,

Methylammonium lead iodide (MAPI3) material is promising for use in photovoltaic (PV) cells because of its efficient optoelectrical properties. However, the ambient air processed MAPI3 layer is unstable to ZnO film mainly during the annealing process. Herein, XRD results showed that the MAPI3 layer on the top of the ZnO photoelectrode is converted into lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2) $  because of the existence of hydroxide agents on the surface of the ZnO layer. For this reason, the  $ \mathrm{MAPI}_3 $  based PSCs showed poor power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 9.95\% $  with a hysteresis effect. In order to tackle this problem, we report here on a carbon nanotube (CNT)-incorporated  $ \mathrm{MAPI}_3 $  perovskite film made in ambient air and is employed as an effective absorber layer. Our results demonstrate that optimized PSC based on CNT additive shows a PCE up to  $ 15\% $ .

high-temperature annealing of electron transport material   $  TiO_{2}  $   (400–500 °C) makes undesirable for the manufacturing of PVK cells on flexible electrodes and increases the fabrication cost [11]. Planar devices with ZnO as an ETM have recently risen as an alternative to   $  TiO_{2}  $   [12–14]. The merits of ZnO ETM such as superior charge collection property, high electron mobility, low temperature process, and favorable energy-level offset with PVK material are enabled to improve the efficiency of planar PSCs [15]. However, ZnO planar PSCs based on   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite still suffer from poor crystallization, charge recombination, and degradation of perovskite layer on top of ZnO [16]. The high-crystallinity and good morphology of   $  MAPI_{3}  $   perovskite are substantial for the device performance. To acquire superior PSCs,   $  MAPI_{3}  $   absorber layer should be highly crystalline structure, less grain boundaries, and covering the ETL completely. Therefore, to develop appropriate planar devices, it is important to skillfully modify the merits of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   layer, demonstrated by the morphology and crystallinity in the fabrication process [17].

Several methods have been reported to reduce degradation and to improve the crystallinity of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   material for PSCs. The utilized strategies involve interfacial modifying via self-assembled monolayer [18–20], modifying of ZnO ETM by an additive [21–23], and using ZnO in nano-rods structures [24–26]. Indeed, all of these modifications are still not sufficient for the fabrication of efficient   $  MAPI_{3}  $   film-based PSCs.

In order to avoid degradation of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer, we report on a simple approach done ambient air to achieve highly crystallized and large-size PVK with excellent charge injection by loading modified-CNT (m-CNT) into the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite precursor. Most importantly, the optimized devices maintain about  $ 82\% $  of the origin PCEs after 7 weeks' storing in a moisture conditions.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Chemicals

Methylammonium iodide (MAI) and 2,2',7,7'-Tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) were provided by Merck. Dimethylformamide (DMF), Li-TFSI and titanium tetrachloride  $ (\mathrm{TiCl}_4) $  were ordered from Dyesol. Pristine double walled-CNT  $ (98\%) $  with diameter  $ \sim 12 - 55\mathrm{nm} $  and length  $ \sim 2 - 12\mu \mathrm{m} $  was provided from Shenzhen Nanotech Port Co., Ltd. Note that all other materials were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich.

# 2.2. Solar cells preparation

We prepared photovoltaics using MAPbI $ _3 $  PVKs with diverse m-CNT amounts as light harvesting films. The planar PSC architecture in this study was FTO/ZnO/MAPI $ _3 $  perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/gold (Au). The patterned-FTO with 12  $ \Omega $ /cm $ ^2 $  resistivity was cleaned using diluted soap, isopropanol and methanol in an ultrasonic device for 10 min successively. Then, the FTO substrates were modified using UV ozone for 12 min. To prepare ZnO films, ZnO was synthesized through a simple precipitation route as used before in reference [12]. ZnO-NPs were dispersed in 50 mL of chloroform and spun above FTO at 5000 rpm for 25 s and baked at 75  $ ^{\circ} $ C for 35 min. For preparing CNT-isopropanol doped perovskite, pristine CNT was chemically modified employing sulfuric acid to functionalize CNT with oxygen groups as depicted in references [27–29]. CNTs were dispersed in DMF solvent using an ultrasonic device for 20 min before used. Then, a mixed solution of lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ , 461 mg) and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 78 mg) in 500-mg DMF was stirred for 1 h at room temperature. Methylammonium iodide (MAI, 50 mg) was dispersed in the solvent of isopropanol (2 mL) with additional 0.0 %, 0.5 %, 1.0 %, and 1.5 % of DMF-CNT dispersion to produce 0.0 %, 0.5 %, 1.0 %, and 1.5 % CNT-doped precursor solutions. Next, the PbI $ _2 $  mixture was deposited on ZnO ETM at 5000 rpm for 25 s. MAI precursor solution was spun onto PbI $ _2 $  layer at 4000 rpm for 40 s and baked in ambient air at 120  $ ^{\circ} $ C for 20 min. After finishing PVK deposition, the hole transport material (HTM) was spin-coated on MAPbI $ _3 $  films at 3000 rpm for 35 s. Spiro-OMeTAD (65 mg) was added in 2 mL chlorobenzene having 16.5  $ \mu $ L LiTFSI/acetonitrile (480 mg/mL) and 26.5  $ \mu $ L 4-tert-butylpyridine. At end, 80 nm of Au layers were deposited to finish the PSC structure. The mesoporous PSCs with architecture FTO/compact-TiO $ _2 $ /mesoporous-TiO $ _2 $ /MAPI $ _3 $ /Spiro-OMeTAD/Au were designed as reported elsewhere [4].

# 2.3. Measurements

Transmission electron microscope (TEM, EM208, Philips) was employed to characterize CNTs morphology. The CNTs structure was monitored via Raman spectrophotometer (Renishaw MicroRaman,  $ \lambda = 514\mathrm{nm} $ ). Chemical bonds were studied using Fourier transform infrared (FTIR, Bruker). UV-vis spectrum was studied via UV-vis spectrophotometer (Ocean Optics). Morphologies of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskites were measured via field emission-SEM (FESEM, MIRA3). XRD (Bruker, D8 advance) was used to measure crystallinity of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films. Optical characteristics of perovskites were measured via UV-vis spectroscopy and photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy (Agilent, Varian Cary Eclipse Fluorescence) in the wavelength range from 700 to  $ 900\mathrm{nm} $  with a wavelength step of  $ 0.2\mathrm{nm} $ . The excitation wavelength employed here was  $ 470\mathrm{nm} $  with a continuous xenon arc lamp. The measurements of J-V of cells were characterized via a Keithley Model 2400 with  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $ . The devices were masked with an area of  $ 0.08\mathrm{cm}^2 $ . The Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) with system of electrochemical workstation (Zennium 400,147) was employed to record Nyquist curves. The charge generation and extraction was measured using EQE (BunkoukeikiCEP-1500).C-V measurements of the devices were collected by a PM 6306 Programmable LRC meter (Fluke, @1 MHz).

Therefore, these hydrophilic groups on the m-CNT contribute to excellent bonds with PVK, resulting in a denser PVK grain, decreased pinholes and grain boundaries area. Fig. 1b reveals the Raman pattern of the m-CNTs where the two typical Raman peaks (D-band at  $ 1336.2\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $  and G-band at  $ 1581.4\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ ) confirm the crystalline structure of functionalized CNT [32]. The absorbance spectrum of m-CNT in Fig. 1c demonstrates two absorption peaks at  $ 242\mathrm{nm} $  and  $ 335\mathrm{nm} $ , which attributed to the electronic transition for the m-CNT [33]. The optical-active merit of m-CNTs is significant for PVK photon absorption if moderate contents of m-CNT are added. The TEM micrograph of m-CNT shows double-walled tubes morphology with a size of nanometers and some nanotubes are twisted with each other. The one dimensional m-CNT loaded in the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite can furnish a rapid electron transport route with excellent conductivity. Obviously, from these results, it can be affirmed that the required m-CNT has been successfully prepared.

However, it leads to small grain size and high surface flaws [34]. Once m-CNTs are uniformly incorporated in the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  matrix, the surface change of the deposited PVK, relative to the pure one, is very clear. But, too much s m-CNT addition is diverse, leading to more defects and separation of PVK grains. The crystallization process of PVK includes two steps: nucleation and crystal growth. High content of m-CNTs can act as heterogeneous nucleation sites of PVK. PVK grains tend to grow independently because of plen- teous nucleation centers, causing more grains with smaller size. Thus, excess m-CNT leads to agglomeration, inducing more pinholes inside the PVK and generating low-quality PVK film. Another explanation can be presented in the current study for impacts of m-CNT. As mentioned previously, CNTs filled the flaw between PVK crystals as matrices may suppress the transformation kinetics of the PVK, which advantages more pinholes-free surface growth of the PVK, leading to an enlarged PVK grain size and enhanced PVK crystallinity [36]. Humidity can permeate the crystal boundaries to decompose the PVK layer. Therefore, increasing the PVK grain size is an efficient strategy to enhance the stability of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film. Moreover, a suitable content of m-CNTs can bridge the PVK film with ZnO ETM to deactivate the oxygen groups on the ZnO surface and improve electron transfer.

All the aforementioned measurements signify the pivotal role that CNTs play in electron extraction and injection, which is demonstrated in Fig. 7c. However, as reflected in Fig. 7d, too much CNT will accumulate and cause more pinholes. And they become the recombination site of photo-induced charges, thus the effective photo-induced electrons tend to be captured by pinholes, thus the carrier injection ability is greatly decreased.

Fig. 8 demonstrates the PCE stability of conventional mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $  , pure   $  MAPI_{3}  $  -based ZnO (control), and 1.0 % CNT modified   $  MAPI_{3}  $  -based ZnO (optimized) devices under controlled humidity (30 and 85 %) at room temperature. The devices were stored in dark conditions for 1200 h except for measuring of J-V curves (once a week, for 10 min). The PCE of the conventional device was 60 % of the initial value after 7 weeks at 30 % RH (Fig. 8a). While, optimized devices showed high long-term stability (negligible drop) compared to the control device (10 % drops). At 80 % RH, the normalized efficiency of the conventional device was further dropped to 10 % after 7 weeks (Fig. 8b). Interestingly, the optimized PSCs based on 1% CNTs maintained about 82 % of the initial PCE value after 7 weeks. Thus, the CNTs modification significantly enhanced the long-term stability of PSCs at different humid conditions. This improvement could be ascribed to the better crystallinity of the CNT-doped   $  MAPI_{3}  $   film (Fig. 3a), which promote prevents the erosion by humidity and maintains the PVK film stable.

XRD spectra of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layers with and without CNTs were conducted as a function of aged time under open, to demonstrate the decomposition pathways of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layers (Fig. 8c-d). The pure  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layers shows unstable and crystallinity decreasing after 60 days storage in ambient conditions and a high reflection of the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  in the 60th day obviously increased. Moreover, two emerging peaks at 1716 and  $ 1604~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $  were detected, as revealed in FTIR image. These findings can verify that CNTs have effectively interacted with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  grains, which is improves the crystallization and the morphology of PVK film.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, an efficient approach has been used for high-quality and stable ambient air-processed  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film synthesis by CNT incorporation. CNTs participate to better crystallized PVK films with fewer pinholes and flaws, which greatly improve the light absorption of single cation  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer. Most importantly, higher charge collection efficiency and lower recombination rate are achieved as well by loading CNTs into PVK precursor solution. It has been proven that CNTs can act not only as electron acceptor but also an electron transfer bridge, which lead to effective separation of photo-induced charges. Consequently, the device with  $ 1.0\% $  CNTs reveals the champion performance with a  $ 52\% $  improvement in PCE compared with the control device. Moreover, oxidized CNTs loaded into the PVK film can also hinder erosion by the moisture of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films, therefore the long-term stability tests show the excellent ambient-stability of the  $ 1.0\% $  CNT-doped PVK film. Our study affirms that incorporating CNTs is a promising strategy to achieve high-quality air-processed  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film with more effective charge injection for stable PSCs.
