# Abstract

As organic–inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) near commercialization, stability challenges during real-world conditions, such as elevated temperatures, still need to be addressed. We have previously reported triarylamine-based hole transport layers (HTLs) doped with a triarylamine-based radical cation salt (EHCz-3EtCz/EH44-ox) lead to enhanced PSC stability at elevated temperatures. While it was shown the radical cation dopant did not need to be identical to the HTL matrix, little was known about dopant exchange to realize maximum impact on device-level properties (e.g., increase the low intrinsic conductivity, mobility, hydrophobic properties, synthetic ease and thermal stability). In this paper, we study the impact of dopant exchange among stable, low cost, high glass transition temperature (  $  T_{g}  $  ), and easily synthesized triarylamine-based HTL and radical triarylamine cation salts as dopants. Using EH44-ox as dopant leads to the improved device-level PCE for all HTL matrices assessed. Moreover,

# Introduction

Organic/inorganic lead halides have shown remarkable development and prospective application in perovskite solar cells (PSCs) because of their excellent properties including solution processable deposition, extremely high optical absorption, outstanding charge carrier mobility, long carrier lifetime, cost effectiveness, and tunable compositions for altering bandgaps.  $  ^{[1-4]}  $   Recent studies have shown that the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs could achieve 25.2%.  $  ^{[5]}  $   PSCs with the standard n-i-p architecture include a transparent conductive oxide front electrode (mainly fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) or indium tin oxide (ITO)) on a rigid or flexible substrate, an electron transporting layer (ETL), a perovskite layer, a hole transport layer (HTLs), and a back electrode of a noble metal or carbon.  $  ^{[6,7]}  $   Among these components, the design and selection of HTLs often play a few crucial roles. First, the HTL allows for efficient hole extraction and suppresses charge recombination, which leads to improved photovoltaic performance. Additionally, in the n-i-p architecture the HTL can serve as a barrier layer to minimize degradation of the perovskite layer from water. Recently much effort has been dedicated to both the development of inorganic  $  ^{[8]}  $   and organic HTLs,  $  ^{[9]}  $   of which we will describe our work on organics.

Organic materials (including small molecule, polymer and polymer-assisted small molecule) are often selected as HTLs because of their advantages such as solution processability and tunable thermal and optoelectronic properties via chemical structural modification.  $  ^{[10-12]}  $   Compared with polymer-based HTLs, conjugated small molecule-based HTLs have additional advantages such as straightforward purification and high batch to batch reproducibility. For example, 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N'-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) is commonly used as the HTL matrix that leads to high device-level efficiency, yet its multi-step and low yield synthesis is projected to be a significant component of the module cost.  $  ^{[13,14]}  $   Moreover, bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (LiTFSI) or tris(2-(1H-pyrazol-1-yl)-4-tert-butylpyridine)cobalt(III) tri[bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide] (FK209) are required to dope the spiro-OMeTAD film in order to generate free carriers to increase its conductivity. Ultimately, spiro-OMeTAD/LiTFSI based HTLs lead to short-lived PSCs for a variety of reasons including Li  $  ^{+}  $  ion diffusion throughout the layers which strongly affects TiO  $  _{2}  $  charge-extraction and hysteresis during operation.  $  ^{[10,15]}  $

Many small molecule-based HTLs with high device performance have been designed from pyrene, thiophene, porphyrin, and carbazole derivatives.  $  ^{[16]}  $   Carbazole derivatives are advantageous due to low cost starting materials, good chemical and environmental stability, and strategic sites for functional group attachment.  $  ^{[17]}  $   For example, Sung investigated two- or three-arm linked carbazole-based HTLs through phenylene-derived central core units. Devices with the carbazole-based HTLs performed nearly as well as devices with spiro-OMeTAD-based HTLs (14.79% and 15.23% respectively).  $  ^{[18]}  $   Gratia et al. utilized methoxydiphenylamine-substituted carbazole as HTLs and obtained a PCE close to 17% in   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  -based solar cells.  $  ^{[19]}  $   Daskeviciene et al. synthesized a low cost carbazole-based conjugated enamine in one step (V950) and obtained PSC PCEs close to 18%.  $  ^{[20]}  $   Magomedov et al. demonstrated a series of HTLs containing two diphenylamine-substituted carbazole fragments linked by a nonconjugated

Previously, we demonstrated a modified device architecture of   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite/HTL/  $  MoO_{x}  $  /Al (HTL = EH44, 9-(2-ethylhexyl)-N,N,N,N-tetrakis(4-methoxyphenyl)-9H-carbazole-2,7-diamine, its corresponding analogous oxidized radical cation salts (EH44-ox), and tert-butylpyridine, tBP).  $  ^{[23]}  $   The best performing device preserved 94% of the initial photovoltaic performance after 1,000 h continuous illumination. This is still one of the most stable PSCs that has been reported without any encapsulation under ambient conditions at room temperature.  $  ^{[10]}  $   A critical piece of achieving this stability is the elimination of commonly used lithium/cobalt salts to dope the HTL and replacing them with pre-synthesized radical triarylamine cations as dopants, such as EH44-ox. Despite this improved stability, device-level power output using this HTL formulation is not as high as devices with lithium-doped HTLs. With improved HTL formulation using EHCz-3EtCz (  $  N^{2},N^{7}  $  -bis(9-ethyl-9H-carbazol-3-yl)-9-(2-ethylhexyl)-  $  N^{2},N^{7}  $  -bis(4-methoxyphenyl)-9H-carbazole-2,7-diamine) as the high   $  T_{g}  $   HTL matrix and EH44-ox as dopant, we have shown both improved PCE and device-level stability at elevated temperatures.  $  ^{[24]}  $   However, little is known about dopant exchange to realize maximum impact on device-level properties, which may increase the low intrinsic conductivity, mobility, hydrophobic properties, synthetic ease and thermal stability. In this work, a carbazole-based HTL with variations on the alkyl substituent locations, together with radical triarylamine salts as dopants are assessed for optimal device-level power output and stability. # Characterization:

Current density-voltage (J-V) curves of the PSCs were measured by a Newport Oriel Sol3A solar simulator with a xenon lamp. The solar simulator calibration to   $  100 \, mW \cdot cm^{-2}  $   was performed using a KG2 filtered NREL-certified mono-Si reference solar cell. PSCs were measured using a 100 ms delay after 10 mV voltage steps (about 0.1 V/s). A measurement system (QEX10, PV Measurements) was used to test incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE). The stability test condition is similar to our previous report.  $  ^{[23]}  $   The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) tests were carried out on a computer-controlled electrochemical workstation (Zen-nium Zahner, Germany) from 10 mHz to 4 MHz with 0.6 V bias voltage. PL measurements were acquired using an optically triggered streak camera system (C5410, Hamamatsu). All the samples were photoexcited using a 517 nm laser at a 200 V voltage and 240 nm/min scan speed. Each of the samples were tested 3-5 times, and the average data is reported.

# Results and Discussion

Carbazole-based HTLs and analogous oxidized radical cation salts were synthesized in a similar fashion as EH44 and EHCz-3EtCz. Detailed information of synthetic methods and yields are provided in the supporting information. We first address the cost of HTLs as this is one of the hurdles that limit PSC commercialization.[25,26] Based on this, we designed the synthesis of our new HTL with few steps and high yield reactions. As a result, we calculate that the cost of our HTL is approximately one fifth of spiro-OMeTAD, as shown in Table S3. We assume this cost savings will be in part extrapolated to the high volume manufacturing of the material. With regards to performance, according to preliminary studies on carbazole-based HTLs, the location of electron donating groups plays a role in the conductivity within the HTLs, specifically with regard to the relative number of trap sites that boost or hinder film conductivity.[24,27] Continuing with this strategy, 9-ethyl- $ N^2 $ ,  $ N^7 $ -bis(9-(2-ethylhexyl)-9H-carbazol-3-yl)- $ N^2,N^7 $ -bis(4-methoxyphenyl)-9H-carbazole-2,7-diamine abbreviated as EtCz-3EHCz, provides direct para-resonance stabilization for the radical triarylamine cation, as shown in Figure 1. Furthermore, in EtCz-3EHCz, the ethylhexyl chains are installed on the peripheral carbazole units rather than central carbazole unit for EHCz-3EtCz. This provides insight into the importance of location and increased amount of hydrophobic alkyl chains, steric hindrance, molecular packing, and relationship to HTL mobility in the amorphous film. As addition of more alkyl chains to the HTL may lead to changes in thermal properties, we first assessed  $ \mathrm{T_g} $  using DSC. # Conclusion

Low cost carbazole-based HTLs have been designed and synthesized for improving PSC stability and performance. Compared with spiro-OMeTAD, our HTL formulations with metal ion-free dopant and multiple 2-ethylhexyl alkyl chains per HTL molecule resulted in devices with similar PCE and much higher stability. We speculate that this increased performance is due to our HTL being metal ion-free and having higher hydrophobicity that minimizes moisture diffusion into the water sensitive active perovskite layer.
