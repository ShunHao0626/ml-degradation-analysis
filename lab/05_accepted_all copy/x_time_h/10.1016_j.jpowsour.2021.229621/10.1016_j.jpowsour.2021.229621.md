# GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT

# ABSTRACT

The interfacial defects passivation of perovskite is one of the most effective approaches to realize highly efficient and stable perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, we propose an easy in-situ secondary annealing method by using a quaternary ammonium salt tetraethylammonium hexafluorophosphate (TEAH) to enhance the photovoltaic performance and device stability. The research manifests that TEAH can homogeneously distribute on the perovskite surface, and effectively passivate the surface and boundary defects. Correspondingly, the hole extraction from perovskite to the hole transport layer (HTL) is greatly improved, hindering the non-radiative charge carrier recombination. With the introduction of TEAH treatment, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the PSC has been elevated from 18.57% to 20.25%, with well suppressed hysteresis. Moreover, the exposed hydrophobic ethyl-terminal groups of TEAH can protect perovskite film from degradation by ambient moisture in some extent. As a result, TEAH treated PSC exhibits good stability, retaining 74.1% of its initial PCE after 680 h aging under ambient air with a relative humidity (RH) of 55–60%.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted intense research interest because of their outstanding photovoltaic performance   $  [1-6]  $  . The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has rapidly increased from 3.8% to the certified 25.5% within the past few years with the great efforts on the perovskite composition engineering, molecular engineering of charge transport layer, optimization of device fabrication process and interface engineering   $  [7-12]  $  . The research manifested that the photovoltaic performance of PSCs, including both PCE and stability, is strongly related to the crystal quality of perovskite films   $  [13]  $  . However, during the preparation of polycrystalline perovskite films with solution process methods, it is inelutable to form shallow defects in the perovskite film, including grain boundary defects and crystallographic defects   $  [14,15]  $  . These defects, especially the defects on the perovskite boundary and surface, have been proved to be the major trigger points for non-radiative recombination   $  [16]  $  , which give rise to the photocurrent hysteresis of the device and are unfavorable for the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ), correspondingly, limiting the device photovoltaic performance to converge towards Shockley-Queisser (S-Q) theory values   $  [17,18]  $  . Moreover, the defects on the perovskite surface would induce the perovskite bonding with water and oxygen, in turn, accelerating the degradation of the perovskite   $  [19,20]  $  . Therefore, developing effective defect passivation methods is critical for constructing the highly efficient and stable PSCs.

Up to now, various of effective defect passivation strategies have been developed, such as the precursor solution component engineering, the controlment of perovskite crystalline behavior, surface defects passivation with ionic liquids, organic ammonium salts, organic small molecular and polymeric semiconductors, and so on [21-31]. For example, most recently, by incorporating 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium tetrafluoroborate (BMIMBF $ _4 $ ) into the perovskite precursor solution, Snaith et al. successfully inhibited of ion migration in the active layer, finally, simultaneously improved the PCE and stability [28]. By using theophylline, caffeine, and theobromine as additives, Yang et al. gave an in-depth understanding of how the molecular configuration influences the passivation effectiveness. They demonstrated that the defects can be effectively passivated when the N–H and C=O were in an optimal configuration [32]. Through treating the crystal surfaces with the Lewis bases (thiophene and pyridine), Snaith et al. found significantly reduced nonradiative electron-hole recombination within perovskite, and propose that this is due to the electronic passivation of under-coordinated Pb atoms within the crystal [33]. These results demonstrated that the lone pair electrons of the sulfur (S), oxygen (O), or nitrogen (N) atoms can delocalize to the 6p empty orbits of Pb $ ^{2+} $  to form coordination bonds, correspondingly, efficiently regulating perovskite crystalline behavior and passivating the surface defects.

Based on this context, herein, we proposed an in-situ secondary annealing treatment method by using tetraethylammonium hexafluorophosphate (TEAH), with the aim of passivating the interfacial and boundary defects for the efficient and stable PSCs. The passivation effects of TEAH were systematically investigated by employing X-ray diffraction (XRD), X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and photoluminescence. The testing results certified that the secondary introduced ammonium cation can efficiently passivate the shallow defects on perovskite surface through coordinating with bared   $  Pb^{2+}  $  , thereby, the defect density of perovskite film was decreased. Consequently, the PSCs with TEAH treatment showed an impressive PCE of 20.25%, which is much higher than the control devices (18.57%). Moreover, the devices with TEAH treatment exhibit good stability, maintaining 74.1% of its initial PCE after 680 h, while the control device dramatically decreasing to 3.5% at 460 h, under the ambient condition with the 55–60% humidity.

# 2. Results and discussion

In this study, the quaternary ammonium salt TEAH was adopted as passivation agent in PSCs, by means of spin-coating its isopropanol (IPA) solution on crystallized perovskite surface then with secondary annealing treatment (see Fig. 1a). The detailed procedure is described in supporting information (SI). Firstly, to investigate the effect of TEAH secondary treatment on film quality, the X-ray diffraction (XRD) for the perovskite films with and without TEAH treatment were examined (see Fig. 1b and S1). The sample without TEAH treatment was labeled as control sample. Two sharp XRD diffraction peaks at  $ 14.0^{\circ} $  and  $ 28.3^{\circ} $  were observed, which can be attributed to the (110) and (220) crystal planes of the perovskite. After TEAH treatment, the characteristic peak intensities for (110) and (220) crystal planes were enhanced while no peak shift was detected, indicating that TEAH treatment could effectively assist in improving the perovskite crystal equality but not incorporated into the perovskite lattice. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurement was carried out to investigate the detailed passivation action of TEAH. As shown in Fig. 1c and d, for the control sample, the Pb 4f core level locating at  $ 137.6\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ 142.5\mathrm{eV} $ , which is ascribed to the  $ \mathrm{Pb}4\mathrm{f}_{5/2} $  and  $ \mathrm{Pb}4\mathrm{f}_{7/2} $ , respectively, were observed. With TEAH treatment, the peaks of the  $ \mathrm{Pb}4\mathrm{f}_{5/2} $  and  $ \mathrm{Pb}4\mathrm{f}_{7/2} $  core level shifted by 0.1 eV and 0.2 eV towards the low binding energy, respectively, verifying the effective passivation of  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  on the film surface. Moreover, the peak of N 1s core level for TEAH +  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  shifted by 0.2 eV towards the high binding energy compared with the pristine TEAH (see Fig. 1d), further confirming the strong coordination effect between  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  and TEAH. The effective passivation effect was also verified in ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) absorption spectra. As shown in Fig. S2, the perovskite film with TEAH treatment represent the same absorption range and edge with the control film, but stronger absorbance intensity in the short wavelength range due to the reduced defects [34]. In addition, we found that the C=O peak at  $ 288.0\mathrm{eV} $  for the perovskite with TEAH treatment became much weaker compared with the control perovskite film (see Fig. S3). The result suggests that the coordination between the perovskite and oxygen/moisture is suppressed to some extent with TEAH treatment, further relieving the degradation of perovskite caused by oxygen/moisture.

The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and atomic force microscopy (AFM) were conducted for the perovskite film with and without TEAH treatment to investigate the effect of TEAH treatment on film morphologies. As shown in Fig. 2, the  $ 0.25\mathrm{mg / mL} $  TEAH treated film shows more homogeneous and smoother surface with the root-mean-square roughness  $ (R) $  of  $ 24.8~\mathrm{nm} $ , compared with the control sample  $ (R = 27.9\mathrm{nm}) $ . With further increasement of TEAH concentration to 0.5, 1 and  $ 2\mathrm{mg / mL} $ , growing number of TEAH crystals aggregate and form obvious particles (see Fig. S4), leading to the rougher surface. Moreover, the energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) results (see Fig. S5) showed that F and P elements are detected on the whole TEAH treated perovskite film, indicating the homogeneous distribution of TEAH on the perovskite surface. More importantly, many small black domains without the F and P elements were observed. We supposed that the small black domains are perovskite crystals and the passivation agent TEAH is easily distributed on the grain boundary, which is beneficial for the passivation of the grain boundary.

Furthermore, the photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy and the trap state density   $  n_{trap}  $   were tested to better illustrate the passivation effect of the in-situ secondary annealing treatment. The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectroscopy of the perovskite films with and without TEAH treatment were shown in Fig. 3a and b, and the relative data were summarized in Table 1. We found that the PL intensities are obviously increased after TEAH treatment, indicating that nonradiative recombination in the perovskite layer was largely suppressed. Firstly, we optimized the TEAH concentration in anti-solvent. As shown in Fig. S6 and Table S1, with the increasement of TEAH concentration, the efficiency of TEAH treated PSC firstly goes up and then decrease down, finally, the optimum concentration is 0.25 mg/mL. With the in-situ TEAH treatment, an impressive PCE as high as 20.25%, with a short-circuit current ( $  J_{sc}  $ ) of 25.11 mA/cm $  ^{2}  $ , a  $  V_{oc}  $  of 1.10 V, a fill factor ( $  FF  $ ) of 73.08%, was obtained. Under the same conditions, the control device showed inferior efficiency of 18.57% (see Fig. 4a and Table 2). It is noted that the TEAH treated device represent a great enhancement on  $  V_{oc}  $ , 50 mV higher than the control device, which is mainly attributed to the effective defect passivation of TEAH inhibiting the non-radiative recombination. This is also confirmed by the Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) test results. As shown in Fig. 4f, the recombination resistance of TEAH treated device (162 Ω) is much larger than that of the control device (35 Ω), indicating charge recombination is greatly suppressed with the introduction of TEAH. As well benefiting from the reduced defects, there is almost no obvious hysteresis for the TEAH treated device, while the hysteresis is more serious for the control device. The incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) measurement are shown in Fig. 4c. The TEAH treated devices exhibit efficient conversion efficiency in the region of 300–800 nm, and the IPCE values are obviously higher than control device. The integrated  $  J_{sc}  $  were calculated to be 22.9 and 23.4 mA/cm $  ^{2}  $  for the control device and TEAH treated devices, respectively, matching well with the  $  J-V  $  test results. To evaluate the reproducibility of the device, 20 devices of each batch were fabricated, and their statistics of PCE,  $  V_{oc}  $ ,  $  J_{sc}  $  and  $  FF  $  values are shown in Fig. 4d and Fig. S7. The corresponding data are summarized in Table S2 and Table S3. The TEAH

treated device exhibit more excellent reproducibility with the higher average PCE of 19.4%, compared with the control device (17.9%).

To further study the effect of TEAH treatment on charge carrier transport during the working process, we tested the  $ J - V $  change tendency under different light intensities (I). As shown in Fig. 5a, by fitting the relationship between  $ J_{sc} $  and I, we found that the TEAH treated device shows larger slope value  $ (\alpha) $  of 0.97 than that of the control device  $ (\alpha = 0.95) $ , indicating more effective carrier transport in the TEAH treated PSC. Simultaneously, we also fit the relationship between  $ V_{oc} $  and I obeying the equation:  $ V_{oc} = \frac{nkT}{e}\ln (I) + $  Constant, where n is an ideal factor, K is Boltzmann constant, T is the absolute temperature and  $ e $  is elementary charge. As shown in Fig. 5b, the TEAH treated device shows a smaller slope of  $ 1.88\mathrm{kT / q} $  compare with the control device (2.18 kT/ q), which signifies that the defect-induced recombination was suppressed by TEAH treatment, leading to higher  $ V_{oc} $ .

In order to examine which ion of the TEAH,   $  [C_{8}H_{20}N]^{+}  $   cation or   $  [PF_{6}]^{-}  $   anion, is requisite for enhancing the device efficiency, we further measured PSCs treated with Tetrabutylphosphonium hexafluorophosphate (TPH) which contains the same anion as TEAH, but with different cation. The photovoltaic data are summarized in Table S4. As shown in Fig. S8, the TPH treated PSC showed similar PCEs as control device. Therefore, the increase in device efficiency is attributed to the presence of   $  [C_{8}H_{20}N]^{+}  $   cations in TEAH.

We also evaluated the stability of the TEAH treated PSCs in air condition (relative humidity of 55–60% and room temperature) with unencapsulated devices, and the normalized PCEs were shown in Fig. 4e. The TEAH treated devices maintained 74.1% of its initial PCE after 680 h, whereas the PCE of the control device drops to 18.8% of the original efficiency at 460 h. On the one hand, this phenomenon may be attributed to the improved hydrophobicity of the perovskite film after TEAH treatment (see Fig. S9). The water contact angles were tested to be 70° and 60° for TEAH treated perovskite and control sample, respectively. The better hydrophobicity can stop the perovskite from degrading by moisture. On the other hand, the improved stability can be ascribed to the good surface defect passivation with TEAH treatment.
