# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite compounds  $ \mathrm{ABX}_3 $  [A = Cs, Formamidinium (FA) or Methylammonium (MA), B = Pb or Sn, X = Cl, Br or I] have been intensely studied due to their excellent photo-electronic properties, such as tuneable band-gap, high absorption coefficient, long carriers lifetime and diffusion length (Stranks et al., 2013; Niu et al., 2015; Farrior et al., 2016; Zhao and Zhu, 2016). With tremendous researches on deposition techniques, morphology control methods and substrate materials (Jeon et al., 2014, 2015; Salim et al., 2015; Saliba et al., 2016; Nie et al., 2015), the up to date power conversion efficiency (PCE) has been pushed to  $ 22.1\% $  just in the short 6 years since it was first applied as a light-absorber (Kojima et al., 2009). Along with the low cost solution processing compatibility, the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) demonstrate a great potential for future photovoltaic industry.

The PSCs can be generally classified into mesoscopic, mesosuperstructured and planar heterojunction types by their device

This work draws attention to environmental influence to function layers interfaces and the band alignment optimization of organic-inorganic metal halide planar perovskite solar cell (PSC), demonstrating an effective route to enhance the PSC photovoltaic conversion efficiency (PCE) and stability. The PCE of the PSC presents a sensitive correlation with the relative humidity (RH) during the electron transport layer (ETL) preparation. Through optimizing the RH condition, the PSCs show outstanding performances using sol-gel   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL prepared under 55 RH%. Moreover, we systematically investigate the Zr doping effect in the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ink with a variation of Zr molar percentages from 0% to 7.5%. A high PCE of 15.06% with a good fill factor of 0.76 is obtained at 2 mol% Zr doping, in contrast to 12.25% of the undoped device. The Mott-Schottky analysis reveals that the elevated open-circuit voltage (Voc) is attributed to the upshifting of flat band potential of   $  TiO_{2}  $   from -4.15 eV to -4.02 eV (vs. vacuum) after doping. Notably, the as-made cell shows low hysteresis and elevated stability with only 15% degradation of PCE (35% for PSC with pyrolyzed   $  TiO_{2}  $  ) after 550 h/14 RH% ageing without encapsulation.

Within these types, the planar PSC shows advantages in its simplified architecture and processing procedure, which is highly suitable for an online manufacturing through both vacuum and/or solution based techniques. In a planar solar cell, the perovskite photoactive layer is sandwiched by an electron transport layer (ETL) and a hole transport layer (HTL) forming a typical p-i-n hetero-structures. The ETL plays an important role as it allows the transport of electrons while blocks the holes. Thus it would influence the carriers injection, collection, transportation, recombination, and then the overall performance of the PSCs (Yang et al., 2016). Applying a high quality compact ETL is obligated to obtain high photovoltaic devices, which can be achieved by the carefully controlling the energy level, trap states, electron mobility, interface and morphology. Numerous n-type materials, such as  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{WO_x} $ ,  $ \mathrm{ZnO_2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ , PCBM,  $ C_{60} $ , have been employed as ETLs for PSCs (Mahmood et al., 2014; Ke et al., 2015; Lai et al., 2015; Wang et al., 2015a, 2015b; Seo et al., 2014). Inheriting from the dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs),  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  remains the most widely used ETL in PSCs due to its low costs and the deeply accumulated knowledge in both fundamentals and processing techniques. Serving as an effective way to adjust the energy levels and reduce the barriers, modification of   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs by doping metal or non-metal elements has attracted considerable attentions (Jeon et al., 2014; Pan et al., 2015; Yang et al., 2016). Yttrium (Y)-doped compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   electrodes as the ETL in PSCs have showed an enhancement in the electron extraction and transport (Zhou et al., 2014). Magnesium (Mg)-doped compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   electrodes exhibited an improvement of both the open-circuit voltage (Voc) and short current density (Jsc) due to upshifted conduction band minimum (CBM) position to achieve better aligned band structure (Wang et al., 2015a, 2015b). Niobium (Nb)-doped mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   electrodes were found offering a fast electron injection and transportation with the preserved electron lifetime (Kim et al., 2015). Fast electron transportations can also be achieved by lithium (Li)-doping, due to the enhanced conductivity of   $  TiO_{2}  $   electrodes (Giordano et al., 2016). As an earth abundant and environmental friendly element, zirconium (Zr) has demonstrated notable effect in doping   $  TiO_{2}  $   by adjusting energy levels, increasing carriers density and reducing recombination for DSSCs and quantum dot solar cells (Kim and Kang, 2011; Liu et al., 2011). The exhibited higher carrier density and longer carrier lifetimes of devices with Zr-  $  TiO_{2}  $   electrode drives further investigation of its application in PSC (Nagaoka et al., 2015). Moreover, the handy sol-gel processed Zr-  $  TiO_{2}  $   ink for the PSC possesses high composition adjustability and ink stability. This provides a convenient application in printing industry for the separated film deposition and heat-treatment steps, comparing to the commonly used hot plate pyrolysis procedure of   $  TiO_{2}  $ . Combined with suitable doping and ink processing techniques, it is possible to fabrication an optimum ETL that could accelerate the development for high speed and low cost printing energy from PSCs.

Thus in this report, we presented a systematic investigation through surface quality control on the Zr doping effect of   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact ETLs and their application performances in planar methylammonium lead halides (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , or   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  ) PSCs. We devoted major concerns on the ambient humidity control during the preparation of the ETLs. We find that the performances of PSCs had an extremely sensitive correlation to the relative humidity (RH) during the ETL preparation, which however has not been reported in literatures. Thus, by employing the optimized Zr-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer, the perovskite devices not only achieved an increased Voc and a good fill factor (FF) but also alleviated the hysteresis. The devices reached a high PCE of 15.06%, an improved average Voc over 1V, and a good stability with only a 15% drop from the original PCE. This paper opens a novel strategy of enhancing the PCE and stability of PSC through the inspecting of the environment induced interface condition and combining the band alignment optimization.

# 2. Experimental methods

# 2.1.Materials

Unless specified, otherwise all chemicals were purchased from either Alfa Aesar or Sigma-Aldrich and used as received. Ziconium(IV) butoxide (80% in 1-Butanol) was purchased from Energy Chemical, Spiro-OMeTAD was purchased from Shenzhen Feiming Science and Technology Co., Ltd., and MAI (CH₃NH₃I) was purchased from Lumtec, Taiwan.

# 2.2. Preparation of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ink

15 mL ethyl alcohol, 2 mL triethanolamine and 4.25 mL titanate n-butoxide were subsequently added into a round-bottom flask with a stirring bar. After stirring for two hours, 5 mL glacial acetic acid and 5 mL deionized water were added drop by drop. The whole solution was stirred overnight and then stored in refrigerator for aging. For the Zr-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   sol-gel, zirconium n-butoxide was added into the   $  TiO_{2}  $   sol-gel with a series of molar ratio of Zr/Ti at 0.5%, 1%, 2%, 3.5%, 5%, 7.5% and 10% before the first stirring stage of the solution, respectively.

# 2.3. Device fabrication

The FTO glass was etched by a laser machine (Universal Laser Systems, VLS2.30), and followed by ultrasonic cleaning through detergent, pure water and ethyl alcohol for 20 min, respectively. The undoped or Zr-doped TiO  $  _{2}  $  compact blocking layer were first deposited on the clean FTO glass by spin-coated at 3000 rpm for 30 s, followed by a thermal treatment at 500 °C for 40 min. We used dehumidifier and humidifier to simulate the change of ambient environment humidity before spin-coating TiO  $  _{2}  $  films. The sprayed TiO  $  _{2}  $  (~30 nm) was be deposited at 450 °C by the pyrolysis of Titanium diisopropoxide (bis-2,4-pentanedionate) which was be diluted into 1/10 using isopropyl by volume, and then cooled to room temperature. The perovskite layers were deposited by spun a 25 μL 45 wt% CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $  DMF solution prepared from PbI  $  _{2}  $  and CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  I in a molar ratio of 1:1 at 6500 rpm for 30 s using a gas-assisted method (Huang et al., 2014), and then annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. A 25 μL spiro-OMeTAD solution was spun on the film at 3000 rpm for 30 s which prepared by dissolving 83.2 mg spiro-OMeTAD, 15.8 μL of a stock solution of 500 mg lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulphonyl)imide in 1 mL acetonitrile and 34 μL 4-tert-butylpyridine in 1 mL chlorobenzene. At last a 60 nm thick of Au was evaporated as a back contact electrode to complete the whole device.

# 2.4. Characterization

The morphologies and microstructures of the prepared undoped or Zr-doped  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  films and the cross-sectional structure of the perovskite solar cells were investigated using a field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM, Zeiss Ultra Plus). The  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  anatase phase was tested by an X-ray diffractometer (XRD, D8 Advance). The surface chemical state of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  was investigated using an X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, VG Multilab 2000X). The Mott-Schottky plots were tested by the electrochemical measurements (Bio-Logic-Science instruments, SP300). The photocurrent density-voltage curves of the perovskite solar cells were measured using a solar simulator (Oriel 94023A,  $ 300\mathrm{W} $ ). The intensity  $ (100\mathrm{mW/cm^2}) $  was calibrated using a standard Si-solar cell (Oriel, VLSI standards). All the devices were tested under AM 1.5G sun light  $ (100\mathrm{mW/cm^2}) $  using a metal mask of  $ 0.16\mathrm{cm}^2 $  with a scan rate of  $ 0.01\mathrm{V/s} $ .

The air moisture could cause chemical and microstructural variations during film formation (Bockmeyer et al., 2006), which may lead to variety on the performance of perovskite devices. # 3.3. Photovoltaic properties of Zr-doped  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  compact layer

The photovoltaic properties and stability of the Zr-doped PSC devices were investigated and the results were shown in Fig. 7. Fig. 7(a) showed the photocurrent density-voltage  $ (J - V) $  curves of the devices made from different concentration of Zr-doped  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL. The detailed photovoltaic parameters were listed in Table 1. The Zr doping concentration has a significant influence on the perovskite PCE (Fig. S6). The best doping concentration was at around  $ 2\mathrm{mol}\% $  , and further increasing of  $ \mathrm{Zr} $  will reduce the photovoltaic performance. The value of Voc has increased mostly because of the up-shifted CBM position of  $ \mathrm{Zr / TiO_2} $  and the better FF comes

from the reduced recombination of electrons and holes and also the efficient electron transport from the   $  Zr/TiO_{2}  $   into FTO. This indicates a synergistic improvement of surface states and band alignment from controlled environment and doping, respectively.

The  $ \mathrm{Zr}(0.02) $ - $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL based devices with optimized band alignment showed the best performance with a high Voc of 1.024 V, a Jsc of  $ 19.35\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $  confirmed by the EQE spectra (Fig. 7b), a FF of 0.76 and a PCE about  $ 15.06\% $ , significantly improved than the undoped  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  sample ( $ 12.25\% $ ). In addition, the  $ \mathrm{Zr}(0.02) $ - $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  device showed a much lower hysteresis (Fig. 7c) than the PSC with the undoped  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and also the commonly sprayed pyrolyzed  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  devices as shown in Fig. S7. The upshift of the CBM position of  $ \mathrm{Zr}(0.02) $ - $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  resulted in a better match of the energy level with perovskite, which reduced the recombination of electrons and holes and enhanced the carriers lifetime (Nagaoka et al., 2015). The doping of the  $ \mathrm{Zr} $  also promoted the electron transport and charge extraction from perovskite. Therefore, the devices showed a lower hysteresis. Moreover, the as-made  $ \mathrm{Zr}(0.02) $ - $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  PSCs exhibited a good stability as shown in Fig. 7f. The device with a PCE of  $ 15.06\% $  that was aged for  $ 550\mathrm{h} $  at  $ 14\mathrm{RH}\% $  without encapsulation showed only  $ 15\% $  reduction of PCE to  $ 12.66\% $ , with a Voc of  $ 0.989\mathrm{V} $ , a Jsc of  $ 18.20\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $  and a FF of 0.70 (Fig. 7d). This has showed a considerable advantage over the sprayed  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  based devices of which the PCE values decreased more than  $ 35\% $  from  $ 15.53\% $  to only  $ 9.99\% $  (Fig. 7e). The  $ \mathrm{Zr}(0.02) $ - $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  had a much larger crystal size than that of the sprayed  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ . This means prepared  $ \mathrm{Zr}-\mathrm{TiO_2} $  had a much lower surface area and then a lower photocatalytic activity, which is part of the cause of the instability of PSCs (Zhao et al., 2015). The doping of the  $ \mathrm{Zr} $  also promoted the electron transport and charge extraction from perovskite. Therefore, the devices showed a lower hysteresis. # 4. Conclusions

In summary, we present efficient planar perovskite solar cells using sol-gel processed  $ \mathrm{Zr / TiO_2} $  films as the compact ETL under the controlled humidity. The  $ \mathrm{Zr / TiO_2} $  film prepared at  $ 55\mathrm{RH}\% $  was found as the optimized ETL for its compact consecutive morphology and low surface energy barrier for carrier transportation. In comparison to the performance of the undoped  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , the  $ 2\mathrm{mol}\% $  Zr-doped  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  based devices exhibited a lower hysteresis and an increased PCE over  $ 15\% $  with a high Voc of  $ 1.024\mathrm{V} $ , a Jsc of  $ 19.35\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $  and a FF of 0.76. It also showed a higher reproducibility and a better stability than the spray deposited  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $

based devices. This enhancement can be attributed to the upshifted conduction band and less defects of the  $ \mathrm{Zr} $  doped  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  compact ETL that was processed under the desired ambient humidity. This paper highlights the importance of ambient environment during device preparation, drawing attention to PSC interfaces modification and band alignment for high efficiency and stable PSC. Moreover, the low cost  $ \mathrm{Zr / TiO_2} $  ink is also presented as a feasible alternative ETL candidate for planar PSCs, which possesses high potential for the printing compatible PSC fabrication.
