# Organic N-Type Molecule: Managing the Electronic States of Bulk Perovskite for High-Performance Photovoltaics

Haiyang Chen, Yu Zhan, Guiying Xu, Weijie Chen, Shuhui Wang, Moyao Zhang, Yaowen Li,* and Yongfang Li

The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of planar p–i–n perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) is commonly lower than that of the n–i–p pero-SCs, due to the severe nonradiative recombination stemming from the more p-type perovskite with prevailing electron traps. Here, two n-type organic molecules, DMBI-2-Th and DMBI-2-Th-I, with hydrogen-transfer properties for the doping of bulk perovskite aimed at regulating its electronic states are synthesized. The generated radicals in these n-type dopants with high-lying singly occupied molecular orbitals enable easy transfer of the thermally activated electrons to the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite for the realization of n-doped perovskites. The n-doping degree could be further enhanced by using the iodine ionized dopant DMBI-2-Th-I. The doping effect could reduce the electron trap density, increase the electron concentration of the bulk perovskite, and simultaneously improve the surface electronic contact. When the DMBI-2-Th-I-doped perovskite is used in planar p–i–n pero-SCs, the nonradiative recombination is significantly suppressed. As a result, the photovoltaic performance improved significantly, as evidenced by an excellent PCE of 20.90% and a robust ambient stability even under high relative humidity. To the best of the knowledge, this work represents the first example where organic n-type dopants are used to tune the electronic states of a bulk perovskite film for efficient planar p–i–n pero-SCs.

Perovskite film is a near-eigenstate crystal with ambipolar charge transporting property.  $  ^{[15]}  $   The film characteristics, and specifically its doping state (n-type, p-type, or intrinsic) near the underlying transport layers, are strongly affected by the polarity of the host substrate.  $  ^{[8,16-18]}  $   As for the planar p–i–n structure, the perovskites crystallized on hole transporting

layers (HTLs, p-type substrates) have shown to be more p-type (or less n-type) in nature,  $  ^{[15-16]}  $  which may lead to an unbalanced ambipolar charge transporting ability. Moreover, many photoelectrons are generated at the HTL/perovskite interface near the light-incident electrode (indium tin oxide (ITO)) side), and these electrons must travel through several-hundred nanometers thick perovskite layer prior to collection by cathode. The long transport distance and prevailing electron-deficient traps in the more p-type perovskite film can more easily capture the electrons than the n-type films.  $  ^{[12]}  $  The electron diffusion length is shorter than the hole diffusion length  $  ^{[19-20]}  $  and, hence, electron losses can be quite severe in the planar p–i–n pero-SCs. Compared with a more n-type perovskite film, a more p-type perovskite film will more likely form an unfavorable n-type contact with the electron transporting layer (ETL) at the charge-extraction region due to the mismatched electron concentration.  $  ^{[17-18]}  $  The aforementioned factors would thus lead to a relatively low short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) in the p–i–n pero-SCs compared with that of the n–i–p structure.

# 1. Introduction

The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) has improved significantly through the use of the n–i–p structure.  $  ^{[1-5]}  $   Meanwhile, the planar p–i–n pero-SCs have also attracted considerable attention

Strategies such as graded heterojunctions and interfacial electronic modification have been proposed as methods of

due to their low-temperature processing and simple device architecture, especially for potential application in the roll-to-roll mass production of flexible pero-SCs.  $  ^{[6-7]}  $   Nevertheless, their PCEs still lag behind those of the n-i-p pero-SCs,  $  ^{[8-12]}  $   owing primarily to the following two factors: i) the considerable density of defects that would enable nonradiative recombination losses both inside the perovskite bulk material and at the interface, thereby resulting in a less-than-ideal open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ).  $  ^{[8,13,14]}  $   ii) The low electron extraction efficiency due to the high density of electron traps in perovskite films and the inferior n-type perovskite charge-extraction region (compared with that of p-type perovskite).  $  ^{[12]}  $

Han and co-workers constructed a gradient n-type [6,6]-phenyl- $ C_{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) at the perovskite interface by dropping a PCBM solution on the precursor perovskite film. This structure enhanced the PCE of planar p–i–n pero-SCs by shortening the electron-diffusion pathway in the perovskite phase and improving the electronic contact at the interface, thereby significantly suppressing the carrier recombination.[12] Zhu and co-workers grew an n-doped perovskite thin film at the interface in order to improve the electron-deficient traps and electronic nature of the contact. Through this method, nonradiative recombination in the p–i–n pero-SC was efficiently reduced, leading to an enhanced  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $ .[8] Although the surface defects of perovskite film grown on p-type substrates were reduced, those inside the perovskite bulk material persisted. Therefore, fabrication of an n-doped perovskite film in planar p–i–n pero-SCs is an effective way for simultaneous reduction of the nonradiative electron recombination both inside the bulk and at the perovskite surface.

Self-doping is a classical method for regulating the electronic states of perovskite by controlling the stoichiometric ratio.  $  ^{[21]}  $   However, precise tuning of p/n type perovskites is difficult as external factors (e.g., the annealing temperature and atmosphere) easily influence the stoichiometric ratios of the perovskite film.  $  ^{[22-23]}  $   The electronic states can also be regulated through partial substitution of Pb by heterovalent Bi or Sb, thereby inducing shallow donor levels with small ionization energies in the bandgap and enhancing the electron density of states.  $  ^{[24-27]}  $   However, doping with metal ions is detrimental to the device stability due to the more severe ion migration (than that occurring in the nondoped material) particularly under operating conditions.  $  ^{[28-29]}  $   Recently, organic n-type dopants of ETL have attracted considerable attention due to their easily tailored structure,  $  ^{[30-31]}  $   good solubility,  $  ^{[32]}  $   and remarkable stability without ion/atom migration.  $  ^{[33-34]}  $   Among them, 1,3-Dimethyl-2-phenyl-2,3-dihydro-1H-benzoimidazole (DMBI) derivatives with superior n-type doping ability were widely used as n-type dopant to tune the electronic properties of ETLs in organic thin-film transistors (OTFTs),  $  ^{[35]}  $   organic solar cells (OSCs),  $  ^{[36]}  $   and pero-SCs.  $  ^{[37-38]}  $   Bao and co-workers designed a (4-(1,3-dimethyl-2,3-dihydro-1H-benzoimidazol-2-yl)phenyl)dimethylamine (N-DMBI) with free radicals to dope PCBM. As a result, the PCBM-based OTFTs realized a five-order-of-magnitude increasement in the electrical conductivity.  $  ^{[39]}  $   Similarly, N-DMBI was used to dope naphthalene diimide (NDI) ETL for increasing its electron mobility, the PCE of related polymer solar cell was obviously enhanced.  $  ^{[36]}  $   A N-DMBI analogue, 4-(1,3-dimethyl-2,3-dihydro-1H-benzimidazol-2-yl)-N,N-diphenylaniline (N-DPBI), was synthesized to stabilize the radicals through the resonance of additional aromatic phenyl rings, and the N-DPBI-doped ETL could further increase the pero-SCs stability.  $  ^{[31]}  $   While Duan and co-workers demonstrated that DMBI derivatives with hydrogen-transfer properties could form radicals in PCBM solution, thereby facilitating PCBM ETL to accept electron and realizing n-type doping.  $  ^{[30]}  $   With this strategy, pero-SCs based on PCBM ETL effectively minimized the   $  V_{oc}  $   loss. However, there are few reports on the use of organic dopants for tuning the electronic properties of bulk perovskite films.

In this work, we designed and synthesized two DMBI derivatives 1,3-dimethyl-2-(thiophen-2-yl)-2,3-dihydro-1H-benzo[d]imidazole (DMBI-2-Th) and its iodine ionized molecule DMBI-2-Th-I for using to n-dope bulk perovskite in planar p-i-n pero-SCs. The attached donor thiophene on DMBI and the ionization with I  $  ^{-}  $  more sufficiently initiated organic radicals in DMBI-2-Th-I than in DMBI-2-Th. This led to an increase in the electron density and high-lying singly occupied molecular orbital (SOMO) energy level of DMBI-2-Th-I, thereby enabling easy transfer of the thermally activated electrons from the organic dopant to the perovskite. Consequently, an n-doped perovskite was realized.  $  ^{[30,39]}  $  The changes in perovskite electronic states could increase the electron concentration, reduce the electron trap density, improve the ambipolar charge transporting property in bulk perovskite, and facilitate the electronic contact of perovskite with ETL. The pero-SCs with a 0.1-wt% DMBI-2-Th-I-doped perovskite active layer exhibited a considerably improved photovoltaic performance, with an optimal PCE of 20.90% and a maximum  $  V_{oc}  $  of 1.13 V. More importantly, the unencapsulated devices also showed excellent moisture stability, with only a slight degradation after being kept for 864 h in ambient atmosphere with a high relative humidity (RH).

# 2. Results and Discussion

The molecular structures of DMBI-2-Th and DMBI-2-Th-I are shown in Figure 1a, and the corresponding synthetic routes and detailed synthetic processes are described in the Material Synthesis section in the Supporting Information. All the molecular structures were well defined by   $  {}^{1}  $  H and   $  {}^{13}  $  C NMR spectra, as shown in Figures S1–S4 (Supporting Information). We also evaluated the thermal stability of these materials. As shown in the thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA) thermograms (Figure S5, Supporting Information), both DMBI-2-Th (decomposition temperature at 5% weight loss (  $  t_{d}  $  ): 159.33 °C) and DMBI-2-Th-I (  $  t_{d}  $  : 209.83 °C) exhibited relatively good thermal stability. Therefore, these stable organic dopants could be used in the perovskite film even during the device fabrication process.

The DMBI derivatives possess the characteristic of efficient hydrogen transfer to form highly reactive organic radicals.  $  ^{[31,35]}  $   When the DMBI derivatives are attached with an electron-rich moiety (e.g., thiophene on imidazole), this ability to form organic radicals will be enhanced due to the enhanced electron-donating ability (Figure 1b). The electron spin resonance (ESR) spectrum was measured to evaluate the organic radicals in DMBI-2-Th and DMBI-2-Th-I, as shown in Figure 1c. It can be seen that DMBI-2-Th exhibits a relatively weak ESR signal. After ionization by I  $  ^{-}  $   in DMBI-2-Th-I, the intensity of the ESR signal enhanced significantly, suggesting that the number of organic radicals had increased. In addition, an efficient n-type dopant for perovskite should exhibit high electron-donating ability and well-matched energy levels, which are closely related to the electron density as well as the SOMO energy level. The corresponding electron density plots and SOMO energy levels of DMBI-2-Th and DMBI-2-Th-I radicals were calculated at the B3LYP/LANL2DZ/6-31G* level using the Gaussian09 package. We also investigated the effect of doping on the perovskite film composition and growth, where a modified two-step sequential deposition method was employed. As shown in Figure S6 (Supporting Information), the lead (II) iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2) $ :methanammonium iodide (MAI) (1.3 M:0.3 M) precursor solution, containing different concentrations of DMBI-2-Th or DMBI-2-Th-I (0.03, 0.05, 0.1, 0.5, and 1.0 wt%), was spin-coated on top of the ITO/poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl) amine] (PTAA). The MAI precursor solution in isopropanol (IPA) was then spin-coated on the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ :MAI precursor film, and subjected to a thermal annealing process to form the resultant perovskite films in nitrogen atmosphere. Here, we denoted the perovskite film containing various concentrations (0.03, 0.05, 0.1, 0.5, and 1.0 wt%) of

DMBI-2-Th/DMBI-2-Th-I as Pero-Th-0.03/Pero-I-0.03, Pero-Th-0.05/Pero-I-0.05, Pero-Th-0.1/Pero-I-0.1, Pero-Th-0.5/Pero-I-0.5, and Pero-Th-1/Pero-I-1, respectively. The perovskite film without dopants was denoted as Pero-pristine.

The level of dopant incorporation in the perovskite films was investigated via time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectroscopy (ToF-SIMS) measurements where the dopant distribution based on the ITO/PTAA/Pero-I-1 structure was evaluated. Through these measurements, the spatial distribution of   $  I^{-}  $  ,   $  S^{-}  $  , and   $  InO_{2}^{-}  $  ions associated with the distribution of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  , DMBI-2-Th-I, and ITO, respectively, was determined. As shown in Figure 2a, the   $  S^{-}  $  depth profile of the Pero-I-1 film shows a continuous and gradual increase accompanied by a significant spatial overlap of the   $  I^{-}  $  profile. This suggests that the DMBI-2-Th-I was successfully incorporated into the perovskite film with a top-down gradient distribution. The distribution of DMBI-2-Th-I in the film is related to the intramolecular exchange process of two-step deposition method, as illustrated in Note S1 (Supporting Information).  $  ^{[41-42]}  $

The effects of dopants (DMBI-2-Th and DMBI-2-Th-I) on the crystallization and morphology of the perovskite films were explored via X-Ray Diffraction (XRD) and Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) of the perovskite films grown on PTAA. # 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, we designed two organic n-type dopants (DMBI-2-Th and DMBI-2-Th-I) to regulate the electronic states of bulk perovskite for efficient p-i-n pero-SCs. The doping mechanism and the relationship between the molecular structure and electronic states of the perovskite were clarified. The results revealed that, compared with DMBI-2-Th, DMBI-2-Th-I with DMBI moiety and iodine ionization provided a stronger electron transfer driving force to the perovskite film, thus leading to a more obvious n-type doping effect. This contributed to significant improvement in the electronic states of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  , including the electron trap density, electron concentration, ambipolar charge transporting property, and electronic extraction efficiency. Consequently, the nonradiative recombination in planar p-i-n pero-SCs was greatly suppressed. As a result, the pero-SCs based on Pero-I-0.1 showed a comprehensively improved photovoltaic performance, delivering a substantially increased PCE from 17.39% to 20.90%. More importantly, the unencapsulated device simultaneously possessed robust ambient stability even under high RH of 70–80%. This n-type organic dopant for doping the bulk perovskite provides a new way to further improve photovoltaic performance and stability of the p-i-n pero-SCs.
