# Stabilization of Perovskite Lattice and Suppression of Sn $  ^{2+}  $ /Sn $  ^{4+}  $  Oxidation via Formamidine Acetate for High Efficiency Tin Perovskite Solar Cells

Shurong Wang, Huanhuan Yao, Weike Zhu, Cheng Wu, Zhiyue Tang, Junke Liu, Liming Ding,* and Feng Hao*

Tin halide lead-free perovskite solar cells (TPSCs) have received tremendous research interest recently due to their nearly ideal bandgap, broad light absorption, non-toxicity, and environmental friendliness. However, the uncontrollable crystallization process and the facile oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   limit the further increase of power conversion efficiency (PCE). To solve these problems, a series of acetates are introduced into the perovskite precursor solution to regulate the crystallization process. It is revealed that formamidine acetate (FAAc) has strong C=O-Sn coordination with   $  Sn^{2+}  $   compared with acetic acid (HAc) and methylammonium acetate (MAAc), which can stabilize the lattice structure, minimize defect states and suppress the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $  . Meanwhile, benefiting from this coordination ability, it not only leads to large-size colloidal clusters in precursor but also slows down the crystallization process and improves the crystallinity of tin halide perovskite films. The device with FAAc achieved an increased PCE from initially 9.84% to 12.43%, and it could maintain 94% of its initial value for 2000 h in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere. This work provides a feasible strategy for depositing high-quality tin perovskite films with low defect density and lattice distortion, which will be crucial for related photovoltaics and other optoelectronic devices.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The tin perovskite films were deposited by a one-step-spin-coating process. Formamidine acetate (FAAc) (5 mmol%) was introduced into the precursor solution. To prove the special effect of FAAc, other precursors with acidic additives like acetic acid (HAc) and methylammonium acetate (MAAc) were also prepared. Firstly, FAAc, FAAc-SnI $ _2 $ , HAc, HAc-SnI $ _2 $ , MAAc, and MAAc-SnI $ _2 $  powder as well as the deposited perovskite films were recorded by Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy to probe the coordination of CH $ _3 $ COO $ ^- $  (Ac $ ^- $ ) with Sn $ ^{2+} $  (Figure 1a,b; Figure S1, Supporting Information). The characteristic peak of C=O stretching shifted from 1654.80 to 1650.74 cm $ ^{-1} $ , implying the strong coordination interaction between Sn $ ^{2+} $  and Ac $ ^- $  in FAAc by Lewis acid-base adduct. $ ^{[14]} $  Meanwhile, the stretching vibration peaks of C=O in HAc-SnI $ _2 $  and MAAc-SnI $ _2 $  also shifted to a lower wavenumber relative to HAc and MAAc, respectively (Figure S1, Supporting Information), which further indicated that the carboxyl group of acetate anion could coordinate with Sn $ ^{2+} $ . Besides, the C=O stretching from a higher wave number position (1718.45 cm $ ^{-1} $ ) also showed a greatly increased intensity by forming the Lewis acid-base adduct. Meanwhile, the N–H stretching of FAAc films in Figure 1b shifted from 3411.76 to 3400.14 cm $ ^{-1} $ , which can

To prove the structural stability of tin perovskite films, the Urbach energy (  $  E_{u}  $  ) in Figure 1c and Figure S2 (Supporting Information) was determined by the equation   $  \alpha=\alpha_{o}\exp\left(\frac{h\nu-E_{g}}{E_{u}}\right)  $  , where   $  \alpha  $   and   $  E_{g}  $   denotes to the absorbance and bandgap, respectively (the plots of films with HAc and MAAc were displayed in Figure S3, Supporting Information). The curve of logarithmic absorbance versus absorbed energy was obtained to calculate the reciprocal of   $  E_{u}  $  . As a result, there was a decreasing tendency of   $  E_{u}  $   with the incorporation of acetates into the tin films. Meanwhile, the   $  E_{u}  $   showed the order of   $  E_{u-control}  $   (63 meV) >   $  E_{u-MAAc}  $   (62 meV) >   $  E_{u-HAc}  $   (57 meV) >   $  E_{u-FAAc}  $   (45 meV) (Figure S3, Supporting Information), which indicated that the introduction of FAAc can reduce the structural defects and defect density, and improve the crystal structural stability.  $  ^{[15]}  $   The A-site cations of MA+ and FA+ showed similar effects on Eu. Though MAAc has stronger coordination with Sn  $  ^{2+}  $   compared to the FAAc, a larger   $  E_{u}  $   was obtained, which may bring unfavorable structural stability and defects. Therefore, FAAc is the ideal choice due to enhanced structural stability and strong coordination ability.

We further probed the antioxidant property by using N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) as a solvent to prepare   $  SnI_{2}  $   solutions (Figure 1d). When exposed to the air, the solution with FAAc slowly turned to deep red compared to the solution without FAAc (control). The red color usually represents the appearance of   $  Sn^{4+}  $   ions.  $  ^{[16]}  $   The color change is a clear evidence of chemical reaction. Therefore, the introduction of FAAc can obviously retard the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $  . To further verify the suppressed oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $ , the ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) absorption of control and FAAc precursors were monitored. Considering oxidation from   $  I^{-}  $  to   $  I_{3}^{-}  $  often occurs after the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $ , we can judge the oxidation degree of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   by observing the appearance speed of two characteristic peaks of   $  I_{3}^{-}  $  at 293 and 365 nm, respectively.  $  ^{[17,18]}  $   After aging in the air for 60 min (Figure 1e,f), there is an obvious   $  I_{3}^{-}  $  peak in the control   $  SnI_{2}  $   solution. The   $  I_{3}^{-}  $  peak in   $  SnI_{2}  $   solution with FAAc only appears after 120 min. This phenomenon agreed with the color change of FAAc precursor, which further confirmed that FAAc could delay the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   to some degree.

We further studied the effect of FAAc on the colloid size of perovskite precursors by using dynamic light scattering (DLS) as shown in Figure 2a,b. The size of the colloid increased with the addition of FAAc, and the larger aggregates may be attributed to the strong coordination between  $ \mathrm{Ac}^- $  and  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $ . Notably, the aggregates in perovskite precursor affect the grain size and nucleation density of crystal nuclei.[19] Considering the high nucleation density in the control precursor, massive particles with small size may lead to poor film quality. On the contrary, larger crystal nucleus and lower nucleation density in FAAc precursor can help to deposit thin films with high crystallinity and high quality.

Furthermore, the strong coordination interaction can also effectively slow down the crystal growth rate of thin films, which is confirmed by monitoring the crystallization process of thin films during the spin-coating process in (Figure S4, Supporting Information). Figure 2c,d shows the atomic force microscopy (AFM) images of control and FAAc films. Compared with the control film, the root means square (RMS) of

FAAc film reduced from 18.47 to 14.20 nm, which is conducive to the contact between perovskite layer and transport layer. The improved contact can reduce the leakage current, and suppress the carrier recombination at the interface.  $  ^{[20]}  $   Due to the improvement in film quality, the contact angle of FAAc film increased from 55.7° to 61.7° compared with the control film, which indicated improved moisture resistance with FAAc (Figure S5, Supporting Information).  $  ^{[21]}  $   The three characteristic peaks at 14.3°, 28.6°, and 43.2° in the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of Figure 2e could be assigned to the (100), (200) and (300) crystal planes of perovskite, respectively. Compared with perovskite films with other additives, FAAc films showed the highest crystallinity, further indicating enhanced film quality. Besides, the introduction of FAAc (Figure 2f) also caused the   $  2\theta  $   peak to move to a higher angle, indicating of a smaller lattice parameter. The incorporation of FAAc into tin perovskites may suppress the A-site-related vacancies in the lattice,  $  ^{[22]}  $   thus enabling to more compact unit cells and stabilized structure, which is consistent with the decreased   $  E_{u}  $  .  $  ^{[13b]}  $   Figure 2g shows the micro-strain (  $  \varepsilon  $  ) on different crystal planes calculated by XRD patterns, and its specific values were summarized in Table S1 (Supporting Information).  $  ^{[23]}  $   The FAAc films showed the smallest   $  \varepsilon  $   value on the (100) and (200) planes compared with other films, indicating decreased lattice distortion. More interestingly, the trend of lattice strain was similar to that of   $  E_{u}  $   in Figure 1c, which further confirmed that lattice strain is related to the defects and structural stability in perovskite films. In addition, the XRD patterns and   $  \varepsilon  $   values with different FAAc concentrations were further explored. FAAc (5 mol%) shows the strongest crystallization and the smallest micro-strain (Figure S6, Supporting Information) and was chosen for further study in this work.

Then, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) on the glass substrate in Figure 2h showed that the PL intensity of FAAc film is about twice that of the control film. In addition, the peak of FAAc film was located at 849 nm, and that of control film was located at 854 nm. Meanwhile, the PL intensity of HAc and MAAc films was both weaker than that of the FAAc film. Herein, the slightly blue-shifted peak and enhanced PL intensity indicated that the introduction of FAAc is beneficial to inhibit defect-induced nonradiative recombination.  $  ^{[24]}  $   Meanwhile, the time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) in Figure 2i demonstrated a prolonged decay time in FAAc film. The average decay time was in order of 2-fold higher than the control film, further indicated less non-radiative recombination and fewer defects in the FAAc film (the specific values are summarized in Table S2, Supporting Information).  $  ^{[25]}  $

# 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, by regulating the crystallization process of tin perovskite films with FAAc, the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   was effectively hindered. The results of FTIR and Urbach energy showed that FAAc had a strong coordination ability with   $  Sn^{2+}  $   with enhanced lattice stability, thus delivering reduced lattice distortion and suppressed   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation. Moreover, the clusters with larger colloid sizes can be formed in the precursor solution with FAAc, which can reduce the nucleation density and slow down the crystallization rate by combining with strong coordination ability. Benefiting from the enhanced film quality, the FAAc films showed low trap density and non-radiative recombination loss and further promoted the collection and transmission of carriers of corresponding devices. Finally, the corresponding devices achieved an excellent PCE of 12.43% with excellent stability. After aging for 2000 h in the   $  N_{2}  $   glove box, it still maintained 94% of the initial PCE. In short, this work sheds light on the pursuit of more efficient and stable lead-free photovoltaics by regulating the crystallization process and suppressing unwanted oxidation.
