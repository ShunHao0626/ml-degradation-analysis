# Abstract

All-inorganic perovskite solar cells have drawn widespread attention for its superior thermal stability. Carbon-based devices are promising to demonstrate excellent long-term operational stability due to the hydrophobicity of carbon materials and the abandon of organic hole-transporting materials (HTMs). However, the difficulty to control the crystallinity process and the poor morphology leads to serious non-radiative recombination, resulting in low  $  V_{OC}  $  and PCE. In this paper, the crystal formation process of all-inorganic perovskites is controlled with a facile composition engineering strategy. By bromide incorporation, high-quality perovskite films with large grain and fewer grain boundaries are achieved. As-prepared perovskite films demonstrate longer carrier lifetime, contributing to lower energy loss and better device performance. Fabricated carbon-based HTM-free PVSCs with  $  CsPbI_{2.33}Br_{0.67}  $  perovskite realized champion power conversion efficiency of 12.40%, superior to 8.80% of  $  CsPbI_3  $ -based devices, which is one of the highest efficiencies reported for the carbon-based all-inorganic PVSCs to date. The high  $  V_{OC}  $  of 1.01 V and FF of 70.98% indicate the significance of this composition engineering method. Moreover, fabricated carbon-based devices exhibit excellent stability, unencapsulated device retains over 90% of its initial efficiency under continuous one sun illumination for 250 hours in  $  N_2  $  atmosphere and keeps ~84% of its original value after stored in ambient environment with RH 15%-20% for 200 hours. This work provides a facile way to fabricate high-performance and stable carbon-based all-inorganic perovskite solar cells.

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) have drawn significant attentions among the academic and industry communities owing to their unique semiconductor properties including high absorption coefficient,  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   tunable bandgap,  $  ^{[4-7]}  $   low exciton binding energies,  $  ^{[8-9]}  $   long carrier diffusion lengths (  $  >1\mu m  $  ),  $  ^{[10-11]}  $   as well as low-temperature and solution-processable fabrication procedure and economical prices.  $  ^{[12-13]}  $   During the past few years, the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PVSCs has reached a value of 25.2%, being comparable to silicon solar cells and other thin film photovoltaic technologies, which demonstrates its large potential as next-generation photovoltaic. However, the volatile organic cations such as Methylammonium (MA  $  ^{+}  $  ), Formamidinium (FA  $  ^{+}  $  ), et al. engender the poor stability of PVSCs, especially under thermal, moisture and continuous light illumination condition.  $  ^{[14-17]}  $   To overcome this hurdle, it is necessary to develop a strategy to significantly enhance the long-term

operational stability of PVSCs.  $  ^{[18-21]}  $   Recently, all-inorganic perovskite has been considered as one of alternatives due to their remarkable thermal stability, which was formed by replacing organic cations in A site with inorganic counterparts (Cs  $  ^{+}  $   ions).  $  ^{[22-29]}  $

Although   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   has drawn the most attentions among scientific communities due to the most suitable bandgap (  $  \sim1.73~eV  $  ) for photovoltaics, the poor phase and moisture stability of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   at room temperature limit its commercialization. The black perovskite phase (  $  \alpha-CsPbI_{3}  $  ) would transfer to yellow non-perovskite phase (  $  \delta-CsPbI_{3}  $  ) with slow cooling or under ambient moisture, or yield a polymorph black orthorhombic phase (  $  \gamma-CsPbI_{3}  $  ) with rapidly cooling to room temperature with the absence of moisture.  $  ^{[25, 30-31]}  $   The poor stability mainly results from the low tolerant factor caused by the small radius of Cs atom. Apart from the phase instability of perovskites, the existence of organic HTMs and metal electrode also causes destructive effects on the operational stability due to the migration of halide ions and metal ions. Carbon electrodes have great potential to effectively tailor and enhance the stability of all-inorganic PVSCs.  $  ^{[6, 32-33]}  $   The hydrophobicity and robustness of a carbon layer effectively protect lead halide perovskite in extreme environmental conditions including high humidity, which is crucial for practically benchmarking PVSC performance. Several reports have shown that carbon-based devices demonstrate excellent stability, however, the low performance limits the development of this field.  $  ^{[23, 32, 34-35]}  $   Recently, the efficiencies of reported carbon-based all-inorganic PVSCs are low, which account for less than 50% of their Shockley-Queisser limit. The inferior efficiency, especially the low open circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) in contrast with quasi fermi level splitting (QFLS), mainly originates from the inevitable defects formed in perovskite film during the crystal formation process, which serves as non-radiative recombination centers and causes serious energy loss. Although several groups have tried to boost the efficiency by B-site doping and additive incorporation, the performance is still lower than their organic-inorganic counterpart.  $  ^{[33, 36-38]}  $   Composition engineering have been widely employed to reduce the trap density in organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite and all-inorganic perovskite. Zhao et al. have incorporated methylammonium bromide into   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite to realize a smooth and pinhole-free perovskite film, resulting in enhanced power conversion efficiency.  $  ^{[39]}  $   Zhang et al. employed CsBr post treatment to passivate the defects in the grain boundaries of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , the   $  V_{OC}  $   was enhanced from 1.10 V to 1.27 V.  $  ^{[40]}  $   Zheng et al. added guanidinium into   $  HPbI_{3}  $ -based all-inorganic perovskite to self-organize a 2D/3D perovskite top interface, resulting in enhanced

performance and stability.  $  ^{[41]}  $   These reports inspired us to boost the efficiency of carbon-based all-inorganic perovskite solar cells by simply tuning the composition.

In this work, we demonstrated a planar architecture wherein the carbon electrode was deposited by a low-temperature process. To achieve high photovoltaic performance, we employed a facile composition engineering strategy to fabricate carbon-based all-inorganic PVSCs by incorporating certain amount of bromide into   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite, achieving high-quality perovskite film with large grain size. X-ray diffraction results indicate that the incorporation of bromide induced high quality of intermediate phase, which enhanced the morphology of perovskite after annealing. Optical measurement shows that bromide incorporation leads to longer carrier lifetime and lower band edge disorder. As a result, carbon-based all-inorganic PVSCs with bromide incorporation showed a high PCE of 12.40 %, with open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.01 V, short circuit current (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 17.30 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and fill factor (FF) of 70.98%, which is among the highest performance for carbon-based HTM-free PVSCs. Additionally, these carbon electrode based PVSCs devices exhibit outstanding stability, the unencapsulated devices kept 90% of their initial efficiency under constant one sun illumination for 250 hours in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere and retained ~84% of their initial value after stored for 200 hours in ambient environment (RH 15%-20%). Moreover, the carbon-based devices demonstrate better stability than normal-structured devices when heated at 85°C in ambient environment, proving the advantage of adopting carbon electrode in all-inorganic PVSCs.

# 2. Results and Discussions

All of the perovskite films in this paper were deposited using a facile one-step method according to the previous work[42]. Cesium iodide (CsI) and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . xDMAI complex were dissolved in DMF with the ratio of 1:1 to form the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  perovskite precursor solution. Different amount of lead bromide  $ (\mathrm{PbBr}_2) $  was added into the solution to replace  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . xDMAI to form  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_{2.67}\mathrm{Br}_{0.33} $  and  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_{2.33}\mathrm{Br}_{0.67} $  perovskite. To confirm the morphology of perovskite films with different composition, the scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was adopted and the top-view SEM images of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $ ,  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_{2.67}\mathrm{Br}_{0.33} $  and  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_{2.33}\mathrm{Br}_{0.67} $  perovskite films on the substrate of  $ \mathrm{FTO/TiO_2} $  are shown in Figure 1a-c. The magnified top-view SEM images are also exhibited in Figure S1a-c. The rough  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  film shows small grains accompanied by several pinholes. The average grain size of one hundred grains is  $ 465.00 \pm 118.67 \mathrm{~nm} $  (Figure S2a). After incorporating small amount

X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurement was conducted to investigate the crystallinity of perovskite with different compositions (Figure 1e). The diffraction peaks located at  $ \sim 14.5^{\circ} $  and  $ \sim 29^{\circ} $  corresponded to the (100) and (200) plane respectively.[44] The zoomed-in region of (100) and (200) lattice planes are shown in Figure S4, the diffraction angles of perovskite were slightly shifted to larger angles when incorporating bromide, proving that the bromide with smaller radius than I has been incorporated into the perovskite lattice. The characterization peak of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_{2.67}\mathrm{Br}_{0.33} $  perovskite are broadened to some extent, which may be due to the reduced crystallite size according to the Debye - Scherrer equation. Normally, the crystallite size of perovskite calculated from XRD pattern is always much smaller than the grain size from SEM and there is not a solid relationship between them, as the crystallite size may represent the size of sub grains.[45] Therefore, this phenomenon will not make a big difference on device performance, which can also be observed in previous report.[46-47]

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, we have incorporated bromide into   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite to achieve high-quality perovskite film. The incorporation of bromide induced high-quality perovskite intermediate phase and contributes to the formation of smooth perovskite film with grain size over 1   $  \mu  $  m, inducing reduced grain boundaries and enhanced carrier lifetime. With bromide incorporation, carbon-based HTM-free PVSCs showed an enhanced PCE of 12.40%, which is among the highest reported efficiencies. The enhanced   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.01 V indicates the significantly alleviated energy loss. Meanwhile, Br-incorporated carbon-based devices demonstrate excellent stability, unencapsulated device retained over 90% of its initial efficiency under continuous one sun illumination for 250 hours and kept ~84% of its initial value after stored in ambient environment with RH 15-20% for 200 hours. This work supplies a facile and effective method to enhance the performance and stability of carbon-based all-inorganic PVSCs.
