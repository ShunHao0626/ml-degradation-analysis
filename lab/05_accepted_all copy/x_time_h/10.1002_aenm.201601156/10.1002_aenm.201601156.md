# Copper Salts Doped Spiro-OMeTAD for High-Performance Perovskite Solar Cells

Meng Li, Zhao-Kui Wang,* Ying-Guo Yang, Yun Hu, Shang-Lei Feng, Jin-Miao Wang, Xing-Yu Gao, and Liang-Sheng Liao*

The development of effective and stable hole transporting materials (HTMs) is very important for achieving high-performance planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, copper salts (cuprous thiocyanate (CuSCN) or cuprous iodide (CuI)) doped 2,2,7,7-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobi-fluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) based on a solution processing as the HTM in PSCs is demonstrated. The incorporation of CuSCN (or CuI) realizes a p-type doping with efficient charge transfer complex, which results in improved film conductivity and hole mobility in spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (or CuI) composite films. As a result, the PCE is largely improved from  $ 14.82\% $  to  $ 18.02\% $  due to obvious enhancements in the cell parameters of short-circuit current density and fill factor. Besides the HTM role, the composite film can suppress the film aggregation and crystallization of spiro-OMeTAD films with reduced pin-holes and voids, which slows down the perovskite decomposition by avoiding the moisture infiltration to some extent. The finding in this work provides a simple method to improve the efficiency and stability of planar perovskite solar cells.

Recently, some inorganic semiconductor materials with good chemical stability and high hole mobility have been reported to act as the HTMs in planar heterojunction solar cells.  $  ^{[39-47]}  $   Among them, copper salts such as cuprous thiocyanate (CuSCN) and cuprous iodide (CuI) have already demonstrated potential applications not only in DSSCs but also in PSCs by their advantages of high conductivity, solution-processability, and good stability.  $  ^{[45,46]}  $   The motivation in this work is to develop an effective material possessing good conductivity and stability simultaneously as the hole transport material in PSCs. Our strategy is to combine the advantages of matchable energy levels of spiro-OMeTAD and good stabilities of CuSCN (or CuI) based on a solution processing. The incorporation of CuSCN (or CuI) into the spiro-OMeTAD realizes a p-type doping with efficient charge transfer complex, resulting in improved film conductivity and hole mobility in spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (or CuI) composite films. In addition, the amorphous nature of the organic small molecule spiro-OMeTAD may affect the cell stability because of the oxygen and moisture infiltration through the aggregated films. Different from the cobalt complex dopant,  $  ^{[27]}  $   the introduction of inorganic copper salts can inhibit the film aggregation and crystallization of spiro-OMeTAD films with reduced pinholes and voids, which slows down the perovskite decomposition due to the avoidance of moisture infiltration to some extent. As a result, a champion device with a PCE of 18.02% and improved cell stability are achieved owing to the obvious improvements of cell parameters in short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) and fill factor (FF).

# 2.1. Spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (or CuI) Composite Films

Organic–inorganic hybrid spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (or CuI) composite films were deposited by spin-coating via mixing various ratio (0–42 mol%) of CuSCN (or CuI) solutions (in propyl sulfide, 40 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) into spiro-OMeTAD solution (in chlorobenzene, 80 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ). Atomic force microscopy (AFM) and scanning electron microscope (SEM) evaluations reveal that the film morphology was not largely affected by introducing CuSCN (or CuI) into spiro-OMeTAD (Figure S1, Supporting Information). Figure 2a shows the UV–vis absorption spectra of the pristine spiro-OMeTAD, hybrid spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (33 mol%), and spiro-OMeTAD:CuI (32 mol%) films with a thickness of 200 nm on quartz substrates. The inset is the magnification of the absorption spectra in the range of 420–500 nm. Both copper salts doped spiro-OMeTAD films show a new absorption band with the maximum peak located at 454 nm, which does not correspond to these copper salts themselves. The new absorption band is originated from the oxidized spiro-OMeTAD owing to the charge transfer interaction between spiro-OMeTAD and CuSCN (or CuI) molecules. It means that a p-type doping can be realized in the spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (or CuI) composite films based on a solution processing. Actually, electrical doping is very efficient for resolving the issues of poor conductivity and low carrier mobility of organic semiconductor materials.  $  ^{[48-52]}  $   Particularly, inorganic materials (i.e., transition metal oxides and copper salts) doped organic semiconductor demonstrated special merits in improving the device performance especially the stability.  $  ^{[53-56]}  $

Furthermore, hole-dominated devices with the structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS (40 nm)/doped and non-doped spiro-OMeTAD (200 nm)/MoO $ _3 $  (10 nm)/Ag were fabricated to investigate the electrical properties of CuSCN (or CuI) doped spiro-OMeTAD films. Figure 2b shows the double logarithmic current density–voltage ( $ J $ -V) characteristics in three hole-dominated devices. The doping ratio of CuSCN and CuI is kept as 33 and 32 mol%, respectively. Obviously, the film conductivity of spiro-OMeTAD is enhanced after CuSCN (or CuI) doping based on a fact that the doped devices demonstrated an increased current density with one order of magnitude. And the CuSCN doped device presents the highest current density in the whole bias range. The hole mobilities in the doped and non-doped spiro-OMeTAD films are further evaluated by the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method. $ ^{[57,58]} $  The hole mobility was extracted by fitting the  $ J $ -V curves in the near quadratic region in Figure 2b. The zero-field hole mobility of pristine spiro-OMeTAD film is calculated to be  $ 6.5 \times 10^{-4} \text{ cm}^2 \text{ V}^{-1} \text{ s}^{-1} $ , which is close to the reported value by a time-of-flight (TOF) technique. $ ^{[59]} $  After doping, the mobility is increased to be  $ 2.6 \times 10^{-3} \text{ cm}^2 \text{ V}^{-1} \text{ s}^{-1} $  for spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (33 mol%) and  $ 1.8 \times 10^{-3} \text{ cm}^2 \text{ V}^{-1} \text{ s}^{-1} $  for spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (32 mol%), respectively (Table S1, Supporting Information). To investigate the effect of CuSCN (or CuI) doped spiro-OMeTAD on the cell stability, degradation tests of the perovskite solar cells without encapsulation were carried out under identical storage conditions (in atmosphere, at room temperature, and the humidity in the range of 25%–30%). The stabilities of PCE,   $  V_{OC}  $  ,   $  J_{SC}  $  , and FF values as a function of time are shown in Figure 4. Obviously, spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (33 mol%) based device presents the best stability among three devices. The PCE remains about 60% of the initial values even after 180 h degradation in the atmosphere. The stability of the spiro-OMeTAD:CuI (32 mol%) based device is slightly worse than the spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (33 mol%) based one due to the worse chemical stability of CuI than CuSCN.  $  ^{[45,47]}  $   In contrast, the reference device only keeps 35% of the initial PCE. It can be seen that the rapid PCE degradation is associated with the significant reductions of   $  J_{SC}  $   and FF. We ascribe it to the degraded perovskite layer due to the corrosion of moisture and oxygen through the unstable spiro-OMeTAD layer.

Many studies have been carried out to investigate the degradation process in perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{[61-64]}  $   The PCE falling is mainly ascribed to two factors. One is the perovskite

layer degradation due to the perovskite decomposition.  $  ^{[63]}  $   Another is the spiro-OMeTAD layer degradation due to the photo-oxidation effect.  $  ^{[64]}  $   Here, we kept the devices in atmosphere by protecting the samples completely with aluminum foils. Therefore, spiro-OMeTAD degradation originated from the photo-oxidation effect could be ignored. The stabilities of the copper salts doped spiro-OMeTAD films were further evaluated by film crystallization and aggregation analysis. Figure 6a–c presents the polarized optical microscopic images of aged (12 h, in drying oven) pristine spiro-OMeTAD, spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (33 mol%), and spiro-OMeTAD:CuI (32 mol%) films deposited on quartz substrates. Severe aggregation and crystallization occurred in the pristine spiro-OMeTAD sample. The moisture can easily penetrate into the perovskite layer through the pinholes and voids in spiro-OMeTAD layer due to the aggregation and crystallization. Noticeably, aggregation and crystallization were suppressed effectively in the spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (or CuI) samples. Actually, the introduction of inorganic materials can solve the issues of instability and aggregation of organic small molecule semiconductor films. Suppressed aggregation and crystallization reduced the pinholes and voids in the spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (or CuI) films, which prevents moisture infiltration to the perovskite layer to some extent. It could be reflected from the cross-sectional SEM images of the aged (180 h) devices as shown in Figure 6d–f. In the pristine spiro-OMeTAD based device, the interface between the perovskite layer and the HTM was badly deteriorated accompanying many large voids. In contrast, due to the suppressed aggregation and improved stability of spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (or CuI) films, the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   layer and related interface still maintained good morphologies.

# 3. Conclusions

We have demonstrated a solution-processed composite film, copper salts (CuSCN or CuI) doped spiro-OMeTAD, as a stable hole transport layer in perovskite solar cells. The p-type doping results in the improved film conductivity and hole mobility in spiro-OMeTAD:CuSCN (or CuI) composite films, which is beneficial for hole transport and extraction. Accordingly, the PCE is largely improved from  $ 14.82\% $  to  $ 18.02\% $  owing to the obvious enhancements in the cell parameters of  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  and FF. In addition, the introduction of inorganic copper salts can suppress the film aggregation and crystallization of spiro-OMeTAD films with reduced pinholes and voids, which slows down the perovskite decomposition due to the avoidance of moisture infiltration to some extent. This simple method is commensurate with simultaneous improvements in efficiency and stability for planar perovskite solar cells.

Solution Preparation: The   $  TiO_{x}  $   precursor solution was prepared by the reported method.  $  ^{[65]}  $   The precursor of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   was synthesized and purified by following previously reported process.  $  ^{[66]}  $   The reference spiro-OMeTAD solution was prepared by mixing 17.5   $  \mu  $  L TFSI-Li solution (520 mg Li-TFSI in 1 mL acetonitrile) and 28.5   $  \mu  $  L 4-tert-butylpyridine with 80 mg spiro-OMeTAD in 1 mL chlorobenzene solution. Also, the copper salts doped spiro-OMeTAD solutions were prepared by mixing

CuSCN or CuI (in propyl sulfide) with the reference spiro-OMeTAD solution with varied ratio from 0 to 42 mol%.

Device Fabrication: Patterned ITO or FTO substrates were thoroughly cleaned with acetone, ethanol, and deionized water for sequence in ultrasonic bath for 15 min, respectively.   $  TiO_{x}  $   precursor was spin-coated on the substrates at 3000 rmp/40 s and then annealed at 150 °C (ITO) or 500 °C (FTO) for 45 min, then a second   $  TiO_{x}  $   layer was spin-coated and annealed at 150 °C (ITO) or 500 °C (FTO) for 2 h. Precursor solution of perovskite was prepared by mixing   $  PbCl_{2}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   powder with a molar ration of 3:1 in DMF solvent (30 wt%). The mixture was kept stirred at 60 °C for 8 h and filtered through 0.22 μm filter before usage. And then precursor solution of perovskite was spin-coated onto the   $  TiO_{x}  $   layer at 4000 rpm/40 s. The precursor film was annealed at 100 °C for 2 h in nitrogen glove box. After annealing, HTLs (spiro-OMeTAD, CuSCN-doped spiro-OMeTAD, and CuI-doped spiro-OMeTAD) in chlorobenzene solution was coated onto the perovskite layer at 3000 rpm/40 s. Finally, the device was transferred to vacuum chamber under vacuum at   $  2 \times 10^{-6}  $   Torr for Ag electrode evaporation. The active area of each device is 7.25 mm².

The IPCE measurement was performed using a system combining a xenon lamp, a monochromator, a chopper, and a lock-in amplifier together with a calibrated silicon photodetector. The absorbance of the HTLs films was measured with an UV-vis spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer Lambda 750). AFM images were obtained using a Veeco Multimode V instrument to evaluate the surface morphology of films in the tapping mode. The field-emission SEM images were obtained from a Quanta 200 FEG. Steady-state PL measurements were acquired using Edinburgh Instruments FLS920 fluorescence spectrometer with a  $ 532~\mathrm{nm} $  pulsed laser as excitation source at the room temperature. The EIS was obtained using an IM6e Electrochemical Workstation (ZAHNER, Germany) with alternating current amplitude of  $ 10\mathrm{mV} $ .
