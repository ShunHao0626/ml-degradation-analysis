# Abstract

Compared to the 3D lead-halide perovskites (MAPbX $ _3 $ , X = I/Cl), two dimensional (2D) perovskites have more flexible structures, with more relaxed limitations in the size of the organic cations and produce interesting variant of photophysical and electronic properties. Here, a universal deposition approach for stable and efficient MAPbX $ _3 $  perovskite solar cells (PeSCs) displaying excellent reproducibility are presented via in-situ formed layered perovskites derived from polymeric ammonium anchor. The critical effect of in-situ formed layered perovskite (PEI) $ _2 $ [PbI $ _4 $ ] on the morphological and interfacial control of the above 3D perovskite are identified, and it is demonstrated that 2D perovskite films promote the formation of above perovskite films composed of micron-sized grains and provide better energy level alignment at the interface. Moreover, the incorporation of great moisture-resistant 2D materials, accompanying uniform and dense MAPbX $ _3 $  films enhances long-term stability of the perovskite solar cells. As a result, a maximum PCE value of 13.8% for MAPbI $ _3 $  solar cells on flexible substrate are achieved in ambient air. It is anticipated that our strategies to design and explore low dimensional perovskites can provide alternative route to address the critical concerns of stability and reproducibility in PeSCs.

# Introduction

Hybrid solar cells employing organic/inorganic perovskite materials, mostly methylammonium (MA) lead halide (e.g.,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ ) with direct bandgaps, high absorption coefficients, long exciton diffusion lengths and excellent charge transport properties as light-harvesters and carrier conductors have been recently recognized as one of the promising next generation photovoltaic technologies.[1, 2] Since the introduction in 2009,[3] perovskite solar cells (PeSCs) using these materials have evolved rapidly to yield power conversion efficiency (PCE) more than  $ 18\% $ .[4, 5] Previous studies of high-efficiency perovskite solar cells have often made use of (mesoscopic) metal oxides to form a mesostructured structure [6-8] To overcome the disadvantage of high temperature process and relatively high cost in mesostructure, planar heterojunction (PHJ) PeSCs were recently demonstrated with comparable performances by exclusion of the mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer[9, 10] and spiro-OMeTAD[11, 12]. However, there are still two critical drawbacks in the field of perovskite photovoltaics: difficulty in controlling the involved crystallization process[13] and the instability of the material to atmospheric moisture.[14]

Although attempts have been made to fabricate perovskite films by optimizing growth kinetics on precursor composition  $  ^{[15, 16]}  $  , solvent choice  $  ^{[17, 18]}  $  , annealing treatment  $  ^{[19, 20]}  $   deposition methods  $  ^{[21, 22]}  $   and interfacial surface  $  ^{[23-25]}  $  , control over crystal growth processes remain challenges for the realization of high reproducibility. However, the impact of the precursors composition on perovskite crystal growth and film formation, and thus on the device performance, is still under scrutiny. In addition to perovskite films fabrication, the delicate interfacial modification is strictly required to achieve better energy level alignment at the interface for charge transport and collection,  $  ^{[26]}  $   and enhance the moisture stability of PeSCs.  $  ^{[27]}  $

Moreover, the moisture sensitivity of the lead perovskite has not yet been addressed because one weakness of the methylammonium lead halide perovskites is their inherent vulnerability to moisture and heat.  $  ^{[28]}  $   Although some work have demonstrated the low level of humidity can facilitate crystallization,  $  ^{[29]}  $   nearly all groups carry out perovskite deposition in a dry atmosphere to avoid exposure to humidity. As we know, the layered

perovskites   $  (n = 1)  $   can be structurally derived from the 3D analogue   $  (n = \infty)  $  , through the choice of organic cations.  $  ^{[30, 31]}  $   Some work proved that two-dimensional (2D) perovskites, by partially substituting the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}  $   with large organic cations presented more resistant to humidity than the methylammonium lead halide (3D).  $  ^{[32]}  $   However, the incorporation of bulky ammonium into lower dimensional perovskites leads to increasing exciton binding energies and decreasing conductivity in certain crystallographic directions, which would significantly sacrifice the photovoltaic properties.  $  ^{[33, 34]}  $   With these considerations in mind, we turned our attention towards their potential as interfacial materials to address the issues of stability, reproducibility, and intralayer charge transport.  $  ^{[35]}  $

Motivated by our observations that novel two-dimensional (2D) perovskites materials can be readily obtained under ambient conditions without annealing by two-step sequential deposition of polymeric ammonium and lead halide solutions. We sought to assess if the layered materials could modulate the film formation of 3D analogue and enhance its moisture stability. Herein, we inserted the branched polyethylenimine hydriodide (PEI·HI) at the interface between the hole transporting layer and the methylammonium lead halide layer to form the 2D perovskites in-situ during deposition of above perovskite precursors. We elucidated the impact of layered perovskites on the morphological evolution of above 3D perovskite films, by establishing relationships between film morphology and differences in precursor of lead composition. Moreover, the 2D materials at interface could have a protective effect on the above perovskite by shielding it from atmospheric moisture and improve hole injection through energy level matching. As a result, we provided an universal approach for stable and reproducible perovskite solar cells of both  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  systems. We anticipated that our strategies to design and explore low dimensional perovskites can provide alternative route to address the critical concerns of stability and reproducibility in PeSCs.

Device Fabrication. ITO (15 ohm/sq) on glass and ITO on PET (30 ohm/sq) were cleaned sequentially with detergent and deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol under sonication for 10 min. Thin MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  perovskite layer was formed by spin-casting 45 wt % PbCl $ _2 $ :MAI (1:3) and PbCl $ _2 $ :PbI $ _2 $ :MAI (1:1:4) mixture in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) at 2k rpm for 45 s (400 nm thickness), and then annealing at 90°C for 1 h. For MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite films, the precursor 45 wt % PbI $ _2 $ :MAI (1:1) was used to produce perovskite solar cells with the same condition as described above. For mixture of GBL and DMSO (7:3, v/v) solvent system, the solution was coated onto the PEDOT:PSS with or without PEI·HI modification by a consecutive two-step spin-coating process with toluene drop-casting at the final stage. Afterward, the PC $ _{61} $ BM (20 mg/mL in chlorobenzene) was then sequentially deposited by spin coating at 1.2 k rpm for 60 s. LiF interlayer and Ag electrode were thermally evaporated on top of the film under high vacuum (<2 ×10 $ ^{-6} $  Torr) through a shadow mask with the thickness of 1 nm and 200 nm, respectively. The device area was defined as 10 mm $ ^2 $ , while the active cell area for the flexible cells are 9.6 mm $ ^2 $ .

Measurement and Characterization. A Keithley 2400 source meter unit was used to record  $ J - V $  characteristics in this study. # Results and discussion

We chose widely-used commercially available polymer, branched polyethylenimine (PEI), for its non-volatile nature and high content of amine groups. Polymeric ammonium PEI·HI was synthesized by reacting PEI with hydroiodic acid (Scheme 1).  $  ^{[36]}  $   We used methanol instead of ethanol in the reaction since the intermediate product has much lower solubility in ethanol and would precipitate in the solution.  $  ^{[3]}  $   The details can be found in Experiment Section and purity of the synthesized PEI·HI was confirmed by   $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR spectroscopy (Fig. S1). The yield of final product was above 90%.

When we blended the large polymeric ammonium (calculated using ammonium content) with   $  PbI_{2}  $   at the mole ratio of 2:1, high-quality films (Fig. S2) can be achieved from the precursor solution through one-step spin coating without annealing. In the XRD pattern of Fig. 1A, we found the intercalation of PEI·HI into   $  PbI_{2}  $   structure with a new peak at   $  8.07^{\circ}  $   (002) and assigned it to the layered perovskites comprised sheet of   $  (\mathrm{PEI})_{2}[\mathrm{PbI}_{4}]  $   (Fig. 1B and Fig. S3).  $  ^{[32]}  $   The orientation of 2D materials on flat substrates strongly favors growth where the layers orient parallel to the substrate, preferential growth along the (110) direction, thus exclusively revealing the (00l) reflections.  $  ^{[37]}  $   The layered perovskite structure and stoichiometry were further confirmed by the Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) spectrum (Fig. 1C) and elemental analysis (Table S1) of the solid complex, respectively. Nevertheless, the 2D perovskites showed new exciton absorption bands with band gap of 2.3 eV (Fig. 1D), compared with 1.5 eV of its analogue   $  (\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3})  $  ,  $  ^{[34]}  $   which ruled out the consideration of light-absorbing materials. More details of the in-depth study of crystal structures, physical and electronic properties of the 2D perovskite family will be reported separately.

Interestingly, when the solution of   $  PbI_{2}  $   was spun on PEI·HI surface, the   $  (PEI)_{2}[PbI_{4}]  $   crystal was in-situ generated with same structure, rather than washing it away. Therefore, we alternatively prepared the layered perovskites using in-situ approach by inserting PEI·HI anchoring layer at the interface and then coating the precursor solution of   $  PbI_{2}:PbCl_{2}:MAI  $   and   $  PbI_{2}:MAI  $  .  $  ^{[38]}  $   We expected that the in-situ generation of layered perovskites from the rapid reaction between polymeric ammonium with   $  PbI_{2}  $   would generally modulate the film formation of above   $  MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   or   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films. And the low intensity of PEI·HI:  $  PbCl_{2}  $   blends revealed the chance of formation of the   $  (PEI)_{2}[PbCl_{2}I_{2}]  $   perovskite phase was slim in our case. It is consistent with the reports that the layered structure   $  PbI_{2}  $   is more reactive with alkylammonium iodide than   $  PbCl_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[39]}  $   It turns out that the relatively slow process of reaction process between   $  PbCl_{2}  $   and PEI·HI results in inhomogeneous films, compared with   $  (PEI)_{2}[PbI_{4}]  $  . For comparison, we also fabricated the   $  MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   films using the common   $  PbCl_{2}:MAI  $   precursor composition.

We first evaluated the impact of layered perovskites on crystal growth changes of the methylammonium lead halide films using a series of X-ray diffraction (XRD) analyses. Stronger reflection intensities, where peaks of 14.11 and 28.41 are assigned to (110) and (220) crystal phase of the 3D perovskites, were clearly obtained in the case of PEI·HI modification over the control and the PEI-modified one (Fig. S5). Moreover, for the samples deposited on the PEI·HI, a new peak was identified at low angles (8.07°) in the XRD pattern of Fig. 2B and 2C, which was consistent with the (PEI) $ _{2} $ [PbI $ _{4} $ ] crystal planes. In contrast, the films deposited with PbCl $ _{2} $ :MAI precursor showed no apparent dependence on the surface modification, since layered perovskite (PEI) $ _{2} $ [PbCl $ _{2} $ I $ _{2} $ ] can hardly be in-situ formed in such case (Fig. 2A). This indicates that the generation of (PEI) $ _{2} $ [PbI $ _{4} $ ] layered perovskites, rather than PEI·HI itself, induce the crystallinity change of above MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _{x} $  or MAPbI $ _{3} $  perovskite films, similar with the intermediate phase of Pb halide–DMF (DMSO) complex. $ ^{[17]} $  Different from Pb halide–DMF (DMSO) complex, the formation of the 3D perovskite is accompanied by the presence of (PEI) $ _{2} $ [PbI $ _{4} $ ] layered perovskites as the “seeding layer” in the whole process, without transformation upon sufficient heating.

To further unveil the effects of   $  (\mathrm{PEI})_{2}[\mathrm{PbI}_{4}]  $   on the above perovskite films, we investigated the optical properties of   $  MAPbX_{3}  $   layer fabricated from various precursors compositions on quartz/PEI·HI films. Fig. 2D, 2E and 2F illustrated the UV-Vis spectra and the corrected photoluminescence (PL) (Fig. S6A, S6B and S6C) responses of all the perovskite films on the glass and quartz substrate with and without surface modification, respectively. All these films showed roughly the same absorption edge at about 780 nm and PL response peak at 770–780 nm.  $  ^{[40]}  $   Both the   $  MAPbX_{3}  $   films deposited from   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  PbI_{2}:PbCl_{2}  $   precursors processed on PEI·HI surface substrate exhibited clearly increased light absorption (color images in Fig. S7) and red-shift of the band-edge photoluminescence, while the films based on   $  PbCl_{2}  $   presented a slightly change on optical properties with or without PEI·HI modification.

Crystallinity feature and optical characterization of the perovskite films supported the hypothesis that the readily in-situ formation of   $  (\mathrm{PEI})_{2}[\mathrm{PbI}_{4}]  $   would influence the overall crystalline quality of the above perovskite films.  $  ^{[41]}  $   By contrast, for the pure   $  PbCl_{2}  $   lead precursors, the generation of   $  (\mathrm{PEI})_{2}[\mathrm{PbCl}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{2}]  $   during the spin-coating of perovskite films was suppressed. Then, we performed scanning electron microscopy (SEM) measurements to evaluate the morphology change, as shown in Fig. 3. In the case using   $  PbCl_{2}:MAI  $   precursor, the introduction of PEI·HI work as the surface modifier with favorable surface coverage to grow pore-free   $  MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   films. In contrast, when   $  PbI_{2}  $   was added in the precursors, resulting   $  (\mathrm{PEI})_{2}[\mathrm{PbI}_{4}]  $   layered perovskites enabled the   $  MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite grow into a different surface morphology with uniform and better crystallite structure, despite slightly reduced coverage the perovskite film. In the higher-magnification SEM image of perovskite film deposited on the PEI·HI surface, we observed that the crystalline grain sizes of the perovskite thin films increased with the content ratio of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   lead precursors. The grain size increased up to several micron for pure   $  PbI_{2}  $   in comparison with the grain sizes of 100 nm obtained using the pure   $  PbCl_{2}  $ . We propose that the formation of 2D perovskite drives the above 3D perovskite crystallization dynamics and induces a preferential growth during crystallization from molecular into larger crystals.

When pristine PEDOT:PSS (WF = 4.9 eV) was used, the energy offset at the hole extraction interface was huge (ca. 0.4 eV) in the MAPbI $ _3 $  (MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $ ) perovskite device. After the evaluation of optical and morphology changes caused by   $  (\mathrm{PEI})_{2}[\mathrm{PbI}_{4}]  $   layer, perovskite solar cells were constructed with a device architecture as commonly used planar configuration: indium tin oxide (ITO)/PEDOT:PSS/MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $  (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  )/6,6-phenyl-C  $  _{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM)/LiF/Ag.  $  ^{[45]}  $   Particularly, all the device fabrication procedure and measurement were processed in ambient air at humidity levels at humidity levels   $  50 \pm 2\%  $   without using of glove-box. The J-V characteristics of the devices with different precursors measured under illumination are shown in Fig. 5A, 5B and 5C, while the average performance are presented in Fig. S9. The photovoltaic performance of all the devices were summarized in Table 1. For the devices based on pure PbCl  $  _{2}  $ , PEI·HI would not form 2D materials and work only as a modifier of relative hydrophobicity on the surface. In contrast, solar cells produced from precursors based on PbI  $  _{2}  $ :PbCl  $  _{2}  $   and PbI  $  _{2}  $   systems, PEI·HI modification enables a perovskite solar cell with an average PCE of relative increase by over 40% and 100% as compared to its reference device, respectively. In both devices with polymeric ammonium insertion, the in-situ generation of   $  (\mathrm{PEI})_{2}[\mathrm{PbI}_{4}]  $   aided the growth of above perovskite films as a seeding layer, rather than just a surface modifier. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra (Fig. 5E and 5F) confirmed that the perovskite based on PbI  $  _{2}  $   and PbI  $  _{2}  $ :PbCl  $  _{2}  $   systems processed on the PEI·HI surface exhibited clearly increased light harvesting across a broad spectral region from 400-800 nm, consistent with the rising absorption spectra.

Although the presence of   $  (\mathrm{PEI})_{2}[\mathrm{PbI}_{4}]  $   layer improved the device performance of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   with a much better PCE of 13.09% than the poor PCE of 4.96% at normal condition, there are still some room for the photovoltaic performance improvement. Particularly, the approach involves the spin-coating of pure DMF solution delivered a bulk dendrite-like structure for the reference perovskite films. Although the formation of   $  (\mathrm{PEI})_{2}[\mathrm{PbI}_{4}]  $   can significantly improve the uniformity of the films, we still can observe incomplete coverage of the perovskite films in the top-view SEM images with small pores inside the perovskite film due to the fast evaporation of the DMF solvent during the spinning process, which is indicative of the difficulty of obtaining perovskite films with dense-grained uniform morphology using a DMF-only system. Furthermore, we used solvent-engineering technology employing a mixture solution of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO):γ-butyrolactone (GBL) (3:7, v/v) to control the morphology.  $  ^{[17]}  $   As expected, when the perovskite was deposited using a mixture solvent, we found pore-free and uniform perovskite films in both the conditions with or without PEI·HI modification (Fig. S10). However, when we took a closer examination of these films by top-surface AFM measurement, the PEI·HI enabled a strikingly different morphology with much more smooth surface with dense   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   grain, as shown in Fig. 6A and 6B, having a root-mean-square roughness decreasing from 12.14 to 6.81 nm. As can be seen from the optical spectra (Fig. 6C), the perovskite deposited from mixed solvent on the PEI·HI surface also exhibited clearly increased absorbance ability across the visible range into the near-infrared wavelengths, consistent with the increase in the EQE curves (Fig. S11). In the case of the DMSO+GBL co-solvent, the device treated with PEI·HI exhibited the highest PCE (Fig. 6D) of 16.06%, with a higher   $  J_{SC}  $  ,   $  V_{OC}  $   and FF. And we expect that higher performance can be achieved using harsh fabrication conditions. This is also indicative of solvent-independent and universal morphology improvement via our layered perovskite interfaces.

The reproducibility of the device performances based on pure   $  PbI_{2}  $   or   $  PbCl_{2}  $   lead precursor was tested separately by fabricating a batch of 50 devices under optimized conditions. In the case of   $  PbI_{2}  $  -containing precursor, the better reproducibility unambiguously verified the uniform morphology from batch-to-batch after the insertion of the PEI·HI layer with relatively small variability (Fig. 7A and 7B). For the devices fabricated with   $  PbCl_{2}  $ :MAI precursor, the effect of PEI·HI on the reproducibility improvement was also observed, since the PEI·HI improved surface coverage with less pinholes in the perovskite films. However, the planar   $  MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   devices based on pure   $  PbCl_{2}  $   without and with PEI·HI treatment exhibited hysteresis in the J-V curves, as shown in Fig. S15 and S16. When the   $  PbI_{2}  $   was added in the precursor, we found the PEI·HI devices with hysteresis-free performance. One cause of photocurrent hysteresis can be the presence of charge accumulation or dielectric polarization in disordered porous perovskite layers and interfacial areas. And the generation of   $  (\mathrm{PEI})_{2}[\mathrm{PbI}_{4}]  $   promoted the formation of high-crystalline-quality perovskite films and ensured that minimal charge carriers were trapped at defect-related trap sites, consistent with the PL results.

On the basis of the above observations, we propose a plausible mechanism (Fig. 8A) to interpret how the polymeric ammonium influences the crystallization process and what role it plays in determining the properties of device operation. The polymeric ammonium can in-situ generate layered perovskites during the processing of above precursor solution. And  $ (\mathrm{PEI})_2[\mathrm{PbI}_4] $  would serve as a multifunctional interface and enhance the photovoltaic properties of PeSCs with three key advantages: 1) Control over the morphology and grain growth of perovskite films; 2) Facilitated hole injection from perovskite into HTL by energy level alignment; 3) Greater moisture resistance of  $ (\mathrm{PEI})_2[\mathrm{PbI}_4] $  at interface. We also fabricated flexible perovskite solar cells on poly(ethylene terephthalate) (PET) substrates (Fig. 8B), due to no high temperature annealing step was required in our structure. With better crystal growth, the perovskite film growth on  $ (\mathrm{PEI})_2[\mathrm{PbI}_4] $  should be less dependent on substrates. And the devices fabricated on the ITO/PET substrate showed the maximum PCE of  $ 13.83\% $  with excellent reproducibility. These results are among the best reported for the state-of-the-art flexible perovskite devices.[47] We also

# Conclusions

In summary, we described a general approach for deposition of uniform perovskite layers and elucidated the effect of layered perovskites on the crystallization of above 3D perovskite films by establishing relationships between film morphology and differences in precursor of lead composition. A combination of XRD patterns and morphology evolution proved that in-situ generation of  $ (\mathrm{PEI})_2[\mathrm{PbI}_4] $ , readily achieved from the deposition of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -containing precursor on the PEI-HI layer, enabled the control over the morphology and grain growth of perovskite films with larger grain size up to microns, and reduce the contact barrier at interface, thus improving hole extraction. Moreover, additional benefits from the greater moisture resistance of  $ (\mathrm{PEI})_2[\mathrm{PbI}_4] $  layer at interface and consequent above perovskite films with densely packed grains result in extended cell operation time. As a result, we achieved a rapid and reproducible fabrication of hysteresis-free and stable perovskite solar cell for both  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  for different solvent systems with PCE over  $ 16\% $ , and the application of this general approach on flexible substrate presented a record PCE of  $ 13.8\% $ . This result is even more encouraging considering that the device retains its performance after long exposure in a humidity environment. Our design of novel two-dimensional perovskite derived from polymeric ammonium affords a facile way with great tunability in perovskite families that are yet to be explored for highly stable and efficient perovskite solar cell.
