# Abstract

Investigations carried out towards the degradation in perovskite solar cells (PSCs), stored in different open air environmental conditions, are reported here. The solar cells were stored in open air dark inside the laboratory (relative humidity   $  47\pm5\%  $   and temperature   $  23\pm4^{\circ}C  $  ), under compact fluorescent lamp (CFL) illumination (irradiance of   $  10\ mW/cm^{2}  $  , relative humidity   $  47\pm5\%  $   and temperature   $  23\pm4^{\circ}C  $  ) and under natural sunlight outside the laboratory. In the outdoor storage the surrounding conditions varied time to time and the environmental conditions during day time (irradiance   $  100\ mW/cm^{2}  $  , relative humidity   $  \sim18\%  $   and temperature   $  \sim45^{\circ}C  $   in the noon) were entirely different from those during night (irradiance   $  0\ mW/cm^{2}  $  , relative humidity   $  \sim66\%  $   and temperature   $  \sim16^{\circ}C  $   in the midnight). The photovoltaic parameters were measured time to time inside the laboratory as per the International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocols. All the photovoltaic parameters like short circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ), open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ), fill factor (FF) and power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the solar cells stored outdoor, decayed more rapidly than those stored under CFL or in dark. # 1. Introduction

The organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted special attention of worldwide scientific community especially those working in the field of solar cells. Energy conversion performance of PSCs has drastically improved from  $ \sim 3.8\% $  to more than  $ 22\% $  just in last few years [1-4]. Not only the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs is approaching to c-Si solar cells but they are also considered to be the most cost effective among the solar cell technology known so far. PSCs are thin, light weight and mechanically flexible and promise to have high throughput via roll to roll fabrication. The light absorbers in these devices are the semiconductors with perovskite structure having a molecular formula  $ \mathrm{ABX}_3 $ , where A is an organic cation, B is an inorganic cation and X is a halide anion like I-, Cl-, Br- or a mixture of these [5-9]. Methyl ammonium lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3) $  is one of the examples of perovskite semiconductors that have shown high PCE in solar cells [10-13]. High power conversion performance of the perovskites comes from their excellent charge transport properties, wide absorption spectrum, high charge carrier mobilities and large charge carrier diffusion lengths [14-18], which generate high current and voltage [16]. Growth of perovskite materials and their crystalline properties have shown tremendous effect on the cell performance [19]. Significant efforts have been made towards ease in processing of perovskite films for improved efficiency in solar cells. Researchers have used various methods and processes to grow high quality perovskite thin films and one step [19], two step sequential deposition [12, 20], hot casting [19], vapor assisted growth [10], and mixed solvent approaches [21] are some of the examples. Two step sequential deposition method is considered to be relatively easier and better to achieve high quality perovskite thin films compared to the single step method. The electron transport layer (ETL) could be compact and smooth giving a planer heterojunction solar cell or mesoporous with a compact layer underneath giving a meso-porous structured solar cell and both the structures have shown high PCE [21, 22].

PSCs have enormous applications from indoor to outdoor energy generation but unfortunately these devices exhibit rapid degradation in their performance, which is keeping them away from being commercialized. Though the PCE has shown tremendous improvement but the stability still remains a serious concern. There have been several reports and reviews on degradation phenomenon and stability in PSCs [23-26]. It is observed that the device stability is affected by a number of parameters like humidity, light intensity, temperature, electrical stress etc. and in actual weather conditions all these parameters would work differently with arbitrary variation in them with time. In the presence of moisture  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  tends to hydrolyze and breaks into  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , where  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  further breaks into  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_2 $  and HI [27]. Frost et al. proposed that  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  breaks into  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_2 $ ,  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and HI via formation of intermediate  $ [(CH_3NH_3^+)_{n-1}(CH_3NH_2)PbI_3][H_3O^+] $  molecule [28]. Application of alternate materials like two dimensional  $ (C_6H_5(CH_2)_2NH_3)(CH_3NH_3)_2Pb_3I_{10} $  perovskite semiconductor in place of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  has shown better stability against moisture [29]. The degradation mechanisms in PSCs are not completely understood yet and it requires more detailed investigations to overcome the issue. Long term stability in PSCs requires more work in both the areas of molecular engineering and device engineering.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Materials

All the materials and solvents except ethanol, used in device fabrication like fluorine doped tine oxide (FTO) coated glass substrates, titanium isopropoxide, methylamine (33 wt% in absolute ethanol), hydroiodic acid (57 wt% in water), lead chloride, poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT), dimethyl formamide (DMF), chloroform, Ag shots were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, USA. Ethanol was purchased from MERCK Chemicals, Germany. All the materials were used as received without any further purification. The mixed halide perovskite   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   was prepared by chemical reaction of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   and   $  PbCl_{2}  $  .   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   was synthesized according to the previously reported procedure [22]. In short 24 ml of methylamine was allowed to react with 10 ml of hydroiodic acid in 250 ml round bottom flask at   $  0^{\circ}C  $   for 2 h under continuous stirring. A yellowish white powder of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   was obtained upon evaporation of solvent using a rotary evaporator. The powder was re-dissolved in absolute ethanol and re-precipitated by addition into diethyl ether. The white precipitate of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   was dried in vacuum oven overnight at   $  60^{\circ}C  $   and used for preparation of perovskite precursor.

# 2.3. Device storage and measurement protocols

We would like to mention here that all the thin film depositions were carried out inside the laboratory in ambient air except the top metal electrode which was deposited by thermal evaporation in vacuum chamber. For degradation studies the sets of non-encapsulated and encapsulated solar cells were stored in different open air conditions like in dark, under CFL illumination inside the laboratory and under direct sunlight outside the laboratory and tested as per the ISOS-D-1, ISOS-L-1 and ISOS-O-1 protocols respectively [40]. In brief as per the ISOS-O-1 protocol the devices are kept outside the laboratory under natural sunlight and tested periodically indoors under a solar simulator. According to ISOS-L-1 protocol the devices are kept under a light source inside the laboratory and tested periodically indoors under a solar simulator. The ISOS-D-1 protocol corresponds to shelf life testing, where devices are stored in dark in ambient conditions and tested periodically under natural sunlight or solar simulator. The solar cells were tested periodically indoors for their photovoltaic performance and after each testing the cells were placed back to their original storing conditions. For present studies, in dark storage there was no light falling on the devices and relative humidity and temperature of the surrounding air were  $ 47 \pm 5\% $  and  $ 23 \pm 4^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  respectively. Under CFL illumination the irradiance of the incident light was  $ 10\mathrm{mW/cm}^2 $  whereas humidity and temperature of the surrounding air were  $ 47 \pm 5\% $  and  $ 23 \pm 4^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  respectively. The humidity and temperatures were within the above mentioned range throughout the storage time in dark and under CFL illumination. There was no difference in the dark and under CFL storage except the continuous incidence of  $ 10\mathrm{mW/cm}^2 $  of illumination under CFL, but the storage conditions under direct sunlight outside the laboratory were entirely different. Series resistance is controlled by bulk transport properties, interfacial energy barriers and interface states, whereas the shunt resistance is controlled by the morphology of the active layer, which decides the leakage current. # 4. Conclusion

Encapsulated and non-encapsulated PSCs were stored in different environmental conditions like in dark inside the laboratory, under CFL illumination inside the laboratory, and under direct sunlight outside the laboratory and tested for the degradation mechanisms as per the ISOS-D-1, ISOS-L-1 and ISOS-O-1 protocols respectively. The inside laboratory conditions like humidity, temperature and illumination intensity, remained within a range with least variation but outdoor the laboratory the humidity, temperature and illumination intensity varied to a great extent. Though the encapsulation provided some protection from surrounding environment that resulted in better stability but irrespective of the encapsulation, the solar cells stored in dark exhibited maximum stability whereas those stored in direct sunlight in actual outdoor conditions exhibited lowest stability.
