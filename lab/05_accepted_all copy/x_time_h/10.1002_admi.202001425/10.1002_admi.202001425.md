# An Interlocking Fibrillar Polymer Layer for Mechanical Stability of Perovskite Solar Cells

Seonju Jeong, Inhwa Lee, Taek-Soo Kim,* and Jung-Yong Lee*

Atmospheric and mechanical stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) must be guaranteed for successful commercialization. A fibrillar polymer, poly[N-9'-heptadecanyl-2,7-carbazole-alt-5,5-(4',7'-di-2-thienyl-2',1',3'-benzothiadiazole)] (PCDTBT), is reported as an efficient hole transfer layer (HTL) which significantly improves air and mechanical stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). PCDTBT fibrils formed at the grain boundaries of perovskite layer induce the highest fracture energies in the PSCs, which provide extrinsic reinforcement and shielding for enhanced mechanical and chemical stability. Debonding energy increases by 30% for the PSCs with PCDTBT fibrils, which fractures at 2.66 J m  $  ^{-2}  $  , compared to the devices without PCDTBT fibrils at 2.09 J m  $  ^{-2}  $  ; more importantly, the threshold debonding driving force of the PCDTBT fibril-based devices is greatly improved by twofold under ambient conditions.

# 1. Introduction

As the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells skyrockets in recent years,  $  ^{[1]}  $  it is timely to pursue their commercialization, especially as an auxiliary power source for portable and wearable devices. For that, most of all, atmospheric stability and mechanical stability of the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) must be secured.

According to several recent reports, efficient PSCs have usually employed 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) and poly(triaryl amine) (PTAA) on perovskite layers as an organic hole-transport layer (HTL).  $  ^{[1c,2]}  $   Although these organic charge transporting materials can provide high PCE to PSCs, they seem to impair the

ohmic contact and accelerates decomposition of perovskite materials.  $  ^{[5]}  $   However, the adhesive and cohesive fracture energies (  $  G_{c}  $  ) of PSCs are known to be below 1.5 J m  $  ^{-2}  $  , which is lower than any other solar cells including copper indium gallium selenide (CIGS), Si, and organic solar cells.  $  ^{[6]}  $   In particular, those organic HTLs are the most fragile elements in PSCs, fracturing in the spiro-OMeTAD layer at 0.42 J m  $  ^{-2}  $   or in PTAA layer at 0.17 J m  $  ^{-2}  $  ; indeed, they are the primary origins of failure in the cells. Therefore, novel HTL materials or structures with excellent bonding strength to the perovskite layers while maintaining as high performance as devices with spiro-OMeTAD or PTAA could advance the commercialization of perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{[7]}  $

Here, we introduce a unique interface engineering technique to induce 3D micro- and nanofibril networks between the perovskite layer and a polymer HTL, which delays debonding at the interface under mechanical stress.  $  ^{[8]}  $   Also, aligned nanofibril networks of polymers can provide enough percolation   $  \pi-\pi  $   pathways in multiple directions, enhancing both charge mobility and electrical conductivity.  $  ^{[9]}  $

In this study, we report the incorporation of a polymer, poly[N-9'-heptadecanyl-2,7-carbazole-alt-5,5-(4',7'-di-2-thienyl-2',1',3'-benzothiadiazole)] (PCDTBT) as an efficient HTL in organic-inorganic metal halide PSCs. PCDTBT has not only a high hole-mobility of   $  \mu_{h}\approx0.02~cm^{2}~V^{-1}~s^{-1}  $  ,[10] compared to other p-type polymers including P3HT (  $  \mu_{h}\approx0.001~cm^{2}~V^{-1}~s^{-1}  $  )[11] and PTAA (  $  \mu_{h}\approx0.004~cm^{2}~V^{-1}~s^{-1}  $  ),[12] but also a remarkable air- and thermal stability due to the large ionization potential.[10] Furthermore, PCDTBT fibrillar aggregates formed at the grain boundaries of perovskite layer provide strong adhesion and electrical paths between perovskite and PDCTBT layers. # 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Formation of PCDTBT Fibrillar Structures

Figure 1a illustrates the device structure of a planar-typed PSC with a configuration of indium tin oxide (ITO)/SnO $ _2 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /PCDTBT/Ag and the chemical structure of PCDTBT, where the PCDTBT layer contains fibrils partially. The perovskite CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  layer was formed by the Lewis acid-base adduct approach using dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). By forming polymer fibrillar structures on the surface of perovskite layers, the conjugation with maximum overlap between the  $ \pi $ -orbitals could be effectively induced, enhancing electrical properties across the layers as discussed below. $ ^{[13]} $  Interestingly, the fibrillar structures could also improve mechanical robustness between the perovskite and the polymer layers because of the corrugated interfaces. $ ^{[14]} $

# 2.4. Stability Tests

Humidity is a critical factor to determine the long-term stability and lifetime of perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{[4a,32]}  $   The stability of unencapsulated perovskite solar cells was investigated, as a function of storage time under ambient conditions (constant relative humidity,   $  \approx30\%  $  ; constant temperature,   $  20\ ^{\circ}C  $  ).

For stability tests of devices, the top electrode of the devices was changed to gold because of the reactivity of silver electrode with halides. As shown in Figure 4f, the PCE of the PCDTBT with fibrils device remained above 70% of the initial value even after 480 h of storage in air. In contrast, the PCE of the spiro-OMeTAD device decreased to 39% of its initial value. As proven in the PL decay time (Figure 4e), the fibrils on the perovskite and PCDTBT interface suppresses traps, and therefore, slows degradation of the device with fibrils, which may contribute to the enhancement of interfacial polarity compatibility.  $  ^{[33]}  $

# 3. Conclusion

PCDTBT film with fibrillar structures was applied as an HTL in PSCs and was compared to the devices made from commonly used doped spiro-OMeTAD materials. The PCDTBT film with fibrils based devices achieved comparable device performance (14.34%) to that (12.34%) for doped spiro-OMeTAD based cells. Besides, the devices containing PCDTBT film with fibrils as HTL showed improved stability against humid environment, resulting from interlocking effect of PCDTBT fibrils formed at the interface between perovskite layer and PDCTBT thin film. Our result suggests that the PCDTBT containing fibrillar structures can be used as an alternative to widely used doped spiro-OMeTAD HTL.
