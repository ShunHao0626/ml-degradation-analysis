Despite the rapid progress in inorganic cesium lead halide perovskite (CsPbX $ _3 $ ) materials originating from excellent thermal stability; their poor phase stability at room temperature and lower efficiency compared to organic-inorganic counterparts still limit their development toward commercialization. Recently, Pb-site doping of inorganic perovskites stand outs for the improvement of aforementioned issues for emerging photovoltaic applications. Herein, we introduce a compositional engineering approach to tune the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br crystallization by directly incorporating iron (II) chloride (FeCl $ _2 $ ) into perovskite precursor. The small amount of FeCl $ _2 $  stabilizes the black  $ \alpha $ -phase to avoid the undesirable formation of the non-perovskite phase owing to Fe $ ^{2+} $ -induced grain size reduction. Besides, the FeCl $ _2 $  incorporation thoroughly aligns the energy level, promote the built-in potential (V $ _{bi} $ ), and reduce the defect states in the perovskite, resulting in a record power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 17.1% with a remarkable open-circuit voltage (V $ _{OC} $ ) of 1.31 V. More importantly, FeCl $ _2 $ -doped CsPbI $ _2 $ Br-based devices exhibit an exceptional operational stability with a retention of over 95% initial PCE after 330 h at maximum power point (MPP) tracking.

# 1. Introduction

Arguably, instability of organic-inorganic perovskite materials is one of the major challenges toward commercial applications of perovskite photovoltaics due to the high volatility and hygroscopicity of organic components in the hybrid perovskite framework   $  [1-5]  $  . Such challenges are closely associated with the thermally unstable nature of methylammonium (MA) and formamidinium (FA) cations, leading to long-term stability issues of perovskite devices   $  [6-9]  $  . The replacement of these organic cations by inorganic ones in all-inorganic perovskite structure may alleviate the instability issue of corresponding devices. Hitherto, various types of all-inorganic perovskite materials have been explored for emerging perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Among them, cesium lead halide-based   $  (\mathrm{CsPbX}_{3}, \text{where X = I, Br, or Cl})  $   light-absorbing materials

have drawn great deal of attention due to their remarkable stability under thermal stress (stable up to 400 °C) and rapid progress in cell performance. Among the all-inorganic Cs-based perovskite materials,   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  -based solar cells have been proposed as the most stable, however their efficiencies are still far to meet practical utilization due to its wide optical bandgap (  $  \sim2.3  $   eV) associated poor light-absorbing ability [10]. On the other hand,   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   possesses relatively narrow bandgap (  $  \sim1.7  $   eV) but it suffers from non-perovskite phase (  $  \delta-CsPbI_{3}  $  ) transition under ambient conditions [11]. Recent efforts demonstrated that mixed-halide   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   composition is the most promising candidate, which exhibits a minimal trade-off between the optical bandgap and phase-stability [12,13].

Ultimately, the incorporation of Pb-site ionic additives into inorganic perovskites has emerged as an ascendant strategy to realize the passivation of defect states, enhancement in long-term stability, and tunability of optoelectronic properties [14-16]. For example, Qi et al. reported manganese  $ (\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}) $  -doped all inorganic perovskite film, showing better crystallinity and morphology, which alleviate the energy loss and facilitate the charge-transfer in perovskites [17]. With appropriate Mn dopant concentration, a PCE of  $ 7.36\% $  was achieved accompanied with good stability in ambient atmosphere, whereas control device showed a PCE of  $ 6.14\% $ . In a similar direction, ytterbium chloride  $ (\mathrm{YbCl}_3) $  employing compositional engineering approach for cesium-based inorganic perovskite film was reported to stabilize the black  $ \alpha $  -phase [18]. The partly substitution of  $ \mathrm{Yb}^{3+} $  with  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  enhanced the tolerance factor and stabilized the black phase. As a result, the champion PCE of  $ 12.4\% $  was obtained with an enhanced stability maintaining  $ 66\% $  of its initial PCE after  $ 400\mathrm{h} $  under ambient condition. In a pioneering work, our group demonstrated that the incorporation of europium  $ (\mathrm{Eu}^{2+}) $  into  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  inorganic perovskite lattice suppresses non-radiative charge carrier recombination by eliminating tail states and thus, a high PCE of  $ 13.71\% $  was achieved with a high open-circuit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{OC}}) $  of  $ 1.27\mathrm{V} $  [1]. Meanwhile,  $ \mathrm{Eu}^{2+} $  doping resulted in an exceptionally high thermodynamic stability, maintaining  $ 93\% $  of the initial efficiency under continuous illumination for  $ 370\mathrm{h} $ . Very recently, calcium chloride  $ (\mathrm{CaCl}_2) $  additive considerably reduced the defect density and increased the carrier lifetime by improving the crystal quality of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film [19]. As a result,  $ 0.5\% $   $ \mathrm{CaCl}_2 $  employing CsPbI\$_2\$Br-based devices showed a record PCE of  $ 16.79\% $  with an admissible stability retaining  $ 90\% $  of the initial efficiency after aging in air over  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $ .

Inspired by these works, we attempted to stabilize the  $ \alpha $ -CsPb $ _2 $ Br perovskite by partial substitution of the Pb-site with very small quantities of FeCl $ _2 $  (0.2%, 0.5%, 1.0%, and 1.5%) for the first time. Cl $ ^- $  ions intentionally employed in this study, which play a substantial role in suppressing the traps to decrease the non-radiative recombination. The role of Cl $ ^- $  ions has been recently revealed by the reduction in the trap-state densities and charge carrier recombination in perovskites which

ameliorate the charge transfer behavior and the performance of perovskite devices [20]. Whereas   $  Fe^{2+}  $   transition metal has attracted intensive attention owing to its rich chemistry and unfilled d-orbital [21]. Moreover, the incorporation of isovalent   $  Fe^{2+}  $   increases the tolerance factor owing to its smaller ionic size compared to   $  Pb^{2+}  $   [22]. The incorporation of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   played a role in minimizing the defect states, increasing the carrier lifetime, and promoting the built-in potential (  $  V_{b1}  $  ), resulting in a high-quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film with inconceivable stability. The fabricated perovskite cells accomplished the champion PCE of 17.1% with a   $  J_{SC}  $   of 15.90, a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.31 V and an FF of 81.80%, which is the highest efficiency in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   based PSCs reported so far and largely higher than that of control device (13.24%). Moreover, the devices based on 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   retained ~96% of their initial PCEs after 300 h aging under continuous illumination.

# 2. Results and discussion

The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite layer in the presence and absence of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   were synthesized via spin coating technique. The good crystallinity of the prepared films acquired through solvent-controlled growth (SCG) pathway. For SCG process, the spin-coated perovskite films were left in glovebox for 20 min before applying the annealing treatment. Further details related to the preparation of the perovskite films as well as the fabrication of devices are given in the supporting information section. The partial replacement of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   (119 pm) by smaller   $  Fe^{2+}  $   (78 pm) and   $  I^{-}  $   (216 pm) by smaller   $  Cl^{-}  $   (181 pm) increases the tolerance factor of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite from 0.85 to 0.96 (  $  CsPb_{1-x}Fe_{x}I_{2}Br  $  ), which facilitates the formation of stable   $  \alpha  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite by exclusively occupying corresponding substitutional sites within the perovskite lattice.

The digital photographs of the synthesized   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solutions with various   $  FeCl_{2}  $   concentration are given in Fig. S1. The obtained photographs indicated that adding of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   with concentrations up to 1.0% results in the existence of clear solution, revealing the high dissolution of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   precursor. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were collected to evaluate the effect of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   doping on the crystal structure of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite. The XRD patterns shown in Fig. 1a demonstrate peaks at   $  14.6^{\circ}  $   and   $  29.6^{\circ}  $   assigned to the (100) and (200) crystal planes of characteristic   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br phase, which is consistent with the previous reports [24,25]. As expected, the main diffraction showed a slight shift for 3%   $  FeCl_{2}  $ -doped perovskite film (Fig. S2), indicating the changed lattice constant due to the doped   $  Fe^{2+}  $   ions with a smaller size than   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions. However, a slight decrease in the peak intensity was obtained, whereas the full width at half maximum (FWHM) value of (100) diffraction peak broadens in case of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   doping, indicating the decrease of crystallite size [26].

The chemical interaction between  $ \mathrm{FeCl}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  perovskite was

revealed by the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements, as indicated in Fig. S3 (a–f). XPS wide range survey spectra of the pristine and 0.5% FeCl₂-doped CsPbI₂Br perovskite films prove the formation of perovskite structure in both films. Fig. S3 (b–f) presents the XPS spectra of Cs 3d, Pb 4 f, I 3d, Br 3d, and Fe 2p, respectively. The high resolution XPS spectra of Cs confirms the appearance of peaks at 724.1 and 738.1 eV binding energy corresponding to Cs 3d (3d₅/₂ and 3d₃/₂) for 0% and 0.5% FeCl₂-doped perovskite. The obtained data exhibits a minor peak shift in case of peaks 0.5% FeCl₂ doping compared to the pristine one, which can be ascribed to the weak interaction between Cs⁺ and the central atom in the octahedron [27]. Notably, the blue-shift (to higher binding energies) in Pb 4 f, I 3d, and Br 3d peaks of the Fe²⁺ incorporated CsPbI₂Br film compared with the pristine CsPbI₂Br film implies modified chemical bonding properties induced by doping [28]. Specifically, the incorporation of FeCl₂ in the CsPbI₂Br perovskite lattice can be confirmed by the existence of FeCl₂ through the two peaks located at 724.7 and 710.4 eV accredited with the Fe 2p core levels. The data gathered from both XRD and XPS measurements confirm that some FeCl₂ ions are interacted inside the lattices of CsPbI₂Br perovskite instead of an unpretentious physical involvement.

The optical properties of the pristine and  $ 0.5\% $   $ \mathrm{FeCl}_2 $ -doped  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  perovskite films were analyzed by the UV-visible absorption spectra measurements (Fig. 1b). The absorption intensity of  $ 0.5\% $   $ \mathrm{FeCl}_2 $ -doped  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  perovskite film enhanced significantly in almost the whole visible spectral range compared to that of pristine perovskite film. Such an increase in absorption ability can be attributed to the improvement in the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film morphology, while the poor absorbance of pristine perovskite can be attributed to the presence of voids reducing effective optical thickness for photo-absorption [29]. A negligible shift ( $ \sim 2\mathrm{nm} $ ) in the absorption edge of both samples was observed, which exhibits the almost same bandgap for both  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  perovskite films ( $ \sim 1.91\mathrm{eV} $ ), in agreement with the theoretical bandgap of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  [30].

The effects of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   incorporation on the charge recombination and exciton lifetimes of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films were evaluated by the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL (TRPL) measurements. Fig. 1e displays the PL spectra of 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film with appreciably enhanced peak intensity and blue-shifted PL emission, indicating a suppressed non-radiative recombination and low defect density because of the precious passivation effect of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   [31–33]. The carrier dynamics of   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film is appraised by TRPL decay curves (Fig. 1d), which are well fitted by using a biexponential function, and the fitted parameters are listed in Table S1. The average lifetime (  $  \tau_{ave}  $  ) of the   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film increased to 37.7 ns compared to pristine film (13.6 ns). Such a three-fold longer decay lifetime indicates a reduced charge recombination as a result of the low defect states [34–36].

The influence of  $ \mathrm{FeCl}_2 $  doping on the crystallization of the prepared

$ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film was monitored by comparing digital photographs of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  films with and without  $ \mathrm{FeCl}_2 $  additive after annealing at  $ 40^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for different times (Fig. 1e). The variation in film color indicated that  $ \mathrm{FeCl}_2 $  addition triggers the crystallization process of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  perovskite. This can be ascribed to the introduction of  $ \mathrm{Fe}^{2+} $  which may distort the  $ [\mathrm{PbI}_6]^{4-} $  octahedral in perovskite crystal, and thus breaks the continuous periodic structure of perovskite at a certain degree [18]. This may result in a smaller grain size, which is beneficial to stabilize the black  $ \alpha $ -phase  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  perovskites due to its higher surface energy [11, 37,38].

This phenomenon is further confirmed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images. Top-view SEM images were conducted for the pristine and 0.5% FeCl $ _2 $ -doped CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite films at different magnifications (Fig. 2a–d). The pristine CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film presented a relatively rough nature with prominent pinholes and deep-grain boundaries on the perovskite surface with large grain size over 500 nm. As stated by Li et. al, large grain size in the high-temperature annealed perovskite film such as CsPbI $ _2 $ Br is not conducive to stabilize the  $ \alpha $ -phase and the device performance can be easily degraded due to the existence of pinholes [18]. On the contrary, densely packed and homogenous coverage can be noticed for 0.5% FeCl $ _2 $ -doped CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite film with smaller grain size distributing in a range of 200–300 nm, as consistent with the XRD peak broadening. Furthermore, high magnification top-view SEM image of the 0.5% FeCl $ _2 $ -doped perovskite film depicts the tightly bound grain boundaries (Fig. S4). To alleviate the effects of SCG technique on the crystallization and film formation of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite, we also prepared the corresponding films by rapid thermal annealing process by directly annealing the samples at 280 °C. Compared to the pristine and 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped perovskite films assisted with SCG technique, the perovskite films fabricated via rapid thermal annealing process displayed micron-sized grains, however, the grains are separated by each other in both films (Fig. S6). However, like SCG technique, we can notice the positive effect of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   towards the formation of perovskite grains in rapid thermal annealing process, which facilitates much better connection between perovskite grains in smaller size. To examine the location and uniformity of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   ions inside the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   matrix, energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) measurements were performed on the 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film and shown in Fig. 2e. The mapping data demonstrated that all the perovskite elements including Cs, Pb, I, Br along with Fe and Cl of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   additive were conformal and uniformly distributed throughout the 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film.

To correlate the effects of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   doping with the photovoltaic performance, we fabricated planar PSCs with a structure of FTO/  $  SnO_{2}/CsPbI_{2}Br/spiro-OMeTAD/Au  $  . Fig. S7 and Fig. 3a show the cross-sectional SEM images of the devices employing pristine and 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films, respectively. Both images demonstrate a reasonable contact between the adjacent layers that enables an effective charge movement within the device. To investigate the effect of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   on the electronic energy state of the perovskite films, we carried out the ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurements. The spectra for secondary electron cut-off (  $  E_{cutoff}  $  ) edge and electron onset (  $  E_{onset}  $  ) edge of the pristine and 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films were collected to ascertain impact of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   on the energy levels of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films (Fig. S8). The   $  E_{cutoff}  $   values of 17.57 and 17.69 eV as well as the   $  E_{onset}  $   values of 1.76 and 1.80 eV with valence band maximum (  $  E_{VBM}  $  ) energy around 3.65 and 3.53 eV were detected for pristine and 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films, respectively. The   $  E_{VBM}  $   data are specified through the equation of   $  E_{VBM} = h\nu - (E_{cutoff} - E_{onset})  $  , where   $  h\nu  $   (21.22 eV) is the photon energy for the excitation light source (He 1α) [39]. Furthermore, the energy level diagram of the pristine and 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films determined from the UPS data were schematized in Fig. 3b with energy levels of adjacent charge transporting layers obtained from previous works [40]. Both the energy levels alignment of the   $  E_{VBM}  $   and conduction band minimum (  $  E_{CBM}  $  ) energy at the interface disclose the upshift toward the vacuum level, and the   $  E_{VBM}  $   matches well the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level of spiro-OMeTAD. The incorporation of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   reduces the energy barrier between the adjacent layers, facilitating more efficient transportation of holes from perovskite to spiro-OMeTAD layer.

Subsequently, we measured current density—voltage (J—V) characteristics of the assembled devices or varying   $  FeCl_{2}  $   content under

standard 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  illumination as depicted in Fig. S9 and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters are listed in Table 1. The enhancement in all the photovoltaic parameters was achieved for the 0.5% FeCl  $  _{2}  $  concentration, which can be ascribed to pinhole-free morphology with desired charge-carrier dynamics. As depicted in Fig. 3c, the champion device employing 0.5% FeCl  $  _{2}  $ -doped CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br perovskite shows an outstanding PCE of 17.1% with a J  $  _{SC}  $  of 15.90 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , V  $  _{OC}  $  of 1.31 V, and a fill factor (FF) of 81.8%, which is much higher than that of control device yielding a PCE of 14.60% (J  $  _{SC}  $  = 15.49 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , V  $  _{OC}  $  = 1.20 V, FF = 78.5%). To the best of our knowledge, this is the record efficiency for CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br-based perovskite cells (Table S2). The steady-state J  $  _{SC}  $  and stabilized PCE at the maximum power point (MPP) confirmed the reliability of the J-V characteristics (Fig. 3d). Such behavior is further confirmed by the incident photon-to-current efficiency (IPCE) measurements and the spectra are shown in Fig. 3e, suggesting better light-harvesting potential. The corresponding integrated J  $  _{SC}  $  values from the respective IPCE are consistent with the measured values from the J-V measurements (within 5% error). Reproducibility is another important factor for the commercialization of the fabricated perovskite devices. Fig. 3f shows the statistical PCE of 20 individual devices derived from pristine or 0.5% FeCl  $  _{2}  $ -doped CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br perovskite films, indicating excellent reproducibility.

To scrutinize the impact of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   doping on the charge transfer/recombination properties of the perovskite films within the device, additional characterizations were performed by electrochemical impedance spectrometry (EIS) at a frequency range of 500 kHz to 1 Hz as well as the capacitance-voltage (C-V) measurements recorded at a frequency of 1 kHz. Nyquist plots of the pristine and 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite-based devices recorded in the dark are shown in Fig. S10. The inset depicts the equivalent circuit diagram used for fitting which demonstrates an equivalent capacitor formed via parallel circuit with transfer resistance (  $  R_{s}/\Omega  $  ) that gained from the high-frequency region and recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}/\Omega  $  ) that attained from the low-frequency region [41]. The fitting results confirm that the   $  R_{s}  $   value is decreased (96–38   $  \Omega  $  ) and the   $  R_{rec}  $   value is increased (6122–15855   $  \Omega  $  ) for the 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite-based device in comparison with the control device (Table S3). These results point out that the interfacial charge transfer is enhanced whereas the recombination route is substantially suppressed in the 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite-based devices [42]. Subsequently, we conducted C-V measurements and the obtained curve associated with the   $  V_{bi}  $   was evaluated by Mott–Schottky (M-S) equation [12]. The   $  V_{bi}  $   value calculated from the fit in M-S plots was enhanced for 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite-based device from 1.10 to 1.22 V as consistent with the increment in   $  V_{OC}  $   (Fig. S11). The enhancement in   $  V_{bi}  $   originating from the low defect density not only indicates larger driving force for the separation of photogenerated carriers but also provides an extended depletion region for the efficient suppression of electron-hole recombination [43,44]. Therefore, the incorporation of the   $  FeCl_{2}  $   directly contributes to the output voltage of corresponding devices [45,46].

As pointed out above, the   $  FeCl_{2}  $   additive can effectively stabilize the   $  \alpha  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite, which is promising to boost the long-term stability of the corresponding perovskite films and ensuing devices under ambient conditions. To avoid the oxidation effect of   $  Fe^{2+}  $ , we performed vacuum-drying process at 50 °C for 24 h to remove the moisture inside the high-purity   $  FeCl_{2}  $   precursor. In the light of these findings, we monitored the operational stability of the unencapsulated devices exposed to incessant illumination under white light emitting diode (LED) source at MPP voltage bias (Fig. 3g). The 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $ -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ -based device retained 95% of its initial efficiency after 330 h while the pristine device degrades quickly and its initial performance declined rapidly to 25% after light soaking for 100 h, which designates the effective stability of the   $  FeCl_{2}  $ -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ -based devices. As demonstrated by previous studies, the existing pinholes/voids on the perovskite surface act as pathways for the diffusion of degradation agents such as moisture and oxygen, leading to accelerated degradation

As obviously shown in Fig. S12, the color of the pristine perovskite sample distressed in the first 30 min and the black   $  \alpha  $  -phase started to change into pale yellow as well as the yellow   $  \delta  $  -phase of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   was detected just after 4 h. This difference was further evaluated by time-dependent UV–vis absorbance measurements and the obtained results are presented in Fig. S13. The absorption intensity of pristine perovskite film entirely disappeared just after 6 h, while no clear variation is observed for the 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped perovskite film even after 96 h. Afterwards, the top-view SEM images of the corresponding aged perovskite films were conducted and illustrated in Fig. S14. In order to elucidate the enhancement in   $  V_{OC}  $  , the space charge limited current (SCLC) experiments were performed for the electron-only and hole-only devices based on altered   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   layers to detect the influence of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   doping on the defect states along with the trap-state density (  $  \eta_{trap}  $  ) of the perovskite films. Fig. 4 displays the dark J-V characteristics of the electron-only (Fig. 4a) as well as the hole-only (Fig. 4b) devices based on the pristine and 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films. The insets depict the corresponding device configurations of FTO/  $  SnO_{2}  $  /  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  /PCBM/Au and FTO/PTAA/  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  /PTAA/Au in case of electron-only and hole-only devices, respectively. The linear relation of J-V curves at low voltage bias matches with the ohmic response and the current reveals a kink point at the intermediate region after a certain voltage i.e., trap-filling limit voltages (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ) [52]. The   $  V_{TFL}  $   values are found to be 0.64 and 0.28 V for pristine and 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films in the electron-only configuration, respectively. Whereas, in case of the hole-only configuration, the   $  V_{TFL}  $   values are 1.29 and 0.77 V for pristine and 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films, respectively. As expected, the   $  \eta_{trap}  $   value of 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film (  $  7.1 \times 10^{15} cm^{-3}  $  ) is found to be two-fold lower than that of pristine perovskite film (  $  1.6 \times 10^{16} cm^{-3}  $  ). Consistently, the transient photovoltage (TPV) decay curves of the control and target devices emphasize the advanced charge mobility in addition to the lower trap density of the 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, indicating a decrease in the undesired charge-carrier

recombination within the device (Fig. 4c) [43,54,55]. These results also provide a promising concept for the reduction of defects with better perovskite morphology for the performance enhancement of the assembled PSCs.

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, we elucidated the promising role of   $  FeCl_{2}  $   as a Pb-site additive for the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite structure to develop the performance and prolong the stability of the perovskite devices. Such a facile strategy resulted in obtaining high-quality photoactive films with favorable energy levels, fewer defect states, and improved surface morphology. Thanks to the tuning of tolerance factor,   $  FeCl_{2}  $   incorporation also effectively stabilized the photoactive   $  \alpha-CsPbI_{2}Br  $   phase. Ultimately, a record efficiency of 17.1% was thoroughly achieved in a planar device architecture based on 0.5%   $  FeCl_{2}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films, which is among the highest efficiencies of all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite employing solar cells. Beyond everything,   $  FeCl_{2}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   devices exhibited a promising operational stability under MPP tracking.
