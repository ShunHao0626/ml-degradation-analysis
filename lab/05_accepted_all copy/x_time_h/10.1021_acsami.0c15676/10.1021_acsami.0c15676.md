# Thiophene-Fused Butterfly-Shaped Polycyclic Arenes with a Diphenanthro[9,10-b:9',10'-d]thiophene Core for Highly Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Samala Venkateswarlu, $ ^{\circ} $  Yan-Duo Lin, $ ^{*,\circ} $  Kun-Mu Lee, $ ^{*,\circ} $  Kang-Ling Liau, and Yu-Tai Tao

Cite This: https://dx.doi.org/10.1021/acsami.0c15676

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Two polycyclic heteroarene derivatives, namely, V-1 and V-2, with a diphenanthro[9,10-b:9',10'-d]thiophene (DPT) core tethered with two diphenylaminophenyl or diphenylamino groups were first synthesized and used as hole-transporting materials (HTMs) in perovskite solar cell (PSC) fabrication. The novel HTMs exhibit appropriate energy-level alignment with the perovskite so as to ensure efficient hole transfer from the perovskite to HTMs. V-2 with the diphenylamino substituent on DPT exhibited impressive photovoltaic performance with a power conversion efficiency of  $ 19.32\% $ , which was higher than that of V-1  $ (18.60\%) $  and the benchmark  $ 2,2',7,7' $ -tetrakis- $ (N,N $ -di- $ p $ -methoxyphenyl-amine)- $ 9,9' $ -spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD)  $ (17.99\%) $ , presumably because of a better hole extraction, higher hole mobility, and excellent

film-forming ability, which were supported by steady-state photoluminescence (PL), time-resolved PL, the hole mobility experiment, scanning electron microscopy, and atomic force microscopy measurements. Meanwhile, V-2-based PSCs exhibited better long-term durability than that with V-1 and the state-of-the-art spiro-OMeTAD, which is ascribable to the excellent surface morphology and hydrophobicity of the film. This systematic study suggests that DPT-based molecules are good potential candidates as HTMs for achieving high-performance PSCs.

KEYWORDS: polycyclic arenes, diphenanthro[9,10-b:9',10'-d]thiophene, perovskite solar cell, hole-transporting materials, herringbone packing motif

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Organic–inorganic hybrid material perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted a great deal of attention in the past decades for their unique characteristics, including their large light absorption coefficient, high charge-carrier mobility, low production cost, long exciton diffusion length, and outstanding power conversion efficiencies (PCEs).  $  ^{1-13}  $   The PCEs of PSCs have increased rapidly from 3.8 to more than 25%, as certified by the National Renewable Energy Laboratory. They are highly regarded as promising alternatives to silicon solar cells.  $  ^{14-17}  $

In general, the conventional architecture (n–i–p) PSCs are composed of electron-transporting materials, perovskite crystal layers, and hole-transporting materials (HTMs). It is generally known that the HTM plays a crucial role in long-term stability and achieving high performance for PSCs. So far, 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenyl-amine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) is the most commonly used organic HTM for high-performance PSCs because it can not only facilitate hole extraction and transfer but also suppress charge recombination. However, the spiro-OMeTAD molecule suffers from complicated synthesis and purification processes, leading to a relatively high commercial price. Thus, it is of limited practical applications.  $  ^{18,19}  $   In light of these drawbacks, a variety of alternative HTMs have been explored, including those based on inorganic HTMs, organic polymers, and small molecular HTMs.  $  ^{20-28}  $   Among these HTMs, organic small molecular

In our previous work, we have observed that a class of polycyclic derivatives containing benzo[3,4]phenanthro[1,2-b]benzo[3,4]phenanthro[2,1-d]thiophene (BPBPT) and diphenanthro[9,10-b:9',10'-d]thiophene (DPT) frameworks show a mobility in the range of 0.16–2.03 cm $  ^{2}  $  V $  ^{-1}  $  s $  ^{-1}  $  for field-effect transistor devices, where near-cofacial π–π stacking is suggested to be one of the reasons to deliver moderate-to-high hole mobility. $  ^{52,53}  $  We believe that the highly cofacial packing motifs and thus greater electronic couplings generally expected from neighboring molecules can also hold promise for their use in PSCs.

Inspired by these advantages, we report the first synthesis of two new DPT-based small molecules end-capped with different arylamino arms, denoted as V-1 and V-2, as shown in Figure 1, as well as their application in PSCs. To the best of our knowledge, the DPT core has never been used as small molecular HTMs for PSCs. Here, the energy-level alignment, X-ray diffraction, thermal stability, and photophysical and electrochemical properties were systematically investigated. Meanwhile, the film morphology of the V series on the perovskite surface was investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and atomic force microscopy (AFM). A smooth and compact film without pinholes has been achieved

by V series HTMs, facilitating a better hole extraction from the perovskite layer. The experimental measurements of hole mobility, steady-state photoluminescence (PL), and time-resolved PL (TRPL) revealed that the V-2 HTM showed a better hole extraction capability. Furthermore, density functional theory (DFT) calculation was performed to elucidate the electronic properties of DPT-based HTMs. The typical n-i-p PSCs with a device structure of FTO/TiO $ _2 $ /MAPbI $ _3 $ /HTM/Ag were fabricated. The device based on V-1 exhibited a promising PCE of 18.60%. Impressively, the PSC device using V-2 showed a remarkable PCE, reaching up to 19.32%, with excellent device stability after aging for 300 h under a constant humidity of 40% in the dark. The PCEs of cells using these new HTMs V-1 and V-2 are superior to that of the state-of-the-art spiro-OMeTAD-based device (17.99%). These results indicate that DPT derivatives are promising  $ \pi $ -linkers for efficient HTMs in PSCs.

These results demonstrated that HTMs V-1 and V-2 had good thermal stability and are suitable for application in PSCs.

2.5. Device Performance. To explore the potential of the V series as HTMs in device applications, conventional PSCs with a configuration of FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $ /mp-TiO $ _2 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /HTM/Ag were prepared, with additives such as 4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP) and lithium bis-(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) incorporated as dopants in the HTM layer (the details of the device fabrication process are presented in the Supporting Information). The cross-sectional SEM pictures of the devices are shown in Figure 8a. The thicknesses of the perovskite and HTM layers are around 450 and 150 nm, respectively. The current density-voltage (J-V) characteristics of PSC devices (shown in Figure 8b) were recorded under AM 1.5 illumination (100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $ ), and the related photovoltaic parameters of PSCs are listed in Table 3. The PSCs with spiro-OMeTAD as the HTM were also fabricated for comparison.

The V-1-based PSCs offered a champion PCE of 18.60%, together with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.10 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.54 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and a fill factor of 0.75 for the reverse scan condition. In contrast, the devices based on V-2 achieved an encouraging PCE of 19.32%, with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.09 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.72 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and an FF of 0.78. Under the same conditions, the device employing spiro-OMeTAD as the HTM layer reached a lower PCE of 17.99%, with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.08 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.51 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and an FF of 0.74. It is noticed that all the photovoltaic parameters of the best device with V-2 were higher than the corresponding values for devices based on V-1 and spiro-OMeTAD except for   $  V_{oc}  $  . The improved   $  J_{sc}  $   of V-2 can be attributed to a higher hole mobility and a better hole extraction ability, which lead to a hole-transfer efficiency higher than those of V-1-based devices, as discussed in the steady-state PL and hole mobility measurement (Figures 6a and S2). The average PCEs of the V-1- and V-2-based devices are 17.67 and 18.86%, respectively, with a low standard deviation (Table 3). This attests the good reproducibility of device performance based on the two new HTMs.

To gain further insight into the interfacial charge carrier extraction and the recombination process in PSCs, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was performed at a bias of 0.7 V under dark conditions. The results were fitted using the equivalent circuit models. As shown in Figure 8e, the high-frequency region corresponds to the transfer resistance   $  (R_{\mathrm{tr}})  $   of the devices. The low-frequency semicircle corresponds to the charge recombination resistance   $  (R_{\mathrm{rec}})  $   of the HTMs/perovskite interface. The   $  R_{rec}  $   values of the devices are in the following order: V-2 > V-1 > spiro-OMeTAD, which indicates that the charge recombination could be effectively reduced in the V-2-based device. This result is in good agreement with the larger   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF values (Table 3), thus contributing to the better photovoltaic performance.

Long-term stability is a crucial issue for the commercial application of PSC devices. We conducted the aging test of PSC devices with V-1, V-2, or spiro-OMeTAD as the HTM for 300 h under ambient conditions without encapsulation with an 85% relative humidity at ca. 25 °C. As shown in Figure 9, the devices with V-1 and V-2 exhibited good long-term stability, retaining 80 and 85% of their initial PCEs after 300 h. In contrast, the device containing spiro-OMeTAD as the HTM retained only 63% of the original PCE during the same time period. Apparently, our V series HTMs outperform spiro-OMeTAD in terms of the PSC device stability, which might be ascribed to the smooth and homogeneous surface on the perovskite layer surface. # 3. CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, we have developed two novel polycyclic heteroarenes as hole-transporting small molecules, namely, V-1 and V-2, based on a DPT central core. Their photophysical, electrochemical, and photovoltaic properties were investigated. V-2 incorporating two diphenylamino groups on the DPT core has a slightly lower HOMO level along with higher hole mobility compared with V-1 and the benchmark material of spiro-OMeTAD. Steady-state PL measurements of perovskite/HTM layers showed much stronger quenching in the case of V-2, indicating more efficient hole extraction and transfer at the interface of perovskite/V-2. Moreover, the TRPL experiment evidenced more efficient collection of photogenerated holes at the interface between the molecular
