# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted considerable attention for their relatively high power conversion efficiencies (PCEs), simple fabrication processes, and cost-effectiveness.  $  ^{[1,2]}  $   The organometallic halide perovskite materials, typically   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbX_{3}  $   (X = Cl, I, or Br),  $  ^{[3]}  $   show wide and strong absorption over the incident solar spectrum, high mobility, extremely long and balanced electron–hole diffusion lengths,  $  ^{[4]}  $   which ensure that the PCEs of PSCs have boosted from 3.81% to

As an HTL, it must be wide band gap, appropriate energy level matched with that of photoactive layer, and effective suppression of diffusion between the electrode material and photoactive layer. In the meantime, it must be ensured that itself can withstand organic solvent and do not react with any adjacent materials.[16] Metal oxide semiconductors including NiO,  $ \mathrm{CuO}_x $ ,  $ \mathrm{CrO}_x $ ,  $ \mathrm{MoO}_3 $ ,  $ \mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{O}_5 $ , and  $ \mathrm{WO}_3 $  [17,18] have been utilized as HTLs in organic solar cells due to their wide band-gap, higher stability, high work function (WF), and good hole-transport properties, which would be alternative hole transport materials for PSCs. At present, a tantalizing reality is that p-type inorganic metal oxide materials (NiO, CuO, and  $ \mathrm{Cu}_2\mathrm{O} $ ) have been introduced as efficient HTLs to replace the PEDOT:PSS layer.[19,20] NiO has a better p-type conducting behavior, but existing strong band edge absorption can affect the light absorption of photosensitive layer when it is used as HTL material.[21] Cuprous oxide ( $ \mathrm{Cu}_2\mathrm{O} $ ) and copper oxide (CuO) are well-known p-type semiconductors.[20] In fact, that p-type CuO has a high concentration of Cu vacancies and stable defects under O-rich conditions is able to facilitate the contact with fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) anode.[22]  $ \mathrm{Cu}_2\mathrm{O} $  is known to be quite stable within certain temperature and oxygen pressure range.[23] Due to its p-type conductivity with hole mobility exceeding  $ 100~\mathrm{cm}^2\mathrm{V}^{-1} $

$ \mathrm{CrO}_x $  thin film is an amorphous complex with  $ \mathrm{Cr}^{3+} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Cr}^{4+} $ , and  $ \mathrm{Cr}^{6+} $  oxidation states at  $ 200^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and exhibits p-type semiconducting behavior because chromium vacancies are the predominating defects.[18,26]  $ \mathrm{Cr_2O_3} $  has excellent stability to resists aggressive oxidizing conditions even aqua regia,[27] and is widely applied to the fields, such as protective coating layer, solar energy materials, and catalytic systems.[28] If used as HTL of PSC, it can effectively suppress the detrimental effects of oxidative and halide-forming species on any adjacent materials, especially provide a buffer to prevent the diffusion between the electrode material and photoactive layer and to shield the bottom contact electrode from chemical etching caused by iodine liberated from the  $ \mathrm{CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $  perovskite layer.[27] By adjusting the fabrication procedure, moreover, its band energy level could be modified to match with that of the electrode and photoactive layer material, which is benefit for improving its ability of hole collecting. At present, using  $ \mathrm{CrO}_x $  as HTL to work directly with  $ \mathrm{CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $  perovskite material has not yet been reported.

Therefore, in this paper, we introduce a new hole transport material of   $  CrO_{x}  $   that is applied to inverted-PSCs through a radio frequency (RF) sputtering method according to the parameters in the reference.  $  ^{[18]}  $   Taking full advantage of   $  Cu_{2}O  $   and   $  CrO_{x}  $  ,   $  CrO_{x}  $   thin films are doped with copper by reactive cosputtering of Cr and Cu from a composite target.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The different diameter copper annuluses are placed on the sputtering area of chromium target to constitute the co-sputtering composite target, as the schematic drawing is shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information). In order to ensure that the annuluses have the same section area for sputtering, the smaller diameter copper wire is made into a bigger annulus, and the bigger diameter copper wire is made into a smaller annulus. Moreover, the section area of each annulus is about one over forty of the sputtering area of chromium target. The as-deposited   $  CrO_{x}  $   films with oxygen flow ratio   $  f(\mathrm{O}_{2})  $   (  $  O_{2}/O_{2}+Ar  $  ) of 40% and substrate temperatures at 200 °C is amorphous chromium oxide thin film, which is consistent with our previous results.  $  ^{[18]}  $   And the copper-doped   $  CrO_{x}  $   (  $  Cu:CrO_{x}  $  ) films have also shown the characteristic of amorphous (X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the films are not shown here).

The optical transmission spectra of   $  CrO_{x}/FTO  $   and   $  Cu:CrO_{x}/FTO  $   films were shown in Figure S2a (Supporting Information). The thicknesses of the HTL films have given from the results of the optimized performance in the PSCs. The   $  CrO_{x}/FTO  $   film has transmittance higher than 70% across the entire visible and NIR spectra. When one copper annulus is placed on the sputtering area of chromium target, the transmittance of   $  Cu:CrO_{x}/FTO  $   film has a slight decrease. With copper annulus increasing, the corresponding film transmittance decreases.

Especially, the transmittance in the range of 300–550 nm decreases much more.

The optical  $  E_{\mathrm{g}}  $  has been evaluated by extrapolating the linear portion of the curve to the energy axis using the relation  $  \alpha = \frac{A}{h\nu} (E - E_{g})^{1 / 2}  $ . [29] The optical  $  E_{\mathrm{g}}  $  values of  $  \mathrm{CrO}_x  $  and  $  \mathrm{Cu:CrO_x-1}  $  films are  $  \approx 3.72  $  and  $  3.70\mathrm{eV}  $ , respectively (shown in Figure S2b, Supporting Information). With copper increasing, their  $  E_{\mathrm{g}}  $  values decrease to  $  \approx 3.65  $  and  $  3.53\mathrm{eV}  $  for  $  \mathrm{Cu:CrO_x-2}  $  and  $  \mathrm{Cu:CrO_x-3}  $  films, respectively. This result is consistent with the variation of transmittance. These results suggest that  $  \mathrm{CrO}_x  $  and  $  \mathrm{Cu:CrO_x}  $  are promising candidates for HTLs in PSCs.

Based on Hall Effect measurement results (see Table S1, Supporting Information),   $  CrO_{x}  $   film exhibits a weak p-type semiconducting behavior due to the predominating defects of chromium vacancies.  $  ^{[18]}  $   With copper doping,   $  Cu:CrO_{x}  $   also presents p-type conductivity, and each   $  Cu:CrO_{x}  $   film's resistivity is smaller, but its Hall mobility is higher than that of   $  CrO_{x}  $   film. Noteworthy,   $  Cu:CrO_{x}-2  $   film demonstrates the smallest resistivity and the biggest hole mobility among the   $  CrO_{x}  $   and   $  Cu:CrO_{x}  $   films, which would be the best candidates for HTL in PSC.

Using the   $  CrO_{x}  $   and   $  Cu:CrO_{x}  $   films described above as HTLs, we have fabricated inverted PSCs based on the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   as photoactive layer material and   $  [^{6}]  $  -phenyl-C61 butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) as the electron transport layer material. The schematic drawing of the device structure is shown in Figure 1a. As illustrated in Figure 1b, the cross-sectional scanning electron micrograph (SEM) image of device shows distinguishable planar layer-by-layer structure. The thicknesses of the grown perovskite layer, PCBM, and Ag electrode are   $  \approx450  $  , 80, and 200 nm, respectively. However, the thickness of HTL is too thin to observe by SEM, and then its position was marked in the SEM image. To obtain uniform and better coverage of high quality   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite film, UV-ozone treatment (5 min) for   $  CrO_{x}/FTO/glass  $   and   $  Cu:CrO_{x}/FTO/glass  $   substrate is used to modify the surface wetting properties of   $  CrO_{x}  $   and   $  Cu:CrO_{x}  $   electrode interlayers. From the optimized performance results in the PSCs, 10 nm   $  CrO_{x}  $   film is the optimal HTL thickness for the PSC (Figure S3, Supporting Information).

The current density–voltage (J-V) characteristics of PSCs are presented in Figure 2a, as the corresponding performance parameters are summarized in Table 1. An open-circuit voltage   $  V_{oc}  $   of 0.93 V, a fill factor FF of 0.67, a short-circuit current density   $  J_{sc}  $   of 14.90 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , a series resistance   $  R_{s}  $   of 1.62 Ω cm  $  ^{2}  $  , and a corresponding PCE of 9.27% are obtained for the PSC with CrO  $  _{x}  $   as HTL. To testify the best performance of copper-doped CrO  $  _{x}  $   film as HTL of PSC, we have fabricated series of devices with different annuluses placed on the chromium target (Figure S4, Supporting Information). Although the transmittance of Cu:CrO  $  _{x}  $  /FTO film has a slight decrease with one annulus placed on the chromium target, the   $  V_{oc}  $  ,   $  J_{sc}  $  , and PCE of the corresponding device increase mainly due to the resistivity decrease of copper-doped CrO  $  _{x}  $   film. When two annuluses are placed on the chromium target, the   $  V_{oc}  $  ,   $  J_{sc}  $  ,   $  R_{s}  $  , FF, and PCE of the corresponding device are improved to 0.98 V, 0.70, 16.02 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , 1.04 Ω cm  $  ^{2}  $  , and 10.99%, respectively. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, efficient PSCs based on   $  CrO_{x}  $   HTL on FTO glass substrate by RF sputtering have been fabricated. A respectable solar-to-electrical PCE of 9.27% has been achieved with a device composed of a Glass/FTO /  $  CrO_{x}  $  /  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  /PCBM/Ag structure. With Cu doping,   $  CrO_{3}  $   and   $  CrO(OH)/Cr(OH)_{3}  $   in   $  CrO_{x}  $   film are suppressed to avoid reacting with the degradation product of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  . This can also increase WF, electrical conductivity, and carrier mobility of   $  CrO_{x}  $   films to enhance the photovoltaic performance (larger fill factor, open circuit voltage, short circuit current, less series resistance, and better stability) of PSC. Consequently, a PCE up to 10.99% has been achieved. This study not only demonstrates a novel HTL system for PSCs but also reveals the importance of HTL doping for high-performance and decently stable optoelectronic devices. The   $  Cu-CrO_{x}  $   is worth for further in-depth study for inorganic metal oxide HTLs of PSC in the near future.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: PbI $ _2 $  (99.99%), hydroiodic acid (57 wt% in water, 99.99%), and methylamine (33 wt% in absolute ethanol) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Ethanol, diethyl ether, acetonitrile, and  $ N,N $ -dimethylformamide were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Corporation Co., Ltd. FTO glass with a sheet resistance of 14  $ \Omega $  sq $ ^{-1} $  was purchased from Asahi Glass (Japan). The purity of copper wire, silver wire, and chromium target were 99.99%. All of the used reagents were analytical grade.

Devices Fabrications: The devices were prepared on FTO glass substrate. The rigid substrate was thoroughly cleaned in an ultrasonic bath with methanol, acetone, and isopropyl alcohol successively for 10 min, and dried in a dry nitrogen stream.   $  CrO_{x}  $   films were prepared through RF sputtering method according to the parameters in the reference.  $  ^{[18]}  $   The RF cosputtering schematic drawings of composite target is shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information). The different diameter copper wires were made into annuluses which have the same section area, then were placed on the sputtering area of chromium target. Mixtures of Ar and   $  O_{2}  $   with oxygen flow ratio   $  f(O_{2})  $   of 40% were introduced into chamber by mass-flow controllers during deposition. The total gas flow rate retained at 10 sccm. The substrate temperature was at 200 °C, and the optimized thicknesses of the   $  CrO_{x}  $   film was 10 nm. The typical   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   two-step deposition method was derived from previous paper.  $  ^{[3,33]}  $   First, methylamine and hydroiodic acid (1:1 equimolar ratio) were stirred at 0 °C for 2 h. Then the precipitate was collected by evaporating at 50 °C for 2 h. Finally, a white power   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   was obtained by washing with diethyl ether three times and drying at 100 °C in a vacuum oven for 24 h. In order that   $  PbI_{2}  $   can better tile on the surface of   $  CrO_{x}  $   film,   $  CrO_{x}  $   films were treated by ultraviolet ozone cleaning machine (Novascan, PSD-UV, USA) under air ambience at room temperature for 5 min prior to spin-casting   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution (dissolved in N,N-dimethyformamide at a concentration of 462 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) at 2000 rpm for 60 s. After drying at 80 °C for 20 min in air, the substrate was dipped into a solution of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   in 2-propanol (10 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) for 3 min, then the substrate was heated at 80 °C for 30 min. Subsequently, the fullerene derivative PCBM (Nano-C, dissolved in chlorobenzene of 20 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) electron transport layer was deposited by spin-coating at 600 rpm for 10 s and at 3500 rpm for 30 s. Finally, a 150 nm thick silver layer was deposited by thermal evaporation at a base pressure of 1 × 10  $  ^{-7}  $   mbar.

Characterization: The crystal structures were examined by XRD (Bruker AXS, D8 Advance) with Cu Kα radiation under operation conditions of 40 kV and 40 mA. Hall coefficient, Hall mobility, and carrier density were determined by Hall Effect measurements in van der Pauw geometry. Measurements were performed at room temperature by a Hall Effect measurement system (Model 7707A, Lake Shore, USA). The transmittance of the films was measured by a UV-vis-NIR spectrophotometer (CARY5000, Varian) in the 200–800 nm wavelength range at room temperature. The morphology of the HTL films was characterized by AFM (SPM-9500J3, Shimadzu, Japan). The morphologies of PSCs were observed by a high-resolution field emission scanning electron microscope (FEI Nova Nano-SEM 450). XPS and UPS were performed using an XPS/UPS system (Thermo Scientific, ESCLAB 250Xi, USA). The surface carbon signal at 284.6 eV was used as an internal standard. UPS was carried out using He I radiation at 21.22 eV from a discharge lamp operated at 90 W, a pass energy of 10 eV, and a channel width of 25 meV. PL spectra were obtained with a 532 nm laser, as the excitation source, pulsed at a frequency of 9.743 MHz. The PSCs were stored in ambient air for a short period of time and then measured under a 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  (AM1.5 simulated irradiation) illumination with a standard ABET Sun 2000 Solar Simulator. The simulated light intensity was calibrated with a Thorlabs optical power meter. The active area was 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $ , which was defined by the area of the Ag electrode. The J-V characteristics were measured by a CHI660D electrochemical workstation (Shanghai, China). The hysteresis of the J-V curves was recorded at a scan rate of 0.1 V s  $  ^{-1}  $ . Scanning was done in both forward direction and reverse direction. In addition, the scans start and finish

under forward bias, and have 2 s stabilization time at forward bias under illumination prior to scanning. The corresponding incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra were measured with a QE/IPCE Measurement Kit system (Newport, USA).
