# Abstract:

Amino group functionalized hydrophobic ionic liquid, 1-ethylamine hydrobromide-3-methylimidazolium hexafluorophosphate (ILPF $ _6 $ ) ionic liquid, was employed as the functional monomer to fabricate a carbon-based hole-conductor-free perovskite solar cell with high performances. The photoactive perovskite layer was facilely acquired by one-step spin-coating the precursor solution on a TiO $ _2 $ /FTO electrode surface, and subsequently annealed at an initial annealing at 60 °C for 3 min followed by annealing at 100 °C for 10 min to crystallize. The perovskite precursor solution was prepared by mixing ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid and methylammonium iodide with an equal molar ratio of PbI $ _2 $ . In the optimized conditions, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) was enhanced from 10.08% to 13.01% by doping ILPF $ _6 $  into the perovskite crystal film. This study demonstrates the multifaceted functions of ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid in improving the efficiency and stability of PSCs. First, ILPF $ _6 $  can react with PbI $ _2 $  to form perovskite and can also play a role of the controlling and crosslinking reagent to obtain large grain size and minimize defects. Secondly, ILPF $ _6 $  improve the loading amount of perovskite crystals on the TiO $ _2 $  film. Third, ILPF $ _6 $  can promote the transport rate of the photogenerated carriers in the perovskite crystal layer. Lastly, ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid with its high hydrophobicity creates a moisture barrier to improve the stability of PSCs effectively. The PSCs were stored under dark in a low relative humidity atmosphere (RH of 20%) at 25 °C without sealing. After 840 h, the PSC can maintain 94% of its initial PCE under the identical intensity illumination. This study demonstrates an excellent strategy to break through the bottleneck of the traditional PSCs and the enhanced effect of ionic liquid for carbon-based PSCs.

Keywords: Ionic liquid; Perovskite; Solar cell; Carbon electrode.

# Introduction

Hybrid organic-inorganic perovskite solar cell (PSC), utilizing all-solid organometallic halide perovskite as light absorbing material, has become a promising solar voltaic technology due to the facile fabrication and high power conversion efficiency (PCE) [1-2]. The perovskite crystal structures used for highly efficient PSCs often possess a general perovskite formula  $ \mathrm{ABX}_3 $ , typical ions for A, B and X can be summarized as following: A presents  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3^+ $  (MA),  $ \mathrm{HC(NH_2)_2^+} $  (FA) or  $ \mathrm{Cs}^+ $ ; B means  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  or  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $ ; X is halogen ion such as  $ \mathrm{Cl^-} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Br^-} $  or  $ \mathrm{I^-} $ . The certified PCE for perovskite solar cells has been dramatically promoted from  $ 3.8\% $  [3] to  $ 23.7\% $  [4] since it was discovered in 2009. The high performances of PSCs mainly depend on the excellent optical properties of the perovskite crystals including: a large light absorption coefficient, direct band gap and bipolar transport properties [5-6]. Thus, numerous efforts have been undertaken to improve the quality of the perovskite layer and thus to enhance the PEC of the perovskite solar cells through a series of post-treatments such as annealing the as-cast films, anti-solvent rinsing and hot-substrate casting [7-10].

Although the aforementioned methods help to achieve high performance PSCs, there are still many challenges need to be overcome before the commercial viability of PSCs, such as the stability, hysteresis and cost. One major drawback of PSCs is that the lead-based perovskite photosensitizers suffering from their inherent instability under ambient operation conditions due to the low formation energy and the high

2

hydrophilicity of the organic cations [11, 12]. Many methods have been developed to improve the stability of PSCs [13]. Among them, 2D perovskite has been demonstrated to be an efficient way. By adopting large aliphatic or aromatic alkylammonium cations, such as 2-phenylethylammonium [14],  $ n $ -butylammonium [15], 4-(aminomethyl) benzoic acid hydroiodide [16] and 5-aminovaleric acid hydroiodide [17] to substitute methylammonium, 2D perovskite crystals can be prepared and the environmental stability of the perovskite can be promoted due to the presence of the stronger Pb-I bond in the 2D inorganic plane. Unfortunately, the poor conductivity of the organic spacer cation decreases the charge transport rate, leading to the undesirable charge accumulation and the radiative recombination loss, and hence causing a significant decrease in efficiency. Therefore, to avoid these shortcomings of 2D perovskite, Sargent demonstrated the perovskite solar cells based on the quasi-2D perovskite  $ \mathrm{PEA}_2\mathrm{MA}_{59}\mathrm{Pb}_{60}\mathrm{I}_{181} $  can exhibit a maximal PCE of  $ 15.3\% $  and longevity [18]. Functionalized  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite film was prepared by spin-coating the precursor solution containing butylphosphonic acid 4-ammonium chloride. The doped reagent acted as a cross-linker to reduce the defects between neighboring grains in the perovskite layer. The additive enhanced the photovoltaic efficiency from 8.8 to  $ 16.7\% $ , and also improved its resistance to moisture [19]. The abovementioned means indicate that the incorporation of a small amount of additives into  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite can effectively improve device efficiency and long-term stability by promoting the strong interactions between lone electron pairs from the Lewis base additive and under-coordinated Pb atoms, forming conducting channels in

3

perovskite films [20-22], or serving as a scaffold to promote the crystallization of the perovskite crystals [23, 24].

Before the advent of carbon-based hole-conductor-free PSCs, without exception, typical PSC fabrication commonly employs noble metal (Au or Ag) as counter electrode or spiro-OMeTAD as the hole-transport layer. Noble metal electrodes are mainly prepared by costly high-vacuum evaporation and are susceptible to the severe corrosion by iodide ion migrating from the perovskite layer. Although spiro-OMeTAD is a versatile material to realize a truly solid state solar cells, it suffers from low conductivity in its pristine form, which can only be overcome by doping with Bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-Butylpyridine (tBP) [25]. However, except for the high cost and the extra fabrication step, Li-TFSI is hygroscopic and very likely to damage the water-sensitive perovskite layer in the humid environment and shorten the device lifetime [26]. Therefore, alternative doping materials are desirable to address the keypoint of the perovskite solar cells to satisfy the commercial requirements.

Carbon has been considered as a good substitute material for traditional metal cathode because of its low cost and appropriate energy level. Various types of carbon materials including graphite, carbon nanotube, fullerene, have been widely applied in the photovoltaic devices, and achieved remarkable improvements on efficiency [27-29]. Meng [30] firstly printed a layer of porous carbon black on perovskite light absorption layer to ensure full contact and reduce the interfacial resistance, and then hot-pressed a layer of industrialized graphite sheet to obtain a PCE of  $ 10.2\% $ .

4

Furthermore, his group [31] fabricated a free-standing thermoplastics carbon film with good conductivity and flexibility through a simple and low-temperature (<100°C) process with a PCE of 13.53%. Han [32] prepared perovskite solar cells entirely by screen printing, in which a layer of porous   $  ZrO_{2}  $   was printed on mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   to form a two-layer framework, which effectively increased the light absorption area of perovskite and also prevented the occurrence of short-circuit. Then, carbon paste was printed on the   $  ZrO_{2}  $   layer, annealing at 400 °C for 30 minutes. After cooling, perovskite precursor was drop-coated on the framework and annealed at 70 °C to form a photoactive layer.

Herein, a hole-conductor-free perovskite solar cell based on a carbon counter electrode was fabricated by doping 1-ethylamine hydrobromide-3-methylimidazolium hexafluorophosphate ionic liquid into the perovskite precursor. Fig.1. A exhibits the schematic for the device structure of the perovskite solar cell based on ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid doped CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  perovskite. Perovskite crystals were excited by light to generate electrons and holes. The electrons were injected into the mesoporous TiO $ _2 $  layer (m-TiO $ _2 $ ), and holes are transported to carbon counter electrode passing through the perovskite layer. The ammonium cation of the functionalized ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid can be anchored chemically on the perovskite grain surface. The reason is that ammonium cations can interact with iodide anions in the PbI $ _6^{4-} $  framework, forming the N-H···I $ ^- $  hydrogen-bonding, which can also serve as a growth-controlling reagent to confine the fast formation of perovskite crystal, thus to favor to forming larger grain size and fewer grain boundaries. In addition, ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid with high

5

hydrophobicity can prevent the corrosion of the perovskite film by moisture. Furthermore, the presence of ILPF $ _{6} $  ionic liquid increases the loading amount of perovskite crystals on the m-TiO $ _{2} $ /FTO electrode surface, which can effectively improve the light harvesting and the charge extraction. This study demonstrated the multifaceted functions of ILPF $ _{6} $  ionic liquid in enhancing the performances of the carbon-based hole-conductor-free PSCs.

The enhanced effect mainly comes from the excellent conductivity of ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid which can accelerate the transfer rate of photogenerated carriers and facilitate the separation of electron-hole pairs. Meanwhile, ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid can also help to control the morphology of the perovskite crystals formed on the m-TiO $ _2 $  scaffold surface. Seen from Fig. 2. In addition, the doping of ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid can improve the contact condition between the perovskite layer and the TiO $ _2 $ /FTO electrode surface. All the aforementioned facts will be benefit for the transfer of carriers and help to improve EQE over the entire range of spectrum. Electrical hysteresis is another well-known phenomenon observed for traditional perovskite solar cells that is usually attributed to several factors including charge trapping, ferroelectricity, ion motion, or capacitive processes during  $ J-V $  characterization [34]. Figure 4D and E show the  $ J-V $  characteristics of the  $ \mathrm{ILPF}_6 $ -free and  $ \mathrm{ILPF}_6 $ -containing PSCs in the forward and reverse scan directions and the detailed photovoltaic device parameters are listed in Table 2. It is apparent that mismatch of  $ J-V $  curves in different scan directions for  $ \mathrm{ILPF}_6 $ -containing PSCs is alleviated, indicating the reduction in the hysteresis, which can be largely contributed to the reduced density of defects and suppressed surface ion motion supported by the previous SEM and XRD results [35].

The PSC doped with ILPF  $  _{6}  $   ionic liquid shows an arc with a smaller radius, indicating lower charge transfer resistance which is consistent with the resistance (Rs) shown in Table 1. Lower charge transfer resistance indicates the photogenerated carriers can be separated more efficiently and thus reduced the recombination to improve the performances of the PSC. The enhanced effect mainly comes from the excellent conductivity of ILPF  $  _{6}  $   ionic liquid which can accelerate the transfer rate of photogenerated carriers and facilitate the separation of electron-hole

pairs. In addition, the contact condition between the perovskite layer and the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / FTO} $  electrode surface was improved, and thus can help to decrease the charge transfer resistance.

Environment stability is the key parameter for PSCs that must be considered for practical application. The ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid doped PSCs were stored under dark in a low relative humidity atmosphere (RH of  $ 20\% $ ) at  $ 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  without sealing. As shown in Fig. 5. 14

initial PCE under the identical intensity illumination. However, the PEC of the ILPF $ _6 $ -free PSC significantly decreases to  $ 52\% $  of the initial value under the same conditions. The superior stability is attributed to the hydrophobic characteristics of ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid, which can effectively blocking the water molecules from interacting with the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite crystals. Another reason is that the ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid enhances the crystallinity of the perovskite crystals and reduces the occurrence of pin-hole or defects at grain boundaries for water to penetrate through. Both the PEC and stability improvements indicate that the ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid is an efficient additive for improving the carbon-based holeconductor- free PSCs.

# Conclusions

In summary, an additive engineering by doping the hydrophobic ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid into the precursor solution was used to facilitate the growth of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite film on the mesoporous TiO $ _2 $  modified FTO electrode surface. The PSC doped with ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid not only exhibits high photoelectric conversion efficiency, but also decreases its susceptibility to moisture. The results obtained from the characterizations of SEM, XRD, UV-Vis spectroscopy, EIS and photoelectrochemical technique all confirmed the merits of ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid for improving the performances of the carbon-based hole conductor-free perovskite solar cells. ILPF $ _6 $  ionic liquid plays multiple roles in this procedure for PSC. Firstly, ILPF $ _6 $  acts as a functional reagent to control the growing process, thus leading to form high quality perovskite crystal with large grain size and fewer defects. Thus, the PEC of the PSC can be remarkably enhanced from  $ 10.08\% $  to  $ 13.01\% $ . Secondly, the

15

hydrophobicity of ILPF  $  _{6}  $   ionic liquid can effectively improve the stability of PSC by limiting the accessibility of water molecule to the perovskite crystals. The finding of this work offers an interesting and effective method to explore low-cost, excellent stability and simple-assembly of PSCs. Further study will concentrate on elucidating the underlying mechanism and promoting the PEC of the ionic additive engineered PSCs.

# Synthesis of 1-ethylamine hydrobromide-3-methylimidazolium hexafluorophosphate  $ (\mathrm{ILPF}_6) $ :

2-bromoethylamine hydrobromide was reacted with 1-methylimidazole at a mole ratio of 1:2 and stirred at  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 12\mathrm{h} $ . After being cooled slowly to room temperature, the solvent was evaporated under vacuum. The precipitate was washed three times with methanol and diethyl ether. The dried precipitate was dissolved into

16

deionized water and saturated NaOH solution was added to adjust the pH value to 10, then   $  NH_{4}PF_{6}  $   was added into the solution, stirred and kept at 40 °C for 24 h, evaporating the solvent under vacuum. The obtained white precipitate was reacted with hydrobromic acid at a mole ratio of 1:2, and stirred at 30 °C for 2 h. After the solvent was removed, the product was washed three times with diethyl ether and dried in vacuum at 60 °C overnight. Fig. S 2 showed the procedure for the synthesis of the product, which was characterized with   $  {}^{1}H  $   NMR and FTIR. All spectra were listed in Figs. S 3 and S 4.

Device fabrication: For solar cell fabrication, the perovskite precursor solutions were prepared by dissolving the mixture of   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (1:1 in molar ratio) in DMF/DMSO mixture solvents (4:1 in volume ratio). To start, fluorine doped tin oxide (FTO) substrates were scrubbed by cleaning agent and sonication in distilled water, acetone and isopropanol, sequentially, and finally dried with   $  N_{2}  $  . Before use, the substrates were treated in an UV-  $  O_{3}  $   oven for 15 min. The compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers were prepared according to previous report [36]. Briefly, FTO substrates were immersed in 40 mM   $  TiCl_{4}  $   aqueous solution at 70 °C for 60 min followed by washing with water and ethanol. The treated substrates were then annealed at 500 °C for 30 min to form a compact blocking layer. The mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste diluted in ethanol (1:4, weight ratio) was spin-coated for 30 s at 4000 rpm and annealed at 500 °C for 30 min. The prepared   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   solution was then spin coated on   $  TiO_{2}  $   coated FTO substrates at 3500 rpm for 30 s. Chlorobenzene was added quickly onto the substrate at 15 s before finishing spin-coating. The resulting transparent adduct films were heated at 60 °C for

17

3 min and then at 100 °C for 10 min. The mixed perovskite films containing ILPF  $  _{6}  $  were prepared using the same procedure as used for the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  film, and the molar ratio of mixed organic ammonium cations to PbI  $  _{2}  $  was maintained at 1:1, in which 1.3 mol L  $  ^{-1}  $  of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ , 0.03 mol L  $  ^{-1}  $  of ILPF  $  _{6}  $ , 1.33 mol L  $  ^{-1}  $  of PbI  $  _{2}  $ . Finally, devices were completed by depositing commercial carbon paste on the top of the perovskite film by the doctor-blading technique, followed by heat-treatment at 100 °C for 30 min. Devices were measured under simulated AM 1.5G, 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  sun light with an active area of 0.04 cm  $  ^{2}  $ . All these procedures were carried out on atmosphere conditions with humidity of 20%.

Characterizations: Photocurrent density-voltage (J-V) curves were measured using a solar simulator equipped with 500 W xenon lamp (Beijing China Education Au-light Co., Ltd, China). Light intensity was adjusted to AM 1.5G one sun (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) illumination. The J-V curves were measured with the CHI660A electrochemical workstation (Chenhua Corp., China) by linear cyclic voltammetry at room temperature in the ambient air. The electrochemical properties of the PSCs were tested and analyzed by electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS, CHI660A) with the frequency ranging from 0.1 Hz to 100 kHz under dark condition at a bias of -0.8 V. Morphology of the samples were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, SU8010, Japan). NMR analysis was conducted on Avance 400 MHz NMR Spectrometer (Bruker, Switzerland). FTIR analysis was carried out with Nicolet IS10 FTIR spectrometer (Thermo Nicolet, USA). UV-Vis spectrum was measured on a UV-2550 spectrophotometer (Shimadzu, Japan). X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns

were acquired by D8 ADVANCE (BRUKER, Germany) with Cu Kα radiation ( $ \lambda = 0.15418 $  nm).
