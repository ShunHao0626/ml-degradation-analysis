# Ultrahigh Open-circuit Voltage for High Performance Mixed-cation Perovskite Solar Cells by Acetate Anions

Rui Fu,  $  ^{ab}  $   Yicheng Zhao,  $  ^{ab}  $   Wenke Zhou,  $  ^{ab}  $   Qi Li,  $  ^{abc}  $   Yao Zhao  $  ^{ab}  $   and Qing Zhao  $  ^{*ab}  $

Mixed-cation perovskite solar cells have a much higher power conversion efficiency than traditional mono-cation perovskite solar cells. However, fabrication of high-quality mixed cation-halide perovskite films is still challenging due to the formation of point defects in annealing process, resulting in a large loss in potential of solar cells. In this work, formamidine acetate, as an additive, is introduced to the perovskite-precursor solution to overcome this issue. The prolonged time-resolved photoluminescence lifetime indicates the fewer defects in the optimized perovskite film with formamidine acetate than that in the control sample. The solar cells using the optimized perovskite layer achieve a maximum power conversion efficiency of  $ 21.9\% $  with a large open-circuit voltage of 1.19 V. For another perovskite film with a low band gap (1.53 eV), the optimized device also shows a large open-circuit voltage of 1.14 V. Our work provides a new strategy to realize the commercial applications of perovskite solar cells.

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite materials have a typical structure with the chemical formula ABX $ _{3} $ , commonly composed of a monovalent cation A (methylammonium (MA $ ^{+} $ ), formamidinium (FA $ ^{+} $ ), Cs $ ^{+} $ , Rb $ ^{+} $ ), $ ^{1-4} $  a divalent cation B (Pb $ ^{2+} $ , Sn $ ^{2+} $ ) $ ^{5} $  and a halide anion X (I $ ^{-} $ , Br $ ^{-} $ , Cl $ ^{-} $ ). $ ^{6,7} $  This mixed cation-halide perovskites have recently attracted tremendous research interest in the photovoltaic (PV) field, $ ^{8-11} $  due to their extraordinary properties, such as high light absorption in the visible range, $ ^{12} $  long carrier diffusion length, $ ^{13-15} $  low exciton binding energy, $ ^{16} $  and a tunable bandgap from 1.3 to 2.3 eV by substituting the cations and/or anions partially, $ ^{17-20} $  etc. The perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has achieved >20% power conversion efficiency (PCE) using these mixed cation-halide perovskite films, particularly a record PCE of 22.1% by using FA $ _{0.95} $ MA $ _{0.05} $ PbI $ _{2.85} $ Br $ _{0.15} $  PV layer. $ ^{21} $  Considering the reflection loss from glass/transparent conductive oxide (TCO) interface, the current density almost reaches its maximum value, while there is still considerable space for open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{OC} $ ) increase in these solar cells. $ ^{22} $  The so-far-reported lowest potential loss is 0.38 V for the passivated emitter and rear silicon solar cells with 1.1 eV bandgap; however, the potential loss is rarely reported below 0.4 V for high-efficiency PSCs over a variety of compositions. $ ^{23, 23, 24} $  We reason that the potential loss in PSCs should be lower than 0.4 V when the defects in perovskite layer are significantly passivated. The defects in

polycrystalline perovskite film include bulk and surface defects and the latter one predominates the Shockley-Read-Hall (SRH) recombination in most cases.

During the crystallization process, annealing is a necessary step for perovskite formation and growth. Driven by heat, part of the organic component in mixed-cation perovskite could escape away from the thin film during the annealing process in the form of methylammonium iodide (MAI), leaving a considerable amount of point defects at the surface. These point defects may lead to complex surface relaxation and reconstruction, resulting in a defect-active area.  $  ^{25, 26}  $   Defect suppression at the surface has the potential to improve the overall performance of PSCs, especially the   $  V_{OC}  $   increase.  $  ^{27-29}  $

Here we put forward an additive-engineering strategy to improve the performance of mixed-cation PSCs by introducing formamidine acetate (FAAc) to the perovskite-precursor solution. We propose a synergetic mechanism for the effect of FAAc on suppressing surface defects. During the annealing process, FAAc tend to react with methylammonium iodide (MAI) to form formidium iodide (FAI) and methylammonium acetate (MAAc); meanwhile, MA $  ^{+}  $  cations could volatilize preferentially in the form of MAAc instead of MAI, owing to a much lower thermal stability of MAAc. Therefore, the number of I $  ^{-}  $  vacancies is reduced at the surface, leading to a reduced recombination rate in the resulting film. Correspondingly, the device efficiency has increased from 20.4% to 21.9% by using this new strategy. The progress mainly comes from the remarkable enhancement of  $  V_{OC}  $  from 1.13 V to 1.19 V. The  $  V_{OC}  $  of 1.19 V is pretty close to the world record for this composition ever reported. $ ^{30} $  We also applied this technique to low-bandgap PSCs, and the  $  V_{OC}  $  is increased from 1.09 V to 1.14 V with a 0.39 V potential loss. Besides, not only the long-term PCE stability but anti-moisture phase stability has been enhanced for the optimized perovskites, due to the high crystal quality. The high reproducibility

The experiment setup and procedures are shown in Fig. 1a. First, we prepared pure perovskite precursor (with a composition of   $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.81}MA_{0.14}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $  ) and FAAc additive solution, respectively. To enhance the solubility of FAAc in DMF/DMSO mixed solvent, we heated the solution and added the hot solution into precursor at a fixed ratio directly. To investigate the effect of FAAc additive on the quality of polycrystalline perovskite film, we fabricated four batches of perovskite films by using different volume ratios of FAAc solution to perovskite precursor solution (0/4/8/16 vol.%). The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns (Fig. S1, ESI†) reveals the typical diffraction peaks of perovskite for all the samples at   $  \sim14.3^{\circ}  $  ,   $  20.2^{\circ}  $  ,   $  24.7^{\circ}  $  ,   $  28.6^{\circ}  $   and   $  32.0^{\circ}  $  , which are assigned to the (110), (200), (202), (220) and (310) planes, respectively. Scanning electronic microscopy (SEM) images (Fig. S2, ESI†) of perovskite films (or solar cell devices) with and without FAAc additive indicate full coverage of the perovskite layers on the substrates in the absence of pin-holes, which is necessary for achieving high-performance devices. All these results demonstrate that there is no obvious change for the crystal morphology in the existence of FAAc additive. We further examined the carrier lifetime in these samples via time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) characterization (Fig. 1b). The sample with 8 vol.% ratio of FAAc has a much longer carrier lifetime (t=252 ns) compared with the control sample (t=170 ns). A longer lifetime means fewer recombination centers in the films. It can be concluded that FAAc additive is favorable for reduction of the defects, which are always considered as the non-radiative recombination centers in PSC field.

To find out which ingredients in FAAc make contributions to the enhancement of device performance, we did several control experiments. Using the same method, we put other two additives in perovskite colloidal solution: FAI solution (dissolved in DMF/DMSO) and DMF/DMSO mixed solvent without any solute. With these two additives, both the  $  V_{OC}  $  and PCE did not change evidently (Fig. 2f) and it is powerfully proved that the most progress of  $  V_{O}  $

$  _{C}  $   and PCE is profited from Ac  $  ^{-}  $   anions, rather than FA  $  ^{+}  $   cations or another extra solvent.

During the annealing process, the organic component of MAI could escape from the perovskite films considering its low melting temperature (270~280 °C).  $  ^{31,32}  $   This character of MAI tends to result in a large quantity of I  $  ^{+}  $  vacancies in perovskite film, especially at the grain boundaries.  $  ^{25,27,33,34}  $   These point defects, which were recently shown to have high affinity to water and oxygen molecules,  $  ^{35-37}  $   can facilitate the decomposition of perovskite crystals in the ambient air. Additionally, the vacancy-related defects are potentially SRH traps within the energy bandgap of perovskites, causing a reduced carrier lifetime that relates to a shorter diffusion length. Here we summarize that engineering defect densities could be a valid tool to realize ambient-air-tolerant and high-performance PSCs.

Based on these discussions and experiments above, we propose a synergetic mechanism of perovskite growth with FAAc additive (Fig. 3a). Compared to pure perovskite precursor, the modified precursor includes FAAc molecules. In the existence of FAAc molecules with a high melting point (165°C) compared to MAAc (Fig. 3b), a replacement reaction could occur in the form of Ac+MA+→MAAc↑. This reaction could protect I⁻ anions from escaping away from the perovskite matrix during the following

As evidenced by thermal gravity analysis (TGA) tests in Fig. 3b, MAAc begins to volatilize at around  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ . According to the curve dW/dT, an obvious decomposition of MAAc happened in the heating process from room temperature to  $ 200^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ . In contrast, organic FAAc shows much better thermal stability with a simple direct phase transition, validated by the sharpness of the TGA curve step. Precisely because the melting (or decomposition) points of MAAc and MAI are far from each other,  $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $  cations would volatilize preferentially with  $ \mathrm{Ac^{-}} $  anions, instead of with I anions. Hence, fewer I vacancies were residual after annealing.

To further verify our hypothesis, we fabricated devices utilizing the same manner except for using MAAc as the Ac  $  ^{-}  $  source supplier. The J-V results show no distinction between samples with and without MAAc additive (Fig. S6a, ESI†). The reason why MAAc has no effect is easy to understand. When doping Ac  $  ^{-}  $  anions in the precursor solution, an excessive amount of MA  $  ^{+}  $  cations is brought into the system equivalently. Ac  $  ^{-}  $  anions prefer to escape together with those MA  $  ^{+}  $  cations from MAAc additive. As a consequence, there is no more extra Ac  $  ^{-}  $  anion remained in the thin film to combine with MA  $  ^{+}  $  cations from precursor (Fig. S6b, ESI†). Therefore, I  $  ^{-}  $  anions would escape away combined with those MA  $  ^{+}  $  cations from precursor. This result highlights the importance of cation selection to realize the above synergetic mechanism for high-performance PSCs.

In strong contrast, the PCE of control device decreased seriously by over  $ 50\% $  during the same time period. To further prove less defect on the modified perovskite active layer, we did Impedance Spectroscopy (IS) test. IS is a well-established and widely used technique in perovskite solar cells. The density of defect states can be derived from the angular-frequency-dependent capacitance using the following equation:

$$
N _ {t} (\omega) = \left(\left(V _ {\mathrm{bi}} - V _ {\mathrm{app}}\right) / q W k T\right) (d C / d \omega) \omega
$$

where  $  V_{bi}  $ , W, and  $  V_{app}  $  represent the built-in voltage, the width of the space charge region, and the applied voltage, respectively. The frequency  $  \omega  $  can be converted into the energy level of defects ( $  E_a  $ ) using the expression:

$$
\omega = A T ^ {2} \exp (- E _ {a} / k T)
$$

Finally, the defect states distribution can be obtained (Fig. 5). From this we could easily conclude that with the help of FAAc additive, the density of defect states greatly decreased. At the same time the deep level defects were suppressed obviously. Thus, FAAc additive is useful for reducing defects of perovskite layer.

# Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated that the performance of PSCs is significantly improved by additive-engineering strategy. An optimized ratio of FAAc additive is beneficial to the growth of high-quality perovskite crystals. I anions could be stabilized by exotic Ac anions, evidenced by the TRPL and TGA results. Significant enhancement in PCE was achieved with the best device PCE of 21.9%, owing to the great improvement of  $  V_{OC}  $ . The champion device has a  $  V_{OC}  $  of 1.19 V, almost the highest  $  V_{OC}  $  ever reported for  $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.81}MA_{0.14}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $  PSCs. More importantly, this simple method is also effective for other hybrid PSC systems with different material composition. A general increase of 50 mV was realized in two material compositions PSCs. Moreover, the high reproducibility of this technique is very friendly to industry production. Thus, this

strategy can be expected to pave the way for the commercial applications of PSCs.
