# Inorganic Rubidium Cation as an Enhancer for Photovoltaic Performance and Moisture Stability of   $  HC(NH_{2})_{2}PbI_{3}  $   Perovskite Solar Cells

Yun Hee Park, Inyoung Jeong, Seunghwan Bae, Hae Jung Son, Phillip Lee, Jinwoo Lee, Chul-Ho Lee,  $  ^{*}  $   and Min Jae Ko  $  ^{*}  $

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on organic monovalent cation (methylammonium or formamidinium) have shown excellent optoelectronic properties with high efficiencies above 22%, threatening the status of silicon solar cells. However, critical issues of long-term stability have to be solved for commercialization. The severe weakness of the state-of-the-art PSCs against moisture originates mainly from the hygroscopic organic cations. Here, rubidium (Rb) is suggested as a promising candidate for an inorganic–organic mixed cation system to enhance moisture-tolerance and photovoltaic performances of formamidinium lead iodide (FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ). Partial incorporation of Rb in FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   tunes the tolerance factor and stabilizes the photoactive perovskite structure. Phase conversion from hexagonal yellow FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   to trigonal black FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   becomes favored when Rb is introduced. The authors find that the absorbance and fluorescence lifetime of 5% Rb-incorporated FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (Rb  $  _{0.05}  $  FA  $  _{0.95}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $  ) are enhanced than bare FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  . Rb  $  _{0.05}  $  FA  $  _{0.95}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $  -based PSCs exhibit a best power conversion efficiency of 17.16%, which is much higher than that of the FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   device (13.56%). Moreover, it is demonstrated that the Rb  $  _{0.05}  $  FA  $  _{0.95}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $   film shows superior stability against high humidity (85%) and the full device made with the mixed perovskite exhibits remarkable long-term stability under ambient condition without encapsulation, retaining the high performance for 1000 h.

Organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite materials (OIHPs) benefit from several properties, such as low manufacturing cost, long carrier lifetime, low exciton binding energy, and suitable band gap   $  E_{g}  $   as a light absorber with high absorption coefficient.  $  ^{[1-6]}  $   Thanks to these advantages, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on OIHP have shown tremendous and various advances in the field of solar cells with high power conversion efficiency (PCE) exceeding 22%.  $  ^{[7-15]}  $   As depicted in Figure 1a, the OIHPs have the chemical formula ABX  $  _{3}  $  , where A is a monovalent organic (methylammonium, CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}^{+}  $  or formamidinium, HC(NH  $  _{2}  $  )  $  _{2}  $    $  ^{+}  $  ) or inorganic (Cs  $  ^{+}  $  ) cation, B is a divalent metal cation (Pb  $  ^{2+}  $   or Sn  $  ^{2+}  $  ) and X is a halide anion (Cl  $  ^{-}  $  , Br  $  ^{-}  $  , or I  $  ^{-}  $  ). Among the various OIHP analogs, methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) is most used as an OIHP absorber. Rubidium (Rb) belongs to alkali metal group and is situated above cesium. It has a smaller ionic radius (1.52 Å) than cesium (1.67 Å). The all-inorganic perovskite   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   is converted at high

temperatures above 300 °C from the orthorhombic (δ-phase) to a cubic (α-phase) structure and the photoactive α-CsPbI₃ with Eg of 1.73 eV has been investigated as a light absorber (Figure 1b).  $  ^{[29,30]}  $   However, CsPbI₃-based PSCs have exhibited limited PCEs (<10%) and structural instability (transforming back to the orthorhombic phase at room temperature).  $  ^{[30]}  $   Meanwhile, RbPbI₃ maintained its yellow δ-phase orthorhombic structure within a temperature range of 298–634 K and with further heating up to 714 K it melted and decomposed without undergoing a phase transition to the black perovskite (Figure 1b).  $  ^{[31]}  $   Due to the intrinsic nonperovskite structure of RbPbI₃, Rb has been rarely investigated as an absorber material for PSCs. Considering the smaller size of Rb than that of Cs, it can be expected that Rb has larger impact on structural change of the inorganic/organic mixed cation system at less mixing content compared to Cs. In addition, RbI as a perovskite precursor is known to be less toxic than CsI  $  ^{[32]}  $   and has a higher solubility in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), which is a common solvent for perovskite precursors (Figure S1, Supporting Information). Very recently, Saliba et al. incorporated for the first time Rb into multiple A-cation mixed perovskite and demonstrated a high PCE of 20.6% for RbCsMAFA mixed perovskite based device.  $  ^{[33]}  $   Catchpole and co-workers also investigated the doping effect of RbI for MAₓFA₁₋ₓPbI₃₋ₓBrₓ perovskite with PbI₂ excess condition.  $  ^{[34]}  $

Here, we focused on the mixing effect of Rb in   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   without other mixed cations and halides and suggested Rb as a promising mixing cation alternative to Cs for efficient and moisture stable absorber of PSCs. X-ray diffraction (XRD) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) data show that Rb inclusion leads to a favorable phase transition from yellow   $  \delta  $  -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   to black   $  \alpha  $  -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  , resulting in enhanced light absorbance. In addition, through the fluorescence lifetime image microscopy (FLIM), we found that Rb-mixed FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   had longer photoluminescence (PL) lifetime compared to the pristine FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   due to suppression of nonperovskite phase formation. At an optimal substituting ratio of 5% Rb, the   $  Rb_{0.05}FA_{0.95}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite solar cell exhibited remarkable enhancement of PCE from 13.56% (FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) to 17.16%, which is attributed to increases in the short circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ), open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ), and fill factor (FF). The Rb-mixed FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film exhibited superior moisture stability compared to FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   at the high relative humidity (RH) of 85%. We demonstrated that the PSC based on   $  Rb_{0.05}FA_{0.95}PbI_{3}  $   showed remarkable stability for 1000 h under ambient conditions at an average RH of 55% and 25 °C, maintaining the PCE performance above 97% of initial PCE.

To elucidate the origin of enhancement in   $  V_{oc}  $  , we employed FLIM on   $  Rb_{0.05}FA_{0.95}PbI_{3}  $   and   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   films coated on glass. Figure 6a,b show the confocal images of the fluorescence lifetime for the perovskite films. Compared to   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  , whole   $  Rb_{0.05}FA_{0.95}PbI_{3}  $   film exhibits longer average lifetime. As shown in Figure 6c, time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) decay spectra reveal that incorporation of Rb into   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   increases the charge lifetime by a factor of 3 over   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  .

Hysteresis has been commonly observed in the  $ J - V $  curves of PSCs and originates from ion migration or the presence of a trap state within the perovskite layer. As shown in Figure S10 in the Supporting Information, the hysteresis of the  $ \mathrm{Rb}_{0.05}\mathrm{FA}_{0.95}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  PSC is alleviated compared to the  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  device and hysteresis index defined in a previous report[36] reduced from 0.163 (FAPbI $ _3 $ ) to 0.047 ( $ \mathrm{Rb}_{0.05}\mathrm{FA}_{0.95}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ ). The suppression of hysteresis in Rb-mixed  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  can be explained by the reduced trap density compared to  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $ . Despite the high performance, the long term stability of PSCs is highly desired for commercialization. We conducted a moisture stability test for the perovskite films without the spiro-OMeTAD upper layer in order to exclude the degradation effect of spiro-OMeTAD. Figure 7a,b shows the absorbance change with time for  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{Rb}_{0.05}\mathrm{FA}_{0.95}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  films kept in a sealed box at  $ 85\% $  RH and  $ 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ . In the case of  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $ , the absorbance gradually decreased and finally degraded completely after  $ 6\mathrm{h} $ . As can be seen in the inset of Figure 7a, the film color changed to yellow, meaning that black phase perovskite converted to the yellow  $ \delta $ -phase and decomposed to  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . On the other hand, there are no obvious changes in the absorbance onset and film color of  $ \mathrm{Rb}_{0.05}\mathrm{FA}_{0.95}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  even after  $ 25\mathrm{h} $  (Figure 7b). As we discussed earlier, the remarkable stability of  $ \mathrm{Rb}_{0.05}\mathrm{FA}_{0.95}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  against moisture may be attributed to the presence of an inorganic component and contraction of the cubo-octahedral volume by incorporation of a small cation, resulting in strong chemical interaction between A-site cations (FA) and iodides. We also observed that better moisture stability of  $ \mathrm{Rb}_{0.05}\mathrm{FA}_{0.95}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  compared to  $ \mathrm{Cs}_{0.05}\mathrm{FA}_{0.95}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film (Figure S14, Supporting Information). This reveals that smaller size of Rb than that of Cs renders the contraction of structural volume more effective at small mixing content ( $ 5\% $ ).

The stabilities of complete devices based on   $  Rb_{0.05}FA_{0.95}PbI_{3}  $   and   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   were evaluated under ambient environmental conditions (average RH = 55% and 25 °C) without any encapsulation. During a stability test for 1000 h, the   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   device severely degraded as evidenced by the optical image of Figure 7c, whereas the   $  Rb_{0.05}FA_{0.95}PbI_{3}  $   device remained almost unchanged visually. Compared to   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   device, Figure 7d shows the superior stability of   $  Rb_{0.05}FA_{0.95}PbI_{3}  $  -based PSC maintaining a PCE >97% of the initial efficiency of the fresh device.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we have demonstrated that inorganic Rb-incorporated FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite exhibits enhanced photovoltaic performances and superior moisture stability compared to the bare FAPbI $ _3 $ . Among the alkali metal elements, Rb (with its smaller size) was successfully inserted as a replacement for Cs in the monovalent cation site of FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite and gave rise to a thermodynamically favorable transition of FAPbI $ _3 $  with reduced enthalpy of transition from the nonperovskite  $ \delta $ -phase to the black  $ \alpha $ -phase. The complete conversion and stabilization of the photoactive black FAPbI $ _3 $  derived by Rb $ ^+ $  incorporation resulted in higher absorbance and increased fluorescence lifetime. With an optimal Rb content, the inorganic/organic A-cation mixed perovskite (Rb $ _{0.05} $ FA $ _{0.95} $ PbI $ _3 $ ) devices showed remarkable enhancement in  $ V_{oc} $  and  $ J_{sc} $ . Furthermore, the presence of a less volatile inorganic element, and distortion and contraction of the cubo-octahedron for (Rb/FA)-cation render the mixed perovskite highly stable against moisture.

# 4. Experimental Section

Fabrication of Perovskite Film and Device: Patterned FTO glass substrate (TEC8, Pilkington) was cleaned by washing with acetone, ethanol, and isopropanol for 20 min, followed by UV-ozone treatment for 20 min. A compact TiO₂ layer (c-TiO₂) was prepared on the FTO substrate by spin coating a titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) solution (75% in isopropanol, Aldrich) diluted in 1-butanol at 2000 rpm for 40 s and subsequent heating at 500 °C for 30 min. The mesoporous TiO₂ layer (m-TiO₂) was prepared on the c-TiO₂ layer by spin coating at 3500 rpm for 40 s using a lab-made TiO₂ paste diluted with anhydrous ethanol (1:3.5 weight ratio) and then annealed at 500 °C for 30 min. The FAPbI₃ solution was prepared by dissolving 1 mmol of PbI₂ (99.9985%, Alfa Aesar) and HC(NH₂)₂I (Dyesol) in DMSO (99.9% Aldrich) and DMF (99.8%, Alfa Aesar) mixed solvent (1:9, v:v). The Rb-mixed perovskite solution was prepared by adding the target amount of Rbl (99.9%, Aldrich) into the FAPbI₃ solution. The perovskite solution was deposited on the m-TiO₂ layer through successive two-step spin coating at 1000 rpm for 5 s and 4000 rpm for 15 s. During the second step, diethyl ether was dripped on the rotating film 7 s before the end of the program. The final film morphology is very sensitive to the antisolvent dripping step. The spin coated film was heated at 150 °C for 10 min to form a dark perovskite. CsI (99.9%, Aldrich) was used for Cs mixed FAPbI₃. Spiro-OMeTAD solution was prepared by dissolving 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (56 mg, spiro-OMeTAD, Merck), 4-tert-butylpyridine (30 mg, 96%, Aldrich), and bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (6 mg, 99.95%, Aldrich) in chlorobenzene (1 mL, 99.8%, Aldrich). The Spiro-OMeTAD solution was coated at spin rate of 2500 rpm for 20 s. Finally, an Au electrode of thickness 80 nm was deposited by thermal evaporation on top of the device with a shadow mask to define the active area.

Characterization: The crystal structure of the perovskite film was investigated by XRD (D/MAX-2500, Rigaku) using 1.5418 Å Cu Kα

radiation. The perovskite crystal structures were visualized using the visualization for electronic and structural analysis (VESTA) program. The cross-sectional image of the device and the surface morphology of the perovskite film were characterized by field-emission scanning electron microscopy (Inspect F, FEI). The absorbance and PL spectra of perovskite films were measured using a UV-vis spectrometer (Lambda 35, PerkinElmer) and a Fluorolog3 photoluminescence spectrometer system with a monochromator (iHR320, HORIBA Scientific) excited at  $ 530~\mathrm{nm} $ . FLIM was measured using an inverted-type scanning confocal microscope (MicroTime-200, Picoquant, Germany). A single-mode pulsed diode laser  $ (470~\mathrm{nm} $  with a pulse width of  $ \approx 100 $  ps and an average power of  $ < 0.1~\mu \mathrm{W}) $  was used as an excitation source. A dichroic mirror (490 DCXR, AHF), a long-pass filter (HQ500lp, AHF), and a single photon avalanche diode (PDM series, MPD) were used to collect emissions from the samples. Time-correlated single-photon counting technique was used to count fluorescence photons. FLIM images were recorded using the time-tagged time-resolved data acquisition method. Exponential fittings for the obtained fluorescence decays (extracted from the FLIM images) were performed using the Symphotime software (version 5.3). DSC curves were obtained using a thermogravimetry-differential thermal analysis instrument (Q600, TA Instruments) under Ar atmosphere with a heating rate of  $ 2^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\mathrm{min}^{-1} $ . The XPS measurements were performed with a scanning XPS microprobe (PHI 5000 VersaProbe, Ulvac-PHI) using an Al Kα monochromator (1486.6 eV). The current density-voltage curve was measured using a Keithley 2400 source measurement unit and a solar simulator equipped with a  $ 180~\mathrm{W} $  xenon lamp (Yamashida Denso, YSS-50A). The light intensity was adjusted by an National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL)-calibrated Si solar cell equipped with KG-3 filter to 1 sun condition (AM 1.5G and  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $ ). A black mask was attached to the devices to avoid light scattering during the J-V measurement. The scan rate for the J-V measurement was set to be  $ 120\mathrm{mVs}^{-1} $ . The active area of each cell was measured using an optical microscope camera (Moticam 1000). The EQE was measured using an EQE measurement system
