# Graphene-Assisted Zwitterionic Conjugated Polycyclic Molecular Interfacial Layer Enables Highly Efficient and Stable Inverted Perovskite Solar Cells

Donghwan Koo, Ungsoo Kim, Yongjoon Cho, Junghyun Lee, Jihyung Seo, Yunseong Choi, Kyoung Jin Choi, Jeong Min Baik,  $  ^{*}  $  Changduk Yang,  $  ^{*}  $  and Hyesung Park  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: As an effective strategy to achieve high performance and operational stability in perovskite solar cells (PSCs), numerous studies have been conducted to develop efficient charge transporting materials that possess desirable conductivity, carrier mobility, and stability. Among the great number of candidate materials, graphene has attracted significant attention owing to its remarkable optoelectrical properties and stability. Based on these advantages, in this study, a graphene-assisted electron transport layer (ETL) was developed through functionalization with an n-type semiconducting small molecule, perylene diimide amino N-oxide (PDINO), which simultaneously improved the performance

and stability of PSCs. The PDINO-functionalized graphene ETL exhibited suitable energy-level alignment with enhanced carrier mobility, thus resulting in an enhanced power conversion efficiency of 21.2% for the inverted structured PSCs. Moreover, through the hydrophobic surface feature and barrier property of graphene and the   $  \pi-\pi  $   interaction of PDINO with the underlying layer of PSCs, notably improved operational stability was achieved using the developed functionalized graphene ETL.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Over the past decade, perovskite materials with desirable features, such as high absorption coefficient, bandgap tunability, price competitiveness, and solution processability, have demonstrated great potential for next-generation optoelectronic device applications.  $  ^{1-4}  $   In this context, perovskite absorbers have been particularly successful in energy harvesting devices, and the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) can, at present, reach over 25% for a conventional structured cell, approaching levels close to that of commercialized silicon-based solar cells,  $  ^{5}  $   while the performances of inverted structured PSCs have also steadily increased to greater than 23% PCE.  $  ^{6}  $   In particular, inverted structured PSCs are receiving prominent interest owing to their various advantages, such as the replacement of costly materials, removal of high-temperature processed hole transport layers, and elimination of ionic dopants that can adversely affect device stability.  $  ^{7-10}  $   Despite such noteworthy progress in efficiency, PSC stability still requires further development to meet the stringent demands of the market.

To overcome the challenging deficiencies of PSCs, considerable efforts have been dedicated to modifying the perovskite absorber layer, e.g., the introduction of additional cations and anions, incorporation of additives, and post-treatments via a gas or solvent vapor.  $  ^{11-15}  $   In addition to the perovskite absorber modulation, another key strategy for

# 2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

2.1. Synthesis of PDINO@GR. The potassium intercalated graphite (  $  KC_{8}  $  ) was prepared by reacting the graphite powder with potassium at 110 °C for 1 h under nitrogen atmosphere. The obtained   $  KC_{8}  $   was added to pyridine solvent and treated at -30 °C for 24 h to form expanded graphene nanoflakes. To functionalize the graphene, the PDINO powder was added to the graphene dispersion, stirred for 24 h, and subsequently sonicated for 1 h in an ice-water bath. Excessive PDINO and pyridine were removed by washing with deionized water and ethanol. The obtained PDINO@GR powder was dispersed in methanol via bath sonication for 24 h in an ice-water bath. The undispersed aggregates were removed by centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 30 min.

2.2. Device Fabrication. All precursors were purchased from Dyesol, Alfa Aesar, TCI, and Sigma-Aldrich and used without further purification. PSCs were fabricated with a configuration of indium tin oxide (ITO)/poly(triaryl amine) (PTAA)/perovskite/PCBM/ETL (ZnO, PDINO, and PDINO@GR)/Ag. The patterned ITO substrate was cleaned with deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol (IPA) by sonication, followed by  $ \mathrm{O}_2 $  plasma treatment. The PTAA solution (2 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  in chlorobenzene (CB)) was spin-coated onto the cleaned

ITO substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 120 °C for 10 min. For the perovskite solution, 1.4 M of formamidinium lead iodide (FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) and methylammonium lead bromide (MAPbBr  $  _{3}  $  ) solution were separately prepared in a dimethylformamide/dimethyl sulfoxide (7:3, v/v) co-solvent and stirred for 2 h. Then, each solution was mixed at a volume ratio of 8:2 (FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /MAPbBr  $  _{3}  $  ) and stirred for 2 h. The perovskite layer was deposited using a two-step and antisolvent method. The as-deposited perovskite film was annealed at 140 °C for 10 min. For the ETLs, the PCBM solution (20 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  in CB) was spin-coated on the perovskite layer at 3000 rpm. Then, the ZnO NPs (dispersed in IPA) and PDINO-based materials (dispersed in methanol) were deposited on the PCBM layer by spin-coating at 4000 and 3000 rpm, respectively, followed by annealing at 100 °C for 5 min. The 100-nm-thick Ag top electrode was deposited via thermal evaporation. During the evaporation of the top electrode, a patterned mask was used, and the defined photoactive area of the device was 0.13 cm  $  ^{2}  $ .

2.3. Measurement and Characterizations. Structural analysis of PDINO@GR flakes was performed via high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM) (JEM-2100F, JEOL). The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra (N 1s and O 1s) of PDINO-functionalized graphene and pristine graphene were obtained using monochromatized Al-Kα radiation source (ESCALAB 250Xi, Thermo Fisher Scientific) under ultrahigh-vacuum conditions (  $  \sim10^{-10}  $   Torr). The Raman spectra of pristine graphene, PDINO, and PDINO@GR were obtained using a confocal Raman microscope (Alpha300R, WITec). The J-V characteristics of the devices were obtained using a Keithley 2635A source meter, and the corresponding external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra were measured by IQE 200B (Oriel Instruments). For the light-intensity-dependent   $  J_{oc}  $   and   $  V_{oc}  $   measurements, neutral density filters were used to modulate the light intensity. Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL (TRPL) spectra were measured by alpha300R (WITec PL setup) and fluorescence lifetime spectrometer (Fluo Time 300, PicoQuant FmbH), respectively. The grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) patterns of PCBM were characterized at PLS-II 6D and 9A U-SAXS beamline in Pohang Accelerator

# 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

To improve the solution processibility of the defect-free unoxidized graphene, PDINO was applied as a mediator to enhance the dispersibility of graphene flakes. The PDI core backbone comprises a planar and electron-deficient   $  \pi  $  -conjugated polycyclic molecular structure that can strongly interact with graphene through the attractions between permanent dipoles of polar groups in PDI and induced dipoles in graphene (Figure 1a). The -NO side terminal group with high polarity endows graphene with good dispersibility in common organic solvents. Such noncovalently functionalized graphene with PDINO (hereafter denoted PDINO@GR) aptly

preserves the intrinsic properties of graphene, compared to oxidized graphene derivatives like graphene oxide or reduced graphene oxide.  $  ^{26}  $   PDINO@GR was synthesized according to methods reported in a previous study.  $  ^{27}  $   In brief, the graphite powder was infiltrated by molten potassium metal, and the intercalation of potassium atoms into graphene interlayers spontaneously proceeded due to the ionization potential gap between graphite and potassium. The interlayer distance of graphene was further expanded in a pyridine solvent, which was subsequently functionalized by PDINO. When using PDINO@GR as an ETL, the excess PDINO and pyridine was removed by a vacuum filtration washing process, and the as-obtained PDINO-functionalized graphene was redispersed in methanol via mild sonication. Further details are provided in the Experimental Section.

We first performed the structural analysis of the as-exfoliated PDINO-functionalized graphene using high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM). As shown in Figure 1b, graphene was exfoliated into a few layers, where the typical flake size of functionalized graphene from PDINO@GR was about  $ 1\mu \mathrm{m} $ . The corresponding selective area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern reveals highly ordered sixfold symmetry (Figure 1c), which demonstrates the high crystallinity of the noncovalently functionalized graphene. The high-resolution N 1s and O 1s XPS spectra of PDINO-functionalized graphene and pristine graphene as a reference were compared, as shown in Figure 1d,e. The presence of prominent signature peaks in N 1s and O 1s spectra from PDINO@GR, compared to those of the featureless spectra of the pristine graphene, confirms that graphene is effectively functionalized with PDINO through the electronic coupling of   $  \pi  $  -conjugated systems. The Raman spectrum of PDINO-functionalized graphene in Figure 1f exhibits two signature Raman modes of graphene at 1580 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   (G band) and 2700 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   (2D band) without a defect-related D band (1345 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ) as well as the representative Raman modes of PDINO observed at 1305 and 1380 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  . This result corroborates the noncovalent functionalization of graphene by PDINO, which can avoid the disruption of the aromatic system of graphene, thus minimizing the additive-induced defect generation that is commonly observed in the covalent functionalization of graphene and oxidized graphene.  $  ^{29,30}  $

Then, we investigated the applicability of PDINO-functionalized graphene as the ETL material in PSCs and the effect of PDINO@GR ETL in the device performance by fabricating inverted structured PSCs (ITO/poly(triaryl amine) (PTAA)/perovskite/PCBM/ETL (ZnO, PDINO, and PDINO@GR)/Ag). The detailed fabrication process is provided in the Experimental Section. The schematics of the completed device and the corresponding flat-band energy-level diagram are shown in Figure 2a,b. The PDINO@GR film exhibited the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels at 4.1 eV and the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy level at 6.4 eV, indicating that the PDINO@GR ETL facilitated electron transfer from the perovskite absorber to the cathode and promoted the suppression of charge carrier recombination through the deep-lying HOMO level. Figure 2c shows the current density–voltage (J-V) characteristics of the best performing devices with various configurations of the ETL under simulated AM 1.5 G solar spectrum conditions at 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  irradiance. The corresponding photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table 1. Compared to the reference device using the ZnO ETL with a PCE of 18.4%, a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 22.5 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.09 V, and a fill factor (FF) of 75.2%, the device with PDINO shows slightly improved PCE of 19.7% with a  $  J_{sc}  $  of 23.3 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , a  $  V_{oc}  $  of 1.10 V, and an FF of 77.0%, thus verifying the functionality of n-type semiconducting PDINO as the ETL. Notably, the PDINO@GR device shows a much improved performance of 21.2% PCE with a  $  J_{sc}  $  of 24.1

mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.13 V, and an FF of 78.1%. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) measurement spectra and associated integrated   $  J_{sc}  $   values further support the aforementioned J-V characteristics results (Figure 2d and Table 1). The PCE distribution profiles of the devices with different ETLs shown in Figure S1 also demonstrate the reliability and improvement of each device performance. In addition, the PDINO@GR device with stable current density and PCE values was maintained at the maximum power point for over 200 s, further indicating that the suggested ETL is a promising candidate material for the interfacial layer in PSCs and enabling high efficiency with hysteresis-free behavior (Figures 2e and S2 and Table S1).

To elucidate the origin of such enhanced device performance with PDINO@GR, we first investigated the charge transport behavior for each ETL using the space charge-limited current method determined from the electron-only device (ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /PVK/PCBM/ETL (ZnO, PDINO, and PDINO@GR)/Ag). As shown in Figure 2f, the calculated electron mobilities of PDINO ( $ 6.61 \times 10^{-4} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ ) and PDINO@GR ( $ 3.01 \times 10^{-3} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ ) were considerably improved in comparison to that of ZnO ( $ 1.33 \times 10^{-4} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ ), which is attributed to the self-organized PDINO film due to the intermolecular  $ \pi-\pi $  interactions and high charge carrier mobility of graphene. $ ^{25} $  Such high electron mobility of the PDINO@GR ETL consequently enables the facilitated charge transfer and reduced charge recombination from the completed device, which can improve the key photovoltaic parameters of  $ J_{sc} $ ,  $ V_{oc} $ , FF, and thus, PCE. Light-intensity (I)-dependent  $ J_{sc} $  and  $ V_{oc} $  characteristics were also investigated to further elucidate the improved charge kinetics of PSCs when using PDINO@GR ETL. The light-intensity-dependent  $ J_{sc} $  follows the power law described as  $ J_{sc} \propto I^{\alpha} $ , where  $ \alpha $  represents the exponential factor. $ ^{31,32} $  As shown in Figure S3a, the increased value of  $ \alpha $  from the PDINO@GR-based device (0.99) compared to that from the ZnO and PDINO devices (0.97 and 0.98, respectively), implies improved charge extraction with reduced bimolecular recombination, as evidenced by the enhancement of  $ J_{sc} $ . In the open-circuit condition,  $ V_{oc} $  varies with light intensity via the following relation,  $ V_{oc} \propto kT/q \ln I $ . As shown in Figure S3b, the lower slope from the PDINO@GR device also corroborates that suppressed bimolecular recombination can be achieved through the PDINO-functionalized graphene cathode interfacial layer. Moreover, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL (TRPL) decay measurements were conducted to further elucidate the effect of each ETL on the charge dynamics, as shown in Figure 2g,h. A significantly lowered PL intensity was observed for PDINO@GR, and the TRPL profiles reveal that the average PL lifetime ( $ \tau_{\text{avr}} $ ) is 3.5 ns for PDINO@GR, 5.8 ns for PDINO only, and 13.1 ns for ZnO. We further investigated the effects of PDINO@GR ETL on the device stability of PSCs under various environmental conditions. Figure 4a,b shows a comparison of the stability of the device with different ETLs with respect to various humidity conditions measured at room temperature (45/85% RH, 25 °C) without encapsulations. The figure shows that the PDINO@GR ETL greatly improves the operational stability of PSCs against humidity. After exposure to ambient air conditions for 1200 h, the ZnO device shows a rapid PCE drop to 50% of its initial efficiency, whereas the PDINO@GR device maintained over 80% of its initial performance (Figure 4a). Moreover, even under harsher environments, the PDINO@GR device still exhibited significantly enhanced stability compared to the other conditioned samples (Figure 4b). ZnO NPs tend to aggregate and form island-like features that readily permeate environmental species, such as oxygen or moisture,  $  ^{25}  $  whereas PDINO forms a uniform thin film that can fully cover the underlying layer (PCBM) through strong intermolecular  $  \pi-\pi  $  interactions. Furthermore, the surface hydrophobicity of PDINO@GR was dramatically increased due to the hydrophobic nature of graphene, resulting in a water contact angle greater than 100°. This modified surface feature notably improved the barrier property of the PDINO@GR ETL, which effectively prevented the permeation of moisture and improved the stability of the PSCs against humid conditions. To further validate the improved device stability against moistures, XRD analyses of the perovskite absorbers were performed after exposure to moisture over an extended period. As shown in Figure 4d–f, the intensity of the  $  PbI_{2}  $  peak at  $  2\theta = 12.6^{\circ}  $ , originating from the decomposition of the perovskite crystals, significantly increased over time for the ZnO condition. Such decomposition of the perovskite structure was still present for the PDINO ETL despite the improved stability over the ZnO-based device. However, the appearance of a distinctive  $  PbI_{2}  $  peak was notably suppressed for PDINO@GR, which supports the encapsulation effect of graphene for the improved device stability under humid conditions.

Furthermore, the device stability under continuous thermal stress was verified, which is another critical factor directly affecting device performance. Figure 5a shows that the thermal stability of the PDINO@GR device is significantly enhanced compared to that of the ZnO and PDINO devices. As is well known, organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite absorbers under constant thermal stresses are vulnerable to thermal decomposition.  $  ^{39,40}  $   Halide ions from the perovskite crystal tend to diffuse into the metal electrode through the CTL, and the metal ions from the electrode also migrate into the perovskite layer to form metal halide adducts, which severely degrades both the device efficiency and stability. Thus, we suppose that the graphene would suppress the diffusion of both halide and metal ions through its excellent chemical stability and barrier property. Figure 5b shows the XRD patterns of thermally stressed perovskite absorbers with different ETLs at 85 °C, illustrating that the intensity of the characteristic AgI peak at   $  2\theta = 23^{\circ}  $   considerably increases with ZnO and PDINO, while the perovskite layer using PDINO@GR exhibits only a marginal AgI signal. This observation indicates that graphene can effectively suppress the interdiffusion of halide and metal ion species in PSCs under accelerated environmental conditions. The slightly improved thermal stability and reduced AgI peak for the PDINO device compared to those for the ZnO device are believed to originate from the uniformly formed PDINO film that can passivate the underlying layers to a certain degree, as observed in the previous moisture stability analysis result.

To further examine thermal stability, time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (ToF-SIMS) elemental depth profiling of the devices was conducted after exposure to continuous thermal stress. As shown in Figure 5c–h, significant diffusion of the halide and metal ions through

ETL to their counterpart layers was observed for the ZnO and PDINO devices, whereas such ion migration was notably inhibited for the PDINO@GR device. These results further indicate that graphene functions as an effective ion diffusion barrier, thus leading to enhanced device stability against thermal stress. Moreover, PCBM molecules are known to readily aggregate with neighboring molecules through strong   $  \pi-\pi  $   interactions under thermally elevated conditions, which can adversely affect the device performance.  $  ^{41,42}  $   Therefore, we characterized the surface morphology of PCBM after 25 h of the thermal treatment at 85 °C after removing the overlying ETLs. As shown in Figure S6, the ZnO-applied PCBM exhibits significant aggregates on its surface after thermal treatment, whereas the PDINO- and PDINO@GR-applied PCBM layers show negligible morphological changes. The strong   $  \pi-\pi  $   interactions between the PCBM and PDINO molecules are believed to reduce the intermolecular interactions within the PCBM molecules at elevated temperatures, thus increasing device stability against thermal stresses.

# 4. CONCLUSIONS

In this study, we demonstrated solution-processable defect-free graphene functionalized with PDINO as an efficient ETL for inverted structured PSCs. The proposed PDINO@GR ETL exhibited suitable energy-level alignment with adjacent layers of solar cells with desirable electron mobility and also improved the crystallinity of the underlying PCBM layer through   $  \pi-\pi  $   interactions, which together resulted in an enhanced device performance of 21.2%, compared to that of the ZnO NP ETL-based reference device (18.4%). Furthermore, the hydrophobic surface nature and barrier property of graphene effectively suppressed the permeation of moisture into the perovskite absorber as well as the interdiffusion of metal and halide ions in the device, and thus, it significantly improved the operational stability of PSCs. The proposed graphene-based electron transporting material, which showed improved device performance and operational stability, is expected to have broad impacts in the practical use of various perovskite-based optoelectronic devices.
