# $ \mathrm{BiBr}_3 $  as an additive in  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  for carbon-based all-inorganic perovskite solar cell

Yue Pei  $  ^{1}  $  , Haiyan Guo  $  ^{1}  $  , Ziyang Hu  $  ^{1}  $  , Jing Zhang  $  ^{1*}  $  , Yuejin Zhu  $  ^{1,2*}  $

1. Department of Microelectronic Science and Engineering, School of Physical Science and Technology, Ningbo Collaborative Innovation Center of Nonlinear Calamity System of Ocean and Atmosphere, Ningbo University, Ningbo 315211, China

2. School of Science and Engineering, College of Science and Technology, Ningbo University, Ningbo 315300, China

Abstract: The stable  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  perovskite solar cell (PSC) has recently attracted wide attention, but its low photoelectric conversion efficiency is still the main concern at present. The quality of the perovskite absorber layer and the energy level matching between perovskite and hole transport layer (HTM) are important factors affecting the efficiency and stability of the PSCs. In this work,  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  film is prepared by multi-step solution spin coating method. In preparation process, a layer of  $ \mathrm{BiBr_3} $  was modified on  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  film before the CsBr coating to form  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $ . This method can not only improve the crystal quality of perovskite, but also adjust the energy level to improve the carrier extraction. As the result, the photoelectric conversion efficiency (PCE) of carbon-based  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  PSCs has been effectively improved to  $ 8.73\% $ . This work demonstrates an effective approach to the preparation of high-quality perovskite films.

Keywords: CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $  perovskite solar cells; interface modification; carbon electrode

[2][3, 4] To solve these problems, the all-inorganic  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  PSCs [5-8] with carbon electrode cell structure [7, 9, 10] have

# 2. Experimental details

# 2.1 Device fabrication

The F-doped  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  glass (FTO Nippo,  $ 14\Omega/\square $ ) was etched with zinc powder and dilute hydrochloric acid ( $ \mathrm{vt}\% = 40\% $ ) before the preparation of the solar cell device. It was then ultrasonically washed 4 times with deionized water and ethanol. After the glass was dried, it was treated with UV-ozone for 15 minutes. The  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  dense layer was spin-coated at 4000rpm for 30 s using the  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  precursor. [21] After annealing at 450 □ for 30 minutes, it was treated with  $ 40\mathrm{mM} $   $ \mathrm{TiCl}_4 $  aqueous solution, then we spin-coated  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  mesoporous layer at 5000 rpm for 30 seconds using dilute  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  slurry, [22] and after annealing at 500 □ for 30 minutes, the  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  layer was obtained.

The perovskite film was from the multi-step solution spin coating method. [14] All operations were carried out in the air without temperature and humidity control. A 1M solution of  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  (N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF)) was spin-coated onto the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  substrate at 2000 rpm for 30 s. After drying at 90 for 1 h, different concentrations (0.01 mg/mL, 0.1 mg/mL, 1 mg/mL) of  $ \mathrm{BiBr_3} $  methanol solution was spin-coated on  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  film at 2000 rpm for 30 s, and dried at 70 for 5 minutes. Thereafter, the 0.07 M solution of CsBr methanol solution was spin-coated on the  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  film and  $ \mathrm{BiBr_3} $  film at 2000 rpm

Journal Pre-proof

for 30 s, and annealed at 250 □ for 5 min. This process was repeated 6 times. The obtained perovskite film was rinsed with isopropanol and dried at 250 □ for 5 min. Finally, a carbon back electrode was deposited by a doctor blade method and annealed at 100 □ for 20 min.

# 2.2 Characterizations and measurement

The surface appearance, cross section images, and atomic energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) of the perovskite films were taken by a Hitachi SU-70 scanning electron microscope (SEM). X-ray diffraction patterns (XRD) were detected by a Bruker D8 Advance X-ray diffractometer using Cu-Kα radiation. X-ray photoluminescence spectra (XPS) plots were recorded by an ESCALAB 250XI (Thermo Scientific). The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra was achieved by a Cary Eclipse Fluorescence Spectrophotometer (Agilent) with the excitation wavelength of 435 nm. The ultraviolet and visible (UV-vis) absorption spectra was tested by a UV-vis spectrophotometer (Cary).

Current-Voltage (J-V) data was recorded by a 4200 system source meter (Keithley) with a sunlight simulator (Newport, 91192A. AM 1.5, 1 sun). The intensity of the light was calibrated by a commercial standard Si cell (Newport). A perovskite solar cell's active area was defined by a black plastic aperture  $ (0.07\mathrm{cm}^2) $ . Fast intensity transient decay was recorded by an electrochemical workstation (Zahner, Germany).

For better understand the morphology change by introducing  $ \mathrm{BiBr}_3 $  in the perovskite layer, the SEM image of the perovskite film modified by different concentrations of  $ \mathrm{BiBr}_3 $  is shown in Fig. 2 (a)-(d). The morphology of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  perovskite without  $ \mathrm{BiBr}_3 $  shows relatively messy crystals with uneven grain size and many grain boundaries. According to previous reports, the grain boundaries may induce shallow states near the band edge of perovskite, hindering holes diffusion and therefore degrading the photoelectric conversion ability. [23, 24] With the introduction of  $ \mathrm{BiBr}_3 $  and the increase of concentrations, the fabricated film becomes more compact with large crystalline size. Because of the different electrochemical property of  $ \mathrm{Bi}^{3+} $ , it may change the bonding of the ions during annealing, which influencing the morphology obtained at last. We applied the particle size statistics for SEM images, as is shown in Fig. 2 (e)-(h). The average grain size is  $ 0.96\mu \mathrm{m} $  (without  $ \mathrm{BiBr}_3 $ ),  $ 1.27\mu \mathrm{m} $  (with  $ 0.01\mathrm{mg / mL BiBr}_3 $ ),  $ 1.53\mu \mathrm{m} $  (with  $ 0.1\mathrm{mg / mL BiBr}_3 $ ),  $ 0.95\mu \mathrm{m} $  (with  $ 1\mathrm{mg / mL BiBr}_3 $ ), respectively. However, when the concentration is further increased, the pinholes appear, resulting in a decrease in the coverage of the perovskite.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary,  $ \mathrm{BiBr}_3 $ , as an additive in  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  perovskite, promotes to form a small amount of  $ \mathrm{CsPb_2Br_5} $ , which not only improves the crystal quality of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $ , but also adjusts energy level and improves carrier extraction. As the results, the  $ \mathrm{J_{SC}} $  and PCE of the solar cell device are improved. It can also maintain the stability and low-cost advantages of the original  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  solar cell, and has a wide application prospect. It also proposes an effective method for interface engineering of perovskite solar cells.
