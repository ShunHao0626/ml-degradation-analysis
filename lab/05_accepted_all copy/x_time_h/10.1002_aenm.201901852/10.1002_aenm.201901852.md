# Efficient Passivation with Lead Pyridine-2-Carboxylic for High-Performance and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Sheng Fu, Xiaodong Li, Li Wan, Yulei Wu, Wenxiao Zhang, Yueming Wang, Qinye Bao, and Junfeng Fang*

# 1. Introduction

Stability has become the main obstacle for the commercialization of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) despite the impressive power conversion efficiency (PCE). Poor crystallization and ion migration of perovskite are the major origins of its degradation under working condition. Here, high-performance PSCs incorporated with pyridine-2-carboxylic lead salt   $  \left(\mathrm{PbPyA}_{2}\right)  $   are fabricated. The pyridine and carboxyl groups on   $  PbPyA_{2}  $   can not only control crystallization but also passivate grain boundaries (GBs), which result in the high-quality perovskite film with larger grains and fewer defects. In addition, the strong interaction among the hydrophobic   $  PbPyA_{2}  $   molecules and perovskite GBs acts as barriers to ion migration and component volatilization when exposed to external stresses. Consequently, superior optoelectronic perovskite films with improved thermal and moisture stability are obtained. The resulting device shows a champion efficiency of 19.96% with negligible hysteresis. Furthermore, thermal (90 °C) and moisture (RH 40–60%) stability are improved threefold, maintaining 80% of initial efficiency after aging for 480 h. More importantly, the doped device exhibits extraordinary improvement of operational stability and remains 93% of initial efficiency under maximum power point (MPP) tracking for 540 h.

The most challenges in the standard stability tests of PSCs are thermal and operational stabilities, which are key issues under real working condition of solar cells and cannot be overcome even by encapsulation strategy.  $  ^{[21,22]}  $   At elevated temperature, the volatility of methylamine (MA) cations leads to the thermal decomposition of methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) perovskite. In addition, light-induced effect on ions distribution can result in phase segregation and poor operational stability. Both of the thermal and operational instability have close relation with grain boundaries (GBs). On one hand, compared with the interior of perovskite grains, the composition at perovskite GBs is mainly terminated by the volatile I  $  ^{-}  $   and MA  $  ^{+}  $   ions in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films, which is easy to be eroded and accelerate its degradation.  $  ^{[23,24]}  $   On the other hand, the GBs have been proved as dominant channels of ions migration.  $  ^{[25]}  $   The low ions migration activation energy (  $  E_{A}  $  ) has provided perovskite many observed unusual phenomena, such as current–voltage hysteresis, light-induced phase separation, and photoinduced self-poling effect.  $  ^{[26]}  $   Ions migrations could also lead to the thermal and operational instabilities of perovskite.

Organic metal halide perovskite materials have attracted great attention in various photoelectric fields owing to their long range carrier diffusion, ideal direct energy band, bipolar transport property, high defect tolerance, and low temperature solution processability, especially in photovoltaic field.  $  ^{[1-6]}  $   The certified efficiency of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has exceeded 24% within the past few years, which is comparable against the silicon-based solar cells. Despite the impressive high efficiency, the stability of PSCs lags far behind the requirement for future commercialization.  $  ^{[3,4,7,8]}  $   In comparison with silicon, organic–inorganic halide perovskite is endowed with soft crystal lattice (low formation energy) and prone to decompose when exposed to oxygen, ultraviolet (UV) illumination, hyperthermia, or electric tension.  $  ^{[3,9,10]}  $   Efforts have been carried out to improve the stability of PSCs such as solvent engineering,  $  ^{[11-13]}  $   interface engineering,  $  ^{[14,15]}  $   composition engineering,

To date, molecular additives with functional groups interacting with component of perovskite have been proved as a feasible method to prepare high-quality perovskite films and mitigate the GBs problems. Many volatile molecules have been introduced to retard the crystal growth and minimize the contents of GBs based on the Lewis acid-based theory, such as   $  H_{2}O  $  ,  $  ^{[27]}  $   dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO),  $  ^{[12]}  $  N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP),  $  ^{[28]}  $   and pyridine.  $  ^{[29]}  $   There are also many studies on nonvolatile small molecules or polymer additives with similar groups to passivate GBs for high-performance PSCs.  $  ^{[30-32]}  $   Our group employed in-situ cross-linking methods to stitch GBs and significantly enhanced operational and moisture stability.  $  ^{[33,34]}  $   In recently, Yang et al employed F-PDI as additive to promote crystal growth and suppress ions migration, which realized high thermal and moisture stability.  $  ^{[35]}  $   However, these organic additives contribute the weak interactions (intermolecular force or hydrogen bond) with composition around GBs. To further prolong the lifetime of PSCs, introducing strong binding action between the additive and perovskite composition is an urgent need, such as the ions bond and stable chemical interaction.

Herein, we introduce an organic lead salt  $ \mathrm{PbPyA_2} $  (Figure 1a) as passivator in one-step antisolvent solution prepared MAPbI $ _3 $  films. The  $ \mathrm{PbPyA_2} $  molecules containing pyridine and carboxyl groups, can strongly interact with the cations in the perovskite precursor, which attributed to the high-quality film preparation

with reduced trap density and prolonged carrier lifetime.  $  ^{[36,37]}  $   The resulting device shows the champion efficiency up to 19.96% with negligible hysteresis. In parallel, the nonvolatile   $  PbPyA_{2}  $   located around GBs can suppress ions migration and increase the hydrophobicity of perovskite for the efficient chemical passivation. Consequently, both thermal and moisture stability are significantly improved. The tolerance of humidity (RH 40–60% for 480 h) and heat (90 °C for 480 h) show nearly threefold improvement compared to the control (without additives). Moreover, the doped device exhibits exceptional operational stability and remains 93% of its initial efficiency under light soaking over the MPP for 540 h, which is one of the most excellent operational stability in the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   based solar cells to our best knowledge. This work proves that passivating the GBs with strong chemical interaction is an efficient channel for high-efficiency and stable PSCs.

High-quality film suggests the low trap density and excellent photoelectrical properties. First, we conduct the space charge limited current (SCLC) examinations with a structure of indium tin oxide (ITO)/P3CT-N/ Perovskite/Au to assess the trap density in the films. As shown in Figure 2c, the devices exhibit ohmic response (linear relation) at low bias and a marked increase of the current injection at the intermediate region since the traps are continuously filled with increasing bias until reaching the trap-filling limit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{TFL}}) $ . And the defects density is calculated from the  $ V_{\mathrm{TFL}} $  based on Equation (1)[44]

$$
n _ {\mathrm{t}} = 2 \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0} V _ {\mathrm{TFL}} / e L ^ {2}
$$

(1)

where   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative dielectric constant of perovskite,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity, e is the electron charge, and L is the thickness of perovskite film. The calculated   $  n_{t}  $   of the control and   $  PbPyA_{2}  $   doped film are   $  1.35 \times 10^{16}  $   and   $  8.83 \times 10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  , respectively. This result directly proves that the additive can lower the trap density.

The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL (TRPL) measurements are performed to study the charge carrier recombination dynamic. The PL intensity of doped perovskite exhibits significant improvement compared with the control (Figure 2d). Simultaneously, the peak position reveals blueshift from 770 to  $ 766\mathrm{nm} $  after incorporation with  $ \mathrm{PbPyA_2} $ . Similar shift is observed in the ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) spectroscopy (Figure S6, Supporting Information) and a small blueshift of band energy occurs when adding  $ \mathrm{PbPyA_2} $ . These results further confirm that  $ \mathrm{PbPyA_2} $  can efficiently minimize trap density and passivate the GBs.[45] Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) is conducted to investigate the electronic structure changes (Figure S7, Supporting Information). After incorporating with  $ \mathrm{PbPyA_2} $ , the work function and conductive energy level both shift to higher binding energy, which indicate that the doped film exhibits more N-type nature and the higher open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $ ) for the related PSCs.[42] We also monitor the PL mapping of the control and doped film with area for  $ 100~{\mu\mathrm{m}}^2 $  (Figure S8a,b, Supporting Information) to investigate the PL intensity distribution. The image of doped film shows the uniform and strong emission while the control film exhibits low emission intensity and uneven distribution, which is well consistent with the steady-state PL measurements. The low trap density could elongate the lifetime of photogenerated carriers and TRPL results are fitted by bi-exponential function of time (Table S1, Supporting Information). The fitted results reveal double enhancement of average lifetime from 55.17 to 124.97 ns after incorporation, which further demonstrate the

lower defects and better photoelectrical properties with the presence of   $  PbPyA_{2}  $  . Moisture instability is also the drawback of perovskite and previous reports have revealed that the water invades toward perovskite mainly through the GBs and surface.  $  ^{[52]}  $   In comparison to the perovskite surface, GBs containing a sizable concentration of defect and opening the tunnels for diffusion of moisture, are more prone to be attacked. PbPyA  $  _{2}  $   molecule is composed by the chelating of Pb  $  ^{2+}  $   ions and pyridine-2-carboxylic ions with the hydrophobic group toward outside, which can increase the hydrophobicity of perovskite (Figure S10a, Supporting Information). In addition, it has proved that the strong interaction can inhibit the reactivity between ambient water molecules and perovskite grains. Compared with the control film, the doped film exhibits more excellent environment stability at 60% humidity ambient condition (Figure S10b,c, Supporting Information). The control film exhibits the

MAPbI $ _3 $ ·H $ _2 $ O (at 10.54°) $ ^{[53]} $  at the fourth day and with conspicuous PbI $ _2 $  peak (around 12.7°) at 14 d while the doped film exhibits negligible peak of PbI $ _2 $  after 14 d. The SEM results (Figure S10d,e, Supporting Information) are in good agreement with the XRD measurements. After applying the same operation, the doped film retains the unchanged morphology, whereas the reference film presents the obvious decomposition around GBs. These results suggest that the hydrophobic PbPyA $ _2 $  with strong interaction can protect the perovskite from eroding by the humidity.

# 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, we have demonstrated that a highly efficient and stable PSCs are enabled with   $  PbPyA_{2}  $   additive. The strong interaction between   $  PbPyA_{2}  $   and perovskite can efficiently control the grain growth and passivate the defects, which benefits for the preparation of high-quality perovskite film with lower trap density and superior photoelectric properties. As a consequence, the champion PCE is improved up to 19.96% with negligible hysteresis. Meanwhile, the strong chemical bonding to GBs also serves as barriers to ions migration and components volatilization for the perovskite films. Besides, the bidentate-anchor   $  PbPyA_{2}  $   molecules facilitate to enhance the hydrophobicity of perovskite film and inhibit the reactivity between ambient water molecules and perovskite grains. The tolerances of heat and humidity are both significantly improved after incorporated with   $  PbPyA_{2}  $ . The PSCs fabricated with doped films show near threefold improvement for the thermal (90 °C) and moisture (RH 40–60%) stability, remaining 80% and 84.1% of initial efficiency after aging for 480 h, respectively.
