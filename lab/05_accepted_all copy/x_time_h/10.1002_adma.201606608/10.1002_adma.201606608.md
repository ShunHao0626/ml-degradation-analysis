# SrCl  $  _{2}  $   Derived Perovskite Facilitating a High Efficiency of 16% in Hole-Conductor-Free Fully Printable Mesoscopic Perovskite Solar Cells

Hua Zhang, Huan Wang, Spencer T. Williams, Dehua Xiong, Wenjun Zhang, Chu-Chen Chueh, Wei Chen,* and Alex K.-Y. Jen*

Despite the breakthrough of over 22% power conversion efficiency demonstrated in organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PVSCs), critical concerns pertaining to the instability and toxicity still remain that may potentially hinder their commercialization. In this study, a new chemical approach using environmentally friendly strontium chloride (  $  SrCl_{2}  $  ) as a precursor for perovskite preparation is demonstrated to result in enhanced device performance and stability of the derived hole-conductor-free printable mesoscopic PVSCs. The   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite is chemically modified by introducing   $  SrCl_{2}  $   in the precursor solution. The results from structural, elemental, and morphological analyses show that the incorporation of   $  SrCl_{2}  $   affords the formation of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}(SrCl_{2})_{x}  $   perovskites endowed with lower defect concentration and better pore filling in the derived mesoscopic PVSCs. The optimized compositional   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}(SrCl_{2})_{0.1}  $   perovskite can substantially enhance the photovoltaic performance of the derived hole-conductor-free device to 15.9%, outperforming the value (13.0%) of the pristine   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   device. More importantly, the stability of the device in ambient air under illumination is also improved.

In this work, we report a simple one-step solution processing strategy to fabricate a new compositional perovskite   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}(SrCl_{2})_{x}  $   (denoted as   $  MAPbI_{3}(SrCl_{2})_{x}  $   hereafter), wherein eco-friendly strontium chloride (  $  SrCl_{2}  $  ) is employed as a precursory inorganic source.   $  Sr^{2+}  $   is very different from   $  Pb^{2+}  $   in terms of how it builds bonds with halogens.  $  ^{[29]}  $   The strong iconicity of Sr combined with the spherical symmetry of its empty valence s-orbital leads to highly nondirectional bonding. This is evidenced by the coordination numbers of   $  SrCl_{2}  $   and   $  SrI_{2}  $ , which are 8 and 7, respectively. Pb, on the other hand, supports highly directional coordination by halogens. In   $  PbCl_{2}  $ ,   $  PbBr_{2}  $ ,   $  PbI_{2}  $ , and perovskites based on these starting materials, Pb is always octahedrally coordinated by halogens with a coordination number of 6. This strong tendency toward octahedral coordination, even when more halogens are added to the system, is exactly what structurally makes possible organic/inorganic lead halide-based perovskites. The seemingly subtle difference in coordination between Sr and Pb means that the incorporation of   $  Sr^{2+}  $   into   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite is likely complex and proceeds through multiple competing mechanisms.

Previously, it has been found that perovskite grain structure is complex and even contains amorphous domains.  $  ^{[30]}  $   It has also been found that more highly ionic metal cations like   $  Al^{3+}  $   preferentially modify grain boundaries.  $  ^{[31]}  $   Based on the divalent charge of   $  Sr^{2+}  $   and these considerations, we propose that   $  Sr^{2+}  $   primarily occupies Pb sites both at grain boundaries and to a lesser extent within grains themselves (Figure 1a). This is supported by our X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) studies which show that at the surface,   $  Sr^{2+}  $   and   $  Cl^{-}  $  species incorporated in the resulting perovskite film follow their stoichiometric composition in precursor solution. Despite the retention of Cl, the MAPbCl  $  _{3}  $   phase that often results from an overburden of Cl in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $    $  ^{[32]}  $   is conspicuously and consistently absent.

This is very different from the case of MACl/PbCl $ _2 $ -assisted perovskite crystallization. As has been shown, iodide intrinsically has a strong tendency to replace Cl within the perovskite film leading to almost complete loss of chloride. $ ^{[33-35]} $  The modification of this intrinsic propensity for substitution shows that Sr plays an important and lasting role within the film, regardless of where the ion primarily resides. This modulation of crystallization kinetics by SrCl $ _2 $  endowed perovskite films with more compact morphology, lower defect concentration, and better pore filling inside the mesoscopic scaffold. These advantages introduced by SrCl $ _2 $ , benefit the hole-conductor-free

MAPbI $ _3 $ (SrCl $ _2 $ ) $ _x $  PVSC consisting of fluorine doped tin oxide (FTO)/c-TiO $ _2 $ /m-TiO $ _2 $ /m-Al $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /m-Carbon (Figure 1b and the corresponding energy level diagram is depicted in Figure 1c) showing that it can achieve a remarkably enhanced PCE of 15.9%, outperforming the value (13.0%) obtained from the pristine MAPbI $ _3 $  device; moreover, the stability in ambient air under illumination is also improved.

In this study, the mixed metal, Pb-based perovskite films were fabricated by one-step solution process, for which the precursor solution comprises a mixture of MAI,   $  PbI_{2}  $  , and   $  SrCl_{2}  $  . Varied content of   $  SrCl_{2}  $   in the precursor solution was investigated, wherein the molar ratio of   $  PbI_{2}  $   to   $  SrCl_{2}  $   ranged from 1:0 to 1:0.3 with a fixed stoichiometric ratio (1:1) of   $  PbI_{2}  $   and MAI sources. Accordingly, compositional   $  MAPbI_{3}(SrCl_{2})_{x}  $   perovskites (  $  0 \leq x \leq 0.3  $  ) (denoted by the used stoichiometry in precursor solution) can be prepared.

The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the prepared perovskite films are shown in Figure S1a (Supporting Information), wherein all the films showed a simplex  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3(\mathrm{SrCl}_2)_x $  phase with a preferred orientation along the (110) direction. Their corresponding full width at half maximum (FWHM) of (110) characteristic was compared in Figure S1b (Supporting Information). As presented, the FWHM clearly changed as varying the content of constituent  $ \mathrm{SrCl}_2 $ . Interestingly, the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3(\mathrm{SrCl}_2)_{0.1} $  film possessed a small FWHM value than other compositions. The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) of these films shown in Figure S2 (Supporting Information) not only affirmed the small FWHM observed in  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3(\mathrm{SrCl}_2)_{0.1} $  film but also revealed its better quality film formation with full surface coverage over the entire substrate. Thus, the optimized blending ratio of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  to  $ \mathrm{SrCl}_2 $  in the precursor solution was found to be 1:0.1, constituting a compositional  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3(\mathrm{SrCl}_2)_{0.1} $  perovskite for further case study.

It has been reported for PVSCs based on mesoscopic structure that the degree of loading of perovskite within the mesoscopic scaffold is critical in influencing device performance.  $  ^{[45-47]}  $   Given the improved wetting ability demonstrated earlier for   $  MAPbI_{3}(SrCl_{2})_{0.1}  $  , it can be expected that the mesoscopic scaffolds will be filled with   $  MAPbI_{3}(SrCl_{2})_{0.1}  $   perovskite more efficiently than the pristine perovskite. This is confirmed by the corresponding cross-sectional SEM images. As shown in Figure 3b, the   $  MAPbI_{3}(SrCl_{2})_{0.1}  $   perovskite within the mesoscopic scaffolds is dense and continuous, whilst the one filled with pristine perovskite shows substantial voids, which will deteriorate the resulting device performance. Through the energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy of a device cross-section, thorough penetration of perovskite from top carbon electrode, center alumina scaffold, to bottom   $  TiO_{2}  $   electron-transporting layer was demonstrated. As shown by the energy-dispersive X-ray (EDS), both Sr and Cl were distributed homogenously with Pb and I throughout the entire film. These improved loading and optoelectronic properties of   $  MAPbI_{3}(SrCl_{2})_{0.1}  $   will be conducive in enhancing the performance of the derived device.

Based on the above observations, we have further verified the efficacy of   $  MAPbI_{3}(SrCl_{2})_{0.1}  $   as the photovoltaic material in the hole-conductor-free fully printable mesoscopic PVSC with a configuration of   $  FTO/c-TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}/m-Al_{2}O_{3}/m-Carbon  $   (Figure 1b). This device architecture has been demonstrated to show superior ambient stability in the literature.  $  ^{[46-48]}  $   Photocurrent density versus voltage (J-V) characteristics under standard AM 1.5G illumination were carried out to assess the device performance. As shown in Figure 4a, the J-V curves measured under both reverse and forward scan for the champion devices based on the pristine and   $  MAPbI_{3}(SrCl_{2})_{0.1}  $   films were illustrated, and their corresponding photovoltaic parameters were summarized in Table 2.

It is evident from Figure 4a that PVSC based on  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3(\mathrm{SrCl}_2)_{0.1} $  film exhibits a superior performance with negligible hysteresis. As shown in Table 2, an optimized  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based PVSC measured under the reverse scan gave a PCE of  $ 13.0\% $ , with an open-circuit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{OC}}) $  of  $ 0.95\mathrm{V} $ , a short-circuit photocurrent density  $ (J_{\mathrm{SC}}) $  of  $ 19.50\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , and a fill factor (FF) of 0.70 while the corresponding  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ ,  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ , and FF values obtained under the forward scan were  $ 0.93\mathrm{V} $ ,  $ 19.46\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , and 0.67, respectively, showing a pronounced  $ 6.5\% $  discrepancy in overall PCE. By contrast, the PCEs of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3(\mathrm{SrCl}_2)_{0.1} $  PVSCs measured under reverse and forward scans were  $ 15.9\% $  and  $ 15.8\% $ , respectively, showing a much lower discrepancy of  $ 0.1\% $ . The average  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ ,  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ , and FF of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3(\mathrm{SrCl}_2)_{0.1} $ -based PVSCs were  $ 1.05\mathrm{V} $ ,  $ 20.18\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ ,

and 0.75, corresponding to an overall PCE of 15.9%. This striking performance is among the highest values recorded for the hole-conductor-free fully printable mesoscopic PVSCs, especially for   $  V_{OC}  $  , which is comparable to the values (1.05–1.10 V) obtained in the planar heterojunction PVSCs.  $  ^{[9-11,49,50]}  $

The greatly enhanced   $  V_{OC}  $   can be attributed to the improved quality of   $  \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3}(\mathrm{SrCl}_{2})_{0.1}  $   film, which possesses less defects and excellent pore-filling as shown earlier. These combined improvements result in more efficient charge carrier generation and transport through the well-interconnected perovskite networks. Similar enhancement has been recently observed in the pristine   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   system, wherein a simple room temperature pyridine vapor treatment was applied to greatly improve the quality of fabricated film.  $  ^{[51]}  $   To clarify this, the dynamics of charge transfer and recombination of the fabricated devices were then investigated via the dependence of the   $  V_{OC}  $   on light intensity. As shown in Figure 4b, the plot of   $  V_{OC}  $   versus log-scaled light intensity is fitted linearly with a slope of   $  1.43 k_{B}T/q  $   and   $  1.69 k_{B}T/q  $   (where   $  k_{B}  $   is the Boltzmann constant, T is absolute temperature, and q is elementary charge) for the   $  \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3}(\mathrm{SrCl}_{2})_{0.1}  $   and pristine-based PVSCs, respectively. In principle, the slope of   $  V_{OC}  $   versus light intensity is more approximate to   $  k_{B}T/q  $   when less Shockley–Read–Hall (SRH) recombination is involved in the device operation.  $  ^{[52,53]}  $   Thus, the   $  \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3}(\mathrm{SrCl}_{2})_{0.1}  $   PVSCs possesses a smaller slope is an indicator of less trap sites exist in the film, which results in the minimized nonradiative recombination losses and a higher   $  V_{OC}  $  .

Figure 4c shows the incident photon to current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra for both champion devices. Apparently, the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3(\mathrm{SrCl}_2)_{0.1} $ -based PVSC exhibits more intensely spectral response in the range from 500 to  $ 800~\mathrm{nm} $  compared to that of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based device. This indicates that the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3(\mathrm{SrCl}_2)_{0.1} $  film with denser morphology harvests light more efficiently, especially for the long-wavelength region, resulting in an increased  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  from 19.48 to  $ 20.18\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ . This augmentation is further verified by the integrated  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ , which is determined from the IPCE spectrum and also included in Figure 4c. Eventually, the combined increase of  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ ,  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ , as well as FF endowed by  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3(\mathrm{SrCl}_2)_{0.1} $  film subsequently enhances the average PCE of devices from  $ 12.6\% $  to  $ 15.9\% $ .

Shown in Figure 4d is the steady-state power output measured at the maximum power point of both PVSCs, wherein the PCEs measured under a constant voltage bias at the maximum power-point are plotted as a function of time. It clearly presents a very rapid response to bias with a stable power output for both devices. More importantly, these stable power output values are consistent with those obtained from the  $ J - V $  measurements, suggesting the accuracy and reliability of the measured performance. To illustrate the reproducibility of these high-efficiency PVSCs, Tables S1 and S2 (Supporting Information) depicted the statistical data obtained from ten devices. The mean value of PCE for the pristine film was  $ 11.5 \pm 1.0\% $  while it increased to  $ 14.9 \pm 0.8\% $  for the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3(\mathrm{SrCl}_2)_{0.1} $ -based PVSCs, as shown in Figure 4e.

We have developed a new type of compositional perovskite,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3(\mathrm{SrCl}_2)_{0.1} $ , by using eco-friendly  $ \mathrm{SrCl}_2 $  as a precursory inorganic source. The incorporation of  $ \mathrm{SrCl}_2 $  enables the tuning of the perovskite crystallization kinetics and facilitates the film growth with inhibited defect generation. This result provides an effective strategy for developing highly efficient and stable PVSCs for commercialization.

# Experimental Section

Materials: Unless stated otherwise, all reagents and solvents were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used as received.

Characterization of the Perovskite Films: The perovskite films were fabricated by the conventional spin-coating technology. The structural analyses of the as-prepared films were performed by XRD measurements in the range from   $  10^{\circ}  $   to   $  80^{\circ}  $   with a step-size of   $  0.02^{\circ}  $   via a Philips X-ray diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation. XPS and UPS measurements were carried out on a Kratos Ultra Spectrometer (AXIS–ULTRA DLD–600W) using monochromatized Al Kα X-ray photons with energy of 1486.6 eV and a discharge lamp (21.2 eV) for XPS and UPS (He I), respectively. The absorbance spectra were obtained from a Shimadzu UV/vis 3600 spectrophotometer with an integrating sphere. Steady-state PL spectrum was obtained from Horiba Jobin Yvon system with an excitation laser beam at 532 nm. The morphological characterizations were investigated by SEM (Zeiss Supra 40) equipped with an EDS detector.

Device Fabrication: Glass substrates coated with prepatterned FTO (Pilkington TEC 15,  $ 15\Omega \mathrm{sq}^{-1} $ ) were cleaned by sequential ultrasonication in a detergent solution, deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol. A  $ 30~\mathrm{nm} $  thick compact layer of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  was then deposited on the cleaned FTO by spray pyrolysis of a solution involving the titanium di-isopropoxide-bis(acetylacetonate) diluted in ethanol (1:25, v/v) at  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  with oxygen as carrier gas. To promote the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  crystallization, it is essential to hold the film at  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for additional  $ 30\mathrm{min} $ . After cooling to room temperature, a  $ 400~\mathrm{nm} $  thick mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer composed of  $ 30~\mathrm{nm} $  sized particles was produced by screen-printing a  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  paste (DSL 30NR-T, Dyesol, Australia, diluted with terpinol, 1:3 mass ratio). After drying at  $ 125^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 5\mathrm{min} $ , the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film was programmed sintering to  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $ . The same procedure was applied to prepare a  $ 500~\mathrm{nm} $  thick space layer using the  $ \mathrm{Al_2O_3} $  paste. Finally, a carbon black/graphite counter electrode with

the thickness of about 8 μm was screen-printed on the top of Al₂O₃ layer and then sintered at 450 °C for 30 min. The device was subsequently completed by infiltrating the 1.2 M perovskite precursor solution via drop casting on top of the carbon counter electrode and then dried at 70 °C for 15 min. A series of content of SrCl₂ in the 1.2 M perovskite precursor solution was prepared according to the stoichiometric ratio 1:1 for PbI₂ and MAI and then varying the molar ratio of PbI₂ to SrCl₂ from 1:0.1 to 1:0.3. For example, the 1.2 M CH₃NH₃PbI₃(SrCl₂)₀.₁ precursor solution was prepared by dissolving MAI (1.2 × 10⁻³ M), PbI₂ (1.2 × 10⁻³ M), and SrCl₂ (0.1 × 10⁻³ M) in 1 mL N,N-dimethyl formamide (DMF) solvent with stirring at 70 °C for 2 h. The resulting devices were measured with a nonrefractive mask with aperture of 0.16 cm² to define the illumination area to be 0.16 cm² to ensure accuracy of measurements.

Device Characterization: The J-V curves were measured under simulated AM 1.5 sunlight at 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  irradiance generated by a solar simulator with a Xe light source (450 W, Oriel, model 9119) and an AM 1.5G filter (Oriel, model 91192). The IPCE spectra were measured by applying a white-light bias of 10% solar intensity onto the sample with a monochromatic incident light modulated at 10 Hz. The steady-state power output of devices were performed on a Keithley 2400 Source Meter with the illumination of a 450 W Oriel xenon lamp under an AM 1.5G condition.
