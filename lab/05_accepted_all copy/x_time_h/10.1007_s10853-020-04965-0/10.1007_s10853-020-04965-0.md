# ABSTRACT

Interface passivation plays a significant role in reducing surface defects in semiconductor materials. In this paper, we present a kind of  $ \mathrm{Al_2O_3} $ -passivated nickel oxide (NiO) film as hole transport layer for the perovskite solar cells (PSCs). The optimal  $ \mathrm{Al_2O_3} $  insulating film between the NiO and perovskite active layers has a capability to decrease the defect states density at the surface of NiO and suppress the carriers recombination at NiO/perovskite interface. The passivation process could apparently improve the efficiencies of corresponding PSCs from 5.64 to  $ 7.12\% $ . Steady-state photoluminescence and transient time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) results demonstrate the restraint of charge recombination at the NiO/perovskite interface with passivation. The Hall effect test further shows that the electrical properties of the NiO film can be improved by adjusting the precursor concentration of the  $ \mathrm{Al_2O_3} $ , which has a certain significance for the application of interface engineering in PSCs. Furthermore, this method has the advantages of simple operation, low cost, and strong reproducibility, which is also good for the working stability of PSCs.

# Introduction

Perovskite solar cell was first reported by Miyasaka in 2009, performing an efficiency of 3.9%. Just a few years later, the efficiency increased to a maximum of 25.2% [1–3]. Organic–inorganic halide perovskite had captured much attention in recent years due to its optional band gap, high absorption coefficient, low

exciton binding energy, and high carrier mobility, which had gradually become an excellent material for the third-generation solar cells   $  [4–10]  $  . However, the recombination of carriers at interface is still a serious hinder in the development of PSCs. Interface defect states are often considered as the main cause for interfacial recombination, hysteresis effects, and carrier lifetime reduction   $  [11–15]  $  .

Three methods are believed to be capable for analyzing and reducing interface recombination. The first method is to reduce the defect density of the matrix material by adding a dopant. For example, Giordano et al. demonstrated that lithium dopant in   $  TiO_{2}  $   electron transport layer could passivate the trap state in the   $  TiO_{2}  $   lattice, and enhanced the open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) from 1.04 to 1.114 V [16]. The second method is to use carrier transport materials with lower defect density to form a better interface. Correa Baena replaced the commonly used   $  TiO_{2}  $   electron transport layer with   $  SnO_{2}  $ , achieving a lower hysteresis effect and a higher   $  V_{oc}  $   (1.14 V) [17]. The third one is to reduce density of defect states between the carrier transport layer and the perovskite material by introducing an ultra-thin passivation layer. White et al. introduced an ultra-thin hybrid PMMA: PCBM between the electron transport layer (ETL) and the perovskite, which effectively suppressed interfacial carrier recombination and significantly reduced the hysteresis effect [18]. Therefore, the introduction of passivation is significant in reducing interface defects.

Passivation is necessary at the location of ETL/perovskite interface, perovskite grain boundary, and HTL/perovskite interface in perovskite solar cell. In the ETL/perovskite interface, an ultra-thin PCBM was successfully introduced for passivation   $  [19]  $  . Li et al. found that the improvement on FF and   $  J_{sc}  $   could be obtained after the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL was passivated by   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $     $  [20]  $  . Beloqui reported that the   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   insulating layer was used in the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   layer and the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer, both of which favored the interface contact   $  [21]  $  . Some researchers also used insulating organic materials to reduce the surface defect state by chemical reaction with the surface of the ETL   $  [22–24]  $  . Sargent et al. reported a contact passivation method that used chlorine-coated   $  TiO_{2}  $   colloidal nanocrystalline thin films to mitigate interfacial recombination in planar   $  PSC_{S}  $   and achieved excellent photovoltaic performance (PCE up to 20.1%)   $  [25]  $  . Controlling carrier trapping induced by defects is an extremely important issue in the development of high-performance solar cells. The perovskite film must present a low carrier defect density at either the grain boundary or the interface with the charge transport layers. In terms of passivation at the grain boundary of the perovskite material itself, some researchers found that perovskite (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  ) can be self-passivated by

unreacted excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   [26]. Simultaneously, compared with   $  PbI_{2}  $ , excess   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   not only exhibited surface passivation by cooperation with Pb, but also reacted with   $  H_{2}O  $   to form the perovskite derivative   $  MAPbI_{2}(OH)  $ , which was effective against high-humidity environments and high stability at high humidity (65%) [27]. Researchers also studied the HTL/perovskite interface passivation. Flynn et al. explored the method of targeted atomic deposition (TAD) to deposit B atom on the surface of NiO materials and demonstrated the passivating mechanism of B atom [12]. Some researchers used small molecules, for example diethanolamine (DEA), to modify the surface of NiO nanocrystalline film, achieving an efficient and stable (no hysteresis) device [11, 28].

Recently, we reported PSCs employed NiO nanocrystals film as hole transport layer   $  [29, 30]  $  . However, the fabricated devices presented a relatively low   $  V_{oc}  $  . Inspired by the passivation mechanism in solar cells, we conducted an ultra-thin   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   film formed by hydrolysis method to passivate NiO film in the inverted planar PSCs.   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   film exhibits an insulating electrochemical property and a wide band gap, which is suitable to passivate the front layer in PSCs. The introduction of the passivation layer improved the electrical properties of the NiO film by ion doping and decreased the charge recombination at NiO/perovskite interface. Compared to the unpassivated device, the   $  V_{oc}  $   was effectively increased. Moreover, the device with excellent stability and small hysteresis was obtained.

# Experimental section

# Synthesis of NiO and preparation of HTL film

NiO nanocrystals (NCs) were prepared via a non-injection method, as reported in our early paper   $  [29]  $  . Firstly,   $  \mathrm{Ni(St)_2}  $   (3 mmol), LiSt (1.2 mmol), and 1-octadecene (3 mol) were added to a three-neck flask and stirred for 2 h at 80 °C. Next, the reaction system was heated to 300 °C and further reacted under argon flow for 3 h. After that, the product was dispersed and extracted three times by hexane and methanol. The NiO NCs would precipitate out after staying for 12 h by adding ethyl acetate. Subsequently, the dispersion was centrifuged and washed three times

using the hexane and ethanol. After freeze drying, the product was dispersed in chlorobenzene solvent with a concentration of 20 mg/mL. Finally, the NiO HTL films were prepared by spin coating and further annealed at 340 °C for 30 min.

# Surface passivation with aluminum oxide

Aluminum sec-butoxide was used as the precursor for   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   deposition, which was diluted to 1, 2, and 3 mmol/L with isopropyl alcohol. The prepared NiO films were immersed in the above solution at 60 °C for 20 min and then washed by isopropyl alcohol. Finally, the dried samples were annealed at 300 °C for 30 min.

# The fabrication of perovskite solar cells

The perovskite layer was prepared by one-step and anti-solvent method. Firstly,   $  PbI_{2}  $   and MAI (1:1 molar ratio) were dissolved in 1 mL of DMSO to form a precursor solution. Secondly, 75   $  \mu  $  L of the precursor solution was spin-coated on the NiO film at 1000 rpm for 10 s and 5000 rpm for 30 s. After 10 s, the filtered toluene was dropped on the precursor layer. Finally, the perovskite film was annealed at 110 °C for 5 min and then at 130 °C for 10 min to form the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite layer. After the perovskite film was deposited on HTL,   $  PC_{61}BM  $   was spin-coated on the perovskite layer at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Then, 45   $  \mu  $  L of BCP was spin-coated on the   $  PC_{61}BM  $   layer at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, the contact electrode Ag was thermally evaporated through a mask to complete the preparation of the solar cell. The fabricated perovskite solar cell had a configuration of FTO/NiO NCs/(or   $  Al_{2}O_{3}-NiO  $   NCs)/MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /PCBM/BCP/Ag.

# Characterization

Field-emission scanning electron microscopy equipped with energy-dispersive spectrometer (FESEM-EDS, Hitachi SU8220) was used to investigate the morphology and composition of NiO- and AlOx-passivated NiO films. The crystal structure of NiO films was characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD, D8 Advance, Bruker). Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and transient time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements were performed by using a fluorescence spectrophotometer (Eclipse,

Cary) and time-resolved single photon counting (FLS 980, Edinburgh, UK). The Hall effect measurement (ezHEMS, UK) was used to further characterize the electrical properties of the passivated NiO film. The current density–voltage (J-V) curves of the PSCs were recorded by an electrochemical workstation (2420 Source Meter, Keithley, USA) and a solar simulator (Oriel Sol 3A, Newport, USA) under AM 1.5G solar illumination at   $  100 \, mW/cm^{2}  $   with a delay time of 40 ms. Forward and reverse scans were conducted from 0 to 1.2 V and 1.2 to 0 V using electrochemical workstation, respectively. The incident photon to current conversion efficiency (IPCE) characteristics were performed by the IPCE/QE system (Newport, USA). The time profile of the steady-state current density output was measured by defining the potential at the maximum output power point.

The result indicates that the component of passivation layer is  $ \gamma $ - $ \mathrm{Al}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3} $  with relatively poor crystallization, as shown in Fig. 1a. We further carried out XRD to analyze the composition of NiO films with or without passivation. When perovskite film is irradiated, electron-hole pairs are excited in perovskite layer. charge recombination by decreasing the contact chance between FTO and perovskite. Secondly, the passivation process could effectively decrease the defect state density on the surface of NiO film. The hysteresis effect is a common phenomenon in the measurement of PSCs under different scan directions. This effect could be induced by several factors, such as defects, ferroelectric effect, and interstitial ions   $  [35, 36]  $  . We carefully investigate the hysteresis effect behavior of PSCs based on NiO as a HTL with and without   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   passivation. The hysteresis factor n is introduced to measure the

hysteresis of the device   $  [38–40]  $  , which is calculated as follows. Considering the variation of device prepared by the same condition, we present a statistics result based on ten cells in each condition:

$$

$$

(3)

As shown in Fig. 7, the hysteresis factors of these devices after passivation are significantly smaller than that of the controlled one. The corresponding average hysteresis factors are 0.230, 0.058, 0.040, and 0.085 as the precursor concentrations are 0, 1, 2, and 3 mmol/L, respectively. In combination with fluorescence spectrum analysis, we believe that the reduction of hysteresis is mainly due to the reduction of defects at perovskite/NiO interface. The presence of interface defects will cause carriers to be trapped and accumulated at the interface, which will slow down the response to voltage. The introduction of the passivation layer effectively reduces the density of defect states at the perovskite/NiO interface, and the response sensitivity is improved, which further decrease the hysteresis effect.

# Working stability of PSCs

The light attenuation and working stability of PSCs were measured in ambient air (about 38% humidity,   $  20 \pm 5  $   °C). The light attenuation property of the best-performing PSC, passivated with 2 mmol/L   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   precursor solution, was recorded by applying a constant voltage, at which the device exhibits the highest power output. As can be seen from Fig. 8a, the current output of the device is very stable for 300 s under standard sunlight (AM 1.5) conditions. # Conclusion

We propose a facile method to improve the performance of inverted planar perovskite solar cells by using   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   as the passivation layer between NiO and perovskite films. The results reflect that the insert of   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   passivation layers can effectively reduce the density of defect states at the interface and improve the hole conduction. The passivation process could apparently enhance the PCE of PSCs from 5.64 to 7.12%. The   $  V_{oc}  $   and   $  J_{sc}  $   of the device with passivation both improve a lot, profiting from the current leakage decrease and charge recombination restraint at NiO/perovskite interface. The hysteresis effect is also suppressed successfully by improving the charge transfer process at NiO/perovskite interface. In addition, by optimization, the fabricated devices exhibit better light stability than unpassivated ones. This method is not only simple in operation, low in cost, good in repeatability, but also satisfying in passivation effect that is crucial for the application of interface engineering in PSCs.
