# Self-Induced Type-I Band Alignment at Surface Grain Boundaries for Highly Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Chao Luo, Yao Zhao, Xianjin Wang, Feng Gao, and Qing Zhao*

The open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is reported to be significantly weakened by carrier loss at the film surface. Here, the moisture condensation at only the upper surface of perovskite films is controlled by constructing an ultralow surface temperature. Then, type-I band alignment can be formed at the surface grain boundaries due to the etching effect of trace amounts of condensed moisture. The beneficially constructed surface type-I band alignment can effectively repel carriers and return them to the inside of the grain, significantly avoiding the carrier loss at films surface. As a result, a superior carrier lifetime exceeding 2.5   $  \mu  $  s is obtained and the   $  V_{OC}  $   of PSC is remarkably boosted from 1.07 to 1.17 V. The minimum   $  V_{OC}  $   deficit of only 0.39 V enables a substantial gain in power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 20.2% to 22.4% in one-step spin-coating methods. Moreover, this innovation is versatile and a champion PCE of 23.2% is also achieved in two-step spin-coating methods.

Although the in situ formed   $  PbI_{2}  $   can indeed effectively enhance the photoelectric properties of perovskite, it has been also found a trade-off between PCE and stability of PSCs, due to the photodegradation of   $  PbI_{2}  $   will introduce undesired voids.  $  ^{[31,33,34,40]}  $   In principle, benefiting from the superb light absorption ability of perovskite, this dilemma can be thoroughly avoided if   $  PbI_{2}  $   is located at the interface of perovskite/hole-transport layer (HTL) because vast majority of light has been absorbed by perovskite when it reaches   $  PbI_{2}  $   (see Note S1, Supporting Information, for details).

Type-I band alignment is a heterojunction formed by a lower-bandgap phase and another phase with higher conduction band (CB) bottom and lower valence band (VB) top. Since there are electron and hole transport barriers from lower-bandgap phase to higher-bandgap phase, both electrons and holes will be efficiently repelled and return to the low-bandgap phase.  $  ^{[27]}  $   The nonradiative recombination defects of perovskite films are mainly located at surface and grain boundaries,  $  ^{[41-44]}  $   especially the interface of perovskite/HTL.  $  ^{[21,45]}  $   Therefore, if the type-I band alignment (formed by low-bandgap perovskite and high-bandgap PbI  $  _{2}  $  ) could be constructed at the upper surface grain boundaries of perovskite film, it is expected that the notorious carrier loss at grain boundaries will be greatly reduced.

Herein,   $  PbI_{2}  $   has been successfully managed to be located at only the upper surface grain boundaries of perovskite film based on the control of moisture condensation at low temperature. As a result, a self-induced type-I band alignment was formed at the   $  PbI_{2}  $  /perovskite surface grain boundary (IPSG). The in situ formed type-I band alignment can effectively avoid the loss of carriers at interface, promote the extraction of carriers at HTL/perovskite interface. Subsequently, the   $  V_{OC}  $   was remarkably enhanced by 100 mV from 1.0 to 1.17 V. Accordingly, the PCE of PSC was boosted from 20.2% to 22.4% in the one-step spin-coating method. Moreover, this strategy is universal that can also work efficiently in the two-step spin-coating method. More importantly, the previously intractable problems of stability deterioration caused by the light-induced decomposition of   $  PbI_{2}  $   could be eliminated based on reasonable control of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   distribution. A storage stability of 2000 h and light stability of 500 h were achieved in the fabricated PSCs. The novel design shed new light on efficient and universal strategy development to further improve the   $  V_{OC}  $   and PCE of PSCs.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The conceived in situ type-I band alignment at the upper surface grain boundaries of perovskite film was achieved by using liquid nitrogen to condensate moisture in the air to etch the surface lattice of the perovskite. In detail, since the temperature of liquid nitrogen is  $ -196^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , it will cause the surrounding temperature to drop rapidly, so the moisture in the atmosphere rapidly condenses near it. As shown in Figure S1 in the Supporting Information, a large amount of frost and ice will soon form around the container of liquid nitrogen. After the perovskite film was inserted into liquid nitrogen for one time (about  $ 0.5\mathrm{s} $ ) (see the Experimental Section in the Supporting Information for details), the surface temperature of the annealed perovskite film was dropped to about  $ 30^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  (Figure 1a). The surface temperature further decreased to about  $ -10^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  when the film was treated twice (Figure 1b-e). By fitting the temperature parameters, it can be roughly inferred that the film surface will reach the same temperature as liquid nitrogen after treating for six times (about  $ 3\mathrm{s} $ ) (Figure 1g). As a result, when the moisture in the atmosphere meets the low-temperature perovskite film, it will condense at the surface of the perovskite film and produce a small amount of water. Therefore, the surface perovskite lattice will be etched to produce  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . The environmental humidity is very important in this work. Based on finite element simulation (Hypermesh + Abaqus) and discrete/heat transfer analysis, a longitudinal temperature difference distribution is built from the top to the bottom of the film after treating for three times, and the upper surface has the lowest temperature (Figure 1f). Moreover, due to the glass barrier, the bottom of the film cannot directly contact with atmosphere, thus the formed  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  was only at the upper surface of perovskite films. As the treatment time continues to increase, the surface temperature of the perovskite film continues to decrease, and the film was frequented taken out of liquid nitrogen (see the Experimental Section in the Supporting Information), resulting in more water produced on the surface of the perovskite film, thus forming more  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . The peeled organic cations will remain in liquid nitrogen or volatilized with  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $ .

The constructed type-I band alignment at the upper surface grain boundary was first confirmed by X-ray diffraction (XRD) (Figure 2a). There was no characteristic peak of   $  PbI_{2}  $   (12.6°) appeared in the control and the sample treated for 0.5 s because the surface temperature of perovskite film is not low enough to condense the moisture in the air and produce   $  PbI_{2}  $  . Interestingly, the peak intensity of   $  PbI_{2}  $   increased sharply as treatment time increased and the intensity ratio of   $  PbI_{2}  $   to the perovskite (001) peak was 1.05 in the sample treated for 1 s (named as the IPSG sample in all the following descriptions). The peak intensity of   $  PbI_{2}  $   was even more than two times stronger than that of the perovskite when the treatment time was more than 2 s. However, too much   $  PbI_{2}  $   is detrimental to photoelectric properties.  $  ^{[19,30]}  $   The XRD results of pure   $  PbI_{2}  $   films with different treatment time (Figure S2, Supporting Information) exclude that the enhanced XRD intensity at 12.6° (Figure 2a) was originated from the enhanced crystallinity of   $  PbI_{2}  $   after low-temperature treatment.

Surface manipulation is an efficient strategy to reduce the carrier loss and improve the photoelectric properties of perovskite film,  $  ^{[6,11]}  $  since the defect density on the surface of perovskite films is more than two orders of magnitude higher than that in the interior.  $  ^{[44]}  $  Space-charge-limited current (SCLC) technique (indium tin oxide (ITO)/perovskite/Au) was conducted (see Figure 3a,b and Note S3, Supporting Information) to further quantify the defect density of perovskite film. The defect density of IPSG sample is  $  5.4 \times 10^{15}  $  cm  $  ^{-3}  $ , more than one order of magnitude less than that of control sample (  $  5.5 \times 10^{16}  $  cm  $  ^{-3}  $ ). Moreover, the surface recombination velocity also drastically decreased by one order of magnitude from  $  1.2 \times 10^{-2}  $  to  $  3 \times 10^{-3}  $  nm s  $  ^{-1}  $  (Table S2, Supporting Information), fully consistent with the significantly diminished surface defect density in IPSG sample. All the above results firmly validate that surface defects are indeed greatly suppressed by the constructed surface type-I band alignment, which is responsible for the enhanced photoelectric properties.

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy, a well-established technique to characterize the interfacial charge conductivity and collection, was conducted to further clarify the benefits of constructed type-I band alignment for perovskite film surface. In Figure 3c, the Nyquist plots with frequency range from  $ 1\mathrm{MHz} $  to  $ 0.01\mathrm{Hz} $  were fitted by an equivalent circuit model (inset in Figure S15 and Table S3, Supporting Information). The IPSG device owns larger semicircle radii and a much larger recombination resistance  $ (R_{\mathrm{rec}}) $  (415  $ \Omega $ ) compared to the

control device (284   $  \Omega  $  ), signifying a more efficient suppression of charge recombination in the IPSG device. Moreover, the time constants of the parasitic recombination (  $  \tau_{rec} = R_{rec} \times C_{rec}  $  ) in Figure 3d, extracted from the Nyquist plots at different bias voltages from -0.6 to 1.05 V (Figure S15, Supporting Information),  $  ^{[5,50]}  $   also indicate that the IPSG sample has longer   $  \tau_{rec}  $   than the control sample at all bias voltages. Consequently, the remarkably longer   $  \tau_{rec}  $   and larger   $  R_{rec}  $   once again confirmed the substantially reduced trap states at surface/interface in IPSG device from electrochemical perspective.

To further understand the impact of constructed type-I band alignment on interface, the carrier transport layers were prepared on top and bottom of the perovskite film. When the ETL was prepared at the bottom of perovskite film, the control and IPSG samples show only a small difference in photoluminescence (PL) peak intensities (Figure S16a, Supporting Information). In contrast, when the HTL was prepared on top of the perovskite film, IPSG sample showed significantly weaker PL emission (Figure S16b, Supporting Information), demonstrating that more carriers were successfully extracted into HTL. Therefore, the constructed type-I band alignment can lower the carrier loss in interface transport process and promote them to be more effectively extracted by HTL. To further reveal the mechanism behind the enhanced photoelectric properties of perovskite films, surface electronic states of perovskite and   $  PbI_{2}  $   films were investigated by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (Figure S18, Supporting Information) and UV absorption spectra (Figures S13a and S19, Supporting Information). However, as shown in the top view energy level alignment diagram, when the type-I band alignment heterojunction is formed at GB, the energy barrier will repel both electrons and holes, and return them to the inside of grain to avoid being trapped at the GB (Figure 4a).  $  ^{[53,54]}  $   Therefore, the type-I band alignment formed at the GB of perovskite film surface can reduce the loss of carriers to a much greater extent.

# 3. Conclusion

By effectively constructing   $  PbI_{2}  $  /perovskite type-I band alignment at only the upper surface grain boundaries in perovskite solar cells, electrons and holes were repelled and return to the inside of the grain to avoid the loss of carriers at grain boundary. The interactions between charge carriers and longitudinal optical phonons at surface were significantly weaken, which is beneficial to the directional movement of carriers and reduce unwanted recombination. All these effects are synergistically responsible for the advanced photovoltaic properties of PSCs. As a result, the carrier lifetime exceeding 2.5   $  \mu  $  s was obtained and a substantial gain in   $  V_{OC}  $   from 1.07 to 1.17 V was achieved. The minimum   $  V_{OC}  $   deficit of only 0.39 V enables the PCE to be markedly boosted from 20.2% to 22.4% for IPSG PSCs, which is an outstanding PCE of PSC based on the nonadditive method. Furthermore, based on IPSG strategy in the two-step spin-coating method, the high FF up to 83.1% and PCE up to 23.2% were achieved. Besides, a storage stability of 2000 h and light stability of 500 h were realized in IPSG PSCs, standing for a high-rank stability of highly efficient perovskite solar cells.

Compared to previous reports of using   $  PbI_{2}  $   to improve the efficiency of PSCs, this novel strategy owns many unique advantages (the difference between surface passivation and bulk passivation based on   $  PbI_{2}  $   is discussed in Note S5, Supporting Information). It is universal that can work efficiently in both one-step and two-step spin-coating methods. The previous problem of utilizing   $  PbI_{2}  $   to improve PCE but having to sacrifice stability has been solved. Moreover, the mechanism behind the type-I band alignment was deeply explored, shedding new light on the reserved knowledge toward the controversial and
