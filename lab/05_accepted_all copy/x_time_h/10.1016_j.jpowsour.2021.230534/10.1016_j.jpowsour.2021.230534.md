# ABSTRACT

Trap state inducing nonradiative recombination is a main obstacle for achieving high-efficiency metal-halide perovskite photovoltaics. Here, we introduced a straightforward strategy by utilizing a functional additive in perovskite antisolvent to ameliorate the quality of perovskite film. The additive Y6, extensively applied in organic optoelectronics as electron donor, possesses capacity to coordinate with the lead ion  $ (\mathrm{Pb}^{2+}) $  through the formation of Lewis adducts. The introduction of nonfullerene Y6 effectively suppressed nonradiative recombination through passivating the electronic defects, such as undercoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  or Pb clusters, abating defects density to acquire high quality films with improved stability, simultaneously enabling a well-matched energy alignment at interface between perovskite/electronic transport material to elevate charge extraction through graded heterojunction structure, ultimately providing planar inverted perovskite solar cells with power conversion efficiency up to  $ 20.6\% $  from current-voltage measurements and a steady-state  $ 20.3\% $  power output. # 1. Introduction

Metal halide perovskites have aroused great research interests because of their exceptional optoelectronic properties and successive improvements in photoelectric devices, especially the perovskite solar cells (PSCs). The state-of-the-art PSCs reach a marvelous record power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to 25.5% by 2021   $  [1-4]  $  . However, this is still well behind the Shockley–Queisser theoretical limit because of defect-assisted nonradiative recombination energy loss in photovoltaic devices   $  [5]  $  . In order to inhibit the nonradiative recombination, different approaches have been introduced, such as compositional and interfacial engineering as well as molecular passivation   $  [6-10]  $  . Molecular passivation, aimed at passivating defects either inside or on surface of perovskite layer, is a common route to boost PSC performance   $  [11,12]  $  .

The defect in interfaces and grain boundaries (GBs) protective layer likely produce non-stoichiometric composition and uncoordinated species   $  [13,14]  $  . When this happens to perovskite, the undercoordinated lead ions (  $  Pb^{2+}  $  ) are left after the evaporation of organic cations and halides anion from the perovskite surface or GBs   $  [15,16]  $  . Undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   have characteristic of Lewis acid, allowing it to accept electrons, while also susceptible to redox reactions, ultimately leading irreversible decomposition of perovskites   $  [17–19]  $  . Noel et al. first proposed that thiophene and pyridine molecules, containing a lone pair of electrons on either nitrogen (N) or sulphur (S) functionalities, could work as a Lewis base to passivate under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   defects   $  [20]  $  . The enhanced performance has demonstrated the passivation of undercoordinated defects by Lewis adduct chemistry is an effective strategy to improve the efficiency and stability of perovskites photovoltaic device   $  [12,21–24]  $  .

Another prospective strategy, antisolvent additive engineering, has been proposed to improve PSCs performance by adding a functional additive into perovskite antisolvent   $  [25–27]  $  . For example, Han et al. proposed perovskite–fullerene graded heterojunction which is conducive to the extraction of photogenerated electrons from light-active layer

Wu et al. demonstrated that the gradient heterojunction was formed through the addition of 4-tert-butylpyridine (τBP) into antisolvent. The coordination between the nitrogen atom of τBP and undercoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  led to high-quality perovskite films with reduced defects and larger grains size [29]. However, despite such promising results, only fairly limited number of additives have been attempted in antisolvent additive engineering.

Herein, we introduced nonfullerene Y6 including both thiophene units and C–N side chains as effective multifunctional additive to passivate perovskite defects. The addition of Y6 could favorably coordinate with undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   through formation of Lewis adduct, reducing trap-assisted nonradiative recombination of perovskite devices. Furthermore, antisolvent additive technique would also achieve efficient interfacial energy level alignment through gradient heterojunction structure to provide better carrier extraction for high-efficiency PSCs. Finally, the introduction of nonfullerene acceptor could bring perovskite devices up to a PCE of 20.6% and a stabilized PCE of 20.3% with a significant increased   $  V_{OC}  $   and improved stability.

The interreaction between perovskite and Y6 was studied by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) emerged in Fig. 2a and b. In

comparison with pristine perovskite film, the Pb 4f and I 3d XPS peaks shifted to higher core level binding energy for perovskite film treated with Y6, which indicates there is extra negative charge around Pb and I atoms. It would ascribe to the formation of Y6–PbI₂ Lewis adduct via coordinate bonding, because the Pb ion can accept the lone pair electrons that possess higher electron affinity, such as electrons in C=O bonds or CN chain molecules. The increase of I atom electron affinity can attribute to reduction of electron withdrawing capacity in Pb atom. Besides passivating the under-coordinated Pb²⁺, Lewis adduct formation can lead to ameliorated growth kinetics of perovskites crystal. In short, the process is mainly about passivation of anion vacancies defect sites (especially the at grain boundaries) and consequently reduce recombination loss and enhance the QFLS as well as VOC. It could be estimated that nonfullerene Y6 would be distributed in gradient near the interface with dripping antisolvent deposition method. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) depth analysis was utilized to investigate energy band structure at relevant interface. Meanwhile, it should be emphasized that conduction band offset (CBO) is crucial to determine voltage and photocurrent of solar cells. It can be observed from 0 s of sputtering time that there is no obvious CBO at perovskite/Y6 surface so that electrons are not hindered by interfacial barrier and can diffuse easily. With the increase of etching time, there is a gradually changing of energy band bending across relevant interface, which can be considered as type-II energy alignment, driving electron transfer effectively (Fig. 2c). In short, the incorporation of Y6 not only affect the interfacial band alignment and charge transfer dynamics, but mainly shortens the required electrons diffusion pathway length (L_D) in the perovskite phase, meaning a thicker absorber layer for greater light harvesting. The conclusion can also be confirmed by the improved IPCE plateau and corresponding larger J_SC values. The scanning electron microscopic (SEM) images of perovskite films with and without Y6 treatment are exhibited in Fig. 2d. The enlarged grains size of perovskite film with Y6 addition is related to crystallization kinetics. Enhanced Lewis acid–base interaction assisted by Y6 addition could increases crystallization activation energy (E_a), which is characterized by the decreased nucleation number and a relatively small amount of nucleation would grow continuously without impingement to form larger grains. To understand optoelectronic characteristics of perovskite films with Y6, the electrical conductivity of perovskite films was inspected with devices of glass/perovskite/Au (Fig. 3a and Table S3). The Y6 devices exhibited a lower average resistance  $ (7.698 \times 10^{8} \Omega) $  than the control  $ (1.939 \times 10^{9} \Omega) $  and the electrical conductivity is associated with scattering processes caused by defects like vacancies, impurities and substitutions. For Y6 device, lower resistance value attribute to better crystallinity and fewer GBs within the same area. High-efficient charge transfer and extraction processes also are key factors for PSCs to achieve the excellent performance. The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) decay were applied to analysis charge dynamical processes. The PL intensity of Y6 involved perovskite film is 1.8 times larger than that of pristine perovskite films. It can be explained by the above that thiophene in Y6 could passivate defects through dipole-ion interaction between lone pair electrons of Lewis bases and undercoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  (Fig. 3b). The TRPL decay in Fig. 3c could be modeled by a bi-exponential formula  $ [Y = A_{1}\exp(-t/\tau_{1}) + A_{2}\exp(-t/\tau_{2})] $ , where slow  $ (\tau_{2}) $  and fast  $ (\tau_{1}) $  decay components are associated with radiative and nonradiative recombination, respectively. Characteristics of biexponential decay in bare perovskite film exhibited

clear with fast (  $  \tau_{1} = 39.8  $  ns) and slow (  $  \tau_{2} = 105.5  $  ns) components, whereas TRPL decay of Y6 involved perovskite film just appear in a single-exponential form and slow decay components increase almost twice (  $  \tau_{2} = 257.3  $  ns) (Table S4). The incremental decay time is essential for longer  $  L_{D}  $  and lower defects density for PSCs efficiency. In short, it indicated that Y6 was indeed remediating nonradiative defects in the perovskite film and the free carrier lifetime is also highly prolonged, all of which contributes to the enhanced  $  V_{OC}  $  and FF observed. The positive light-soaking effect can be observed in the control and Y6 devices. For the control, PCE values increased by   $  \sim2\%  $   in first few hours and then decreased below the initial efficiency after 20 h, the Y6 PSC retained 80% of the initial PCE under the same conditions. More surprising is that the efficiency of Y6 devices could keep close to initial value for more than 48 h. Y6 PSCs exhibited excellent light stability than the control, because the decreased defects density assisted by Y6 could protect perovskite layer from light-induced ion diffusion degradation and associated trap-state induced recombination. It is worth noting that perovskite degradation overtakes the Y6 passivating effect with further loss of MA  $  ^{+}  $   and the formation of PbI  $  _{2}  $  , then the perovskite films would accelerate decomposition and eventually degrades entirely (Fig. 4c and d). To investigate heat stressing stability of PSCs, the encapsulated devices were enclosed in argon atmosphere and aged at 85 °C. Thermal decomposition usually originated from the GBs with high defect density and the volatilization of I  $  ^{-}  $   and formation of moving ions are susceptible to elevated temperature. A quick efficiency degradation process was observed for both the control and Y6 devices. The PCE of the control devices decreased rapidly to   $  <4\%  $   after 24 h, whereas the PCE of Y6 devices maintained about 16% absolute during some aging period. Y6 is considered to play a key role in increasing the ion migration barrier, which helps to inhibit the escape of volatile ions from perovskite crystal (Fig. 4e and f).

In conclusion, we developed a facile and effective antisolvent additive strategy to simultaneously enhance electron extraction and passivate defect state for producing highly efficient, stable PSCs. Nonfullerene Y6, functioning as electron extraction materials and Lewis base small molecule, was introduced into the perovskite precursor solution. The use of such multifunction additive facilitates the coordination between undercoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  through formation of Lewis adduct, playing a key role in terms of defect-assist nonradiative recombination

suppression and grain growth control. Moreover, the efficient electron extraction is enabled by graded heterojunction structure with a well-matched energy level. Accordingly, the resulting inverted PSCs deliver an enhanced PCE as high as  $ 20.6\% $  and improved stability under heat and continuous light stress. Our research shows that nonfullerene based antisolvent additive strategy is an feasible method to produce high-quality perovskite films that guarantees high-performance PSCs with a high efficiency and long-term stability.
