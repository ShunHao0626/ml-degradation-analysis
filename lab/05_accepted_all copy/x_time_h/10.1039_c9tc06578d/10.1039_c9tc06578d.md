# Abstract

The solution-processed perovskite films inevitably induce defects, adversely affecting the device performance and environmental stability. Motivated by the small molecular passivation agents, a systematical work is carried out to reveal the role of P4VP polymer in perovskite solar cells. The introduction of P4VP polymer can reduce the defects at the surfaces and grain boundaries by coordinating with undercoordinated lead sites. The substantially reduced defect density leads to reduced nonradiative recombination, and extended electron diffusion length. Furthermore, the good matching of energy levels at the perovskite/PCBM interface is achieved, which favors efficient electron extraction and suppressed the holes transfer. As a result, the power conversion efficiency increases from  $ 17.46\% $  to  $ 20.02\% $  after defect passivation and improved energy level alignment with a significantly enhanced fill factor of  $ 82.61\% $  and improved open circuit voltage of 1.09. # Introduction

Recent progresses have witnessed the rapid development of three-dimensional (3D) perovskite solar cells with device efficiency from 3.8% in 2009  $  ^{1}  $  up to the current certified results of 25.2%  $  ^{2}  $ , which attributed to many advancements on composition engineering, morphology control, and interface engineering. However, the long-term instability against moisture, heat, and light is a big issue for perovskite solar cells, thus slowing down the commercialization  $  ^{3-6}  $ . Generally, the perovskite films are fabricated by solution-processing methods and low-temperature treatment, thus unavoidably inducing many defects at surfaces and grain boundaries. These defects will become charge carrier trap centers and cause non-radiative recombination, thus significantly impairing photo-physical properties of perovskite films and resulting in poor device performance  $  ^{7-10}  $ . Furthermore, the defects at surfaces and grain boundaries make the perovskite film more vulnerable to water and oxygen, triggering the degradation of perovskite film and its corresponding device performance  $  ^{11-14}  $ .

To tackle the defect-induced poor device performance and device instability, many attempts have been made, which follow the ideas including the improvement of crystallinity and grain sizes  $ ^{15-17} $ , introduction of Lewis base electron donors (e.g., pyridine, thiophene, and fullerenes) to passivate the defects  $ ^{18-21} $ , and interface modification to improve the energy level alignment  $ ^{22-24} $ . Small molecules, such as pyridine and thiophene, have been proved to be effective passivation agents by providing the lone pair electrons to coordinate with the under-coordinated Pb sites  $ ^{21} $ . Compared to small molecules, polymers (e.g., poly(propylene carbonate) (PPC)) can form more stable and reliable interactions with the perovskite grains and could construct cross-link grains to improve the film stability, as has been reported by Yang Yang's group  $ ^{25} $ . Additionally, another polymer Poly-4-vinylpyridine (P4VP)  $ ^{17} $  contains the commonly used passivation agents, pyridine, thus allowing that P4VP polymer shares the similar defect passivation effect with that of pyridine, that is, the lone pair electrons of nitrogen in P4VP could coordinate with the under-coordinated Pb sites.

These motivations have inspired our work, in which we introduced P4VP interlayer to passivate the defects at the surfaces and grain boundaries and improve the energy level alignments at perovskite/transport layer interface. The optical characterizations demonstrate that P4VP modified perovskite film exhibits suppressed nonradiative recombination and efficient charge extraction, resulting in a strikingly increased electron diffusion length. The defect density, charge transfer and recombination dynamics were investigated by the space charge limited current (SCLC), electronic impedance spectra (EIS) and transient photovoltage (TPV), further confirming that P4VP could effectively passivate the surface defects. Furthermore, a good interfacial energy level alignment was achieved after the incorporation of P4VP polymer, which is verified by ultraviolet photoemission spectra (UPS). The synergistic effect of reduced defect density and good energy level alignment contributes to highly efficient and stable perovskite solar cells, with a champion efficiency of 20.02%. The significant improvement of device performances was mainly ascribed to the greatly improved fill factor (FF) of 82.61% and open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.09 V. In addition, the non-encapsulated devices exhibit enhanced moisture stability (60±5% humidity) and thermal stability (85 °C), suggesting that the hydrophobic properties of P4VP interlayer can efficiently improve the device stability.

# Results and Discussion

Fig. 1a and b present the top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of perovskite films without and with P4VP modification. Here, we denote the reference perovskite film without P4VP passivation as control and perovskite film with X mg/ml P4VP modification as P4VP@X. The control film exhibits a smooth and compact morphology with widely distributed grain sizes. After P4VP modification, the grain boundaries were linked together by the P4VP polymer, and most of the smaller grains were merged with each other. Further, the effect of P4VP interface layer on the crystallinity of perovskite films were estimated by the x-ray diffraction technique, with results shown in Fig. 1c. It can be observed that the XRD diffraction peaks have a negligible difference, demonstrating an ignorance influence of P4VP interface layer on the crystallization behavior of perovskite films.

The effects of P4VP interface layer on the capability of carrier recombination and extraction are analyzed by the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra of bare perovskite films and PCBM quenched perovskite films. As shown in Fig. 1d, the PL spectra of P4VP@0.10 films exhibit much higher intensity than that of control samples. This implies that non-radiative recombination is effectively suppressed by P4VP interface layer. The TRPL results (Fig. 1e and Table S1) exhibit some differences in the carrier lifetime between control and P4VP@0.10 films. By fitting the TRPL spectra with bi-exponential function, the extracted carrier lifetime for P4VP@0.10 film is 22.67 ns, which is much longer than that of control film (13.14 ns). The prolonged carrier lifetime was attributed to the decreased trap density and reduced non-radiative recombination due to the P4VP-induced defect passivation  $ ^{18,19} $ . When the perovskite film was covered with PCBM layer, the PL intensity and carrier lifetime were substantially reduced, with fitted lifetimes of 2.44 ns and 2.17 ns for control and P4VP@0.10 films (Fig. S1). The efficient PL quenching and shortened carrier lifetime for P4VP@0.10 film suggests a more efficient charge carrier extraction at the perovskite (P4VP)/PCBM interface. The estimated longer diffusion length for P4VP@0.10 film reveals that P4VP can effectively passivate the defects, thus creating superb pathways for long distance charge transport, resulting in enhanced device performance.

Where   $  V_{TFL}  $   is the onset voltage of the trap-filled limited region,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity,   $  \varepsilon_{r}  $   is the relative dielectric constant, and L is the thickness of the film. The electronic impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were carried out to gain in-depth understanding of the charge transport dynamics in solar cells. In general, EIS spectra for PSCs shows a single high frequency semi-circular response in the dark light situation with an extra low frequency semi-circle appearing under illumination. Fig. 2d provides the Nyquist plots of control and P4VP@0.10-based devices, which were performed in dark condition with the frequencies ranging from  $ 100\mathrm{Hz} $  to  $ 10\mathrm{MHz} $ . By fitting the impedance spectra with the equivalent circuit as shown in the inset of Fig. 2d, we can extract the series resistance  $ R_{\mathrm{s}} $  and charge transport resistance  $ R_{\mathrm{ct}} $ . The values of  $ R_{\mathrm{s}} $  for control and P4VP@0.10 device are  $ 33.82\Omega $  and  $ 28.11\Omega $ , respectively, which indicated that P4VP interface layer could effectively modify the interface contact and thus reduce the contact resistance. The extracted charge transport resistance  $ R_{\mathrm{ct}} $  for control and P4VP@0.10 devices are  $ 578\Omega $  and  $ 508\Omega $ , respectively. The relatively lower  $ R_{\mathrm{ct}} $  in P4VP@0.10 devices signifies better charge transport, which could explain the improved FF in P4VP modified devices. Then, the transient photovoltage (TPV) measurements were carried out to elucidate the recombination dynamics, with the derived recombination lifetimes shown in Fig. 2e. Generally, the technique of TPV decay can provide more information about carrier dynamics, through which we can learn about the charge recombination rate. The relatively longer carrier recombination lifetimes for P4VP@0.10 based device indicate slower charge recombination rate due to the reduced defect density, which has been evidenced by the SCLC results.

Meanwhile, the valence band maximum with respect to the Fermi level (  $  E_{F}  $  ) in the P4VP@0.10 film was also shifted relative to the control film. Combining the UV-Vis absorption spectra (Fig. S2) and UPS results, the energy-level diagrams of control and P4VP@0.10 film were constructed (Fig. 3b). Such shifts in   $  E_{vac}  $   and   $  E_{F}  $   is likely to originate from the change in the ratio of PbI-terminated surface to organic-terminated surface, which have been reported by previous works   $  {}^{36, 37}  $  . Generally, PbI-terminated surfaces exhibit lower   $  E_{v}  $   and   $  E_{F}  $   than that of organic-terminated surfaces. Therefore, the introduction of P4VP allows a larger faction of surface to be terminated with P4VP organic polymer. Based on the above results, we propose a possible trap states type and passivation mechanism. As shown in Fig. 3c, the loss of iodides results in some under-coordinated Pb atoms at the surface of perovskite film, which are likely to act as non-radiative recombination centers and impair the device performance. When the P4VP interface layer was capped on the perovskite film, the I-vacancies are significantly reduced due to the electrostatic interaction between the negative N atom in the P4VP and positive   $  Pb^{2+}  $   (Fig. 3d). Therefore, this case is expected to allow the surface to be terminated with some organic polymer, which is in good agreement with the UPS results. The lower level of I-vacancies in the P4VP@0.10 film allows the reduced defect density and reduced non-radiative recombination, further increasing the carrier lifetime and enhancing the carrier diffusion length, which is evidenced by the data presented above.

# Conclusion

To conclude, we demonstrate that the introduction of P4VP polymer into the perovskite/PCBM interface is an effective method to improve both the device performance and stability. The incorporation of P4VP polymer is found to passivate the defects, leading to reduced defect density and suppressed non-radiative recombination, thus giving rise to improvement of   $  V_{oc}  $  . Moreover, after P4VP introduction, the energy level alignment at perovskite/PCBM interfaces was successfully modulated, thus favoring efficient charge carrier transfer from perovskite to PCBM, which could account for the significantly improved FF. As a result, the P4VP-modified perovskite solar cells achieve a PCE of 20.02% compared to the control device of 17.46% efficiency, which mainly attributed to the drastically improved FF and   $  V_{oc}  $  . Also, the P4VP modified devices exhibit enhanced moisture stability and thermal stability, with 80% PCEs keeping after stored in ambient air (60±5% humidity) for 500 h and 78% remaining under the continuous heat treatment at 85°C for 200 h.

# Experimental

# Materials and solution preparation

The hole transport layer poly(bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine) (PTAA, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp) were prepared by dissolving 2 mg PTAA in 1 ml Chlorobenzene (CB, 99.8%, Acros) solvent. The perovskite precursor solution was prepared by dissolving lead iodide (PbI  $  _{2}  $  , 98%, TCI) and methylammonium the iodide (MAI, 99.998%, Deysol) with a molar ratio of 1.08 into the mixed N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%, Acros) and Dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO, 99.8%, Acros) (9:1 v/v ratio). Poly-4-vinylpyridine (P4VP, 46.7K Mw, American Dye Source, Inc) solutions with different concentrations were prepared by dissolving into isopropanol (IPA, 99.8%, Acros). PC  $  _{61}  $  BM (99.5%, Nano-C) with a concentration of 20 mg/mL was dissolved in CB.

# Solar cell fabrication

Patterned ITO glass substrates (sheet resistance   $  \leq10\ \Omega\ cm^{-2}  $  ) were cleaned consecutively in acetone, detergent, deionized water, and isopropanol ultrasonic bath, and then dried at 70 °C for overnight. After 15 min UV-Ozone treatment, the PTAA solution was spin coated on the substrate at 5000 rpm for 30s and annealed at 150 °C for 10 min. To improve the wetting property of the perovskite precursor solution, the PTAA film was pre-wetted by spinning 70   $  \mu  $  l DMF at 4000 rpm for 10s. Subsequently, the perovskite precursor solution was spun coated at 4000 rpm for 30s, and then the anti-solvent chlorobenzene was drop onto the films at 22s before the end the spin-coating step, followed by a thermal annealing treatment at 100 °C for 10 min. After the film cooled to room temperature, the P4VP solution was spin coated on the perovskite film at 5000 rpm for 30s. Then, the PCBM solution was spin coated at 1000 rpm for 60 s to form a 80 nm electron transporting layer. Finally, the sample was transferred into a vacuum chamber for further deposition of Al (100 nm) to finish the device fabrication process.

# Device characterization

The photovoltaic device performance was analyzed by the Keithley 2400 Instrument under simulated AM1.5G sunlight at  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $ , which was calibrated by the standard monocrystalline silicon solar cell. The photocurrent density-voltage  $ (J - V) $  curves were measured in glovebox from both forward and reverse scan direction. To calibrate the short current  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $ , the external quantum efficiency (EQE) of solar cells were measured by device with a lock-in amplifier coupled with a monochromator. The steady-state output of PCE and short current was recorded under constant bias voltage operation that corresponds to a maximum power. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was conducted using precision impedance analyzer (Agilent 4294 A) with the frequency range from  $ 100\mathrm{Hz} $  to  $ 10\mathrm{MHz} $  in the dark condition. For the device stability, the non-encapsulated devices were stored in nitrogen-filled glovebox, ambient air with a humidity of  $ 55 - 65\% $ , and continuously annealing at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  in a nitrogen atmosphere, respectively, to investigate the storage stability, humidity stability, and thermal stability.
