# Design of Low Bandgap   $  CsPb_{1-x}Sn_{x}I_{2}Br  $   Perovskite Solar Cells with Excellent Phase Stability

Gangshu Chen, Pengwei Li, Tangyue Xue, Meng Su, Junjie Ma, Yiqiang Zhang,* Tianhao Wu, Liyuan Han, Mahmoud Aldamasy, Meng Li, Zehua Li, Jiale Ma, Shuyao Chen, Yao Zhao, Fuyi Wang, and Yanlin Song*

Novel all-inorganic Sn-Pb alloyed perovskites are developed aiming for low toxicity, low bandgap, and long-term stability. Among them,   $  CsPb_{1-x}Sn_{x}I_{2}Br  $   is predicted as an ideal perovskite with favorable band gap, but previously is demonstrated unable to convert to perovskite phase by thermal annealing. In this report, a series of   $  CsPb_{1-x}Sn_{x}I_{2}Br  $   perovskites with tunable bandgaps from 1.92 to 1.38 eV are successfully prepared for the first time via low annealing temperature (60 °C). Compared to the pure   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , these Sn-Pb alloyed perovskites show superior stability. Furthermore, a novel   $  \alpha  $  -phase-stabilization mechanism of the inorganic Sn-Pb alloyed perovskite by reconfiguring the perovskite crystallization process with chloride doping is provided. Simultaneously, a dense protection layer is formed by the coordination reaction between the surface lead dangling bonds and sulfate ion to retard the permeation of external oxygen and moisture, leading to less oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   in perovskite film. As a result, the fabricated all-inorganic Sn-Pb perovskite solar cells (PSCs) show a champion power conversion efficiency of 10.39% with improved phase stability and long-term ambient stability against light, heat, and humidity. This work provides a viable strategy in fabricating high-performance narrow-bandgap all-inorganic PSCs.

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted much attention due to their rapid improvement of

stability than that of organic-inorganic counterparts, especially at high temperatures above 150 °C, due to the absence of volatile and hygroscopic organic cations. Therefore, the investigation of all-inorganic PSCs grabbed great attention in the last

These shortcomings seriously restrict the further development of PSCs.

All-inorganic Sn-Pb binary perovskites have the characteristics of narrow bandgaps, low toxicity, and high carrier mobility. Moreover, they exhibit promising photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{[14]}  $   Consequently, different types of all-inorganic Sn-Pb binary perovskites were introduced into photovoltaic devices. In 2017, Liang et al. first applied   $  CsPb_{0.9}Sn_{0.1}IBr_{2}  $   (  $  \approx  $  1.79 eV) into PSCs and a remarkable PCE of 11.33% was achieved. Unfortunately, it is demonstrated that   $  CsPb_{0.9}Sn_{0.1}I_{2}Br  $   remained inactive yellow phase even annealed above 400 °C.  $  ^{[15]}  $   Afterward, Li et al. adopted   $  CsPb_{1-x}Sn_{x}IBr_{2}  $   perovskite structure as an active layer, and the solar cells fabricated by using   $  CsPb_{0.75}Sn_{0.25}IBr_{2}  $   delivered the highest PCE of 11.53%.  $  ^{[16]}  $   Recently, Hu et al. reported a stable   $  CsPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3}  $   perovskite with low-bandgap (1.38 eV) via interface functionalization, resulting in a PCE improvement of 13.37%.  $  ^{[17]}  $   Although Sn-Pb binary inorganic PSCs have made great progress, there are still intractable issues, such as: i) inorganic perovskites are susceptible to undergo a phase transition from cubic to orthorhombic phase under atmospheric environment;  $  ^{[18]}  $   ii) the Sn-based perovskite is known for the facile oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   to   $  Sn^{4+}  $   in air, which results in serious self-doping effect in the absorber layer.  $  ^{[19]}  $   These unfavorable factors, undermining the efforts to realize high-performance devices, should be fully addressed.

In this work, we synthesized a series of   $  CsPb_{1-x}Sn_{x}I_{2}Br  $   perovskites by a green anti-solvent spin-coating method with low annealing temperature (60 °C). The corresponding films present tunable bandgaps from 1.92 to 1.38 eV. Compared to the pure   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , these Sn-Pb alloyed perovskites show superior stability. We then stabilized the cubic phase via reconfiguring the grain boundary and refilling iodine vacancies Cl ions originated from CsCl additive. Moreover, the formation of surface compact   $  PbSO_{4}  $   layer by the reaction between under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   and sulfate ion can block the external oxygen and moisture, resulting in suppression effect of the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   in perovskite film and largely prevent the migration of halide anions at the interface. The first-principle calculations further show that dense charge clouds around well-bonded   $  PbSO_{4}  $   can be used as a “bridge” for boosting charge transfer. As a result, the PCSs with an inverted structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/perovskite/sulfate/PCB  $  _{61}  $  M/BCP/Ag is highly

stable and the champion device shows a PCE of 10.39%. The unencapsulated optimized   $  CsPb_{0.55}Sn_{0.45}I_{2}Br  $   devices retained over 92.5% of its initial PCE after being stored in a   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere for 2000 h. Meanwhile, the devices also exhibited excellent light, humidity (40–50%), and thermal stability (85 °C).

# 2. Results and Discussion

We fabricated the Sn-Pb alloyed inorganic perovskite by a green anti-solvent spin-coating method. The ideal crystal structure of   $  CsPb_{1-x}Sn_{x}I_{2}Br  $   inorganic perovskite is presented in Figure 1a, where the typical perovskite   $  ABX_{3}  $   structure (Pm3m) is considered as the   $  BX_{6}  $   octahedrons orderly connecting corner X anions in the three-dimension space.  $  ^{[5]}  $   Generally, the   $  Cs^{+}  $  cations at A sites are highly ionic and have negligible contribution to the band structure. In contrast, the bandgap is mainly dominated by the B cations and X anions. Owing to the higher orbital energy level of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   cations than that of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   cations, partially substitution of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   with   $  Sn^{2+}  $   can generate stronger coupling with I/Br np orbitals. This will generate narrow bandgap by pushing the valence band maximum (VBM) up and shift the conduction band minimum (CBM) downward.  $  ^{[20]}  $   Therefore, we optimized the Sn/Pb ratio of the alloyed perovskite films and evaluate their optoelectronic and crystallographic properties. Obviously, as shown in Figure 1b, the replacement of Pb with Sn can play a good role in adjusting the optical absorption edge (650 to 900 nm) of perovskite films, and the corresponding bandgap (  $  E_{g}  $  ) (1.92 to 1.38 eV) is narrower than pure Pb-based inorganic counterparts (Figure 1c; Figure S1, Supporting Information). Similarly, the typically bandgap “bowing” effect is observed in inorganic   $  CsPb_{1-x}Sn_{x}I_{2}Br  $  .  $  ^{[21]}  $   In addition, the stability of perovskite structure is related to the Goldschmidt tolerance factor (  $  \tau  $  ):   $  \tau=\frac{(R_{\mathrm{A}}+R_{\mathrm{x}})}{\sqrt{2}(R_{\mathrm{B}}+R_{\mathrm{x}})}  $  ,  $  ^{[22]}  $   where   $  R_{A}  $ ,   $  R_{B}  $ , and   $  R_{X}  $   represents the ionic radius of A, B, and X in perovskite   $  ABX_{3}  $ , respectively. Owing to the smaller ionic radii of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   (112 pm), the   $  \tau  $   of Sn-Pb alloyed perovskite can be avallably increased, leading to a more stable octahedral skeleton (  $  [BX_{6}]^{4-}  $  ) (Figure 1c; Table S1, Supporting Information). To confirm the Sn/Pb ratios, as shown in Figure S2 (Supporting Information), we given the X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS) measurement to verify it. The XPS spectra of the films suggest that the binding energy peak intensity of Sn 3d core levels significantly increase while the Pb 4f core levels decreases gradually with the substitution of Sn. Figure S3 (Supporting Information) exhibits better moisture resistance of Sn-Pb alloyed perovskites compared with their neat Pb counterparts under 40–50% humidity, corroborating the enhanced stability of the Sn-Pb alloyed perovskite phase caused by stretching and shrinking within the lattice. More importantly, we further compared the thermal stability of   $  CsPb_{0.55}Sn_{0.45}I_{2}Br  $ ,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films by continuous heating at very high temperature (150 °C) in a   $  N_{2}  $   glove box for different times (Figure 1d). Predictably, the   $  CsPb_{0.55}Sn_{0.45}I_{2}Br  $   with benign structural stability and relatively low bandgap (1.50 eV) showed the best device performance (Figure S4 and Table S2, Supporting Information). However, high intrinsic vacancy concentrations and defect states still exist in their bulk and surface caused by the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $ , resulting in poor device performance and stability. According to the Shockley–Queisser limit based on detailed balance considerations for single junction solar cells, the highest efficiency of 31.64% can be reached at a bandgap energy of 1.50 eV.  $  ^{[23]}  $   This all-inorganic Sn-Pb perovskite solar cell has a lot of space for improvement. Thus, we adopted the synergistic strategies of bulk doping and surface coordination to suppress the self-doping effect, stabilize the crystal structure, and resist the moisture/  $  O_{2}  $   attack. As illustrated in Figure 2a, The similar halogens ions (  $  Cl^{-}  $  ) were incorporated into   $  CsPb_{0.55}Sn_{0.45}I_{2}Br  $   perovskite to reconfigure grain boundaries (GBs), refill iodine vacancies, and bond with   $  Sn^{2+}  $ . Therefore, relying on the addition of CsCl, larger grain sizes (  $  \approx1\mu m  $  ) can be obtained and defect sites (  $  I^{-}  $  vacancies and   $  Sn^{4+}  $   cations) could be reduced. This strategy leads to high-crystallinity inorganic

Sn-Pb binary perovskite. The   $  CsPb_{0.55}Sn_{0.45}I_{2}Br  $   perovskite with CsCl additive is expressed as   $  CsPb_{0.55}Sn_{0.45}I_{2}Br  $  -CsCl to facilitate the following discussion. The next strategy is to form water-insoluble   $  PbSO_{4}  $   thin film by coordinating hydrophobic sulfate on the surface, denoted as   $  CsPb_{0.55}Sn_{0.45}I_{2}Br  $  -CsCl-S. The functionalization of the surface is expected to not only passivate   $  Pb^{2+}  $   dangling bonds but also produce a hydrophobic barrier layer. Thus, the compact   $  PbSO_{4}  $   protecting layer can inhibit   $  I^{-}  $  migration, prevent   $  Sn^{2+}  $   from oxidation, and resist   $  O_{2}  $  /moisture attack.

The increased prolonged decay lifetime indicates that multi-functionalization treatments reduce Sn vacancies and improve the suppression of trap-assisted nonradiative recombination in the bulk and on the surface of the perovskite films. It further corroborates our functionalization approaches are effective ways to prepare high-quality inorganic Sn-Pb binary perovskites.

The passivation mechanism of Cl ions is further investigated by calculating the relative energy and DOS. As presented in Figure 4a, the binding energy of the Sn–Cl bond (2.449 eV) is much higher than that of the Sn–I bond (1.884 eV), suggesting that Cl ions preferentially bound to   $  Sn^{2+}  $   forming more stable structure and further protect it from oxidation to   $  Sn^{4+}  $  . Then the DOS of typical internal defects is calculated by introducing the deep defect level of   $  I^{-}  $  vacancy defects in Figure 4b,c. Uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   can be passivated by   $  SO_{4}^{2-}  $   to form very strong chemical bonding bonds and occupy the reverse defect sites on the surface, thus reducing the recombination sites of charge carriers. Notably, the presence of dense charge clouds at well-bonded   $  PbSO_{4}  $   indicates an excellent capability of charge capture, which contributes to charge transfer between layers and thus improves charge carrier collection.

Noted that the   $  CsPb_{0.55}Sn_{0.45}I_{2}Br-CsCl-S  $   devices demonstrated an impressive thermal and illumination stability with only attenuate around 5% maintained at 85 °C and 7% under constant illumination after storing 300 h in a glove box, respectively. $ \mathrm{CsPb}_{0.55}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.45}\mathrm{I}_2\mathrm{Br}-\mathrm{CsCl} $  devices decreased by  $ 8\% $  and  $ 13\% $ ,  $ 15\% $ , and  $ 18\% $  under the same condition, respectively. Thus, all-inorganic systems for mixed Sn-Pb with full defect passivation in the bulk and on the surface of the perovskite can be an eco-friendly and promising strategy for efficient and stable PSC fabrication.

# 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, we have demonstrated a new series of Sn-Pb alloyed all-inorganic perovskite   $  (\mathrm{CsPb}_{0.55}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.45}\mathrm{I}_{2}\mathrm{Br})  $   with high-performance by introducing CsCl additive and coordinating   $  SO_{4}^{2-}  $   onto surface. This strategy stabilizes the perovskite phase, promotes the crystallization and morphology of perovskite layers, and improves the device energy alignment, leading to fewer defect sites caused by   $  I^{-}  $  vacancies and   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation. More importantly, the surface modification of perovskite by hydrophobic lead sulfate can effectively passivate uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $ , protect   $  Sn^{2+}  $   from oxidation and raise the ion infiltration barrier. In addition, the first-principles calculations indicate that the well-bonded   $  PbSO_{4}  $   with dense charge clouds can serve as a good link for charge transfer. As a result, these strategies greatly reduce trap density and stabilize   $  \alpha  $  -phase. Finally, the optimized   $  CsPb_{0.55}Sn_{0.45}I_{2}Br-CsCl-S  $   devices exhibited a champion PCE of 10.39% with remarkable ambient-air and long-term stability. This work provides a bright prospective for low-bandgap and stability inorganic photovoltaic materials.

ToF-SIMS analysis for depth profiling and 2D imaging was conducted on a ToF-SIMS V spectrometer (IONTOF GmbH, Munster, Germany). The J-V characteristics of the solar cells were performed via a Keithley 2400 source meter under standard AM 1.5 G solar irradiation at 100 mW·cm  $  ^{-2}  $  (EnliTech, Class 3A solar simulator), and the active area was defined by a shadow mask (0.04 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ). The EIS characteristics were determined on an electrochemical analyzer (ZAHENR, Germany). The EQE measurements of devices were conducted by a monochromatic illumination optical system (EnliTech, QE-R), calibrated by Si reference solar cell.

First-Principles Calculations: Based on DFT, the structure and energy calculations were performed by the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP 5.4.4) code. The cut-off energy for the plane-wave basis of 400 eV and a Monkhorst-Pack k-point mesh were chosen in the Brillouin zone sampling for treating pseudo potentials with the type of Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof and the single-point energy calculation. And the perovskite solar cells was substituted by using   $  4 \times 4 \times 2  $   supercells. The geometric convergence criteria for maximum force and energy change were set as less than 0.05 eV Å  $  ^{-1}  $   and   $  10^{-5}  $   eV, respectively. In addition, The Brillouin zone integration was performed by using Gamma point for structure optimization, and employing   $  2 \times 2 \times 2  $   k-meshes for calculating DOS, respectively. A vacuum layer of 16 Å was added vertical to the (001) surface of perovskite.
