# $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.88}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.12}\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}(\mathrm{PF}_6)_x $  Interlayer Formed by Ion Exchange Reaction between Perovskite and Hole Transporting Layer for Improving Photovoltaic Performance and Stability

Jiangzhao Chen, Seul-Gi Kim, and Nam-Gyu Park*

Interface engineering to form an interlayer via ion exchange reaction is reported. A   $  FA_{0.88}Cs_{0.12}PbI_{3}  $   formamidinium (FA) perovskite layer is first prepared, then   $  FAPF_{6}  $   solution with different concentrations is spin-coated on top of the perovskite film, which leads to a partial substitution of iodide by   $  PF_{6}^{-}  $  ion. The second phase with nominal composition of   $  FA_{0.88}Cs_{0.12}PbI_{3-x}(PF_{6})_{x}  $   is grown at the grain boundary, which has island morphology and its size depends on the   $  FAPF_{6}  $   solution concentration. The lattice is expanded and bandgap is reduced due to inclusion of larger   $  PF_{6}^{-}  $  ions. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) is significantly enhanced from 17.8% to 19.3% as a consequence of improved fill factor and open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ). In addition, current–voltage hysteresis is reduced. Post-treatment with   $  FAPF_{6}  $   reduces defect density and enhances carrier lifetime, which is responsible for the improved photovoltaic performance and reduced hysteresis. The unencapsulated device with post-treated perovskite film demonstrates better stability than the pristine perovskite, where the initial PCE retains over 80% after 528 h exposure under relative humidity of around 50–70% in the dark and 92% after 360 h under one sun illumination.

In the past several years, unprecedented power conversion efficiency (PCE) improvement from 3.8% in 2009  $  ^{[1]}  $  to a certified 23.3% by now  $  ^{[2]}  $  has been witnessed for perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on organic-inorganic metal halide perovskites with intriguing and unique optoelectronic properties, which was triggered by pioneering report on solid-state PSCs with a PCE of 9.7%.  $  ^{[3]}  $  To date, most of high-efficiency PSCs are fabricated based on mesoporous architecture of FTO/bl-TiO  $  _{2}  $ /mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $ /perovskite/spiro-MeOTAD (or PTAA)/Au (or Ag), where FTO, bl, mp, PTAA, and spiro-MeOTAD stand for fluorine doped tin oxide, blocking, mesoporous, polytriarylamine and 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene, respectively,  $  ^{[4-9]}  $  and composition engineering has been found to play a vital role in the realization of stable and high-performance PSCs.

[35-38] Inspired by the above successful compositional and interfacial engineering along with ion exchange reaction using molecular ion   $  BF_{4}^{-}  $ , we are motivated to develop novel efficient methods to further improve device performance and stability.

In this work, we report on interface engineering via ion exchange reaction using molecular ion   $  PF_{6}^{-}  $  . Since   $  BF_{4}^{-}  $  was reported to be able to be incorporated in anion site in halide perovskite,  $  ^{[16,39-41]}  $     $  PF_{6}^{-}  $  ion can be also considered as a possible candidate because   $  BF_{4}^{-}  $  and   $  PF_{6}^{-}  $  are commonly classified into noncoordinating anions.  $  ^{[42]}  $   Iodide ion in   $  FA_{0.88}Cs_{0.12}PbI_{3}  $   is partially exchanged with   $  PF_{6}^{-}  $  to introduce thin   $  FA_{0.88}Cs_{0.12}PbI_{3-x}(PF_{6})_{x}  $   layer between perovskite and spiro-MeOTAD HTM. Carrier life time is increased and defect density is decreased by introduction of an interlayer having   $  PF_{6}^{-}  $  ion, which eventually lead to higher FF and   $  V_{oc}  $  . In addition, current–voltage hysteresis is reduced and stability is much improved.

In other words, the thin interlayer   $  FA_{0.88}Cs_{0.12}PbI_{3-x}(PF_{6})_{x}  $   was formed on top of   $  FA_{0.88}Cs_{0.12}PbI_{3}  $  .

Photovoltaic parameters of devices based on different concentrations of FAPF $ _{6} $  post-treatment are compared and shown in Figure 5 and corresponding champion and average photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table S3 in the Supporting Information. Short-circuit photocurrent ( $ J_{sc} $ ) does not seem to be affected by post-treatment because of similar values before (22.72 ± 0.26 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ) and after (22.74 ± 0.29 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  for 1 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ , 22.78 ± 0.27 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  for 7 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ , 22.88 ± 0.23 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  for 14 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ , and 22.86 ± 0.26 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  for 28 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ ) as can be seen in Figure 5a. Little change in  $ J_{sc} $  is consistent with little change in absorbance (see Figure 3a) because post-treatment affects only the surface of the FA $ _{0.88} $ Cs $ _{0.12} $ PbI $ _{3} $  film. Average  $ V_{oc} $  increases continuously from 1.006 ± 0.009 to 1.053 ± 0.009 V as FAPF $ _{6} $  concentration increases from 0 to 28 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ , which is due to reduced nonradiation recombination along with significantly enhanced carrier lifetime (see Figure 3h and Table S2 (Supporting Information)). For higher concentrations, poor hole extraction is expected because inefficient charge separation is observed (see Figure S4 in the Supporting Information), which might be detrimental to FF. The average PCE is enhanced from 17.27 ± 0.36% to 18.55 ± 0.38% by post-treatment with 7 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  FAPF $ _{6} $ . It is noted that as compared to the pristine perovskite post-treatment is confirmed to show higher PCE regardless of concentration.

It is well known that a large number of grain boundaries (GBs) inevitably exist in polycrystalline perovskite films during crystallization. It is widely recognized that there are a lot of defects especially for deep-level defects at GBs $ ^{[63]} $  and degradation of perovskite films preferentially occurs at GBs $ ^{[64,65]} $  because ion migration is much faster at GBs than bulk. $ ^{[66,67]} $  Consequently, many efforts have been made to reduce and passivate GBs to enhance stability and reduce hysteresis of PSCs. $ ^{[67,68]} $  Device stability is not only related with components comprising a device but also interface defects and recombination. $ ^{[69]} $  As we discussed previously, FA $ _{0.88} $ Cs $ _{0.12} $ PbI $ _{3-x} $ (PF $ _{6} $ ) $ _{x} $  interlayer preferentially grew along GBs (Figure 4), which can passivate the defects at GBs and protect perovskite film from moisture, leading to improved moisture stability. We have demonstrated a novel interface material   $  FA_{0.88}Cs_{0.12}PbI_{3-x}(PF_{6})_{x}  $   that was formed in-situ on the perovskite film using ion exchange reaction during spin-coating. After introduction of the interlayer, photovoltaic performance was improved due to better   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF. In addition, stability under high relative humidity or one sun illumination was much improved. Improved performance and stability were mainly attributed to the reduced defect density and the enhanced carrier lifetime. Ion exchange reaction using molecular ion   $  PF_{6}^{-}  $  led to second phase on the top of the pristine perovskite layer as island morphology, which eventually resulted in PCE of 19.25%.

# Experimental Section

Materials: Formamidinium iodide FAI (FA:NH = CHNH₃) was synthesized as following: 13 mL of hydroiodic acid (57 wt% in water, Sigma-Aldrich, 99.99%) (in excess) was dropped slowly to a solution of 3 g formamidinium acetate (Aldrich, 99%) in methanol cooled at 0 °C and stirred at 0 °C for 2 h. The reaction was allowed to further proceed for 5 h at room temperature. The resultant solution was concentrated by rotary evaporator until dark brown precipitate was obtained. The resulting crude precipitate was washed several times with diethyl ether until the color of the precipitate changed to white. The product was purified by recrystallization from ethanol. After filtration, the white precipitate was collected and dried at 50 °C under vacuum for 24 h before use. Formamidinium hexafluorophosphate (FAPF₆) was purchased from Greatcell Solar company and used as received without further purification. All the other materials were purchased from commercial sources and used as received.

Device Fabrication: FTO-coated glass substrates (Pilkington, TEC-8, 8 Ω per sq) were patterned by laser etching and then cleaned successively by sonication for 15 min with detergent, deionized water, and ethanol. After the substrates were further cleaned with UV ozone treatment for 20 min, TiO₂ blocking layer (bl-TiO₂) was deposited on the FTO-coated glass by repeatedly spin-coating the solution of titanium disopropoxide dis(acetylacetonate) (Sigma-Aldrich, 75 wt% in isopropanol) in 1-butanol (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.8%) for three times at 2000 rpm for 20 s, and dried at 125 °C for 5 min. A mesoporous TiO₂ (mp-TiO₂) layer was deposited on top of the bl-TiO₂ layer by spin-coating a diluted TiO₂ paste (50 nm sized TiO₂ nanoparticles, terpineol, ethylcellulose, and lauric acid with a nominal composition of 1.25:6:0.9:0.1 in wt%) in anhydrous 1-butanol (0.1 g mL⁻¹) at 2000 rpm for 20 s, which was dried at 125 °C for 5 min and then annealed at 550 °C for 1 h. After cooling down to room temperature, the mp-TiO₂ film was exposed to ultraviolet/ozone (UVO) for 20 min before being treated with 0.02 M aqueous TiCl₄ (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.9%) solution at 90 °C for 10 min. The TiCl₄-treated film was cleaned with deionized water and annealed at 500 °C for 30 min. The mp-TiO₂ film was treated again by UVO for 20 min prior to deposition of perovskite film. 1.5 M FA₀.₈S₀.₁₂PbI₃ perovskite precursor solution was prepared by dissolving FAI (0.2269 g), CsI (0.0468 g, Alpha Aesar, 99.99%), and PbI₂ (0.6085 g, Alpha Aesar, 99.9985) in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (0.106 mL, Sigma-Aldrich, 99.9%), and N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) (0.894 mL, Sigma-Aldrich, 99.8%), where concentration of precursor solution was calculated according to the content of Pb²⁺ and the molar ratio of DMSO/Pb²⁺ is 1:1. Before preparation of perovskite films, hydroiodic acid solution (57 wt% in water, Sigma-Aldrich, 99.99%) was added into the perovskite precursor solution, where V(precursor):V(hydroiodic acid) = 20:1. Perovskite film was prepared through spin-coating as-prepared precursor solution at 4000 rpm for 25 s where 500 μL diethyl ether was poured onto films at 15 s before program ended and then the films were annealed at 150 °C for 30 min. For FAPF₆-treated devices, 50 μL different concentrations of FAPF₆ (1, 7, 14, and 28 mg mL⁻¹) in IPA was spin-coated on the as-prepared FA₀.₈S₀.₁₂PbI₃ perovskite film at 5000 rpm for 30 s and the resultant films were annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. Subsequently, the FAPF₆-treated films were washed with IPA at 5000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 100 °C for 5 min. After cooling down to room temperature, 20 μL of 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobifluorene (spiro-MeOTAD) solution was spin-coated atop the perovskite layer at 3000 rpm for 30 s. The spiro-MeOTAD solution was prepared by mixing 72.3 mg spiro-MeOTAD (Merck), 28.8 μL of 4-tert-butyl pyridine (t-BP), and 17.5 μL of lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI) stock solution (520 mg Li-TSFI in 1 mL acetonitrile (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.8%)) in 1 mL chlorobenzene. Finally, Au electrode was deposited on the spiro-MeOTAD-coated film by thermal evaporation at a constant evaporation rate of 0.3 Å s⁻¹. FA₀.₈S₀.₁₂PbI₃₋ₓ(PF₆)ₓ (x = 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.40, 0.60, and 0.88) films were independently prepared by the same method with control FA₀.₈S₀.₁₂PbI₃ perovskite film except for the partial replacement of FAI with FAPF₆.

Characterization: Photocurrent density-voltage (J-V) curves were measured using a solar simulator equipped with 450 W Xenon lamp (Newport 6279 NS) and a Keithley 2400 source meter. Light intensity was adjusted to AM 1.5G one sun (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) illumination with an NREL-calibrated Si solar cell. A metal mask with an aperture area of 0.125 cm  $  ^{2}  $  was applied on top of the cell. The IPCE spectra measurement was performed on an IPCE system (PV measurements) under DC mode, where the monochromatic beam was supplied by a 75 W Xenon lamp (USHIO). UV-visible spectra were obtained with UV/Vis spectrometer (Lambda 45, Perkin Elmer). SEM measurement was performed on a field-emission SEM (JSM7000F). Steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) measurements were carried out using a fluorescence lifetime spectrometer (QuantaurusTau C11367-12, HAMAMATSU) with the excitation of a 464 nm laser (PLP-10, HAMAMATSU) pulsed at a frequency of 10 MHz for state-state PL and 500 kHz for time-resolved PL. Time-resolved PL was detected using a time-correlated single-photon counting (TCSPC) technique to measure

the spontaneous photoluminescence decay. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) spectra were obtained by Bruker AXS (D8 advance, Bruker Corporation) using Cu Kα radiation at a scan rate of   $  4^{\circ}  $   min  $  ^{-1}  $  . XPS were obtained on ESCALAB 250 XPS system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with Al Kα X-ray radiation (1486.6 eV). Impedance spectra were measured in the dark using an Autolab 302B in the frequency range of 0.1 Hz to 1 MHz. The ToF-SIMS measurement (TOF-SIMS, Model-TOF-SIMS 5 of ION-TOF GmbH, Germany) were carried out with the pulsed primary ions from a Cs  $  ^{+}  $   (1 keV) liquid-metal ion gun for the sputtering and a Bi  $  ^{+}  $   pulsed primary ion beam for the analysis (25 keV). The sputtering was performed on a   $  150 \times 150 \mu m^{2}  $   area and the analysis was carried out on a   $  40 \times 40 \mu m^{2}  $   area. It is noted that the thickness of each layer can be determined by the erosion rate of the cesium primary ion beam but the rate is different in different materials. Consequently, the thickness of each layer cannot be simply decided by the sputtering time. Considering the extremely high sensitivity of ToF-SIMS and the inevitable rough of each layer, all the ion intensity curves extended their regions and overlapped at the interfaces.
