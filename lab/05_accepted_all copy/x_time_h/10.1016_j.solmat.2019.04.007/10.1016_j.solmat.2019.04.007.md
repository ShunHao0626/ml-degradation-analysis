# 1. Introduction

The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on lead trihalide perovskite materials (MAPbX $ _3 $ , MA = CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3^+ $ ; X = Cl $ ^- $ , I $ ^- $ , Br $ ^- $ ) has been improved rapidly, which has increased to more than 23% in the past few years by optimizing the preparation process of perovskite films and tuning the perovskite compositions [1-4], Despite the encouraging PCE of these small-area PSCs fabricated in the laboratory, the PSCs are still a long way from practical application. One main reason may be that the perovskite materials are too sensitive to the external environment, such as humidity and temperature [5-7], which is not conducive to the stability of device performance. The other is that some technologies involving the preparation of these high-efficiency PSCs, such as high temperature sintering of TiO $ _2 $  electron transport layer (ETL) [8,9] and vacuum deposition for perovskite layer [10,11], are too complicated and expensive, which is not conducive to the large-scale production and cost reduction of PSCs. In order to overcome these two problems, a great deal of effort has been made to study the stable and efficient PSCs prepared by low temperature solution method. Compared with organic materials, inorganic materials tend to have better stability and charge mobility. Therefore, one of these efforts is to use ETL, such as ZnO [12,13], TiO $ _2 $  [8,14,15], SnO $ _2 $  [16,17], CdS [18,19], or inorganic hole

# ABSTRACT

To improve the power conversion efficiency (PCE) and stability of inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs) prepared in humid air (RH = 55%), a new zinc oxide (ZnO):TIPD composite film is inserted between PCBM layer and Ag electrode for the first time. Compared to the single PCBM layer, the formed PCBM/ZnO:TIPD electron transport layer (ETL) has better coverage on the perovskite layer and more effective charge extraction ability from the perovskite layer. The insertion of ZnO:TIPD film effectively suppresses the charge recombination at perovskite/ETL interface. Besides, the PCBM/ZnO:TIPD layer has better ability to prevent moisture from penetrating into the perovskite layer than single PCBM film. For these reasons, the PCE and stability of the PSCs with the PCBM/ZnO:TIPD are significantly improved compared to the PSCs with single PCBM (or ZnO:TIPD) layer. The highest efficiency of the cell with PCBM/ZnO:TIPD ETL reaches   $  (13.7 \pm 0.4)\%  $   in ambient atmosphere, which is 1.20 times that of the PSCs with PCBM only.

Besides, other strategies, such as solvent treatment of perovskite layer and composite electron transport layer [22,23], have also been reported to improve cell efficiency and stability.

Recently, the   $  CuInS_{2}  $   (CIS) film prepared by low temperature solution method has been used by our group as an effective HTL for inverted PSCs [24,25], which can also effectively promote the stability of PSCs in ambient atmosphere with air and 55% relative humidity compared to the commonly used HTL, such as PEDOT:PSS [11]. Nevertheless, this inverted PSCs with   $  CuInS_{2}  $   HTL and PCBM ETL are still poor in PCE and stability. One main reason is that the PCBM layer does not completely cover the perovskite layer causing serious charge recombination and PCBM itself is poor in resisting moisture penetration into perovskite layer. Moreover, it is also not a good way to increase the coverage of PCBM on perovskite thin films by increasing the thickness of PCBM layer due to the low charge mobility (typically   $  2 \times 10^{-3} \, cm^{2} \, V^{-1} \, s^{-1}  $  ) of PCBM [26,27]. Therefore, the electron transporting material with better coverage and faster charge transport ability than PCBM is needed for further improvement of the performance and stability of PSCs with   $  CuInS_{2}  $   HTL. # 2. Experimental details

# 2.1. Materials and precursor preparation

Titanium (diisopropoxide) bis(2,4-pentanedionate) (TIPD), indium acetate   $  (\mathrm{In}(\mathrm{OAc})_{3}, 99.99\%)  $  , copper iodide (CuI, 99.999%), thiourea

(≥99.0%), 1-propionic acid (≥99.5%), n-Butylamine (≥99.0%), Aluminium oxide (  $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $  , 20 wt% in isopropanol) and silver wire (Ag, 99.999%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Methylammonium iodide (MAI, ≥99.5%) and lead iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  , ≥99.9%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp (China). [6,6]-phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (  $  PC_{60}BM  $  , 95 wt %) were purchased from Luminescence Technology Co. ZnO (20 wt% in isopropanol) was purchased from Aladdin Holdings Group. N,N-dimethyl formamide (DMF, anhydrous) and Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, anhydrous) were used as main solvents. Indium tin oxide-coated glass slides (ITO, ≤15 Ω/square) are cleaned before use.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

ITO substrates were first etched by Zn powders and cleaned, then the   $  CuInS_{2}  $   precursor solution was prepared by dissolving   $  In(OAc)_{3}  $ , CuI, and thiourea in a mixture of n-Butylamine and 1-propionic acid, which was spin-coated onto the ITO films at 2000 r.p.m. for 30 s. After that, the films were dried at 150 °C for 10 min, then heated to 250 °C and held for 15 min. More details are described elsewhere [24].   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   isopropanol dispersing solution was diluted by isopropanol at a volume ratio of 1–6, then spin-coated on ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}  $   film at 4000 r.p.m. for 30 s and annealed at 150 °C for 10 min to obtain ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}  $   film. The perovskite precursor using   $  PbI_{2}  $   was dissolved in mixed solvents of composition DMF: DMSO = 9:1 (v/v) (40% by weight) with a molar ratio of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I/PbI_{2} = 1:1  $ , which was spin-coated on the ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}  $   film. After the first 10 s of the spin coating process, the ethyl ether solution was quickly dropped at the film. After that, the samples were dried at 100 °C for 5 min to form ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films. Then, the CBZ solution containing PCBM (20 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $ ) was spin-coated on ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films to form ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM  $   films. Some of these samples were used to prepare ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM/ZnO:TIPD  $   thin films. ZnO isopropanol dispersing solution was first diluted with anhydrous isopropanol in accordance with the volume ratio of 1:200, then ZnO:TIPD isopropanol solution was prepared by adding 10 μl TIPD solution to 2 ml ZnO isopropanol diluents, which was further spin-coated on the ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM  $   to obtain ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM/ZnO:TIPD  $   thin films after drying (at 100 °C for 5 min). For the preparation of ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/ZnO:TIPD  $   film without PCBM, the as-prepared ZnO:TIPD isopropanol solution was directly spin-coated on the ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film. Finally, for the ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM  $ , ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM/ZnO:TIPD  $   and ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/ZnO:TIPD  $   films, 100 nm Ag electrode is evaporated to complete the device structure in a vacuum condition. The effective area for all as-prepared PSCs is 0.04 cm  $  ^{-2}  $ .

To investigate the effect of ZnO:TIPD film on the electron-hole separation process at the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/ETL  $   interface, the PL spectra and time-resolved PL decay transients of the ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM  $   and ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM/ZnO:TIPD  $   film are measured, as shown in Fig. 4a and b, respectively. The film with ZnO:TIPD film shows a decreased PL emission intensity than that of the film with only PCBM layer, implying a more effective charge transfer. This transfer process can be quantitatively analyzed by the charge recombination lifetime (  $  \tau  $  ) obtained by fitting the time-resolved PL decay data. Fig. 4b shows that the ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM/ZnO:TIPD  $   film exhibits a shorter   $  \tau  $   (13.77 ns) compared to that (16.30 ns) of the ITO/  $  CuInS_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PCBM  $   film. Obviously, for both films, the photogenerated holes in perovskite can be transferred to the   $  CuInS_{2}  $   layer through the   $  CuInS_{2}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   interface, and the photogenerated electrons can be transferred to the ETL layer through the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/ETL  $   interface. The only difference between these two films is the ETL layer. The former film contains both PCBM and ZnO:TIPD as ETL, but the latter film contains only PCBM. Therefore, the decrease of charge recombination lifetime is mainly attributed to ZnO:TIPD layer, which improves the separation efficiency of charges at the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/ETL  $   interface and promotes charge transfer from the perovskite layer to the PCBM/ZnO:TIPD layer.

It can be clearly seen that, for the forward scanning J-V curves, the PCE of the PSCs with PCBM/ZnO:TIPD layer is significantly improved by ZnO:TIPD layer compared to the PSC with only PCBM layer. Besides, the short-circuit photocurrent  $ (J_{\mathrm{sc}}) $  and fill factor (FF) have increased from  $ (18.5\pm 0.3)\mathrm{mA cm}^{-2} $  and 0.68 to  $ (20.3\pm 0.3)\mathrm{mA cm}^{-2} $  and 0.73, respectively. The main reason for the increased PCE,  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  ,and FF may be the more effective charge transfer in the cell with ZnO:TIPD layer from the perovskite to the ETL layer (i.e., ZnO:TIPD layer) compared with those parameters of the cell with only PCBM ETL, as confirmed by the PL lifetime (Fig. 4) and C-AFM results (Fig. 5). The low  $ V_{oc} $  (0.86) and FF (0.39) indicate that the charge recombination inside the PSC with only ZnO:TIPD layer is very serious. Since the electron mobility (130–230 cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ ) of ZnO is much higher than that (2 × 10 $ ^{-3} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ ) of PCBM and the conduction band level of ZnO matches that of the perovskite, the serious charge recombination in the cell with only ZnO:TIPD layer may be mainly due to less effective electron extraction from the perovskite. In addition, incomplete coverage of single PCBM layer on the rough CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  film surface may also result in direct contact between Ag electrode and the perovskite (Fig. 2b), and then the charge recombination may occur at the perovskite/Ag interface. The effect of ZnO:TIPD layer on the electronic charge transport of the cells is further studied by comparing J-V hysteresis, as shown in Fig. 6a. For the ITO/CuInS $ _2 $ /Al $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /PCBM/Ag cell, the PCE is (10.1 ± 0.5)% for reverse scan condition, which is only 89% of the forward scan efficiency (i.e., (11.4 ± 0.4)%). For the cell with ZnO:TIPD layer, the PCE is (13.1 ± 0.5)% for reverse scan condition, which is 96% of the forward scan efficiency (i.e., (13.7 ± 0.4)%). Apparently, the reverse scan efficiency of the cell with ZnO:TIPD layer is closer to the forward scan efficiency compared to the cell with only PCBM, suggesting a more efficient electron collection in the cell with ZnO:TIPD layer [34,35], which is consistent with the above results (Figs. 4 and 5). Fig. 6b shows the time-dependent variation of the normalized PCE for as-prepared PSCs, which was investigated by the J-V characteristics without any encapsulation process under glove-box conditions. The PCE of the cell with ZnO:TIPD layer gradually declined to 35.71% of the initial efficiency within hours, which is higher than that 82.12% of the cell with PCBM only. The improved stability can be attributed to two reasons. On the one hand, ZnO optimizes the contact properties of PCBM/Ag interface, promoting the effective extraction of charges [36,37]. On the other hand, the addition of TIPD reduces the voids between ZnO nanoparticles, hinders the infiltration of moisture into the perovskite active layer, and thus slows down the decay of the active layer.

Fig. 7a shows the dark current measure for as-prepared PSCS. The cell with ZnO:TIPD layer exhibits a higher rectification factor and a lower leakage current, suggesting a less charge recombination [38]. The charge recombination kinetics was further studied by the dependence of  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  and  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  on light intensity  $  I  $ . Fig. 7b and c shows the measured  $  \mathrm{In}(J_{\mathrm{sc}})  $  and  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  vary logarithmically with the natural logarithm of light intensity  $  \mathrm{In}(I)  $ , respectively. It can be seen from Fig. 7b that the slopes for the cells with and without ZnO:TIPD layer are 0.98 and 1.01, respectively, suggesting that the photo-generated charges do not have charge recombination. The relationship between  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  and the  $  \mathrm{In}(I)  $  shows that the slopes are 1.06 and 1.11  $  k_{\mathrm{b}}T / e  $  for the cell with and without ZnO:TIPD layer, respectively, which indicates that the monomolecular and bimolecular recombination processes exist in both cells and a greater degree of monomolecular recombination exists in the cell without ZnO:TIPD layer.

The measured EIS data were further fitted with an equivalent circuit shown in Fig. 8b [24]. The fitting parameters including  $  R_{\mathrm{s}}  $ ,  $  R_{\mathrm{CT1}}  $ , and  $  R_{\mathrm{CT2}}  $  were presented in Table 2. For comparison, the EIS spectra of the cell with only ZnO:TIPD layer was also measured (Fig. S6) and the obtained EIS data were further fitted (Table S2).  $  R_{\mathrm{s}}  $  is the series resistance of the PSCs,  $  R_{\mathrm{CT1}}  $  and  $  R_{\mathrm{CT2}}  $  represent the interface charge-transfer resistance at the CuInS $ _2 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  and CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /ETL interfaces, respectively. From the results of fitting (Table 2), the charge transfer resistance at

these two interfaces decreases significantly after deposition of ZnO:TIPD film on PCBM layer, which is beneficial to the electron-hole separation and charge extraction from the perovskite layer to the  $ \mathrm{CuInS}_2 $  and ETL layers and thus effectively improves the  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $ , FF, and PCE. However, from Table S2, it can be found that the charge transfer resistance at the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $ :TIPD interface is larger than that at the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 / \mathrm{PCBM} $ , indicating that the single ZnO:TIPD film does not exhibit better charge extraction ability than PCBM. The main reason may be that the particle size of PCBM is much smaller than that of zinc oxide, which makes PCBM more closely contact with perovskite, facilitating charge transfer from the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  to PCBM. EIS results demonstrate that the deposited ZnO:TIPD film combined with the PCBM has stronger charge extraction ability compared to the single PCBM layer.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, the inverted ITO/CuInS $ _2 $ /Al $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /PCBM/ZnO:TIPD/Ag solar cells are fabricated by low temperature solution method in humid air (RH = 55%), in which the ZnO:TIPD composite film was inserted as an electron transport material between the PCBM layer and Ag electrode. Our experimental results demonstrate that the introduction of ZnO:TIPD composite film effectively improved the electron-hole separation at the CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /ETL interface and charge extract from the CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  films to the PCBM layer, which mainly helped in increasing the charge separation in the PSCs and thus contributed to the enhancement of PCE. The highest PCE of (13.7 ± 0.4)% was achieved by the PSCs with both PCBM and ZnO:TIPD layers, which shows an 20% improvement compared to that ((11.4 ± 0.4)% achieved by the PSCs with single PCBM layer. Besides, due to the good barrier effect of ZnO:TIPD film on moisture permeation and more efficient charge extraction, the PSCs with ZnO:TIPD film showed much better stability in humid air (RH = 55%).
