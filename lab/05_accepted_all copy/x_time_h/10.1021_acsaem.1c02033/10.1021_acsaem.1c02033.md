# TADF Molecule as an Interfacial Layer with Cascade Energy Alignment Enabling High Open-Circuit Voltage for 3D/2D Perovskite Solar Cells

Xia Chen,  $  ^{||}  $   Jiasen Zhang,  $  ^{||}  $   Chang Liu,  $  ^{*}  $   Qiang Lou, KangHui Zheng, Xu Yin, Lin Xie, Pan Wen, Cuirong Liu,  $  ^{*}  $   and Ziyi Ge  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: The minimum interface recombination and maximum carrier extraction of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are important for achieving a better power conversion efficiency (PCE). According to the recent investigations, 3D/2D hybrid perovskite systems have been recognized as an effective approach to improve the efficiency and stability of PSCs. However, a large highest occupied molecular orbital energy level gap between a 2D perovskite and the hole transport layer (HTL) spiro-OMeTAD would cause energy losses at the interface, which limit open-circuit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{oc}}) $  and thus PCE of the PSCs. In this work, we utilized a thermally activated delayed fluorescence molecule M1 stacked on the 3D/2D hybrid perovskite films to engineer the 3D/2D perovskite/HTL interface. The ultrathin interfacial layer of M1 forms a cascade energy alignment between 3D/2D perovskites and a HTL, as a means to circumvent energy losses, which consequently improves the efficiency of PSCs from 19.56 to  $ 21.48\% $  with an outstanding increase of  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  from 1.18 to  $ 1.23\mathrm{V} $ . The charge separation and carrier recombination in PSCs were analyzed by photoluminescence and impedance characterization, from which, we deduce that a suitable energy level structure can reduce interface charge recombination and promote a minimal open-circuit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{oc}}) $  loss, which facilitate the improvement of PSC performances.

KEYWORDS: perovskite solar cells, energy level alignment, less recombination loss, high open-circuit voltage, interface

# INTRODUCTION

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted considerable attention with remarkable progress of power conversion efficiency (PCE) over 25% being achieved recently.  $  ^{1-4}  $   They are considered to be the most competitive in further commercialization because of their low cost, outstanding photoelectronic characteristics, flexible processing, and portable feature.  $  ^{5-8}  $   Traditional 3D/2D perovskite hybrid systems have been extensively studied due to their high efficiency and excellent stability.  $  ^{9-12}  $   However, a large energy level gap between the perovskite film of the 3D/2D hybrid system and the hole transport layer (HTL) would lead to energy losses at the interface, which limit the photovoltaic efficiency of PSCs.  $  ^{13}  $   Typical PSCs consist of multiple layers with interfaces stacked together, and the charge extraction and transfer of the devices are affected by the interfaces between multilayers, especially

Snaith et al. gave an insight into the high-mobility hole extraction layer (SWCNTS) based on a perovskite absorber to suppress interface recombination by quickly removing holes in the interface region, this buffer layer improves the charge-selective contact and perovskite energy level offset to reduce the recombination loss, leading to the voltage loss of PSCs less than 340 mV.  $  ^{[22]}  $   Besides, in order to suppress the carrier recombination, a dual-functional nanomaterial 2D black phosphorene is introduced to the perovskite interface, which forms favorable band energy alignment and energy cascade for efficient carrier extraction.  $  ^{[23]}  $   Up till now, the energy level arrangement between the perovskite layer and the adjacent charge transport layer still needs to be optimized. Accessing a simultaneous defect passivation to suppress non-radiative recombination and interface engineering to adjust the energy level arrangement would be an effective strategy to reduce the voltage loss of PSCs.

In this study, we stacked a thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) material at the perovskite/HTL interface, and the material was named M1. It is proved that M1 has the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy level suitable for HTL, so that the perovskite/HTL interface ensures a good charge transport characteristic. M1 consists of 9,9-

dimethyl-9,10 dihydroacidine and benzophenone as a donor and an acceptor, respectively, as shown in Figure 1d, the introduced dodecyloxy chain increases the solubility, resulting in better film formation and higher film quality. On the one hand, there are reports that M1 endows the non-doped organic light-emitting diodes with ultrahigh device performance, indicating that M1 molecules have good carrier transport capabilities.  $  ^{24,25}  $   On the other hand, the electricity-absorbing group endows the molecules with a preferred arrangement and a high degree of orderly alignment, which enhance molecular interactions and promote hole transfer. We believe that the advantage of M1 as the interface layer between perovskite/HTL is that it has energy level cascade alignment and outstanding carrier mobility, which counteract the interface charge recombination and make the device show high efficiency and low voltage loss. By evaluating the photovoltaic performance and hole mobility, and using ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS), electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) analysis, and photoluminescence (PL) measurements, we confirmed that the increase in   $  V_{oc}  $   is mainly attributed to the appropriate energy level structure that reduces the recombination of the interface charge. This also creates a foundation for future interface energy level modification research.

# CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, we reported a TADF molecule M1 as a interfacial layer between the perovskite and HTL. The photovoltaic performance and carrier/recombination dynamics of PSCs were characterized using UPS, EIS analysis, and PL measurements. The thin layer of M1 with a well-matched energy level alignment at perovskite/HTL enhances hole extraction and reduces interfacial carrier recombination, which contribute to a high open-circuit voltage. The PSCs with the 3D/2D/M1/HTL structure exhibited the best PCE of 21.48% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.23 V, higher than those of 3D (19.56%, 1.18 V) and 3D/2D PSCs (20.60%, 1.21 V). Our work provides a simple strategy to adjust energy alignments at perovskite/HTL and gives insights into interfacial engineering toward achieving high   $  V_{oc}  $   values.

Material Synthesis.   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloidal solution (15 wt % in water) was purchased from Alfa Aesar, and dimethylformamide (DMF), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), isopropanol (IPA), and chlorobenzene (CBZ) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Device Fabrication. The ITO-coated glass substrates were cleaned in the order of surfactant aqueous solution, DI water, acetone, and ethanol in an ultrasonic bath for 15 min.
