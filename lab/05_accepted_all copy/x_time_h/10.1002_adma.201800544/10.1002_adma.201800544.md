# Grain-Boundary “Patches” by In Situ Conversion to Enhance Perovskite Solar Cells Stability

Lang Liu, Sheng Huang, Yue Lu, Pengfei Liu, Yizhou Zhao, Congbo Shi, Siyu Zhang, Jiafeng Wu, Haizheng Zhong, Manling Sui, Huanping Zhou, Haibo Jin, Yujing Li,* and Qi Chen*

The power conversion efficiency of organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells has increased rapidly, but the device stability remains a big challenge. Previous studies show the grain boundary (GB) can facilitate ion migration and initiate device degradation. Herein, methimazole (MMI) is employed for the first time to construct a surface “patch” by in situ converting residual   $  PbI_{2}  $   at GBs. The resultant MMI–  $  PbI_{2}  $   complex can effectively suppress ion migration and inhibit diffusion of the metal electrodes. The origin of the surface “patch” effect and their working mechanisms are investigated experimentally and theoretically at the microscopic level. It hence demonstrates a simple and effective method to prolong the device stability in the context of GB engineering, which could be extensively applied to perovskite-based optoelectronics.

Perovskite solar cells have attracted great attention due to their excellent optoelectronic properties and ease of fabrication.  $  ^{[1]}  $   Within only a few years, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells has boosted from 3.8% to 22.7%, making the organic–inorganic halide perovskites (PVSK) one of the most promising photovoltaic materials.  $  ^{[2]}  $   However, their

We previously demonstrated the reversible reaction between PbI $ _{2} $  and CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ I (MAI) to form perovskite, wherein the MAI preferentially escaped from GBs and formed PbI $ _{2} $  upon annealing.[14c] Upon the MMI treatment, the residual PbI $ _{2} $  further served as reservoir to form the MMI–PbI $ _{2} $  complex like patches at GBs (Figure 1a). It should be noted that residual PbI $ _{2} $  facilitates the formation of MMI–PbI $ _{2} $  complex. After the identical treatment, samples without residual PbI $ _{2} $  did not exhibit detectable signal of the complex in the XRD pattern (Figure S1, Supporting Information). However, the crystallographic orientation changed after MMI treatment, wherein the intensity of (220) plane enhances significantly (Figure S1, Supporting Information). Moreover, MMI treatment is preferred to be conducted on the perovskite films. And if the MMI added into the precursor solution during film growth, we did not obtain MMI–PbI $ _{2} $  complex in the resultant films (Figure S2, Supporting Information). To better understand the conversion process, we also investigated the influence of reaction time on the formation of MMI–PbI $ _{2} $  complex. We extended the holding time of MMI/IPA on the surface of perovskite films. Figure S3a in the Supporting Information show the XRD patterns of the film treated with different holding time. The relative content of MMI–PbI $ _{2} $  complex could be evaluated by the peak intensity ratio regarding MMI–PbI $ _{2} $  complex (9.85°) to the (110) plane of MAPbI $ _{3} $  (14.08°). As shown in Figure S3b in the Supporting Information, the relative intensity increased gradually with prolonged holding time. However, further prolonged holding time led to radical change of morphology (Figure S4b,c, Supporting Information) in the resultant film. Therefore, in our experiment, we held the MMI/IPA solution for 3 s to achieve the tradeoff between the morphology and the content of MMI–PbI $ _{2} $  complex (Figure 1c; Figure S4a, Supporting Information).

Since the   $  PbI_{2}  $   interacts with MMI through Pb–S coordination,  $  ^{[20]}  $   we also conducted Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) to provide detailed evidence. The FTIR spectra (Figure S5, Supporting Information) suggested the fingerprint regions of S=C stretching at 742 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for pristine MMI was shifted to 737 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   after MMI reacted with   $  PbI_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[21]}  $   This down shift of the S=C vibration in MMI is indicative of the formation of an MMI–  $  PbI_{2}  $   complex, wherein the S=C bond strength is weakened upon interaction with   $  PbI_{2}  $  . In addition, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) pattern also confirmed the interaction between Pb and S. Figure 1e shows the high-resolution spectra of the Pb 4f. For the reference film, two main peaks located at 138.0 and 142.8 eV were observed, which were assigned to the Pb 4f 7/2 and Pb 4f 5/2, respectively. Besides, another two trace peaks located at 136.0 and 141.3 eV were also found after scrutiny, which were assigned to the metallic Pb.  $  ^{[22]}  $   It indicated the existence of   $  V_{1}  $   in the reference, and both serve as

nonradiative recombination centers.  $  ^{[23]}  $   In the sample with MMI treatment, the existence of S at the surface was evidenced by observation of obvious signals from S 2p at 162.6 and 163.8 eV. Moreover, the peaks from Pb 4f shifted to higher binding energy as compared to that of reference. It thus confirms the MMI molecule anchors to the perovskite surface via Pb–S coordination. Interestingly, we did not see any detectable signals from metallic Pb in the samples with MMI treatment. We tentatively assume the MMI treatment could reduce   $  V_{I}  $  , as will be further discussed later.

In addition, MMI treatment enhances the quality of the resultant polycrystalline film as well. On one hand, the polycrystalline film showed an average size of 300 nm slightly larger than that of the reference (Figure 1b,c; Figure S6, Supporting Information). The increase of grain size may be ascribed to the Ostwald ripening process which was popularly used to enhance the perovskite film quality.[24] Generally, the Ostwald ripening process involves two coupled steps: the dissolution of small-sized crystals due to the higher surface energy and the formation of large-sized crystals to reduce surface energy. In our case, the addition of MMI could greatly promote the dissolution of unstable small  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  crystals with the assistance of coordination between  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  and S, which could efficiently promote the coarsening process and therefore lead to larger grains. On the other hand, the sample with MMI treatment exhibited better optical properties. As shown in Figure S7a in the Supporting Information, the MMI-treated film demonstrated higher absorbance than that of the reference film. The photoluminescence (PL) intensity also increased significantly (Figure S7b, Supporting Information). We further conducted the time-resolved PL (TRPL) measurements, as shown in Figure S7c in the Supporting Information, the MMI-treated sample displayed longer life time. We fitted the decay curves (Figure S7c, Supporting Information) by the biexponential equation:  $ Y = A_{1}\exp (-t / \tau_{1}) + A_{2}\exp (-t / \tau_{2}) $ , where the short lifetime  $ \tau_{1} $  stands for the surface nonradiative recombination, and the long lifetime  $ \tau_{2} $  is the bulk recombination (Table S1, Supporting Information).[17] For the MMI-treated film, the surface/bulk nonradiative recombination lifetime was 33.9/269.8 ns, which was much longer than that of the reference film (4.9/125.3 ns).

Device performance has been improved significantly by forming the complex as “patches” upon MMI treatment. We evaluated the shelf lifetime, wherein devices without encapsulation were stored in nitrogen under dark condition. As shown in Figure 3a, the efficiency of reference device gradually decreased to zero after 1440 h storage. Related J-V curves and performance parameters were provided in the Figure S12 in the Supporting Information. While the device with surface patches still maintained about 94% of its initial efficiency even after 2184 h (over 3 month) storage, which confirmed MMI treatment could effectively enhance device long-term stability. We further explored the device stability upon illumination, which is essential for solar cells. As shown in Figure 3b, the efficiency for reference device dropped rapidly, almost close to zero after only 360 h continuous illumination at 1 sun. While the MMI-treated device maintained almost 80% of its initial efficiency even under continuous illumination for 672 h (other parameters were also provided in Figure S13 in the Supporting Information). We inspected the J-V curves of the devices at different illumination time (Figure S14, Supporting Information). For the reference device, an obvious injection “barrier” was observed, which was possibly caused by the formation of insulating AgI due to iodine migration.  $  ^{[26]}  $

The complex patch improves the device stability upon illumination, and we try to reveal the underlying mechanisms. We first investigated the intrinsic stability of the as-treated film upon MMI treatment. It is reported that the perovskite degrades and releases iodine in the presence of oxygen and light,  $  ^{[5]}  $   and iodine absorbs at 500 nm. During the test, the films were immersed in toluene under continuous illumination at 1 sun. In the reference case, the toluene solution turned

pink and the dark brownish perovskite film gradually turned to yellow (Figure S15a, Supporting Information). By checking the UV-absorption spectrum (Figure 4a), the reference solution exhibited an obvious characteristic absorption peak at around  $ 500\mathrm{nm} $ , whose intensity become stronger with prolonged illumination time. XRD measurements further show the reference film degraded rapidly (Figure S15c, Supporting Information), which suggests that the reference suffered severe degradation upon illumination. In the MMI-treated sample however, negligible degradation was observed: the toluene solution was colorless after  $ 3\mathrm{h} $  illumination without detectable iodine; and the film was still dark brownish with strong XRD signal intensity (Figure S15b,d, Supporting Information). It thus infers that MMI treatment could effectively suppress film degradation.

Subsequently, simulation was performed to explore mechanistic insights regarding how MMI improves the intrinsic stability of the perovskite film. Though polycrystalline film exhibits good defects tolerance, there are shallow defects (e.g.,   $  V_{I}  $  ) generated inevitably at the GBs during device processing and operation. Our calculation results show the   $  V_{I}  $   defects were apt to form in the typical perovskite film, showing the negative formation energy in Figure 4b. It agrees well with our observation of the metallic Pb signal in the XPS spectrum (Figure 1e). These   $  V_{I}  $   defects would further adsorb oxygen to create the   $  O_{V}  $   defects. They further snatched the photogenerated electron to decompose the film via superoxide species.  $  ^{[25,26]}  $   Upon MMI treatment however, the degradation process was significantly suppressed due to the following reasons. First, the formation of   $  V_{I}  $   was extremely difficult (Figure 4b). As shown in Figure 4c, the S would interact with Pb and I. In addition to the enhanced intrinsic film stability, we also found the MMI treatment could significantly promote electrode stability. After about 1 month continuous illumination in nitrogen, we found the electrode of the reference devices changed radically. As shown in Figure S18 in the Supporting Information, we found the silver lost its metallic luster, and the underlying purple hole-transport layer (HTL) could be directly observed. In contrast, the MMI-treated sample demonstrated negligible change. To gain the insight into the aging mechanism, we used the high angle annular dark field scanning transmission electron microscopy (HAADF-STEM) and energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) spectroscopy to analyze the spatial distribution of Ag in the aged devices (Figure 5). The perovskite containing Pb and I elements was confined in a layer of  $ \approx 500\mathrm{nm} $ , and the

Ag electrode layer with   $  \approx  $  100 nm thick was above the perovskite which was separated by HTL. Surprisingly, significant Ag signals were detected in the ITO layer in the reference after 1 month illumination (Figure 5a). It indicates substantial diffusion of Ag through the entire perovskite layer to the front electrode. In the sample with MMI treatment however, the silver just stayed in a confined layer without apparent diffusion (Figure 5b).

The line scanning profiles further provide a quantitative analysis to confirm this conclusion. As shown in Figure 5c,d, the reference exhibited a sharp Ag peak with a width of only  $ \approx 50\mathrm{nm} $  at its half maximum (distance  $ = 100~\mathrm{nm} $ ), whereas the Ag width remains  $ \approx 100~\mathrm{nm} $  for the sample with MMI treatment. Furthermore, a distinct bump was observed at the far end of ITO side for the reference, which was in good agreement with the EDX elemental mapping. It thus suggests the top Ag electrode diffused all the way through the device and became thinner itself. The detailed diffusion mechanism is related to the GB,[7] which is under investigation now. However, as for the MMI-treated sample, we did not detect traceable Ag signal over the device rather than the top electrode layer. It is thus claimed that the MMI treatment could effectively inhibit Ag electrode diffusion.

In summary, the GB engineering is demonstrated to improve the stability and efficiency of perovskite solar cells by adopting surface “patches,” which are formed at the GBs by in situ conversion of residual   $  PbI_{2}  $   to the complex upon MMI treatment. As a result, the device achieved a best PCE of 20.10% (stabilized at 19.86%) with negligible J-V hysteresis, which is attributed to the improved film quality and enhanced carrier behavior at the HTL/perovskite interface due to MMI treatment. Moreover, the as-treated device exhibited prolonged lifetime, which retained about 80% efficiency after 672 h continuous illumination at 1 Sun with silver electrodes. It reveals that the “patches” effectively suppress metal electrode diffusion and ion migration, as evidenced by HAADF-STEM and EDX analysis. We believe this simple and feasible GB engineering technique could be extensively applied to other mixed perovskite optoelectronics, paving the way for practical use of these materials.
