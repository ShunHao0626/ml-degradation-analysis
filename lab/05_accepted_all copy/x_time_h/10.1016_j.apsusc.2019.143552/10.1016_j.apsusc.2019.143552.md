# Abstract

ZnO as electron transfer layer in perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has a lot of advantages including high electron mobility and low processing temperature. However, because of the reported instabilities of perovskite layers deposited on ZnO, the application of ZnO in perovskite solar cells has been restricted. In this paper, highly ordered ZnO nanorod arrays have been synthesized by a polymer soft template method, and then modified by ZnSe via a successive ion layer adsorption and reaction method (SILAR). Field emission scanning electron microscope, X-ray diffractometer, UV-vis spectrophotometer, fluorophotometer and photovoltaic measurements were used to study the effects of ZnSe modification on the morphology, structure, light absorption and charge transfer of perovskite films and the performance of the PSCs based on ZnO nanorod arrays. The photoelectric conversion efficiency (PCE) of the PSC with ZnSe modification significantly enhanced by 27%, as compared to that of the pristine one, which is attributed to the suitable band alignment, better carrier separation and higher charge transfer efficiency with ZnSe modification. Meanwhile, ZnSe modification can also improve the air stability of the PSCs. This study promotes the development of ZnO nanorod arrays based PSCs with some new approaches and provides a useful reference.

Keywords: ZnO nanorod arrays; ZnSe modification; Successive ion layer adsorption and reaction method; Perovskite film; Perovskite solar cell.

# 1. Introduction

Exploring an effective way to utilize solar energy is needed imperatively with the intensification of the global energy crisis. Hybrid halide perovskite perovskite materials have got more and more attention in recent years due to their long diffusion length   $  [1-3]  $  , wide absorption range, cheapness, and easy process   $  [4, 5]  $  . These characteristics make them outstanding candidates for fabricating highly efficient photovoltaic devices   $  [6-10]  $  . Zinc oxide

(ZnO) is a wide band-gap semiconductor ( $ \sim 3.37 $  eV) and has a large exciton binding energy ( $ \sim 60 $  meV). In addition, ZnO has a high electron mobility (200-205 cm $ ^2 $  $ \cdot $ V $ ^{-1} $ s $ ^{-1} $ ) [11] which is much higher than that of traditional TiO $ _2 $  (0.1-4cm $ ^2 $  $ \cdot $ V $ ^{-1} $ s $ ^{-1} $ ) [12], which is conducive to decrease the electrical resistance and increase the electron transport efficiency. Therefore, ZnO has been widely used in light-emitting diodes [13], field effect transistors [14], ultraviolet lasers [15], piezoelectric gated diode [16] and solar cells [12, 17, 18]. Especially, in photovoltaic application, ZnO is considered as an extremely promising material for ETL in PSCs. ZnO nanorod arrays (NAs) have been widely used as ETL over the past years due to its excellent properties including fast electron mobility, high light absorption and giving a direct channel for electron extraction [19]. Many synthesis methods of ZnO NAs with controlled morphology have been developed, such as solution-based synthesis methods [20, 21], gas-solution-solid method [22], chemical vapor deposition [23], electrochemical deposition method [24], pulsed laser deposition [25], and so on. In this paper, highly ordered ZnO NAs were prepared by a simple, cheap PVP soft template method.

Generally, the performance of PSCs based on ZnO NAs is lower than that of mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  based PSCs owing to the serious charge recombination and the instability of perovskite/ZnO bilayer. In order to further improve the performance of PSCs, researchers have done a lot of meaningful works such as tuning the bandgap of the perovskite layer by designing the iodide concentration gradient to enhance hole extraction [26], exploring 2D lead-free hybrid halide perovskite using superatom anions with tunable electronic properties [27], utilizing CdSe quantum dot/PCBM composite as ETL [28]. Besides these methods, developing the core/shell heterostructures for ETL materials is an important method to improve the performance of PSC due to the fact that the band alignment of core/shell heterostructures can separate electrons and holes into different spatial regions, which may increase the carrier lifetime [29, 30]. Core/shell heterostructures like  $ \mathrm{ZnO / TiO_2} $  [31-33],  $ \mathrm{ZnO / CdS} $  [34] and  $ \mathrm{ZnO / SnO_2} $  [35, 36] have been utilized for ETL materials in PSCs owing to their better ability of light-trapping, longer carrier lifetimes and better chemical stability compared to those of the pristine nanostructures. Zinc selenide (ZnSe) is an II-VI semiconductor with a high electron mobility  $ (200 - 280~\mathrm{cm}^2\cdot \mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1}) $  [37], which is a useful characteristic to ETL in PSCs. Moreover, ZnSe can block ultraviolet light to inhibit the decomposition of the perovskite absorber owing to that ZnSe has a high absorbance in the

ultraviolet region. More importantly, the valence and conduction band of ZnSe are staggered relative to those of ZnO, which is of great benefit to the electron transmission, suppressing charge recombination. The heterostructure with ZnO and ZnSe is of great benefit to the electron transmission from ZnSe to ZnO due to that the redistribution of electrons in a ZnO@ZnSe core/shell heterostructure via Fermi level alignment would cause marked upward shifts of the conduction band edges of ZnO and downward shifts of the conduction band edges of ZnSe [38]. Therefore, ZnO@ZnSe core/shell heterostructure has a great potential to enhance the photoelectric conversion efficiency (PCE) and stability of ZnO NAs based PSCs by promoting separation of photogenerated carriers, reducing carrier recombination and preventing direct contact between  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and ZnO. Although many researches on the ZnO/ZnSe heterostructure have been reported for photocatalysis [39-41], the study on utilizing ZnO@ZnSe NAs as ETL materials in PSCs was rarely reported until now.

# 2. Experimental works

# 2.1 Preparation of ZnO@ZnSe NAs

The complete scheme of ZnO@ZnSe NAs fabrication is shown in Fig.1 (a). Laser-patterned FTO glasses (sheet resistivity of   $  14\Omega  $   per sq) were cleaned sequentially by acetone, absolute ethanol and deionized water, and then they were dried with   $  N_{2}  $  . ZnO NAs were synthesized following our previous work [42]. Firstly, we dissolved zinc acetate   $  (\mathrm{Zn}(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{COO})_{2}\cdot2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O})  $   in ethanol to prepare ZnO seed solution. Next, ZnO seed solution was coated on the FTO substrates, and the substrates were annealed at   $  150^{\circ}C  $   for 15 min. Repeat this process three times, and then the films were annealed at   $  350^{\circ}C  $   for 30 min to obtain ZnO seed layer.

$ \mathrm{Zn(CH_3COO)_2\cdot 2H_2O} $  and Polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) were dissolved in deionized water at  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  to obtain  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  growth solution, and the PH value of growth solution was adjusted by adding a certain amount of ammonia  $ (\mathrm{NH}_3\cdot \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}) $  under continuous magnetic stirring. The  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  seed layer was put face down into the growth solution at  $ 99^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for some time. Afterward, they were taken out and thoroughly rinsed with deionized water several times. Finally, the film samples were put in tube furnace and annealed under  $ \mathrm{O}_2 $  condition to obtain  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  NAs.

$ \mathrm{ZnO@ZnSe} $  NAs have been fabricated by successive ion layer adsorption and reaction method (SILAR). Firstly, sodium selenosulfate  $ (\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{SeSO}_3) $  solution was obtained by

refluxing selenium (Se) powder in mixed aqueous solution of sodium sulfite   $  \left(\mathrm{Na}_{2}\mathrm{SO}_{3}\right)  $   and hydrazine hydrate   $  \left(\mathrm{N}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{4}\cdot\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}\right)  $   at 120 °C. Next ZnO NAs were alternately dipped into   $  \mathrm{Zn}(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{COO})_{2}\cdot2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}  $   aqueous solution for 5 min and   $  Na_{2}SeSO_{3}  $   solution for 5 min. Then, they were washed by deionized water and dried by   $  N_{2}  $  . Finally, ZnO NAs coated with ZnSe layer were obtained through SILAR cycles.

# 2.2 Preparation of PSC based on ZnO@ZnSe NAs

The complete scheme of the fabrication of solar cells based on ZnO@ZnSe NAs in air is displayed in Fig.1 (b). The light absorbing layer,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , was prepared by two-step sequential deposition method [43]. Firstly, lead iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  ) was dissolved in N,N-Dimethylformamide (DMF) under stirring at 70°C to prepare   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution (1 M).   $  PbI_{2}  $   infiltrated into ZnO@ZnSe NAs by spin-coating, and the films were annealed for 10 min. Then the films were dipped into a solution of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   in isopropyl alcohol (10 mg·ml  $  ^{-1}  $  ). Next, the samples were annealed for 10 min. Afterwards, the Co-doping Spiro-OMeTAD as hole transport layer (HTL) material was deposited on   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film by spin-coating method. Finally, Au layer with ~80 nm thickness was thermally evaporated onto hole transport layer as cathode.

# 3.2 The effects of ZnSe modification on perovskite film based on ZnO NAs

To investigate the effects of ZnSe modification on the interface contact between perovskite and ZnO NAs, the SEM images of the ZnO and ZnO@ZnSe NAs based perovskite films are characterized. Fig.4 (a) and (b) show the overhead view SEM images of the ZnO and ZnO@ZnSe NAs based perovskite films, respectively. It can be seen that the ZnO NAs based perovskite film has lower surface coverage and more holes, which lead to direct contact between the ZnO ETL and the Spiro-OMeTAD HTL, resulting in the serious charge recombination problem and decreasing the device performance. After ZnSe modification, the coverage of the ZnO and ZnO@ZnSe NAs based perovskite film was improved obviously, and the surface of perovskite film is compact and uniform, which is favorable to reduce charge recombination. The cross-sectional views of the ZnO and ZnO@ZnSe NAs based perovskite films are exhibited in Fig.4 (c) and (d), respectively. It's obvious that the perovskite can not penetrate into ZnO NAs completely, resulting in many voids between ZnO NAs, which will become the recombination centers of carriers. But after ZnSe modification, the perovskite can penetrate into the gaps between nanorod arrays well. In summary, the ZnSe modification can improve the coverage and permeability of the ZnO and ZnO@ZnSe NAs based perovskite films. This result can be ascribed to that the surface roughness of ZnO NAs improved significantly after ZnSe modification and the rougher surfaces of ZnO@ZnSe NAs

Journal Pre-proof

can provide larger contact area with perovskite and adsorb more perovskite into or above nanorod arrays, resulting in higher coverage of compact and uniform perovskite films.

The UV-vis absorption spectrums of ZnO and ZnO@ZnSe NAs are exhibited in Fig.5 (a). As shown in Fig.5 (a), ZnO NAs display an increased absorbance in ultraviolet region after ZnSe modification. Consequently, ZnSe can be used as a UV-blocking layer to hinder the perovskite films decomposition. In addition, the change of the absorbance of ZnO NAs in the visible light region is not obvious after ZnSe modification. So ZnSe modification doesn't impede the light harvesting of perovskite film. Fig.5 (b) shows the UV-vis absorption spectrums of  $ \mathrm{ZnO / CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $  and  $ \mathrm{ZnO@ZnSe / CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $  films. An absorption edge at about  $ 785~\mathrm{nm} $  is observed from each sample, which is the characteristic absorption of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ , revealing that the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite films were formed. A significant absorbance promotion is observed after ZnSe modification, which is due to the better quality of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film. The enhanced absorbance can produce more photogenerated carriers in visible light region, resulting in the improvement of cell performance. The PL spectrums of the films of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ ,  $ \mathrm{ZnO / CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $  and  $ \mathrm{ZnO@ZnSe / CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $  were carried out at room temperature, and the results are summarized in Fig.5 (c). The PL peak at  $ \sim 785~\mathrm{nm} $  corresponds to the characteristic emission of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  [45], which agrees with the UV-vis absorption spectra results. It is found that the intensity of the emission peak of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film is significantly weakened when  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  combines with ZnO NAs, suggesting that the charge transport from  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film to ZnO NAs is efficient. We compared the maximum PL intensity of the composite films with that of pure  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film to calculate the PL quenching efficiency (PL-QE). The PL-QE of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film based on ZnO NAs is improved from  $ 54\% $  to  $ 82\% $  after ZnSe modification. Because of the PL quenching originated from the carrier extraction across the interface between the perovskite layer and the ETL [46, 47], the efficiency of carrier extraction by  $ \mathrm{ZnO / CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $  is effectively improved after ZnSe modification. These results suggest that ZnSe modification can promote the carrier transfer between the interface of the perovskite layer and the ZnO NAs.

# 3.3 The effects of ZnSe modification on the device performance

PSCs based on ZnO and ZnO@ZnSe NAs were fabricated in the air environment with ~40% relative humidity. The structure and energy level diagram of the ZnO@ZnSe NAs based PSC (FTO/ZnO/ZnO@ZnSe/CH₃NH₃PbI₃/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au) are exhibited in Fig.6 (a) and (b), respectively. In the sunshine condition, free electrons are generated in the CH₃NH₃PbI₃ film, and then they are extracted by ZnO@ZnSe NAs. Fig.7 (a) shows the J-V curves of ZnO and ZnO@ZnSe NAs based PSCs with respect to scan direction, and the device performance parameters such as open circuit voltage (VOC), short-circuit current density (Jsc), fill factor (FF), and PCE values are summarized in Table 1. It can be seen that the ZnO NAs based device demonstrates a PCE of 6.65% (6.16%) with a Voc of 0.72 (0.72) V, a Jsc of 17.88 (17.61) mA·cm⁻², and a FF of 0.52 (0.48) when it is measured under the reverse (forward) voltage scan, respectively. Meanwhile, the hysteresis is observed in both the ZnO and  $ \mathrm{ZnO@ZnSe} $  NAs based PSCs, and ZnSe modification has reduced the hysteresis of the ZnO NAs based device. To further study the interfacial carrier transmission and recombination in ZnO and ZnO@ZnSe NAs based PSCs, EIS analysis was carried out. The EIS results of ZnO and ZnO@ZnSe NAs based PSCs are exhibited in Fig.7 (c). To better understand the role of the ZnSe layer, the processes of carrier transmission and recombination in these cells are displayed in Fig.8. Upon formation of core/shell  $ \mathrm{ZnO@ZnSe} $  NAs (Fig.6 (b)), the conduction band edge of ZnO locates between the valence band and conduction band of ZnSe, and electrons may transfer from the higher conduction band of ZnSe into the lower conduction band of ZnO [49], which will enhance the ZnO conduction band, and thereby can improve the Voc of the PSC. On the other hand, the free electrons reinject into the valence band of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  materials more easily in ZnO NAs based PSCs attribute to a lower interfacial energy barrier, leading to serious interface recombination, as shown in Fig.8 (a). But in  $ \mathrm{ZnO@ZnSe} $  NAs based PSCs, the ZnSe layer has a barrier action on ZnO nanorods, and provides a physical separation of the carriers, thereby hindering the recombination in interface to improve the Voc, as shown in Fig.8 (b). Moreover, ZnSe modification can improve the quality of perovskite films and then enhance their visible light absorption, which demonstrates that it can produce more photogenerated carriers in the visible region, thus results in a good enhancement in photocurrent. effectively improve the performance of ZnO based PSCs.

The maximum PCE of 11.10% (with Voc of 0.94 V, Jsc of 20.75 mA·cm  $  ^{-2}  $  and FF of 0.57) is obtained for ZnO@ZnSe NAs based PSC with the compositional of  $  Cs_{0.2}FA_{0.3}MA_{0.5}Pb(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_{3}  $  perovskite, as shown in Fig.9, which is above average efficiency of PSCs based on ZnO nanorods reported in many literatures [19, 50-52]. But the value is lower than that of mesoporous  $  TiO_{2}  $  based PSCs. One reason is that the carrier recombination in ZnO NAs based PSCs is more serious than that of mesoporous  $  TiO_{2}  $  based PSCs due to that the carrier lifetimes of ZnO NAs based PSCs (  $  10^{-5}-10^{-3}  $  s) is much shorter than that of mesoporous  $  TiO_{2}  $  based PSCs (  $  10^{-2}-10^{0}  $  s) [53], resulting in a lower cell performance. In addition, the nanorod arrays' density and length make a great influence on perovskite infiltration, which is very different from the mesoporous structure. The nanorod arrays with long length and high density will make against the infiltration of perovskite precursor materials and full reaction, which results in the lower PCE value. Furthermore, the whole preparation process of our PSC was completed in air. Perovskite precursor materials such as  $  PbI_{2}  $  and  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  will absorb water when they are exposed to the ambient atmosphere, leading to the degradation of device performance [54, 55]. More importantly, the moisture and oxygen in air may cause the decomposition of perovskite material [56]. Therefore, We believe that the PCE of our devices will be improved by the following two ways. On the one hand, optimizing the zinc acetate concentration and reaction time to adjust the density and length of nanorod arrays. On the other hand, adding polymer additives into perovskite films to improve the tolerance of perovskite materials to humidity.

How to improve the stability of PSCs is also a crucial problem for the application of PSCs. In order to study the stability of PSCs, the devices were placed in ambient environment (RH ~40%, 25 °C) and the PCE of the devices was tested once 24 h. Moreover, the normalized PCE was collected, as shown in Fig.10. It can be seen that the PCE of the device based on bare ZnO NAs dramatically reduced to less than 40% after 168 h. However, the device based on ZnO@ZnSe NAs retained more than 80% of its initial PCE after 168 h under the same condition. Rapid performance degradation of ZnO NAs based PSC is attributed to the hydroxyl groups and oxygen vacancies on ZnO surface, which can cause the decomposition of perovskite films. However, ZnSe can be used as a barrier layer to prevent direct contact between ZnO NAs and perovskite absorber, and inhibit perovskite

Journal Pre-proof

decomposition. In addition, ZnSe is able to block ultraviolet light and delay the degradation of perovskite due to that ZnSe has a wide absorption in the UV region. Therefore, the stability of PSC improved after ZnSe modification.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we have put forward to a facile and efficient strategy to enhance the efficiency and stability of ZnO-based PSCs by ZnSe modification. To begin with, ZnO NAs have been synthesized via PVP soft template method and then ZnSe layer was coated on ZnO NAs by SILAR method to form ZnO@ZnSe NAs. The PSC with ZnSe modification shows much improved  $  V_{OC}  $  and PCE compared with those of the pristine one. Particularly, the ZnSe modified PSC exhibits a higher PCE (8.44%), which is about 27% higher than that of the unmodified one (6.65%). Furthermore, the PCE of the ZnSe modified PSC has been improved to 11.10% by multiple cation perovskite absorber. It is believed that ZnSe modification makes a better device performance due to that ZnSe has a higher conduction band edge, which causes the ZnO conduction band upward by Fermi level alignment, leading to better separation of the carriers and increases carrier transfer efficiency to reduce the interface charge recombination. And the improved stability of perovskite films was obtained by the ZnSe modification. Compared to the PCE of the unmodified one which decreased to 40% after 168 h, the PCE of the ZnSe modified PSC retained above 80%. The better stability of ZnSe modified PSCs can be attributed to the ZnSe modification can inhibit the decomposition of perovskite films. This study provides a useful reference for the development of the PSCs based on ZnO NAs.
