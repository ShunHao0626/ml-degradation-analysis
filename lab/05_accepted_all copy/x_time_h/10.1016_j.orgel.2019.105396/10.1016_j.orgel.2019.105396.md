# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) as representative of the third generation solar cells has developed remarkably in the past ten years and the power conversion efficiency (PCE) increased from 3.8% to over 24% [1-4]. Inverted planar heterojunction PSCs are more eye-catching for its advantages of low cost [5], low temperature process [6] as well as negligible hysteresis [7]. Up to now, the efficient inverted PSCs is mostly based on PTAA and NiO  $  _{x}  $  as hole transport layer (HTL) [8,9]. Among them, the PTAA shows more potential in achieving high efficient inverted PSCs and the record PCE of 21.51% was achieved [9].

Although the excellent performance of PTAA, the hydrophobic nature of PTAA will make nonwet of perovskite precursor on it because of mismatched surface energy [10,11]. Therefore, there are many difficulties in film formation for one-step deposition processing. Some surface modification, such as (9,9-bis(3-(N,N-dimethylamino)propyl)-2,7-fluorene)-alt-2,7-(9,9-dioctylfluorene) (PFN) insertion [11] and oxygen plasma treatment [10], were carried out for enhancing the

# ABSTRACT

Full coverage of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer could be easily obtained by two-step method, while the perovskite precursor stoichiometry ratio is very sensitive to the performance of the perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, we demonstrated the high quality  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film can be formed on the hydrophobic poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA) without any surface treatment and dopant. We systematically adjusted the stoichiometry ratio of the first  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ :MAI precursor from 1.1:0.3 to 1.3:0.3 and the surface morphology as well as optoelectronic properties of perovskite films were also measured. Benefits from the more uniform surface, higher crystallinity and lower trap states, the high photoelectric conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 18.38\% $  and lower hysteresis were achieved based on the optimized ratio of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ :MAI. That is one of highest performance of the inverted PSCs based on free-dopants both in  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  active layer and PTAA hole transport layer. In addition, the devices also showed good stability, and the PCE could maintain  $ 73.85\% $  of its initial value when they were exposed in air and  $ 95.63\% $  in  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $  for  $ 216\mathrm{h} $ . This work suggested a facile precursor stoichiometry engineering to obtain efficient and stable  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  PSCs based on free-dopants.

Meanwhile, the dopants are very expensive (e.g. F4TCNQ, ~\$ 7000 g $  ^{-1}  $ ). Thus, achieving efficient PSCs with undoped PTAA is very important for its commercial application.

In this work, the efficient and stable inverted PSCs based on all-solution interface and undoped PTAA was confirmed, in which the conventional two-step method was modified and the high quality of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films were formed. We systematically adjusted the stoichiometry ratio of the first  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ :MAI precursor and optoelectronic properties of perovskite films were also measured. The results showed that the stoichiometry ratio of the first  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ :MAI can dramatically impact on the photoelectric properties of the final perovskite films. The high PCE of  $ 18.38\% $  and negligible hysteresis were achieved based on optimized ratio of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ :MAI. To our best knowledge,  $ 18.38\% $  is one of the highest PCE among the inverted PSCs without any dopant. In addition, the PSCs device also showed good stability even in air or  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $ , and the PCE can maintain  $ 73.85\% $  of its initial value exposed in air for  $ 216\mathrm{h} $  and  $ 95.63\% $  in  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $ . Hence, our work suggests a facile method to obtain efficient and stable  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  PSCs, which shows great potential for application in the fabrication of highly efficient and stable inverted PSCs.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

Polytriarylamine (PTAA, >99%), methylammonium (MAI, 99.5%) and 2,9-dimethyl-4,7-diphenyl-1,10-phe-Nanthroline (BCP, >99%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp (Xi'an, China). Fullerene derivative [6,6]-phenyl C  $  _{61}  $  -butyric acid methy ester (PC  $  _{61}  $  BM, 99.5%) was purchased from Nano-c Corp. (Westwood, USA). Lead iodide (PbI  $  _{2}  $  , 99%) was purchased from Weiyi Chemical Technology Corp (Tianjin, China). Solvents such as N,N-Dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%, super dry), Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.8%, super dry), chlorobenzene (CB, 99.8%, super dry) and so on, were purchased from J&K Seal (Beijing, China) and the materials were used without any further purification.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

Here, the inverted device configuration as shown in the inset of Fig. 3a was utilized. Firstly, indium tin oxide (ITO) substrates were ultrasonically cleaned in acetone, detergents, distilled water and isopropanol (IPA) for 15 min, respectively. The ITO substrate dried by nitrogen and treated by UV-ozone for 10 min. After that, the substrate was transported into a glove box, in which the PTAA solution (6 mg ml  $  ^{-1}  $  in toluene) was spin coated upon substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. The perovskite active layers were prepared with a modified two-step methods. This method can be divided into two processes. Firstly, different molar ratio of PbI  $  _{2}  $ /MAI changing from 1.1:0.3 to 1.3:0.3 was dissolved in the mixture of DMF/DMSO (volume ratio = 1:0.1) solvent, which called first precursor. The seed films were prepared by spin-coating the first precursor on PTAA thin film at 6000 rpm for 20 s. After that, 70 μl MAI solution (40 mg ml  $  ^{-1}  $  in IPA) was dropped onto the center of the rotating seed film at 4000 rpm for another 40 s and the formed film was annealed at 100 °C for 30 min. To prepare the electron transport layer (ETL), PC  $  _{61}  $  BM was dissolved in CB (20 mg ml  $  ^{-1}  $ ) and spin coated on the prepared perovskite layer at 3000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at 90 °C for 30 min. Finally, the BCP solution (0.5 mg ml  $  ^{-1}  $  in IPA) was deposited by spin coating at 4000 rpm for 30 s and then a strip of Ag cathode (100 nm) was formed via thermal evaporation(0.5 Å s  $  ^{-1}  $ ) under a vacuum of 6 × 10  $  ^{-4}  $  Pa. The device active area is 0.04 cm  $  ^{2}  $ .

# 2.3. Characterization

The UV–vis absorbance spectra was measured by UV–vis spectrophotometer (UV–vis) (PerkinElmer, Lambda 365, USA). The X-ray diffraction (XRD) spectra was measured by XRD test instrument (Bruker, D8 Advance, Germany) with a Cu Kα radiation source under 40 KV and 44 mA. The current density-voltage (J-V) characteristics were measured by the solar simulator (Enlitech, SS-F5-3A, Taiwan) under the illumination of AM 1.5G. Light intensity was   $  100 \, mW cm^{-2}  $   calibrated by a standard silicon solar cell. The monochromatic incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) was obtained by the IPCE measurement system (Enlitech, QE-R, Taiwan). The steady state photoluminescence (PL) spectra and time resolved PL spectra (TRPL) were measured by fluorescence spectrophotometer (Edinburgh, FS 5, China). The morphology was characterized by atomic force microscope (AFM) (Bruker, Multimode 8, Germany) using a tapping mode. The electrochemical workstation (Chenghua, Chi760e, China) was employed to obtain the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) of the devices. In the EIS measurement, the devices impedance were measured by applying 5 mV AC voltage whose frequency is swept from 1 Hz to   $  10^{6}  $   Hz under zero bias and dark state. To evaluate the stability of the devices, the devices were exposed in air (30–50% RH) or   $  N_{2}  $   for 216 h without encapsulation and their J-V curve was tested at specified intervals.

# 3. Result and discussion

In this work, the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   film was deposited by modified two-step method. Firstly, the first precursor was dropped onto the HTL to form the seed film. Fig. 1a-c show the morphology of the seed films and the corresponding   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   films. The root-mean-square surface roughness (RMS) of seed films prepared from molar ratio (  $  PbI_{2}:MAI  $  , thereafter is the same) of 1.1:0.3, 1.2:0.3, 1.3:0.3 are 8.03 nm, 7.26 nm and 14.27 nm, respectively. The uniform   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   films were formed after furtherly reacting with MAI and the RMS of the perovskite film based on different molar ratio (1.1:0.3–1.3:0.3) of the first precursor are 12.03 nm, 12.64 nm and 10.69 nm, respectively. The grain size of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   based on 1.1:0.3 seed film is smaller and poor uniform than other two kinds.

In order to further investigate the crystallinity of perovskite film, the XRD measurement was performed and the results are shown in Fig. 1d. XRD peaks locate at  $ 2\theta = 13.98^{\circ} $ ,  $ 28.22^{\circ} $  and  $ 31.67^{\circ} $  are observed in all kinds of perovskite films, which indicates that a tetragonal  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  was formed [21]. The XRD intensity of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  that prepared from molar ratio of 1.2:0.3 and 1.3:0.3 seed films are stronger than that of 1.1:0.3. The strongest XRD peaks appear in the molar ratio of 1.2:0.3, which may shows the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  has more complete crystallinity and benefits to achieve the excellent photovoltaic performance.

Fig. 2a is steady photoluminescence (PL) of the perovskite films based on different molar ratio. For the PL measurement, the perovskite films were fabricated on bare glass substrate. It is clearly seen that the PL of perovskite film based on 1.1:0.3 molar ratio shows lowest PL intensity, which confirm that there are a serious non-radiative recombination loss in that film. Usually, the non-radiative recombination may ascribe to the trap states and shallow trapping levels. Thus, we estimate that the MAPbI $ _{3} $  film based on 1.1:0.3 has many trap states or gap states, which will results abnormal hysteresis and low performance. The intensity of PL peaks of the films prepared from 1.2:0.3 and 1.3:0.3 are almost the same. Comparing with the film based on 1.3:0.3 molar ratio, the PL peak of the film based on 1.2:0.3 has a blue shifted from 775 nm to 769 nm, which shows that there are less density of trap states or shallow trapping levels in that film [22]. Furthermore, the time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) was measured and the results are shown in Fig. 2b. The results suggest that the life-time of perovskite based on 1.2:0.3 is longest ( $ \tau $  = 52.667 ns), which longer than 1.1:0.3 ( $ \tau $  = 1.156 ns) and 1.3:0.3 ( $ \tau $  = 11.575 ns). # 4. Conclusion

We had prepared a high quality  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film on the hydrophobic PTAA HTL using a modified two-step method. The stoichiometry molar ratio of the first  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ :MAI precursor affect the surface morphology as well as optoelectronic properties of the final perovskite films. The results of the AFM, PL, TRPL, XRD and EIS confirmed that the optimized ratio of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ :MAI is 1.2:0.3. At that ratio, the high PCE of  $ 18.38\% $  and lower hysteresis were achieved. That is one of the highest performance of the inverted PSCs based on free-dopants both in  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  active layer and PTAA hole transport layer (HTL). In addition, the devices based on optimized molar ratio also showed good stability when exposed in air or  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $ . This work suggests a method for preparing the efficient and stable PSCs based on free-dopant  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and PTAA.
