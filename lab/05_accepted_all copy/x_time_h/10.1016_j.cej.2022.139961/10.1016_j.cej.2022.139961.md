# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PerSCs) have received much attention in recent years due to their extraordinary optoelectronic properties, cheap raw material, easy fabrication, and great possibility in low-cost photovoltaic power plants. The power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of the state-of-the-art PerSCs have been significantly improved to over 25 %, resulting from the recent advancements in interface modification, composition engineering, and film formation engineering [1-4]. In PerSCs, the crystallinity and film morphology of perovskite are seen as the determining factors that govern the PCEs [5-8]. The crystallinity can be adjusted by partially replacing iodine with chlorine or bromine to expand the grain size while reducing the grain boundaries [9-11]. Perovskite with different morphology (such as nanowires) can be obtained using mixed solvent systems to realize faster carrier separation and higher lateral conductivity [12-14]. Perovskite solar cell (PerSC) is currently-one of the most promising third-generation photovoltaic technologies. However, the defects derived from the inhomogeneous growth of perovskite film exacerbate the charge recombination and ion migration in the devices, thus restricting their power conversion efficiency (PCE) and stability. In this work, we introduce nitrogen-doped carbon nanofibers (N-CNFs) via electrospinning technology to guide the preferential orientation of perovskite crystals for the first time. N-CNFs with abundant nitrogen-containing functional groups provide plentiful nucleation sites and fibrous templates, which guide the preferential orientation growth of crystals and diminish the defects density in the perovskite film. The PCE of the champion device achieved a remarkable improvement from 21.40 % to 24.08 % and demonstrated less than 5 % degradation after 600 h of continuous exposure to the ambient environment. This study provides a novel strategy to realize efficient and stable PerSCs by controlling the heterogeneous nucleation and preferentially oriented growth of perovskite crystals with electrospinning N-CNFs.

In this work, as shown in Fig. 1, nitrogen-doped carbon nanofibers (N-CNFs) with controllable sizes were prepared via electrospinning and introduced as shape templates into the perovskite films to guide the crystallization with preferential orientation. Firstly, N-CNFs with substantial nucleation nitrogen-site could regulate the specific nucleation and crystalline process to guide the preferential orientation growth of perovskite crystals. Secondly, N-CNFs can coordinate with lead ions and reduce the trap states caused by insufficient coordination of lead ions to prevent the migration of ions. After doping with N-CNFs, the PerSC devices reached a champion PCE of 24.08 % with an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.16 V, a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 25.53 mA·cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and a fill factor (FF) of 81.52 %, and exhibited degradation of less than 5 % after storage for 600 h in an ambient environment, significantly improved than the undoped control device with PCE of 21.40 % and degradation of near 80 %. In one word, this work provides an effective strategy to induce preferentially oriented growth of perovskite crystals for efficient and stable PerSCs with the help of N-CNFs prepared by electrospinning.

# 2.2. Preparation of N-CNFs

The electrospinning solutions were prepared by dissolving PAN in DMF solvent. The prepared solution of PAN was fed into a 10 mL plastic syringe connected to a 21G stainless steel blunted needle. A positive voltage and a negative voltage were applied to the aluminum collector and the tip of the syringe needle, respectively. The distance between the needle tip and collector was maintained as a gap with a steady flow rate of   $  0.5 \, mL \cdot h^{-1}  $  . The polymer fibers were obtained under the above electrospinning conditions, and the obtained polymer fibers were subjected to two-step high-temperature treatment in a tube furnace. The collected nanofiber was pre-oxidized in the air at   $  250 \, ^{\circ}C  $   for 60 min, and then pass nitrogen gas to raise the temperature at   $  5 \, ^{\circ}C \cdot minute^{-1}  $   to   $  800 \, ^{\circ}C  $   for another 60 min, and N-CNFs can be obtained.

# 2.3. Solar cell fabrication

The ITO substrates were washed sequentially with detergent (2 % hellmanex in water), deionized water, and ethyl alcohol, and finally cleaned by UV-ozone treatment for 15 mins. Five times diluted   $  SnO_{2}  $   solution was spin-coated on ITO substrates at 3000 rpm for 30 s, and then baked on a hot plate in ambient air at 150 °C for 10 min. The   $  SnO_{2}  $   solution was spin-coated again, followed by baking on a hot plate in ambient air at 150 °C for 30 mins, and cooled to room temperature. The   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution (600 mg   $  PbI_{2}  $   powder dissolved in 1 mL DMF with 92.3 μL DMSO) was spin-coated on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   substrates in the nitrogen-filled glove box and then annealed at 70 °C for 10 mins. N-CNFs with different concentrations (0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 mg·mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) were respectively added to the   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution. Then 100 μL of FAI solution (68 mg FAI powder and 10 mg MACl powder dissolved in 1 mL IPA) was immediately spin-coated on the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film at 5000 rpm for 20 s, immediately annealed at 150 °C for 20 min to form perovskite films. After cooling to room temperature, the spiroOMeTAD solution was deposited on perovskite films at 3000 rpm for 30 s, where the spiroOMeTAD solution was prepared by dissolving 72.3 mg of spiroOMeTAD powder in 1 mL of chlorobenzene with 28.8 μL of tBP, 17.5 μL of Li-TFSI solution (520 mg Li-TFSI in 1 mL of acetonitrile). Finally, 80 nm of silver was deposited by thermal evaporation. The photoactive area of the device is around 0.04 cm  $  ^{2}  $  .

To further enhance the device performance, additional MAI was introduced into the FAI solution to optimize the perovskite crystallization processes, resulting in an enhanced PCE of 21.40 % (  $  V_{OC} = 1.10  $   V,   $  J_{SC} = 25.39  $   mA·cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , FF = 76.91 %) as shown in Fig. 5a, much higher than the control device giving a PCE of only 19.2 %. Further introducing N-CNFs, the best PCE of 24.08 % (  $  V_{OC} = 1.16  $   V,   $  J_{SC} = 25.53  $   mA·cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , FF = 81.52 %) can be achieved, and there is no obvious hysteresis in J-V curves between forward and reverse scans, resulting narrow efficiency distribution. The statistical photovoltaic parameters (  $  V_{OC}  $   and PCE) distribution indicates all photovoltaic parameters of the devices with N-CNFs are higher than the control device (Fig. S13). As shown in Fig. 5b, the integrated   $  J_{SC}  $   obtained from EQE for the device without N-CNFs is 23.69 mA·cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , while the integrated   $  J_{SC}  $   increases to 24.20 mA·cm  $  ^{-2}  $   by introducing N-CNFs. From time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra (Fig. 5c), the average lifetime for perovskite film without N-CNFs is 365.53 ns. In comparison, the average lifetimes increase to 609.98 ns, 844.25 ns, 755.58 ns, and 756.81 ns, for perovskite film with N-CNFs concentrations of 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3 mg·mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , respectively (Table S1). All these photophysical findings indicate that the non-radiative recombination loss of perovskite film with N-CNFs is significantly greatly suppressed.

To clarify the simultaneously enhanced   $  V_{OC}  $  ,   $  J_{SC}  $  , and FF for PerSCs with N-CNFs, electron-only devices with the structure of ITO/  $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite/PCBM/Ag were fabricated and the dark current–voltage (I-V) curves were measured to investigate the defect density (Fig. S14). The defect density can be calculated from the following formula:

$$
n _ {t} = 2 \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0} V _ {T F L} / (e L ^ {2})
$$

where  $  V_{TFL}  $  is the trap-filled limit voltage,  $  \varepsilon  $  is the relative dielectric constant of FAPbI $ _3 $  (46.9),  $  \varepsilon_0  $  is the vacuum permittivity,  $  e  $  is the electron charge, and  $  L  $  is the thickness of the perovskite film [55,56]. The  $  V_{TFL}  $  of the device with 0.2 mg·mL $ ^{-1} $ N-CNFs is 0.310 V, and the  $  n_t  $  is calculated to be 1.78 × 10 $ ^{-16} $  cm $ ^{-3} $ . While for the device without N-CNFs, the  $  V_{TFL}  $  is 0.583 V, corresponding to the  $  n_t  $  of 3.36 × 10 $ ^{-16} $  cm $ ^{-3} $ . Obviously, the defect density of the device with N-CNFs is significantly decreased.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, efficient and stable PerSCs are achieved by incorporating N-CNFs to guide the perovskite crystalline orientation. The interaction between the C—N groups and   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions provides a heterogeneous nucleation site as an intermediate phase, guiding grain growth and increasing grain size. With the help of N-CNFs, the perovskite film exhibits enhanced charge carrier lifetime and suppressed non-radiative recombination due to the reduced defect density. The PerSCs with N-CNFs show simultaneously enhanced   $  V_{OC}  $  ,   $  J_{SC}  $  , and FF, resulting in a significantly improved PCE of 24.08 %. Moreover, the device with N-CNFs demonstrates excellent stability with less than 5 % PCE degradation after continuous exposure to the ambient environment for 600 h. Our findings suggest that introducing N-CNFs to control the heterogeneous nucleation and to guide preferentially perovskite crystal growth is an effective strategy to realize efficient and stable PerSCs.
