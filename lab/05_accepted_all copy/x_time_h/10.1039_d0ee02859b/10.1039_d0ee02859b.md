# Broader context

Organic-inorganic halide perovskites have outstanding properties such as high absorption coefficient, tuneable bandgaps, and high charge carrier mobility; therefore, solar cells that use organic-inorganic halide perovskites as light absorbers are more efficient than other types of solar cells. However, the commercialization of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is impeded because moisture, light, and heat can easily degrade perovskites. In particular, the heat accumulated inside the PSCs degrades perovskites and adversely affects the morphology of hole-transporting materials (HTMs). In this study, we introduce a novel strategy involving HTM-infiltrated metal oxide nanoparticles to alleviate this stability issue. We test several HTMs and find that our approach is effective regardless of HTM types. In addition, the thermal behaviour of  $ \mathrm{Al_2O_3@HTM} $  is systematically investigated using an infrared thermal imaging camera, time-domain thermoreflectance method, and synchrotron-based grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering analysis. This study is the first report to discuss how to effectively remove the heat, which is the main cause of perovskite decomposition. Notably, the  $ \mathrm{Al_2O_3@dopant-free} $  HTM based device without encapsulation retains  $ 91\% $  of its initial efficiency under harsh conditions after 31 days. Our finding has not been described in previous studies, so it can provide new insights that will help guide the development of highly stable PSCs.

# Heat dissipation effects on the stability of planar perovskite solar cells†

Kyoungwon Choi,  $  ^{ID}  $   ✝ Junwoo Lee, ✝ Hyuntae Choi, Guan-Woo Kim, Hong Il Kim* and Taiho Park  $  ^{ID}  $   ✝

Special attention should be devoted to the stability of the perovskite solar cells, which is a major limitation affecting their commercialization. The stabilities against moisture and light have been substantially improved by optimizing the charge-transporting layer and utilizing encapsulation techniques; however, thermal stability has not yet been secured. Herein, we report a novel approach involving metal oxide nanoparticles infiltrated by hole-transporting materials to enhance device stability. Unlike conventional methods that prevent the thermal decomposition of perovskite, we intend to eliminate the main cause of efficiency drop by dissipating the heat accumulated inside the device. In particular, we observe that metal oxide nanoparticles can effectively reduce the device temperatures and maintain the morphology of hole-transporting materials because of their high thermal conductivity and porous scaffold structure. The experiments demonstrate that our devices without encapsulation retained 91% of their initial efficiency after 31 days at 85 °C and 85% relative humidity, which is the condition to simulate a real operating environment. These results show significant potential for the development of highly stable perovskite solar cells with reasonable efficiency.

Recently, the use of inorganic or dopant-free materials and further avoiding charge-transporting layers have been considered desirable strategies for improving the thermal stability of PSCs.  $  ^{18-20}  $   Each of these strategies achieve a lifetime stability of 1000 h, but accelerated aging tests have been conducted under different conditions, rendering the expectation of actual lifetime impossible.  $  ^{21}  $   Only a few studies have monitored unencapsulated devices under extreme conditions similar to a real operating environment, and they have still not secured long-term stability of the order of hundreds of hours.  $  ^{11,22,23}  $   Previously, we achieved the remarkably stable unencapsulated PSCs using passivation materials and dopant-free polymers.  $  ^{11}  $   Unfortunately, most studies, including ours, have only focused on solving the situation in which perovskite degradation occurs. Under operation conditions, sunlight will increase the temperature of devices and change the morphology of hole-transporting materials (HTMs), thereby reducing in photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{4}  $   Hence, releasing the heat accumulated inside the device and minimizing heat-induced morphology transition is crucial to obtain highly stable PSCs.

Heat dissipation is an effective strategy for increasing the lifetime of electronic devices including fluorescent lamps, light-emitting diodes (LEDs), and Si photovoltaics.  $  ^{24}  $   For organic electronics, the distance from active layers to surfaces is very short, with the heat variation in each layer relatively small compared to inorganic electronics. Unlike the expectation that heat sinks are useless because of low thermal gradients, some studies reveal that heat dissipation is also effective in organic electronics.  $  ^{25,26}  $   Although heat dissipation is a potential strategy for increasing the thermal stability of PSCs, research on heat release in PSCs remains currently unavailable.

In general, thermal conduction is generated by the transfer of lattice vibrations and the movement of electrons, so metals and crystals show high thermal conductivity. Among them, aluminium oxide nanoparticles   $  \left(\mathrm{Al}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3}\right.  $   NPs) exhibit relatively high thermal conductivity   $  (20-30\;\mathrm{W}\;\mathrm{m}^{-1}\;\mathrm{K}^{-1})  $   and electrical insulation, enabling their use as a cooling filler within devices.  $  ^{[27,28]}  $   Recently, some research groups applied the   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   layer as an encapsulation material for protecting perovskites from external moisture and oxygen.  $  ^{[15,16,29]}  $   However, investigation on heat dissipation effects by HTMs after incorporating   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs is unreported.

Here, we employ HTM-infiltrated   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs to elevate the stability of planar PSCs and investigate the device performance. The heat dissipation impact of the   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs was examined using an infrared thermal imaging camera (IR camera) and a time-domain thermoreflectance (TDTR) method.  $  ^{30,31}  $   To optimize the thickness of the porous layer, we fabricated devices with varied concentrations of   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs and demonstrated efficiencies of 21.2% and 19.9% for   $  Al_{2}O_{3}@spiro-OMeTAD  $   and   $  Al_{2}O_{3}@asy-PBTBDT  $ , respectively. Interestingly, the devices containing   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs yielded very high open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) values of approximately 1.19 V with Spiro-OMeTAD and 1.16 V with Asy-PBTBDT. In a previous study, our group systematically analysed and reported the importance of a dopant-free HTM for highly stable PSCs and developed it as a green solvent processable HTM.  $  ^{11,32}  $

This study is a step forward and suggests a strategy to secure the humidity and thermal stability of highly efficient planar PSCs regardless of HTM types. Furthermore, experiments demonstrated that our PSCs lacking encapsulation appreciably maintain their initial efficiency under a harsh condition. Our findings offer an understanding of heat dissipation in PSCs for the first time and provide new insights with potential to guide the development of highly stable planar PSCs.

# Results and discussion

The photo-thermal response of each component layer was monitored using an IR camera under 1 sun illumination (AM 1.5G, 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) for 2 hours (Fig. 1a). The initial temperature was set to 20 °C (ambient air) for all layers prepared on the glass substrate and subsequently raised by light irradiation. Over time, the temperature difference emerged between the layers, with the highest temperature of 74 °C in the perovskite layer ascribed to its excellent light absorption (Fig. 1b). Prolonging the irradiation time will produce higher temperature in the perovskite layer. Thus, the temperature of PSCs in a real environment may attain a temperature above 75 °C due to the high intensity and continuous exposure to sunlight.  $  ^{31}  $   The temperature rise within PSCs directly accounts for decreased performance.  $  ^{7}  $   To alleviate this problem, a new architecture involving Al  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $  NPs (<50 nm particle size, Fig. S1, ESI†) stacked on the perovskite layer, before deposition of the HTM is proposed (Fig. 1c), and Fig. 1d displays a cross-sectional scanning electron microscopy (SEM) image of the PSC from the HTM-infiltrated Al  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $  NPs. Hereinafter, we denote the HTM-infiltrated Al  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $  NPs as Al  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $  @HTM. Considering ideal operation of PSCs, porous Al  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $  scaffolds dissipate the heat from the perovskite layer to the environment and holes are effectively transported through the HTM region. Furthermore, additional expected benefits of the Al  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $  NPs include: blocking the influx of external moisture and oxygen,  $  ^{15,29}  $   suppressing back recombination,  $  ^{16,33,34}  $   and preventing a morphological change of HTMs.  $  ^{35}  $

The time-dependent cooling test was conducted at temperatures decreasing from 85 to 20 °C to verify the heat dissipation effect of   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs. Samples corresponding to the glass/perovskite/HTM (Spiro-OMeTAD, conventional HTM) and the glass/perovskite/  $  Al_{2}O_{3}@HTM  $   were prepared for a comparative analysis of the heat transfer property. Both sample types were heated to 85 °C on a hotplate to simulate the temperature rise by sunlight,  $  ^{31}  $   and then cooling effects were monitored after moving samples to the 20 °C plate (Fig. 2a and Fig. S2, ESI†). The significant difference in the surface temperature of two samples is clearly visible. The rate of decreasing the temperature is accelerated after incorporation of   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs, suggesting a potential for use as thermally conductive fillers for these materials.  $  ^{24}  $

To understand the carrier dynamics of PSCs with different HTMs, steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements were performed. Compared to the perovskite film without HTM, a decrease in the PL intensity is more pronounced when the perovskite contacted the HTM, suggesting that the efficient charge extraction occurs at the perovskite-HTM interface.  $  ^{4,9,35}  $   As presented in Fig. S17a (ESI†), the PL peak intensity of perovskite films showed no significant difference with or without   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs, indicating that the incorporation of   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs does not inhibit charge extraction at the interface.  $  ^{16}  $   For an in-depth study on the charge transfer rate, the transient PL decay of the perovskite films in contact with different HTMs was investigated. The TRPL spectra of each sample were fitted using a bi-exponential equation involving a fast decay time (  $  \tau_{1}  $  ) and slow decay time (  $  \tau_{2}  $  ) components.   $  \tau_{1}  $   is attributed to the quenching of free carriers at the interface near the HTM layer, while   $  \tau_{2}  $   is assigned to charge recombination in the perovskite.  $  ^{11}  $   Although the   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs were introduced on the perovskite layer, no significant difference exists in   $  \tau_{1}  $   from 3.21–3.65 ns (Fig. S17b and Table S1, ESI†). These results indicate well-maintained charge extraction, consistent with the static PL data.

The interfacial properties in the PSCs were further explored using electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) to analyse

the internal resistance and the charge transfer process.  $  ^{7}  $   The EIS measurements were performed for two device configurations at a bias of   $  V_{OC}  $   and in the dark. Fig. S17c and d (ESI†) shows the Nyquist plots for the devices and the corresponding equivalent circuit model. This circuit comprises the series resistance (  $  R_{s}  $  ), the contact resistance (  $  R_{co}  $  ), and the recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ). The   $  R_{co}  $   in the high-frequency region corresponds to the charge transfer resistance and the   $  R_{rec}  $   in the low-frequency region is attributed to the recombination resistance at the interface of the perovskite/charge-transporting layer.  $  ^{11}  $   In the Nyquist plots, the PSC containing   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs showed a slightly higher   $  R_{s}  $   value (16.4 Ω) than the reference device (14.2 Ω), and this additional resistance is probably due to the thicker HTM layer.  $  ^{42}  $   Interestingly, with the incorporation of   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs, the diameter of the second semicircle significantly increased, indicating high recombination resistance at the interface.  $  ^{43}  $   The   $  Al_{2}O_{3}@HTM  $   based PSC exhibited a recombination resistance (219.6 Ω) higher by a factor of 1.48 than the control PSC (148.1 Ω), while   $  R_{co}  $   decreased from 9.7 to 6.1 Ω supporting the observation that the PSC with   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs yields higher   $  V_{OC}  $   than that without   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs.

Device stability is the main issue impeding the practical utilization of PSCs and the use of Spiro-OMeTAD requiring additional dopants is a prominent obstacle.  $  ^{32,43-45}  $   We conducted stability tests of encapsulated devices with doped Spiro-OMeTAD under various conditions (i.e.,   $  85 \pm 5\%  $   relative humidity (RH), white LED, and   $  85^{\circ}C  $  ) to validate whether encapsulation is effective for improving the stability of PSCs (Fig. 3d).  $  ^{6,11}  $   Our results demonstrate that encapsulated devices exhibit excellent humidity and light stability, maintaining   $  >90\%  $   of their initial efficiency after 1000 hours, because the encapsulation glass blocks the ingress of moisture and oxygen that promote decomposition of perovskites.  $  ^{46-48}  $   However, a drastic PCE drop occurred at high temperature because of a morphological change in the doped Spiro-OMeTAD, and not perovskite degradation (Fig. 3e and Fig. S18, ESI†). The cause of rapid morphological changes in the HTM was investigated by comparing the thermal behaviour of the unencapsulated and encapsulated devices (Fig. S19, ESI†). The temperature response clearly differs for two devices with confirmation that encapsulation inhibits heat release due to the poor transfer of air between the device and the encapsulation glass,  $  ^{25}  $   thereby adversely affecting the HTM. These results suggest that heat is more important than moisture among factors affecting the long-term stability of the device.

Previously, our group reported unencapsulated PSCs involving a polymeric HTM (Asy-PBTBDT) (Fig. S20, ESI†) instead of Spiro-OMeTAD, which showed remarkably high thermal stability.  $  ^{[11,32]}  $   As shown in AFM images (Fig. S21a and b, ESI†), the morphology of Spiro-OMeTAD was largely deformed after thermal annealing, due to the dopants used to improve device performances. In the case of Asy-PBTBDT, the morphological change is not as severe as that of Spiro-OMeTAD, but some unwanted chain motion is caused by heat (Fig. S21c and d, ESI†). Therefore, we adopted the Asy-PBTBDT as the dopant-free HTM and introduced Al₂O₃ NPs to maximize the stability of PSCs. We fabricated dopant-free PSCs containing Al₂O₃@asy-PBTBDT.

# Conclusions

In summary, we systematically investigated the effects of the   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs that can be used as thermally conductive materials. The   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs dissipated heat, which adversely affects the perovskite and HTMs, from the device efficiently. The HTM-infiltrated   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs exhibited excellent thermal properties including stable morphology of the HTMs at high temperature. Since the   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   is an insulator, we optimized the concentration of   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs to minimize the efficiency reduction of PSCs. Consequently, two PSCs with efficiencies of 21.2% for the   $  Al_{2}O_{3}@spiro-OMeTAD  $   and 19.9% for the   $  Al_{2}O_{3}@asy-PBTBDT  $   were demonstrated. Notably, the   $  Al_{2}O_{3}@asy-PBTBDT  $   based device without encapsulation retained 91% of its initial efficiency under a harsh condition similar to a real operating environment after 31 days. We suggested the importance of heat dissipation in PSCs for the first time. Our findings are unprecedented and propose ways to achieve high thermal stability of PSCs with minimal efficiency reduction.
