# 1. Introduction

Organometallic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are currently the most popular photovoltaic devices based on the all-solid-state mesoscopic and planar heterojunction of n-type metal oxide/perovskite/hole transport material (HTM)/counter electrode (CE), which has great potential for low-cost solar electrical energy generation [1-4]. In the PSCs, the perovskite (e.g.,  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x - y}\mathrm{Br}_x\mathrm{Cl}_y $ ,  $ \mathrm{NH_2CHNH_2PbI_3} $ ) functions as the light absorber for generating electron-hole pairs, the n-type metal oxide layer (e.g.,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $ ) as an electron transporting material and the fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) as an electron collection layer, and the HTM (e.g.,

spiro-OMe-TAD, CuI) as selective contacts for extracting holes and the CE (e.g., Au, Ag) as the hole collection layer [5]. The classical PSCs, consisting of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / NH_2CHNH_2PbI_3 / PTAA / Au} $ , had achieved a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 20.1\% $ , mainly due to the unique properties of perovskite materials: direct band gap of  $ \sim 1.55\mathrm{eV} $ , high light absorption coefficient ( $ >104~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ ), long charge diffusion length ( $ >1\mu \mathrm{m} $ ), and low exciton binding energy [6]. However, such high-performance PSC still adopts expensive organic HTM and noble metal CE, which is not suitable for commercial application [7].

In order to reduce the materials cost and simplify the device architectures, HTM-free carbon CE based PSCs were exploited, since the abundant carbon materials have been successfully applied in the high-performance energy-conversion and storage devices [8, 9]. In particular, the carbon CEs with the merits of low cost, simple manufacturing process, and suitable energy level show great potential application in perovskite solar cells [10, 11]. Han et al. firstly developed a carbon black/graphite CE via a printable process and applied the CE into the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ / $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3/\mathrm{ZrO}_2/ $ carbon HTM-free PSC, achieving a high PCE of  $ 6.64\% $  and showing good stability in ambient atmosphere [12]. A low-temperature-processed carbon electrode ( $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) was fabricated by Ma's group, by scraping the conductive carbon ink onto a  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  layer. A PCE of  $ 9.0\% $  was delivered by the low-cost and simply fabricated  $ \mathrm{TiO_2/CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $ /carbon devices [13]. Meng and co-workers prepared an all-carbon CE, which is composed of mesoscopic carbon layer and flexible graphite paper, by the screen-printing technique and showed a PCE of  $ 10.2\% $

[14]. A flexible carbon CE was further constructed by directly hot pressing (  $  < 100\ ^{\circ}C  $  ) a free-standing thermoplastic carbon film onto the perovskite layer, and the PCE was renewed to 13.5% [15].

To be a high-performance carbon CE in HTM-free PSCs, the effective back contact, superior electrical conductivity, and fast carrier mobility, as well as high charge selectivity are highly desirable [14, 16, 17]. The energy level match at the interface of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3/ $  carbon is a prerequisite for these properties and thus becomes the primary concern in designing a carbon CE in PSCs [18]. Different carbons exhibited a difference on the work function (Fermi level), such as carbon black (4.6-5.0 eV) [19, 20], carbon nanotubes (4.7-5.0 eV) [21, 22], graphite (4.4-4.7 eV) [23], graphene oxide (4.9 eV) [24] and graphene (4.2-4.6 eV) [25], etc. It is generally believed that carbon with a Fermi level closer to the valence band of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  (5.4 eV) would yield a p-type conductivity to CE and thus facilitate the charge separation process. For instance, Yang and Mathews's group fabricated multilayered graphene and carbon nanotube hole extraction electrodes for PSCs in view of their suitable Fermi level at ca. -5.0 eV, which demonstrated effective hole extraction [16, 21]. Li et al prepared the single walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs)/graphite/carbon black (1:4:1 in mass) composite CEs. In such a carbon CE, SWCNTs acted as a hole transport layer, which increased the work function and charge extraction, and resulted in a PCE up to  $ 14.7\% $  [26]. Actually, the charge extraction only took place at the carbon/perovskite interface, and the charge conductivity mainly implemented in the bulk carbon. However, it is still very difficult

4

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

for the carbon CE to simultaneously possess high surface hole extraction and bulk electrical conductivity due to its consistent carbon structure. Thus, it is very essential to distinctively tune the internal and external chemical constitution and structure of carbon to improve the intrinsic activity of a carbon CE, including the surface charge extraction and the bulk electrical conductivity. To the best of our knowledge, no relative research has been reported up to date.

Chemically doping heteroatoms into the carbon structure is an effective way for modulating the electronic properties of carbon and draws considerable attention in recent years [27]. The main function of heteroatom doping is to lower or raise the Fermi level of carbon via the addition of acceptors or donors, respectively [28]. Besides, the heteroatom doping-induced charge redistribution, regardless of a higher (such as N: 3.04) or lower (such as B: 2.04, P: 2.19) electronegativity than that of carbon (2.55), could break electroneutrality and enhance conductivity of carbon [29, 30]. Recent researches demonstrated that the role of P atoms could enhance n-type behavior and B atoms could promote hole extraction [31-33]. The introduction of surface B doping and bulk P doping may endow high charge extraction and conductivity for the carbon CE and thus avoid charge recombination at the carbon/perovskite interface.

Herein, in this study, the B/P co-doped carbon (BPC) CE for the HTM-free PSC was constructed by coating a B-doped carbon layer onto the P-doped carbon (PC), in which the PC was synthesized via a hydrothermal route, followed with a carbonization and ball milling process. In BPC CE, the B-doped carbon layer with

5

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

relatively high work function was used as hole extractor and the P-doped bulk carbon as conductive substrate. The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and impedance measurements demonstrated that the BPC CE showed better hole extraction and conductivity than that of pure carbon CE. The BPC CE based PSC showed a PCE of 6.78%, higher than those of PSCs with pure carbon (3.72%), PC (5.39%) and BC (5.20%) CEs, and rivaling that of Au-based solar cell (7.22%). The abundant availability, low cost and excellent properties of BPC CE provide a promising prospect for their further applications in perovskite solar cells.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1 Materials

Lead iodide (aladdin, 99.99%), methylammonium iodide (CH₃NH₃I, Dalian HeptachromaSolarTech Co., Ltd., China), titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) ([(CH₃)₂CHO]₂Ti(C₅H₇O₂)₂, Sigma Aldrich, 75 wt % in 2-propanol), TiO₂ paste (Dyesol 18 NR-T), dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%), γ-butyrolactone (Macklin, 99.9%), 2-propanol, absolute ethanol, HCl, H₃PO₄, H₃BO₃, sucrose and zinc powder (median 6-9 μm, 97.5%) were used without further purification.

# 2.2 Synthesis of pure carbon, P-doped carbon, B-doped carbon and B/P co-doped carbon

Phosphorus-doped carbon was synthesized by a typical procedure. Briefly, 2.6 g phosphoric acid was dissolved in 30 ml deionized water, and 3.0 g sucrose was added

6

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

into the phosphoric acid aqueous solution under continuous magnetic stirring. The resultant solution was then transferred into a 50 ml Teflon-lined autoclave, and heated at 180 °C for 12 h in a furnace. The hydrothermal product was dried at 120 °C for 12 h and subsequently annealed at 900 °C for 3 h in flowing N₂. The as-prepared black powder was manually grounded with a mortar and pestle for 1 h, and then milled in the 100 ml ceramic vessel filled with 50 g ∅ 20 mm, 40 g ∅ 10 mm and 10 g ∅ 5 mm zirconia balls. The milling was operated in argon with a rotation speed of 450 rpm and ball/carbon mass ratio of 20 : 1 for 24 h. Finally, the phosphorus-doped carbon (PC) was obtained. The BPC was prepared by soaking 2 g PC into 30 mL 0.2 M H₃BO₃ solution, followed by the addition of 0.2 g sucrose. The solution was dried at 100 °C for 12 h and annealed at 900 °C for 3 h in N₂ atmosphere with a heating rate of 2 °C min⁻¹. Pure carbon was prepared by annealing the sucrose at 900 °C for 3 h in flowing N₂, followed with a similar ball milling process to that of PC. The BC was prepared with the similar procedure to that of BPC except by using pure carbon instead of PC.

# 2.3 Preparation of carbon CEs

The carbon paste was typically prepared as follows. 0.20 g BPC (pure carbon or PC) was firstly dispersed in 1 mL γ-butyrolactone solution, magnetically stirred for 10 min and continually sonicated for 30 min to prepare a dispersion solution. Next, 20 mg PbI₂ and 8 mg CH₃NH₃I were successively added to the above solution and stirred continuously for 30 min. The resulting paste was coated onto the current collector of aluminum foil by the doctor-blade method and dried in vacuum at 100 °C for 45 min to obtain the carbon CEs. Then the prepared carbon CEs were pressed under a

# 2.4 Fabrication of solar cells

Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass (sheet resistance 14 Ω sq  $  ^{-1}  $  , Nippon Sheet Glass, 2 cm × 1.5 cm) was masked by coating 3M magic tape on both sides, and the formed gap between the tape with ca. 2 mm width was filled with Zn powder. The FTO layer was then etched away by dripping ~3 M HCl over the Zn. The etched FTO glass slides were rinsed with detergent, water, and then ethanol, dried in air, and heated at 500 °C for 10 min to remove any retained organics. The compact TiO  $  _{2}  $   blocking layer with ca. 70 nm in thickness was deposited onto the FTO by spin-coating a solution of titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) (75 wt %) in anhydrous ethanol (1 : 20 v/v) at 2000 rpm for 30 s. The film was dried at 120 °C for 15 min and then annealed at 500 °C for 30 min. A ca. 400 nm mesoporous TiO  $  _{2}  $   layer was spin-coated from a diluted TiO  $  _{2}  $   paste (Dyesol 18 NR-T, 1 : 2 dilution by weight in anhydrous ethanol) at 2000 rpm for 60 s. The films were dried at 120 °C for 15 min and annealed at 500 °C for 30 min. The double-layer TiO  $  _{2}  $   films were treated with 40 mM TiCl  $  _{4}  $   aqueous solution, recalcined at 450 °C for 30 min and cooled to room temperature.

The  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  was deposited into the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  films by a two-step sequential method. Firstly,  $ 0.8\mathrm{M} $   $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  in DMF was spin-coated onto the films at 2000 rpm for 60s, followed by annealing at  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 30 min to remove the DMF solvent. Subsequently, the  $ 12\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  solution of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  in 2-propanol was dropped onto the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  until it was fully soaked, maintained for about 2 min, and then rinsed with

2-propanol thoroughly. After heating the films at 100 °C for 45 min, the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   was crystallized in the   $  TiO_{2}  $   mesoscopic film. Meanwhile, a layer of perovskite capping layer, which could separate the   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer from the counter electrode, was formed on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   mesoporous layer.

Perovskite solar cell (PSC) was assembled as sandwich-like structure by pressing carbon CE onto the prepared  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  photoanode at  $ 1.5\mathrm{MPa} $ . The completed PSCs were then scraped (typical area of  $ \sim 0.25~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ ) to separate the active area of solar cell from the surrounding material before testing. The process from  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  deposition to PSC fabrication was conducted in a  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $  glove box. As reference, the PSC completed by evaporating a gold contact in a vacuum chamber as CE was also prepared.

As shown in Figure 2b, the B 1s spectrum was deconvoluted into three peaks at 191.0, 192.0 and  $ 192.9\mathrm{eV} $ , which can be attributed to the form of  $ \mathrm{BC}_3 $ ,  $ \mathrm{BCO}_2 / \mathrm{BC}_2\mathrm{O} $  and boron oxide, respectively [34]. It suggested the electrical conductivity of carbon increased upon P-doping. In order to evaluate the charge transport properties of different carbon materials in perovskite solar cells, the carbon-based perovskite solar cells have been fabricated according to the procedure in Figure 4a. The architecture of the prepared carbon-based perovskite solar cell, as illustrated in Figure 4b, is FTO glass/compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  (C- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ )/mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  (M- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ )/ $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3/ $ carbon. Under illumination, the perovskite layer of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  absorbs the light and is excited, generating the electron-hole pairs. Subsequently, the electrons are injected into the conduction band of M- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  (-4.0 eV), and are then transported to the FTO (-4.4 eV). Meanwhile, the holes are extracted by the carbon electrode (Fermi level: -4.81-5.14 eV) from the valance band (-5.4 eV) of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ . Finally, the electrons and holes are collected at the FTO and carbon electrodes, respectively[39].

# 4. Conclusions

To conclude, the B/P co-doped bilayer carbon CE has been successfully applied as a hole extractor in HTM-free perovskite solar cell and demonstrated superior photovoltaic performance to the pure carbon and P-doped carbon. The B doping contributed to the increase of work function of carbon surface, which is beneficial for improving the p-type conductivity of carbon, and the P doping decreased the sheet resistance of bulk carbon. As a result, the utilization of B/P co-doped bilayer carbon CE with the acceptor-like B doped carbon outer layer and donor-like P doped carbon inner layer leads to the enhanced hole extraction of the carbon surface contacting with perovskite and increased electrical conductivity of the bulk carbon, thus improving the photovoltaic performance of the BPC-based PSCs. The  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / CH_3NH_3PbI_3 / BPC} $  CE perovskite solar cell without hole-transporting material and Au electrode provided a power conversion efficiency of  $ 6.78\% $  under AM 1.5,  $ 100~\mathrm{mWcm^{-2}} $  condition, higher than those of the PSCs with PC (5.39%), BC (5.20%) and pure carbon (3.72%) CEs, and comparable to that of Au-based device (7.22%). With the outstanding advantages of low cost, simple fabrication, and good stability, the BPC is a promising CE candidate for HTM-free PSCs, which would accelerate the commercialization process of PSCs.
