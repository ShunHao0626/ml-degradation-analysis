# Dopant-free hole transporting materials with supramolecular interactions and reverse diffusion for efficient and modular p-i-n perovskite solar cells

The rational design of dopant-free organic hole-transporting layer (HTL) materials is still a challenge for realizing high-efficient and stable p-i-n planar perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs). Here, we synthesized two   $  \pi  $  -conjugated small-molecule HTL materials through tailoring the backbone and conjugated side chain to carefully control molecular conformation. The resultant BDT-TPA-sTh containing a planar fused benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']dithiophene (BDT) core and a conjugated thiophene side chain showed the planar conformation. X-ray crystallography showed a favorable stacking model in solid states under the parallel-displaced   $  \pi-\pi  $   and additional S-  $  \pi  $   weak-bond supramolecular interactions, thus achieving an obviously increased hole mobility without dopants. As an HTL material in p-i-n planar pero-SCs, the marginal solubility of BDT-TPA-sTh enabled inverse diffusion into the perovskite precursor solution for assisting the subsequent perovskite film growth and passivating the uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ion defects. As a result, the planar p-i-n pero-SCs exhibited a champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 20.5% and enhanced moisture stability. Importantly, the BDT-TPA-sTh HTL material also showed weak thickness-photovoltaic dependence, and the pero-SCs with blade-coated BDT-TPA-sTh as a HTL achieved a 15.30% PCE for the 1-cm  $  ^{2}  $   modularized device. This HTL material design strategy is expected to pave the way toward high-performance, dopant-free and printing large-area planar p-i-n pero-SCs.

# 1 Introduction

The organic-metal halide perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) have gained significant attention in industry and academia due to their rapidly rising power conversion efficiencies

(PCEs) [1-8]. The planar p-i-n pero-SC, as one of the various typical device structures, has attracted enormous attention because of its low-temperature solution-fabrication process, reduced hysteresis, and low-cost properties [3,9-13]. The hole-transporting layer (HTL) of the planar p-i-n pero-SC, particularly the organic HTL, greatly promotes the development of device performances because of its easy tailoring

Among them, the conventional poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) poly-styrenesulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) HTL slowly faded out of interest due to its acidic and low work-function nature [17]. To address these issues, some neutral polymer HTLs with deep highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) levels, such as poly(bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine) (PTAA), have been exploited, and they are playing the leading role in the pursuit of high PCE and high stability [18–21].

Compared with polymer HTLs, organic small-molecule materials possess remarkable advantages, such as well-defined chemical structures, no batch-to-batch variation, and fine-tuned molecular geometries, making them more likely to yield high-performances and to be reproducible HTL materials   $  [22–24]  $  . Up to now, most small-molecule HTL materials were designed based on a symmetrical structure containing a central core and triphenylamine (TPA) moiety branches   $  [25–29]  $  , where their optoelectronic properties, such as the intermolecular interactions, hole mobility, and charge extracting ability, were generally tuned by choosing various cores. When using the single atom with   $  \sigma  $   orbitals as the central core, it could stabilize electronic properties of the whole molecules due to the bond interaction between the low-lying   $  \sigma^{*}  $   orbital of the central atom and the   $  \pi^{*}  $   orbital of the neighboring conjugated branches. For example, the HTL materials, Si-OMeTPA with a Si central core   $  [12]  $   and an 4,4',4"-tris(N-3-methylphenyl-N-phenylamino) triphenyl amine (m-MTDATA) with N central core   $  [30]  $  , were demonstrated to present good stability and well matched HOMO energy levels with the valence band (VB) of perovskites. However, these starburst-shaped HTLs usually exhibit large twisting molecular conformations due to the weak steric hindrance stemming from the small size of the single atom core. The low coplanarity would lead to an unfavorable intermolecular interaction, thus decreasing the hole mobility (  $  \mu_{h}  $  ) of HTLs. The conjugated aromatic groups with the increased size were also reported as the central core. As expected, the aromatic core can suppress the twisting of its adjacent branches to a certain extent probably due to the less crowded space, which is beneficial to enhancing   $  \pi-\pi  $   interactions between the core and its neighbors   $  [16,31]  $  . To further increase the coplanarity of HTL materials,   $  \pi  $  -conjugated polycyclic planar units with a large size and much delocalized   $  \pi  $   orbitals were incorporated. The resultant symmetrical molecules with the enhanced coplanarity and conjugation would facilitate intermolecular interactions, and thus improve the   $  \mu_{h}  $   of the related HTLs   $  [32]  $  . Thus,   $  \pi  $  -conjugated polycyclic planar units with pronounced planarity, such as truxene   $  [33]  $  , imide-functionalized thiophene   $  [14]  $  , and fluoranthene   $  [34]  $  , are usually selected as the core of HTL materials. For example, Chen et al.   $  [33]  $   incorporated truxene in a   $  \pi  $  -conjugated HTL (Trux-OMeTAD), giving a   $  C_{3h}  $   symmetrical molecular geometry, which obviously in-

creased the hole mobility of the HTL due to the favorable face-on   $  \pi-\pi  $   stacking. The pero-SCs based on this HTL without dopants achieved a PCE of 18.6%. Guo et al. [14] developed an imide-functionalized thiophene core for enhancing intermolecular   $  \pi-\pi  $   stacking to increase the hole mobility of an HTL (MPA-BTTI). Consequently, the MPA-BTTI-based p-i-n pero-SCs displayed a remarkable efficiency of 21.17%. Therefore, it is particularly important to develop new polycyclic planar   $  \pi  $  -conjugated organic small molecules or other efficient strategies to enhance the intermolecular interactions for forming a favorable stacking model, thus improving the hole mobility and related device performances. Benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']dithio-phene (BDT) with tri-fused rings possesses a high planarity, which could enhance intermolecular   $  \pi-\pi  $   interactions when using as a central core [35]. Meanwhile, BDT units provide multiple active sites like 4 and 8. These symmetric sites enable the conjugated branches to attach on BDT aimed at extending   $  \pi  $   conjugation [36]. On the other hand, Lewis-base S atoms in BDT unit can passivate the uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   defects in perovskites [15]. These merits promote BDT to be great potential in designing high-performance HTL materials of pero-SCs.

In addition, supramolecular interactions are usually used to stabilize molecular conformations and enhance molecular stacking through weak-bond interactions   $  [37–40]  $  . In 2016, Nazeeruddin et al.   $  [37]  $   found that short intermolecular S  $  \cdots  $  S interactions in HTLs enabled the enhancement of hole transporting abilities. Jen et al.   $  [38]  $   developed a dipolar chromophore (BTPA-TCNE) and demonstrated that the dipole-dipole interactions formed by the push-pull effect could facilitate antiparallel molecular packing for realizing dopant-free HTL materials in n-i-p pero-SCs. However, such supramolecular strategies for designing HTL materials of p-i-n pero-SCs have rarely been reported, and the resultant self-assembly HTLs assisted by weak-bond interactions are less influenced by the inhomogeneous HTLs on a large scale. Moreover, HTL materials should have good wettability to perovskite solution when fabricating large-area pero-SCs   $  [41]  $  . Therefore, designing polycyclic planar   $  \pi  $  -conjugated organic small-molecule HTLs with supramolecular interactions and good wettability may provide a new path to realize high-performance p-i-n pero-SCs even on a large scale.

In this work, two  $ \pi $ -conjugated small-molecule HTL materials, BDT-TPA- $ s $ Th and BDT-TPA- $ s $ TPA, contain a fused BDT core-based backbone (BDT-TPA) and the conjugated side chains of 2-ethylhexyl-thienyl groups ( $ s $ Th) and TPA with methoxyl end groups ( $ s $ TPA), respectively. As shown in Figure 1(a), the BDT core with high planarity was used to enhance molecular  $ \pi-\pi $  interactions, while the terminal TPA in the backbone and conjugated side chains was incorporated to increase hole transportation abilities and fine-tune molecular conformations, respectively. # 2 Results and discussion

Figure 1(a) depicts the chemical structures of the two BDT-TPA-backbone-based HTL materials BDT-TPA-sTh and BDT-TPA-sTPA with different side chains (sTh and sTPA). Both the HTL materials were simply synthesized by Stille or Suzuki coupling reactions according to the synthetic routes shown in Schemes S1 and S2 in the Supporting Information online. The molecular structures of the two HTL materials were confirmed by   $  {}^{1}  $  H and   $  {}^{13}  $  C NMR spectra as well as matrix assisted laser desorption ionization-time of flight (MALDI-TOF) mass spectrometry in Figures S1–S6 (Supporting Information online). Both the HTLs exhibited good thermal stability, giving the decomposition temperatures (  $  T_{d}  $  ) of 414.4 °C for BDT-TPA-sTh and 440.3 °C for BDT-TPA-

$ s\mathrm{TPA} $  at  $ 5\% $  weight loss (Figure S7). Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) thermograms (Figure S8) revealed that the two HTL materials were amorphous in nature because there was no melting peak or crystallization peak observed in the heating and cooling processes. The slightly red-shifted maximum absorption peak  $ (\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}) $  of BDT-TPA- $ s\mathrm{Th} $  (437 nm) in dilute chlorobenzene (CB) solution, compared with that of BDT-TPA- $ s\mathrm{TPA} $  (417 nm), indicates that the thiophene moiety with the property of the improved coplanarity of the main chain could more efficiently enhance molecular conjugations (Figure 1(b)) [39,42]. This behavior is consistent with the molecular simulation results (Figure S9(a)) [43], where the electron density of the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of BDT-TPA- $ s\mathrm{Th} $  is more widely distributed in  $ s\mathrm{Th} $  units. As for the absorption spectra of the films, BDT-TPA- $ s\mathrm{Th} $  showed a larger red-shifted  $ \lambda_{\mathrm{max}} $  with changing from solution to film than BDT-TPA- $ s\mathrm{TPA} $ , indicating that a stronger intermolecular interaction occurred for the BDT-TPA- $ s\mathrm{Th} $  molecules in a solid state, which is also evidenced by its improved hole mobilities (Figure 1(c) and Table S1, Supporting Information online). Interestingly, the  $ \mu_{\mathrm{h}} $  of both HTLs were dramatically increased by more than one order of magnitude after the thermal annealing process. Particularly for the BDT-TPA- $ s\mathrm{Th} $  HTL, the 20-times-higher hole mobility than that of the as-cast film suggests a more efficient intermolecular interaction enhanced by the thiophene-conjugated side chain under the thermal annealing process. In addition, as shown in Figure 1(d), the different conjugated side chains have a slight influence on the oxidation potentials. The resultant similar

To study the enhanced molecular interaction in depth, the molecular geometry and the intermolecular stacking model were investigated. The density functional theory (DFT) calculations were conducted to evaluate the optimized geometrical structures of the two HTL materials. As shown in Figure S9(b), the BDT core and its two adjacent phenylene  $ (m\mathrm{Ph}) $  groups in the backbone can form a large and planar  $ \pi $ -conjugated system. The various conjugated side chains showed a negligible effect on the dihedral angles between the BDT core and the diphenylamine/mPh group, which was  $ \sim 20^{\circ} $  and  $ \sim 47^{\circ} $ , respectively (Figure S9(c)). The similar BDT-TPA backbone conformation in both HTL materials indicated that the backbone did not affect  $ \pi-\pi $  interactions. Therefore, the red-shifted UV-Vis absorption spectra and the enhanced hole mobility of the BDT-TPA- $ s $ Th film compared with those of the BDT-TPA- $ s $ TPA film suggest that an additional interaction occurred in the BDT-TPA- $ s $ Th HTL. To reveal this behavior, a suitable crystal of BDT-TPA- $ s $ Th was obtained by slow evaporation at room temperature. The molecular geometry of the BDT-TPA- $ s $ Th crystal is presented in Figure 2(a), and full crystallographic details are shown in Table S2. # 3 Conclusions

In conclusion, we designed a  $ \pi $ -conjugated small-molecule HTL material with a symmetric structure by rationally selecting the planar BDT core, TPA terminal groups, as well as conjugated  $ s $ Th side chains. The conformation and stacking model of the resultant BDT-TPA- $ s $ Th was directly observed by X-ray crystallography measurement from its single crystal. The pronounced planarity with parallel-displaced  $ \pi-\pi $  and additional S- $ \pi $  supramolecular interactions between neighboring molecules contributed to an improved hole mobility. We also found that the marginal solubility of BDT-TPA- $ s $ Th in perovskite solution enabled inverse diffusion into the perovskite film, which could be used to further passivate the uncoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  ion defects by Lewis-base S-atoms in BDT-TPA- $ s $ Th without damaging the under-layer HTL. The p-i-n planar pero-SCs using BDT-TPA- $ s $ Th without dopants as the HTL not only realized a high PCE and improved moisture stability but also demonstrated the suitability for blade-coated technology to fabricate large area devices. We believe that this HTL design concept through supramolecular interactions and inverse diffusion will pave the way for new HTL materials of perovskite-based optoelectronic devices.
