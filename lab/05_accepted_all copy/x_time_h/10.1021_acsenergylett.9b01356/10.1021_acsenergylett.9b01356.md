# ABSTRACT

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite absorbers with unique and intriguing optoelectronic property have received increasing attention due to their low cost and high power conversion efficiency (PCE).  $  ^{1-8}  $   Mainly benefiting from the great efforts on engineering of device structure, solvent, composition, additive and interface, PCEs of PSCs were strikingly enhanced from original 9.7%  $  ^{9}  $   to presently certified 24.2%  $  ^{10}  $   within the only several years. However, there is still considerable room between this efficiency value and theoretical PCE exceeding 30%.  $  ^{11}  $   It is widely recognized that non-radiative recombination at the grain boundaries (GBs) or surface of perovskite films as well as interface should be mainly responsible for the above PCE gap.  $  ^{12-17}  $   Additionally, it was reported that degradation of perovskite films preferentially occurs at GBs because of trap-assisted non-radiative recombination.  $  ^{11, 18-24}  $   This indicates that it could be an effective strategy for improving the stability of PSCs to reduce GB density via modulating perovskite crystallization and thus increasing grain size. To further enhance the device efficiency and stability, it is highly required to minimize the non-radiative recombination losses through further improvement of perovskite film quality.

Towards this goal, various strategies have been developed, such as perovskite composition engineering,  $  ^{25}  $   precursor solvent,  $  ^{26}  $   anti-solvent engineering,  $  ^{27}  $   fundamental procedure optimization  $  ^{28}  $   and additive engineering.  $  ^{29}  $   Among them, additive engineering has made significant contributions to the enhancement of PCE and stability over the past several years. However, most of the reported additives usually only play one function instead of simultaneous realization of several functions (such as

Here, we introduce silica crosslinking agents functionalized by different terminal group, including 3-cyanopropyltriethoxysilane (CPTS), trimethoxy(propyl)silane (PTS) and (3-mercaptopropyl)trimethoxysilane (MPTS), into  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  precursor solution in sequential deposition process, aiming at enhancing device PCE and stability. We discovered an effective and reproducible multifunctional additive engineering strategy, where crosslinking agents with different terminated functional groups were introduced into  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  precursor solution. Among the investigated additives, the MPTS with -SH functional group exhibited the best performance and was simultaneously conferred by three functions of crystallization and stability improvement along with defect passivation. As a result, the device with MPTS exhibited a PCE of as high as  $ 20.8\% $ , which is much higher than  $ 18.4\% $  of the control device, which was mainly attributed to reduced non-radiative recombination losses as a consequence of improved crystallinity and carrier lifetimes. In addition, greatly improved thermal and moisture stability were observed upon incorporation of MPTS. Our study also proves indirectly that iodide vacancy dominates as defects in perovskite film.
