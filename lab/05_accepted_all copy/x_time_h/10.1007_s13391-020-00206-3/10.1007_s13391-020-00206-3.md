# Efficient and Stable Ideal Bandgap Perovskite Solar Cell Achieved by a Small Amount of Tin Substituted Methylammonium Lead lodide

Yongqi Yin  $  ^{1,2}  $   · Sufang Fu  $  ^{1}  $   · Sheng Zhou  $  ^{1}  $   · Yuling Song  $  ^{1}  $   · Lin Li  $  ^{1}  $   · Mingyi Zhang  $  ^{1}  $   · Jie Wang  $  ^{2}  $   · Pandiyarajan Mariyappan  $  ^{3}  $   · Saad M. # Abstract

Ideal bandgap  $ \sim 1.4\mathrm{eV} $  of lead-tin (Pd-Sn) organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite absorber is essential to further enhance the power conversion efficiency of perovskite solar cells (PVSCs). However, due to the facile oxidation of  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $ , large amount of Sn substituted Pb based PVSCs suffer with low stability in ambient environment. In this work, we realize an ideal bandgap perovskite by introducing a small amount (10 mol%) of  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  to methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI₃). A large grain size  $ \mathrm{MAPb}_{0.9}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.1}\mathrm{I}_3 $  films with strong absorbance can be obtained. As a result, the best performance up to  $ 18.3\% $  efficiency is achieved. Importantly, the  $ \mathrm{MAPb}_{0.9}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.1}\mathrm{I}_3 $  cell retains  $ 90\% $  of its performance after operation at the maximum power point under full 1-sun illumination for  $ 500\mathrm{h} $ , in contrast to the quick degradation of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  cell. This study demonstrates the promising potential of stable and efficient ideal band-gap Pb-Sn PVSCs.

# 1 Introduction

Organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) have attracted great attention due to their favorable properties and facile solution processability. In addition, many different kinds of perovskite nanocrystals have been reported by bottom-up approaches in recent years   $  [1-4]  $  . power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PVSCs has been improved from 3.8% to over 25% within a few years   $  [5–14]  $  . It is mainly due to the rapid development of methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) (MA=CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  ) based metal halide PVSCs. Despite such a rapid progress, the theoretical limit of PCE of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   based PVSCs with a band gap of 1.6 eV is about 31% according to the Shockley–Queisser (S–Q) detailed balanced calculations   $  [15]  $  . Moreover, due to the parasitic optical and electrical losses in solar cells, the PCE of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   based PVSCs is approaching to the practical limit.

To overcome the theoretical limit of Pb based PVSCs, lead–tin (Pd–Sn) based perovskites with a tunable bandgap from 1.2 to 1.4 eV are expected to be a promising candidate. The ideal band gap of 1.4 eV Pb–Sn absorbers with a theoretical PCE limit of 33% can be obtained by adjusting the ratio of Pb and Sn in Pb–Sn perovskites [16–23]. Recently, Pb–Sn perovskites with chlorine and guanidinium thiocyanate additives have shown striking efficiencies of 18.4% and 20.4%, respectively [24, 25]. Although with the improved efficiency, the low band-gap Pb–Sn perovskites are unstable in air due to the oxidation of bivalent tin (  $  Sn^{2+}  $  ). The shelf lifetime of the best Pb–Sn based devices in ambient atmosphere is about 80 h [24]. Rationally, prerequisites for obtaining ideal band gap Pb–Sn perovskites are a small amount of Sn in the Pb–Sn perovskites, which should be beneficial for improving their robustness against ambient environment [26].

In this work, a small amount of Sn has been introduced into  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ , by adjusting Sn/Pb molar ratios  $ x $  ( $ x = 0, 0.05, 0.1 $ ) to obtain an ideal bandgap  $ \mathrm{MAPb}_{1 - x}\mathrm{Sn}_x\mathrm{I}_3 $  perovskite film.  $ 10\mathrm{mol}\% $  of Sn substituted  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite film shows the larger grain size with high absorbance. The PCE of  $ \mathrm{MAPb}_{0.9}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.1}\mathrm{I}_3 $  PVSCs is successfully increased up to  $ 18.25\% $ . Most importantly, the  $ \mathrm{MAPb}_{0.9}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.1}\mathrm{I}_3 $  cells retain  $ 90\% $  of their initial performance after  $ 500\mathrm{h} $  of operation at the maximum power point under full 1-sun illumination.

# 2 Results and Discussion

An anti-solvent dripping process was used to deposit   $  MAPb_{1-x}Sn_{x}I_{3}  $   (x=0, 0.05, 0.1) films on the poly(ethylene dioxythiophene): poly(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT: PSS) hole transporting layer. The details are shown in experimental section. Figure 1a–c shows the surface morphology of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ ,   $  MAPb_{0.95}Sn_{0.05}I_{3}  $ , and   $  MAPb_{0.9}Sn_{0.1}I_{3}  $   films observed by the field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM). The   $  MAPb_{0.9}Sn_{0.1}I_{3}  $   film has a little larger grain sizes of 300–500 nm compared to   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   film with grain sizes of 200–400 nm. Since the grain boundaries are considered as the source of traps and defects in previous reports, [27, 28] such larger sized grains with less grain

boundaries inside the film can promote the charge transfer at associated interface. Figure 1d shows wide-angle X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns. Six dominant characteristic peaks possessed by all films at angles of  $ 14.2^{\circ} $ ,  $ 20.1^{\circ} $ ,  $ 23.5^{\circ} $ ,  $ 24.5^{\circ} $ ,  $ 28.4^{\circ} $  and  $ 31.9^{\circ} $  can be assigned to the crystallographic planes of (110), (122), (211), (202), (220) and (222) of the tetragonal structure of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  [29]. The intensity of diffraction peak at  $ 2\theta = 14.2^{\circ} $  of  $ \mathrm{MAPb}_{0.9}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.1}\mathrm{I}_3 $  film is the strongest among all films, indicating the best crystallinity of  $ \mathrm{MAPb}_{0.9}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.1}\mathrm{I}_3 $  film. The improvement in the crystallinity and grain size of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite films can be attributed to the addition of the small amount of Sn.

Ultraviolet–visible (UV–Vis) absorption spectra were measured to study the absorbance and band-gap of all the perovskites films, as shown in Fig. 2a. MAPb $  _{0.9}  $ Sn $  _{0.1}  $ I $  _{3}  $  film presents an obvious extended absorption range (up to 900 nm) and a strongest absorbance in the visible wavelength range. The bandgap of MAPbI $  _{3}  $ , MAPb $  _{0.95}  $ Sn $  _{0.05}  $ I $  _{3}  $  and MAPb $  _{0.9}  $ Sn $  _{0.1}  $ I $  _{3}  $  films are calculated to be 1.60, 1.50 and 1.46 eV by Kubelka–Munk-transformed diffuse reflectance spectra, as presented in Fig. 2b. The bandgap of MAPb $  _{0.9}  $ Sn $  _{0.1}  $ I $  _{3}  $  (1.46 eV) is almost an ideal bandgap for solar cells according to the S–Q calculations [15]. Moreover, the energy level of all perovskite films was estimated by photoelectron spectroscopy (PES) in Fig. 2c. With increasing the amount of Sn, the valence band (VB) of all perovskites are gradually increased from – 5.45 to – 5.12 eV. The corresponding energy level diagram of the studied devices with a configuration of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/Perovskites/Phenyl-C $  _{61}  $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM)/bathocuproine (BCP)/Ag were illustrated in Fig. 2d. The energy band-gap mismatch value between MAPb $  _{0.9}  $ Sn $  _{0.1}  $ I $  _{3}  $  perovskite film and PEDOT: PSS (0.02 eV) is much smaller than that (0.35 eV) of MAPbI $  _{3}  $ , which can reduce the carrier recombination in the interface of perovskites and PEDOT:PSS [30].

Finally, we studied the ambient stability of the fabricated devices. Figure 3c shows their normalized PCEs as a function of the storage time in ambient environment with a humility of 40% and temperature 25 °C. Promisingly, all the cells maintain over 90% of their initial efficiency after 360 h, implying the substitution of Pb with a very small amount of Sn does not affect the ambient shelf life. For comparison, we also fabricated 20% Sn substituted MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  (MAPb  $  _{0.8}  $  Sn  $  _{0.2}  $  I  $  _{3}  $  ) PVSC to test its ambient stability. Although the fresh MAPb  $  _{0.8}  $  Sn  $  _{0.2}  $  I  $  _{3}  $  PVSC shows a promising PCE of 17.49%, the PCE is rapidly dropped to 31% of its initial value after exposing to ambient atmosphere for 120 h (Figure S1). This suggests the concentration of Sn should be kept as small as possible to enhance the ambient stability of Pb–Sn mixed PVSCs. Moreover, we fabricated 10 individual cells for devices with different Pb and Sn ratio to investigate the reproducibility of all the devices, the corresponding photovoltaic characteristics for devices were depicted in Figure S2. It is found that all the devices show a

high reproducibility. Finally, the operation stability of all devices was examined in a   $  N_{2}  $   environament at maximum power output point under AM 1.5 light-soaking. As shown in Fig. 3d, 92% and 75% of initial PCE are maintained for the   $  MAPb_{0.9}Sn_{0.1}I_{3}  $   and   $  MAPb_{0.95}Sn_{0.05}I_{3}  $   cells even after 500 h, whereas the performance of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   device quickly drops to 32% after 500 h. This might be due to the stronger Sn–I bond (205 kJ mol  $  ^{-1}  $  ) than the Pb–I bond (142 kJ mol  $  ^{-1}  $  ) [31], implying that Pb–I bond is easier to break than Sn–I bond under light illumination or external bias. This phenomenon indicates a promising operation stability of Pb–Sn PVSCs under the light bias if we can prevent the moisture and oxygen, laiding a solid foundation for the development of efficient and stable Pb–Sn PVSCs.

# 4 Experimental Section

# 4.1 General Methods

The following chemicals including PEDOT:PSS (Clevious, AL 4083), SnI $ _2 $  (99.99%, Sigma-Aldrich), CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I (MAI) (>98%, Tokyo Chemical Industry Co., Japan), PCBM (99.5%, Lumtec Co., Taiwan), bathocuproine

(>99%, Wako) were used as received form commercial sources. All solutions were filtered with a 0.2   $  \mu  $  m PVDF filter prior to use.

# 4.2 Solar Cell Fabrication

ITO glass substrates were cleaned by detergent, DI water, acetone, and isopropanol for 15 min via ultra-sonicated, respectively. After cleaning, the ITO substrates were treated by air plasma cleaner for 15 min. PEDOT:PSS layer was deposited on the ITO glass at 4000 rmp for 60 s, and then annealed at 150 °C for 15 min in air. To prepare MAPb  $  _{1-x}  $  Sn  $  _{x}  $  I  $  _{3}  $  (x=0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2) perovskites layer, perovskite solutions were prepared by dissolved different precursors of MAI, PbI  $  _{2}  $ , SnI  $  _{2}  $  in their correct stoichiometry in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF):Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO)=4:1 solvent with a total concentration of 1 M.
