# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells based on organolead halide light harvester, as a economical substitute to silicon solar cells have recently received extensive attention due to excellent photoelectric conversion efficiency, giveaway prices, easy fabrication, light weight and being used as flexible devices.   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , which has the advantages of high extinction coefficient, high carrier mobility, long carrier diffusion distance, long carrier lifetime, adjustable band gap and a variety of preparation methods, was attempted for the first time in liquid dye-sensitized solar cells in 2009, which resulted in PCE of 3.8% [1]. The PCE of this kind of perovskite solar cell was significantly increased to 6.5% by optimizing   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   concentration, perovskite annealing temperature and electrolyte composition in 2011 [2]. But, the long term stability of liquid dye-sensitized solar cells is poor because the perovskite materials easily dissolve in the polar liquid electrolyte. Hence, by replacing the liquid electrolyte with solid hole conductor (Spiro-OMeTAD), all-solid-state   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  -sensitized solar cell exhibited a PCE of 9.7%, along with excellent long-term stability [3]. The certified PCE of all-solid-state perovskite solar cell has increased rapidly to 22.1% within the past five years [4–10]. Other efforts have been made to improve the performance

The serious charge recombination originates from the thermal instability of perovskite/ZnO and the low electron injection efficiency of ZnO. It is the important issue to be improved in ZnO-based perovskite solar cell (PSC). In this paper, the core-shell structure of  $ \mathrm{ZnO@TiO_2} $  nanorod arrays (NRs) is designed as electron transport layer (ETL) for PSC. A novel synthesis of PSC based on  $ \mathrm{ZnO@TiO_2} $  NRs in ambient atmosphere was proposed. The photoelectric conversion efficiency (PCE) of the core-shell device is  $ 50.46\% $  higher than that of common ZnO nanorod device. This is due to the improved interface contact between nanorods and perovskite layer, and the suppression of charge recombination. The PCE of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  modified device shows still more than  $ 83\% $  after  $ 168\mathrm{h} $ , compared to that of the pristine one which decreased to less than  $ 50\% $ . This is due to  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  modification which can serve as a buffer layer to avoid direct contact between perovskite films and ZnO NRs, and inhibits the decomposition of perovskite film on ZnO NRs. Both theoretical calculation and Raman test result show that the interaction between  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  is mainly the bonding between I atoms of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  slabs and Ti atoms of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  surface at  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 / \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  interface. The mechanism of carrier transport and recombination in the PSC based on ZnO and  $ \mathrm{ZnO@TiO_2} $  NRs was also discussed. These results highlight the potential of  $ \mathrm{ZnO@TiO_2} $  NRs as ETL for all-solid-state PSC with high efficiency and good stability.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Preparation of ZnO NRs

ZnO NRs were synthesized by a modified polymer soft template method reported previously by Min Zhong [26].

Firstly, FTO substrates (25 mm * 20 mm * 2mm) were ultrasonically cleaned with acetone, absolute ethanol and distilled water, and then dried with nitrogen. Zinc acetate dihydrate (Zn(CH₃COO)₂·2H₂O) with a certain quality was dissolved in ethanol solution. Next the precursor solution was spin coated onto the FTO substrates, and the substrates were subsequently annealed. This process was repeated three times to increase the film thickness, and the final films were annealed in air at 350 °C for 30 min. Finally, ZnO seed layers were obtained, which were utilized as the substrates for the growth of ZnO NRs.

Secondly, the PVP-  $  Zn^{2+}  $  complex solution was prepared by mixing equimolar zinc acetate dihydrate aqueous solution with Polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP, MW 1,300,000) aqueous solution under magnetic stirring at 60 °C. Ammonia (  $  NH_{3}\cdot H_{2}O  $  , 0.90 g/mL) was added to adjust the pH value of the PVP-  $  Zn^{2+}  $  complex solution to a certain value. The ZnO-seeded substrates were horizontally placed face down on the suspension bracket in the beakers which contained the mixed solution as the growth solution. The beakers were placed in water bath maintained at 99 °C for several minutes. Then the substrates were taken out and rinsed thoroughly with de-ionized water. Finally they were put into the tube furnace in an oxygen atmosphere. The temperature of tube furnace increased from room temperature to 420 °C with the heating rate of 5 °C/min, and maintained at 420 °C for 30 min during the annealing process before reducing to room temperature.

# 2.2. Preparation of  $ \mathrm{ZnO / TiO_2} $  NRs

$ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanocrystalline shell layer was coated on the surface of  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  NRs by a sol-gel method. Firstly, the FTO substrates covered with  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  NRs were inverted in beakers which contained titanium (IV) butoxide solution with isopropanol as solvent in a certain volume ratio under strong stirring. Water and titanium precursor solution were mixed in a certain  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} / \mathrm{Ti} $   $ (\mathrm{OC}_4\mathrm{H}_9)_4\mathrm{M} $  ratio to cause the hydrolysis reaction and polycondensation reaction to produce  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanoparticles on  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  nanorod surface. # 2.3. Perovskite film and cell fabrication

The whole fabrication process was carried out in ambient atmosphere. A two step sequential deposition method was used to fill the perovskite into   $  ZnO/TiO_{2}  $   NRs. Firstly,   $  PbI_{2}  $   was dissolved in N,N-dimethylformamide at a certain concentration under stirring at 70 °C. The solution was kept at 70 °C during the whole procedure. Then the   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution infiltrated into the   $  ZnO/TiO_{2}  $   NRs by spin-coating method and the sample was dried at 70 °C for 30 min. After cooling down to the room temperature, the sample was dipped into a solution of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   in isopropanol (10 mg/ml) for 2 min, and then the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   solution was spin-coated on the sample. Afterwards the film was dried at 70 °C for 30 min. Next, the hole transport materials (HTM) was prepared by spin-coating 50   $  \mu  $  l co-doped spiro-MeOTAD solution on the perovskite film. Finally, an 80 nm thick gold layer was thermally evaporated on the HTM to form the back contact.

We also use computational study and Raman test to further investigate the interaction of the interface between   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $

perovskite and   $  TiO_{2}  $  . The first-principles method was employed to explore the bonding mode of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/TiO_{2}  $   interface. The calculated cell parameters of tetragonal   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite are that   $  a = 8.94 \AA  $  ,   $  b = 8.94 \AA  $   and   $  c = 12.69 \AA  $  . In our work, the (0 0 1) surface of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   is made up of   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  . Furthermore, XRD result shows that the nanostructure of   $  TiO_{2}  $   is anatase phase. First the interface between anatase   $  TiO_{2}  $   (0 0 1) and   $  PbI_{2}  $   (0 0 1) was considered. The   $  TiO_{2}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   interface is built by connecting   $  TiO_{2}  $   slabs with nine layers   $  PbI_{2}  $   termination (five   $  PbI_{2}  $   and four   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   layers). The optimized stable geometrical structure is shown in Fig. 10(a) and (b). As shown in Fig. 10(a), the interaction between   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}  $   is mainly through Pb atoms of   $  PbI_{2}  $   slabs and under-coordinated O atoms of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface. Another condition was also shown in Fig. 10(b), which demonstrates that the interaction between   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}  $   is mainly through I atoms of   $  PbI_{2}  $   slabs and under-coordinated Ti atoms of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface. Moreover, another interface between anatase   $  TiO_{2}  $   (0 0 1) and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (0 0 1) was also considered, as shown in Fig. 10(c) and (d). The   $  TiO_{2}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   interface is built by connecting   $  TiO_{2}  $   slabs with seven layers   $  PbI_{2}  $   termination (three   $  PbI_{2}  $   and four   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   layers). The interaction between   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite and   $  TiO_{2}  $   interface under this condition is through I atoms of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   slabs and Ti atoms of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface (Fig. 10(c)) or H atoms from   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   slabs and O atoms from the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface (Fig. 10(d)).

Table 4 shows that the maximum efficiency achieved by our work is  $ 10.24\% $ , which is higher than the average efficiency level reported previously [16,32-36]. One cause for this not particularly high PCE is that only nanorod arrays with proper density and length can improve the penetration of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  solution and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  solution into nanorod arrays to complete the reaction of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $ , which is very different from the mesoporous structure. High-density and long-length of nanorod arrays will hinder the permeation of reactive raw materials to make the reaction complete, resulting in the poor cell performance. Next, the carrier lifetime of the PSC based on ZnO nanorods ( $ 10^{-5} $  to  $ 10^{-3} $  s) is shorter than that of the PSC based on mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  ( $ 10^{-2} $  to  $ 10^0 $  s) [37], demonstrating that the charge recombination in the PSC based on ZnO nanorods is more serious than that in mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  based PSC, which is adverse to the cell performance. The other reason is that the whole device fabrication process was carried out in ambient air. As is known to all that both  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite and Co-doped Spiro-OMeTAD materials are very sensitive to water and oxygen. For example, the moisture and oxygen in ambient atmosphere can directly react with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and drive it to negative direction.  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  would be decomposed to  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and then the perovskite film shows a bad morphology with many pinholes and defects which will cause serious charge recombination that is definitely bad for the cell performance. The further increase of the efficiency of the PSC synthesized in this work can be achieved by optimizing the density and length of nanorod arrays and using a glove box.

To further find the facts to confirm the higher   $  V_{OC}  $   and the higher FF of the PSC based on   $  ZnO@TiO_{2}  $   NRs, the EIS measurement in which the frequency ranges from 1 kHz to 100 kHz with 5 mV AC amplitude under a constant light illumination of 100 mw/cm  $  ^{2}  $   was performed to investigate the charge recombination at the interface of nanorods/perovskite, as shown in Fig. 15(a).

The equivalent circuit is exhibited in Fig. 15(b) and the detailed parameters are summarized in Table 5. The effect of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  modification

on the recombination resistance  $ (\mathrm{R_{ct1}}) $  at ZnO NRs/perovskite interface corresponding to the low frequency region [38] in Nyquist plots is appreciable. The  $ \mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{ct1}} $  of ZnO NRs based PSC  $ (834.7\Omega) $  is less than that of  $ \mathrm{ZnO@TiO_2} $  NRs based PSC  $ (923.2\Omega) $ , indicating that  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  modification has reduced the recombination rate between ZnO and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ . We attribute the enhanced  $ V_{OC} $  and  $ FF $  to an enhancement of  $ \mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{ct1}} $  due to  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  modification.

To study the influence of   $  TiO_{2}  $   modification on the stability of ZnO NRs based PSC, photovoltaic tests were carried out once 24 h without package under ambient conditions with relative humidity ranging from 30% to 55%, as shown in Fig. 16. As can be seen from Fig. 16, there is still more than 83% of the PCE of the PSC modified with   $  TiO_{2}  $   after 168 h, compared to that of the pristine one, which declines to less than 50%. The maximum PCE of   $  TiO_{2}  $   modified PSC and unmodified PSC is 8.14% and 5.41%, respectively. We attribute the improved device stability to   $  TiO_{2}  $   modification, which can serve as a buffer layer to avoid direct contact between perovskite film and ZnO NRs, and suppress the decomposition of perovskite film on ZnO NRs.

In order to understand the advantages of  $ \mathrm{ZnO@TiO_2} $  core-shell structure NRs as ETL in PSC better, the schematic diagrams of carrier transport and recombination in the PSC based on  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  and  $ \mathrm{ZnO@TiO_2} $  NRs were exhibited in Fig. 17. The perovskite can contacts directly with FTO electrode due to the existence of pinholes in the  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  layer, as shown in Fig. 17(a). Because of a low interfacial energy barrier, the free electron can re-inject into the valence band of the perovskite easily, leading to serious interface recombination. Moreover, carrier will stay at the  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  layer due to the mismatch of the work function between perovskite and  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $ , which will influence the electron transport in the  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  layer, causing charge accumulation and recombination at the  $ \mathrm{ZnO}/ $  perovskite absorber interface. On the other hand,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  shell layer can eliminate the direct contact between FTO and the perovskite absorber effectively as can be seen in Fig. 17(b). Moreover, the band offset of the  $ \mathrm{ZnO/TiO_2} $  interface will further contribute to the injection and transmission of electrons in the ETL, and lower the charge recombination at the  $ \mathrm{ZnO}/ $  perovskite interface as well. Thus, the overall charge recombination in the PSC is suppressed by  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  modification, and eventually the cell performance is increased considerably.
