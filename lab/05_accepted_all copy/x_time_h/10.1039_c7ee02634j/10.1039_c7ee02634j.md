# Novel quadruple-cation absorber for universal hysteresis elimination for high efficiency and stable perovskite solar cells

Tongle Bu  $  ^{a}  $  , Xueping Liu  $  ^{a}  $  , Yuan Zhou  $  ^{a}  $  , Jianpeng Yi  $  ^{a}  $  , Xin Huang  $  ^{a}  $  , Long Luo  $  ^{a}  $  , Junyan Xiao  $  ^{a}  $  , Zhiliang Ku  $  ^{a}  $  , Yong Peng  $  ^{a}  $  , Fuzhi Huang  $  ^{a*}  $  , Yi-Bing Cheng  $  ^{a,b}  $   and Jie Zhong  $  ^{a*}  $

$  ^{a}  $  State Key Laboratory of Advanced Technology for Materials Synthesis and Processing, Wuhan University of Technology, Wuhan 430070, PR China

# Abstract

Organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have made a striking breakthrough with the power conversion efficiency (PCE) over 22%. However, before moving to commercialization, the hysteresis of PSCs, characterized as an inconsistent photovoltaic conversion property at varied electric field, should be eliminated for stable performance. Herein, we present a novel quadruple-cation perovskite absorber,   $  K_{x}Cs_{0.05}(FA_{0.85}MA_{0.15})_{0.95}Pb(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_{3}  $   (labeled as KCsFAMA), with which the hysteresis in PSCs can be fully eliminated irrespective of electron transportation layers. The incorporation of potassium intensively promotes the crystallization of perovskite film with grain size up to   $  \sim1\mu m  $  , doubled compared to the K free counterparts. Further characterization revealed that less interface defect density, longer carrier lifetime and fast charge transportation have all made contributions to hysteresis-free, stable and high PCE (20.56%) KCsFAMA devices. Moreover, we present a 6 x 6 cm  $  ^{2}  $   sub-module with the KCsFAMA composition achieved a high efficiency of 15.76% without hysteresis. This result suggests the quadruple-cation perovskite is a highly attractive candidate for future developments of efficient and stable PSC modules.

# Introduction

Hybrid organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite materials, which have excellent optoelectric properties such as high light extinction coefficient,  $  ^{1}  $  long charge diffusion length,  $  ^{2,3}  $  long carrier lifetime and high mobility,  $  ^{4}  $  have been widely employed in photovoltaic applications with various

configurations such as mesoporous and planar structures.  $  ^{5}  $   Through the incorporation of additional   $  I^{-}  $  ions to the perovskite films, a record power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 22.1% has been achieved recently.  $  ^{6}  $   This is a big step forward to the commercialization of the perovskite solar cells (PSCs), however, other issues, such as the stability and the J-V hysteresis, are still needed to be addressed,  $  ^{7}  $   especially for the simple planar structured PSCs.  $  ^{8}  $

Hysteresis, actually, can be considered as a type of instability of photovoltaic conversion of solar cell when working under varied external electric field. This phenomenon is not usually observed in silicon solar cells or other thin film solar cells, even some similar semiconductors are involved. Current works aiming to eliminate the hysteresis in PSCs are majorly focused on balancing the charge transport between the electron transport layer (ETL)/perovskite and the hole transport layer (HTL)/perovskite via the ETL/HTL modification and the perovskite interfaces treatments.  $  ^{9,10}  $   For example, the typical   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL based planar PSCs present severe hysteresis are normally ascribed to the low charge extraction of ETL. Works such as ions doping, interface modification or passivation have been applied to address the issue.  $  ^{11-13}  $   However, these modifications present a distractive direction from the focus of perovskite absorber, which is the key cause for the instability of the PSCs. The hysteresis is proposed to be originated from not only the unbalanced charge transport, but also ferroelectric effects, ion migrations, carrier trappings, capacitive effects, and etc.  $  ^{14,15}  $  , which are all strongly related to the quality of the perovskites. Moreover, high quality perovskite absorber, that induces fewer defects and also restrict the ions migration with reduced dominating ions transfer channels at grain boundaries, is also crucial to achieve the high stability and the device performance.  $  ^{16-18}  $   Thus, it would be highly attractive to obtain efficient, hysteresis-free and stable PSCs simultaneously by the measure of producing a superior perovskite film that is enabled to supersede the affection of the ETL/HTL layers and acquire the charge transportation properties similar to those sophisticated inorganic solar cells.

Recently, forming hybrid-cation/anion perovskite and additives incorporation have emerged as an effective strategy to promote perovskite quality. For example, thiourea or thio-semicarbazide with methylammonium additives to methylamine lead iodide (MAPbI $ _3 $ ) perovskite showed increased grain size and device performance. $ ^{19,20} $  Cadmium (Cd) and aluminium (Al) ions have been demonstrated to increase the MAPbI $ _3 $  crystal size resulting in an increased

photoluminescence and reduced density of electronic defects.  $  ^{[21, 22]}  $   Lead thiocyanate   $  (\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{SCN})_{2})  $   and PCBM also increased the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   crystal size and improve the fill factor of the device performance.  $  ^{[23, 24]}  $   Through blending formamidine lead iodide   $  (\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3})  $   and methylamine lead bromine   $  (\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})  $  , superior stability can be achieved for this new category of mixed perovskite solar cells (labeled as FAMA perovskite).  $  ^{[25]}  $   With the doping of Cs and/or Rb in the mixed perovskite it has markedly improved the PCE and stability of the PSCs.  $  ^{[26, 27]}  $   Notably, a recent report achieved an average PCE of 20.2% and the encapsulated device retained 95% of efficiency at 85°C for 500 hours under a full solar illumination and the maximum power point tracking.  $  ^{[27]}  $   Although the above mentioned routes have improved the efficiency and/or stabilities of the devices, unfortunately, notable hysteresis still exists and cannot be fully removed via such perovskite film optimizations. This is probably due to the impure crystalline phases, small crystal size or the existence additives such as the organic agents in the perovskite film somehow inhibiting the charge transport and etc.  $  ^{[28, 29]}  $

To tackle this problem, we have successfully demonstrated stable and hysteresis-free PSCs via a simple preparation of a potassium included quadruple-cation perovskite   $  \left(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{X}}\left(\mathrm{Cs}_{0.05}\left(\mathrm{FA}_{0.85}\mathrm{MA}_{0.15}\right)_{0.95}\mathrm{Pb}\left(\mathrm{I}_{0.85}\mathrm{Br}_{0.15}\right)_{3}\right)\right.  $   labeled as KCsFAMA, where Pb is regarded as a divalent metal). The grain size of KCsFAMA perovskite are significantly increased comparing to the CsFAMA and the FAMA perovskites. We present PSC devices with the configuration of FTO/ compact   $  SnO_{2}  $   (sol-gel)/ perovskite/ Spiro-OMeTAD/ Au. Without any modification at ETL and interface, the hysteresis observed for CsFAMA and the FAMA perovskites was fully eliminated in KCsFAMA devices. This phenomenon is also observed in devices with   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL. The devices with KCsFAMA absorber presented a balanced charge transports, high carrier lifetime and also reduced the trap densities, thus attained a high PCE of 20.56% (0.16 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) and 15.76% for 6 x 6 cm  $  ^{2}  $   module without hysteresis.

To form a quadruple-cation perovskite film, the KI precursor was added to the CsFAMA perovskite ink with desired concentration (see details in support information). distributed across the perovskite film, the same as Cs element. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns (Figure 1a) show the FAMA, CsFAMA and KCsFAMA almost had identical diffraction patterns, indicating the black perovskite phase has been formed. For all the monovalent inorganic perovskites (APbI $ _3 $ ), only Cs is matched to sustain the perovskite structure. Even rubidium (Rb) which is a little smaller than Cs could not form the “established perovskite”. $ ^{27,30} $  Undoubtedly, KPbI $ _3 $  could not form the “established perovskite” according to the Goldschmidt tolerance factor because for the K ion it is 0.8 even smaller than Rb. $ ^{27} $  Actually, no black phase of the KPbI $ _3 $  perovskite was formed even the temperature was above 300  $ ^{\circ} $ C, and it melted when the temperature was over 450  $ ^{\circ} $ C (Figure S2). Understandably, the XRD peaks (Figure 1a inset) of the CsFAMA perovskite has a slight right-shift compared with the FAMA perovskite which means the Cs cations have replaced some of the FA/MA cations and shrunk the lattice of perovskite. While with the incorporation of the K ions, all the peak intensities have increased significantly and showed a slight left-shift (Figure 1a). The left shift of the peaks indicates the enlargement of the d-spacing caused by forming KCsFAMA perovskite. However, the exact positions where K ions stay is not clear for now. It probably takes more than one K ion to occupy A position and/or function as interstitial ions of the perovskite lattice. This proposal could be supported by the further left shifting of (101) peaks as the increasing amount of K in KCsFAMA perovskite (Figure S3). The insertion of the K ions into the lattice have also been confirmed by the X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS), as the obvious peak shift of Pb 4f, I 3d and Br 3d, suggesting disturbed interaction between those ions with K inclusion (Figure S4).

In order to further understand the properties of KCsFAMA perovskite film, the ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) spectra have been conducted to examine the light harvesting changes among the different perovskites (FAMA, CsFAMA, and KCsFAMA) (Figure 1b). The KCsFAMA films exhibit an enhanced light absorption, and a small red shift occurs which means a reduced bandgap (Figure S5). The slightly broaden of the light absorption as well as the higher absorption would make a slight contribute to the photocurrent. Figure 1c showed the scanning electron microscope (SEM) image of the dense FAMA perovskite film with the grain size of  $ \sim 550~\mathrm{nm} $ . With the additive of the Cs, the grain size of the perovskite film (CsFAMA, Figure 1d) became a little smaller of  $ \sim 500~\mathrm{nm} $  as the presence of the Cs ions accelerates the nucleation of the perovskite

to produce more nuclei and thus smaller crystals. Interestingly, the grain size of KCsFAMA increased (  $  \sim1\mu m  $  ) and even larger than the FAMA sample as shown in Figure 1e. In general, the minor doping could decrease the activation energy for the crystallization.  $  ^{29}  $   Thus, with the increasing amount of K, it accelerated the crystallization of perovskite, resulting in an increased grain size. However, with the further increase of the K cations, the   $  K^{+}  $  existed as impurity between the grains to inhibit the grain growth. Therefore, the grain size decreased with the further increasing of the amount of K, as clearly shown in Figure S6. The increased grain size is mainly induced by   $  K^{+}  $  instead of   $  I^{-}  $  because the perovskite added with the same amount of HI did not show any increase in the grain size (Figure S7). The larger grain with the better crystallinity would benefit the performance of the solar cells.

The photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements were performed to further understand recombination behaviours of photo-excited carriers in the perovskite films. An obvious increased PL intensity was obtained in KCsFAMA perovskite films compared to the CsFAMA and FAMA perovskite films (Figure 2a), which reveals the much less defects in the KCsFAMA perovskite films. The TRPL spectra also exhibit a much longer lifetime in the KCsFAMA perovskite films than that of the CsFAMA or FAMA perovskite films (Figure 2b). These PL results demonstrated the KCsFAMA perovskite film present fewer irradiative defects as the K incorporation produced high crystallinity confirmed by the XRD data.

The planar perovskite solar cells based the structure of FTO/ compact SnO $ _2 $ / perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/ Au as illustrated in Figure 3a have been fabricated via a modified green antiovent process. $ ^{32} $  The corresponding cross-sectional SEM image (Figure 3b) has clearly showed the device configuration with the different layers of (i) FTO, (ii) compact SnO $ _2 $ , (iii) KCsFAMA perovskite with large grains, (iv) Spiro-OMeTAD, and (v) Au, respectively. The photovoltaic characteristics and the distribution of results for the FAMA, CsFAMA and KCsFAMA devices are plotted in Figure 3c-f, and the average photovoltaic parameters are listed in Table 1. All the devices were tested under 1 sun AM1.5G illumination with a mask of 0.16 cm $ ^2 $ . An obvious increase in V $ _{oc} $  from 1.085 V for the FAMA cells and 1.097 V for the CsFAMA cells to 1.132 V for the KCsFAMA cells has been observed in Figure 3c. This increased V $ _{oc} $  can be attributed to the self-passivation properties of the K cations, because improved the crystallinity

and increased the grain size of KCsFAMA film (Figure 1). This means less grain boundaries and fewer defects at the interfaces and then reduced recombination with the extended charge carrier lifetime as confirmed by the PL tests. The distributions of   $  J_{sc}  $   of the different devices are showed in Figure 3d. It is worth noting that a smaller amount of the K cation incorporation slightly increased the   $  J_{sc}  $ , but it eventually decreased the   $  J_{sc}  $   when the amount increased further. The photovoltaics characteristics of the champion devices of the FAMA, CsFAMA, KCsFAMA have been listed in table S2, and the typical J-V curves are showed in Figure 4a. The KCsFAMA device has achieved a high PCE over  $ 20\% $ , with a  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  of  $ 1.132\mathrm{V} $ , a  $ \mathrm{J_{sc}} $  of 22.95 mA/cm², and a FF of 0.79. Figure 4b shows the EQE spectra of the corresponding perovskite devices, and the integrated photocurrent densities are  $ 22.30\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $ ,  $ 22.38\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $ , and 22.52 mA/cm² for the FAMA, CsFAMA and KCsFAMA perovskite devices, respectively, which well matches with the  $ \mathrm{J_{sc}} $  measured by the J-V curves (Figure 4a). The corresponding steady-state output current densities at the maximum power point of the devices are showed in Figure 4c. The KCsFAMA perovskite device not only shows a very stable power output but also an extremely fast photoresponse compared to the devices without the K. This rapid photoresponse reflects much more efficient charge extraction in KCsFAMA devices comparing to FAMA, CsFAMA samples that adopt the same cell configuration. Furthermore, the long-term stability of the KCsFAMA devices is extensively enhanced compared to FAMA and CsFAMA devices when stored under ambient air condition with  $ 10\mathrm{RH}\% $  without encapsulation over 1000 hours, indicating that the K incorporation helps to stabilize the perovskite crystals. Therefore, the application of quadruple-cation KCsFAMA perovskite absorber has greatly improved both the PCEs and stability of perovskite solar cells.

common hysteresis in planar perovskite devices. Both the FAMA (18.94% for reverse scan (RS), 15.31% for forward scan (FS) and a hysteresis index (HI, shown in Table S2) of 0.123) and CsFAMA (19.21% for RS, 15.52% for FS, and a HI of 0.114) perovskite devices show obvious hysteresis. However, there is almost no hysteresis present in KCsFAMA solar cell (Table S2 and Figure 4a), with a high PCE of 20.56% (FS) and 20.17% (RS), giving a HI of only 0.016. It was found that with the increasing of the doping content of the K, the HI decreased accordingly (Table S2). The FAMA devices had an obvious hysteresis, even no improvement was obtained by the Cs doping, similar to the results reported by Grätzel et al.  $  ^{33}  $  Notably, the hysteresis was dramatically inhibited when the high quality quadruple-cation KCsFAMA absorber was constructed. We further testified this conclusion by preparing solar cells with TiO  $  _{2}  $  ETL (without any modification), which is well-known for causing hysteresis in planar PSCs. The results lead to an identical conclusion that KCsFAMA absorber inhibit the hysteresis irrespective to the ETL layers (Figure S9), while in FAMA or CsFAMA devices the hysteresis remain.

As the hysteresis is originated from the defects and the carrier trappings, capacitive effects, $ ^{14,15} $ , we believe that the decreased hysteresis was ascribed to the increased charge conductivity and reduced defects with the incorporation of K cation. As showed in Figure 5a, the KCsFAMA perovskite film has a better conductivity than the CsFAMA and FAMA, which demonstrated the fast carries transport in K incorporated perovskite films. In order to accurately assess the trap density in these devices, we have fabricated an electron-only device with a glass/ ITO/ PCBM/ perovskite/ PCBM/ Au architecture and a glass/ ITO/ PEDOT:PSS/ perovskite/ Spiro-OMeTAD/ Au hole-only device. All these devices' dark J–V characteristics were measured to obtain the electron and hole trap densities. $ ^{17} $  Figure 5b and 5c show the typical dark J-V characteristics of electron-only and hole-only devices with different perovskites. The trap densities were estimated by the space-charge-limited-current (SCLC) technique. $ ^{34} $  The linear region at the low bias corresponds to the ohmic response. A trap filling process is identified by the marked increase of the current injection at the intermediate region. The trap density levels are continuously filled by increasing the bias until reaching the trap-filling limit voltage ( $ V_{\text{TFL}} $ ). The trap density can be calculated from the following relation: $ ^{3} $

$$
\mathrm{V} _ {\mathrm{TFL}} = \mathrm{en} _ {\mathrm{t}} \mathrm{L} ^ {2} / 2 \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0}

where L is the thickness of the perovskite films,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative dielectric constant of perovskite (taken as the value of 32 for MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   from a previous report  $  ^{13}  $  ), and   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity. The electron and hole trap density in the KCsFAMA perovskite are calculated to be   $  7.87 \times 10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $   and   $  3.94 \times 10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  , respectively, which are much lower than the FAMA (  $  1.57 \times 10^{16}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $   and   $  1.77 \times 10^{16}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  ) and the CsFAMA (  $  1.28 \times 10^{16}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $   and   $  1.47 \times 10^{16}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  ) perovskite. We consider this reduced trap density a result of high quality of the perovskite crystals and the possible passivation effect from the K doping. The reduced trap densities contribute to reduce and eventually eliminate hysteresis in mixed perovskite devices.  $  ^{35}  $

To further understand the photovoltaic responses of KCsFAMA perovskite solar cells, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements which are considered as a very useful technique to reveal the underlying carrier transport behaviors in the PSCs have been performed on the FAMA, CsFAMA and KCsFAMA PSCs, respectively.  $  ^{36}  $   The Nyquist plots showed in Figure 5d were obtained under AM 1.5 G with an applied bias voltage of 1.0 V close to their   $  V_{oc}  $  . There are two different semicircles in the Nyquist plots which are located at the high and low frequency ranges. In general, the high frequency one corresponds to the hole transport in spiro-OMeTAD, and the other one is related to recombination occurring at the interfaces by fitting with an equivalent circuit of a series resistance (Rs) in series with two RC elements,  $  ^{37,38}  $   the fitted Rs and Rrec (recombination resistance) were plotted with respect to applied forward biases as shown in Figure 5e-f. The value of the Rs in the KCsFAMA perovskite was much lower compared to the FAMA or CsFAMA perovskite devices, while the Rrec in the KCsFAMA device is much higher than the Non-K cation incorporated devices. Then the EIS data clearly shows the improved charge transport and reduced recombination in perovskite devices with K doping. We also measured the corresponding J–V characteristics under different incident light intensities ranging from 0.05 sun to 1 sun (Figure S10), a more monotonically increase in Voc with the light intensities of the KCsFAMA device also demonstrated the low recombination rate compared to the FAMA or CsFAMA devices.  $  ^{39}  $   Therefore, the accelerated charge transport and reduced recombination devoted to eliminate hysteresis in K incorporated mixed perovskite devices.

# Conclusion

In summary, we have demonstrated a novel quadruple-cation perovskite absorber (KCsFAMA) with the incorporation of K, Cs, FA and MA as the A-position cations to attain a high efficiency and elimination of hysteresis. We found this perovskite composition enables highly crystallized absorber films with the grain size over  $ 1\mu \mathrm{m} $ , doubled compared to the K-free counterparts. This also contributed to the enhanced light absorption and longer carrier lifetime. A hysteresis-free highly efficient PSC was obtained based on a planar structure device with a PCE of  $ 20.56\% $  (FS) and  $ 20.17\% $  (RS). This hysteresis elimination is irrespective to the ETLs (both sol-gel  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $ ) and no surface modification or doping are required. Characterizations suggest that the eliminated hysteresis can be ascribed to the enhanced conductivity which accelerated the charge transport and distribution, as well as the reduced carrier recombination and decreased trap density in the perovskite and its interfaces. The KCsFAMA also exhibited outstanding stability which has no decrease in PCE over 1000 hours stored under ambient air ( $ 10 \pm 5 $  RH%) without encapsulation. Furthermore, we presented a high PCE of  $ 15.76\% $  (FS) and  $ 15.56\% $  (RS) were achieved for the large size  $ 6 \times 6~\mathrm{cm}^2 $  modules with an active area of  $ 20~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ . These results suggest that with high quality KCsFAMA absorber it is possible to achieve highly efficient, hysteresis free and stable
