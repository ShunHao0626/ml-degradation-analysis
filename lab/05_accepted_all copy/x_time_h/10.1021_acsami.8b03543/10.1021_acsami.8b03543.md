# Improved Stability of Interfacial Energy-Level Alignment in Inverted Planar Perovskite Solar Cells

ABSTRACT: Even though poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) has been commonly used as a hole extraction layer (HEL) for p-i-n perovskite solar cells (PSCs), the cells' photovoltaic performance deteriorates because of the low and unstable work functions (WFs) of PEDOT:PSS versus those of a perovskite layer. To overcome this drawback, we synthesized a copolymer (P(SS-co-TFPMA)) ionomer consisting of PSS and tetrafluoropropylmethacrylate (TFPMA) as an alternative to conventional PEDOT:PSS. The PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) copolymer solution and its film exhibited excellent homogeneity and high phase stability compared with a physical mixture of TFPMA with PEDOT:PSS solution. During spin coating, a self-organized conducting PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) HEL evolved and the topmost PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) film showed a hydrophobic surface with a higher WF compared to that of the pristine PEDOT:PSS film because of its chemically bonded electron-withdrawing fluorinated functional groups. Interestingly, the WF of the

conventional PEDOT:PSS film dramatically deteriorated after being coated with a perovskite layer, whereas the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) film represented a relatively small influence. Because of the superior energy-level alignment between the HEL and a perovskite layer even after the contact, the open-circuit voltage, short-circuit current, and fill factor of the inverted planar p-i-n PSCs (IP-PSCs) with PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) were improved from 0.92 to 0.98 V, 18.96 to 19.66 mA/cm², and 78.96 to 82.43%, respectively, resulting in a 15% improvement in the power conversion efficiency vs that of IP-PSCs with conventional PEDOT:PSS. Moreover, the IP-PSCs with PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) layer showed not only improved photovoltaic performance but also enhanced device stability due to hydrophobic surface of PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) film.

KEYWORDS: inverted planar perovskite solar cells, hole extraction layer, PEDOT:PSS, tetrafluoropropylmethacrylate, PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA)

# INTRODUCTION

Organometal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted substantial research interest in the field of photovoltaic (PV) devices because of their high power conversion efficiency (PCE), low cost, and easy processing.  $  ^{1-3}  $   Because of their superior optical and electronic properties, the PCE values of PSCs have rapidly increased from 3.8 to a certified 22.7%.  $  ^{4}  $   Most PSCs with PCE values greater than 20% have relied on a high-temperature-sintered mesoporous (>450 °C) TiO  $  _{2}  $   scaffold as the electron extraction layer.  $  ^{5-8}  $   However, the corresponding high-temperature sintering process is unsuitable for flexible modules and large-area fabrication via roll-to-roll processes. In this regard, many researchers have fabricated planar PSCs using low-temperature processable charge extraction layers (CELs) in conventional n-i-p PSCs  $  ^{9-13}  $   or inverted p-i-n PSCs.  $  ^{14-17}  $   For inverted planar p-i-n PSCs (IP-PSCs), poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly-

(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) has commonly been used as a low-temperature solution-processable hole extraction layer (HEL). Because of its high conductivity, optical transparency, and simple low-temperature solution processability, PEDOT:PSS has been used in various optoelectronic devices before being incorporated into IP-PSCs.  $  ^{18-23}  $   Nevertheless, IP-PSCs with PEDOT:PSS exhibit lower PCE values than those of conventional n-i-p PSCs, mainly because of the low open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of approximately 0.9 V  $  ^{24-27}  $   of the IP-PSCs with PEDOT:PSS; this voltage mainly originates from the mismatch between the work function (WF) of PEDOT:PSS (  $  \sim  $  4.8–5.1 eV) and the valence-band maximum (VBM) of the perovskite materials (e.g., VBM  $  _{MAPbI_{3}}  $   = 5.43 eV), poor

The incorporation of an electron-withdrawing agent such as a perfluorinated ionomer (PFI) into PEDOT:PSS has been investigated as an alternative approach to tuning the WF of the PEDOT:PSS layer to better match the VBM of perovskite materials.  $  ^{35,36}  $  This simple mixing method could improve the PSC performance via reducing the hole extraction energy barrier between the perovskite and the PEDOT:PSS layer; however, a physically mixed PEDOT:PSS-based HEL solution and its derived film exhibit poor long-term stability because of the poor miscibility of the hydrophilic PEDOT:PSS component with the hydrophobic PFI.

Here, we synthesized a chemically linked copolymer ionomer named P(SS-co-TFPMA), which is composed of PSS and tetrafluoropropylmethacrylate (TFPMA). We utilized the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) mixture ionomers as an HEL, as demonstrated in IP-PSCs (Figure 1a). First, the solution and film stability of PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) were greatly improved in terms of phase separation compared to that of the conventional physically mixed solution and a film with the same composition. In addition, the copolymerization using a

fluorinated monomer with various molar ratios and its self-organized film formation enables systematic control of the WF of the HEL. Furthermore, our copolymerization enables control of the surface composition and WF by employing a much smaller amount of fluorinated compounds than conventional additive systems, which in turn leads to the superior energy-level alignment with the perovskite film. Moreover, the WF of the PEDOT:PSS film dramatically deteriorated after coating of the perovskite layer, whereas the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) film represented a relatively small influence. Because of the energy-level alignment between the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) and the perovskite layer, the   $  V_{OC}  $   of the IP-PSC device with PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) increased from 0.92 to 0.98 V, which resulted in an improvement in the PCE from 13.82 to 15.88%. In addition, the IP-PSCs PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) layer exhibited improved device stability because of a hydrophobic surface of PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) film.

# EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

Preparation of the P(SS-co-TFPMA) Copolymers and the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) Solution. For the synthesis of conductive PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA), 4-styrenesulfonic acid sodium salt hydrate (NaSS), 2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropylmethacrylate (TFPMA, 371998, 99%), and 3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene (EDOT, 483028, 97%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used as received without further purification. The reaction initiators, sodium persulfate (NaPS) and iron(III) sulfate pentahydrate   $  \left(\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\left(\mathrm{SO}_{4}\right)_{3}\cdot\mathrm{SH}_{2}\mathrm{O}\right)  $  , were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich and used without further purification. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99%) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used as received. Double-distilled deionized water was used throughout this study.

In the first step, a series of P(SS-co-TFPMA) copolymers containing different mole ratios of TFPMA per polymer chain were prepared by radical copolymerization in the presence of the water-soluble initiator NaPS. The TFPMA contents in the P(SS-co-TFPMA) were varied by varying the ratio of the TFPMA in the PSS copolymers from 0 to 30. The polymerization was performed at 80 °C for 24 h in a four-necked double-jacketed glass reactor (150 mL) equipped with a mechanical stirrer. In the reactor, NaSS was dissolved in 100 mL of UPW and was stirred at 250 rpm. Within 15 min of the preparation of the NaPS solution, the initiator solution was prepared by dissolving 0.065 g of KPS in 2 mL of high-purity water. The reaction was continued with stirring for 24 h. After the reaction period, the solution-phase product of the copolymers was ion-exchanged using 31 g of positive ion exchangers for 2 h at room temperature to remove the unreacted initiators, which were then filtered through a 30 μm mesh. In the second step, the PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) are synthesized at a 1:6 PEDOT-to-PSS mass ratio. The reactor containing PSS or P(SS-co-TFPMA) polymer solutions was purged with argon (99.999%) and mixed at 300 rpm. The initiators, 0.772 g of NaPS dissolved in 2 mL of UPW and 0.0053 g of Fe₂(SO₄)₃·5H₂O dissolved in 3 mL of UPW, were added to the reactors. After EDOT was added as the monomer, the solution gradually changed color to dark blue. After approximately 24 h of polymerization, PEDOT:PSS, PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) was purified by ion exchange with 31 g of positive ion exchangers and 25 g of negative ion exchangers for 2 h at room temperature, followed by filtering through a 30 μm mesh. The products were stirred for 15 min by ultrasonication with a tip-sonicator to obtain a well-dispersed solution.

Characterization of the PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) Films. The chemical structures of the PSS and P(SS-co-TFPMA) copolymers were analyzed by FT-IR (Bruker, Vortex 70) in the wavenumber range 2000–400 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  with a resolution of 2 cm  $  ^{-1}  $ . The FT-IR analysis was performed with powdered P(SS-co-TFPMA) samples mixed with KBr powder and pelletized. The FT-IR absorption spectra were acquired in transmittance mode.

The surface of the films was characterized by atomic force microscopy (AFM) (Park Systems, XE-100). The instrument was operated in tapping mode to obtain the images. The root-mean-square   $  (R_{\mathrm{q}})  $   of the films was calculated using the software provided by the instrument manufacturer. The contact angles of water on the PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) films with different amounts of TFPMA were measured on a KRÜSS DSA 100 drop-shape analyser (KRÜSS, German) using deionized water and the sessile drop method. Pretreated glass slides were selected as substrates. The measurements were performed at five different positions for each sample, and the averaged values are reported. The elemental compositions of the PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) film surfaces were analyzed by X-ray photoemission spectroscopy (XPS) (Thermo U.K., K-alpha) using monochromatic Al Kα X-ray radiation. Raman spectra were recorded with a Horiba Jobin Yvon LabRam Aramis spectrometer. The 633 nm line of a He–Ne laser was used as the excitation source. The Raman-scattered light signal was collected in a backscattering geometry using a 100× microscope objective lens. The Raman excitation beam spot size was approximately 1 μm in diameter. To obtain the WF and the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) region of the PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) films, UV photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurements were conducted using a SPECS PHOIBOS 150 and PHI 5700 hemispherical analyser. For UPS measurements, an ultraviolet discharge lamp was used as an excitation source (He I, 21.22 eV) and a sample bias of -10 V was applied to obtain the secondary electron cut-off (SEC) in normal emission geometry. The spectrometers were calibrated with respect to the Fermi step of clean Au and the half-width of the Fermi step of 0.09 eV, respectively. The base pressure of the analysis chamber was maintained at less than   $  6.0 \times 10^{-10}  $   Torr.

Preparation of the Perovskite Solution. Methylammonium iodide (MAI) was commercially purchased from Dyesol, and lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2, 99.999\%) $ , dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO,  $ 99.5\% $ ), and  $ N,N $ -dimethylformamide (DMF,  $ 99.8\% $ ) were commercially purchased

from Sigma-Aldrich. All chemicals were used without further purification. The 1 M MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  solution, which is composed of MAI powder and PbI  $  _{2}  $ , was stirred in a mixture of DMF and DMSO (9:1 v/v) at 70 °C for 1 h before use.

Fabrication of the IP-PSC Devices. ITO glass substrates were cleaned with detergent, acetone, and 2-propanol in a sonic bath and UV/ozone treatment (Altech LTS) for 30 min. The synthesized PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) solutions were then spin coated onto the ITO glass substrates at 3000 rpm for 30 s. These substrates were then heated at 150 °C for 10 min on a hot plate in ambient air. The thickness of all HELs showed 40 nm. To form the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  layer, the prepared perovskite precursor solution was spin coated onto PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) layers in two steps (1000 rpm for 20 s and 5000 rpm for 40 s). During the second spin-coating process, toluene (1 mL) was dropped onto the rotating substrate 35 s before the end of the spin-coating procedure. After spin coating, all samples were annealed at 100 °C for 10 min on a hot plate. Then, PC  $  _{60}  $  BM and bathocuproine (BCP) layers were deposited sequentially by spin coating onto the perovskite layer. Finally, an Ag electrode with a thickness of 150 nm was deposited onto the BCP layer under a  $  2 \times 10^{-6}  $  Torr vacuum in a thermal evaporator.

IP-PSC Device Measurements. The current–voltage (J-V) characteristics of the IP-PSCs were measured with a Keithley Series 2400 source meter under AM 1.5 white light (1 sun, 100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) illumination. A solar simulator (Oriel Sol 3A, class AAA) with a filtered 450 W xenon lamp was used as the light source, and proper adjustments were made with a Si reference cell (VLSI standards, Oriel P/N 91150V). The aperture (11.430 mm  $  ^{2}  $  ) was placed on the bottom of the cell during the J-V measurements to improve the accuracy of the measurements. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) was obtained using a solar cell QE/IPCE measurement system (Zolix Solar Cell Scan 100). Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was conducted using an impedance analyzer (IVIUM Tech.). A small alternating current (AC) voltage of 20 mV was applied at 0 V under 1 sun illumination (AM 1.5G, 100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $  ). The AC voltage was applied with a frequency ranging from 10 Hz to 1 MHz. The data for Mott–Schottky plots of the perovskite samples were collected at 10 kHz.

Figure 2i shows the contact angle images of water on the PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) films. The average contact angle was obtained through five measurements. Wettability is known to be affected by surface structure, roughness, and surface free energy.  $  ^{45}  $   Films prepared with higher concentrations of TFPMA showed a relatively larger contact angle and greater hydrophobic character compared with pristine PEDOT:PSS. The contact angle increased dramatically from   $  28.82 \pm 7.73  $   to   $  47.08 \pm 8.17^{\circ}  $   when the TFPMA concentration was increased to 30 mol % because of the hydrophobic character of the fluorinated chains.

We used X-ray photoemission spectroscopy (XPS) to study the surface chemical composition of the PEDOT:PSS and the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) films.  $  ^{46}  $   As shown in Figure 2j, the introduction of TFPMA was confirmed from the peak at 688.5 eV (F 1s), which originates from the fluorinated group. The signal intensity of the fluorine in the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) films increased with increasing TFPMA mole ratios in the P(SS-co-TFPMA) chains. S 2p spectra associated with the S atoms of the thiophene in PEDOT (lower peaks) and those of the sulfonate in PSS (higher peaks) (Figure S3) showed that the PEDOT ratio decreased with an increased ratio of TFPMA in PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA).  $  ^{42,47}  $   In addition, a shift of the higher energy S 2p peaks for PSS from 167.6 to 167.8 eV was observed, suggesting that   $  -SO_{3}^{-}H^{+}  $   was formed rather than   $  -SO_{3}^{-}  $  .  $  ^{48-50}  $   Because the F atoms of TFPMA possess much higher electronegativity than C, S, or O, the F screens the ionic bond between the PEDOT  $  ^{+}  $   and the PSS  $  ^{-}  $  .

Raman spectra of the PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) films are shown in Figure S4. No substantial differences were observed between the spectra of the PEDOT:PSS and the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) films in the wavenumber range from 1400 to 1500 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  . Peak shifting of wavenumbers is commonly attributed to changes in the polymeric structure of PEDOT.  $  ^{[51,52]}  $   The benzoid structure has a coil conformation, whereas the quinoid structure has a linear or expanded-coil structure. The strong band assigned to

the symmetric   $  C_{\alpha}=C_{\beta}  $   stretch at 1429 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   remains stable with the increasing ratio of fluorinated monomer. This result suggests that structure of PEDOT chains do not change and maintain in the chemical state. Thus, the fluorinated monomer does not affect the polymeric structure of PEDOT and the coil conformation of the PEDOT chains remains the same.

On the basis of the aforementioned results, we proposed a model, as shown in Figure 3a, to fully explain the scheme of the morphological comparison of PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) films. The suggested scheme for the morphology of PEDOT:PSS film is that it consists of a layered structure, with a free linear PSS-rich top layer and a conductive PEDOT-rich bottom layer. The PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) films have a layered structure mostly similar to the PEDOT:PSS films, but these films may have a free coiled P(SS-co-TFPMA)-rich layer at the top of the film. The TFPMA, as a fluorinated monomer, with hydrophobic properties, appears to influence the PSS copolymer shape and the synthesis of PEDOT chains. Moreover, the PEDOT chains were relatively more aggregated in the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) films because of the coil shape of P(SS-co-TFPMA), unlike the linear PSS. Thus, the surface roughness, which is dependent on the morphological change in the films, is higher from PEDOT:PSS to  $ 30\mathrm{mol}\% $  PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA).

# CONCLUSIONS

In this study, we demonstrated that the synthesis of PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) ionomer mixtures enabled systematic control of their WF values. By synthesizing a P(SS-co-TFPMA) copolymer ionomer, we obtained a homogeneous and stable PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) solution and its derived thin film without phase separation, in contrast to the physical mixture of TFPMA and PEDOT:PSS solution and its film. The WF of the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) films was increased by the electron-withdrawing fluorinated unit delocalized in the surface of the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) film. In addition, the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) film exhibited a more stable WF value even after the deposition of the perovskite layer (from 5.44 to 4.48 eV) compared with the WF value of the PEDOT:PSS film before and after deposition of the perovskite layer (from 5.31 to 4.17 eV). As a result, the WF of the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) film could be matched well with the VBM of the perovskite materials, leading to efficient hole extraction and an increased  $  V_{\mathrm{OC}}  $  of the IP-PSC devices. Interestingly, the devices with PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) exhibited improved device stability because the hydrophobic surface by a coiled P(SS-co-TFPMA) rich layer at the top of the PEDOT:P(SS-co-TFPMA) film prevents the  $  \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}  $  molecules' diffusion to perovskite layer. We believe that this study

introduces a new route to solving the interface problems between PEDOT:PSS and the perovskite layer and to improving the PCE and device stability of IP-PSC devices based on PEDOT:PSS HELs.
