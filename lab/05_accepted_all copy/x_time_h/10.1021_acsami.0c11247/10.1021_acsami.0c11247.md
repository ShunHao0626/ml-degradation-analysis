Keywords: perovskite solar cells, stacked devices, electrode, interface, PEDOT:PSS, hexamethylenediammonium diiodide

Abstract: As an indispensable component of perovskite solar cells (PSCs), the commonly used Au and Ag electrodes still have some problems such as high cost and the instability issue with regard to being corroded by iodide ions. In this paper, we report a stacking perovskite solar cells (S-PSCs), which can avoid the use of precious metal electrodes and reduce the cost of devices and the requirements of equipment compared to conventional PSCs. The S-PSCs is composed of two semi-cells: a photoanode and a counter electrode (CE). For stacked devices, effective contact of the photoanode/CE interface is very important to the performance of the device. We used PEDOT:PSS as the electrode and modified it by hexamethylenediammonium diiodide (HDADI  $  _{2}  $  ) to improve its physical and electrical properties. # Introduction:

As one of the frontiers in photovoltaics, PSCs have attracted great attention from researchers due to their PCE skyrocketing from 3.8% to 25.2% in past ten years.  $  ^{1-7}  $   At present, reducing production cost, simplifying equipment requirements, as well as improving the long-term stability are important issues urgently to investigate.  $  ^{8-11}  $   The choice of electrode materials will affect the performance, cost and even stability of PSCs. Gold or silver is usually used as counter electrode (CE) responsible to collecting photoinduced charges,  $  ^{12-16}  $   however, which are expensive and need to be deposited under high vacuum conditions, largely increasing the production cost, and unfavorable to the popularization of PSCs. In addition, these metal electrodes are a source of instability due to the fact that Au or Ag electrode is prone to be corroded by the iodine ions from the perovskite layer.  $  ^{17-22}  $   Hence, it is urgently required to develop some economical electrode alternatives and advanced cell architectures to advance the commercialization of PSCs.  $  ^{23}  $

# Experimental Sections:

# Preparation of PEDOT:PSS dispersion:

Weigh 20 mg PEDOT:PSS solid particles (Sigma-Aldrich) and add them to 5 ml 2-propanol solution (99.5%, Sigma-Aldrich). A proper amount of ball milling beads were added into the container and ball-milled for 5 h to prepare a uniform and dispersed PEDOT:PSS slurry.

# Hexamethylenediammonium diiodide (HDADI  $  _{2}  $  ) modified PEDOT:PSS

20 mg PEDOT:PSS solid particles and 7.44 mg HDADI $ _2 $  powder (Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp, abbreviated as Xi'an PLT) were added into 5 ml 2-propanol solution. After ball-milled for 5 h, the modified PEDOT:PSS (H-PEDOT:PSS) slurry was obtained.

# Fabrication of S-PSCs:

# Characterizations:

The surface morphology of the PEDOT:PSS film was characterized by a field-emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM, HITACHI SU8220). The chemical structures of PEDOT:PSS was analysed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo ESCALAB 250). The electrical properties of the PEDOT:PSS electrode were tested using a Keithley 2601 source. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) spectrum were measured by monochromatized HeI radiation. Photoluminescence (PL) spectra were measured by fluorescence spectrometer (FLS920). The time-resolved PL decay were measured by the TPL 200. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were measured by an electrochemical workstation (Zennium Zahner, Germany) in dark. The current density-voltage  $ (J - V) $  curves of S-PSCs were measured by a Keithley 2450 source meter.

Figure 1. Flow chart of the preparation process of S-PSCs.

We fabricated S-PSCs using the conductive polymer PEDOT:PSS as electrode. Figure 1 shows the preparation process of the device. The S-PSCs consist of two independent semi-cells (semi-cell A: FTO/PEDOT:PSS; semi-cell B: FTO/SnO₂/perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD/PEDOT:PSS). The electrode slurry was prepared by milling PEDOT:PSS particles in 2-propanol solution for several times to realize uniformity. The counter electrode (semi-cell A) was prepared by spraying PEDOT:PSS on FTO. The photoanode (semi-cell B) was prepared by spin coating SnO₂, perovskite, and spiro-OMeTAD on FTO layer by layer and spraying PEDOT:PSS on the top layer subsequently. The two semi-cells were stacked together to form a S-PSC. Good contact between the photoanode and counter electrode (CE) is the key to S-PSCs. Therefore, we modified PEDOT:PSS by ammonium salt modification. Hexamethylenediammonium diiodide (HDADI₂) was added to the electrode slurry to produce the modified material (denoted as H-PEDOT:PSS).

The XPS spectra consists of two S 2p bands, each band composed of two peaks, S 2p3/2 and S 2p1/2. $ ^{29} $  The ratio of S 2p3/2 peak areas for PEDOT and PSS can be used to estimate their contents on the film surface. $ ^{30} $  Compared with pristine PEDOT:PSS film, the relative S 2p3/2 ratio of the film significantly increased from 0.26 to 0.38 after treatment with HDADI $ _{2} $ . According to the previous literature, $ ^{31} $  pristine PEDOT:PSS is composed of conductive PEDOT cores and insulating PSS barriers, and the PEDOT is separated by PSS. An amine salt can shield the coulombic gravitation between them. $ ^{27} $  Ion exchange occurs after PEDOT:PSS treatment with an HDADI $ _{2} $  solution. The strong interaction between HDADI $ _{2} $  and PEDOT:PSS leads to the phase separation of conductive PEDOT chains and insulating PSS chains, which eventually causes the formation of the PEDOT-rich domains. Therefore, the relative S 2p3/2 ratio is larger for H-PEDOT:PSS. Furthermore, the S 2p binding energy of H-PEDOT:PSS shift to blue by about 0.3 eV. This is mainly due to the alter in the chemical environment of sulfur atoms. $ ^{28} $

LSV test was used to compare the conductivity of PEDOT:PSS electrode before and after modification. The electrode film was sandwiched between two FTO electrodes, as shown in Figure 3a, and then, LSV curves were recorded. The modified film exhibits a higher current than pristine film under the same voltage (Figure 3a), indicating a better conductivity of H-PEDOT:PSS than PEDOT:PSS. This also further validates the results of XPS analysis, the enrichment of PEDOT chain results in the formation of a conductive network, so the conductivity is improved. The electronic band structures of the two electrode materials were studied by UPS. Figure 3b displays the UPS spectrum for the two electrodes. The HOMO energy level of PEDOT:PSS is calculated to be -5.03 eV, and that of H-PEDOT:PSS is -5.20 eV. The detailed calculation process is shown in Figure S2. Figure 3c presents the energy level alignment at the HTL/electrode interface. Since H-PEDOT:PSS and spiro-OMeTAD have similar HOMO energy levels (-5.20 eV versus -5.22 eV), a good energy level alignment is formed between them. Such good band structure matching provides an energy-barrier-free interfacial channel for the extraction of photoinduced holes. Thus, the energy loss during interfacial hole transport is reduced. In contrast, the HOMO energy levels of PEDOT:PSS and spiro-OMeTAD are significantly different (-5.03 eV versus -5.22 eV), forming an obvious energy barrier, which leads to poor hole extraction ability. We fabricated S-PSCs using PEDOT:PSS and H-PEDOT:PSS as electrode materials. The corresponding device structure of the S-PSCs is shown in Figure 4a. We optimized the thickness of the electrode by controlling the spraying amount of H-PEDOT:PSS slurry, and the photovoltaic parameters are shown in Table S1. The efficiency of the S-PSCs is the best when the spraying amount of H-PEDOT:PSS on photoanode and CE is 250   $  \mu  $  l. Under the optimal conditions, we systematically characterize the performance of S-PSCs. In this study, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were used to study the effect of the different electrodes on the S-PSCs charge transport. The Nyquist plots of the relevant S-PSCs are shown in Figure 4b. There are two arcs in the figure, the low-frequency arc (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) is related to the charge recombination process, while the high-frequency arc (  $  R_{ct}  $  ) corresponds to the charge transport process.  $  ^{[26,36-37]}  $   Figure 4b shows that the recombination resistance   $  R_{rec}  $   increased after PEDOT:PSS was modified by the HDADI  $  _{2}  $  . A higher   $  R_{rec}  $   means the charge recombination is inhibited in the S-PSCs. The transmission resistance   $  R_{ct}  $   of H-PEDOT:PSS-based device is smaller, this suggests that the H-PEDOT:PSS electrode is more conducive to charge transfer.

# Conclusion:

In conclusion, we used PEDOT:PSS instead of a noble metal as the electrode to prepare a new type of S-PSCs. To improve the performance of S-PSCs, PEDOT:PSS electrode was modified by ammonium salt modification. The physical and electrical properties of the electrode were optimized by HDADI $ _2 $  modification. The HDADI $ _2 $ -modified PEDOT:PSS improves interface contact between the photoanode and counter electrode (CE), and the energy level is better matched, so the S-PSCs performance is significantly improved. Under an active area of 1 cm $ ^2 $ , the PCE of S-PSCs can reach 15.21%. In addition, the S-PSCs have good humidity and thermal stability and structural stability. The PCE of the device can still be maintained above 70% of the initial value after ageing for 400 h at 85 °C (humidity: 40-70%). Moreover, the photoanode and CE of the S-PSCs were repeatedly disassembled and assembled for 500 times, and the efficiency of the cell was almost unchanged.
