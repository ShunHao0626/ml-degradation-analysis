# Custom-tailored hole transport layer using oxalic acid for high-quality tin-lead perovskites and efficient all-perovskite tandems

Jingwei Zhu  $  ^{1\dagger}  $  , Yuliang Xu  $  ^{1\dagger}  $  , Yi Luo  $  ^{1}  $  , Jincheng Luo  $  ^{1}  $  , Rui He  $  ^{1}  $  , Changlei Wang  $  ^{2}  $  , Yang Wang  $  ^{3}  $  , Kun Wei  $  ^{4}  $  , Zongjin Yi  $  ^{1}  $  , Zhiyu Gao  $  ^{1}  $  , Juncheng Wang  $  ^{1}  $  , Jiayu You  $  ^{1}  $  , Zhihao Zhang  $  ^{1}  $  , Huagui Lai  $  ^{5}  $  , Shengqiang Ren  $  ^{1}  $  , Xirui Liu  $  ^{6}  $  , Chuanxiao Xiao  $  ^{6,7}  $  , Cong Chen  $  ^{1}  $  , Jinbao Zhang  $  ^{4}  $  , Fan Fu  $  ^{5}  $  , Dewei Zhao  $  ^{1*}  $

All-perovskite tandem solar cells (TSCs) have exhibited higher efficiencies than single-junction perovskite solar cells (PSCs) but still suffer from the unsatisfactory performance of low-bandgap (LBG) tin-lead (Sn-Pb) subcells. The inherent properties of PEDOT:PSS are crucial to high-performance Sn-Pb perovskite films and devices; however, the underlying mechanism has not been fully explored and revealed. Here, we report a facile oxalic acid treatment of PEDOT:PSS (OA-PEDOT:PSS) to precisely regulate its work function and surface morphology. OA-PEDOT:PSS shows a larger work function and an ordered reorientation and fiber-shaped film morphology with efficient hole transport pathways, leading to the formation of more ideal hole-selective contact with Sn-Pb perovskite for suppressing interfacial nonradiative recombination losses. Moreover, OA-PEDOT:PSS induces (100) preferred orientation growth of perovskite for higher-quality Sn-Pb films. Last, the OA-PEDOT:PSS-tailored LBG PSC yields an impressive efficiency of up to 22.56% (certified 21.88%), enabling 27.81% efficient all-perovskite TSC with enhanced operational stability.

# INTRODUCTION

Organic metal halide perovskites have drawn tremendous attention owing to their excellent optoelectronic properties and low-cost solution processability. The certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of single-junction perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been boosted to 26.1% (1–5), approaching the Shockley-Queisser (S-Q) radiative efficiency. Monolithic all-perovskite tandem solar cells (TSCs), consisting of a wide-bandgap (WBG;   $  E_{g} \sim 1.7  $   to 1.9 eV) top subcell and a low-bandgap (LBG;   $  E_{g} \sim 1.2  $   to 1.3 eV) bottom subcell, provide the potential to break the S-Q limit of single-junction PSCs (6–10).

Benefiting from the unremitting efforts, the certified efficiencies of all-perovskite TSCs have surpassed the record efficiency of single-junction PSCs (11–13). Recently, researchers have made huge progress in WBG PSCs (12–17). Jiang et al. (14) fabricated high-quality WBG perovskite films via gentle gas-quench method for 20.3% efficient WBG PSCs with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.33 eV, corresponding to a   $  V_{OC}  $   deficit of 0.42 V with respect to 1.75 eV bandgap. After integration with 1.25-eV LBG subcell, the resulting TSC achieved a PCE of 27.1% with

a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 2.2 V. However, as another essential part, the development of LBG PSCs still lags behind that of WBG counterparts.

The efficiency of LBG mixed tin-lead (Sn-Pb) PSCs has exceeded 23%; however, the heterogeneous nucleation and accelerated crystallization, as well as   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation of Sn-Pb perovskite film severely limit the efficiency and stability improvement of LBG PSCs and all-perovskite tandems (18). So far, the dominant strategies for fabricating high-performance Sn-Pb PSCs mainly focus on the following aspects: (i) optimizing the preparation strategy and compositions toward high-quality Sn-Pb perovskite absorbers (19–23), (ii) balancing the crystallization and nucleation rates between Sn- and Pb-based perovskite components by retarding the crystallization of   $  Sn^{2+}  $  -containing species (24, 25), (iii) suppressing the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   to   $  Sn^{4+}  $   and healing the specific defects of Sn-Pb perovskite films by targeted selection of additives and interfacial modifiers (11, 26–31), and (iv) developing previously unidentified charge-selective materials (32–34).

Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrenesulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) is the most widely used hole transport layer (HTL) in Sn-containing PSCs (35). Although the hygroscopic and acidic nature of PEDOT:PSS possibly accelerates the oxidation and degradation of LBG Sn-Pb perovskites, to date, most of the state-of-the-art Sn-Pb mixed PSCs are still using PEDOT:PSS as HTL (11, 18, 21, 36). Apparently, the optical-electrical properties of PEDOT:PSS are essential for efficient Sn-Pb PSCs. Notably, the surface characteristics of HTLs likely regulate the perovskite growth (32, 37). However, the detailed study on such as whether and how PEDOT:PSS affects the growth process of Sn-Pb perovskites and the performance of LBG PSCs and all-perovskite tandems is lacking, and the underlying mechanism remains uncovered.

Recently, some studies have demonstrated that neutralizing the acidity of PEDOT:PSS can effectively improve the performance of PEDOT:PSS and enhance the stability of perovskite films and devices (38–40). Notably, PEDOT:PSS is composed of PEDOT and PSS, and

Previous studies have shown that introducing acids into PEDOT:PSS can effectively reduce insulating PSS chains and enhance the conductivity of PEDOT:PSS (43, 44). The transfer of proton from acids to  $ \mathrm{PSS}^{-} $ of PEDOT:PSS would induce the formation of neutral PSSH, resulting in phase separation between insulating PSS and conducting PEDOT (43, 44). Such a phase separation results in conformational changes of PEDOT chains and reduction of energy barrier for carriers for enhanced conductivity of PEDOT:PSS. Here, we report a facile strategy of modifying PEDOT:PSS with a weak oxalic acid (OA) with low boiling point as HTL for high-quality Sn-Pb perovskite absorbers as well as high-performance LBG PSCs and all-perovskite tandems. The OA treatment notably reduces interfacial nonradiative recombination losses by tuning the work function (WF) of PEDOT:PSS and enhancing the phase separation between PEDOT and PSS segments for an ordered reorientation, fiber-shaped film morphology with efficient hole transport pathways. On top of that, Sn-Pb perovskite deposited on the OA-PEDOT:PSS substrate exhibits (100) preferred orientation growth owing to the change of the surface properties of PEDOT:PSS, leading to an improved quality of perovskite film. As a result, OA-PEDOT:PSS-tailored LBG PSC yields an efficiency of up to 22.56% (certified 21.88%), enabling 27.81% efficient all-perovskite TSC with enhanced operational stability by integration with 1.77-eV WBG subcell.

# Improved surface morphology and optoelectronic properties of HTLs

The influence of OA modification on the surface morphology of the PEDOT:PSS film is first investigated. As shown in Fig. 1A, the PEDOT:PSS film shows uniformly distributed grain-like morphology owing to the well-mixed PEDOT and PSS segments with imperceptible phase separation. However, the OA-PEDOT:PSS film exhibits ordered reorientation and fiber-shaped surface morphology (Fig. 1B). The difference of above surface morphology is more obvious in the atomic force microscopy (AFM) images (Fig. 1, C and D), in which the bright and dark regions correspond to the hard domains of PEDOT ionomers and soft areas of PSS chains, respectively. To further demonstrate that the ordered reorientation and fiber-shaped region correspond to PEDOT, conductive AFM (C-AFM) of different PEDOT:PSS films was performed and shown in fig. S2. Apparently, the control PEDOT:PSS sample shows the lower current value. After introducing OA into PEDOT:PSS, the OA-PEDOT:PSS film displays the obviously enhanced current value and shows more bright regions with well-ordered fiber-like

morphology. Cross-checking the ordered reorientation and fiber-shaped region corresponds to PEDOT in Fig. 1D.

Notably, the presence of excessive insulating PSS chains would decrease the conductivity of PEDOT:PSS (41, 42). The random distribution of PEDOT and PSS in PEDOT:PSS is not conducive to efficient hole transport; meanwhile, the ordered reorientation and fiber-shaped surface morphology of OA-PEDOT:PSS provide accelerated and continuous hole transport pathways (45). As shown in fig. S3, the higher conductivity of OA-PEDOT:PSS compared to that of PEDOT:PSS further demonstrates that the fiber-shaped surface morphology would accelerate hole transport. Moreover, we further quantify the conductivity of PEDOT:PSS by four-point probes. The OA-PEDOT:PSS film yields a higher conductivity of  $ 6677.22\mathrm{S}\mathrm{m}^{-1} $  than that  $ (6195.78\mathrm{S}\mathrm{m}^{-1}) $  for the control one (fig. S4). This is mainly ascribed to that the reduced insulating PSS chains in the OA-PEDOT:PSS film via the bondings between  $ \mathrm{H^{+}} $  in OA and  $ \mathrm{PSS^{-}} $  in PEDOT:PSS (43, 44).

The schematic of the self-assembly process of the OA-PEDOT:PSS film is depicted in Fig. 1E. After introducing OA into PEDOT:PSS, OA would process ionization reaction to produce protons (  $  H^{+}  $  ), hydrogen oxalate ions (  $  HC_{2}O_{4}^{-}  $  ), and oxalate ions (  $  C_{2}O_{4}^{2-}  $  ), as follows:   $  H_{2}C_{2}O_{4} \rightarrow HC_{2}O_{4}^{-} + H^{+}  $  ;   $  HC_{2}O_{4}^{-} \rightarrow C_{2}O_{4}^{2-} + H^{+}  $  . Benefiting from the bondings between   $  H^{+}  $  with   $  PSS^{-}  $  and between   $  C_{2}O_{4}^{2-}  $   with PEDOT  $  ^{+}  $  , the curled PSS segments would be notably stretched, the columbic interaction between the PEDOT and PSS segments would obviously decrease, and the phase separation would be notably enhanced for an ordered reorientation. After annealing at 160°C for 20 min, we obtained the OA-PEDOT:PSS film with fiber-shaped surface morphology; meanwhile, OA completely escaped from the film owing to its low sublimation temperature.

To further highlight the effect of OA for changing the morphology of PEDOT:PSS, we also added glycolic acid (GA) and ethylene glycol (EG) into PEDOT:PSS, denoted as GA-PEDOT:PSS and EG-PEDOT:PSS, respectively. However, GA-PEDOT:PSS and EG-PEDOT:PSS films show almost the same surface morphology with control PEDOT:PSS film; meanwhile, the OA-PEDOT:PSS sample displays uniformly distributed grain-like morphology, demonstrating obvious phase separation between insulating PSS and conducting PEDOT in the OA-PEDOT:PSS sample (fig. S5). This is mainly due to the fact that OA has a higher ionization constant of   $  5.9 \times 10^{-2}  $   compared to those of   $  1.5 \times 10^{-4}  $   for GA and   $  2.5 \times 10^{-16}  $   for EG (46–48). OA can generate more protons than GA and EG under the same conditions, as follows:   $  H_{2}C_{2}O_{4} \rightarrow HC_{2}O_{4}^{-} + H^{+}  $  ;   $  HC_{2}O_{4}^{-} \rightarrow C_{2}O_{4}^{2-} + H^{+}  $  . Owing to the stronger and more bondings between   $  H^{+}  $  and   $  PSS^{-}  $  as well as between   $  C_{2}O_{4}^{2-}  $   and PEDOT  $  ^{+}  $  in OA-PEDOT:PSS, the target PEDOT:PSS film displays an ordered reorientation and fiber-shaped surface morphology.

To further understand the mechanism, we simulated distribution of PSS chains, binary acid, and hydrogen ions around PEDOT molecules after introducing OA, as shown in Fig. 1F and note S1. The enhanced distance between the ends of PSS chains from 1.73 to  $ 1.80\mathrm{nm} $  demonstrates the stretch of PSS segments in OA-PEDOT:PSS (Fig. 1G), cross-checking the proposed mechanism of self-assembly process of the OA-PEDOT:PSS film with ordered reorientation and fiber-shaped surface morphology.

OA also regulates the surface potential (SP) and WF of PEDOT:PSS. Kelvin probe force microscope (KPFM) was used to measure the SP of different samples. Compared to  $ 108\mathrm{mV} $  for PEDOT:PSS film (Fig. 1H), the OA-PEDOT:PSS film shows a higher  $ \mathrm{SP}_{\mathrm{mean}} $  of  $ 316\mathrm{mV} $  (Fig. 1I), indicating an enhanced WF of OA-PEDOT:PSS. # Enhanced quality of LBG Sn-Pb perovskite films

To explore the effect of PEDOT:PSS on the growth and film quality of perovskites, we fabricated Sn-Pb perovskite films on PEDOT:PSS and

OA-PEDOT:PSS. Henceforth, LBG Sn-Pb perovskites on PEDOT:PSS and OA-PEDOT:PSS are denoted as PEDOT:PSS/LBG and OA-PEDOT:PSS/LBG. The PEDOT:PSS/LBG and OA-PEDOT:PSS/LBG samples exhibit similar surface roughness (fig. S7). However, the OA-PEDOT:PSS/LBG film shows more uniform grains and a cleaner surface (Fig. 2, A and B). As shown in Fig. 2 (C and D), the OA-PEDOT:PSS/LBG film also exhibits more uniform SP distribution.

We further characterized the morphology and mapped the photoluminescence (PL) of buried surfaces of perovskite films to evaluate the effect of HTL substrate on the surface morphology and crystallinity of the perovskite films. The PEDOT:PSS/LBG sample shows disordered and messy surface morphology with uneven grains (Fig. 2E and fig. S8), as well as obvious inhomogeneous PL mapping (Fig. 2G). However, for the OA-PEDOT:PSS/LBG sample, the surface morphology and grain uniformity have been notably improved (Fig. 2F), and the intensity and uniformity of the PL mapping have also been enhanced (Fig. 2H) because of the suppressed interfacial nonradiative recombination losses.

# Photovoltaic performance of all-perovskite TSCs

To further demonstrate the potential of the OA-PEDOT:PSS as HTL in enhancing the photovoltaic performance of perovskite device, we fabricated all-perovskite TSC by combining OA-PEDOT:PSS/LBG bottom subcell with 1.77-eV   $  FA_{0.8}C_{s0.2}PbI_{1.8}Br_{1.2}  $  -based WBG top subcell, which has an architecture of glass/ITO/MPA2FPh-BT-BA (denoted as 2F)/WBG perovskite/  $  C_{60}  $  /ALD-  $  SnO_{2}  $  /indium zinc oxide (IZO)/OA-PEDOT:PSS/LBG perovskite/  $  C_{60}  $  /ALD-  $  SnO_{2}  $  /Cu (Fig. 4A). Figure 4B shows the corresponding cross-sectional scanning electron microscopy (SEM) image of device. The 1.77-eV WBG PSC yields a PCE of 18.83 (18.70)% with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.32 (1.31) V, a   $  J_{SC}  $   of 17.95 (17.87) mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and an FF of 79.49 (79.89)% of under reverse (forward) scan voltage (fig. S15), and the corresponding EQE-integrated   $  J_{SC}  $   of 17.36 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   (fig. S16).

As shown in Fig. 4C, the OA-PEDOT:PSS-tailored TSC has a  $  V_{\mathrm{OC}}  $  of 2.14 (2.14) V, a  $  J_{\mathrm{SC}}  $  of 15.69 (15.68) mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and an FF of 82.83 (82.58)\%, yielding a PCE of 27.81 (27.71)\%, while the PEDOT:PSS-based tandem device presents a relatively lower PCE

of 25.85 (25.61)% with a  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $  of 2.08 (2.06) V, a  $  J_{\text{SC}}  $  of 15.23 (15.29) mA cm $  ^{-2}  $ , and an FF of 81.61 (81.32)%. The corresponding integrated  $  J_{\text{SCs}}  $  of WBG and LBG subcells in OA-PEDOT:PSS-tailored TSC are 15.48 and 15.42 mA cm $  ^{-2}  $ , respectively (Fig. 4D). As shown in Fig. 4 (E and F), the control TSCs have an average PCE only of 24.62 ± 0.86% with an average  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $  of 2.07 ± 0.02 V, while our optimized TSCs yield an average PCE of 27.07 ± 0.68% with an average  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $  of 2.13 ± 0.02 V. The PCE (27.81%) and  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $  (2.14 V) are also one of the highest-reported PCEs and  $  V_{\text{OCS}}  $  of all-perovskite tandems.

We further tested maximum power point (MPP) tracking of encapsulated all-perovskite tandems in ambient air (Fig. 4G). The OA-PEDOT:PSS-tailored tandem device maintained 80% of its initial efficiency after 292 hours of continuous operation under 1-sun illumination at the MPP, while the control tandem device has an obviously shorter lifetime of 160 hours, which we attribute to the suppressed nonradiative interfacial losses and improved perovskite film quality in OA-PEDOT:PSS-tailored bottom subcell.

# DISCUSSION

We have demonstrated a facile OA treatment of PEDOT:PSS for efficient Sn-Pb PSCs and all-perovskite tandems. Benefiting from WF adjustment and morphology improvement by enhancing the phase separation between PEDOT and PSS segments for fiber-shaped film with efficient hole transport pathways, the more ideal hole-selective contact formed with Sn-Pb perovskite enables efficient hole extraction and suppresses interfacial nonradiative recombination losses. Moreover, the OA-PEDOT:PSS substrate induces (100) preferred orientation growth of perovskite with higher film quality. As a result, OA-PEDOT:PSS-tailored LBG PSCs achieve a champion efficiency of up to 22.56% (certified 21.88%), enabling 27.81% efficient all-perovskite TSCs with enhanced operational stability by integrating with 1.77-eV WBG subcells. This work provides a facile strategy to suppress interfacial nonradiative recombination losses and regulate perovskite growth for high-performance Sn-Pb PSCs and all-perovskite TSCs.

AFM and KPFM images of different films were obtained by Bruker Nano Inc. DI Multi Mode 8. The micromorphology of buried surface was characterized by SEM (SU-70, Japan Hitachi Nake high-tech enterprise). PL intensity (990 nm) mapping was obtained by Vis-NIR-XU (Nanophoton Corporation) with an excitation at 532 nm. The exfoliation process of the perovskite films is the same as that of our previous work (56). The exfoliation technique of perovskite buried interfaces by blade-coating a bilayer film composed of self-polymerized epoxy resin on the surface of perovskite. Specifically, poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA; Sigma-Aldrich) precursor was prepared by dissolving 0.4 g of PMMA in 1 ml of CB and stirred for 6 hours and then blade-coated on the surface of perovskite film. Epoxy precursor was prepared by mixing diglycidyl ether bisphenol A type (Sigma-Aldrich), n-octylamine (Sigma-Aldrich), and m-xylylenediamine (Sigma-Aldrich), and then the epoxy layer was blade-coated on the prepared perovskite film and annealed at 70°C for 10 min. After 12 hours, the epoxy was completely solidified at room temperature. Last, perovskite film was exfoliated from glass/ITO substrate by a glass nipping plier. A nanosensor PointProbe Plus Electrostatic Force Microscopy (PPP-EFM) tip coated with Pt/Ir metal was used for C-AFM measurements. Topography and current sensing images were measured simultaneously. The local current at specific points was measured as a function of applied electrical bias (+1 V). All measurements took place inside an argon-filled glovebox with water and oxygen levels maintained below 0.01 ppm.
