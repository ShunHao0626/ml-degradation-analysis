# ABSTRACT  $  ^{1}  $

We investigated strontium oxide (SrO) as an interlayer material to enhance the UV stability of a  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite solar cell. Moisture and over  $ 400~\mathrm{nm} $  wavelength of light were excluded to investigate the effect of UV light only. Two different interlayer fabrication processes were examined to optimize the performance of this solar cell. Devices fabricated by dipping for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $  in SrO solution exhibited photoconversion efficiencies of  $ 15.5\% $ , whereas those fabricated with 60-min dipping showed photoconversion efficiencies of  $ 15\% $  and exhibited local Sr agglomeration. Devices with SrO displayed lower initial efficiencies than those without any SrO layer  $ (17.6\%) $ , However, a device without SrO retained only  $ 34.4\% $  of its initial efficiency after  $ 100\mathrm{h} $  of UV exposure. In contrast, SrO-incorporated devices retained almost  $ 60.0\% $  of their initial efficiency. # 1. Introduction

In recent time, organic-inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskite materials based on the  $ \mathrm{ABX}_3 $  crystal structure have received tremendous attention because of a significant growth in their efficiency (over  $ 23\% $  ) [1]. The A site is typically occupied by  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3 $ ,  $ \mathrm{HC(NH_2)_2} $ , CS, and Rb, while B and X sites are generally occupied by Pb, Sn, and I, and by Br and Cl, respectively [2]. The perovskite materials typically exhibit high absorption coefficients (approximately  $ 10^{5}\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ ) [3-7], long diffusion lengths (approximately  $ 1\mu \mathrm{m} $ ) [8, 9], and direct band gaps. They are usually fabricated by various methods such as spin coating and evaporation [8, 10-12]. The development of solid-state hole transfer materials and the optimization of fabrication processes [3, 10, 13, 14] has led to a significant increase in the power conversion efficiency (from  $ 3.81\% $  to  $ 23.3\% $ ) [1, 15]. Perovskite/Si and perovskite/perovskite tandem structures have also been actively investigated [16-19].

However, the performance of perovskite solar cells easily degrade when exposed to moisture [20], light [21, 22], heat [23], and electric field [24]. The photocatalytic effect of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  electron transfer layer (ETL) incorporated with UV-light has been reported to be the origin of perovskite degradation caused by light. It is suspected that the perovskite/ $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  interface is location where the degradation commences. The energy band gap of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  is  $ 3.2\mathrm{eV} $  and this is well matched with the energy of the around  $ 400\mathrm{nm} $  wavelength UV-light. The  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovksite light absorber are made up with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3^+ $ ,  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $ , and  $ \mathrm{I}^- $  ions. When  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  is exposed to UV-light, it can extract electrons from the  $ \mathrm{I}^- $  ions in the perovskite and initiate materials deconstruction [21]. The addition of an interlayer ( $ \mathrm{Al_2O_3} $ ,  $ \mathrm{MgO} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Sb_2S_3} $ , and CsBr) at the perovskite/ $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  interface is considered to enhance the power conversion efficiency and UV stability. Therefore, controlling the interface and morphological features like pore size and thickness of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer has strong effect on not only device performance but also device stability. [20, 21, 25-28]. Replacing  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  to the another material like SnO,  $ \mathrm{Al_2O_3} $ ,  $ \mathrm{BaSnO_3} $  and  $ \mathrm{C_{60}} $  based is also reported as a strategy to enhance UV stability [29-31]. In this work, we examined a strontium oxide (SrO) interlayer to enhance the UV stability. SrO was reported to improve the efficiency when used as an interlayer in DSSCs. Additionally, SrO has recently been used to improve the perovskite efficiency, although the stability of SrO as an interlayer has not been investigated enough. Hence, we examined the effect

# 2. Materials and Methods

# 2.1 Device fabrication

All the details of the fabrication process can be found in the previous studies [10, 22, 32]. In brief, we firstly fabricated differently optimized c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  thickness in the range of 20 – 50 nm for devices fabricated by 1-step and 2-step spin coating respectively. Sequentially, spin coated mesoporous  $  TiO_{2}  $  (m-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) was immersed in a  $  Sr(NO_{3})_{2}  $  (99.995% trace metal basis, Sigma Aldrich) saturated solution for 30 min and 60 min and washed in DI water. This was followed by calcination at 450 °C for 30 min [27, 33]. The  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  perovskite layer was formed by using a 2-step sequential deposition method and a 1-step anti-solvent dripping method [10, 32]. Additionally, Spiro-MeOTAD was used as a hole transfer layer and a 100-nm-thick Au electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation. Furthermore, N,N-dimethylformamide (anhydrous, 99.8%), dimethyl sulfoxide (anhydrous, ≥99.9%), isopropyl alcohol (IPA, anhydrous, 99.5%), Li-(bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonamide (TFSI, 99.95% trace metals basis), chlorobenzene (1 mL, 99.8%), 4-tert-butyl pyridine (28.8 μL, 96%), Li-TSFI solution (17.5 μL, 520 mg Li-TSFI in 1 mL anhydrous acetonitrile, 9.8%), and diethyl ether (anhydrous, ≥99.7%) were purchased from Sigma Aldrich. Moreover,  $  PbI_{2}  $  (99.9985% metal basis, Alfa Aesar), methylammonium iodide (MAI, Dyesol), 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenyl-amine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-MeOTAD, HANALINTECH) were also used.

Traces of the solvent were removed by performing the glove box regeneration process twice. In order to verify the effect of the SrO interlayer on the UV stability of perovskite solar cells, devices with and without the SrO layer were exposed to UV-light. We conducted the UV exposure experiment in an inert gas atmosphere by using 365-nm UV light. Additionally, UV stability experiments were performed for  $ 100\mathrm{h} $ . The L-IV measurement results during the 100-h UV exposure are shown in Fig. 4 and Table 2. Perovskite solar cells without the SrO interlayer degraded significantly after 100-h UV light exposure and retained only  $ 34.4\% $  of their initial performance. However, devices incorporating the SrO interlayer retained approximately  $ 60\% $  of their initial power conversion efficiency. With respect to the solar cell parameters, the fill factor and short-circuit current density  $ (\mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{sc}}) $  exhibited the most significant degradation, while  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  displayed the least degradation. The increased  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  and stability are attributed to the enhanced electron transfer and perovskite/m- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  interface passivation. However, SrO-incorporated devices still exhibit performance degradation, possibly due to the instability of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite material itself. This can be improved by cation and halide mixing [37]. Furthermore, it is necessary to stabilize UV-exposed devices for measurement under 1 sun. The UV-exposed devices exhibit a continuous recovery when they were exposed to 1 sun because of the neutralization of the

The effect of the SrO interlayer on perovskite degradation was also examined by using spatially resolved  $ \mu $ -PL. For this analysis, a new set of devices was prepared without Au electrodes. Devices with structures comprising FTO/c- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2/\mathrm{m}-\mathrm{TiO}_2/ $ perovskite/Spiro-MeOTAD and FTO/c- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2/\mathrm{m}-\mathrm{TiO}_2/\mathrm{SrO}/ $ perovskite/Spiro-MeOTAD films were used. Each device was characterized in the pristine state and after  $ 84\mathrm{h} $  of UV exposure in an inert gas atmosphere at room temperature. Fig. 5 shows that the films without the SrO interlayer exhibit severe degradation in the  $ \mu $ -PL intensity. This indicates reduced band-to-band recombination caused by defect generation in the devices [34, 35]. The peak position is also shifted for films without the SrO interlayer. In contrast to the IV measurement result, the SrO-incorporated film exhibited almost no change in the PL peak intensity and peak position. This result is attributed to the degradation site. In the case of UV degradation, degradation is initiated at the  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2/ $ perovskite interface. Therefore, despite the degradation in the performance of the SrO-incorporated device, the PL intensity is maintained because it mainly originated from the bulk [21, 22, 25, 34]. A schematic of the effect of the SrO interlayer on degradation is shown in Fig. 6. The SrO interlayer passivates the degradation sites at the perovskite/m- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  interface and reduces UV degradation [21, 25].

In this study, a SrO interlayer coating method was investigated to optimize the performance of a perovskite solar cell. The results indicate that the results associated with the 30-min immersion process exceed those obtained with  $ 60\mathrm{min} $  of immersion. The SrO-incorporated perovskite solar cells exhibited a lower initial performance and higher UV stability than those without any SrO interlayer. The effect of the SrO interlayer on UV stability was examined in an inert atmosphere. Without the SrO coating, the devices maintained only  $ 35\% $  of their initial efficiency during  $ 100\mathrm{h} $  of UV exposure. In contrast, the SrO-incorporated perovskite device maintained approximately  $ 60\% $  of the initial efficiency. Moreover, the device with the SrO interlayer did not exhibit any degradation in the PL peak intensity and position. This indicates that UV degradation is initiated at the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite interface, and UV stability is enhanced by interface passivation. Ultraviolet stability can be further improved by the development of an interlayer material that is more conductive and can reduce lattice mismatch. Additionally, the development of an interlayer material that aids electron transfer across the interface

# Highlights

- Perovskite UV stability was tested under conditions except moisture, oxygen and wavelengths exceeding 400 nm.

- With high resolution  $ \mu $ -PL mapping technique, direct evidence is provided about the area perovskite UV degradation begins.

• UV degradation is initiated at the ETL/perovskite interface.

• Effect of the SrO interlayer on UV stability was examined in an inert atmosphere.

• UV stability is enhanced by SrO interface passivation.
