# A Thioxanthenothioxanthene Based Hole-Transporter with 2D Molecular Stacking for Efficient and Thermostable Perovskite Solar Cells

Aibin Zheng,  $  ^{\dagger}  $   Ming Ren,  $  ^{\dagger}  $   Yuyan Zhang,  $  ^{\dagger}  $   Yaohang Cai, Jing Zhang, Yi Yuan, Ming Lei, and Peng Wang  $  ^{*}  $

Department of Chemistry, Zhejiang University, Hangzhou 310028, China

Supporting Information Placeholder

ABSTRACT: Maintaining persistent thin-film morphology under certain thermal stress is desirable for durable operation of multilayer organic optoelectronic devices. We herein report a thioxanthenothioxanthene-centered hole-transporter (TXTX-OMeDPA) characteristic of two-dimensional molecular stacking in single crystal. TXTX-OMeDPA can be solution-processed into smooth thin films with suitable energy level and good hole mobility, allowing for the fabrication of perovskite solar cells with an impressive power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 22.2% when measured under illumination of AM 1.5G sunlight. The remarkable morphological stability of TXTX-OMeDPA based thin film ensures good stability for perovskite solar cells not only stored at 60 °C in the dark but also operated under equivalent full sunlight at 60 °C.

Lead halide based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have stimulated much recent research interest as they could evolve into a cost-effective photovoltaic technology with great promise.  $  ^{1-5}  $   For selective hole-extraction and electron-blocking, it is crucial to integrate a hole-transport layer into the PSC architectures via solution or vapor deposition.  $  ^{6-30}  $   Solution-processable molecular semiconductors have been actively explored for PSCs,  $  ^{9-26}  $   however only a few have been used for devices with over 21% power conversion efficiencies (PCEs).  $  ^{9-12}  $   Moreover, it seems challengeable to fabricate long-term stable PSCs with molecular semiconductors at an elevated temperature, i.e. > 55 °C, probably due to the energetic instability of an amorphous organic thin film.  $  ^{31-33}  $

In our previous work,  $  ^{25}  $  we designed a perixanthenoxanthene (PXX) centered molecular semiconductor (PXX-OMeDPA, Figure 1a), which has a higher hole mobility and a 120 meV deeper HOMO energy level in comparison with state-of-the-art hole-transporter spiro-OMeTAD. Unfortunately regulating the two material parameters (hole mobility and energy level) does not bring forth significant improvements on photovoltaic parameters, mainly owing to the adverse impact of charge recombination. Moreover, severe device degradation in the light-soaking tests at 60 °C was noted for PSCs with both spiro-OMeTAD and PXX-OMeDPA (Figure S1). The breakdown of PXX-OMeDPA based PSCs can be mainly ascribed to the heat-induced growth of organic dendrites (Figure S1), which we

think is intrinsically linked with the columnar stacking of PXX-OMeDPA molecules as found in the crystallographic analysis. Similarly, spiro-OMeTAD is characteristic of one-dimensional stacking; its thin film also lacks morphological stability.  $  ^{26,42}  $   The morphology deterioration of spiro-OMeTAD also leads to severe photovoltaic degradation at elevated temperatures.  $  ^{34,35}  $

Our subsequent study has shown that thioxanthenothioxanthene (TXTX) single crystal falls into the  $ P2_{1}/n $  space group, whereas PXX single crystal belongs to the  $ C $  1 space group albeit only two-atom difference. The highly anisotropic PXX single crystal (Figure S2) is characteristic of a typical herringbone type arrangement with two types of molecular orientation. TXTX exhibits a columnar-stacking mode along the  $ b $  axis, and has four types of molecular orientation. Apart from the  $ \pi-\pi $  stacking of molecules in the same orientation, there are abundant intermolecular interactions for molecules in different orientations along the  $ ac $ -plane, which is vertical to the stacking direction. This expands the dimensionality of molecular stacking in single crystal. The remarkable packing variation (Figure S2) of TXTX and PXX has motivated us to design a TXTX centered hole-transporter  $ N^{3}, N^{3}, N^{0}, N^{9} $ -tetrakis(4-methoxyphenyl)thioxantheno[2,1,9,8-klmna]thioxanthene-3,9-diamine (TXTX-OMeDPA, Figure 1a) with a possibly distinctive solid state structure. When used in PSCs, TXTX-OMeDPA achieves a PCE over 22% at the AM 1.5G conditions and excellent durability at 60 °C.

As illustrated in Scheme S1, we prepared TXTX from 2,2'-bis(methylsulfinyl)-1,1'-binaphthalene  $  ^{36}  $  at excellent yield of 90% via intramolecular C–H thioarylation.  $  ^{37}  $  Subsequent duplicate bromination and duplicate amination afforded TXTX-OMeDPA in total yield of 83%. On the basis of optimized molecular geometries in THF (Figure S3) with density functional theory (DFT), it is acquired that the theoretical HOMO energy level of TXTX-OMeDPA is -4.79 eV, which is 80 meV deeper than that of spiro-OMeTAD. The HOMO energy levels were also estimated by cyclic voltammetric measurements (Figure S4). The electrochemical HOMO alignment is roughly consistent with the theoretically predicted one. The melting point (  $  T_{m}  $  ) and glass transition temperature (  $  T_{g}  $  ) of TXTX-OMeDPA are derived from differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) thermograms presented in Figure S5, being 278 °C and 134 °C, respectively, hole-transport both of which are higher than the corresponding values (  $  T_{m} = 249  $  °C;  $  T_{g} = 121  $  °C) of spiro-OMeTAD. When bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (HTFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP) for air doping of hole-transporting organic semiconductors are added, the  $  T_{g}  $  of doped TXTX-OMeDPA sample drops to 89 °C and that of the spiro-OMeTAD counterpart is 72 °C (Figure S6).

X-ray crystallographic analysis was implemented to reveal the molecular structure and packing mode of TXTX-OMeDPA in perfectly ordered solid. The unit cell parameters are tabulated in Table S1. In TXTX-OMeDPA single crystal (Figure S7), two propeller-shaped DPA are equivalently connected to a nearly planar TXTX core, leading to a centrosymmetric molecular geometry. Note that using molecular geometries extracted from single crystal, the DFT calculated HOMO energy level (Figure S7) is -4.54

eV. TXTX-OMeDPA molecules crystallize into the monoclinic   $  P\ 2_{1}/c  $   space group and present a highly slipped stacking along the bc plane (Figure 1b), forming a 1.268-nm-thick molecular lamella (Figure 1c).

Various noncovalent interactions (NCIs) in all types of dimers in the TXTX-OMeDPA single crystal were analysed by the quantum theory of atoms in molecules (QTAIM).  $  ^{38,39}  $   Molecular graphs are presented in Figures 1d,1e,S6. The energy decomposition analysis (EDA)  $  ^{40,41}  $   gives a quantitative description of these NCIs as listed in Table S2. In a molecular lamella, each molecule is surrounded by six molecules, forming two types of dimers (dimers 1 and 2, Figure 1b). Dimer 1 has the shortest intermolecular centroid distance (d) of 8.66 Å with a dihedral angle of 11.5° between two TXTX planes. Multiple CH  $  \cdots\pi  $   and C  $  \cdots  $  S interactions in dimer 1 lead to a sizable intermolecular interaction energy (  $  E^{int}  $  ) of 735 meV. Dimer 2 is composed of two translationally equivalent molecules, with a d of 12.10 Å. There are CH  $  \cdots\pi  $   interactions between the methoxyphenyl aromatic CH and the TXTX core in dimer 2, yielding a   $  E^{int}  $   of 630 meV. In addition, there are weak NCIs (  $  E^{int} < 320  $   meV) in dimers 3–5 (Figure S8) for molecules belonging to two adjacent lamellas. Overall, the   $  E^{int}  $   along the bc plane is remarkably stronger than that along the a direction, underpinning two-dimensional molecular assembling in lamellar TXTX-OMeDPA single crystals (Figure S9). In contrast, both PXX-OMeDPA and spiro-OMeTAD exhibit typical one-dimension columnar stackings and tend to form needle-shaped single crystals.  $  ^{25,42}  $

The peak at   $  2\theta = 12.6^{\circ}  $   which corresponds to the 100 plane of TXTX-OMeDPA single-crystal has proved the presence of 2D molecular stacking. In contrast, only a broad peak can be

observed for the spiro-OMeTAD counterpart (Figure 2b), indicating amorphous characteristic and random molecular orientations.

The kinetics of hole extraction with TXTX-OMeDPA from perovskite deposited on   $  TiO_{2}  $   derived from time-resolved PL (TRPL) traces (Figure 4) is slightly slower than that of spiro-OMeTAD. The yields of hole extraction (Table S3) are 98.0% with TXTX-OMeDPA and 98.4% with spiro-OMeTAD, respectively.

We constructed PSCs with a simple structure of glass|FTO|TiO  $  _{2}  $  |FA based perovskite|hole-transporter|Au. The fabrication and encapsulation (Figure S12) details are provided in the Supporting Information. The thickness of hole-transport layer was optimized by use of 20, 30, 40, or 50 mM TXTX-OMeDPA in chlorobenzene. The cells fabricated with 40 mM TXTX-OMeDPA presented the highest PCE on average (Figure S13). The surface morphology of hole-transporter deposited on perovskite was investigated by (atomic force microscopy) AFM and scanning electron microscopy (SEM). TXTX-OMeDPA formed a uniform and pinhole-free film with root-mean-square (RMS) roughness value of 7.5 nm, while some pinholes existed in the spiro-OMeTAD counterpart albeit lower RMS of 6.5 nm (Figure S14).

The typical J-V curves at the 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , simulated AM 1.5G conditions of encapsulated cells with TXTX-OMeDPA and spiro-OMeTAD as hole-transporters are presented in Figure 5a. The TXTX-OMeDPA based device possesses a short-circuit photocurrent density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 24.78 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.15 V, and a fill factor (FF) of 0.78, yielding a PCE of 22.2%. The   $  V_{oc}  $   of spiro-OMeTAD based device is reduced by 30 mV and the FF is lowered by 3%, affording a PCE of 20.8%. Only small hysteresis was observed between the forward and reverse scans (Figure S15) for our PSCs. The one-sun integrated current densities (  $  J_{sc}^{EQE}  $  ) from the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra (Figure 5b) are about 24.2 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , in good agreement with the   $  J_{sc}  $   values obtained from the J-V curves. Furthermore, the maximum power point (MPP) tracking (Figure S16) under AM 1.5G illumination was conducted, affording a steady-state PCE output of 22.0% for the TXTX-OMeDPA cell and 20.5% for the spiro-OMeTAD cell. The average photovoltaic parameters of 12 cells are tabulated in Table S4. The narrow distribution of photovoltaic parameters (Figure S17) suggests good cell-to-cell reproducibility.

The improvement in FF of the TXTX-OMeDPA based cell can be attributed to lower hole transport resistance (  $  R_{H}  $  , Figure 5c), which are derived from impedance spectra with an equivalent circuit (Figure S18).  $  ^{51,52}  $   Meanwhile, the recombination resistance (  $  R_{re}  $  , Figure 5d) of the TXTX-OMeDPA cell is over three times larger than that of the spiro-OMeTAD counterpart at the same potential bias. The

profoundly attenuation of charge recombination generates an enhanced   $  V_{oc}  $  .

Preliminary thermal stability of TXTX-OMeDPA based PSCs stored at 85 °C in the dark for one week were first measured, showing an averaged 77% retention ratio of initial PCE as presented in Figure S19, which is better than that of 65% for cells with spiro-OMeTAD and is comparable to that of cells with poly[bis(4-phenyl) (2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA). Note that PTAA based cells present an lower averaged initial PCE of 19.2%. It can also be concluded from Figure S19 that overloading of additives (HFTSI and TBP) has an adverse influence on both device efficiency and 85 °C thermal stability.

We next investigated the durability of PSCs stored at 60°C in the dark. As illustrated in Figure 4e, the TXTX-OMeDPA based cell maintains 94% of its initial PCE after 1000 h thermal aging. The PCE degradation is mainly associated with ~4% decrease of FF (Figure S20), probably owing to the increase of hole transport resistance. The reductions of both   $  J_{sc}  $   and   $  V_{oc}  $   are negligible. In contrast, the retention ratio of the spiro-OMeTAD cell is only 71%. We also note that both cells present inappreciable degradation if stored at room temperature.

It can be concluded from impedance spectra analysis (Figure S21) that thermal aging brings forth a reduced   $  R_{re}  $   and an enlarged   $  R_{H}  $   for both cells, however the variation degree is significantly attenuated for the TXTX-OMeDPA based cell. SEM measurements were carried out to scrutinize the heat-induced microscopic morphology change of the hole-transport layer in PSCs. Figure 5c displays a uniform and pinhole-free hole-transport layer for the TXTX-OMeDPA cell even after aging at 60 °C for 1000 h, which is actually very similar to the one stored at room temperature (Figure 6a), suggesting a prominent morphological durability. In contrast, there are tiny pinholes for the 60 °C aged spiro-OMeTAD counterpart (Figure 6d). The better conformality of TXTX-OMeDPA could stem from an elevated   $  T_{g}  $   and peculiar molecular orientations.

In summary, we have synthesized a thioxanthenothioxanthene based molecular semiconductor with good hole mobility and thermostable thin film morphology due to two-dimensional molecular assembling. The TXTX-OMeDPA based perovskite solar cell is characteristic of reduced transport resistance and enlarged interfacial recombination resistance, affording an impressive efficiency of over 22% at the AM 1.5G conditions. The morphological persistence of TXTX-OMeDPA based thin film confers PSCs with good stability at the aging conditions of both dark storage at 60 °C and operation under light-soaking at 60 °C.
