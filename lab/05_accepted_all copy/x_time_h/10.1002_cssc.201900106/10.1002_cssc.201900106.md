# Fabrication of efficient and stable perovskite solar cells under high humidity environment via a trace-doping of large-sized cations

Xueping Liu  $  ^{[a]}  $  , Jiang He  $  ^{[a]}  $  , Pengfei Wang  $  ^{[a]}  $  , Yuhao Liu  $  ^{[b]}  $  , Junyan Xiao  $  ^{[c]}  $  , Zhiliang Ku  $  ^{[a]}  $  , Yong Peng  $  ^{[a]}  $  , Fuzhi Huang  $  ^{*[a]}  $  , Yi-Bing Cheng  $  ^{[a,d,e]}  $   and Jie Zhong  $  ^{*[a]}  $

Abstract: The solution processed organic-inorganic lead halide perovskites have shown photovoltaic performance above 23%, attracting great attention. However, the champion devices require to be fabricated in a controlled inert/dry atmosphere. Developing highly efficient and stable perovskite solar cells under high humidity atmosphere conditions for future commercialization is still challenging, especially for   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) that is vulnerable to moisture. In this study, we incorporate a large-sized tert-Butylammonium (C(CH  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}^{+}  $  ) organic cation into the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   crystalline structure, which could form a more stable 3D crystalline structure and alleviate the decomposition by the humidity. It delivers a power conversion efficiency of 19.3% prepared under a humidity environment condition of 50% RH, as well as improved humidity and thermal stability. Our work provides a facile strategy to improve perovskite performance and stability by introducing a new chemical additive for future application of perovskite solar cells.

# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have drawn enormous attention in the photovoltaic community worldwide due to their unique optoelectronic properties, simple solution fabrication process and low manufacturing costs for large-scale production. PSCs have now reached a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 23.7%,  $  ^{[1-2]}  $  which enables them as a leading contender for photovoltaic applications. Nevertheless, practical applications of PSCs require further research on both process conditions and long-term durability.  $  ^{[3-4]}  $  Nowadays, the most significant methods to improve environmental stability is exploring for new perovskite

materials with better intrinsic stability and developing novel preparation process, including additive engineering strategies,  $  ^{[5-7]}  $   interface engineering,  $  ^{[8-12]}  $   and process optimization.  $  ^{[13-16]}  $   However, high quality and stable perovskite films normally are required to be fabricated inside a costly glovebox that blocks water and oxygen. This becomes a stumbling block towards commercialization.  $  ^{[17-18]}  $

Tremendous studies have revealed that promoting perovskite film morphology and crystalline quality is an effective strategy to improve the intrinsical stability of perovskite materials, which provides more possibilities for highly efficient and stable device. For example, the addition of SCN $ ^{-} $ , Lewis acid or trimethylammonium chloride into perovskite precursor can significantly increase the grain size and device performance. $ ^{[19-21]} $  An incorporation engineering strategy is introduced by employing large cation in lead iodide perovskites to stabilize three-dimensional crystalline structure and improve the PCE, such as Cs $ ^{+} $  and/or Rb $ ^{+} $  or Gua $ ^{+} $ . $ ^{[22-24]} $  However, these facile solution processes normally require to be conducted in nitrogen glovebox. To fabricate the perovskite film in moisture-laden air which is more affordable for the manufacturing, it is still a task of great challenge. Yang et al. found that moisture could assist grain growth of solution processed films, thus facilitate the crystal growth and enhance photovoltaic performance. $ ^{[25]} $  Preheating the substrate has also been discovered to play a predominant role in determining photovoltaic performance under atmospheric environment. $ ^{[17, 26-27]} $  Besides that, using lead thiocyanate (Pb(SCN) $ _{2} $ ) precursor could prepare high-quality CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3-x} $ (SCN) $ _{x} $  perovskite films under high humidity conditions. $ ^{[28]} $  Although above mentioned routes show great potential for preparing high efficiency and high reproducibility PSCs, many of these improved processes ineluctably increase the complexity of the device fabrication process and their PCEs are still not outstanding enough (below 19%). Furthermore, long-term stability and environmental stability are not mentioned in these studies, which are also a barrier to application as the lead halide perovskite materials are very sensitive to humidity and easy to degrade in humid conditions. $ ^{[29-30]} $  Therefore, it is a great challenge to develop a facile process to fabricate stable devices resistant to hostile operation environment.

To alleviate these problems, we have successfully introduced a simple method to realize high-quality methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI $ _3 $ ) perovskite films and stable devices by introducing a large-sized C(CH $ _3 $ ) $ _3 $ NH $ _3^+ $  (t-BA) cation to form a stable PSC architecture. $ ^{[31-32]} $  The t-BA cation could be inserted in the crystal unit partially and substitute MA cation to form a stable crystalline structure as observed via X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns. # Results and Discussion

In order to confirm the t-BA cations enter the MAPbI $ _3 $  crystal lattice, XRD patterns of t-BA cation doped MAPbI $ _3 $  and (t-BA)PbI $ _3 $  have been shown in Figure 1. All the characteristic peaks are identical and there is no additional impurity phase in the doped samples, which indicating the black perovskite phase has been formed. The enlarged (110) and (220) lattice plane diffraction peaks show a significant shift to lower angles along with the amounts of additives increase, compared with the pure MAPbI $ _3 $  samples. The left-shift of the peaks indicates the enlargement of the d-spacing, which should be caused by the t-BA cations substituting MA cations to form a modified three-dimensional perovskite structure. It has been found that MA cations could be partially replaced by EA (ethylammonium) or Gua (guanidinium), forming a MA $ _x $ EA $ _{1-x} $ PbBr $ _3 $ /MA $ _{1-x} $ Gua $ _x $ PbI $ _3 $  structure. $ ^{[24, 33-34]} $  The t-BA cation has a quite similar chemical structure, $ ^{[35]} $  thus it could occupy A site in perovskite structure. This will extend the unit cell volume, resulting in the left-shift of the XRD peaks.

In addition, it is found that the XRD peak intensities increase obviously with the incorporation of t-BA cations, while gradually decrease when the content increases to 0.1%. A proper amount of large-sized t-BA cations could be beneficial for obtaining high-quality perovskite films. In order to deeply understand the effect of doping contents on the grain morphology and crystal quality of perovskite, the scanning electron microscope (SEM) images of the perovskite film with different amount of t-BAI are shown in Figure S1. The grain size increases and tends to be uniform with the increase of the additives till to 0.01% molar mass, and then decrease with the further increase of t-BA cations (0.1%~10%).

Thus, with the proper doping of t-BAI, it could accelerate the crystallization of perovskite and result in an increased grain size.

To further investigate how the additive influences the trap state density and charge mobility in perovskite films thus improve the overall performance, we collected the dark current-voltage characteristics for both electron-only device with a glass/FTO/PCBM/perovskite/PCBM/Au architecture and hole-only device with a glass/FTO/PEDOT:PSS/perovskite/Au architecture.  $  ^{[20, 38-39]}  $   We measured the J-V response of perovskite films in the space-charge-limited-current (SCLC) regime.  $  ^{[38, 40]}  $   At low voltages, the linear J-V response is ohmic. At intermediate voltages, the current quickly increases non-linearly and signals the transition onto the trap-filled limit (TFL). It could be concluded that all the available trap states are completely filled by the injected carriers, and the kink point is defined as trap-filled limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ). The trap state density is determined by the   $  V_{TFL}  $   using the following equation:  $  ^{[41]}  $

# $ V_{\mathrm{TFL}} = en_{t}L^{2} / 2\varepsilon \varepsilon_{0} $

Where  $  e  $  is the elementary charge of the electron,  $  n_t  $  is the trap-state density.  $  L  $  is the perovskite film thickness,  $  \varepsilon  $  is the relative dielectric constant of perovskite (taken as 32 for MAPbI $ _3 $  from previous reports, $ ^{[38,42]} $  and  $  \varepsilon_0  $  is the vacuum permittivity. The  $  V_{\text{TFL}}  $ 's obtained from Figure 5a are 0.72 and 0.50 V for without and with additives and the related electron trap densities are  $  2.34 \times 10^{16} \text{ cm}^{-3}  $  and  $  1.62 \times 10^{16} \text{ cm}^{-3}  $ , respectively, indicating that a much lower electron trap density in the doped 0.01% t-BAI device than the control device. For comparison, we also estimated the hole trap density as shown in Figure 5b. We found that with the introducing of 0.01% t-BAI, the hole trap-state density significantly decreases from  $  8.77 \times 10^{16} \text{ cm}^{-3}  $  to  $  4.54 \times 10^{16} \text{ cm}^{-3}  $ . These results prove that molecular passivation at grain boundaries remediates defects in the perovskite film, which is expected to diminish charge recombination and account for the increased FF in the corresponding solar cells. On the other hand, the evaporation of partial MAI during annealing may leave structural vacancies in the perovskite, the doped t-BAI in the crystal structure can endow a better passivation effect. As a result, that a proper amount of t-BAI additives could improve the perovskite film quality and lead to lower defect densities.

A small amount of t-BAI in perovskites can clearly reduce trap-assisted recombination, which suggests that such a process can be efficient as the energy level alignment is suitable to form a cascading charge transfer.

# Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated a novel large-sized cation doping into  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  could allow the fabrication PSCs in high humidity condition with simultaneously improved performance and environmental stability. The incorporation of large t-BA cations could partially substitute MA cations thus form a more

stable perovskite crystalline structure. The perovskite with trace doping of 0.01% t-BAI preserves an excellent optoelectronic property, leading to a high PCE of 19.3% and a high FF of 0.8. This work provides a simple strategy to fabricate high efficiency PSCs under a high humidity environment of 50% RH and realize both good humidity and heat stabilities, thus provide a competitive method for industrial applications.

# Experimental Section

# Materials

$ \mathrm{SnCl_2\cdot 2H_2O} $  was purchased from Aladdin. Tert-Butylammonium Iodide (t-BAI) was purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Methylammonium iodide (MAI) was purchased from Lumtec, Taiwan. Lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2) $  was purchased from TCI. Spiro-OMeTAD was purchased from Shenzhen Feiming Science and Technology Co., Ltd. Other chemicals were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich or Alfa Aesar. All the chemicals were used as received.

# Solar cell fabrication

FTO glass was etched by a laser machine (Universal Laser Systems, VLS2.30), and followed by ultrasonic cleaning through detergent, pure water and ethyl alcohol for 20 min, respectively. They were then dried with dry-air gas flow and then treated by UV-ozone for 15 min to remove the last traces of organic residues. The compact SnO₂ blocking layer was achieved by chemical bath deposition (CBD) as reported previously.[34] The (t-BA)PbI₃ was prepared from a precursor solution containing 1.5 M t-BAI and 1.5 M PbI₂ in anhydrous co-solvent of DMF/DMSO (4:1, by volume). MAPbI₃ perovskite precursor was prepared by dissolving 1.5 M MAI and 1.5 M PbI₂ in mix solvent. The perovskite absorber was deposited on the UV processed SnO₂ substrates by spun a 25 uL perovskite solution at 4000 rpm for 30 s with an accelerated speed of 4000 rpm, and 100 μL anti-solvents of ethyl acetate was dropped at the first 10th second. The films were then annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. This whole process was completed in a ventilation cabinet with a humidity of 50%. After cooling down, a 25 μL Spiro-OMeTAD solution, which was prepared by dissolved 73 mg Spiro-OMeTAD into 1 mL chlorobenzene followed by the addition of 18 μL Li-TFSI (pre-dissolved as a 520 mg/mL stock solution in acetonitrile), 29 μL FK209 (predissolved as a 300 mg/mL stock solution in acetonitrile) and 30 μL 4-tert-butylpyridine, was spun on the corresponding perovskite films at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, a 80 nm of gold was evaporated on the top of Spiro as the back electrode to complete the whole devices.

# Characterizations

X-ray diffraction spectra were measured with an X-ray diffractometer (D8 Advance, Bruker, Germany) using Cu Ka radiation. The surface morphologies of the perovskite films and cross-sectional structure of the perovskite solar cells were investigated using a field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM, Zeiss Ultra Plus).
