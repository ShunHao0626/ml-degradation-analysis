# Manipulating Nucleation and Crystal Growth of Inorganic Perovskite Solar Cells

Xinwen Zhang,  $  ^{II}  $   Chengbin Fei,  $  ^{II}  $   Lening Shen, Pramod Baral, Sankaranarayanan Nair Vijayaraghavan, Feng Yan, Xiong Gong, and He Wang  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Inorganic metal halide perovskite materials as sunlight absorbers for solar cells exhibit better thermal stability than organic-inorganic hybrid counterparts. Pure cesium lead triiodide (CsPbI $ _3 $ ), with the most suitable band gap, suffers phase instability under an ambient environment. Nucleation and crystal growth are two crucial steps in fabricating a solution-processed perovskite film. A high-quality perovskite film with good morphology makes a significant impact on the efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells. Dimethylformamide (DMF) is a commonly used aprotic solvent. However, it is difficult to obtain a high-quality inorganic perovskite film using DMF as a single solvent due to its slow evaporation and strong coordination with  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $ . Here, we investigate dimethylacetamide (DMAc)/DMF as a cosolvent to prompt nucleation during the spin-coating process, leading to higher nucleation density and better surface coverage. In addition, we introduce CsBr in dimethylammonium lead triiodide (DMAPbI $ _3 $ )/CsI precursors to slow down the crystal growth process. CsBr does not increase the film band

gap but leads to a pinhole-free film with better crystallinity. Through nucleation and crystal growth engineering, the power conversion efficiency of inorganic perovskite devices is improved to  $ 17.67\% $ , and ambient environment stability is significantly enhanced.

KEYWORDS: inorganic perovskite solar cell, nucleation, crystallization, high efficiency, stability

# INTRODUCTION

Recently, organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have reached a power conversion efficiency of 26%.  $  ^{1}  $   To date, the main challenge toward the commercialization of PSCs is their long-term stability as it tends to degrade under extreme heat or humid environments.  $  ^{2-5}  $   Replacing an organic cation with an inorganic cation is considered to be an effective strategy to improve the thermal stability of PSCs.  $  ^{6,7}  $   Particularly, a high annealing temperature of films typically represents a good thermal tolerance. For instance, the annealing temperature required to convert organic–inorganic hybrid MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   and FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   into the desired perovskite phase is around 100 and 150 °C, respectively, while CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   needs a higher annealing temperature of more than 200 °C.  $  ^{8-10}  $   Among inorganic halide perovskites, CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   has the most suitable band gap (1.73 eV) for solar cell application.  $  ^{11}  $   However, CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   favors a yellow phase in an ambient environment rather than a black phase due to the low tolerance factor.  $  ^{12}  $

Perovskite formation involves nucleation and the crystal growth process.  $  ^{13-15}  $   A uniform and well-covered perovskite film is essential to fabricate a good photovoltaic device.  $  ^{16}  $   The surface coverage is a result of a combination of several factors. Among perovskite solvents, DMF and dimethylacetamide (DMAc) share similar chemical properties, both being in the category of aprotic solvents comprising relatively good electron-pair donor properties, with each having a donor strength of around 24.  $  ^{34,35}  $   Both DMF and DMAc have similar solubility and boiling points: boiling point of 153 °C for DMF  $  ^{36}  $   and 165 °C for DMAc.  $  ^{37}  $   The molecular structures of DMF and DMAc are shown in Supporting Information Figure S1. Both DMF and DMAc have a carboxamide with the general structure R1-C(=O)-NR2R3, where R2 and R3 are methyl groups. The difference between DMF and DMAc is the R1 group. DMAc has a methyl group connected with carbon (Figure S1a), while DMF has a hydrogen atom connected with carbon (Figure S1b).  $  ^{38}  $   The polarity of the solvent is dependent on the relative content of polar groups and nonpolar groups. The greater the proportion of polar groups in the molecule, the larger the polarity of the molecule. The polarity of the solvent also determines the coordination ability.  $  ^{37}  $   DMF has a stronger polarity than DMAc, indicating that the coordination between DMF and Pb  $  ^{2+}  $   is stronger than that between DMAc and Pb  $  ^{2+}  $  . Based on this, the impact on the nucleation process caused by the boiling point, polarity, and evaporation should be balanced. During film fabrication, the nucleation process should be precisely controlled for the formation of an optimized film. If the nucleation is too slow, the concentration of the nucleus will be low and the initially appeared nucleus will have enough space to grow into a large size crystal. However, the low concentration of the nucleus will lead to a large number of pinholes and low surface coverage.  $  ^{39}  $   If the nucleation is too fast, the concentration of the nucleus will be high, and the time and space left for the crystals to grow will be limited, leading to a fully covered perovskite film with a small grain size and more grain boundaries.  $  ^{37}  $   The perovskite stability will be affected since the degradation starts from the grain boundaries.  $  ^{40}  $   In our experiment, we found that by replacing a small amount of DMF with DMAc, the nucleation rate is prompted, encouraging the formation of more nuclei, resulting in a uniform perovskite film with better surface coverage, stronger crystallinity, higher photoluminescence (PL) intensity, and longer charge carrier lifetime.

CsPbI $ _3 $  has four different phases, cubic( $ \alpha $ ), tetragonal ( $ \beta $ ), and two orthorhombic phases (a metastable black  $ \gamma $  and a nonperovskite yellow  $ \delta $  phase). $ ^{41} $  The black phase spontaneously tends to transform to the nonperovskite yellow phase at room temperature and humid environment. $ ^{42} $  Stabilizing  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  in an ambient environment is necessary. It has been reported that by properly incorporating small ions like Br $ ^- $  and Sn $ ^{2+} $  to tune the tolerance factor to a more suitable value, the ambient environment stability has been successfully improved. $ ^{42-45} $  Surface passivation is also an effective method to protect the perovskite from moisture degradation. Li et al. reported synthesizing extra-long-term stable cubic CsPbI $ _3 $  by inducing polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) surface passivation. $ ^{46} $  Here, we introduced a Br $ ^- $  ion with a relatively small radius into a perovskite precursor to regulate the crystallization process of CsPbI $ _3 $  and further improve stability. Interestingly, we found that even though 30% CsBr was doped in the perovskite precursor (dimethylammonium lead triiodide (DMAPbI $ _3 $ )/CsI), there was almost no Br left in our final CsPbI $ _3 $  film after annealing at 210  $ ^{\circ} $ C for 6 min. Surprisingly, the photovoltaic performance and ambient stability of CsPbI $ _3 $  PSCs show significant improvement. We conclude that Br is released during the film annealing, and the crystallization

process is regulated, leading to larger grain size and better crystallinity. By tuning the incorporation amount of CsBr, the perovskite film quality is significantly improved. In this work, we introduced DMAc to speed up the nucleation and CsBr as growth-controlling agents to slow down crystallization, leading to a pinhole-free perovskite layer. The optimized solar cells achieved an efficiency of 17.67%.

Since crystallization and surface morphology may influence the charge carrier transport and recombination, femtosecond laser transient absorption (TA) experiments were carried out to study the carrier dynamics in the film. Figure 2b displays the image of TA spectra and  $ \Delta T / T $  of the perovskite prepared by a mixed solvent and a single solvent. The power of the pump laser at  $ 400\mathrm{nm} $  was  $ 10~\mu \mathrm{J} / \mathrm{cm}^2 $ . Two different signals were observed. The positive signal comes from ground-state bleaching and stimulated emission, and the negative signal comes from excited-state absorption. For films fabricated by single and dual solvents, the ground-state bleaching is located at  $ 703\mathrm{nm} $ , and the kinetics is almost the same (Figure 2c). The curves were fitted with a four-exponential function, one arising from hot carrier cooling and three others associated with exponential decay (Table S1).

Transient absorption probes the carrier dynamics in a few nanoseconds, while time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) tells the carrier dynamics in hundreds of nanoseconds and even longer. Figure 2d plots TRPL curves obtained from two different host solvent preparations. The DMF-fabricated film exhibits 2 ns ( $  \tau_1  $ ) and 18 ns ( $  \tau_2  $ ), while a higher value is obtained from the DMA/DMAc-fabricated film: 8 ns ( $  \tau_1  $ ) and 62 ns ( $  \tau_2  $ ) (Table S2). As a result, the average carrier lifetime ( $  \tau_{average}  $ ), estimated from  $  \tau_{\text{ave}} = (A_1\tau_1^2 + A_2\tau_2^2)/(A_1\tau_1 + A_2\tau_2)^{50}  $  increased from 17.4 ns for the DMF solvent to 58.6 ns for the DMF/DMAc solvent, attributed to the strong suppression of nonradiative recombination and improved

surface coverage. As compared to the control sample with the weak PL signal (Figure 2e), the film prepared with the DMF/DMAc dual solvent exhibits increased PL intensity, which is 1.4 times higher than that of the single solvent. The obvious PL enhancement again proves the enhanced film quality.

Compared to polar aprotic solvent DMF, DMAc exhibits low polarity with relatively weak coordination to   $  Pb^{2+}  $   and is easy to escape during the nucleation process, which can accelerate the nucleation rate of the perovskite and give a better film morphology. To probe the effects of DMAc on device performance, solar cells with an architecture of FTO/F-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  /Spiro-OMeTAD/Al  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $  /Ag were fabricated. Current density–voltage (J-V) curves are shown in Figures 2f and S5a,b. The champion device fabricated with the DMF solvent showed a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 19.37 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 0.99 V, and a fill factor of 0.74, resulting in an efficiency of 14.16%. The champion device fabricated with the DMF/DMAc solvent showed a significant improvement: a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 19.66 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.05 V, and a fill factor of 0.76, resulting in an efficiency of 15.63%. The device performance is consistent with our characterization that the DMF/DMAc-prepared film has a better surface morphology and enhanced crystallinity.

# EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

Materials. DMF (99.8%, anhydrous), DMAc (99.8%, anhydrous), HI (57 wt % in water, 99.95%), and   $  (\mathrm{NH}_{4})_{2}\mathrm{TiF}_{6}  $   were purchased from Millipore Sigma. CsI (99.999%) was bought from Alfa Aesar. Lead iodide   $  (\mathrm{PbI}_{2}, 99.99\%)  $   was purchased from Tokyo Chemical Industry Co., Ltd (TCI). Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) and Spiro-OMeTAD (99%) were purchased from Advanced Election Technology.

Synthesize DMAPbI $ _3 $  Crystal. Two grams of PbI $ _2 $  was dissolved in 5 mL of DMF, and then 1.6 mL of HI was slowly added into the PbI $ _2 $  solution. The prepared solution was stored in a dark environment for 1 week. The synthesized crystals were washed with isopropyl alcohol (IPA) three to four times, transferred into a vacuum chamber, and annealed at 60 °C for 24 h.

Device Preparation. Perovskite Precursor Preparation. Pristine perovskite: 0.8 M CsI and 1 M DMAPbI  $  _{3}  $  crystals are dissolved in the solvent DMF solvent.

Optimized perovskite: 0.8 M CsI (70%)/CsBr (30%) and 1 M DMAPbI  $  _{3}  $  crystals are dissolved in the solvent (DMF(v)/DMAc(v) = 9/1).

For other perovskites, 0.8 M CsI/CsBr (8/2,7/3, or 6/4) and 1 M DMAPbI  $  _{3}  $  crystals are dissolved in the solvent DMF.

FTO was cleaned with cleaning powder, deionized water, alcohol, and acetone for 15 min for each step. The dried FTO was put under UV ozone for 15 min to remove the organic residue. Fluorinated   $  TiO_{2}  $   (  $  F-TiO_{2}  $  ) was fabricated using a chemical bath: the cleaned substrate was immersed in a 0.2 M   $  TiCl_{4}  $   aqueous solution with 0.5 g of   $  (\mathrm{NH}_{4})_{2}\mathrm{TiF}_{6}  $   at 65 °C for 30 min; the substrate was washed with deionized water and dried with airflow. Then, the substrates were annealed at 450 °C for 30 min, cooled to room temperature, and transferred to a nitrogen-filled glovebox. Further, 50 μL of the precursor was dropped on a compact F-  $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate and spin-coated at 2500 rpm for 25 s, and ethyl acetate was added as an antisolvent at 15th s. The films were transferred onto a hot plate at 60 °C for 2 min and then annealed at 210 °C for 6 min. After cooling to room temperature, the Spiro-OMeTAD layer was deposited on top of the perovskite, as reported elsewhere.  $  ^{53}  $   Finally, 1 nm Al and 80 nm Ag were thermally evaporated with a 0.1 cm  $  ^{2}  $   active area. J-V curves were measured by a Keithley 2612B under AM 1.5 G and 100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $   illumination with a xenon solar simulator.

Characterization. The scanning electron microscopy image was obtained by a JEOL JSM-7100F. XRD was measured with a PANalytical X-ray diffractometer at a step size of   $  0.01^{\circ}  $  . The UV–vis absorption spectra were obtained by an Agilent UV spectrophotometer (Cary 60). Steady-state PL and TRPL were obtained by a homely integrated Horiba setup, including a lifetime controller (DeltaFlex), a 405 nm pulsed laser diode, a spectrometer (iHR320), a photon counting detector, and a homely built sample compartment. The femtosecond transient absorption was collected using a collinear pump–probe setup (Ultrafast Systems), similar to our previous report.  $  ^{[34]}  $   The 400 nm pump was produced by doubling the frequency of the Ti:sapphire laser pulse (Coherent Astrella, 800 nm, 5 KHz), while the supercontinuum white light probe in the visible region was generated by focusing a small part of laser fundamental onto a sapphire. The time delay between the pump and the probe was controlled by a mechanical stage and then arrived at the same point on the sample. The cross-correlation between the pump and the probe was around 100 femtoseconds. EQE was obtained by a QEX10 measurement system with a 300 W steady-state xenon lamp as the source light.

# CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we report a method utilizing DMAc to prompt nucleation for better surface coverage and CsBr to slower the crystallization rate for better crystallinity during the fabrication of highly efficient, long-term, and stable   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   solar cells. The carrier dynamics at various time scales demonstrates that the introduction of dual solvent DMF/DMAc and CsBr could significantly improve the carrier lifetime. The majority of CsBr is evaporated during the annealing and does not disrupt the crystal structure and optoelectronic behavior of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films. A champion efficiency of 17.67% for the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite solar cell is achieved by tailoring the content of DMAc and CsBr in the perovskite precursor. The ambient environment stability is further improved through this nucleation and crystallization manipulation. Hence, our work suggests an easy way to improve the efficiency and stability of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells, which is advisable for inorganic single junction cells and perovskite/silicon tandem cells.
