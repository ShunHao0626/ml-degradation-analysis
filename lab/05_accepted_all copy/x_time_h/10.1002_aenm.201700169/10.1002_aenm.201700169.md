# Efficient Plastic Perovskite Solar Cell with a Low-Temperature Processable Electrodeposited TiO  $  _{2}  $  Compact Layer and a Brookite TiO  $  _{2}  $  Scaffold

Ssu-Yu Lin, Tzu-Sen Su, Tsung-Yu Hsieh, Pei-Chia Lo, and Tzu-Chien Wei*

Recent research on fabricating scaffold-type perovskite solar cells on plastic substrates has reported noteworthy progress in replacing the high-temperature processing of   $  TiO_{2}  $   scaffolds and compact layers with various low-temperature processes. Herein, recent progress in the laboratory is reported regarding the development of electrodeposited   $  TiO_{x}  $   compact layers and brookite   $  TiO_{2}  $   scaffolds, both of which can be processed under 150 °C without greatly sacrificing their photovoltaic performance. Through systematic characterization of device properties and careful optimization of the fabrication conditions, a record-high 15.76% power conversion efficiency of a plastic   $  TiO_{2}  $   scaffold-type perovskite solar cell is demonstrated. In addition, bending durability and preliminary stability tests on this plastic perovskite solar cell show promising results and indicate clear directions for future improvement.

# 1. Introduction

Lightweight, flexible photovoltaic (PV) devices are considered attractive renewable energy devices because they can be produced with high-throughput printing processes and they enable the usage of solar power on curved surfaces. Compared with heavy and brittle silicon PV devices, which are made using energy-consumptive processes, flexible PVs can be made using energy-saving, solution-based processes. Moreover, their bendability allows energy generation on curved surfaces, which is advantageous for the integration of wearable electronics. To date, fabrication on flexible substrates has been proposed for almost all of the PV technologies ever developed, regardless of whether those technologies are at the stage of laboratory research, industrial prototyping, or pilot production. Generally, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of a flexible PV is lower than that of its rigid counterpart, primarily owing to the limitations of the flexible substrates: plastic sheets are limited by low thermal budgets and metal foils are limited by back-illuminated optical losses. Apart from substrate problems, its flexibility is, ironically, another reason that causes low PCE. To maintain the mechanical stability of the functional layers (including the active layer, electron transport layer (ETL), and hole transport

These factors result in low photocurrents and low PCEs. Moreover, the leakage of liquid electrolyte is another troublesome factor that jeopardizes the long-term stability of a plastic DSSC. A light absorber with a high extinction coefficient may be feasible for harvesting adequate incident photons in a thin domain.[1] Liquid electrolytes are commonly replaced with solid-state HTLs to prevent leaks in plastic DSSCs.

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) were discovered in 2009,  $  ^{[2]}  $  and within seven years a PSC achieved an impressive PCE of 22.1%  $  ^{[3]}  $  (http://www.nrel.gov/). The light absorber of a PSC, an organometal halide perovskite, captures almost all visual light within a range of a few hundreds of nanometers because it has an unrivalled extinction coefficient.  $  ^{[4]}  $  Combined with its low exciton binding energy  $  ^{[5,6]}  $  and low formation temperature,  $  ^{[7]}  $  PSCs are regarded as a favorable candidate for plastic PVs. PSCs can be roughly classified according to their structures into three categories: mesoporous scaffold type, meso-superstructured type, and planar type. The mesoporous scaffold type is considered the most promising in terms of efficiency, reproducibility, and hysteresis control.  $  ^{[8-10]}  $  The first plastic PSC was developed by Kumar et al. in 2013.  $  ^{[11]}  $  Claiming that ZnO could be easily processed at low temperatures, they used ZnO nanorods to replace  $  TiO_{2}  $  as the ETL, as well as the hole-blocking layer of a planar PSC. # 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Device Architecture and Fabrication Process

# 2.2. Characterization and Evaluation of ED-TiO $ _x $  CL

A favorable CL should be ultrathin and pinhole-free, as well as have a high carrier density. To meet the aforementioned goals with a low-temperature processable device, anodic electrodeposition of   $  TiO_{x}  $   from an aqueous   $  TiCl_{3}  $   bath was used; this method was taken from our previous study, in which ED-  $  TiO_{x}  $   was deposited on rigid FTO glass.  $  ^{[23]}  $   A key improvement to apply ED-  $  TiO_{x}  $   electrodeposition on ITO-PEN is the pH adjustment of the depositing bath, because pristine   $  TiCl_{3}  $   aqueous solution is so acidic that the ITO layer is corroded in seconds. This work is digressively discussed in the Supporting Information, wherein it is demonstrated that ITO-PEN can survive a deposition bath with pH = 4.8. The structural identification, Mott–Schottky measurements, thickness determination, and hole-blocking effect of an ED-  $  TiO_{x}  $   layer are summarized in Figure 2. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns in Figure 2a indicate that the as-deposited ED-  $  TiO_{x}  $   was amorphous and transformed to the anatase phase when it was purposely sintered at 500 °C for 30 min in air; therefore its original chemical identity was titania. The Mott–Schottky plots of FTO/ED-  $  TiO_{x}  $   and of a   $  TiO_{2}  $   CL that was spin-coated and then sintered on FTO glass (FTO/SP-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) are illustrated in Figure 2b. Curve fitting reveals that FTO/ED-  $  TiO_{x}  $   had a lower negative flat band potential and higher carrier density than FTO/SP-  $  TiO_{2}  $ , both of which would be advantageous for an efficient, unidirectional charge transfer from BK-  $  TiO_{2}  $   to ITO-PEN.

The results of an X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy analysis are provided in Figure S5 (Supporting Information), and they reveal that the stoichiometric ratio of Ti and O was less than 2 because of the existence of trivalent Ti; compared with regular   $  TiO_{2}  $  , the inadequate oxidation state induced higher carrier concentration and thereby exhibited higher bulk conductivity than   $  TiO_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[24]}  $   Moreover, the higher carrier concentration reduced the width of the depletion zone (W) according to   $  W = \sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}(E - E_{\mathrm{f}})}{eN}}  $  , where N is the concentration of carriers,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity,   $  \varepsilon_{r}  $   is the dielectric constant, and   $  E_{f}  $   is the Fermi level, resulting in enhanced carrier tunneling as well as low contact resistance at the interface.  $  ^{[25]}  $   As shown in Figure 2c, the thicknesses of the ED-  $  TiO_{x}  $   films fabricated with 30, 50, and 70 s of deposition (hereafter ED-  $  TiO_{x}  $  -30, ED-  $  TiO_{x}  $  -50, and ED-  $  TiO_{x}  $  -70) were 19, 34, and 46 nm, respectively, matching a linear deposition rate of 0.67 nm s  $  ^{-1}  $  . This remarkable deposition rate is   $  \approx  $  167 times faster than atomic layer deposition, which is a competitive low-temperature process to deposit a high-quality   $  TiO_{2}  $   CL.  $  ^{[26]}  $

To probe the effectiveness of the hole blocking of ED-TiO $ _{x} $ , a simple and sensitive cyclic voltammetry (CV) was used. # 2.3. Characterization and Evaluation of BK-TiO $ _2 $  Scaffold

A commercial brookite suspension was first applied as a mesoporous scaffold for PSCs by Kogo et al.  $  ^{[21]}  $   They found that BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $   was able to form efficient ETLs and scaffolds to support perovskite at 150 °C, which is safe for heat-sensitive ITO-PEN. In their research, the perovskite absorber was one-step synthesized and an average PCE of 11% on the plastic substrate was achieved. Here, we applied the same BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $   raw material (which is commercially available) as the scaffold for PSC, but optimized its performance to fit a sequentially deposited perovskite absorber. The structure, morphology, and band position of the BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $   scaffold were precisely characterized and the results are presented in Figure 3. First, Figure 3a shows the XRD pattern of BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $   powder, in which the major characteristics of brookite TiO  $  _{2}  $   at 2θ of 25.4°, 30.8°, 36.3°, 40.2°, 46.1°, and 48.0° are clearly resolved, matching the orthorhombic phase of brookite TiO  $  _{2}  $   of JCPDS No.29–1360. Next, Figure 3b presents the transmission electron microscopic (TEM) images of BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $   and the benchmark anatase TiO  $  _{2}  $   nanoparticles from this study (30TA, G24i power, UK), which are commonly utilized in rigid PSCs. The images reveal that the BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $   is plate-like, with particle sizes ranging from 16 to 18 nm, whereas anatase TiO  $  _{2}  $   appears as typical truncated bipyramids with sizes ranging from ≈25 to 30 nm. The plate-like BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $   forms denser scaffolds than bipyramidal anatase TiO  $  _{2}  $   and thus offers multiple advantages, including more efficient charge extraction, superior electron pathways (even in the absence of sintering treatment), and probably an exceptional hole-blocking effect because of this dense packing.

The high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) images of single BK-TiO $ _2 $  and anatase TiO $ _2 $  nanoparticles presented in Figure 3c confirm their dimensions and shapes, and show the representative lattice spaces of 3.5 Å for anatase (101) crystal orientation, and of 3.5 and 2.9 Å for brookite (120) and (121), respectively; all values are in favorable agreement with XRD analysis. The band structure of the BK-TiO $ _2 $  was determined by UV-visible spectroscopy and photoelectron yield spectroscopy. A detailed discussion is provided in the Supporting Information, and the corresponding energy-level diagram of each layer in the plastic PSC is illustrated in Figure 3d. Notably, the conduction band position of BK-TiO $ _2 $  is  $ -3.91 $  eV (versus vacuum), which is slightly down-shifted compared with that of anatase TiO $ _2 $  ( $ -3.86 $  eV); thus, the BK-TiO $ _2 $  scaffold still served as an efficient ETL for perovskite absorber energetically.

The thickness of BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $  scaffold was easily altered by adjusting the spin-coating speed; its effect on PV performance is outlined in Figure 3e and Table 1. One can speculate that higher spinning speeds result in thinner BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $  scaffolds. Moreover, a relatively thick BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $  scaffold created at a spinning speed of 2000 rpm (BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $ -2000) showed a relatively low average  $  J_{SC}  $  of 18.44 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , which suggests that BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $ -2000 may be too thick to extract and transfer electrons from a perovskite absorber efficiently. Meanwhile, the BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $  scaffolds made at 4000 and 6000 rpm (hereafter, BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $ -4000 and BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $ -6000, respectively) delivered almost the same level of average  $  J_{SC}  $  (  $  \approx  $  19 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). Upon closer examination, we found that the BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $ -6000 had a slightly superior  $  V_{OC}  $  and FF, hinting that the penetration of subsequent PbI  $  _{2}  $  spin-coating in the thicker scaffold formed at 4000 rpm may have been a bottleneck. Consequently, the BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $ -6000 scaffold seems to have the optimal thickness, and the six devices (Entry 4) made at this setting had an average  $  J_{SC}  $  of 19.11 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , an average  $  V_{OC}  $  of 1.07 V, an average FF of 0.75, and an average PCE of 15.29%.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, efficient plastic PSCs based on ED-TiO  $  _{x}  $  compact layers and brookite TiO  $  _{2}  $  mesoporous scaffolds were successfully fabricated on commercial ITO-PEN sheets through a low-temperature process. These structures achieved a record-high PCE of 15.76% with mitigated hysteresis, without the introduction of new materials, other than TiO  $  _{2}  $  (the pristine material when modern PSC was invented), to serve as the scaffold and CL. The keys to achieving such highly efficient devices on plastic substrates are: roughness control at each step of film coating, favorable band positions of all functional layers, and reliable hole blocking from both ED-TiO  $  _{x}  $  and BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $  contributions. Our investigation of the bending and long-term stability of this plastic PSC was markedly favorable, with the degradation patterns attributed to either the ITO-PEN or to volatile additives in the HTL. Overall, however, the core of the plastic PSC, including the ED-TiO  $  _{x}  $ , BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $ , and perovskite, maintained robust stability, as shown by the PV parameters. In short, this study introduced a straightforward, scalable, low-temperature process for the fabrication of efficient and durable plastic PSCs. Future research on these systems should focus on replacing troublesome ITO-PEN sheets, applying proper sealing materials, as well as optimizing the thickness of each functional layer, aiming for further increases the the PCE and stability.

# 4. Experimental Section

Substrate Preparation: ITO-PEN sheets (14–15   $  \Omega  $   sq  $  ^{-1}  $   Peccell Technologies, Inc.) were etched with zinc powder and diluted HCl to obtain the required electrode pattern. The sheets were then sequentially cleaned with acetone and deionized water under sonication for 30 min. Before electrodeposition, each ITO-PEN sheet was treated with UV ozone (customized, Kingo Electrical Enterprise Co., LTD.) for 5 min to remove the organic residues.

$  TiO_{x}  $   Compact-Layer Deposition: A thin   $  TiO_{x}  $   CL was anodically electrodeposited on each previously described ITO-PEN sheet. The bath contained 0.15 M   $  TiCl_{3}  $   aqueous solutions (  $  \geq  $  12%   $  TiCl_{3}  $   basis in HCl, Sigma-Aldrich) and 0.018 M ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA, Sigma-Aldrich), which served as a stabilizer to increase the solubility of   $  TiCl_{3}  $  . The electrodeposition was conducted in a three-compartment cell comprising an Ag/AgCl (3 M NaCl, 0.175 V vs normal hydrogen electrode) reference electrode, a platinum foil (1 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) counter electrode and an ITO-PEN-sheet working electrode. During the deposition, the bath was adjusted and kept at pH = 4.8 by   $  Na_{2}CO_{3}  $   under   $  N_{2}  $   purging. The deposition current was constant at 30   $  \mu  $  A cm  $  ^{-2}  $   for different times. When the electrodeposition was completed, the ITO-PEN/ED-  $  TiO_{x}  $   sheet was rinsed using deionized water and then ethanol, and finally desiccated in air.

Plastic PSC Fabrication: Each previously described ED-TiO  $  _{4}  $  -coated substrate was spin-coated with a mesoporous scaffold using a 6.5 wt% binder-free brookite suspension (PECC-B01, Peccell Technologies, Inc.) at 2000, 4000, or 6000 rpm for 30 s, and then dried at 150 °C for 30 min. Each bilayer-coated substrate was again treated with UV ozone for 3 min. PbI  $  _{2}  $   (99.9985%, Alfa Aesar) was then spin-coated onto each bilayer-coated substrate using a solution of 1.2 M PbI  $  _{2}  $   mixed with DMF (N,N-dimethylformamide, 99.8%, Merck) and DMSO (dimethyl sulfoxide, 99.9%, J. T. Baker) at 6000 rpm for 10 s, followed by drying at 70 °C for 30 min. After cooling to room temperature, the substrate was immersed into a 63 × 10  $  ^{-3}  $   M MAI (homemade)  $  ^{[34]}  $   2-propanol solution for 50 s, and then rinsed with 2-propanol, dried by gaseous nitrogen, and baked at 85 °C for 20 min, sequentially. The HTL was spin-coated onto a previously prepared photoelectrode in a solution containing 75 × 10  $  ^{-3}  $   M 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD, >99%, Lumtec, Taiwan) in chlorobenzene (99.8%, Sigma-Aldrich) at 3000 rpm for 20 s. The dopants in the HTL solution were 25 × 10  $  ^{-3}  $   M bis(trifluoromethane)-sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI, 99.95%, Sigma-Aldrich) and 120 × 10  $  ^{-3}  $   M 4-tert-butylpyridine (96%, Sigma-Aldrich). The device was subsequently stored in a dry atmosphere (temperature: 25 °C, relative humidity: 10%) overnight, after which the gold terminals were thermally evaporated through a stainless steel shadow mask.

Characterization and Measurement: The structures of the ED-TiO  $  _{x}  $  CL and the BK-TiO  $  _{2}  $  scaffold were confirmed by XRD (Ultima IV, Rigaku Corp., Japan). Film morphology was investigated by FESEM (Hitachi SU-8010, Japan) and AFM (C3000, Nanosurf, Switzerland), UV–vis transmittance spectra of the ITO-PEN substrate with and without an ED-TiO  $  _{x}  $  CL were measured by a UV–vis spectrophotometer (U3900, Hitachi, Japan). The crystal phase contrasts of the TiO  $  _{2}  $  scaffold layers were performed with TEM (ARM200F, Jeol, Japan), and the work function of the material was determined by a photoelectron yield spectroscopy system (BIP-KV201, Bunkou Keiki Co. Ltd., Japan). IPCE were measured using home-built system, in which current was preamplied by a current amplifier (SR570) and measured by a source meter (Keithley 2400). It should be noted that 10 sets of  $  J_{SC}  $  (interval 50 ms) were collected sequentially after illuminating the device for 3 s and then averaged for calculation of IPCE. A 300 W Xe lamp (Model 6258, Newport Oriel) combined with an Oriel cornerstone 260 1/4 m monochromator (Model 74100) provided a monochromatic beam (dc mode) for the device under test conditions. The beam power intensity was calibrated with a power meter (Model 1936-C, Newport Oriel) equipped with a Newport 818-UV photodetector. PL spectra were scanned using commercial fluorometer (Edinburgh, FLS920).
