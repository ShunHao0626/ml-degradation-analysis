# ABSTRACT

High performance perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with p-i-n device architecture often employ poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine (PTAA) as the hole-transporting layer. However, PTAA has a low intrinsic carrier concentration and generally requires dopants to enhance the conductivity, but most dopants will induce deleterious effects especially on the stability and hysteresis behavior of the devices. Here, we synthesize a novel conjugated carbazole-based molecule (TCzP) as dopant of PTAA to improve the conductivity and stability simultaneously for high-performance PSCs. Specifically, TCzP contributes to the reduction of energy barrier between PTAA and perovskite and to the formation of high-quality perovskite films owing to its intrinsic hydrophilicity for better association with the polar perovskite precursor. Therefore, PSCs by TCzP-doping exhibit significantly improved power conversion efficiency up to 18.3% in comparison to 16.0% of the device using dopant-free PTAA. Moreover, TCzP-doped PTAA leads to lower hysteresis and much better long-term stability up to 500 h of the PSCs in air. We believe this work would pave a new way towards high-performance inverted PSCs

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have received enormous attention and made breathtaking developments over the past decade due to the superior optoelectronic properties of perovskite materials, such as high optical absorption coefficients, small exciton binding energy, tunable band gaps, outstanding charge-carrier transport, and long charge diffusion lengths [1-7]. Inverted PSCs with p-i-n structure are even more attractive because of their facile fabrication and excellent steady-state output [8-14] with the aid of hole-transporting materials to reduce charge recombination and facilitate hole collection for significant enhancements in the open-circuit voltage  $ (V_{oc}) $  and fill factor (FF) of the devices [15].

Among various hole-transporting materials, poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine (PTAA) has been widely adopted as the hole-transporting layer (HTL) owing to its favorable hydrophobicity and strong physicochemical interactions with perovskites [16,17]. However, PTAA, which is an amorphous polymer, generally suffers from low intrinsic carrier concentrations and thus low conductivity [18]. Also, the strong hydrophobicity of PTAA leads to high charge recombination losses in the PSCs [19]. Moreover, the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level of PTAA (~5.2 eV) is lower than that of perovskite layer (~5.8 eV), resulting in much energy losses and inferior PCE performance. To achieve high-performance PSCs, various dopants have been introduced into PTAA to increase its conductivity and thereby enhanced charge transport [16,20]. For example, Huang et al. firstly used conjugated F4-TCNQ as a dopant of PTAA in PSCs and successfully improved PCE by reducing the device's series resistance [21]. Today, inverted PSCs based on F4-TCNQ dopant have reached a recorded PCE of 21% [16]. Moreover, Ko et al. used Li-TFSI/t-BP as PTAA dopants to achieve high-performance PSCs with maximum PCEs up to 16.47% [22]; Luo et al. employed a fluorine-containing hydrophobic Lewis acid dopant (LAD) in PTAA-based n-i-p PSCs to get a PCE of 19.01% [23]. However, F4-TCNQ is very expensive ($7000/g) and poorly soluble in common solvents [24,25]. Li-TFSI is hygroscopic and highly soluble, which may accelerate the degradation of HTL and perovskites [23,26]. Also, Li ions will increase hysteresis effects [18]. The low boiling point of t-BP may lead to high evaporation during PSC operation or storage, resulting in low stability of the devices [26]. Furthermore, t-BP also corrodes the perovskite materials for even worse device stability in practical application environments [27]. LAD with high hydrophobicity will induce poor perovskite growth in inverted PSC structure [23], leading to high defect density of the perovskite films. Therefore, developing easily-available and high-performance dopants for PTAA is critical to overcome these challenges described above for fabricating highly efficient and stable inverted PSCs.

Conjugated molecules, with rich and highly tunable molecular structures and photoelectric properties, have been widely used in optoelectronic devices   $  [28]  $  . However, few conjugated dopants have been added to PTAA to obtain improved PCEs with good device stability. Herein, a novel conjugated carbazole-based molecule, 9-(diphenylphosphanyl)-3-(1,2,2-triphenylvinyl)-9H-carbazole (TCzP), was synthesized and explored as a novel dopant of PTAA. The conjugated carbazole molecule can facilitate charge transport by eliminating the energy level mismatch between the perovskite and HTL. More importantly, TCzP with polar phosphine group can induce the growth of high quality perovskite films with suppressed non-radiative recombination due to its low hydrophobicity and favorable interactions with the polar perovskite precursor. As a result, inverted PSCs with an FF of 0.79 and PCE up to 18.3% were achieved, which are among the best performances of the reported inverted PSCs using novel dopants. Furthermore, the

# 2. Results and discussion

To develop a high-performance conjugated dopant for PTAA, we introduce a triphenylethylene unit into a carbazole core to form the basic conjugated structure with high hole conductivity and then grafted a diphenylphosphine unit via direct N–P coupling to adjust the energy levels and charge transport properties of the conjugated molecule (Fig. 1a). Thus designed conjugated molecule of TCzP was facilely synthesized in three steps with high total yield of 56% (Scheme S1). Structural characterizations were established on the basis of   $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR,   $  {}^{13}  $  C NMR and HRMS (Figs. S1–S3). We estimated the axis length of TCzP is 13.5–18.2 Å, which is slightly longer than that of unit of PTAA (9.5–10.3 Å). Therefore TCzP with a suitable size comparable to that of PTAA unit, shows good dispersibility after doping in PTAA with enhanced conductivity for charge transport. From the absorption spectra and steady-state photoluminescence (PL) in Fig. 1b, TCzP film efficiently absorbs light from 250 to 400 nm, and the emission peaked at 490 nm is in the range of perovskite's absorption [29]. Therefore, TCzP can absorb ultraviolet light and convert it to visible light, while perovskite film with a higher external quantum efficiency (EQE) in the visible range results in better light-harvesting for increased short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) [8].

It is well known that the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level between PTAA (-5.2 eV) and that of perovskite (-5.8 eV) is unmatched with a high energy level barrier up to 0.6 eV, leading to a high energy losses for inferior performance [30]. The HOMO energy level of TCzP layer was measured to be -5.5 eV by cyclic voltammetry (CV) (Fig. 1c and Fig. S4); this is very close to the perovskite's valence band. Density functional theory (DFT) computation of TCzP based on the optimized ground state geometry (Fig. 1d and 1e) was also performed to reveal the key molecular design in reaching the ideal HOMO energy level. The calculated HOMO matches well with the CV measurement and the frontier orbital electronic density distributions show that the carbazole core and triphenylethylene unit dominate HOMO and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) is mainly determined by triphenylethylene unit (Fig. 1d and 1e, Fig. S4). Without diphenylphosphine grafting, much higher HOMO up to -5.0 eV will be resulted (Fig. S5), owing to the strong electron donating property of both carbazole and triphenylethylene. These findings indicate that TCzP inherits high hole mobility and an excellent charge transport capacity from carbazole core and triphenylethylene unit, while diphenylphosphine grafting effectively tune the HOMO to the suitable range for efficient hole transporting of PSCs.

In light of the well-designed energy level and hole mobility of TCzP, we doped it into PTAA to adjust the HOMO energy level and conductivity of the HTL for improved photovoltaic performance of PSCs. TCzP with high solubility in chlorobenzene can be facilely mixed at the desired TCzP/PTAA mass ratios to make the TCzP-doped PTAA HTLs. The conductivities of the dopant-free and 1 wt% TCzP-doped PTAA films were determined from the current-voltage characteristics of devices prepared from ITO/(dopant-free and 1 wt% TCzP-doped PTAA)/Al (Fig. S6). The conductivity values of the dopant-free and 1 wt% TCzP-doped PTAA were  $ 7.30\mathrm{d}^{-4} $  and  $ 1.50\mathrm{AA}^{-3}\mathrm{mScm}^{-1} $ , respectively. The significantly improved charge transporting capability of the TCzP-doped PTAA was further investigated using the space charge limited current (SCLC) method based on the hole-only devices with the structure of ITO/dopant-free PTAA (1 wt% TCzP-doped PTAA)/MoO₃/Al structures (Fig. 1f). Encouraged by these advantages of the perovskite films on the TCzP-doped PTAA surface, we fabricated the related PSCs with the following device structure: ITO/HTL/(CsPbI $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.05} $  [(FAPbI $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.85} $  (MAPbBr $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.15} $ ] $ _{0.95} $ /PCBM/C $ _{60} $ /LiF/Al. The elemental molar ratio of fresh perovskite film was confirmed by XPS measurement, which is close to the stoichiometric value (Fig. S10 and Table S1). The device architecture and energy level diagram are illustrated in Fig. 3a and b, respectively; PCBM and C $ _{60} $  act as the electron transport materials, and LiF serves as the electron-injecting materials. The control device based on dopant-free PTAA shows a PCE of 16.0% with the  $ V_{oc} $  and  $ J_{sc} $  of 1.08 V and 19.47 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , respectively. Excitingly, the performance of the TCzP-based PSCs is significantly improved, and the best doping concentration is 1 wt% (Fig. S11 and Table S2). The champion device showed a high PCE up to 18.3% with a  $ J_{sc} $  of 21.19 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ,  $ V_{oc} $  of 1.10 V, and FF of 0.79 (Table 1). To the best of our knowledge, this PCE is among the best of an inverted PSC based on a novel PTAA dopant reported to date. The improved shunt resistances ( $ R_{sh} $ ) and series resistances ( $ R_s $ ) calculated by J-V curves prove the reason of increased FF and  $ J_{sc} $  (Table 1) [9]. However, the further increase of the TCzP concentration (2 wt%) slows down the charge transport, leading to the increased  $ R_s $  and lowered PCE (Table S2). Meanwhile, the device based on widely utilized F4-TCNQ was fabricated and characterized. It was found that the PCE of the TCzP-based device outperforms the device based on F4-TCNQ dopant (17.4%) (Fig. S12). Thus our designed conjugated dopant can be as an alternative to the widely utilized dopants. Fig. 2c shows the J-V curves of the PSCs based on dopant-free and 1 wt% TCzP-doped PTAA HTL in different scan directions. The TCzP-doped device exhibits negligible hysteresis with a hysteresis factor ( $ \frac{\text{PCE}_{\text{reverse}} - \text{PCE}_{\text{forward}}}{\text{PCE}_{\text{reverse}}} $ ) of 0.028, while the control device shows higher hysteresis (0.040). The reduced hysteresis should be due to the suppressed ion migration by the TCzP-doping (Fig. 2f). To ensure the reliability of the J-V curves, the stabilized and steady  $ J_{sc} $  and PCE at the maximum power point of the optimized device were measured with a bias of 0.93 V under continuous illumination for 100 s (Fig. 3e). The optimal device shows a steady-state  $ J_{sc} $  of 19.50 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  with a stabilized PCE up to 18.1%. These values are close to the  $ J_{sc} $  and PCE obtained from the J-V measurements. Meanwhile, the external quantum efficiency (EQE) was

From the dark  $ J-V $  curves for the inverted PSCs (Fig. 4c), the addition of TCzP to PTAA suppresses the leakage current significantly, which should be due to the decreased charge recombination. To further understand the charge recombination losses, the dependence of the  $ V_{oc} $  on the light-intensity was measured (Fig. 4d). It is known that when the slope of the  $ V_{oc} $  versus light intensity curve is near kT/q, bimolecular recombination is the dominant loss mechanism. If the slope is greater than kT/q, there is an additional trap-assisted Shockley-Read-Hall (SRH) recombination [34]. The slopes of the devices based on the dopant-free and 1 wt% TCzP-doped PTAA are 1.23 kT/q and 1.18 kT/q, respectively. This result indicates that trap-assisted recombination was lower in the PSCs based on TCzP-doped PTAA, leading to a higher PCE. To evaluate the extent of charge recombination in the devices, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was performed at bias of 0.8 V under dark conditions to detect the charge transport resistance [35–38]. # 3. Conclusions

In summary, a novel conjugated molecule of TCzP was designed and facilely synthesized and used as the dopant of PTAA aimed to enhance the conductivity and eliminate the energy level mismatch between the perovskite and HTL. Interestingly, the TCzP-doping of PTAA also improves the quality of perovskite films, leading to larger grain sizes and lower surface roughness. Thus, the hole extraction was improved and non-radiative recombination was effectively suppressed in the TCzP-based devices. As a result, a high PCE of  $ 18.3\% $  was achieved in the inverted PSCs based on TCzP-doped (1 wt%) PTAA. Compared to the control PSC using pure PTAA, the TCzP-based PSC exhibits relatively lower J-V hysteresis and much better long-term stability up to  $ 500\mathrm{h} $ . These exciting Results by the first attempt using conjugated molecules as dopant of PTAA not only highlight the great potential of this new strategy in developing HTL dopants, but also shed new insights into the perovskite crystal growth, ion migration suppression and energy level modulation of the PSCs, providing important clues for the development
