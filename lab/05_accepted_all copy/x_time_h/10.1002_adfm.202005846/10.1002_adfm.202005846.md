# Interfacial Structure and Composition Managements for High-Performance Methylammonium-Free Perovskite Solar Cells

Shunde Li, Zhou Liu, Zhi Qiao, Xiao Wang, Lei Cheng, Yufeng Zhai, Qiaofei Xu, Zhimin Li, Ke Meng, and Gang Chen*

The methylammonium (MA)-free perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have drawn broad attention due to their excellent thermostability. However, the efficiency of these devices is inferior to most state-of-the-art PSCs. Herein, the photovoltaic performance of the MA-free PSCs is enhanced by constructing interfacial capping layers with a pair of alkylammonium halides, n-propylammonium (PA) iodide and propane-1,3-diammonium (PDA) iodide. The structure and composition of the interfacial layers are comprehensively investigated and their correlation with the device performance is presented in terms of defect passivation efficacy, energy level alignment, and hydrophobicity for moisture resistance. The PSC devices based on the PAI and  $ \mathrm{PDAI}_2 $  treated MA-free perovskite films demonstrate better power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) and stabilities than the reference devices without the interfacial layers. Although the PAI-treated devices exhibit the highest PCE of  $ 21.1\% $ , the  $ \mathrm{PDAI}_2 $ -treated PSCs demonstrate the exceptional thermal and humidity stabilities.

# 1. Introduction

Over the past decade, metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have rapidly become a star photovoltaic technology with power conversion efficiency (PCE) boosting from 3.8% to more than 25%.  $  ^{[1,2]}  $   The facile solution-processed fabrication method also makes PSCs a promising commercial solar cell contender.  $  ^{[3]}  $   The organic cations methylammonium (MA) and formamidinium (FA) are extensively used in all the major state-of-the-art PSCs.  $  ^{[2,4]}  $   Although the optical bandgap of FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (1.45–1.48 eV in thin films)  $  ^{[5,6]}  $   is closer to the theoretically optimal bandgap (1.34 eV for semiconductors)  $  ^{[7,8]}  $   than that of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (1.58 eV)  $  ^{[9]}  $  , the undesirable conversion from the perovskite phase (  $  \alpha  $  -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) to the photoinactive phase (  $  \delta  $  -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) at room temperature has restrained the direct application of FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   in PSCs.  $  ^{[10,11]}  $   The   $  \alpha  $  -to-  $  \delta  $   phase transition has been

So far, a variety of ammonium halides have been utilized to form interfacial capping layers at the perovskite and charge transport layer interfaces to passivate defects and improve the efficiency and stability of PSCs. For instance, the wide bandgap 2D perovskite layers could be formed by spin-coating the solutions of n-butylammonium iodide,  $  ^{[23]}  $   phenethylammonium iodide (PEAI),  $  ^{[24]}  $   phenyltrimethylammonium iodide,  $  ^{[25]}  $   n-hexylammonium bromide  $  ^{[26]}  $   and 1,6-diaminohexane ammonium iodide  $  ^{[27]}  $   on the perovskite films. # 2. Results and Discussion

The 3D MA-free perovskite films with the composition of  $ \mathrm{Rb}_{0.05}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.1}\mathrm{FA}_{0.85}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  (denoted as RbCsFA) are fabricated by the one-step anti-solvent assisted deposition method, where the  $ \mathrm{Rb^{+}} $  and  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $  ions are used to stabilize the  $ \alpha $ -FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite. $ ^{[12]} $  To introduce an interfacial capping layer, the solution of the alkylammonium halide (PAI or PDAI $ _2 $ ) in isopropanol (IPA) is spin-coated onto the RbCsFA perovskite film. A schematic diagram of the PSC device structure and the post-treatment procedure is shown in Figure 1a. The SEM images in Figure 1b-d show that the perovskite films are composed of grains with the size of  $ \approx 300~\mathrm{nm} $  and their surface morphologies are modified following the alkylammonium halide treatments. In Figure 1f, two new diffraction arcs appear at q = 0.34 and   $  0.68 \, \AA^{-1}  $   (marked by the red arrows) after the PAI treatment. As previously reported, the 3D perovskite films would undergo a 3D to 2D transformation following the alkylammonium halide treatment.  $  ^{[23,35]}  $   The XRD peaks of   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{FAPb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{7}  $   in Figure 1h match exactly with the new diffraction peaks of the PAI-treated film, indicating the 2D   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{FAPb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{7}  $   perovskite capping layer is formed over the RbCsFA film. In contrast, the diffraction arcs in Figure 1g and the integrated peaks in Figure 1i located at q = 0.40, 0.81, and   $  1.22 \, \AA^{-1}  $   (marked by the black arrows) of the PDAI  $  _{2}  $  -treated film do not match the diffraction patterns of the PDA-based 2D perovskites (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The appearance of the low-angle diffraction peaks also rules out the formation of a 3D perovskite structure.  $  ^{[41]}  $   It has been found that the organic-inorganic hybrid material can be formed by the short-chain alkyl diammonium molecules and lead halide.  $  ^{[42]}  $   Instead of forming 2D layered perovskite motif, the hybrid material has the infinite 1D ribbons of corner-sharing PbI  $  _{6}  $   octahedra which are surrounded by diammonium molecules and isolated halide atoms. In this case, an additional   $  \cdot NH_{2}  $   group in the diammonium PDA  $  ^{2+}  $   (compared to the monoammonium PA  $  ^{+}  $  ) can interact with the isolated iodide atom through the N–H…I hydrogen bonding, which promotes the 1D structure formation.  $  ^{[42]}  $   In Figure 1i, the organic–inorganic hybrid of   $  (\mathrm{PDA})_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{10}  $   exhibits the characteristic diffraction peaks at 0.40, 0.81, and   $  1.22 \, \AA^{-1}  $  , matching exactly with the additional diffraction peaks for the PDAI  $  _{2}  $  -treated film.

When the X-ray incident angle is set to 0.05°, the crystallographic details of the perovskite films at shallow surface can be obtained.  $  ^{[39,40]}  $   Figure S2 (Supporting Information) shows the 2D GI-XRD patterns of the perovskite film taken at 0.05°. Apparently, the diffraction intensities of   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{FAPb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{7}  $   and   $  (\mathrm{PDA})_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{10}  $   are higher than those of   $  \alpha  $  -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  . From the relative intensities of the XRD peaks taken at different incident angles (different penetration depths) and the peak area ratios of   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{FAPb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{7}  $   at 4.8° and   $  (\mathrm{PDA})_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{10}  $   at 5.6° to the peak for RbCsFA at 14.1° in Figure S3 (Supporting Information), it can be concluded that the newly formed   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{FAPb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{7}  $   and   $  (\mathrm{PDA})_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{10}  $   locate mainly near the surfaces of the RbCsFA perovskite films. The GI-XRD results obtained at various incident angles are in good accordance with the SEM measurements (Figure 1b-d), showing surface

capping layers formed following the alkylammonium halide treatments.

While the GI-XRD technique can provide abundant crystallographic information, the PL technique is more sensitive to the low crystallinity contents. The requirement for producing clear XRD signals is that the irradiated sample has a wide range of periodic structures, which is not strictly required for generating PL signals. As shown in Figure 1j, the PL peak at 806 nm corresponding to the RbCsFA perovskite stays unvaried after the alkylammonium halide treatments. There are three new peaks developed at 580, 639, and 681 nm following the PAI treatment, which are ascribed to the PL emissions from the 2D (PA) $ _{2} $ (FA) $ _{n-1} $ Pb $ _{n} $ I $ _{3n+1} $  perovskites with  $ n = 2 $ , 3, and 4, respectively. Since only the diffraction signal of (PA) $ _{2} $ FAPb $ _{2} $ I $ _{7} $  is detected in the GI-XRD measurement (Figure 1h), it suggests that the 2D capping layer mainly contains the  $ n = 2 $  perovskite crystals with the  $ n = 3 $  and 4 perovskites dispersed around. It is attributed to the fact that the  $ n = 2 $  perovskite is kinetically more favorable. $ ^{[43,44]} $  In contrast, there is no additional PL emission peak appearing in the PDAI $ _{2} $ -treated film (Figure 1j). Compared with the reference film, the PAI and PDAI $ _{2} $ -treated films show 4.6 and 2.2 times increase in the PL intensities of the RbCsFA perovskite, revealing the reduction of defects and trap states that suppresses the non-radiative recombination. $ ^{[45]} $

Next, the effect of the alkylammonium halide treatments on photovoltaic performances is investigated by fabricating the PSC devices in the configuration of indium tin oxide (ITO) /SnO $ _2 $ /perovskite/2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-pmethoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) /Au (for more details see Methods). The typical current density-voltage (J-V) curves and the detailed photovoltaic parameters of the champion devices are shown in Figure 2a and Table 1. Under the standard AM 1.5G solar illumination, the champion PCEs of 18.9%, 21.1%, and 19.8% are obtained for the reference, PAI-treated, and PDAI $ _2 $ -treated devices, respectively. The substantial enhancements in PCEs initiated by the PAI and PDAI $ _2 $  treatments are reflected mainly in the gains of  $ V_{oc} $  (see Figure 2b and Figure S4, Supporting Information). Compared to the reference device ( $ V_{oc} $  = 1.00 V), the champion devices attained from the PAI and PDAI $ _2 $  treatments have  $ V_{oc} $  of 1.10 and 1.04 V, respectively. The short-circuit current density ( $ J_{sc} $ ) is barely changed for the reference (24.08 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ), PAI-treated (24.30 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ) and PDAI $ _2 $ -treated (24.35 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ) devices. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) and integrated current density plotted as a function of wavelengths are shown in Figure 2c. The integrated  $ J_{sc} $  derived from the EQE spectra are 23.09, 23.55, and 23.48 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  for the reference, PAI-treated and PDAI $ _2 $ -treated devices respectively, which are consistent with the values obtained from the J-V curves in Figure 2a. The J-V curves taken in the forward and reverse scanning directions are given in Figure S5 (Supporting Information), which shows the reduction of hysteresis following the PAI and PDAI $ _2 $  treatments. The stabilized maximum power outputs of the champion devices are recorded in Figure 2d. Compared to the reference device (17.3%), the elevated PCEs of 20.9% and 19.2% are achieved for the PAI and PDAI $ _2 $ -treated devices. The device photovoltaic performance improvements following the alkylammonium halide treatments are consistent with the PL results, which is related to the reduction of defects and trap states in the RbCsFA perovskite films.

To understand the charge transport kinetics in the devices, the energy level alignments between the perovskite films and charge transport layers is studied. The optical bandgaps are obtained from the ultraviolet–visible (UV–vis) absorption spectra by applying the Kubelka–Munk method.  $  ^{[51,52]}  $   In Figure 3d-f, the absorption band edges of the reference, PAI-treated and PDAI  $  _{2}  $  -treated films are found to be 1.54 eV, ascribed to the RbCsFA perovskite (consistent with the PL results in Figure 1j). In addition, the band edges (  $  E_{g}  $  ) at 2.11 and 2.71 eV observed for the PAI and PDAI  $  _{2}  $  -treated films are attributed to the (PA)  $  _{2}  $  FAPb  $  _{2}  $  I  $  _{7}  $   and (PDA)  $  _{3}  $  Pb  $  _{2}  $  I  $  _{10}  $   capping layers. The valence band maximum (VBM) values are acquired from ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS), as shown in Figure 3g. The VBM values of the reference, PAI-treated and PDAI  $  _{2}  $  -treated films are -5.83, -5.66, and -5.81 eV, respectively. Accordingly, the conduction band minimum (CBM) values of the reference

and the PAI and  $ \mathrm{PDAI}_2 $ -treated films are 4.29, 3.55 and  $ 3.10\mathrm{eV} $  respectively, as calculated by  $ \mathrm{CBM} = \mathrm{VBM} - E_{\mathrm{g}} $ . The energy level alignments between perovskite and charge transport layers are schematically constructed in Figure 3h-i. It is evident that the photo-excited electrons in the conduction band of RbCsFA would be effectively blocked from transferring to HTL due to the higher CBM values of the  $ (\mathrm{PA})_2\mathrm{FAPb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_7 $  and  $ (\mathrm{PDA})_3\mathrm{Pb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{10} $  capping layers. Whereas the  $ \mathrm{PDAI}_2 $ -treated film retains the similar VBM as RbCsFA, the up-shifted VBM of  $ (\mathrm{PA})_2\mathrm{FAPb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_7 $  leads to a preferable energy level gradient between RbCsFA and HTL, which would facilitate the hole extraction and transferring. The improved hole transferring capability of the PAI-treated PSC has generated better photovoltaic performance than the  $ \mathrm{PDAI}_2 $ -treated device.

The excellent moisture resistance introduced by forming the (PDA) $ _{3} $ Pb $ _{2} $ I $ _{10} $  capping layer accounts for the tremendous stability improvement of the PDAI $ _{2} $ -treated device. Interestingly, the PDA cation was also found to be an effective in-bulk passivation agent. $ ^{[34]} $

In addition, the device retains 94% of its initial PCE in ambient air with RH = 50–60% for 192 h (Figure S9, Supporting Information), which is slightly higher than the PAI-treated device but lower than the PDAI  $  _{2}  $  -treated device.

Overall, the underlying mechanisms of the alkylammonium halide treatments to improve the efficiency and stability of PSCs are schematically illustrated in Figure 5. A 2D perovskite capping layer mainly composed of   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{FAPb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{7}  $   is formed after the PAI treatment, while the   $  PDAI_{2}  $   treatment facilitates the formation of the   $  (\mathrm{PDA})_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{10}  $   capping layer with no FA cation. The efficiency enhancements are due in part to the photoexcited electrons being blocked from transferring to HTL by the   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{FAPb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{7}  $   and   $  (\mathrm{PDA})_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{10}  $   capping layers as illustrated in Figure 5. Compared to the   $  PDAI_{2}  $   treatment, the PAI-treated film contains less defects and trap states and forms a preferable energy level gradient for hole transportation, which leads to the better diode ideal factor and the enhanced   $  V_{oc}  $   and PCE. The inferior PCE of the   $  PDAI_{2}  $  -treated device is also due to the stronger exciton binding energy of the 1D   $  (\mathrm{PDA})_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{10}  $   layer than that of the 2D   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{FAPb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{7}  $   layer.  $  ^{[57,58]}  $   On the other hand, the   $  (\mathrm{PDA})_{3}\mathrm{Pb}_{2}\mathrm{I}_{10}  $   capping layer is more effective in blocking water molecules from entering the RbCsFA perovskite film, which gives rise to better device stabilities.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, the highly efficient and stable MA-free PSCs are fabricated by constructing the interfacial capping layers at the RbCsFA perovskite and HTL interfaces with a pair of alkylammonium halides. By spin-coating the solutions of PAI and  $ \mathrm{PDAI}_2 $ , the  $ (\mathrm{PA})_2\mathrm{FAPb}_2\mathrm{I}_7 $  and  $ (\mathrm{PDA})_3\mathrm{Pb}_2\mathrm{I}_{10} $  capping layers are formed over the RbCsFA perovskite films, resulting in substantial improvements in device photovoltaic performances. The device PCEs have been enhanced from  $ 18.9\% $  for the reference to  $ 21.1\% $  and  $ 19.8\% $  for the PAI and  $ \mathrm{PDAI}_2 $ -treated PSCs, mainly ascribed to the improvements in  $ V_{oc} $ . The efficiency enhancements are due to the reduction of defects and trap states in the RbCsFA films and the intermediate energy bands installed between perovskite and HTL by the  $ (\mathrm{PA})_2\mathrm{FAPb}_2\mathrm{I}_7 $  and

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: All the reagents and chemicals were used as received, including PbI₂ (99.99%, Tokyo Chemical Industry Co., Ltd), formamidinium iodide (FAI, 99.5%, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.), cesium iodide (CsI, 99.999%, Sigma-Aldrich), rubidium iodide (RbI, 99.8%, abcr GmbH), n-propylammonium iodide (PAI, 97.0%, TCI) and propane-1,3-diammonium iodide (PDAI₂, 98.0%, TCI), tin (IV) oxide colloidal dispersion (SnO₂, 15% in H₂O colloidal dispersion, Alfa Aesar), 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-pmethoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene(Spiro-OMeTAD, Borun, LTD.), N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), chlorobenzene, 4-tert-butylpyridine, Li-TFSI and acetonitrile were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich.

Devices Fabrication: The ITO substrates were cleaned by the sequential sonication in detergent, deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol (IPA) for 15 min respectively. After that, the substrates were dried in   $  N_{2}  $   flow and cleaned by the UV–ozone treatment for 10 min before use. The tin (IV) oxide colloidal dispersion precursor solution was diluted by   $  H_{2}O  $   to 2.67% which was filtered by the 0.22   $  \mu  $  m syringe filter before use and then spin-coated onto ITO substrates at 3000 rpm for 30 s, and finally baked on a hot plate at 150 °C for 15 min in ambient air. This process is repeated after the sample is cooled to obtain the ITO/  $  SnO_{2}  $   substrates. The   $  Rb_{0.05}Cs_{0.1}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $   (the concentration of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   is 1.1 M) precursor solutions were prepared in a stoichiometric ratio of 5:10:85:110 for Rbl, CsI, FAI, and   $  PbI_{2}  $   in anhydrous DMF:DMSO 4:1 (v:v). The perovskite solution was spin-coated in a two-step program (10 s at 1000 rpm, ramp at 200 rpm s  $  ^{-1}  $   and 20 s at 4000 rpm, ramp at 2000 rpm s  $  ^{-1}  $  ). During the second step, 200   $  \mu  $  L of chlorobenzene was dropped on the spinning substrate 5 s prior to the end of the program. The substrates were then annealed at 100 °C for 10 min in a nitrogen-filled glove box. For alkylammonium halide treatments, the PAI and   $  PDAI_{2}  $   powder is dissolved in IPA to form 4 and 1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   solutions and spin-coated onto the perovskite surface at a spin rate of 4000 rpm s  $  ^{-1}  $   without further processing. The HTL layer was formed by spin-coating the Spiro-OMeTAD precursor solution at 3000 rpm for 30 s. The Spiro-OMeTAD solution was prepared by dissolving 72.3 mg of Spiro-OMeTAD, 17.5   $  \mu  $  L of a stock solution of 520 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   LiTFSI/acetonitrile, and 28.8   $  \mu  $  L of tBP in 1 mL of chlorobenzene. Finally, a 60 nm thick gold film was thermally evaporated as the metal electrode under a pressure of   $  1 \times 10^{-4}  $   Pa on top of the Spiro-OMeTAD film to form the back contact by using a shadow mask to pattern the electrode.

Characterizations: The SEM images were taken by a Joel 7800 SEM. The GI-XRD data were recorded at the BL14Bl beamline of Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF). The photon energy was 10 keV (wavelength = 1.2398 Å) at incident angles range from 0.05° to 1° and the diffracted X-rays were collected by the 2D Mar225 CCD detector. The powder XRD spectra (  $  \theta  $  -2  $  \theta  $  scans) were taken on a D8 powder X-ray diffraction system (PANalytical Inc.) with Cu K  $  \alpha  $  (  $  \lambda  $  = 1.5418 Å) as the

X-ray source. Photoluminescence (PL) signal was analyzed by a Horiba Jobin Yvon system. Absorption spectra were recorded using an Agilent Technologies Cary Series UV–vis–NIR Spectrophotometer. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopic (UPS) analyses were carried out using thermoFisher ESCALANTM 250Xi. The contact angle measurements were assessed by a drop shape analyzer (KINO, SL200KS) at ambient temperature. The J–V curves were measured by a Keithley 2400 source meter under AM 1.5 G illumination at 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  using a SS-F5-3A (Enli tech) solar simulator. The light intensity was calibrated using a certified silicon diode. The effective area of the device was measured to be 4.85 mm  $  ^{2}  $ . All the devices were measured without preconditioning such as light-soaking and applied bias voltage. Steady-state power conversion efficiency was calculated by measuring stabilized photocurrent density under constant bias voltage. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) was measured using specially designed system (Enli tech) under DC mode.
