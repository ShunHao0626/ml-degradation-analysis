# Carbon-Based Stable CsPbIBr  $  _{2}  $  Solar Cells with Efficiency of over 10% from Bifunctional Quinoline Sulfate Modification

Deng Wang, Wenjing Li, Xuping Liu, Guodong Li, Lei Zhang, Ruoshui Li, Weihai Sun, Jihuai Wu, and Zhang Lan  $  ^{*}  $

Cite This: ACS Appl. Energy Mater. 2021, 4, 5747–5755

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: The mixed-halide   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   all-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted widespread attention in view of their excellent stability and suitable band gap. However, the inherent high traps in perovskite films lead to serious nonradiative recombination and limit their future development. In this study, a passivation strategy is proposed to prepare   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSC with high efficiency, excellent long-term stability, and low hysteresis, which uses the in situ formed   $  PbSO_{4}  $   protective layer and the passivation of undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   defects by introducing quinoline sulfate (QS). Due to the effective passivation, the QS-passivated   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   film with a larger grain size exhibits a better humidity resistance compared with the control one. The champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the carbon-based PSC without a hole transporter reaches 10.41% with an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.298 V. Especially, it can retain over 98% of the primeval PCE after nearly 1000 h of aging in the ambient atmosphere.

Therefore, our work may promote the application of all-inorganic PSC in commercial development.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has increased from 3.8 to 25.5% in the last decade, with the achievement of dazzling results and initial commercialization.  $  ^{1,2}  $   However, the long-term stability issue is the key factor hindering the complete commercialization of PSCs. Usually, the skeleton of mixed-halide perovskite contains hydrophilic organic cations (e.g.,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}  $  ), which can reduce the stability when stimulated by external conditions such as heat, oxygen, and moisture.  $  ^{3-5}  $   Partial or complete replacement of organic cations by inorganic cesium ions has been proved to be an effective way to improve device stability. The all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{x}Br_{3-x}  $   (x is in the range of 0–3) perovskite has the characteristics of high electron mobility and excellent thermal stability, which have attracted the attention of various researchers.  $  ^{6,7}  $   Among them,   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   has a light absorption capacity similar to that of hybrid perovskites and a suitable band gap of   $  \sim1.73  $   eV. Zhao et al. proved that introducing an effective volatile additive dimethylammonium iodide (DMAI) can deliver an efficiency of over 19%.  $  ^{8}  $   Nevertheless,   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   is prone to phase change, which leads to unstable device performance.  $  ^{9}  $   Tang et al. studied   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   in depth and proved that it has excellent phase stability and good humidity resistance; what is unsatisfactory is that the wider band gap (  $  \sim2.3  $   eV) results in poor photoelectric conversion efficiency.  $  ^{10-12}  $   Recently, mixed-halide perovskites   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   with bromide

The degradation of perovskite is usually caused by surface or bulk defects that are highly reactive to water and oxygen, which leads to a decrease in device performance.  $  ^{19-21}  $   Many organic molecules and polymers have been used to passivate the surface defects of the perovskite film and achieve superior results.  $  ^{22,23}  $   For example, fullerene derivative PCBM has been widely used to improve device efficiency and stability.  $  ^{24-27}  $   The poorly coordinated Pb positions on the surface can be passivated by Lewis basic electron donors (such as thiophene and pyridine), thereby reducing the defects and improving the performance.  $  ^{28-31}  $

Inspired by these works, we proposed a passivation strategy by introducing quinoline sulfate (QS). On the one hand, the   $  SO_{4}^{2-}  $   ions can react with   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions on the surface of the perovskite to form an inorganic   $  PbSO_{4}  $   passivation-protective layer, which not only passivates internal defects but also improves the stability; on the other hand, the quinoline moieties can further passivate the defects of undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions or Pb clusters. Compared with the pristine   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   film, the QS-treated   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   film (  $  CsPbIBr_{2}/QS  $  ) with improved grain size and crystallinity exhibits a reduced density of trap states, extended carrier lifetime, and better humidity stability. The champion PCE of PSCs with a structure of indium-doped tin oxide conductive glass (ITO)/  $  TiO_{2}/CsPbIBr_{2}/QS  $  /carbon without hole-transporter layers yields 10.41% efficiency, which is obviously higher than that of the control (8.26%). Especially, it can maintain over 98% of the original PCE after nearly 1000 h of aging in the ambient atmosphere. This work presents an effective passivator with

dual functions for preparing efficient and stable all-inorganic PSCs.

# 3. CONCLUSIONS

We developed an effective interface passivation strategy using the QS passivator to achieve high photovoltaic performance (with a considerably high PCE of 10.41%), low hysteresis, and good long-term stability of the carbon-based   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSC. The QS treatment of the perovskite film could not only in situ form a thin inorganic lead sulfate protective layer but also passivate the undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions by both sulfate and quinoline ions. SEM and atomic force microscopy (AFM) images prove that the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}/QS  $   grain sizes and crystal quality have been improved. The reduced density of trap states and extended carrier lifetime are confirmed by the measurements including TRPL, TPV, TPC, and M–S. At the same time, the formed inorganic   $  PbSO_{4}  $   passivation layer together with the modification of hydrophobic quinoline groups can effectively improve the stability of the PSC in the ambient atmosphere, which retains more than 98% of the primary PCE after   $  \sim1000  $   h storage.
