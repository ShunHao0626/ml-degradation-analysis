# Lattice-Matching Structurally-Stable 1D@3D Perovskites toward Highly Efficient and Stable Solar Cells

Peng Liu, Yeming Xian, Weinan Yuan, Yi Long, Kun Liu, Naveed Ur Rahman, Wenzhe Li,* and Jiandong Fan*

The stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been identified to be the bottleneck toward their industrialization. With the aim of tackling this challenge, a 1D  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -bipyridine (BPy)(II) perovskite is fabricated, which is shown to be capable of in situ assembly of a 1D@3D perovskite that is promoted by a  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -dimethyl sulfoxide complex with a skeletal linear chain structure. The as-prepared 1D@3D perovskite is observed to demonstrate extremely high stability under external large electric fields in humid environments by means of an in situ characterization technique. This stability is associated with its well lattice-matching heterojunction structure between 1D and 3D heterojunction domains. Importantly, ion migration is alleviated through blocking of the ion-migration channels. Accordingly, the 1D@3D hybrid PSC shows a power conversion efficiency of  $ 21.18\% $  maintaining remarkably high long-term stability in the presence of water, illumination, and external electric fields. This rational design and microstructure study of 1D@3D perovskites provides a new paradigm that may enable higher efficiency and stability of PSCs.

In the structure of perovskites, the basic building blocks of   $  [PbX_{6}]^{4-}  $   octahedra are organized in different ways to exhibit

0D, 1D, 2D, and 3D structures.  $  ^{[13-16]}  $   It should be noted that, here, the 2D, 1D, and 0D perovskites on molecular level definitions are different from the morphological 2D nanosheets, 1D nanowires, and 0D nanoparticles based on 3D APbX  $  _{3}  $   that were reported elsewhere.  $  ^{[17,18]}  $   Particularly, the 2D multilayered halide perovskites are attained by stacking n perovskite layers along the [h00] direction of the archetypal 3D structure, spaced out by long separated ammonium cations. The unit layers are stacked together by a combination force of hydrophobic and/or Coulombic to maintain the structure integrity.  $  ^{[19]}  $   Recently, the 2D and/or 2D–3D halide PSCs emerged as a promising class of stable and efficient devices, which enable to realize the efficiency as high as 16.92% and 22.1% for 2D and 2D–3D halide PSCs, respectively.  $  ^{[20,21]}  $

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with unprecedented progress have realized power conversion efficiency (PCE) approaching 25.2%, which is analogous to the state-of-art crystalline silicon solar cells.[1] However, its long-term stability in the ambient working conditions intrinsically remains extremely poor against the environmental/external restrictions, e.g., moisture, oxygen, UV light, and electric field.[2-6] The poor stability was assumed to be associated with its crystal structure, wherein the basic building blocks, i.e., metal halide  $ \left[\mathrm{PbI}_{6}\right]^{4-} $ -octahedra, were arranged in 3D structures. The smaller interstitial ions, e.g.,  $ \mathrm{MA}^{+}/\mathrm{FA}^{+}/\mathrm{Cs}^{+} $ , were embed in  $ \left[\mathrm{PbI}_{6}\right]^{4-} $ -octahedral frame by means of

Following this line of thought, the 1D perovskites have the   $  [PbX_{6}]^{4-}  $   octahedra structurally linked in a 1D chain that may share the corner, edge, or face, and the A-site organic cations were interleaved among the octahedra.  $  ^{[22,23]}  $   The structural dimensionalities of 1D perovskites can be single-chain (n = 1), double-chains (n = 2), triple-chains, and infinite-chains (n = ∞) edge/face-shared   $  [PbX_{6}]^{4-}  $   octahedra, respectively. Generally, 1D organic metal halides with corner/edge/face-shared   $  [PbX_{6}]^{4-}  $   octahedra have higher stability than 3D organic–inorganic metal halides, which could be associated with its structural merits: i) the linearly arranged   $  [PbX_{6}]^{4-}  $   by means of “shoulder to shoulder” can improve the skeleton strength of

Van der Waals forces, usually own lower decomposition activation energy and high freedom degree of ions migration, which inevitably leads to inferior stability of 3D perovskite itself.  $  ^{[7-9]}  $   In addition, the neutral iodine atoms are easily pushed forward into the nearby lattice to form a large number of iodine vacancies, relying on the decoupling of electrons and holes.  $  ^{[10]}  $   Distinct from the band-like nature of the high-mobility electronic transport in perovskite material, the ionic migration exhibits obvious hopping conduction with varying activation energies under external environment.  $  ^{[11,12]}  $   In this scenario, structure-induced ion migration has a detrimental role in the perovskite decomposition and thereafter quenching the performance of solar cells.

The structurally well-matched 1D@3D perovskite can effectively block the pathway of ion migration from building block to the interfacial domain and thereby alleviate the ion migration of perovskite thin film. Likewise, we inventively self-develop an in situ characterization system that allows to explore the morphology/composition/structure evolution of perovskite thin film within the reciprocal influence of external water/oxygen/electric field/pressure. By which, the 1D@3D perovskites demonstrate superior stability in comparison to its counterpart of traditional 3D perovskites. The corresponding solar cell devices consequently exhibit high efficiency and stability toward scaling-up the perovskite products.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. 1D Intermediates Microstructure and Crystallization Kinetics

Herein, we designed and self-prepared a novel   $  PbI_{2}  $  -bipyridine (BPy) precursor by means of simply mixing   $  PbI_{2}  $   and BPy, which was afterward dissolved into the perovskite precursors with different concentrations (we referred 0, 1, 25, 50, 75, 100, 150 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , respectively). The detailed experiments can be found in the Experimental Section. Figure 1a exhibits the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of as-prepared triple cation (Cs/MA/FA) perovskite thin films with different concentrations of   $  PbI_{2}  $  -BPy additives. The phenomena can be observed as follows: i) Aside from the well-known diffraction peaks of 3D perovskites, the diffraction peaks at 5.88° and 7.63° that belong to the   $  PbI_{2}  $  -BPy tend to be observable starting from the dopant concentration

of 75 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , which suggests the successful incorporation of PbI  $  _{2}  $  -BPy additive (Figure 1c,d). ii) There is no any clear peak shift with varying the PbI  $  _{2}  $  -BPy precursor concentration, which implies that the incorporation of PbI  $  _{2}  $  -BPy additives does not structurally affect the 3D perovskite lattice. iii) We took the diffraction peak of fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass as the reference, and the diffraction intensity of (100) and (110) planes of perovskite tends to clearly increase, whereas the peak intensity of (111) plane remains unvaried with increasing the PbI  $  _{2}  $  -BPy concentration as shown in the inset of Figure 1a. The incorporation of PbI  $  _{2}  $  -BPy obviously improves the crystallized quality of perovskites along the (100) and (110) planes in this case, which is likely associated with the atom arrangement manner in the heterojunction domain and we will discuss it afterward.

In order to further explore the crystallization kinetics of hybrid perovskite and how the   $  PbI_{2}-BPy  $   structurally affects the crystallization orientation of triple cation 3D perovskites, we prepared the corresponding single crystals of   $  PbI_{2}-BPy  $ . The corresponding single-crystal structures of   $  PbI_{2}-BPy/PbI_{2}-DMSO  $   (dimethyl sulfoxide) additives and/or complexes were resolved and refined by SHELXT and OLEX2,  $  ^{[28-30]}  $   which are shown in Figure 1b and Figure S1 in the Supporting Information, and the corresponding crystallographic parameters are also presented in Tables S1–S10 in the Supporting Information and crystallographic information file files. Herein, we found three types of   $  PbI_{2}  $ -based complexes as discussed in the following. All these single crystals of   $  PbI_{2}-BPy  $   and   $  PbI_{2}-DMSO  $   complexes exhibit 1D linear arrangement along their   $  [PbN_{2}I_{4}]^{2-}  $   and   $  [PbOI_{5}]^{3-}  $   octahedron orientation, respectively (Figure 1b). Particularly,   $  PbI_{2}  $   can complex with BPy in water to form two kinds of   $  PbI_{2}-BPy  $   complexes, which we refer to   $  PbI_{2}-BPy(I)  $   and   $  PbI_{2}-BPy(II)  $   perovskites. During the crystallization process,   $  PbI_{2}  $   is also able to complex with DMSO to form   $  PbI_{2}-DMSO  $   complex with respect to their different coordination abilities. In the case of   $  PbI_{2}-BPy(I)  $   complex, Pb atom is coordinated with both four I atoms and two N atoms, the corresponding bond lengths are 3.198 and 2.511 Å, respectively, which accordingly gives rise to a distorted   $  [PbN_{2}I_{4}]^{2-}  $   octahedron and monoclinic symmetry (C2/c space group). As shown in Figure 1b, the distorted   $  [PbN_{2}I_{4}]^{2-}  $   octahedra demonstrates 1D zigzag arrangement along the [100] via sharing the I-I edges of octahedra (n=1). Additionally, the BPy group arranges symmetrically along both sides of the distorted octahedra.   $  PbI_{2}-DMSO  $   complex demonstrates similar structure as   $  PbI_{2}-BPy(I)  $ , but with   $  [PbOI_{5}]^{3-}  $   octahedra and orthorhombic symmetry (Pnma space group). In particular, the Pb atom is observed to coordinate with both five I atoms and one O atom (herein, the occupation rate of O at these two nonspecial positions is 50%), the corresponding bond lengths are 3.332 and 2.577 Å, respectively. Again, the different bond length leads to the distorted   $  [PbOI_{5}]^{3-}  $   octahedra and thereafter zigzag arrangement along the [001] via sharing the I-O-I face of octahedra (n=2). With the thermal treatment on   $  PbI_{2}-BPy  $   and 3D perovskite precursor, the   $  PbI_{2}-BPy(I)  $   would tend to structurally isomerize to   $  PbI_{2}-BPy(II)  $   by means of exchange of O-I in the   $  PbI_{2}-BPy(I)  $   and   $  PbI_{2}-DMSO  $   complexes, in which, the Pb atom is observed to coordinate with six I atoms to form traditional   $  [PbI_{6}]^{4-}  $   octahedra. # 2.3. In Situ Characterization and 1D@3D Perovskite Stability

Unexpected ion migration is considered to be one of the important aspects for the instability of perovskite devices. Directional movement of ions, e.g.,   $  I^{-}  $   ions, would occur under the polarization of the electric field. In addition, the 3D perovskites are sensitive to water and easily decomposed in the humid environment. Herein, we innovatively built an in situ characterization system by means of combining low vacuum scanning electron microscopy (LoV-SEM) with a power source meter, which provides the possibility of characterizing the evolution of morphology and composition of perovskite thin films with different applied potential and controlled humid environment with pressure of 100 Pa (R.H. ≈3%). It is speculated from the energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) results that the production of iodate causes oxygen accumulation in the anode region. Once the electric field is removed, Cs  $  ^{+}  $  ions would migrate to the positive electrode region where the negative charge is concentrated, resulting in an increase in the Cs  $  ^{+}  $  ions concentration.  $  ^{[34]}  $  Afterward, the presence of water would facilitate the decomposition of 3D perovskites upon applied biases. In particular, water molecules would interact with Pb and prompt the release of I atoms, which causes the structure to be completely disintegrated.  $  ^{[35]}  $  The SEM images demonstrate the completely irreversible decomposition of controlled 3D perovskites under highly external biases, and the EDS results provide the element contribution of decomposed 3D perovskite. The potential reaction is likely as follows

$$
\mathrm{CsMAFAPbX} _ {3} \xrightarrow [ \mathrm{H} _ {2} \mathrm{O} ]{\mathrm{E-Field}} \begin{array}{l} \mathrm{Cs} ^ {+} + \mathrm{FA} ^ {+} + \mathrm{OH} ^ {-} + \mathrm{XO} _ {x} ^ {\prime} \\ + \mathrm{PbX} _ {2} + \mathrm{X} _ {2} + \mathrm{CH} _ {3} \mathrm{NH} _ {2} \end{array}
$$

Comparably, the 1D@3D perovskite remains exactly the same morphology and element distribution under exactly the same condition, even under 200 V mm  $  ^{-1}  $  that far exceeds the real work condition of PSCs (Figure 3c). It turns out that the as-prepared 1D@3D perovskite thin film owns superior stability against highly external biases and water in comparison to its counterpart of the traditional 3D perovskite. Herein, the hydrophobic organic group surrounding the 1D  $  [PbI_{6}]^{4-}  $  octahedra skeleton is capable of protecting the perovskite from contacting with water molecules, thereby improving the stability of 1D@3D perovskite under a humid atmosphere. Likewise, the pyridine group in 1D perovskite can form ion bonds and/or hydrogen bonds with the I  $  ^{-}  $  and A-site ions to improve their migration activation energy. Therefore, the successful incorporation of 1D perovskite with closely rigid-skeleton structure can effectively alleviate the ion migration and thereafter protect the 3D perovskite against water corrosion under highly external biases.

Currently, the water and/or light stability of perovskite device is the foremost obstacle toward its final commercialization. Herein, we explored the long-term stability of the 1D@3D perovskite thin film in the interaction condition of light/water/oxygen, which was simultaneously compared with the pristine 3D perovskite thin film. Figure S8a–d in the Supporting Information demonstrates the morphology evolution of the pristine 3D perovskite and 1D@3D hybrid perovskite aging in dry air and 365 nm UV light for 120 h, respectively. same condition. In order to further study how the organic functional group in 1D perovskite promotes the photo-oxygen stability of the 1D@3D heterojunction structure, we simply mixed the 3D perovskite single-crystal powder with the 1D perovskite single-crystal powder and tested it under the same photo-oxidation conditions (Figure S8e, Supporting Information). The corresponding UV-vis spectra demonstrate that the absorption intensity of pristine 3D perovskite is significantly reduced while 1D@3D perovskite remains almost the same absorption ability. It was reported that perovskite generated O $  _{2}^{-}  $  free radicals under the excitation of UV light, which further catalyzed the oxidative decomposition of perovskite itself. Simply mixed 1D and 3D perovskite single-crystal powder can effectively inhibit the photocatalytic degradation of 3D perovskite. $ ^{[36]} $  It can be inferred that BPy in 1D perovskite as an electron donor may have the positive effect of quenching oxygen radicals and thereby improving the photo-oxygen stability of 3D perovskite.

# 2.4. Device Performance

In a planar heterojunction device, charge carrier transport generally is explained by drift in the depletion region under the built-in electric field and by diffusion in the quasi-neutral

region to the corresponding charge carrier transport layer.  $  ^{[38,39]}  $   Intercept is associated with the built-in potential   $  (V_{\mathrm{bi}})  $   of the device according to the Mott–Schottky equation:  $  ^{[40]}  $     $  1/C^{2}=2(V_{\mathrm{bi}}-V)/A^{2}e\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}N_{\mathrm{A}}  $  , where C is the capacitance under applied voltage (V), A is the area of device,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative permittivity,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity, and   $  N_{A}  $   is the carrier concentration. Figure S12a in the Supporting Information shows the Mott–Schottky   $  (1/C^{2}-V)  $   curves of the pristine 3D perovskite and 1D@3D hybrid perovskite. By fitting, the corresponding   $  V_{bi}  $   is 0.95 and 0.3 V, respectively. A larger   $  V_{Bi}  $   in 1D@3D hybrid perovskite device will result in a stronger built-in-electric field, which would definitely facilitate the charge carrier extraction.

To further understand the effect of 1D perovskite incorporation on the photoelectric performance of 1D@3D perovskites, we calculated the trap-state density (  $  N_{trap}  $  ) by means of characterizing the dark I-V curves of pristine 3D and 1D@3D perovskites, respectively (Figure S12b,c, Supporting Information). In which, the current increased linearly with the voltage up to a clear kinkpoint. Further increasing the voltage over the kink-point, the current exhibits a more rapid exponential rise, which is due to filling of the trap states by the injected carriers. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, we innovatively synthesize a new type of 1D@3D hybrid perovskites by means of introducing a controlled amount of 1D  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -BPy into the 3D perovskite precursor system. The as-prepared 1D@3D hybrid perovskite is demonstrated to own superior stability in the humid environment under external applied electric field in comparison to the pristine 3D perovskites, which are confirmed through a self-build in situ characterization system. Such characterization technique enables to provide a sophisticated platform for exploring the long-term stability of perovskites and elucidate the dynamic process and mechanism of perovskite decomposition. The corresponding 1D@3D PSCs accordingly have both high stability and efficiency. The current study opens the possibility of controlling the growth of 1D@3D perovskite with well lattice matching and high stability by a low-cost solution-processing approach. Significantly, this approach can be widespread as a standard route for the fabrication of 1D@3D hybrid perovskite thin film-based solar cells with long-term stability while high efficiency, pushing the perovskite forward for photovoltaic applications and beyond.
