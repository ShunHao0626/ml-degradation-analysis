# 1. Introduction

Past decade has witnessed rapid surge in the research activities on hybrid organic-inorganic lead halide based perovskite solar cells with power conversion efficiency already crossing  $ 23\% $ , which is nearly five times higher than the earliest devices reported in 2009 (Kojima et al., 2009; W.S. Yang et al., 2017). The primary light absorbing layer in these devices is based on lead based halide perovskites which possess excellent opto-electronic attributes such as high absorption coefficient, long

Among various perovskite materials available methyl ammonium lead iodide (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  or MAPI) has been the most common and extensively explored perovskite material owing to its simple synthesis technique and ease of device fabrication in regular and inverted structure. However, thermal and moisture stability constraints have led researchers to explore mixed cation/halide perovskites. In this manuscript, we report excellent thermal and moisture stability of MAPI solar cells by incorporating organic: phenyl-C70-butyric acid methyl ester (PC $ _{70} $ BM) and inorganic: NH $ _4 $ Cl additives in the photo-absorber layer. Our investigations reveal that the use of NH $ _4 $ Cl results in large grain size hence reducing the grain interface or boundary area whilst PC $ _{70} $ BM passivates the charge traps and bonds with the defect-inducing halogen atoms at the grain boundaries. The combined effect of the two additives results in significant improvement in the life time of unencapsulated planar device with 80% retention in the efficiency after 500 hrs of accelerated stability testing at 85 °C in 15–20% RH, and 85% efficiency retention after 100 days of moisture exposure in the ambient conditions (50–60% RH and 20–25 °C). This is believed to be due to suppression of migration of ions which is also manifested in reduced hysteresis in the devices. The champion device with the two additives showed 90% increase in power conversion efficiency (PCE) of ~17% as compared to pristine planar device (9.0%), attributed to bigger grain size and passivation of charge traps at grain boundaries. Detailed microscopy analysis revealed the unambiguous presence of Cl ions in the perovskite layer on addition of NH $ _4 $ Cl. Thus, using single-step coating method, we have achieved excellent stability for slightly chlorine doped and PC $ _{70} $ BM passivated CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  based perovskite solar cell with reduced hysteresis.

Generally, researchers have reported perovskites (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ ) films prepared from lead acetate based precursors possessing smooth/ uniform surface and with good surface coverage (W. Zhang et al., 2015). Fast removal of methyl ammonium acetate (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ Ac) as byproduct during single-step spin coating leads to formation of smooth and pin hole free perovskite films but with smaller grain size. Further rapid crystallization induces large defects and high trap densities within perovskite layer (Qing et al., 2015) which act as recombination centers along with traps at the grain boundaries, thus reducing device's photovoltaic performance (Shao et al., 2014). Large difference between carrier diffusion length of single crystal and polycrystalline film further emphasized the role of grain boundaries and the same applies to trap density (Xu et al., 2018). Polycrystalline CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  perovskite films show defect densities of  $ \sim $ 10 $ ^{-16} $  cm $ ^{-3} $  and diffusion lengths of ca. 100 nm whereas single crystals show defect densities of  $ \sim $ 10 $ ^{-9} $  cm $ ^{-3} $  and diffusion lengths of over 175  $ \mu $ m (Dong et al., 2015; Leijtens et al., 2016; Shi et al., 2015; Stranks et al., 2013; Xing et al., 2013). Also, ion migration occurring via the grain boundaries of perovskites causes hysteresis behavior in the current voltage characteristics of PSCs (Shao et al., 2016).

One of the ways to mitigate these consequences of trap states and to reduce the hysteresis is by reducing the number of grain boundaries i.e. by increasing grain size. Larger grains exhibit higher charge carrier mobility, longer charge carrier lifetime and large open circuit voltage thus leading to enhanced device efficiencies (Bai et al., 2017; Bi et al., 2015; Yuan et al., 2016). Moreover, grain boundaries also provide passage for the moisture permeation which results in degradation of the perovskite films (R. Yang et al., 2017). Various techniques such as additive engineering, hot casting and solvent annealing promote formation of larger grains with uniform distribution which make perovskite films resistant to moisture degradation. Xu et al. used  $ \mathrm{NH}_2\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{PO}_2 $  (ammonium hypophosphite) as additive and increased grain size up to  $ \mu \mathrm{m} $  range. Additive addition results in formation of intermediate complexes which enhances the grain growth rate thus enabling larger grain size. (Xu et al., 2018). Previously, Wen et al. used hydroidic (HI) acid as additive and utilized two-step coating method to achieve larger grain size thus achieving power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 18.2\% $ , nearly  $ 2\% $  higher than control samples (Wen et al., 2018). Solvent vapour annealing methods have been also used to obtain large grains in mixed halide perovskite  $ (\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Br}_x) $  and exhibited PCE of ca.  $ 18\% $  (Nie et al., 2015; Tu et al., 2017). Similarly, hot casting method has also been shown to result in pinhole-free millimeter (mm) sized grains with enhanced PV performance thus reaching efficiency of  $ 18\% $  (Nie et al., 2015).

Another issue with MAPI is its chemical decomposition to   $  PbI_{2}  $   and MAI. Although the decomposition temperature of MAPI is about 300 °C, decomposition at surface and grain boundaries (GB) starts at much lower temperature, perhaps because these sites offer lower energy barrier for decomposition reaction, resulting in non-stoichiometric composition, impurity phases, cation/anion vacancies and presence of dangling bonds (Baikie et al., 2013; Shao et al., 2014) which act as charge traps at the grain boundaries. One way to reduce trap density at the grain boundaries is by their passivation with additives such as PCBM and its derivatives. Pb-I antisite defects as well as trimer defects with neighboring iodine atom, may also form in the perovskite films (Ranjan et al., 2020).   $  PC_{70}BM  $   forms bonds with these defective halides which is thermodynamically favored and thus suppresses the formation of deep traps mainly at the grain boundaries (Xu et al., 2015). The filling of large fullerene molecules at the grain boundaries restricts the ion migration via the grain boundaries and subsequently reduces the hysteresis to a larger extent (Fang et al., 2017; Shao et al., 2014; Xu et al., 2015).

In addition to the use of PCBM, replacing iodide ions in MAPI with chloride ions reduces excess of iodine in the perovskite films, which reduces trap state density and thus suppresses the recombination centers. Additionally, incorporation of smaller  $ \mathrm{Cl^-} $  ions in the perovskite

lattice decreases its lattice constant which significantly increases the formation energy of interstitial defects (Bouchard et al., 2017). Researchers have shown that better fluorescence lifetime in  $ \mathrm{Cl^-} $  rich domains than in  $ \mathrm{I}^- $  rich domains results from lower recombination rates in former (de Quilettes et al., 2015). This is further supported by the fact that mixed halide perovskites  $ (\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x) $  compared to iodide only perovskites  $ (\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3) $  show higher carrier diffusion length, better environmental and thermal stability (Qing et al., 2015) hence improved photovoltaic performance. Furthermore, incorporation of  $ \mathrm{Cl^-} $  also results in preferred orientation of grains (Bouchard et al., 2017).

Motivated by the role of   $  Cl^{-}  $  in improving various characteristics of MAPI and its devices, we have incorporated   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   for   $  Cl^{-}  $  doping and   $  PC_{70}BM  $   for passivation together for the first time in lead acetate precursor and have studied their combined effect on the quality of perovskite film and its subsequent effect on device performance in planar device configuration. We find that addition of   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   slows down the reaction kinetics in the precursor solution leading to formation of perovskite with enough time for the formation of smooth and uniform films having grain size in micrometer range and hence with reduced grain boundary density. In addition,   $  PC_{70}BM  $   reduces deep traps at the grain boundaries by forming bonds with defective halide. The planar device fabricated with these additives in perovskite layer resulted in excellent PCE of 17.0% with significantly reduced hysteresis in comparison to control samples which exhibited substantial hysteresis and low efficiencies of ca. 9.0%. In addition, unencapsulated solar cell devices using MAPI w/additives demonstrated excellent thermal stability by retaining 80% of its initial PCE when subjected to 500 h thermal stability test at 85 °C and 15–20% relative humidity. Moreover, unencapsulated device with additives retained ~85% of initial PCE even after 100 days when stored under ambient conditions with RH of 50–60%.

# 2. Experimental details

# 2.1. Device fabrication

Methyl ammonium iodide (MAI) was purchased from Osilla while lead acetate trihydrate   $  (\mathrm{Pb(OAc)_2})  $  , Spiro-OMeTAD, anhydrous dimethylformamide (DMF), titanium isopropoxide (TTIP), ammonium chloride   $  (\mathrm{NH_4Cl})  $   were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and [6, 6]-phenyl-C70-butyric acid methyl ester   $  (\mathrm{PC}_{70}\mathrm{BM})  $   was procured from nano-C. Fluorine-doped-tin oxide (FTO) coated glass sheet were purchased from Global Nanotech. Zn dust and 2 M HCl solution were used to do patterning of FTO. After cleaning with soap solution, patterned substrates were subsequently ultrasonicated with hot DI water, acetone and isopropanol (10 min for each step). The   $  TiO_{2}  $   film was coated at 4000 rpm for 40 s on patterned substrate after ozonization for 15 min. The precursor solution was prepared by mixing 350   $  \mu  $  L of TTIP in 5 mL of ethanol in slightly acidic medium and around 50 nm thickness of film was achieved. The substrates were annealed first for 10 min at 120 °C followed by increase of hot plate temperature to 500 °C. The substrates were kept for 30 min at this temperature. Then, the substrates were transferred to the   $  N_{2}  $   glove box. To prepare perovskite films, a 40 wt.% perovskite solution was prepared by mixing MAI and   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_2}  $   in 3:1 ratio in DMF solvent with addition of   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   in different amounts. The solution was stirred for 1 h at 55 °C inside the glove box. Just before coating perovskite, 10   $  \mu  $  L of   $  PC_{70}BM  $   solution were mixed in perovskite (20 mg/mL of   $  PC_{70}BM  $   solution in chlorobenzene). Perovskite films were spin coated at 2500 rpm for 40 s using this solution. The thin film sample was bench dried for 15 min followed by annealing at 100 °C for 5 min on hot plate inside the glove box only. The hole transport material (HTM) was then deposited at 3000 rpm for 40 s through spin-coating 72.3 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   solution of Spiro-OMeTAD in chlorobenzene doped with 28.8   $  \mu  $  L of tert-butylpyridine and 17.7   $  \mu  $  L of lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) solution (520 mg in 1 mL of acetonitrile). The samples were then taken out of the glove box and were kept overnight in a desiccator for oxidation, to induce additional doping of HTM by oxygen.

One should observe a red shift in the PL spectra if there is radiative recombination due to shallow traps while passivation of the traps will lead to a blue shift in the PL spectra due to band edge transition. We used an excitation wavelength of  $ 470~\mathrm{nm} $  whose penetration depth is around  $ 100~\mathrm{nm} $  while the thickness of perovskite film is around  $ 300~\mathrm{nm} $ . Fig. 5(a) shows the schematic of incident light from perovskite side and glass side. PL spectra from perovskite side is expected to represent the characteristic of bulk of perovskite whereas PL spectra from glass side will highlight the interfacial properties of perovskite with that of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  owing to penetration depth and diffusion length of charge carriers as discussed above (Shao et al., 2014). As Fig. 5(b) shows for sample without any  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  and  $ \mathrm{PC}_{70}\mathrm{BM} $  i.e.  $ \mathrm{PC_0NH_0} $ , when light is incident from perovskite side, PL emission intensity is very high whereas PL quenches when light is incident from the glass side, latter being due to  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  as electron extracting layer. In contrast, as shown in Fig. 5(b), PL quenching is intensified for sample with  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  as well as  $ \mathrm{PC}_{70}\mathrm{BM} $  ( $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_{10} $ ), irrespective of whether the light is incident from glass or perovskite side, also shown in Fig. 5(c). This confirms that increase in the size of grains in  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_{10} $  film improves the carrier diffusion length and therefore augments PL quenching. In addition, it also substantiates that  $ \mathrm{PC}_{70}\mathrm{BM} $  is homogeneously distributed throughout the thickness of perovskite layer either in bulk or at grain boundaries. Similar interpretation has been provided by Xu et al. when light was incident from both sides (Xu et al., 2015). Further, Fig. 5(d) shows the dependence of PL spectra on the  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  content and we clearly observe a blueshift in PL spectra for  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_5 $ ,  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_{10} $  and  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_{15} $  compared to  $ \mathrm{PC_0NH_0} $  which suggests that  $ \mathrm{PC}_{70}\mathrm{BM} $  is passivating the shallow traps in the perovskite film. The role of Cl in achieving improved electron transfer is evident in improved PL quenching of  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_5 $  and  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_{10} $  films compared to control. However, higher Cl content in  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_{15} $  leads to the formation of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbCl}_3 $  phase, hampering the electron transport. This deteriorates the device performance as can also be seen later in Table 1 (Xu et al., 2017). Hence, large Cl content in  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_{15} $  along with the presence of pinholes decreases the PL quenching.

To further confirm the passivating role of  $ \mathrm{PC}_{70}\mathrm{BM} $  in perovskite films, we performed conducting atomic force microscopy (c-AFM) of  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_{10} $  perovskite sample on grains as well as at the grain boundaries and the results are shown in Fig. S2(a). From dark I-V data shown in S2 (b) and (c), we clearly see that increase in the applied positive bias leads to an increase in the current which supports the fact that  $ \mathrm{PC}_{70}\mathrm{BM} $  at the grain boundaries provides a path for electron exit due to its superior electron transport characteristics (Xu et al., 2015). # 3.3. Device performance

To investigate the combined effect of   $  PC_{70}BM  $   and   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   on the photovoltaic performance of solar cell devices, devices with planar configuration FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /Perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au were fabricated. In these devices,   $  TiO_{2}  $   was used as electron transport layer while Spiro-OMeTAD is used as a hole transport layer. Fig. 6(a) and (b) shows the corresponding device structure and the band diagram. Table 1 summarizes average photovoltaic performance parameters and those for champion devices. Fig. 6(c) shows the J-V curves of champion devices for all the samples. The   $  PC_{0}NH_{0}  $   devices showed average PCE of 7.4% in reverse scan (RS) (9.0% for champion device) and 4.5% in forward scan (FS) with hysteresis index (HI) of 0.41.

The HI, expressed as  $ \mathrm{HI} = \frac{J_{\mathrm{e}}R(0.8V_{\mathrm{ec}}) - J_{\mathrm{e}}F(0.8V_{\mathrm{ec}})}{J_{\mathrm{e}}R(0.8V_{\mathrm{ec}})} $ , addresses the discrepancy between forward and reverse scan. It determines the quality of films in terms of halide defects and vacancy induced trap states which result in halide migration and cause hysteresis in J-V characteristics. When amount of  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  was increased to  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_5 $ , the PCE increased to  $ 11.5\% $  and HI reduced to 0.32. Furthermore, for  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_{10} $  the average PCE improved to  $ 15.6\% $  in RS and  $ 12.3\% $  in FS while HI reduced to 0.08 which is more than four-fold reduction than  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_5 $  and pristine devices. Low HI in  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_5 $  and  $ \mathrm{PC}_{0.3}\mathrm{NH}_{10} $  indicates high quality film with fewer  $ \mathrm{I}^- $  defects and vacancies i.e. lower defect density.

Fig. 6(d) shows the J-V characteristics of champion device of the best performing composition i.e. PC $ _{0.3} $ NH $ _{10} $  in forward and reverse scans. At this composition, the increment in PCE is a combined effect of increase in V $ _{oc} $ ,  $ ^{sc} $  as well as FF as evident from Table 1 and can be attributed to large grain size due to incorporation of Cl $ ^{-} $  as well as passivation of grain boundaries by PC $ _{70} $ BM which enables enhanced charge transport and reduces recombination at the grain boundaries. This results in champion device with excellent PCE of 17.0% in RS and 13.1% in FS for PC $ _{0.3} $ NH $ _{10} $  device. # 3.5. Stability analysis

Generally, thermal aging in perovskite solar cells accelerates the movement of ions in the crystal lattice of perovskite and halide ion diffusion to the electrodes, resulting in composition that is non-stoichiometric, thus causing decomposition of perovskite phase. Furthermore, at high temperature MA diffuses through the film and leaves perovskite layer followed by decomposition of perovskite to   $  PbI_{2}  $   phase. Also, thermal degradation mainly occurs at the grain boundaries due to migration of these ions under thermal stress (Park et al., 2017). Thus, to investigate the effect of   $  PC_{70}BM  $   and   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   addition on thermal stability of our devices, unencapsulated devices of   $  PC_{0}NH_{0}  $   (control sample) and   $  PC_{0.3}NH_{10}  $   (best performance with additive), were stored under dark at 85 °C and 15–20% humidity and were tested for device performance after regular time interval. The results, as shown in Fig. 9, clearly demonstrate that thermal stability of   $  PC_{0.3}NH_{10}  $   devices is significantly better than   $  PC_{0}NH_{0}  $   devices and the devices retained approximately 80% of their original photovoltaic performance (PCE,   $  V_{oc}  $  ,   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF) even after 500 h of thermal stressing while   $  PC_{0}NH_{0}  $   degraded completely within 200 h (Fig. 10(a)–(d)). In addition to thermal stability, we also conducted stability analysis in the presence of moisture. The unencapsulated,   $  PC_{0}NH_{0}  $   and   $  PC_{0.3}NH_{10}  $   devices were kept in a box with 50–60% RH under dark at ambient temperature. As Fig. 10(a)–(d) show, the efficiency of control devices (  $  PC_{0}NH_{0}  $  ) decreased to 50% of original PCE within 10 days whereas   $  PC_{0.3}NH_{10}  $   devices retained 85% of initial efficiency even after 100 days. Hence, lower number of grain boundaries assisted by their passivation by   $  PC_{70}BM  $   reduces water ingress in the   $  PC_{0.3}NH_{10}  $   devices making them substantially moisture resistant and stable.

# 4. Conclusions

In this study, we have conclusively shown that the addition of dual additives i.e.   $  PC_{70}BM  $   and   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   renders excellent thermal and long-term stability to the planar perovskite solar cells in addition to improvement in the device efficiencies. This is attributed to chlorine induced grain size enhancement in additive containing perovskite films as well as preferred (110) orientation while   $  PC_{70}BM  $   causes passivation of grain boundaries resulting in alleviation of defect density and better charge transport characteristics, supported by detailed optical, morphological and electrical characterization. EPMA analysis unambiguously showed the presence of chlorine in the perovskite layer. The devices with dual additive perovskite films show substantially improved PCEs to   $  \sim17.0\%  $   in the best device and much reduced hysteresis index compared to the control samples. The unencapsulated devices consisting of perovskite with additives maintained 80% of their initial efficiencies even after 500 h when stored at 85 °C in 20% RH. In addition, devices with additives also showed excellent resistance to humidity as manifested by retained performance even after 100 days when stored in 50–60% RH at 20–25 °C.
