# Multi-functional interface modulation through thiol functionalized covalent organic frameworks for efficient and durable perovskite solar cells†

Bo Yu, iD Kai Wang, Yapeng Sun and Huangzhong Yu iD *

Inhibiting metal electrode corrosion and reducing cathode interface charge loss are of significant importance for achieving stable and efficient p-i-n perovskite solar cells (PVSCs). Herein, two-dimensional (2D) thiol-functionalized covalent organic frameworks (SH-COFs) are used as an advanced barrier layer to provide an integrated solution for the above issues. The SH-COF rich in thiol groups can form close contact with the surface of Ag electrodes due to its strong chemical coordination, thus improving the corrosion resistance of metal electrodes. The inserted SH-COF interlayer also effectively hinders the ion migration and chemical reaction at the perovskite/metal electrode interface. Meanwhile, the SH-COF interlayer provides optimized interface contact and a cascade energy band arrangement, which is beneficial for promoting electron extraction and reducing non-radiative recombination at the cathode interface. Therefore, SH-COF-modified inverted PVSCs achieve power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of 24.12% and 21.63% for 0.15 cm $  ^{2}  $  and 1 cm $  ^{2}  $ -sized devices. Moreover, the optimized devices retain 93.3% and 90.7% of their initial PCEs after aging at 85 °C for 1200 h and maximum power point tracking at 65 °C for 1000 h, respectively. This work provides a promising strategy for overcoming the performance and long-term stability limitations of p-i-n PVSCs to promote their commercialization by SH-COF-modification.

# Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) are emerging as a game changer in the renewable energy community, since the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of single-junction PVSCs has exceeded 26%, which can rival the performance of commercial silicon-based cells.  $  ^{1-3}  $   The record value of PCE is constantly being broken, meaning that it is no longer the dominant limitation for commercializing PVSCs. Therefore, more research perspectives are gradually turning to improving the long-term stability and reliability of PVSCs, as well as optimizing existing packaging techniques.  $  ^{4-6}  $

Particularly, the halide-metal interdiffusion and chemical reactions at the perovskite/metal electrode interface are considered as fatal drawbacks for the long-term operation of PVSCs.  $  ^{7,8}  $   This is mainly because the outward diffusion of halide ions under sustained stress causes severe corrosion of metal electrodes, while the inward diffusion of metal ions also induces deep defect states in the perovskite absorber and exacerbates its decomposition, thus reducing the overall reliability of the device performance.  $  ^{9}  $   Implanting physical barrier

materials, such as   $  MoO_{x}  $  , Al-doped ZnO (AZO), Bi, and graphitic carbon nitride, has proved to be an effective strategy to restrain this pernicious diffusion.  $  ^{10-13}  $   Unfortunately, the inferior electronic properties of these barrier materials often lead to a large hysteresis in solar cells. Moreover, the top-contact electron transport layers (ETLs), which are in direct contact with metal electrodes, have not been fully explored for p-i-n PVSCs. Compared to other competitors, PCBM has attracted much attention in p-i-n PVSCs due to its solution processability and good compatibility with perovskite.  $  ^{14}  $   However, as an effective top-contact ETL, PCBM still has some shortcomings, such as poor film-forming properties, mismatched work function and weak hole blocking ability, which inevitably lead to interface charge loss at the cathode interface.  $  ^{15,16}  $   Although hole blocking materials (e.g., BCP, LiF, nano-carbon materials, and nanocrystalline metal oxides) have been introduced to address the above issues, these blocking materials are inadequate in providing long-term stable protection against ion migration and metal electrode corrosion.  $  ^{17-20}  $   Consequently, combining all the above considerations and proposing an integrated solution strategy is expected to facilitate the industrial application of PVSCs.

Wu et al. were the first to construct and use three-dimensional (3D) highly conjugated spirobifluorene-based COFs to optimize PVSCs, thus obtaining improved photovoltaic performance and excellent leakage prevention in the fabricated devices.  $  ^{[23]}  $   Zhang et al. synthesized two-dimensional (2D) COF nanosheets in situ on the surface and grain boundaries of a perovskite film to regulate crystallization and reduce defect density, which enabled the efficiency of PVSCs to reach 22.04%.  $  ^{[24]}  $   Guo et al. used sulfhydryl functionalized COFs to simultaneously modulate the top and bottom interfaces of the perovskite film, finally achieving a champion PCE of up to 24.26%.  $  ^{[25]}  $   However, so far, the application of COF materials in the photovoltaic field has mainly focused on optimizing the perovskite active layer, including passivating surface defects, controlling perovskite crystallization and suppressing halogen separation.  $  ^{[26-28]}  $   It is still a great challenge to develop functional COFs that integrate various chemical functions to deal with metal electrode corrosion and interface energy loss in PVSCs.

Herein, we for the first time report a cathode interface modification strategy by using 2D thiol-functionalized conjugated COFs (SH-COFs) as an advanced barrier layer to achieve stable and efficient p-i-n PVSCs. The dense thiol groups in SH-COFs can establish strong chemical coordination with the Ag surface and form a corrosion inhibition film, which is beneficial for protecting the Ag electrode and alleviating its chemical/electrochemical corrosion process. The SH-COF molecules distributed at the ETL/cathode interface also form a stable shielding network to prevent ion migration and chemical reactions between the Ag electrode and perovskite absorber. In addition, due to the better interface contact and matched energy level provided by the SH-COF interlayer, accelerated electron extraction and relieved charge accumulation are effectively realized at the cathode interface. Encouragingly, inverted PVSCs based on the SH-COF interlayer deliver an impressive PCE of  $ 24.12\% $ , accompanied by excellent ambient, thermal and operational stabilities. Moreover, the SH-COF modified PVSCs achieve a champion PCE of  $ 21.63\% $  for  $ 1\mathrm{cm}^2 $  large-size devices. This work provides a potential strategy for solving various limitations of the cathode interface to accelerate the industrialization of p-i-n PVSCs.

# Results and discussion

The synthesis of SH-COF molecules involves the polymerization of 1,3,5-triformylphloroglucinol (TFP) and 2,5-diaminobenzene-1,4-dithiol dihydrochloride (DBD) under solvothermal conditions (Fig. S1, ESI†). Fig. 1a displays the ball-and-stick structural model of SH-COF molecules. It can be observed that SH-COF is rich in thiol groups, which can be expected to be stably adsorbed on the surface of the Ag electrode by forming strong covalent bonds.  $  ^{29}  $   Furthermore, the conjugate structure and   $  \pi-\pi  $   interaction in SH-COF also contribute to rapid charge transfer.

The crystalline structures of the as-synthesized COFs were elucidated by conducting powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD). The experimental PXRD result shows that SH-COF has a sharp diffraction peak at 28.35°, corresponding to the (001) plane, which originates from the π-π stacking between layers (Fig. S2, ESI†).  $  ^{30,31}  $   X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was also performed to study the chemical structure characteristics of SH-COF. For the C 1s XPS spectrum, the peaks at the binding energy of 284.5, 284.9, 285.7 and 288.1 eV correspond to the C-C(sp²), C-C(sp³), C-N and C=O of SH-COF, respectively (Fig. 1b). The N 1s peak at 400.1 eV is attributed to C-N. The O 1s XPS spectrum displays a peak at 531.2 eV corresponding to C-C=O, and a peak at 534.2 eV attributed to adsorbed oxygen (Fig. S3, ESI†).  $  ^{32,33}  $   As illustrated in Fig. 1c, the Fourier-transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra show the disappearance of the aldehydic C-H (2890 cm⁻¹) and aldehyde C=O (1638 cm⁻¹) stretching vibrations of the TFP monomer during the synthesis of SH-COF. Besides, the characteristic peaks at 2576, 1578, and 1265 cm⁻¹ in SH-COF can be attributed to the stretching vibration modes of the S-H, C=C, and C-N bands, respectively. These results well support the successful construction of SH-COF.  $  ^{31,34}  $

Furthermore, atomic force microscopy (AFM) was performed to detect the morphology of SH-COF, which shows an ultra-thin nanosheet structure with a uniform thickness of about 1.5 nm (Fig. 1d). The high-resolution transmission electron microscope (HRTEM) images of SH-COF confirm a honeycomb-like configuration with irregular hexagonal arrays (Fig. S4, ESI†). The high-angle annular dark field (HAADF) image of SH-COF presents a stacked layered structure, and the energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS) mapping reveals a homogeneous distribution of C, N and S elements in the selected micro-region (Fig. 1e). The Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) specific surface region of SH-COF extracted from   $  N_{2}  $   adsorption-desorption isotherms is   $  50.6 \, m^{2} \, g^{-1}  $   (Fig. S5, ESI†). The smaller specific surface area of SH-COF can be attributed to the space steric hindrance caused by the introduction of thiol functional groups, which restricts the adsorption and diffusion process of   $  N_{2}  $   molecules.  $  ^{35}  $   Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) also verifies that SH-COF has a relatively good thermal stability (Fig. S6, ESI†).

The surface morphology of the perovskite/PCBM films without and with SH-COF was explored by top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (Fig. S7, ESI†). It can be observed that SH-COF molecules mainly fill the micropores of the PCBM film and the grain boundaries of the perovskite film, which can passivate the surface defects of the perovskite film and realize a smoother contact interface. Furthermore, the surface roughness of the films was further analyzed by recording AFM images (Fig. S8, ESI†). The root-mean-square surface roughness (RMS) of the perovskite/PCBM film decreases from 7.52 to 6.96 nm after the introduction of SH-COF. Smaller roughness means better cathode interface contact, which is beneficial for promoting the extraction and transfer of electrons.  $  ^{35,36}  $   The results from EDS mapping also verify that SH-COF has a uniform distribution on the surface of the perovskite/PCBM film (Fig. S9, ESI†). The results of charge density difference analysis indicate that there is a significant charge transfer from the Ag surface to SH-COF (Fig. 2d), which is consistent with the XPS analysis results. This charge transfer will also lead to the formation of a dipole moment at the SH-COF/Ag interface, and the dipole direction is from SH-COF to Ag, which will further enhance electron extraction from the ETL to the cathode.  $  ^{39,40}  $   These results well demonstrate that SH-COF molecules can stably adsorb on the surface of the Ag electrode through strong covalent interactions, which is beneficial for enhancing interface contact and promoting interface charge transfer, as well as providing a reliable chemical barrier for the Ag electrode.

To reveal the interfacial charge transport and recombination dynamics between perovskite and different ETLs, steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL (TRPL) measurements were performed for perovskite films covered with PCBM, SH-COF and PCBM/SH-COF layers, respectively. As exhibited in Fig. 4a, it can be clearly observed that the PL intensity of the pristine perovskite film decreases sharply after the deposition of different ETLs. Notably, the perovskite film exhibits a significant PL quenching effect upon contact with SH-COF, signifying that charges are efficiently extracted from perovskite to SH-COF.  $  ^{5,48}  $   Furthermore, the PL quenching of the perovskite/PCBM/SH-COF film is slightly stronger than that of the perovskite/PCBM film, which is attributed to the higher charge extraction rate caused by the cascade energy alignment (Fig. 4i). This conclusion is supported by TRPL spectra measurements in Fig. 4b, and the fitting results are summarized in Table S1, ESI.  $  ^{\dagger}  $   Compared with the pristine perovskite film (63.59 ns), the perovskite films deposited with SH-COF, PCBM and PCBM/SH-COF exhibit significantly reduced carrier

lifetimes of 18.79, 14.21 and 9.84 ns, respectively, which is consistent with the trend of steady-state PL results. Interestingly and importantly, the perovskite/PCBM/SH-COF film has a faster bi-exponential decay than any other perovskite/ETL films, further supporting the superior electron extraction capability of the PCBM/SH-COF layer.  $  ^{20,49}  $

The dark current-voltage curves of electron-only devices were used to evaluate the interfacial charge trapping densities in the devices without and with SH-COF (Fig. 4c). The trap density  $ (N_{\mathrm{trap}}) $  can be calculated from the trap-filled limit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{TFL}}) $  using the following formula:  $ N_{\mathrm{trap}} = 2\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_rV_{\mathrm{TFL}} / qL^2 $  where  $ q $  is the elementary charge,  $ L $  is the film thickness, and  $ \varepsilon_0 $  and  $ \varepsilon_r $  denote the vacuum permittivity and the relative dielectric constant.[50] Specifically, the  $ V_{\mathrm{TEL}} $  values for the devices without and with SH-COF are determined to be 0.252 and  $ 0.189\mathrm{V} $ , and the calculated  $ N_{\mathrm{trap}} $  values are  $ 5.047\times 10^{15} $  and  $ 3.795\times 10^{15} $   $ \mathrm{cm}^{-3} $ , which also strongly prove that decreased trap-state densities and fewer nonradiative recombination sites occur in PVSCs with SH-COF interlayers. These findings support that the introduction of a SH-COF interlayer can improve the charge extraction efficiency and further inhibit nonradiative recombination at the perovskite/cathode interface.

This optimized interface contact will accelerate electron extraction and inhibit interface charge accumulation, thus evidently reducing non-radiative recombination and minimizing energy loss at the ETL/cathode interface.

In contrast, the device after inserting SH-COF exhibits significantly improved photovoltaic performance, with a  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  of  $ 1.173\mathrm{V} $ , a  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  of  $ 25.02\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , and an FF of  $ 82.18\% $ , resulting in a PCE of  $ 24.12\% $ , which is one of the best for PVSCs with an internal blocking layer (Table S4, ESI†). In addition, the hysteresis index  $ (\mathrm{HI} = (\mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{reverse}} - \mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{forward}}) / \mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{reverse}}) $  of the fabricated device decreases from  $ 1.94\% $  to  $ 0.29\% $  after introducing the SH-COF interlayer. The enhanced photovoltaic performance and reduced hysteresis behavior in the SH-COF modified device can be attributed to the optimized energy level arrangement and suppressed ion migration at the perovskite/cathode interface.

To further evaluate the universality and scalability of our strategy, we fabricated large-area cells with an active area of 1.0 cm $  ^{2}  $ , and the device with SH-COF delivers a remarkable PCE of 21.63%, which is higher than that of the control device (20.09%) (Fig. 5c and Table S5, ESI†). The integrated  $  J_{sc}  $  values extracted from the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra for the control and SH-COF modified devices are 24.33 and 24.59 mA cm $  ^{-2}  $ , respectively, consistent with the measured  $  J_{sc}  $  values under a solar simulator (Fig. S18,† ESI†). By applying a bias

voltage near the maximum power point, the SH-COF modified device yields a steady-state PCE of  $ 23.86\% $  with a  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  value of  $ 24.20\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , which is well matched with the PCE obtained from the  $ J - V $  curves (Fig. 5d). Moreover, the statistical distribution of the measured PCEs based on 20 individual devices validates the enhancement and excellent reproducibility of the SH-COF modified device (Fig. S19, ESI†).

To profoundly unravel the intrinsic mechanism for performance improvement in PVSCs, light intensity dependent J-V measurements were conducted to inspect the charge recombination details in both PVSCs. The linear relationship between   $  J_{sc}  $   and incident light intensity (both on logarithmic scales) can be used to ascertain the scaling in the power law of   $  J_{sc} \propto I^{\alpha}  $  , where   $  \alpha  $   is the exponential factor and I is the light intensity (Fig. S20, ESI†).  $  ^{54}  $   The   $  \alpha  $   value of the SH-COF modified device (0.993) is closer to 1 than that of the control device (0.978), reflecting enhanced carrier collection and reduced bimolecular recombination in the presence of the SH-COF interlayer. Fig. 5e displays the dependence curves of   $  V_{OC}  $   versus logarithmic light intensity, and the linear fitting follows the equation   $  V_{\mathrm{OC}} = n(k_{\mathrm{B}}T)\ln(I)/q + const  $  , where n, T, and   $  k_{B}  $   denote the ideality factor, absolute temperature and Boltzmann constant, respectively.  $  ^{55}  $   The fitting slope decreases from 1.71   $  k_{B}T/q  $   of the control device to 1.43   $  k_{B}T/q  $   of the SH-COF modified device, which is attributed to the effective suppression of trap-induced recombination loss due to faster charge transfer and decreased charge accumulation, thus leading to increased FF of the device. The stability of PVSCs also plays a crucial role in promoting their large-scale industrial applications. Consequently, we systematically evaluate the long-term stability and reliability of unencapsulated cells under various environmental conditions. As previously reported, the rich thiol groups in SH-COF can enhance the hydrophobicity of perovskite films, thus further improving the moisture resistance of devices.  $  ^{25,59}  $   This is confirmed by the water contact angle increasing from 65.4° for the control film to 91.4° for the SH-COF modified film (Fig. 6a). Besides, we tracked the moisture stability of these devices in a humid air environment with RH = 60 ± 5% and 25 °C. The SH-COF modified device exhibits superior moisture stability, retaining 96.1% of its initial PCE after 1200 h, whereas the PCE of the control device declines to 65.2% of its initial value. The thermal-stability measurements of these devices were performed under the conditions of continuous annealing at 85 °C in a N₂ atmosphere. As shown in Fig. 6b, the PCE of the control device decreases to about 80% of its original value after 460 h. However, due to the significant inhibition effect of SH-COF on the diffusion of metal electrodes, the SH-COF modified device still retains 93.3% of its original PCE after 1200 h. The storage stability of unencapsulated devices is also significantly

improved, which is reflected by the fact that the control and SH-COF modified devices retain 76.4% and 96.5% of their initial performance after 2400 h of storage, respectively (Fig. 6c). Furthermore, the operation stability of the devices is evaluated by maximum power point tracking under one-sun illumination in a   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere. As presented in Fig. 6d, the SH-COF modified device also maintains 90.7% of the initial PCE after 1000 h of continuous operation, signifying its superior operational stability. Under the conditions of high temperature and high humidity (85 °C and 85% RH), the SH-COF modified device still shows better long-term operation stability, maintaining an initial efficiency of 86.5% after 120 h, while the control device only retains 54.2% of its initial efficiency (Fig. S22, ESI†). These results strongly support that the introduction of a SH-COF interlayer is an effective strategy to improve the photovoltaic performance and overall reliability of p-i-n PVSCs.

# Conclusion

In summary, we have developed an innovative method to inhibit metal electrode corrosion and improve the photovoltaic efficiency of PVSCs by using thiol-functionalized COFs as the cathode interface modification layer. The SH-COF rich in thiol groups can establish close contact with Ag electrodes and chelate migrated Ag ions by forming strong Ag-S bonds, thereby inhibiting the corrosion of metal electrodes and preventing halide-metal interdiffusion at the perovskite/cathode interface. Moreover, the SH-COF interlayer also enables improved interface contact and cascade energy band arrangement, which is beneficial for reducing interface charge accumulation and enhancing electron collection efficiency at the cathode interface. As a result, inverted PVSCs with the SH-COF interlayer exhibit a satisfactory PCE of 24.12% and improved device stability under ambient, thermal and long-term operating conditions. Furthermore, the fabricated large-area PVSCs (1 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) also show a champion PCE of 21.63%. Our work shows that the well-designed functional COFs can simultaneously improve the photovoltaic efficiency and operation stability of PVSCs. More importantly, this work has further expanded the application scope of COF materials in PVSCs, which may stimulate interest in exploring advanced COFs for high-performance photovoltaic devices.
