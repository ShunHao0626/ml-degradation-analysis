All-inorganic lead halide perovskites with potential stability improvement is promising candidate for optoelectronic applications. However, fundamental questions remain over the component interaction in the perovskite precursor solution due to the limitation of the most commonly used solvents of N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). Here, we report a simple and very effective interaction tailoring strategy for all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   perovskites by involving ionic liquid solvent methylammonium acetate (MAAc). We found that C=O has strong interaction with lead (  $  Pb^{2+}  $  ) and the N-H...I hydrogen bonds formation are observed. The interaction enables the stable perovskite precursor solution and allows the high-quality production of pinhole-free, large grain size, and flat all-inorganic perovskite films by retarding the crystallization. With a one-step, without the necessity for antisolvent treatment, air-processing approach regardless of humidity, the corresponding photovoltaic cell exhibits a high efficiency of 17.10% along with long operation stability over 1500 h under continuous light illumination.

# Introduction

Organic–inorganic hybrid perovskites have gained a rapid progress for thin-film photovoltaic in just a few years due to their broad spectral absorption, high charge carrier mobility, small exciton binding energy, and long exciton diffusion length  $  ^{[1]}  $  . With the enormous efforts, certificated 25.2% power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been achieved in 2019  $  ^{[2]}  $  , which is competitive to the market's dominant single-junction crystalline-Si (c-Si) solar cell. However, the big challenge of organic–inorganic hybrid PSCs is the operational thermal instability caused by the organic cations, greatly limiting the commercial application and further development  $  ^{[3]}  $  .

It has been previously suggested that substituting the organic segments with inorganic components   $  Cs^{+}  $  to construct all-inorganic cesium lead halide perovskites is an effective way to improve the stability due to the absence of volatile organic component and the higher formation energy  $  ^{[4]}  $  . It is shown that all-inorganic perovskites, e.g.,   $  CsPbX_{3}  $  , X = Cl, Br, I, still keep crystal structure well and show pronounced PCEs even at temperatures exceeding   $  400^{\circ}C^{[5]}  $  , opening up new perspectives toward the commercialization of PSCs. Therefore, many strategies have been explored for achieving high performance, including evaporation technique  $  ^{[6]}  $  , doping strategy  $  ^{[7]}  $  , interface engineering  $  ^{[8]}  $  , post-annealing method  $  ^{[9]}  $  , etc. The aim is to obtain high-quality perovskite films with pinhole free and large crystal grain, which in turn serves high performance. However, researchers just focus on the films or device structure itself but little attention has been paid to the precursor solution chemistry, which is the origin of high-quality films and hence the high device performance.

The limitation of precursor solution chemistry stems from the most used common solvents of DMF and DMSO due to the limited solubility of lead halides and cesium halides. Generally, a common concept is gradually demonstrated that coordination compound   $  PbI_{2}\cdot DMSO  $   or   $  PbI_{2}\cdot DMF  $   is formed by the interaction between lewis acid   $  PbI_{2}  $   and lewis base such as DMF or DMSO  $  ^{[10]}  $  . Lewis acid   $  Pb^{2+}  $   in   $  PbI_{2}  $   accepts the lone pair electrons originated from sulfur, nitrogen, and oxygen

bearing lewis bases to form adduct  $  ^{[11]}  $  . DMSO is expected to from more stable adduct than DMF because the electron donating methyl group in DMSO lead to the stronger lewis basicity  $  ^{[12]}  $  . The coordination interaction, therefore, plays a key role in controlling perovskite crystallization. However, very little is known about that tailoring such interaction might translate into the overall enhancement of the optoelectronic conversion efficiency and stability of the all-inorganic perovskite films.

Here, we report a simple and very effective interaction tailoring strategy by involving an ionic liquid solvent MAAc. Different from the commonly used DMF and DMSO solvents, oxygen with lone pair electrons (C=O) on Ac  $  ^{-}  $  has strong interaction with PbI  $  _{2}  $  and the N–H...I hydrogen bonds are observed because of the presence of numerous methylammonium (MA  $  ^{+}  $ ) in the MAAc precursor solution. A one-step, without the necessity for antisolvent treatment, air-processing approach regardless of humidity is successfully developed with production of pinhole-free, large grain size, and flat perovskite films due to such effective interaction. A series of all-inorganic wide band gap PSCs are demonstrated by controlling the ratio of I and Br, with the best-performing PCEs of 13.82% (CsPbI  $  _{1.5}  $  Br  $  _{1.5}  $ ), 15.82% (CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br), and 17.10% (CsPbI  $  _{2.5}  $  Br  $  _{0.5}  $ ), which are one of the highest values reported for CsPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Br  $  _{x}  $  PSCs. Moreover, all PSCs exhibited high open-circuit voltage (V  $  _{oc}  $ ) over 1.30 V, indicating the suppressed charge recombination. Most importantly, the CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br PSCs still maintain over 85% of their initial efficiency with long operation stability over 1500 h under continuous light illumination. We anticipate that tailoring component interaction by ionic liquid solvent provides a new way to construct all-inorganic perovskite films with high quality for optoelectronic device applications.

Stage I

In the low temperature annealing stage (Stage II, 100°C-120°C), as the annealing temperature increases, MAAc in the   $  Cs_{1-x}MA_{x}PbAc_{x}I_{2-x}Br  $   intermediate perovskite phase is slowly evaporated, and   $  Cs_{1-x}MA_{x}PbAc_{x}I_{2-x}Br  $   (x>1/2) turns to be   $  Cs_{1-y}MA_{y}PbAc_{y}I_{2-y}Br  $   (y<x) and the other intermediate phase   $  CsPbI_{3-2t}Br_{2t}  $   appears. Due to the evaporation of MAAc, the CsI and   $  PbI_{2}  $   gradually incorporate into the lattice to form   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ . With the increase of annealing temperature, more CsI and   $  PbI_{2}  $   could incorporate into the lattice.

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathrm {Cs_ {1 - x} MA_ {x} PbAc_ {x} I_ {2 - x} Br + (x - y + 1)CsI + (1 - t)PbI_ {2} + tPbBr_ {2} \rightarrow Cs_ {1 - y} MA_ {y} PbAc_ {y} I_ {2 - y} Br + } \\ \mathrm {CsPbI_ {3 - 2t} Br_ {2t} +(x - y)MAAc(y <  x, t <  3 / 4)} \end{array} \tag {StageII}
$$

In the moderate temperature annealing stage (Stage III, 120°C-150°C), MAAc in the   $  Cs_{1-y}MA_{y}PbAc_{y}I_{2-y}Br  $   (y<0.5) intermediate perovskite phase is continuously evaporated. More   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite phase is formed accompanied by the disappearance of   $  Cs_{1-y}MA_{y}PbAc_{y}I_{2-y}Br  $   (y<0.5) and   $  CsPbI_{3-2t}Br_{2t}  $   (t<3/4) intermediate perovskite phases. With the gradual disappearance of the intermediate phase, the crystal tends to be stabilized.

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathrm {Cs_ {1 - y} MA_ {y} PbAc_ {y} I_ {2 - y} Br + (y - z)CsI + CsPbI_ {3 - 2t} Br_ {2t} +(q - t)PbBr_ {2} \rightarrow Cs_ {1 - z} MA_ {z} PbAc_ {z} I_ {2 - z} Br + } \\ \mathrm {CsPbI_ {3 - 2q} Br_ {2q} +(y - z)MAAc(z <  y, q > t)} \end{array} \tag {StageIII}
$$

Stage IV

Having shown the excellent optoelectronic properties and film formation mechanism, we move to fabricate all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with the device structure of FTO/  $  SnO_{2}  $   (20 nm)/  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   (600 nm)/Spiro-OMeTAD (80 nm)/  $  MoO_{3}  $   (5 nm)/Ag (100 nm) (Supporting Information, Figure S15). Note that MAAc film is fabricated in ambient air with humidity around 20-70% while DMF:DMSO film is prepared in a   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glovebox by the classic antisolvent method. As shown in Figure 4a, the DMF:DMSO device shows a standard PCE of 13.06% with open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.11 V, short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 15.59 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , and fill factor (FF) of 75.32%, which is comparable to the previous reports  $  ^{[22-23]}  $  . Impressively, the MAAc device exhibits an impressive PCE of 15.83% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.32 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 16.52 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , and a FF of 72.40%, which is one of the best PCEs as far as we know (Table S1). More importantly, the device is highly resistant to humidity, the corresponding device parameters are summarized in Table S2. By controlling the ratio of I and Br, we achieved the best-performing PCEs of 13.82% (  $  CsPbI_{1.5}Br_{1.5}  $  ) and 17.10% (  $  CsPbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}  $  ), as shown in Figure 4b, indicating the universal method for all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   PSCs. The corresponding device parameters are summarized in Table 1. # Conclusion

In summary, we propose an effective interaction tailoring strategy by MAAc ionic liquid solvent. Highly efficient and stable all-inorganic perovskite devices with PCE of 17.10% and   $  V_{oc}  $   over 1.30 V is obtained by a simple one-step approach in ambient air. The strong Pb-O interaction and the N–H...I hydrogen bonds between MAAc and   $  PbI_{2}  $   are observed, which are responsible for the high-quality perovskite films with low trap density, and thus the high device performance. Moreover, the discovered interaction exhibits excellent stability both in the perovskite precursor solution and the final perovskite films. The component interactions in MAAc provide an alternative opportunity to tailor this scientifically and technologically interesting class of all-inorganic perovskites for potential applications.
