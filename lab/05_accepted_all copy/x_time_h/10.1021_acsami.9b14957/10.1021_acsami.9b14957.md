# ABSTRACT

All-inorganic perovskite (CsPbX $ _3 $ , X=Br or I) solar cells demonstrate superior stability while the power conversion efficiency (PCE) lags behind the organic-inorganic hybrid counterparts mainly due to the limitation of narrow absorption bands. To broaden its absorption spectrum and improve its PCE, all-inorganic perovskite/organic integrated solar cells utilizing CsPbI $ _2 $ Br as ultraviolet-visible light absorber and PBDTTT-E-T:IEICO as near-infrared light absorber are demonstrated in this work. The integrated solar cells exhibit a broadened photo-response to over 900 nm attributing to the integration of PBDTTT-E-T:IEICO. The additional absorption enhances the short-circuit current density from 14.78 mA/cm $ ^2 $  to 15.98 mA/cm $ ^2 $ , resulting greatly improved PCE of 14.03% for integrated solar cells, much higher than that of the control perovskite solar cells (12.53%) and organic solar cells (7.51%). In-depth understanding of the charge transfer dynamic process in CsPbI $ _2 $ Br/PBDTTT-E-T:IEICO film is comprehensively analyzed by photoinduced transient absorption spectroscopy. Furthermore, the air stability and thermal stability of the integrated solar cells are greatly enhanced. For unencapsulated integrated solar cells, the PCE still preserve 95% of its initial value after aging for 300 hours in an ambient environment and the PCE retains about 90% of its original value even after aged at 85 °C for 180 hours in nitrogen.

However, until now there is no effective approach to solve this problem. Herein, we proposed an unique absorption extending strategy of directly integrate all-inorganic perovskite with near-infrared sensitive organic bulk heterojunction layer to construct   $  CsPbI_{2}Br/PBDTTT-E-T:IEICO  $   all-inorganic perovskite/organic integrated solar cells (ISCs), where the perovskite and organic active layers were directly contacted without interconnection layer.  $  ^{[20-27]}  $   After integrating PBDTTT-E-T:IEICO on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  , the photo-response of the ISC was successfully broadened to over 900 nm benefiting from the additional light harvesting of low bandgap organic active layer, and the greatly improved PCE of 14.03% has been achieved due to the effective charge transfer between   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and PBDTTT-E-T:IEICO, much higher than that of the controlled PSCs (12.53%) and organic solar cells (OSCs) (7.51%). Device fabrication: The ITO glass was continuously ultrasonically washed twice in detergent, tap water, pure water and ethanol for 15 minutes each time. The cleaned ITO glass was blown dry with nitrogen and the treated with ultraviolet ozone for 10 min so that the aqueous solution of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   precursor could be wetted on the ITO glass. The   $  SnO_{2}  $   precursor solution was spin-coated on the ITO substrate at 2500 rpm for 30 s, and then annealed at 150 °C for 30 min to obtain   $  SnO_{2}  $   electron transport layer (ETL). Subsequently, the substrate was transferred into a nitrogen-filled glove box. The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution was spin-coated on   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL (1000 rpm 5 s + 2500 rpm 30 s). Then, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film was transferred into a vacuum chamber for vacuum solvent evaporation (8 min). The vacuum atmosphere is provided by an oil pump and degree of vacuum shows on the vacuum gauge is -0.1MPa. After that, the samples were annealed at 160 °C for 10 min to form   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   active layer with a thickness about 300 nm.

For ISCs, the PBDTTT-E-T:IEICO solution was spin-coated on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   layer at 1800 rpm for 60 s and subsequently annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. For PSCs, the deposition of spiro-OMeTAD (73.5 mg/mL in chlorobenzene; 1 mL spiro-OMeTAD mixed with 29 μL tBP and 17.5 μL Li-TFSI (520 mg/mL in acetonitrile)) was spin-coated on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   layer at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, 100 nm Ag was evaporated on spiro-OMeTAD hole transport layer (HTL). For OSCs, the PBDTTT-E-T:IEICO solution was spin-coated on   $  SnO_{2}ETL  $  . Instruments and characterization: Ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) absorption spectra were measured using Shimadzu UV-2450. Photoluminescence (PL) measurements were performed using FluoroLog-3 Modular Spectrofluorometer from HORIBA Scientific and the wavelength of excitation light is 500 nm. The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film for UV photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) test was deposited on ITO substrate and excited by He I (21.2 eV) UV source. Transient absorption (TA) spectroscopy was recorded using PHAROS HIRO HARPIA femtosecond transient absorption system. In TA measurement, a 460 nm monochromatic high-energy pump pulse (  $  I_{p}  $  ) was firstly used to trigger the photoreaction of the sample. Then, a weak probe pulse was utilized to pass the sample and recording the initial probe pulse intensity (I0 probe) and probe pulse intensity at the delay time of t (It probe). The surface topography of each layer was detected using Agilent 5500 atomic force microscope (AFM). The current-voltage characteristic (J-V) curves of ISCs, PSCs and OPVs were measured by Keithley 2400 digital source meter. The AM 1.5 G (100 mW/cm²) standard sunlight was supplied by SAN-EI (AAA grade) solar simulator and the illumination intensity was calibrated with a standard Si solar cell. The active area of the devices for J-V test is 4 mm² and scanning speed in J-V test is 100 mV/s. External quantum efficiency (EQE) was measured by QE-R systems (Enli Tech.).

# CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we developed the all-inorganic perovskite/organic ISCs utilizing   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and PBDTTT-E-T:IEICO active layers to harvest more photons and therefore enhance device performance. The photo-response of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs is broadened from 680 nm to over 900 nm by integrating with PBDTTT-E-T:IEICO.
