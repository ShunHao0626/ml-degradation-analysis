# Nonionic  $ \mathrm{Sc}_3\mathrm{N}@\mathrm{C}_{80} $  Dopant for Efficient and Stable Halide Perovskite Photovoltaics

Kai Wang, $ ^{1\#*} $  Xiaoyang Liu, $ ^{2\#} $  Rong Huang, $ ^{2} $  Congcong Wu, $ ^{1} $  Dong Yang, $ ^{1} $  Xiaowen Hu, $ ^{3} $  Xiaofang Jiang, $ ^{3} $  James C. Duchamp, $ ^{2} $  Harry Dorn $ ^{2*} $  and Shashank Priya $ ^{1*} $

$  ^{1}  $  Materials Research Institute, Pennsylvania State University, University Park, PA 16802, United States

$ ^{2} $ Department of Chemistry, Virginia Tech, Blacksburg, VA 24061, United States

$  ^{3}  $  South China Academy of Advanced Optoelectronics, South China Normal University, Guangzhou 510006, P.R. China

One major challenge in the commercialization of halide perovskite solar cells is the device instability against moisture. Efficient perovskite solar cells usually incorporate the ionic dopants within the charge transfer material to secure their electrical conductivity. Typical ionic dopants are hygroscopic, resulting in significant moisture-adsorption and accelerated perovskite degradation. Herein, we report a nonionic dopant,  $ \mathrm{Sc}_3\mathrm{N}@\mathrm{C}_{80} $ , consisting of a hydrophobic fullerene cage that encapsulates the metal salt for achieving moisture resistance and highly electrically conductive hole transfer layer (HTL). The direct electronic transaction between  $ \mathrm{Sc}_3\mathrm{N}@\mathrm{C}_{80} $  and spiro-OMeTAD renders a drastically improved conductivity and a lower Fermi-level of HTL for minimizing the Schottky barrier. The hydrophobicity of  $ \mathrm{Sc}_3\mathrm{N}@\mathrm{C}_{80} $  also decreases the moisture wettability. Perovskite solar cells using the  $ \mathrm{Sc}_3\mathrm{N}@\mathrm{C}_{80} $ -doped HTL exhibit an efficiency surge from  $ 18.15\% $  to  $ 20.77\% $  (champion cell exhibiting  $ 21.09\% $ ), and improved device stability (survival efficiency over  $ 17\% $  after continuous illumination for 800 hours). The bifunctional hydrophobic  $ \mathrm{Sc}_3\mathrm{N}@\mathrm{C}_{80} $ -dopant provides the pathway towards efficient and stable perovskite photovoltaics.

Photovoltaics employing organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite photoactive materials have captured global attention owing to their extraordinary device efficiency. A power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 24.2%  $  ^{1}  $  has been recorded in 2019. However, the severe moisture sensitivity of the halide perovskite materials still limits the device operational lifetime, presenting challenges in their transition to industry.  $  ^{2-6}  $  In particular, the hygroscopic ionic dopants that are commonly employed in the hole transport layer (HTL), 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobi-fluorene (spiro-OMeTAD), usually accelerate the device degradation. The intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD is electrically unfavorable for charge transport due to its low hole mobility (  $  \mu_{h}  $  ) of  $  \sim10^{-5}  $  cm  $  ^{2}  $  V  $  ^{-1}  $  s  $  ^{-1}  $ . Thus, complex doping and processing is required to improve its electrical properties for photovoltaic application. To date, various ionic dopants have been introduced to improve the electrical properties of spiro-OMeTAD. Bach et al. demonstrated the doping effect in spiro-OMeTAD by adding antimony salt to introduce additional free carriers into the system.  $  ^{7}  $  Henry et al. found the addition of lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulphonyl)imide (LiTFSI) could significantly boost the charge carrier mobility  $  ^{8}  $ . Additionally, ionic dopants such as silver bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (AgTFSI),  $  ^{9}  $  SnCl  $  _{4}  $ ,  $  ^{10}  $  CuSCN,  $  ^{11}  $  CuI,  $  ^{12}  $  KMnO  $  _{4}  $ ,  $  ^{13}  $  Keggin-type polyoxometalates (POMs)-phosphovanadomolybdate (H  $  _{4}  $  PMo  $  _{11}  $  V·nH  $  _{2}  $  O)  $  ^{14}  $ , and lithium-ion endohedral fullerene (Li  $  ^{+}  $  @C  $  _{60}  $ )  $  ^{15}  $  have been reported to enhance the electrical properties of the spiro-OMeTAD. Nevertheless, the hygroscopic nature of these ionic dopants imparts negative effect on the stability, which is critical issue in transition of perovskite photovoltaic technology. There are also parasitic issues arising from the ionic doping of HTL. For example, the LiTFSI itself is not a direct p-dopant and requires additional oxygen to be involved to complete the redox doping reaction. This reaction occurs uncontrollably under the processing environment and becomes reversible during the device operation depending upon the ambient conditions. All these behaviors are detrimental towards the reliability of the perovskite solar cell.

Here, we report the application of the endohedral metallofullerenes (EMFs),   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $  , that can act as the non-ionic dopant for spiro-OMeTAD in halide perovskite solar cells. The EMF consists of a fullerene   $  sp^{2}  $   hybridized carbon cage and atoms, ions, or clusters encapsulated in the cage. The variability of the encapsulated species imparts multifunctional properties which have driven the use of EMFs in optoelectronics,  $  ^{16}  $   magnetic resonance imaging contrast agents,  $  ^{17}  $   electron-spin quantum computer  $  ^{18}  $  , atomic clock  $  ^{19}  $  , etc. In this study, we employ the trimetallic nitride template (TNT) EMF, i.e., the   $  I_{h}-(\mathrm{C}_{80})^{6-}  $   incorporating the trimetallic scandium nitride

The non-ionic and hydrophobic nature of the   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $   TNT EMF delivers strong moisture resistance and provides high device stability. In addition,   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $   has been reported to exhibit excellent ability to stabilize the charge separated states with a low reduction potential,  $  ^{20}  $   making it good p-type dopant for the spiro-OMeTAD oxidation along with a suppressed reversible reaction. Direct electron transfer from spiro-OMeTAD to   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $   gives rise to oxygen-free oxidation of spiro-OMeTAD HTL that delivers significantly improved electrical conductivity and a lower-lying Fermi-level which reduces the Schottky barrier for hole extraction. Both of these effects are found to synergistically favor the energy flow in the solar cell and consequently elevate the PCE to 20.77% for   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  -based perovskite solar cells. The hydrophobic nature of   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $   doped HTL eliminates the hygroscopic issues and renders a survival PCE of over 17% after 800 hours of continuous one-sun illumination exposure in ambient, with relative humidity (RH) ranging from 25% to 60%.

The TNT EMFs have been shown to display the promise to complex with organic donors and provide electron transfer from the donor to the EMF.  $  ^{[21,22]}  $   In this study, the electron transfer between   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $   and spiro-OMeTAD was investigated via a photophysical measurement. The oxidation of spiro-OMeTAD was studied by the UV/Vis-NIR absorption titration measurement. As shown in Figure 1A, by gradually increasing the concentration of   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $   (from 0 to 1.0 mg/mL) in the spiro-OMeTAD-chlorobenzene solution, characteristic absorptions ca. 500 nm assigned to the oxidized spiro-OMeTAD  $  ^{\bullet+}  $   increase accordingly.  $  ^{[15,23]}  $   The oxidized spiro-OMeTAD  $  ^{\bullet+}  $   states were further verified by comparisons between the in-situ measured spectrum of   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $   doped spiro-OMeTAD and the calculated spectrum with spectral superposition of independent   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $   and spiro-OMeTAD samples (Fig. S1). It should be noted that typical ionic dopant such as LiTFSI usually renders two characteristic absorption peaks corresponding to the singly and doubly oxidized spiro-OMeTAD at   $  \sim524  $   nm and   $  \sim688  $   nm,  $  ^{[23-25]}  $   respectively. Here we observe a wide range of high absorption from 420 nm to 700 nm in the   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $   doped spiro-OMeTAD. This can be ascribed to the multiple spatial conformations of the oxidized spiro-OMeTAD, as the spatial coordination between these large molecules could take various conformations. In-depth understanding of the electron transfer between the  $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $  and spiro-OMeTAD was obtained by performing the Density Functional Theory (DFT) computations on the HTL system. As predicted by the DFT simulations (Figure 1D), the total electron density of the spiro-OMeTAD molecule decreases with a corresponding gain of the electron density at the complexation region of the  $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $ . Clearly,  $  \pi-\pi  $  electron transfer is evident between the  $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $  and spiro-OMeTAD systems. This is consistent with EMFs potential to form ground state charge transfer complexes with organic electron donors.  $  ^{29}  $  As illustrated in Fig. S2, the molar excess of spiro-OMeTAD facilitates multi-site complexation with the nearly spherical  $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $  (0.82 nm dia.) and a general electron transfer was evidenced in all cases of multi-site complexation. In addition, the spiro-OMeTAD geometry originates from a tetrahedral site allowing the unpaired electron of a [spiro-OMeTAD]  $  ^{+}  $  radical to be efficiently delocalized over the entire molecule, which can be visualized by the stabilized spin density distribution (Fig. S4). As a result, the  $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $  instantly interacts with spiro-OMeTAD, electronically rendering intermediate radical states that facilitate the charge transport.  $  ^{30}  $  In this manner, no additional oxygen is needed, which is in distinct contrast to the case of LiTFSI-doped spiro-OMeTAD, where oxygen penetration and ambient exposure can drastically deteriorate the electrical homogeneity and result in a reverse doping issues for HTL.  $  ^{31,32}  $  In conjunction, the hydrophobic nature of the  $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $  is also expected to benefit the long-term device stability when compared to the hygroscopic LiTFSI dopant. As compared in Figure 1E, the  $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $ -doped spiro-OMeTAD HTL exhibits a water contact angle of 105.6° compared to 71.2° for LiTFSI-doped HTL. The enhancement in moisture non-wettability and corresponding hydrophobic feature suggests a larger barrier for water penetration in the HTL, which can protect the inner perovskite from moisture. The electrical conductance of HTLs are crucial for charge transport in the solar cell devices. We employed conducting-atomic force microscopy (c-AFM) to investigate both the surface morphological and electrical landscapes of different HTLs. Figure 2A-C displays the topography of spiro-OMeTAD HTLs doped by different dopants. Perovskite solar cells based on different HTLs were fabricated in a planar heterojunction architecture of FTO-glass/SnO $ _2 $ /MAPbI $ _3 $ /HTL/gold (Figure 4A), as shown in the cross-sectional scanning electron microscopy (SEM) image in Figure 4B. The SnO $ _2 $  was utilized as the electron extraction layer as it displays a deeper conduction band, leading to a barrier-free band alignment with perovskite and thus less voltage loss. $ ^{43} $  It should be noted that here we employed a novel perovskite ink of CH $ _3 $ NH $ _2 $  vapor pretreated perovskite in acetonitrile solution, according to our previous reports (detailed in the Experimental Section). $ ^{44} $  The HTLs were chosen from the pristine spiro-OMeTAD, LiTFSI-doped spiro-OMeTAD, and Sc $ _3 $ N@C $ _{80} $ -doped spiro-OMeTAD. The Sc $ _3 $ N@C $ _{80} $ -dopant concentration effect on the solar cell performance was initially investigated through the photocurrent density-voltage (J-V) characteristics as illustrated in Figure 4C. As the concentration of Sc $ _3 $ N@C $ _{80} $  increases from 0 to 1.0 mg/mL, the solar cells exhibit a gradual increase in short-circuit current density (J $ _{SC} $ ) and open-circuit voltage (V $ _{OC} $ ). The detailed photovoltaic performance parameters have been summarized in Table 1. The reference device utilizing pristine spiro-OMeTAD HTL displays a modest PCE of 14.19% with J $ _{SC} $  of 19.81 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , the V $ _{OC} $  of 1.06 V and fill factor (FF) of 0.68, consistent with recently reported values. $ ^{45} $  As the Sc $ _3 $ N@C $ _{80} $ -dopant concentration increases from 0 to 1.0 mg/mL, we found the device performance displayed a monotonous enhancement with simultaneously improved J $ _{SC} $ , V $ _{OC} $  and FF. This can be understood in terms of the Sc $ _3 $ N@C $ _{80} $ -doping effect on reduction in Fermi-level (Figure 3A) and increase in electrical conductivity (Figure 2H). The V $ _{OC} $  in solar cells is determined by the separation of quasi-Fermi levels of electrons and holes upon illumination. $ ^{36} $  The energy flow at the cathode side is secured by the barrier-free contact between SnO $ _2 $  and perovskite that fosters a higher quasi-Fermi level of electrons. $ ^{43} $  The lower-lying Fermi-level (closer to the valence band maximum of MAPbI $ _3 $ ) of Sc $ _3 $ N@C $ _{80} $ -doped spiro-OMeTAD HTL gives rise to a minimized Schottky barrier after contact with MAPbI $ _3 $ , displaying less energy loss during charge extraction and thus a deeper quasi-Fermi level of holes. $ ^{36} $  Consequently, a larger electron and hole quasi-Fermi level separation renders a maximum V $ _{OC} $  of 1.16 V in perovskite solar cells employing Sc $ _3 $ N@C $ _{80} $ -doped spiro-OMeTAD HTLs. This happens because higher concentrations of   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $   tend to form aggregations that roughen the HTL surface and render larger electrical inhomogeneity at the interface (Fig. S11). To manifest the advantage of the   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $  -dopant over the conventional LiTFSI-dopant, next we characterized the solar cell performance through J-V characteristics, statistics, hysteresis and stability measurement. Figure 4E compares the J-V characteristics of perovskite solar cells employing LiTFSI or   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $  -doped (1.0 mg/mL) spiro-OMeTAD HTLs, under one-sun illumination (AM1.5G). An incident photon conversion efficiency (IPCE) with integrated photocurrent density has also been measured to verify the photocurrent density (Figs. S12 & S13). The   $  J_{SC}  $   from IPCE spectra are calculated to be 22.97 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and 21.52 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   for solar cells with   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $   and LiTFSI-dopants, respectively. Due to its hydrophobicity, the   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $  -doped spiro-OMeTAD HTL is expected to shield the moisture more effectively. By contrast,   $  Sc_{3}N@C_{80}  $  -based device displayed a significantly improved device stability with a survival PCE of 17.03% after 800 hrs continuous illumination, which is among the highest survival PCEs in published literature.  $  ^{51,52}  $

Such a doping effect renders significantly improved electrical conductivity that has been visualized by c-AFM measurement. In conjunction with the lower-lying Fermi level detected by PESA, there is a synergistic effect on the improvement of PL quenching and a resultant efficient hole extraction. As a result, high solar cell PCE of  $ 20.77\% $  with a champion value of  $ 21.09\% $  has been achieved. In parallel, the hydrophobic nature of the  $ \mathrm{Sc}_3\mathrm{N}@\mathrm{C}_{80} $  enables the HTL with stronger water-shielding ability and a higher device stability. Our approach provides new direction in achieving efficient and stable perovskite solar cells and further opens the possibility of active tunability by changing the encapsulated metals within the EMFs. In any case, the facile electron transfer from spiro-OMeTAD to EMF leads to a highly efficient charge transfer layer, which preserves the high efficiency of solar cell device. The hydrophobic characteristic of the metallofullerene molecules provides the device with an outstanding stability. An additional advantage of metallofullerene based device is the material tunability that allows the fullerene cage to encapsulate different metal salts to modulate the electrical properties of the resultant HTL, since the charge transfer ability of HTL depends on the internal metal cluster in the EMF. This tunability with different lanthanide metal based metallofullerenes ( $ \mathrm{M}_3\mathrm{N}@\mathrm{C}_{80} $ ) can be utilized to optimize the efficiency and stability of the HTL as well as the overall photocell performance.
