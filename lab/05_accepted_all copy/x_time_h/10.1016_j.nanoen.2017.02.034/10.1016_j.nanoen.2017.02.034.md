# Large-grained perovskite films via   $  FA_{x}MA_{1-x}Pb(I_{x}Br_{1-x})_{3}  $   single crystal precursor for efficient solar cells

Bai-Xue Chen, Wen-Guang Li, Hua-Shang Rao, Yang-Fan Xu, Dai-Bin Kuang*, Cheng-Yong Su MOE Key Laboratory of Bioinorganic and Synthetic Chemistry, Lehn Institute of Functional Materials, School of Chemistry, Sun Yat-sen University, Guangzhou 510275, P. R. China

* E-mail: kuangdb@mail.sysu.edu.cn; Fax: (+86) 20-8411 3015.

Abstract Organometal trihalide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on multiple-site alloyed perovskite currently attract a surge of research interests owing to their extreme high solar-to-electric power conversion efficiency (PCE). Additionally, perovskite single crystals, proven to possess lower defect density, are highly anticipated to realize the full potential of perovskite materials. Herein, we for the first time proposes a facile and operable cooling-induced crystallization method to fabricate dual-site alloyed  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}Pb(I_xBr_{1 - x})_3} $  single microcrystals with tunable bandgaps, which will later be employed as encouraging precursors to form high quality perovskite films with extended grain sizes and less grain boundaries. The corresponding solar cells showcase advanced charge transport and retarded recombination, leading to satisfying photovoltaic performance and heartening device stability (a champion PCE of  $ 18.3\% $  and a satisfying maintenance of  $ \sim 83\% $  initial PCE after 2000 h aging without encapsulation),

# 1. Introduction

Heretofore, organometal trihalide perovskites which come with exceptional photoactive attributes such as wide spectrum absorption range [1], high extinction coefficient [2] and long electron-hole diffusion length [3], have achieved extraordinary photovoltaic performance from original  $ 3.8\% $  [4] to currently  $ 22.1\% $  [5] and extended extensive scopes towards sensors [6], light-emitting diodes [7], lasers [8] and photodetectors [9, 10] synchronously. Recent reports have revealed that the impressive optoelectronic properties and remarkable efficiencies are closely associated with the perovskite structures [11-13] and compositions [14,15] on the basis of a general  $ \mathrm{AMX}_3 $  formula which is comprised of a monovalent cation,  $ \mathrm{A} = [\mathrm{Cs}^+; \mathrm{Rb}^+; $  methylammonium (MA); formamidinium (FA)] [16-19]; a divalent metal  $ \mathrm{M} = (\mathrm{Pb}^{2+}; \mathrm{Sn}^{2+}) $  [17,18]; and an anion  $ \mathrm{X} = (\mathrm{Cl}^-; \mathrm{Br}^-; \mathrm{I}^-; \mathrm{SCN}^-) $  [12-26]. Amongst all these perovskites, despite pure perovskites like  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ ,  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $ , or  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  have been variedly reported for efficient perovskite solar cells (PSCs), their respective shortcomings like unsatisfying bandgap width, instability towards light, heat and moisture [27-29] confine their commercial applications.

Recently, perovskites alloys by partially substituting A, B, or X sites with their corresponding elemental or molecular analogues have become the focus to offer a promising route for highly efficient and stable PSCs. For instance, preparing a  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}PbI_3} $  alloyed perovskite could feasibly pursue larger light absorption range than pure  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  meanwhile attaining superior phase and thermal stability to pure  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  [30-34]. Moreover, the incorporation of inorganic  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $  cation makes for high-purity perovskite phases and enhanced resistance towards temperature and humidity [35,36], while mixing Br anion favors a lifted open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $ ) [37]. In addition, latest reports have disclosed dual-site alloyed perovskite like  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}Pb(I_xBr_{1 - x})_3} $  [38,39] which combined nearly all the merits of extended absorption, enhanced ambient stability and decreased trap-assisted recombination, contributing to intriguing photovoltaic performance. However, the conventional precursor solution to prepare  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}Pb(I_xBr_{1 - x})_3} $  film was attained by dissolving different percentages of constituent elements ( $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , FAI,  $ \mathrm{PbBr}_2 $ , MABr) in DMF/DMSO solution, whose homogeneity is affected by their respective solubility, implying an adverse impact on precise stoichiometry. Very recently, a hot trend of devising single crystal-based PSCs has attracted myriad concerns due to the larger grain sizes and less grain boundaries in perovskite films, favoring minimal defect densities [40-44]. Hence, preparing uniform and robust precursors to fabricate defect-free and highly crystalline perovskite film is vastly desirable for practical PSC manufacturing.

Inspired by these issues, herein, the phase-pure and bandgap-tunable  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}Pb(I_xBr_{1 - x})_3} $  single microcrystals with precise stoichiometry were prepared via a simple cooling-induced crystallization (CIC) approach, which were dissolved in mixed DMF/DMSO as a precursor solution of perovskite film. The resultant  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}Pb(I_xBr_{1 - x})_3} $  single microcrystals derived perovskite films possessed extended grain sizes and less grain boundaries, which achieved an

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Synthesis of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Br  $   and   $  NH=CHNH_{3}I  $

The  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{Br} $  was synthesized by blending  $ 30~\mathrm{mL} $  of hydrobromic acid (55% wt aqueous) and  $ 40~\mathrm{mL} $  of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_2 $  solution and stirring in ice-water bath for  $ 2\mathrm{h} $ , and then crystallized by reduced pressure distillation. The  $ \mathrm{NH} = \mathrm{CHNH}_3\mathrm{I} $  was synthesized by a similar approach but substituting the hydrobromic acid and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_2 $  solution with hydroiodic acid (57% wt aqueous) and FAAc. Such crude products were purified by recrystallization with diethyl ether and dried in vacuum.

# 2.2. Synthesis of   $  FA_{x}MA_{1-x}Pb(I_{x}Br_{1-x})_{3}  $   single microcrystals

The commercialized   $  \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_{2}}  $  , FAAc, and afore-prepared MABr were dissolved in excessive hydroiodic acid (57% wt aqueous) and hydrobromic acid (55% wt aqueous) mixed solution at 100 °C with continuous stirring, while cooled down to room temperature, the small and luster   $  FA_{x}MA_{1-x}PbI_{x}Br_{1-x}  $   crystallites came out and precipitated at the bottom. Such resultants were purified by recrystallization with diethyl ether and dried in vacuum for 48 h. The value of x in   $  FA_{x}MA_{1-x}Pb(I_{x}Br_{1-x})_{3}  $   were decided by the ratio of FAAc to MABr.

# 2.3. Fabrication of perovskite film

The single microcrystals derived precursor solution was prepared by dissolving 1.35 M as-synthesized   $  FA_{x}MA_{1-x}Pb(I_{x}Br_{1-x})_{3}  $   crystallites in a mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO with a volume ratio of 4:1, while the raw materials derived precursor solution was prepared by dissolving commercialized   $  PbI_{2}  $  ,   $  PbBr_{2}  $  , as-synthesized MABr and FAI in the same mixed solvent. The perovskite film was obtained by dropping chlorobenzene (200   $  \mu  $  L) onto the spinning substrate after the spin-coating step 20s at 5000 rpm. The substrate was then heated at 150 °C for 15 min on a hotplate and then cooled down to room temperature.

# 2.4. Fabrication of perovskite solar cell

FTO glass (8 Ω per square) was pretreated with 4M HCl solution and zinc powder. The etched substrates were then cleaned with deionized water, alcohol and acetone via an ultrasonic process. The Nb-doped TiO₂ compact layer was formed by spin-coating the obtained colloidal solution [45] diluted three fold in anhydrous ethanol at 2000 rpm for 60 s, followed by annealing at 500 °C for 1 h in ambient air. The Nb-doped TiO₂ compact layers were immersed in a 0.04 M aqueous solution of TiCl₄ at 70 °C for 30 minutes before being sintered at 520 °C for 30 minutes. The HTM solution comprised 72.3 mg mL⁻¹ spiro-OMeTAD (2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N di-p-methoxyphenyl-amine)-9,9'-spirobifluoren-e)/chlorobenzene with addition of 28.8 μL 4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP) and 17.5 μL bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSi) solution (520 mg in acetonitrile) was then deposited onto the perovskite films by spin-coating at 5000 rpm for 60 s. Finally, the gold electrode was deposited by magnetron sputtering.

# 3. Results and discussions

# 3.1. Preparation and characterization of   $  FA_{x}MA_{1-x}Pb(I_{x}Br_{1-x})_{3}  $   single microcrystals.

A cooling-induced crystallization (CIC) protocol (depicted in Fig. 1a) was devised to prepare  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}Pb(I_xBr_{1 - x})_3} $  single microcrystals with different x values, whose polyhedron morphology was shown in Fig. S1. According to the XRD patterns presented in Fig. 1b, it is obvious that the incorporation of  $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $  cation has feasibly stabilized the  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}Pb(I_xBr_{1 - x})_3} $  microcrystals as single perovskite phase at room temperature throughout the entire composition range, whereas the pure  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  crystallite still laid as a non-perovskite phase. Additionally, a monotonic shift of the (100) plane to higher diffraction degree is observed with the increase of Br content, which is inconsistent with the shift of the lattice constant by introducing a fraction of smaller halide anions. The experimentally observed stoichiometry of  $ (\mathrm{FAPbI}_3)_x(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_3)_{1 - x} $  were measured by  $ ^1 H $  NMR spectra [32] (seen in Fig. S2), whose results were tabulated and compared with the theoretical ratio in Table S1, showing a tiny difference from the ideal composition due to the solubility variety of different raw constituents. Moreover, a conspicuous blue-shift has been discovered in the UV-vis and PL patterns (shown in Fig. S3) with the investment ratio of Br increased from  $ 10\% $  to  $ 75\% $ , indicating a tunable bandgap from  $ 1.51\mathrm{eV} $  to  $ 1.65\mathrm{eV} $ , reconciling to the compositional variation of  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}Pb(I_xBr_{1 - x})_3} $  single microcrystals. Fig. 1c shows the high-resolution TEM image with its corresponding selective area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern (inset) of  $ \mathrm{FA_{0.84}MA_{0.16}Pb(I_{0.84}Br_{0.16})_3} $  microcrystals. The diffraction pattern exhibits a square array of dots, which implies its single crystalline characteristics of this

# 3.2. Characterization of   $  FA_{x}MA_{1-x}Pb(I_{x}Br_{1-x})_{3}  $   single microcrystals-derived perovskite films.

Fig. 2a schematically describes the route of fabricating  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.84}\mathrm{MA}_{0.16}\mathrm{Pb(I_{0.84}Br_{0.16})_3} $  films by two different precursor solutions; one is obtained by single microcrystals' dissolution while the other is attained by directly dissolving the raw materials of commercialized  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ ,  $ \mathrm{PbBr}_2 $ , synthesized FAI and MABr, respectively, which achieves similar composition of final perovskite films (Seen in Fig S4). Impressively, the average colloidal sizes of the clusters which act as crystal nucleus during the formation of perovskite films are  $ \sim 493 $  nm and  $ \sim 1360 $  nm for the raw material- and the single microcrystal derived precursor solution, respectively, according to the dynamic light scattering (DLS) results (seen in Fig. S5). Consequently, the grain sizes of the corresponding single microcrystals (SMC) derived perovskite films are  $ 750\sim 1500~\mathrm{nm} $ , nearly 3 times large than that of raw materials (RM) derived perovskite films ( $ 300\sim 500 $  nm), shown in Fig. 2b-c (SEM images). Furthermore, as plotted in Fig. S6 and Table S2, the hole- and electron-dominated devices were prepared to evaluate the carrier mobility of these two perovskite films by conducting space charge limited current method [43-45]. A pronounced increase of both hole- and electron-mobility for SMC derived perovskite film, demonstrating an improved charge transport caused by higher crystallinity and large grain sizes in perovskite films.

# 3.3. Photovoltaic performance of single microcrystals-derived PSCs.

The single microcrystals- and raw materials derived perovskite films are further applied as planar perovskite solar cells with the structure of FTO/ Nb-doped  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ( $ \sim 85~\mathrm{nm} $ )/  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1-x}Pb(I_xBr_{1 - x})_3} $  ( $ \sim 500~\mathrm{nm} $ )/ Spiro-MeOTAD ( $ \sim 150~\mathrm{nm} $ )/ Au ( $ \sim 100~\mathrm{nm} $ ) (seen in Fig. S7a). The ascertained optimal composition of  $ x = 0.84 $  has been demonstrated by the reduced defect of perovskite film and outstanding photovoltaic performance shown in Fig. S8 and Table S3. Fig. 3a shows the current density-voltage ( $ J - V $ ) characteristics of the best-performing PSCs devices based on these two types of PSCs with optimized composition, whose extracted performance parameters are shown in Table 1. Moreover, the statistical distributions of performance characteristics obtained from 30 cells are displayed in Fig. S9. Such  $ \mathrm{FA_{0.84}MA_{0.16}Pb(I_{0.84}Br_{0.16})_3} $  SMC derived PSCs have achieved outstanding performance claiming an average  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  of 23.4 mA/cm², an  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  of 1006 mV and a fill factor ( $ FF $ ) of 0.74, yielding an average PCE of 17.6%, whereas the PCE of the champion cell is 18.3% ( $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} = 23.2~\mathrm{mA / cm^2} $ ,  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} = 1044~\mathrm{mV} $ ,  $ FF = 0.75 $ ). As for the RM derived PSCs, an average PCE of 14.9% was obtained, with an average  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  of 23.0 mA/cm²,  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  of 979 mV and  $ FF $  of 0.66 (champion PCE=15.2%). Such pronounced increase of  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  and  $ FF $  stems from the reduced defect-assisted trapping recombination and the enhanced carrier mobility caused by less grain boundaries and larger grain sizes, respectively. The EIS measurement was further conducted to interpret the interfacial charge-transport process and carrier recombination [46]. The Nyquist plots of these cells were measured at each  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  bias in dark and depicted inside Fig. 3b. The values of  $ R_{\mathrm{HTM}} $  and  $ R_{\mathrm{rec}} $  fitted from such Nyquist plots were tabulated in Table S4. Notably, the  $ R_{\mathrm{HTM}} $  of RM-derived perovskite cells ( $ 68.37~\Omega~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ ) is a bit larger than that of SMC-derived perovskite cells ( $ 61.28~\Omega~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ ), pointing out a weaken charge transport at HTM/Perovskite interface for RM-derived devices caused by lower carrier mobility

# 3.4. Recombination process of single microcrystals-derived PSCs.

To investigate the influence of perovskite film quality on carrier recombination character, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements [50,51] which indicate the defect states and recombination process of perovskite films were carried out, whose results were depicted in Fig. 4a and b. Apparently, the SMC-derived perovskite film has presented higher PL intensity than RM-derived perovskite film, indicating less defects existed inside. Furthermore, time constants  $ \tau_{\mathrm{e}} $  are obtained by fitting TRPL curves to evaluate exciton lifetime of perovskite. As a result, the fitted time constants  $ \tau_{\mathrm{e}} $  of SMC-derived and RM-derived perovskite are calculated as 1295.61 ns and 871.00 ns, respectively, demonstrating a retarded recombination process for the former. Overall, both the ameliorated PL quenching and slow PL decay of single microcrystals derived perovskite film are ascribed to the decreased trap-site recombination centers thanks to the reduced grain boundaries by utilizing single-crystal precursors, which corresponds to the aforementioned SEM results.

# 3.5. Device stability measurements.

The aging test of perovskite solar cells prepared by these two different precursors were conducted to assess their device stability in ambient condition (dry air, dark), presenting their respective potential in commercialization. As shown in Fig. 5b, the  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.84}\mathrm{MA}_{0.16}\mathrm{Pb(I_{0.84}Br_{0.16})_3} $  single microcrystals derived PSCs has remained  $ \sim 92\% $  and  $ \sim 83\% $  of the initial photovoltaic performance after  $ 700\mathrm{h} $  and  $ 2000\mathrm{h} $  aging test, respectively, whereas the raw materials derived perovskite cell has only preserved  $ \sim 77\% $  and  $ \sim 62\% $  of the initial efficiency, in accordance with the preferable stability of perovskite itself for the former. EIS and TPC measurements [54] were further applied to elucidate the changes at the interfaces and inside the perovskite films for PSCs during the aging. As shown in Fig. S13a, the  $ R_{\mathrm{rec}} $  values decreases obviously from 179 to  $ 154\Omega $   $ \mathrm{cm}^2 $  after  $ 700\mathrm{h} $  aging and continues declining to  $ 131.8\Omega \mathrm{cm}^2 $  after  $ 2000\mathrm{h} $  aging for the RM

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, preparing  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}Pb(I_xBr_{1 - x})_3} $  single microcrystal precursor via cooling-induced crystallization offers a simple, reproducible and efficient approach for the design of high quality perovskite films with extended grain sizes and reduced grain boundaries. The  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.84}\mathrm{MA}_{0.16}\mathrm{Pb(I_{0.84}Br_{0.16})_3} $  single microcrystal derived perovskite solar cells exhibit impressive photovoltaic performance of  $ 18.3\% $  which is much superior to that of conventionally RM-derived PSC (15.2%). The enhanced  $ FF $  for SMC-derived PSC are ascribed to the three times larger perovskite grains, which contribute to an advanced carrier transport. Moreover, the reduced defect density caused by less grain boundaries undoubtedly results in the lifted  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $ , indicating a retarded recombination process. On the other hand, the single microcrystal precursor also motivates the strengthened stability of PSCs on account of highly-stabilized perovskite
