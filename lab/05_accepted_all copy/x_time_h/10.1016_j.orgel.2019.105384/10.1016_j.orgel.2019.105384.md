# Abstract

A fullerene derivative,  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM (bis-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester), is introduced as a dopant in the methylammonium lead iodide ( $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ ) perovskite active layer to form a perovskite solar cell, demonstrating increased power conversion efficiency by  $ 12\% $  compared to neat  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based devices, reduced photocurrent hysteresis and long-term stability in an unpacked environment. By measuring the ultrafast carrier dynamics of neat  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ ,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ : PCBM,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ :  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM active layers with transient absorption spectroscopy, we found that the  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM as the electron acceptor in perovskite active layer leads to reduced trap-assisted recombination and effective charge transfer into the electron acceptor. The carrier kinetics in the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ :  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM active layer is not seriously deteriorated during the exposure in atmospheric environment. High performance in perovskite solar cells well correlates improved microscopic ultrafast carrier dynamics, providing a deep insight to uncover the functioning of hybrid solar cells.

# Keywords

$ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM, Ultrafast dynamics, Charge transfer, Charge recombination, Hybrid perovskite solar cells

# 1. Introduction

Nowadays, the production of energy becomes an urgent problem due to the approaching absence of fossil fuel. Photovoltaic cells as the potential devices of renewable solar energy have drawn more attention from the public and commercial attraction [1]. Especially, hybrid organic-inorganic perovskite-based solar cells (PSCs) are of high optical absorption efficiency, exceptionally long carrier diffusion length, convenient manufacture [1-3], and achieving over  $ 24\% $  power conversion efficiency (PCE) to date [4-7]. However, poor stability and severe degradation in atmospheric air, as well as the toxicity of lead are still the obstacle for the commercialization of PSCs [8-15]. The efficiency and stability of PSCs closely correlates with the crystalline quality of perovskite, thus, extensive efforts have been made to improve the film-processing techniques. Doping perovskite with the electron acceptor as a composite light-harvesting layer could result in uniform, compact and trap-free perovskite active films [16-20]. Fullerene derivatives, as electron acceptor dopants, can effectively passivate the charge trapping state in the PSCs to achieve a highly uniform perovskite layer, significantly enhancing the conductivity and stability as well as less hysteresis of PSCs [17,21]. The  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM (bis-analog of phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM)), can take effect in passivating pinholes and trap sites when blended with perovskites, and serves as the electron acceptor in carrier transportation processes [22,23]. However, the investigations concerning ultrafast carrier dynamics in doped perovskite layers are still lacking. It is quite necessary to investigate how the doping of the electron acceptor does influence carrier dynamics in light-absorbing layers.

Despite the lack of investigations of doped perovskite layers, there are lots of studies concerning ultrafast carrier dynamics of PSCs, most of which focused on the carrier relaxation in perovskite layers and interfacial transportation processes between the perovskite/transporting layer   $  [5,24-29]  $  . In general, the microscopic carrier evolution in PSCs can be temporally traced as follows: the absorption of light triggers the light-harvesting reaction instantaneously. The electron-hole pairs are formed upon exciton dissociation within picoseconds, and the electron and hole rapidly relax to the

edges of the conduction band (CB) and valence band (VB). With the assistance of electron and/or hole transporting material (ETM/HTM), the electron-hole pairs can be effectively separated due to the charge transfer into ETM/HTM, accompanying with relatively slow recombination. When blended with dopant materials like fullerene derivatives, polymers or nanostructures, which act as electron acceptors, the high-efficiency electron transfer and reduced trap-assisted recombination can both be achieved in PSCs [20,30].

Exploiting with the ultrafast spectroscopy, the carrier dynamics and interfacial reactions of the PSCs have been widely investigated, and attracted inter-discipline concerns from material science, spectroscopy and physical chemistry communities. Many time-resolved methods, consisting of transient absorption spectroscopy (TAS), time-resolved terahertz spectroscopy, transient photoluminescence spectroscopy, microwave photoconductivity measurement, had been used for investigations of the carrier cooling, charge recombination, charge transfer processes in PSCs in the time scale from femtoseconds to microseconds [31-36]. In recent work, the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  (methylammonium lead iodide):  $ \mathrm{C}_{70} $  architecture was realized, and the trap-assisted recombination was investigated with time-resolved optical spectroscopy, manifesting effective electron transportation and reduced trapping recombination [30]. Doping Au/Ag nanostructures into mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer of  $ \mathrm{MAPbBr}_3 $  (methylammonium lead tribromide) PSCs could enhance the photo-induced carrier generation, accelerate electron injection into ETM and suppress electron/hole recombination [20].

In this work, we highlight the feasibility of a fullerene dopant,  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM, as the electron acceptor in perovskite active layer.  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM can assist the growth of perovskite layer with less defects and cross-link with the perovskite structure to effectively resist atmospheric moisture and oxygen [22]. By correlating the microscopic carrier dynamics with the macroscopic performance of PSCs, it allows to explore deep insight into the influence of the  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM dopant on the carrier dynamics. The TAS measurements of neat  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ ,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ : PCBM and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ :  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM active layers exhibit that the lifetime of carrier population is obviously reduced due to the  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM doping, implicating the suppressed trap-assisted

recombination and great electron-extracting property in the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ :  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM active layer. Besides serving as the electron acceptor material, the  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM also acts as a component to protect the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ :  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM active layer from atmospheric degradation. Meanwhile, the degraded active layers, exposed in ambient air for 24 hours, are investigated with the TAS technique. The transient kinetics is not significantly modified due to the atmospheric degradation, suggesting that the conductivity is not seriously deteriorated in the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ :  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM active layer.

# 2. Experimental details

# 2.1. Sample preparation

$ \mathrm{Ni(CH_3COO)_2\cdot 4H_2O} $  powder (99.99%),  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{CH}_2)_2\mathrm{OH} $  (99.5%),  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and DMSO are purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Co. Ltd.  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  (MAI) is purchased from YOUXUAN Technology Co. Ltd. PCBM,  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM and Bphen are purchased from Nichem Fine Technology Co. Ltd. GBL is purchased from ACROS ORGANICS Co. Ltd.

To prepare the perovskite precursor solution doped with PCBM or  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM,  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  are dissolved with a molar ratio of 1:1 in GBL and DMSO ( $ v/v $ , 7:3) mixed solvent, and  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM or PCBM is added with the concentration of 6 mg/mL accordingly, then stir the mixture overnight at  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 8 h and filtered through with a  $ 0.45\mu \mathrm{m} $  filter before further procedures.

$ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  precursor is processed by dissolving  $ 50~\mathrm{mg} $   $ \mathrm{Ni(CH_3COO)_2\cdot 4H_2O} $  powder into  $ 1\mathrm{mLCH}_3\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{CH}_2)_2\mathrm{OH} $  solution with  $ 12~\mu \mathrm{L} $   $ \mathrm{NH}_2(\mathrm{CH}_2)_2\mathrm{OH} $  as a stabilizing agent to enhance its solubility.

The PSCs based on the structure of  $ \mathrm{FTO / NiO_x / } $  active layer/PCBM/Bphen/Ag, shown in Fig. 1, are fabricated to test the photovoltaic performances of the PSCs, including PCE, photocurrent hysteresis and stability. Three kinds of active layers,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ ,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ : PCBM and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ :  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM, are used for comparison. The components in active layers are also presented in Fig. 1.  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  and PCBM are selected

# 2.3.TAS experimental conditions

All TAS measurements are carried out in the commercial ultrafast spectroscopy (Helios Fire, Ultrafast Systems, Inc.), an automatic femtosecond transient absorption spectrometer. The femtosecond laser amplifier (Astrella, Coherent, Inc.) delivers 800 nm laser pulse with pulse duration of  $ \sim 35 $  fs. A part of laser energy is guided into the optical parametric amplifier (TOPAS, Coherent, Inc.), which produces the pump pulse with center wavelength of  $ 500~\mathrm{nm} $ . The rest of the femtosecond laser enters the Helios

The representative differential spectra of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ :  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM sample at different pump-probe time delays are shown in Fig. 6, which are extracted from corresponding 2D contour plot in Fig. S1 in Supplementary Material. The transient absorption (TA) signal depicts the transient transmission change due to the different time delay, which reflects the time evolution of the photoexcited states population.

The electron acceptor spatially separated the electron and hole in the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  active layer, and the reduction of spatial overlap should lower the recombination probability at early times. # 4. Conclusion

The presence of  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM, as an additive in methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI $ _3 $ ) active layer, is found to promote power conversion efficiency by  $ 12.19\% $  relative to the neat MAPbI $ _3 $ -based perovskite solar cell, reduce hysteresis, and sustain  $ 87.12\% $  of its initial power conversion efficiency in an unpacked environment. By comparing the transient absorption spectroscopy of MAPbI $ _3 $  active layer, MAPbI $ _3 $ : PCBM active layer and MAPbI $ _3 $ :  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM active layer, enhanced charge transfer in the MAPbI $ _3 $ / $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM interface and decreased trap-mediated recombination are found in the MAPbI $ _3 $ :  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM active layer. Also, the carrier relaxation and recombination dynamics of the MAPbI $ _3 $ :  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM active layer are not deteriorated during the exposure in atmospheric environment. The fullerene electron acceptor, doped in MAPbI $ _3 $  active layer, promotes the performances of PSCs, and the improvement is validated and understood with ultrafast spectroscopy from microscopic perspective.
