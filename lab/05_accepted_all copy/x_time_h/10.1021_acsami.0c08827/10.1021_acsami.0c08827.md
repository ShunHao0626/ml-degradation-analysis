# ABSTRACT

The perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on cesium lead bromide  $ (\mathrm{CsPbBr}_3) $  with outstanding environmental stability and low preparation cost are regarded as one of the most promising photovoltaic device for commercial applications. However, the performance of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  PSCs can be badly deteriorated by the intense charge recombination arising from the ionic defects at the grain boundaries of perovskite film. To cope up with this issue, we adopt an amino acid of L-lysine with two amino and one carboxyl groups as a chemical additive to incorporate into perovskite film to

Further, the grain size of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite is boosted from 688 to over 1000 nm after L-lysine incorporation as a result of the decreased nucleation rate and the sufficient growth of perovskite, which effectively reduces the grain boundaries for load defects. This work provides an effective multifunctional additive for imperfection passivation and grain size enlargement of perovskite to build PSCs with high efficiency and stability.

# INTRODUCTION

In a very short time span, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has skyrocketed from 3.8% to 25.2% at an astounding speed by optimizing preparation processes, developing high performance photovoltaic materials and designing new device structures.  $  ^{1-5}  $   It is

Unfortunately, the instability of organic-inorganic hybrid PSC under conditions of high temperature, humidity etc poses a hurdle toward its commercialization, mainly because the organic components contained in the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite are easily volatile in such extreme environment, which can further lead to the destruction of the perovskite structure. All inorganic   $  CsPbI_{x}Br_{3-x}  $   (x = 0, 1, 2 or 3) halides are synthesized by replacing organic ions (e.g.   $  MA^{+}  $  and   $  FA^{+}  $  ) in hybrid perovskite with inorganic   $  Cs^{+}  $  to strengthen thermal-tolerance, but the   $  CsPbI_{x}Br_{3-x}  $   (x > 0) perovskite is easily turn to non-photoactive layer in a humid condition.  $  ^{6-8}  $   Therefore, air-stable   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   is considered as a promising perovskite material in PSCs because of its extraordinary thermal and humid stability.

However, the wide energy band gap of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   (  $  E_{g} = 2.25 \sim 2.37  $   eV) gives rise to a narrow absorption range making the efficiency of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs much inferior than the organic-inorganic hybrid devices.  $  ^{9}  $   Although the iodine substitution for bromine can broaden the light absorption range of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite, at the same time, the stability of perovskite will be severely impaired. In addition to the intrinsical wide   $  E_{g}  $   nature of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite, the charge recombination within perovskite films is also a dominant reason in retarding the efficiency improvement of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs. Therefore, the suppression of the charge recombination resulting from the defects at grain boundaries of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film is a fundamental importance to get PCE rocketed without giving up the stability of PSCs. # CONCLUSIONS

To sum up, we developed an efficient additive engineering technique for trap states dual-passivation and grain size enhancement of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film by the incorporation of L-lysine. The two amino and one carboxyl groups of L-lysine simultaneously passivate the uncoordinated   $  Br^{-}  $  and   $  Pb^{2+}  $   (  $  Cs^{+}  $  ) through hydrogen-bonding and Lewis-base electronic passivation of metal ions, respectively, which remarkably decreases the defects density and thereby inhibits the charge recombination. The grain size of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film is increased to more than 1 micron due to the slow nucleation rate and sufficient growth resulting from the high crystallinity and stability as well as porous structure of   $  PbBr_{2}  $   incorporated with L-lysine, leading

Preparation of L-lysine Incorporated  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  Precursor Solution. A series of  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  precursor solution with different L-lysine addition ratios (0%, 1%, 3% and 5%) were synthesized by dissolving different concentration of L-lysine to  $ 1\mathrm{M} $   $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  in DMF solution. Firstly, the fluorine-incorporated tin oxide (FTO) substrate was etched with Zinc powers and 2 M HCl solution to produce an etched line. A multi-step spin-coating method was employed to synthesize the perovskite layer. Concretely, 1 M   $  PbBr_{2}  $   DMF solution containing different concentrations of L-lysine was deposited onto the pre-heated (80 °C) substrate by spin-coating at 2000 rpm for 30 s and heated at 80 °C for 30 min to remove the residual solvent.
