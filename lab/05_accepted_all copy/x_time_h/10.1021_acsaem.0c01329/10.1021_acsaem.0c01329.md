# Abstract

In this study, we used a bifunctional organic disulfide, di-5-(1-methyltetrazole) disulfide (  $  T_{2}  $  ), in perovskite films to improve the performance and stability of planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs). The results indicate two major functions of   $  T_{2}  $   in PSCs: improving the perovskite crystal quality due to the interaction between   $  T_{2}  $   and perovskite, and eliminating   $  Pb^{0}  $   and   $  I^{0}  $   defects by redox reaction, allowing for the regeneration of   $  T_{2}  $   in the process. We achieved a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs with   $  T_{2}  $   of 20.67%, a 15 % increase compared to the control device (17.9%). Furthermore, PSC devices with   $  T_{2}  $   exhibited enhanced operational stability and storage stability. During 140s of operational, there was no evicent decrease of PCE. The device maintained 95% of its initial efficiency without encapsulation under ambient conditions (20°C, 40% humidity) for 2 months.

# Introduction

Organic/inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have recently attracted widespread interest due to their high absorption coefficient, tunable band gap, long exciton lifetime, bipolar charge transport, and easy preparation process. Over the past few years, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has improved from 3.8% to 25.2%.  $  ^{1-7}  $   PSCs are an extremely promising alternative to next-generation solar cells.

Typically, the perovskite materials have an  $ \mathrm{ABX}_3 $  crystal structure, where A is a monovalent cation such as methylammonium cation  $ (\mathrm{MA}^{+}) $ , formamidinium cation  $ (\mathrm{FA}^{+}) $ , caesium cation  $ (\mathrm{Cs}^{+}) $  or their mixtures; B is a bivalent metal cation such as lead cation  $ (\mathrm{Pb}^{2+}) $

Qin et al. propose a benzoquinone additive to improve the quality of the perovskite and suppress trap formation during device operation. Both the PCE and the stability of PSCs have been enhanced as a result. $ ^{25} $  Typically, most of the additives studied to eliminate the Pb $  ^{0}  $  include functional groups (-OH, -COOH, NH $  _{4}^{+}  $ ) and atoms with long-pair electrons, which not only suppress the formation of Pb $  ^{0}  $  but also improve the perovskite film quality by interacting between additives and perovskite. $ ^{29-33} $  To date, however, most proposed additives are sacrificial agents that are consumed quickly during operation. In 2019, to solve this problem, Wang et al. Resorted to inorganic europium ion pairs as a reusable additive for oxidizing Pb $  ^{0}  $  and reducing iodine I $  ^{0}  $  defects, significantly improving device stability. $ ^{17} $  The redox couple, thiolate (T $  ^{-}  $ )/disulfide (di-5-(1-methyltetrazole) disulfide, T $  _{2}  $ ), reported by Mingkui Wang and co-workers, was first used in dye-sensitized solar cells as redox couple, achieving impressive PCE. $ ^{34} $  We believe

In this paper, we applied   $  T_{2}  $   in a perovskite precursor solution to produce free-of-defects perovskite films with better morphology. Resisted recombination and reduced trap density resulted in a PCE of over 20%. Furthermore, operational stability and storage stability were improved due to the cyclic regeneration characteristics of the oxide reaction between   $  T^{-}/T_{2}  $   and the trap center (  $  Pb^{0}  $   or   $  I^{0}  $  ), which can maintain 95% of its initial efficiency after 2 months in ambient conditions (  $  20^{\circ}C  $   and 40% humidity). This paves a new way to improve both device performance and long-term durability by usage of functional organic redox couple.

We also performed X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements to determine the changes in the chemical bonding of the perovskite films with and without   $  T_{2}  $   (Figure 4). The four main peaks of Cs (Cs 3d), Pb (Pb   $  4f_{7/2}  $  , Pb   $  4f_{5/2}  $  ), I (I   $  3d_{5/2}  $  , I   $  3d_{3/2}  $  ), and Br (Br 3d) are shown in Figure 4a, b, c, and d, respectively. Evidently, the binding energies of Cs, Pb, I, and Br all shifted to a higher position, a result of a change in the electron cloud density, which indicates that there is an interaction between the   $  T_{2}  $   and the perovskite, further suggesting that the stability of the perovskite is enhanced. In Figure 4b, two weak shoulder peaks in perovskite films without   $  T_{2}  $   are marked with a yellow circle at a binding energy of 136.5 eV and 142.8 eV, corresponding to the characteristic peak of metal lead (  $  Pb^{0}  $  ).  $  ^{17}  $

The   $  T_{2}  $   can be reduced to   $  T^{-}  $  , and   $  T^{-}  $   can be oxidized to   $  T_{2}  $   by   $  I^{0}  $  , bringing about the regeneration of   $  T_{2}  $   as well as the elimination of   $  Pb^{0}  $   and   $  I^{0}  $   defects. While the control sample is hardly change. It means that   $  T_{2}  $   can promote electron transfer for   $  Pb^{0}  $   to   $  I^{0}  $  . We can infer that the addition of   $  T_{2}  $   is beneficial for improving the stability of PSC devices by means of the above mentioned cyclic regeneration mechanism.
