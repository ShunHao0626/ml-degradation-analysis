# Interface Engineering of Air-Stable n-Doping Fullerene-Modified TiO  $  _{2}  $  Electron Transport Layer for Highly Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Bingjie Wang, Jianming Yang,  $  ^{*}  $   Linyang Lu, Wei Xiao, Haiyan Wu, Shaobing Xiong, Jianxin Tang, Chungang Duan, and Qinye Bao  $  ^{*}  $

As one common electron transport material for planar n-i-p perovskite solar cell, titanium dioxide (TiO  $  _{2}  $  ) compact layer has several challenging issues, such as surface hydroxyl groups, high defect density, and unmatched energy levels, causing severe energy loss and poor stability at contact. To solve these problems, the authors introduce a thin [6,6]-phenyl-C  $  _{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) interlayer doped with an air stable n-type dopant, 3-dimethyl-2-phenyl-2,3-dihydro-1H-benzoimidazole (DMBI) to modify the TiO  $  _{2}  $   surface. The state-of-the-art characterizations demonstrate such modification significantly improves charge transfer at MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /TiO  $  _{2}  $   interface together with smaller energy level offset, leading to suppressed charge recombination. High-quality perovskite film with larger crystal grain size grows on the n-doped PCBM/TiO  $  _{2}  $   attributed to the better surface affinity. As a result, the average power conversion efficiency of perovskite solar cell exhibits a prominent improvement from 17.46% to 20.14%, with an enhancement in all device photovoltaic parameters. In addition, the stability of the device with n-doped PCBM/TiO  $  _{2}  $   is much better than that of the control device with the bare TiO  $  _{2}  $   due to hydrophobicity nature of PCBM and low defect densities in the perovskite film and at the interface. This work indicates that many further device performance improvements should be conceivable by focusing on the perovskite interface.

Titanium dioxide (TiO $ _2 $ ) is one common electron transport layer (ETL) widely used in PSCs; $ ^{[31]} $  however, there are some drawbacks of solution-processed TiO $ _2 $  that actually impede device performance. The sur-

face hydroxyl groups of   $  TiO_{2}  $   cause chemical degradation of the first few nanometers of the perovskite layer at the contact.  $  ^{[32]}  $   When exposed to ultraviolet light, oxygen vacancies could form as deep level trap states in   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer, triggering severe nonradiative recombination loss.  $  ^{[33]}  $   The relatively low conductivity of   $  TiO_{2}  $   also leads to decreased efficiency and strong hysteresis behavior.  $  ^{[34]}  $   Therefore, the bare   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer is not an ideal ETL employed in PSCs. Interfacial modification can effectively passivate surface defect (trap) states of the metal oxide, and tune interfacial electronic structures at perovskite/  $  TiO_{2}  $   contact to enhance charge extraction and device stability. For example, Tavakoli et al. deposited an amorphous layer of a-  $  SnO_{2}  $   on top of   $  TiO_{2}  $   through which the enhanced efficiency and stability of PSCs have been demonstrated.  $  ^{[35]}  $   Fullerene derivatives were reported as effective interlayer materials to modify   $  TiO_{2}  $   via passivating surface defects and reducing recombination channels at interface,  $  ^{[36-38]}  $   resulting in stable efficiencies with low hysteresis.  $  ^{[39,40]}  $

Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL are further performed to characterize the electron extraction efficiency at perovskite/TiO  $  _{2}  $  before and after interlayer. In Figure 2a, MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ /n-doped PCBM/TiO  $  _{2}  $  shows the smallest PL intensity, indicating that n-doped PCBM interlayer helps enhancing charge transfer at interface. Moreover, the PL peaks shows a blue shift from 768 to 756 nm, which could be attributed to n-doped PCBM induced passivation of the traps.  $  ^{[38]}  $  Figure 2b shows the Time-resolved PL (TRPL) spectra consisting a fast decay component and a slow decay component obtained by fitting the decay curves with a bi-exponential function. The related parameters are summarized in Table S1 (Supporting Information). The MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ /TiO  $  _{2}  $  displays the average decay lifetime of 130.67 ns. After PCBM and n-doped PCBM modification, the average lifetimes dramatically decrease to 28.81 and 26.93 ns, respectively. The results imply more efficient charger transfer from perovskite to TiO  $  _{2}  $  and thus less charge recombination loss.

To explore the role of interlayer on the top perovskite film, the top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was carried out to study the film morphology in Figure 3a–c. Perovskites that directly grow on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface show the average crystal grain size of 200 nm. Clearly, the high-quality perovskite films with larger crystal grain size of 250 and 300 nm are observed when depositing on the surface of   $  PCBM/TiO_{2}  $   and n-doped   $  PCBM/TiO_{2}  $ , respectively. Especially for   $  MAPbI_{3}/n-doped PCBM/TiO_{2}  $ , the largest grains exhibit a size over 500 nm. Larger grain sizes with reduced grain boundaries yield less defects, which is the key issue for the enhanced performance in PSCs. The corresponding perovskite film on n-doped   $  PCBM/TiO_{2}  $   becomes smooth, where the root-mean-square surface roughness is 10.2 nm, in contrast to 20.8 nm of the perovskite film on the bare   $  TiO_{2}  $ , see Figure S3 (Supporting Information). Such improvement in the morphology is related to the improved affinity of perovskite to n-doped   $  PCBM/TiO_{2}  $ .  $  ^{[47]}  $   The perovskite precursor solution on   $  PCBM/TiO_{2}  $   and n-doped   $  PCBM/TiO_{2}  $   produce the contact angles of 47.9° and 35.5°, respectively, indicating that the addition of n-type dopant DMBI is beneficial to improve the affinity of perovskite to PCBM (Figure S4, Supporting Information). The crystalline properties of the perovskite films are studied by X-ray diffraction (XRD). In Figure 3d, all perovskite films show clear and sharp diffraction featured peaks, and there is no additional

peak, indicating the formation of the typical cubic perovskite phase crystal structure. It is noted that the perovskite film coated on n-doped PCBM/TiO  $  _{2}  $  has the strongest intensity of the (110) peak, confirming an improved perovskite crystallization. The full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the (110) peaks are 0.467, 0.445, and 0.433 for the perovskites coated on the TiO  $  _{2}  $ , PCBM/TiO  $  _{2}  $ , and n-doped PCBM/TiO  $  _{2}  $ , respectively, in good agreement with the grain size obtained from SEM. Moreover, the optical absorption property is also enhanced in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ /n-doped PCBM/TiO  $  _{2}  $ , where the ultraviolet-visible absorption spectrum exhibits a pronounced increased absorption over the wavelength range of 400–500 nm (Figure S5, Supporting information). The optical absorption of the TiO  $  _{2}  $ , PCBM/TiO  $  _{2}  $ , and n-doped PCBM/TiO  $  _{2}  $  show negligible change because the PCBM layer is very thin. The improved absorption is thus expected to originate from the enlarged gain size of the perovskite crystals.  $  ^{[38]}  $

Figure 4a shows the  $ J-V $  curves measured under simulated AM 1.5G illumination (100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $ ), and the device performance parameters are listed in Table 1. The control device with the bare TiO $ _2 $  features an average PCE of 17.16% with a  $ V_{OC} $  of 1.06 V, a  $ J_{SC} $  of 22.91 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and an FF of 72%, which is comparable to the results in literature. $ ^{[48,49]} $  The device with PCBM interlayer has a PCE of 18.94%. The control device suffers from a serious hysteresis index (HI) of 0.12, whereas in contract, the HI of the device with n-doped PCBM significantly diminishes to 0.03 (Figure S7, Supporting Information). We attribute the enhancement of photovoltaic parameters to improved charge transport at perovskite/TiO $ _2 $  interface and less trap states in the perovskite film with the enlarged crystal grain sizes. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, we have put forward a simple strategy to improve both efficiency and stability of n-i-p planar perovskite solar cell by introducing an air-stabile n-type DMBI-doped PCBM as a modification interlayer between   $  TiO_{2}  $   and perovskite layers. The film charge conductivity and mobility are clearly increased by the doping process. The n-doped PCBM layer significantly diminishes the energy level offset at the   $  MAPbI_{3}/TiO_{2}  $   interface, reducing the potential energy loss at the contact. PL and TRPL measurements further reveal the enhanced charge transfer efficiency from perovskite to   $  TiO_{2}  $   and thus the less charge recombination loss. High-quality perovskite film with enlarged grain size deposited on doped   $  PCBM/TiO_{2}  $   is observed attributed to the ameliorative affinity of perovskite to PCBM. Such interface engineering improves all photovoltaic parameters including   $  V_{OC}  $ ,   $  J_{SC}  $ , FF, and HI, resulting in a remarkable improvement in the average PCE from 17.46% to 20.14% with a maximum

stabilized power output of 19.77%. The device stability is simultaneously enhanced due to the hydrophobicity of PCBM and the lower defect density in the perovskite films. This work highlights the effect of interface between charge transport layer and perovskite layer on the device efficiency and stability, and it indicates that many further device performance improvements should be conceivable by focusing on the perovskite interface.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: Lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ ) was obtained from TCI. Methylammonium iodide (MAI), PCBM, and Spiro-OMeTAD were obtained from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) solution, dimethylsulphoxide (DMSO),  $ N,N $ -dimethylformamide (DMF), chlorobenzene (CB), and DMBI were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. All materials were used as received. The processes of film fabrication and annealing were carried out in a N $ _2 $  atmosphere. The CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  solution was prepared by mixing MAI and PbI $ _2 $  (1:1) in the mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO (4:1 v/v). The best doping ratio of DMBI was found to be 2 wt%.

Device Fabrication and Characterization: The patterned FTO substrates were ultrasonically cleaned in detergent, deionized water, acetone, isopropanol, and absolute ethanol for 15 min, respectively. Then they were cleaned by UV-ozone treatment before using. The titanium dioxide (  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) as an electron transport layer was fabricated by spin-coating the titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) solution at 2000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 500 °C for 30 min. The PCBM or n-doped PCBM was spin coated onto   $  TiO_{2}  $   at 6000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 70 °C for 5 min. The deposition of the perovskite layer was carried out using a two-step spin coating containing a slow process at 1000 rpm for 10 s and a fast process at 4000 rpm for 30 s. During the second step, 150 μL of chlorobenzene was poured on the film at the 15 s, which was known as the antisolvent method. The resulting films were annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. For the hole-transporting layers, Spiro-OMeTAD was deposited by spin-coating at 4000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, the devices were completed by vacuum evaporating   $  MoO_{3}  $   (8 nm) and Ag (100 nm) as the top electrodes with the active area of 0.0725 cm². The current density–voltage curve of the perovskite solar cell was measured using a Keithley 2612 under an Air Mass 1.5G source. The EQE spectra were measured by a solar cell spectral response measurement system and calibrated by a silicon cell in advance.

Film Characterization: Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) was performed in an ultrahigh vacuum system using Hel (21.22 eV) as the source. The SEM images were obtained by Carl Zeiss-Merlin field emission-SEM instrument. C-AFM measurement was captured using Veeco MultiMode V with a bias voltage of 2 V. The XRD patterns were collected by PANalytical 80 equipment. The steady-state photoluminescence was measured by Horiba Jobin Yvon FL-3. TRPL views were captured by a time-dependent single photon counting system. Absorption spectra were conducted with the PerkinElmer Lambda 750.
