# 1. Introduction

Hybrid organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been the focus of intense research over the past few years, and made tremendous progress after the first report in 2009, with the record efficiency of 3.8%.  $  ^{[1]}  $   The power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) up to 23.7% has been achieved by PSCs, which is far above other emerging photovoltaic technologies.  $  ^{[2]}  $   Generally, the most efficient perovskite solar cells utilize a mesoscopic or planar structure, with a sandwich-type configuration, in which the light harvesting perovskite layer is located between two charge selecting layers to enhance the selective blocking and extraction of charges into the external circuit. In a conventional n-i-p structure, a p-type hole transporting material (HTM), on the top of perovskite layer is in contact with a metal back contact.  $  ^{[3]}  $   Different categories of organic, polymeric and inorganic HTMs have been developed to collect the photon-generated holes from perovskite active layer.  $  ^{[4]}  $   To date, 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD), has been the most studied HTM and the best molecule to achieve highly efficient PSCs with good reproducibility.  $  ^{[5]}  $   However, its potential application in commercialization is hindered by the disadvantages of high cost, chemical and thermal instability and low conductivity and hole mobility. Therefore, developing novel HTMs with high stability, facile synthesis routes and good hole mobility are paramount.

Metal phthalocyanines (MPcs) are highly thermal, chemical, and photostable  $  p  $ -type semiconductors, with intense absorption in the near-infrared region and have been intensely used in thin film transistors and organic solar cells, $ ^{[6]} $  which make them an interesting class of HTMs for PSCs. $ ^{[7]} $  These low cost materials also offer relatively high hole mobility and high hydrophobicity. $ ^{[8]} $  MPc's special properties can be adjusted by introducing various peripheral and

Solution processable substituted MPcs brought easy deposition methods with lower production costs for perovskite device. In our previous work, tetra propyl substituted CuPc has been employed as dopant free HTM in a planar structured PSC, reaching to a PCE of as high as 17.8%, with an improved stability against humidity.  $  ^{[10]}  $

Sun and coworkers have introduced 4-butylphenoxy and 4-butoxyphenoxy groups as non-peripheral substituents to CuPc and the isomer mixtures of CuPc-Bu and CuPc-OBu materials have been prepared and used as dopant free HTMs in PSCs.  $  ^{[11]}  $   They have reported PCEs of 14.3% and 17.6% for remarkably stable devices based on CuPc-Bu and CuPc-OBu HTMs, respectively. Employing substituted ZnPcs, as solution processable HTMs in PSCs, Torres and Nazeeruddin et al. have also achieved remarkable efficiencies up to the value of 17.5%, proving the great potentials of this class of MPc compounds.  $  ^{[12]}  $   An impressive PCE of 18.8% has been achieved in a recent study, when an isomer-pure tetra tert-butyl substituted CuPc has been employed as HTM in a mixed-ion perovskite solar cell with an outstanding long-term stability, reaching the highest PCE ever reported for Pc based HTMs.  $  ^{[13]}  $   The simultaneous presence of isomers of a compound in HTM film of a PSC can cause morphological and energetic disorders in its active layer, leading to degrading effect on the short circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ).  $  ^{[14]}  $   Therefore, in comparison with isomer mixture, isomer-pure materials can potentially enhance the photovoltaic performance of PSC devices.  $  ^{[15]}  $   Gratzel et al. have employed a purified isomer of a

As a Lewis acid, bis-PCBM accepts electron from negatively-charged PbX  $  ^{3-}  $   defects or undercoordinated halide ions and passivate the surface trap states. Therefore, in comparison with isomer mixture, isomer-pure materials can lower various unfavorable disorders in thin films, increase their quality, and potentially enhance the photovoltaic performance of PSC devices. To date, researches on isomer pure HTMs are rarely reported due to the difficulties in their synthesis, purification and characterization.

In this work, we seek to provoke once again the idea of reaching higher performances for PSCs by the use of isomer-pure HTMs, compared to their isomer mixtures. # 2- Results and discussion

# 2-1- Synthesis and characterization

The structures of tetra n-butyl substituted ZnPc compounds in the forms of isomer-pure (RE-ZnBu $ _4 $ Pc) and isomer mixture (ZnBu $ _4 $ Pc) are depicted in Figure 1a. 2,9,16,24-tetra-n-butyl-Zn (II) phthalocyanine (RE-ZnBu $ _4 $ Pc) was prepared according ring expansion method, shown in Scheme 1. The ring expansion reaction of subphthalocyanine (SubPc) is an efficient method in preparation of unsymmetrical AB $ _3 $ -type phthalocyanine compounds. $ ^{[19]} $  Isomer purification by common chromatography for ZnBu $ _4 $ Pc compound is not possible after the preparation. Here, for the first time, we suggest the use of the ring expansion method to obtain an isomer-pure A $ _4 $ -type Pc compound. In the present study, the isomer-pure tri-n-butyl substituted boron SubPc and tetra-n-butyl substituted ZnPc, as the first step's and the final products of the reaction, respectively, were both directly obtained through the ring expansion synthetic route with no further purification steps. Scheme 2 shows the synthetic procedure of the isomer mixture ZnBu $ _4 $ Pc.

The SubPc compound and the newly synthesized HTMs were characterized by   $  {}^{1}  $  HNMR and MS spectroscopy and the expected spectral signals were observed for all three samples (Figure S1-S8). Using the   $  {}^{1}  $  HNMR spectroscopy, it is possible to characterize the exact positions of n-butyl substituents in both BBu  $  _{3}  $  SubPc and ZnBu  $  _{4}  $  Pc.

Apparent from the   $  {}^{1}  $  HNMR spectrum of BBu  $  _{3}  $  SubPc, the splitting pattern for each peak is distinct (Figure S1). In the symmetrically n-butyl substituted SubPc ring, there are three kinds of aromatic proton, expecting to have two doublet and one singlet splitting pattern, which is exactly observed in the region of 7.65 to 8.60 ppm. In the region for aliphatic protons in the spectrum of BBu  $  _{3}  $  SubPc, there exist four peaks with the patterns of triplet (2H, 2.95 ppm), multiplet (2H, 1.82 ppm), multiplet (2H, 1.47 ppm) and triplet (3H, 1.01 ppm), which are assigned to the CH  $  _{2}  $   group attached to phenyl ring, two middle CH  $  _{2}  $   groups and the terminal CH  $  _{3}  $   group, respectively. These peaks confirm the configuration with C  $  _{3v}  $   symmetry.

During the ring expansion reaction, the subphthalocyanine ring is unraveled, and a single phthalonitrile ligand is inserted therein. In the traditional phthalocyanine synthesis method, the phthalonitrile ligands are freely combined to form phthalocyanine, in different ratios of four configurations, with symmetries of  $ \mathrm{C_{4h}} $ ,  $ \mathrm{D_{2h}} $ ,  $ \mathrm{C_{2v}} $ ,  $ \mathrm{C_s} $ . However, in this study, a single configuration of tetra n-butyl zinc phthalocyanine was obtained through ring expansion method. The  $ ^1 $ HNMR and two-dimensional NMR spectroscopy were performed to determine the specific stereo configuration of  $ RE\text{-ZnBu}_4\mathrm{Pc} $ . 12 sets of peaks for aromatic protons can be observed from  $ ^1 $ HNMR spectrum of  $ RE\text{-ZnBu}_4\mathrm{Pc} $  (Figure S2). The configurations with the symmetries of  $ \mathrm{C_{4h}} $  and  $ \mathrm{D_{2h}} $  should present only three peaks in their aromatic region; two doublets and one singlet. Therefore, these two configurations cannot be the accepted for the synthesized  $ RE\text{-ZnBu}_4\mathrm{Pc} $ . 6 kinds of aromatic protons, and consequently 6 peaks are expected for a tetra substituted Pc with  $ \mathrm{C_{2v}} $  symmetry, due to the presence of rotating axis. Based on the obtained  $ ^1 $ HNMR spectrum, the configuration with  $ \mathrm{C_s} $  symmetry can be considered as the most probable and matched choice for  $ RE\text{-ZnBu}_4\mathrm{Pc} $ .

Two-dimensional COSY NMR test was also performed to determine the exact position of each proton in the  $ ^{1} $ HNMR spectrum of  $ RE $ -ZnBu $ _{4} $ Pc (Figure S3). Through cross peaks and their positions in the spectrum, the coupled nuclei can be detected in the spectrum, which is a great help to recognize the chemical shifts for each of the protons in the compound. From the COSY NMR spectrum of  $ RE $ -ZnBu $ _{4} $ Pc, and focusing on the aromatic proton region, four groups of protons with the chemical shifts of 7.04-6.55, 6.89-6.44, 6.82-6.38, 6.68-6.26, representing 8 protons, can be observed, having coupling effect on each other. These peaks are obviously related to the protons on one side of benzene rings in the Pc compounds. With respect to n-butyl substituent attached to benzene ring, the protons on ortho position are supposed to be more shielded and appear in lower chemical shifts (6.55, 6.44, 6.38 and 6.26). The protons on meta positions are the ones observed in higher chemical shifts (7.04, 6.89, 6.82 and 6.68). The remaining four peaks are the ones related to the protons on the other side of the benzene ring with no coupling effect. Appearing of four separate groups of protons for four benzene rings confirms the different electronic environment of each benzene ring in  $ RE $ -ZnBu $ _{4} $ Pc, and proves the C $ _{S} $  symmetry for this compound. This interpretation was also supported by the theoretical calculations performed by gaussian software for the prediction of  $ ^{1} $ HNMR spectrum of  $ RE $ -ZnBu $ _{4} $ Pc (Figure S4).

# 2-2- UV–vis absorption and fluorescence spectra

Both characteristic bands of phthalocyanine compounds, the B (Soret) band at 300–400 nm, and the Q band at 600–800 nm, were seen in ultraviolet–visible (UV–vis) spectra of   $  RE-ZnBu_{4}Pc  $   and   $  ZnBu_{4}Pc  $ , both in solution and as thin films on FTO glass (Figure S9). Figure S10 shows the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) decay spectra of  $  RE-ZnBu_{4}Pc  $  and  $  ZnBu_{4}Pc  $  coated on perovskite film. Strong PL quenching were observed for both HTM materials, with a slightly higher ability for the isomer-pure  $  RE-ZnBu_{4}Pc  $ , suggesting its more efficient charge carrier extraction and faster interface charge separation (Figure S10a). These contribute to a better  $  J_{SC}  $  and fill factor ( $  FF  $ ) in the PSCs (as stated in section 2-5). As a crucial factor for an efficient hole collection and transportation from perovskite layer to the back contact electrode, the hole mobilities of these two HTMs were determined using the space charge-limited current (SCLCs) method.  $ J-V $  curves are shown in Figure S12. The obtained hole mobility of  $ RE-ZnBu_{4}Pc $  and  $ ZnBu_{4}Pc $  are  $ 7.59 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~cm}^{2} \mathrm{~V}^{-1} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} $  and  $ 4.78 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~cm}^{2} \mathrm{~V}^{-1} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} $ , respectively, which are higher than that of spiro-OMeTAD ( $ 8.09 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~cm}^{2} \mathrm{~V}^{-1} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} $ ). [27] According to the results, higher mobility was achieved for the isomer-pure HTM, which is in line with the reported values for different isomers of tetra hexyl substituted vanadyl phthalocyanines, published by Dong and coworkers.[18] In their study, the highest hole mobility has been obtained for the isomer  $ I-3 $ , in which the arrangement of four hexyl substituents in non-peripheral positions of Pc ring, is completely similar to the arrangement of n-butyl groups in peripheral positions of  $ RE-ZnBu_{4}Pc $ . The higher hole mobility obtained for  $ RE-ZnBu_{4}Pc $  can also be explained by the higher crystallinity of this compound in thin film state, originated from its isomer purity (see section 2-3- Film morphology).

# 2-4- Film morphology

X-ray diffraction technique was used to study the crystallinity of new HTMs in both powder and thin film forms. As shown in Figure 2, similar XRD patterns were observed for the isomer-pure and mixture HTMs, however in all cases of powder, thin film deposited on  $ \mathrm{SiO}_2 $  surface and on perovskite layer, the intensity of peaks was considerably higher for  $ RE\text{-ZnBu}_4\mathrm{Pc} $  than  $ \mathrm{ZnBu}_4\mathrm{Pc} $ , proving its tendency for higher crystallinity of solid state in powder and as-fabricated thin film, which could be advantageous for carrier transporting.

As a crucial factor to their potential commercial applications, long-term stability of the PSCs was also studied and the efficiency of the devices was measured during 750 hours of storage at  $ 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and under relative humidity of  $ 75\% $  (Figure 5d) without encapsulation. All these results support the idea of higher chances for achieving more efficient and stable PSCs come from using isomer-pure HTMs instead of their isomer mixture materials.

# 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, an isomer-pure  $ \mathrm{A_4} $ -type MPc compound was successfully synthesized and characterized, and employed in perovskite solar cell as a dopant free HTM. Using ring expansion method, and with no further purification steps, the isomer-pure tetra n-butyl substituted ZnPc  $ (RE - ZnBu_{4}Pc) $  was prepared, and its exact structure was determined with the help of NMR

spectroscopy. The optical, thermal and electronic properties of  $  RE - ZnBu_{4}Pc  $ , along with the isomer mixture  $  ZnBu_{4}Pc  $  were investigated. The photovoltaic performance of PSCs based on  $  RE - ZnBu_{4}Pc  $  and  $  ZnBu_{4}Pc  $  were studied and the results were promising. Improvements were observed in  $  J_{SC}  $  and  $  FF  $  values of  $  RE - ZnBu_{4}Pc  $ -based devices, compared to those of the devices using isomer mixture HTM, and resulted in an enhancement of the average efficiency from 9.51% to 11.49% and increase of device stability at 25°C and under 75% relative humidity without encapsulation. The perovskite devices with  $  RE - ZnBu_{4}Pc  $  also offer better reproducibility for fabrication process. It is demonstrated that the higher efficiency of isomer-pure HTM-based PSCs was originated from the higher tendency of isomer-pure HTM materials to form thin film with higher crystallinity, better quality and uniformity and with less roughness, which leads to a compact and homogeneous MAPbI $ _3 $  film, with more clear grain structure.

For the first time, the isomer-pure 2,9,16,24-tetra-n-butyl-Zn (II) phthalocyanine  $ (RE - ZnBu_{4}Pc) $  is synthesized through ring expanding of symmetric tri-n-butyl-substituted boron subphthalocyanine and used as hole transporting materials (HTMs) in planar conventional perovskite solar cells (PSCs), offering higher efficiencies and better long-term stabilities for these devices. Employing isomer-pure compounds as HTMs in PSCs can also improve the reproducibility of device fabrication process.
