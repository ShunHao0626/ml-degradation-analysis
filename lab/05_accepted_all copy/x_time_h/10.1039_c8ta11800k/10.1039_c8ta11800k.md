# Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have shown an unprecedented rise in power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to 23.3% based on the optimizations of perovskite absorber layer  $  ^{1,2}  $  and device architecture.  $  ^{3-5}  $  Typically, a planar PSC device consists of a perovskite light-absorber layer,  $  ^{6,7}  $  an electron-transporting layer (ETL)  $  ^{8-10}  $  and a hole-transporting layer (HTL),  $  ^{11-13}  $  which are sandwiched between two electrodes. The aforementioned three layers mainly determine the device efficiency and stability of PSCs. Particularly, the unsatisfied device stability of PSCs, which is mostly induced by ionic migration,  $  ^{14-16}  $  light irradiation, moisture corrosion and oxygen infiltration under ambient conditions,  $  ^{17-21}  $  is a serious issue to restrict the potential application of PSCs.  $  ^{22-25}  $  In addition to the cell stability, the current-voltage hysteresis, originated from the charge traps, ferroelectric properties of the perovskites, and the ion migration in the devices, is another critical issue to influence the device performance.  $  ^{26-34}  $

Although HTL plays a positive role in hole transportation and extraction, the HTL itself is also one of the main sources of cell stability and current-voltage hysteresis. So far, most reported HTL materials for PSCs cannot work effectively in their pristine form. 2,2,7,7-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine) 9,9-bifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) is a widely employed HTL in n-i-p structure PSCs by its matchable energy level and non-reactivity to perovskite layer. # Flower-Like MoS $ _2 $  Nanocrystals: A Powerful Sorbent of Li $ ^+ $  in Spiro-OMeTAD Layer for Highly Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Lu-Lu Jiang  $  ^{a}  $  , Zhao-Kui Wang  $  ^{b*}  $  , Meng Li  $  ^{b}  $  , Chun-He Li  $  ^{a}  $  , Peng-Fei Fang  $  ^{a*}  $  , and Liang-Sheng Liao  $  ^{b*}  $

2,2,7,7-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine) 9,9-bifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) is a widely employed hole-transporting layer (HTL) in typical n-i-p structure perovskite solar cells (PSCs). And the p-type dopants of bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI) and tert-butylpyridine (tBP) have to be utilized to improve the hole mobility and film conductivity of Spiro-OMeTAD. However, the incorporation of these additives will cause the issue of deliquescence, strongly speeding up the degradation of Spiro-OMeTAD layer and thus the perovskite layer. Herein, we developed a modified Spiro-OMeTAD layer by  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  with 2H semiconducting phase and flower-like microstructure. The addition of  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  played an important role in improving the hole mobility and enhancing the film stability of Spiro-OMeTAD. The resulting PSCs based on  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $ -modified Spiro-OMeTAD HTL delivered a champion power conversion efficiency as high as  $ 20.18\% $ . More importantly, the incorporation of  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  could suppress the  $ \mathrm{Li}^+ $  migration in HTL owing to the strong adsorption ability and large specific surface area of flower-like  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanoparticles. As a result, the fabricated PSC without encapsulation exhibited promising stability by remaining almost  $ 85\% $  of the initial PCE even after  $ 300\mathrm{h} $  exposure in air while the reference device only keeping about  $ 30\% $  of its initial PCE.

# Experimental

Synthesis of   $  MoS_{2}  $  : All the chemicals   $  (\mathrm{(NH_{4})_{6}Mo_{7}O_{24}\cdot4H_{2}O}  $   and   $  CH_{3}CSNH_{2})  $   used in the synthesis of   $  MoS_{2}  $   were obtained from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd, Shanghai without further purification. The flower-like   $  MoS_{2}  $   was prepared by the method as follows. 1.2410 g   $  (\mathrm{NH_{4})_{6}Mo_{7}O_{24}\cdot4H_{2}O}  $   and 1.0629 g thioacetamide (TAA)   $  [CH_{3}CSNH_{2}]  $   were mixed together and then vigorously stirred for 0.5 h until forming a homogeneous solution. Subsequently, the resulting liquid was moved into a Teflon reaction caldron (50 mL) and heated at 220 °C for 6 h. After the reaction, a black precipitate was obtained and then washed using ethanol and water respectively for three times before being dried at 80 °C in a vacuum chamber.

# Devices and characteristics

The   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite layer was deposited by a one-step spin-coating method including anti-solvent step.   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (99%),   $  PbI_{2}  $   (99.999%), Spiro-OMeTAD, bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)-imide lithium salt (Li-TFSI), tri(2-(1H-phyrazol-1-yl)-4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) and the solvents of dimenthyl sulfoxide (DMSO), 1,4-butyrolactone (GBL) (anhydrous, amine free; 99.9%), chlorobenzene and acetonitrile were purchased from Sigma Aldrich. All chemicals were used as received without any further purification. The precursor solution was prepared by dissolving   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   and   $  PbI_{2}  $   (1.2 mol :1.2 mol) in (DMSO: GBL) 3:7 (v/v) to make the precursor solution of perovskite layer. The Spiro-OMeTAD solution was prepared by mixing 90 mg in 1 mL chlorobenzene with 23 μL Li-TFSI and 36 μL tBP. The   $  MoS_{2}  $   precursor solution was prepared by adding 50 mg   $  MoS_{2}  $   in 3 mL acetonitrile solvent.

Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) was used as substrates cathode with a sheet resistance of   $  15 \Omega/sq  $  . The cleaned FTO-coated glass substrates were treated by UV-Ozone for 20 min. The   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer was coated on the surface of substrates by soaking in titanium tetrachloride precursor solution (ultrapure water has turned into ice) and deposition in an oven pre-heated to   $  70^{\circ}C  $   for 1 h, finally rinsing and drying. The perovskite precursor solution was deposited on   $  FTO/TiO_{2}  $   substrates via spin-coating (2000 rpm for 20 s and then 4000 rpm for 40 s). Chlorobenzene was dropped onto the substrates during the second step after 20 seconds. An instant darkening of film was obtained quickly after annealing at   $  100^{\circ}C  $   for 10 min in the glovebox. And then Spiro-OMeTAD layer was deposited on the top of perovskite layer at

5000 rpm for 40 s. Finally, 100 nm Ag electrode was evaporated through a shadow mask to define the device area of 9 mm  $  ^{2}  $  .

Characteristics: X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the perovskite films and   $  TiO_{2}  $   were performed by a PANalytical 80 equipment (Empyrean, Cu Ka radiation). The film surface morphologies were evaluated by atomic force microscopy (AFM, Veeco Multimode V instrument) and scanning electron microscope (SEM, Quanta 200 FEG, FEI Co.). The UV-Vis absorption spectra were collected using a Shimadzu spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer Lambda 750). The steady state photoluminescence (PL) spectra of the perovskite films on different HTLs were collected using Horiba Jobin-Yvon LabRAM HR800 with a 480 nm excitation wavelength. Current density-voltage (J-V) characteristics of PSCs were obtained using a programmable Keithley 2400 source meter under AM 1.5G solar irradiation (100 mW/cm²). Room temperature Hall-effect measurements were performed with a Lake Shore Model 8404 Hall-Effect Measurement System with DC and AC magnetic fields of ±1.7 T (DC) and ~1.2 T RMS (AC, 100 mHz), respectively. Hall mobility was extracted from the measured Hall voltage and the measured 4-point resistivity for the particular excitation current and magnetic field (Hall factor set to unity). Sheet carrier concentration was extracted likewise from the Hall voltage. TOF-SIMS measurements (Model TOF-SIMS 5, ION-TOF GmbH) were performed with the pulsed primary ions from a   $  Cs^{+}  $   (2 keV) liquid-metal ion gun for the sputtering and a   $  Bi^{+}  $   pulsed primary ion beam for the analysis (30 keV).

The optoelectronic properties of Spiro-OMeTAD: MoS₂ film are firstly investigated to better understand the charge transfer between Spiro-OMeTAD and MoS₂. Electron spin resonance (ESR) spectroscopy is a powerful technique to evaluate the charge states among different molecules. Figure 2a gives the plots of ESR signals as a function of magnetic field for pristine Spiro-OMeTAD and Spiro-OMeTAD: MoS₂ solutions. Pristine Spiro-OMeTAD displays no other obvious radical signals in its ESR spectra. In contrast, Spiro-OMeTAD: MoS₂ sample presents very strong signals at 326.1 mT and 326.6 mT, which are obvious evidences of efficient charge transfer between Spiro-OMeTAD and MoS₂. A comparison among the ESR signals in as-prepared and aged (72 h) Spiro-OMeTAD and Spiro-OMeTAD: MoS₂ samples in dark and illumination (5 min) conditions, further confirms the stable charge transfer behavior between Spiro-OMeTAD and MoS₂ (Supporting Information, Figure S2). Efficient charge transfer will result in improved hole mobility and film conductivity of the Spiro-OMeTAD layer. Figure 2b shows the J-V characteristics of Spiro-OMeTAD and Spiro-OMeTAD: MoS₂ based on hole-only devices with a structure of FTO/PE-DOT: PSS/Spiro-OMeTAD/MoO₃/Ag. The improvement of film conductivity could be reflected from the enhancement of

current density in the whole bias range especially the space charge limited current (SCLC) region. The hole mobilities  $ (\mu) $  in Spiro-OMeTAD and Spiro-OMeTAD:  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  films are extrapolated to be  $ 3.3\times 10^{-3} $  and  $ 6.8\times 10^{-3}\mathrm{cm}^2\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{S}^{-1} $ , respectively, according to Mott-Gurney's equation of  $ \mu = (8JL^3) / (9\varepsilon \varepsilon_0V^2) $  by using the  $ J - V $  data in the SCLC region. Where  $ \varepsilon_0 $  is the vacuum permittivity,  $ \varepsilon $  is the dielectric constant of Spiro-OMeTAD film, and  $ L $  is the thickness of Spiro-OMeTAD layer. The Hall effect measurements shown in Figure 2c further verify the enhancement of electrical conductivity in Spiro-OMeTAD:  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  film. In addition, the Hall mobility in the Spiro-OMeTAD:  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  film exhibits a lower decay process compared with that in the pristine Spiro-OMeTAD film. This indicates that  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $ -doped Spiro-OMeTAD film possess good stability, which enables corresponding PSCs to have a better device stability. The bandgap of the Spiro-OMeTAD and Spiro-OMeTAD:  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  films were extrapolated from the optical absorption spectra as shown in Figure S9. The bandgap of Spiro-OMeTAD film has no changes with the intervention of  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $ .

Besides the high efficiency, the device stability especially the operational stability in ambient air is another critical concern for perovskite solar cells. To study the effect of   $  MoS_{2}  $   incorporation on the device stability, the PCEs of the reference and the control devices (without encapsulation) are measured every 12 h until 300 h aging in air. The devices are stored in a glass oven keeping the humidity at 40% and the temperature at 25 °C during the measurement intervals. As shown in Figure 3f, the   $  MoS_{2}  $  -included device exhibits an excellent long-term stability compared with the reference device. After 300 h aging, the PCE of the reference device decreased severely from 17.79% to 5.45%, only keeping 30% of the initial value. To better clarify the effect of   $  MoS_{2}  $   incorporation on the stability of Spiro-OMeTAD layer, the top SEM images of the Spiro-OMeTAD films with and without   $  MoS_{2}  $   incorporation are evaluated. The Spiro-OMeTAD films are deposited onto the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   layer with an overnight oxidative process in order to reflect the practical device situation. Figure 4a and 4b shows the SEM images of Spiro-OMeTAD film and Spiro-OMeTAD:   $  MoS_{2}  $   film, respectively. As shown in Figure 4a, many corroded traces are observed onto the surface of Spiro-OMeTAD film. Due to the amorphous nature of the Spiro-OMeTAD film, the moisture and oxygen are easily intruded into the Spiro-OMeTAD layer. Continuous intrusions from moisture and oxygen cause the generation of corroded traces, which will accelerate the decomposition of perovskite film. In contrast, less corroded traces are observed onto the surface of Spiro-OMeTAD:   $  MoS_{2}  $   film. Since the sensitivity to moisture and oxygen, severe aggregation and crystallization will be happened for those organic-based small molecule films. Actually, the incorporation of inorganic-based materials can relieve the issues of instability and aggregation of the organic-based films.  $  ^{[12,13,17]}  $   In present case, suppressed aggregation and crystallization by   $  MoS_{2}  $   incorporation reduces the pinholes and voids in the Spiro-OMeTAD layer. Figure 4d and 4e presents the SEM images of aged (72 h in air) Spiro-OMeTAD film and Spiro-OMeTAD:   $  MoS_{2}  $   film, respectively.

After a long time erosion, the pinholes and voids in the Spiro-OMeTAD layer become larger and larger. Compared to the   $  MoS_{2}  $  -included sample, the pristine Spiro-OMeTAD film presents a more coarse morphology (Supporting Information, Figure S4). The difference of the surface morphologies between two samples is further confirmed by their corresponding optical microscope images (Supporting Information, Figure S5). And the layered device configuration could be reflected by a cross-sectional SEM image of the device as presented in Figure S6. The thickness of Spiro-OMeTAD and Spiro-OMeTAD:  $  MoS_{2}  $   layers was about 350 nm, and 380 nm, respectively. The additives of Li-TFSI and tBP have the issue of strong deliquescence, which is prone to attract water molecules and thus accelerate the degradation of the Spiro-OMeTAD layer. Noticeably, many clusters appear on the surface of the Spiro-OMeTAD. From the XRD patterns as shown in Figure 4c and 4f, the emerging clusters are confirmed to be the aggregated   $  PbI_{2}  $  . After long time intrusions, the moisture and oxygen can penetrate into the perovskite layer through those pinholes and voids, resulting in a partial decomposition of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   and thus the generation of   $  PbI_{2}  $  . In contrast, few   $  PbI_{2}  $   clusters are observed on the surface of the Spiro-OMeTAD:   $  MoS_{2}  $   film, indicating an excellent film stability owing to the suppressed intrusion paths for moisture and oxygen.

In addition,   $  Li^{+}  $  migration from Spiro-OMeTAD to perovskite layer is a challenging issue due to the good liveness of   $  Li^{+}  $  ions. The reduced HI in the Spiro-OMeTAD:   $  MoS_{2}  $   based devices (Figure 3e) suggests an inhibited   $  Li^{+}  $  migration behavior owing to the incorporation of   $  MoS_{2}  $ . To better understand the role of   $  MoS_{2}  $   in suppressing the   $  Li^{+}  $  migration, an investigation on the elements (especially   $  Li^{+}  $ ) distributions in the reference and the control devices is carried out. Figure 5a and 5b plots the time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectroscopy (TOF-SIMS) depth profiles (lengthwise distributions) including Li, Mo, S, and I elements in the aged (200 h in air) Spiro-OMeTAD and Spiro-OMeTAD:   $  MoS_{2}  $   based device, respectively. As shown in Figure 5a, obviously,   $  Li^{+}  $  ions have been migrated from the Spiro-OMeTAD layer to the perovskite layer. And a few of I ions are distributed in the Spiro-OMeTAD layer, indicating that the poor stability of the pristine Spiro-OMeTAD film could not avoid the I-migration from the perovskite layer to the Spiro-OMeTAD layer. From the TOF-SIMS depth profile (Figure 5a) of the Spiro-OMeTAD:   $  MoS_{2}  $   based device,   $  S^{2-}  $   and   $  Mo^{4+}  $   are mainly located in the Spiro-OMeTAD layer. In sharp contrast, there is nearly no   $  Li^{+}  $  ions in the perovskite layer, indicating an effective suppression of   $  Li^{+}  $  ion migration from the Spiro-OMeTAD layer to the perovskite layer. And the I-migration from the perovskite layer to the Spiro-OMeTAD layer is also restrained since very few I-ions are observed in the Spiro-OMeTAD layer.

Combined with the comprehensive effects, we think that   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanoparticles can play three positive functions on the Spiro-OMeTAD layer. First,   $  MoS_{2}  $   can serve as a p-type dopant for Spiro-OMeTAD to enhance the film conductivity and increase the hole mobility. Second, the inorganic nautre of   $  MoS_{2}  $   can improve the film stability of the organic based amorphous Spiro-OMeTAD film. Third, the flower-like   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanoparticles with layered petals can act as powerful sorbents for   $  Li^{+}  $  ions by their large specific surface area. Figure 6 gives a schematic of the absorbent function of flower-like   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanoparticles for   $  Li^{+}  $  ions in the Spiro-OMeTAD layer. In the case of pristine Spiro-OMeTAD based device, the moisture is easily penetrated into the perovskite layer through the intrusion paths in the Spiro-OMeTAD layer due to the ease aggregation and crystallization of the amorphous Spiro-OMeTAD film. When   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanoparticles are doped into Spiro-OMeTAD layer, the layered petal structure of   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanoparticles can adsorb and immobilize   $  Li^{+}  $  ions in the Spiro-OMeTAD layer. This means that the incorporation of   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanoparticles could suppress the   $  Li^{+}  $  migration from the Spiro-OMeTAD layer to the perovskite layer, resulting in reduced intrusion paths for moisture and oxygen to the perovskite layer. We ascribe it to the main reason on the

improved cell stability and the reduced current hysteresis in the Spiro-OMeTAD: MoS  $  _{2}  $  based PSCs.

# Conclusion

In summary, we have demonstrated an effective method to enhance the device performance of perovskite solar cells by modifying the Spiro-OMeTAD layer with flower-like   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanoparticles. The incorporation of   $  MoS_{2}  $   realized efficient charge transfer with Spiro-OMeTAD molecules, giving enhanced film conductivity and increased hole mobility in the Spiro-OMeTAD films. The resulting PSCs delivered a champion PCE as high as 20.18%. In addition, the incorporation of   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanoparticles largely improved the film stability of the Spiro-OMeTAD layer, resulting in remarkably prolonged cell stability by maintained 86% of the initial efficiency even after 300 h aging without encapsulation in air. More importantly, the flower-like   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanoparticles with layered petals can act as powerful sorbents for   $  Li^{+}  $  ions, which could suppress the   $  Li^{+}  $  migration from Spiro-OMeTAD to perovskite layer, resulting in recuded current hysteresis of the PSCs. The finding in this work provides an effective strategy to improve the efficiency and stability of Spiro-OMeTAD based PSCs.
