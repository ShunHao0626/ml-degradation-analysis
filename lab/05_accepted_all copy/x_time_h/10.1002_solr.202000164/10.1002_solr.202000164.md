Abstract: Due to its excellent thermal stability and high performance, the inorganic cesium lead mixed-halide (ABX $ _3 $ , where A=Cs, B=Pb and X= I/Br) all inorganic perovskite solar cells (IPVSCs) attracted much interest in the optoelectronic applications. However, the film quality, enough absorption by desired film thickness and nature of partial replacement of cations determine the stability of the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite films. Here, we employed a hot air method to control the thickness and morphology of the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite thin film and the A-site (herein, Cs $ ^+ $ ) cation is partially incorporated by rubidium (Rb $ ^+ $ ) cation for making stable black-phase in ambient condition. The Rb-cation incorporated Cs $ _{1-x} $ Rb $ _x $ PbI $ _2 $ Br (x=0 to 0.03) perovskite thin films exhibited high crystallinity, uniform grains, extremely dense and pinhole free morphology. The fabricated device having Cs $ _{0.99} $ Rb $ _{0.01} $ PbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite composition with poly(3-hexylthiophene-2,5-diyl) (P3HT) as a hole transporting layer (HTL) exhibited 17.16 % power conversion efficiency (PCE), which is much higher than the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br based IPVSCs. # 1. Introduction

From the last several years, lead-halide based perovskite as a promising candidate for low-cost photovoltaics has been proved from its rapid improvement in power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 3.8 % to 29.1 %. [1-3] The certified highest 25.2 % PCE has been reported for mixed-cation mixed-halide hybrid perovskite solar cells (HPVSCs), making it a sustainable challenger to the conventional silicon solar cells.[4,5] However, the organic components in the hybrid perovskites such as formamidinium (FA) and methylammonium (MA) cations are inherently unstable owing to humidity and thermal stress.[6] The replacement of these organic components with inorganic cations (Cs, Rb) to form all inorganic lead halide perovskite materials may fundamentally increase the stability of PVSCs.[7] Nonetheless,  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  has an appropriate band gap ( $ \mathrm{E_g} $ ) of 1.73 eV suitable for efficient photovoltaic application but suffered from unstable  $ \alpha $ -phase at ambient temperature.[8] On the other hand, the  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  perovskite displays exceptional high stability but it shows band gap of 2.3 eV, which is not enough to absorb the solar spectrum effectively.[9, 10] Many attempts have been made for  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  based IPVSCs using bulk as well as quantum dots for achieving better efficiency.[11-15] Although, the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  QDs show high stability in ambient condition, however, synthesis protocol and its thin films fabrication is tedious job.[16] Therefore, a relatively higher band gap ( $ \mathrm{E_g} = 1.92 $  eV) of the mixed-halide  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  perovskite composition and its thin film synthesis is not only exhibit much higher stability[17] but also presents better efficiency due to increased Goldschmidt tolerance factor 't' and pull down the phase transition temperature.[18] Therefore, the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  attracted tremendous attention in thermally stable inorganic perovskite research area. It is well known that the fabrication of highly efficient PVSCs, the quality of the perovskite thin film is very important aspect.  $  ^{[31]}  $   For example, the incomplete surface coverage and pin-holes may result in low stability, shunting pathways and low absorption. Furthermore, insufficient thickness and high density of unwanted flaws causes a low charge collection efficiency and high recombination rate. To resolve these problems, researchers developed different methods such as gas-assisted,  $  ^{[32]}  $   vacuum flash method,  $  ^{[33]}  $   solvent-engineering,  $  ^{[34]}  $   co-evaporation,  $  ^{[35]}  $   heat assisted,  $  ^{[36]}  $   and ion doping,  $  ^{[37]}  $   to obtain a uniform, highly compact and pinhole free perovskite thin films. The quality of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   thin films and defect free crystallinity dominates the photovoltaic performance. Obtaining the grain size more than 1   $  \mu  $  m for the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  thin film by the solvent engineering process is not difficult but vertical growth in the same dimension is a difficult task. Currently, temperature assisted   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   thin film by hot spin coating method with 200 nm film thickness of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite yielded 14.81 % PCE,  $  ^{[38]}  $   while, anti-solvent treatment enhances the film thickness up to 450 nm with 16.07 % PCE in a nitrogen atmosphere.  $  ^{[19]}  $   However, the deposited film thickness is not sufficient for enough light harvesting. # 2. Results and discussion

# 2.1. Morphological study

To form the high quality perovskite thin films, we deposited all inorganic   $  Cs_{1-x}Rb_{x}PbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite thin films by the spin coating method with dynamic hot-air method.  $  ^{[39]}  $   Figure 1 (a) shows the cubic crystal structure of the   $  Cs_{0.99}Rb_{0.01}PbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite. As per our previous report, during spin-coating we applied 150 °C hot air by hot air gun to obtain a uniform, dense and pinhole free perovskite film instead of the conventional solvent engineering process. Later, these films annealed at 280 °C for 10 min on the hot plate, Figure 1 (b). The morphological analysis was analyzed by scanning electron microscopic (SEM) technique. Thus, the   $  Rb^{+}  $  incorporation enhances the thermodynamic stability of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite.

# 2.3. Structural and compositional analysis
