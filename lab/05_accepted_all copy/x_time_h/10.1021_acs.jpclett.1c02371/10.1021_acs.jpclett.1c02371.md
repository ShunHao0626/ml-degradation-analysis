# Metal Oxide-Induced Instability and Its Mitigation in Halide Perovskite Solar Cells

Sampreetha Thampy, Weijie Xu, and Julia W. P. Hsu*

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have emerged as a promising photovoltaic technology for sustainable energy solutions because of their impressive power conversion efficiency and a path to be manufactured by low-cost, high-throughput methods. To reach PSCs' full potential for practical implementation, it is crucial to solve the issues related to long-term operational stability. Given that PSCs consist of many layers of dissimilar materials which form multiple internal interfaces, it is prudent to examine whether there exist interfacial interactions, most importantly between transport layers and perovskite absorbers, that can trigger instability and affect device performance. In this Perspective, we bring to the attention of the PSC research community the lesser-known interfacial degradation of halide perovskites promoted by contact with metal oxide transport layers and highlight the deleterious effects on the PSCs' performance and stability. We also discuss various mitigation strategies that have shown promise for achieving high-performing and stable PSCs.

With a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) surpassing 25% in a short time,  $  ^{1}  $  halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are heralded as the next-generation photovoltaic technology. However, their operational lifetime lags far behind the expected decades of inorganic solar cells, making stability the critical obstacle for realizing PSCs as a commercially viable technology. Because the organic cations in organic–inorganic halide perovskites are inherently chemically reactive, they degrade rapidly under extrinsic stressors, including moisture, oxygen, light, or heat, resulting in the loss of volatile components, leaving behind photo-inactive lead halide.  $  ^{2-5}  $  Given the reactive nature of the organic cations, recently tremendous efforts have been directed toward designing all-inorganic halide perovskites as they are considered chemically more stable, but their PCE is still inferior to that of the organic–inorganic PSCs.  $  ^{6,7}  $  Moreover, the major challenge with the use of inorganic CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  in PSCs is that they suffer from structural instability at room temperature, leading to a loss in performance.  $  ^{5-7}  $  Based on a comprehensive understanding of the degradation mechanisms of the perovskite materials by themselves, i.e., bulk degradation,  $  ^{8,9}  $  substantial efforts have been directed toward mitigating the extrinsic instability of PSCs through multiple approaches, such as advanced encapsulation methods  $  ^{3,4,10}  $  or new perovskite formulations via compositional engineering to produce innately stable absorbers.  $  ^{11-13}  $  Despite considerable progress in these engineering and material design approaches, the long-term stability is improved only from minutes to thousands of hours, which is still insufficient for their practical

It is important to realize that PSCs include not just the perovskite active layer but also other layers comprising transparent conducting oxide, hole and electron transport layers (HTLs and ETLs), and metal electrodes, with multiple interfaces formed between materials that have drastically different physical and chemical properties.  $  ^{2,6,14}  $   The electrochemical reactivity of these interfaces can present additional sources of PSC instability.  $  ^{4,6,14,15}  $   Most importantly, perovskite is sandwiched between charge transport layers in both p-i-n and n-i-p device architectures, and hence, any reaction at the transport layer/perovskite interfaces could induce interfacial degradation that can have serious implications on the device performance and operational stability of PSCs. Therefore, such degradation processes will undermine any aforementioned strategies to design and synthesize stable halide perovskite materials by themselves. In this Perspective, we focus on the interfacial degradation of perovskite absorbers triggered by commonly used metal oxide transport layers and their detrimental effects on the PSC performance, stability, and perovskite decomposition pathway.

Metal oxides are preferred transport layer materials over organics because of their high optical transmission and inherent stability and have been shown to produce highly efficient PSCs.  $  ^{15-18}  $   However, the importance of interfacial interactions between the perovskite and metal oxides in a device has largely been overlooked. Our recent work on both methylammonium (MA) and formamidinium (FA) cation-based halide perovskites and other studies revealed undesirable reactions between the metal oxides and organic cations, leading to poor device characteristics and acceleration of perovskite degradation under typical device processing/fabrication or light soaking conditions.  $  ^{4,19-23}  $   Furthermore, metal oxides can also trigger structural instability and irreversible morphological changes in inorganic perovskite absorbers, leading to poor device performance under continuous illumination.  $  ^{24}  $   In light of the findings from published degradation studies based on metal oxide transport layers, we discuss the photo- and thermally induced interfacial degradation and pathways and their deleterious effects on the overall device performance and stability. We also review promising mitigation strategies that have been employed to alleviate this metal oxide/perovskite interface-specific degradation in PSCs to improve their performance and operational stability.

Direct evidence of interfacial reactions between NiO and halide perovskite is provided in our recent work.  $  ^{[19,20]}  $   Notably, contact with NiO significantly lowered the stability of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (MAI) and FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (FAI) by   $  \sim  $  100 °C (Figure 2a). Furthermore, NiO altered the energetics of the reaction pathway, resulting in interfacial degradation products (Figure 2a, bottom) that were different from the thermodynamically favored products in bulk degradation (Figure 2a, top). Thus, NiO accelerates interfacial degradation in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   and FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   not far above temperatures commonly used during device fabrication.  $  ^{[19,20]}  $   PSCs with NiO  $  _{x}  $   HTL are also susceptible to photodegradation under continuous illumination over an extended period. For instance, the encapsulated NiO  $  _{x}  $  /MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Cl  $  _{x}  $   device showed a loss in PCE because of decrease in   $  J_{sc}  $ , maintaining only 73% of its initial efficiency for 1000 h under accelerated aging test conditions of 85 °C and continuous 1 sun illumination at MPPT.  $  ^{[33]}  $   The cause of such device instabilities during illumination is relatively unexplored

for   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based devices. We speculate that such photoinstability could arise from the light-induced degradation at the   $  NiO_{x}  $  /perovskite interface. As reported by Boldyreva et al., both   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   and the mixed-cation–mixed-halide perovskites underwent significant light-induced degradation when deposited atop   $  NiO_{x}  $   HTL.  $  ^{21}  $   The phase compositions of the   $  NiO_{x}  $  /MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (Figure 2b, top left) and   $  NiO_{x}  $ /CsMAFAPb(BrI)  $  _{3}  $   (Figure 2b, top right) films showed photodegradation within 200 h of illumination, and the formation of substantial amounts of PbI  $  _{2}  $   was observed under 1000 h of light soaking conditions. Furthermore, the time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (ToF-SIMS, Figure 2b, bottom left) analysis revealed accumulation of   $  NiI_{2}^{+}  $  ions in the perovskite layer, a product of the reaction between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ .  $  ^{21}  $   Additionally,   $  NiO_{x}  $   promoted substantial light-induced crystallization in mixed-cation–mixed-halide perovskites, leading to dramatic changes in the film morphology under light exposure (Figure 2b, bottom right). This phenomenon was attributed to the chemical interaction between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and perovskite, creating defects that accelerated perovskite film crystallization.

Because of its high work function,   $  MoO_{x}  $   is commonly used as an HTL in silicon and organic solar cells.  $  ^{34,35}  $   But it has not been successfully employed as a stand-alone HTL in PSCs. This is because the   $  MoO_{x}  $  /perovskite interface is very reactive, resulting in poor device performance.  $  ^{36-38}  $   For instance, Shulz et al. observed a significant drop in   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF that correlated to a   $  \sim60\%  $   decrease in PCE in   $  MAPbI_{3}/MoO_{x}/CuPc  $   devices compared to   $  MAPbI_{3}/CuPc  $   devices.  $  ^{37}  $   It was proposed that the significant reactivity at the   $  MAPbI_{3}/MoO_{x}  $   interface induced degradation, resulting in the formation of substoichiometric   $  MoO_{3-x}  $   and accumulation of   $  PbI_{2}  $   at the interface, which severely deteriorated device parameters. Additionally, the presence of   $  PbMoO_{4}  $ ,   $  PbN_{2}O_{2}  $ , and PbO as byproducts was reported.  $  ^{39}  $   Instead,   $  MoO_{x}  $   is generally paired with electrode metals or used as a hole contact on top of organic HTLs in n-i-p architecture for efficient hole extraction and to improve device stability. We note that recent studies revealed light-induced instability in devices with   $  MoO_{x}  $   as an interlayer on top of organic HTLs at high operational temperatures.  $  ^{40,41}  $   Schloemer et al. observed rapid decay of device parameters, primarily   $  J_{sc}  $   in their   $  (FA_{0.79}MA_{0.16}Cs_{0.05})_{0.97}\mathrm{Pb(I_{0.84}Br_{0.16})_{2.97}}/spiro-OMeTAD  $ , EH44, or 3EtCz/  $  MoO_{x}  $   due to the buckling of HTL/  $  MoO_{x}  $   interface under continuous illumination (  $  \sim0.77  $   sun, AM1.5G) for >1100 h at 70 °C, leading to delamination.  $  ^{40}  $   Similarly, Tepliakova et al. reported a 24% drop in PCE of   $  CsFAPbI_{3}/PTAA/MoO_{x}  $   device after 3200 h of continuous light soaking conditions using metal halide lamp (30 mW/cm², 45 °C), and a further 25% decrease in PCE was observed when illumination was continued for another 1000 h using a light-emitting diode (100 mW/cm², 50 °C) in an inert   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere.  $  ^{41}  $   These studies pinpoint the redox reactivity of   $  MoO_{x}  $   as a cause for performance deterioration and instability of PSCs.  $  ^{40,41}  $   Importantly, these findings call for a search for new oxide HTL materials to design efficient and stable PSCs.

# Recent findings call for a search for new oxide HTL materials to design efficient and stable PSCs.

ETL/Perovskite Interface. Metal oxides such as   $  TiO_{2}  $  , ZnO, and   $  SnO_{2}  $   have been used as ETLs for PSCs.  $  ^{4,6,18}  $   Among these oxides,   $  TiO_{2}  $   is the most popular material in the n-i-p architecture, but they offer serious challenges for commercial deployment. Under thermal stress,   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   in contact with   $  TiO_{2}  $   showed similar stability as   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   alone; while   $  TiO_{2}  $   was shown to trigger interfacial degradation, the reaction could not be sustained because of the strong binding strength of the

decomposed molecules (e.g., HI) on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface that prevented further reaction with the perovskite.  $  ^{19,20}  $   Therefore, only the bulk degradation pathway was observed at higher temperatures. Despite its better thermal stability than NiO in the dark,   $  TiO_{2}  $ , with a bandgap of 3.2 eV, is an efficient photocatalyst under UV light for the decomposition of organic compounds,  $  ^{42,43}  $   which implies that it would have a propensity to trigger degradation of perovskite and cause a critical issue for the PSCs' long-term photostability. An early report showed that the encapsulated   $  TiO_{2}/MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   device experienced a rapid decrease in   $  J_{sc}  $   (Figure 3a, right) within 5 h of illumination, resulting in less than 10% of its initial PCE (Figure 3a, left) compared to the device when a UV filter was used.  $  ^{44}  $   Additionally, adverse hysteresis is often observed in the J-V curves of   $  TiO_{2}  $   devices (Figure 3b).  $  ^{45}  $   The source of the hysteresis was attributed to the migration of oxygen vacancies in   $  TiO_{2}  $   to the interface and their accumulation under electrical bias (Figure 3b, inset).  $  ^{45}  $   Direct evidence of oxygen ion migration from   $  TiO_{2}  $   to the perovskite layer under electrical bias (Figure 3c) was reported by Jung et al.  $  ^{46}  $   The authors concluded that the migration of oxygen ions (  $  O^{2-}  $  ) from   $  TiO_{2}  $   to the perovskite layer led to the reduction of   $  TiO_{2}  $   to   $  Ti_{2}O_{3}  $   while causing substantial structural changes in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ , including amorphization and formation of the PbI  $  _{2}  $   phase (Figure 3c). Consequently, the compact-  $  TiO_{2}  $ /mesoporous-  $  TiO_{2}/MAPbI_{3}  $   device exhibited a reduction in PCE with a pronounced hysteresis after applying a forward bias for 1 h under dark conditions.  $  ^{46}  $   Such phenomena can indeed negatively impact performance and device stability in the long run under realistic operating conditions. In the case of the   $  TiO_{2}/(FA_{0.79}MA_{0.16}Cs_{0.05})_{0.97}\mathrm{Pb(I_{0.84}Br0.16)}_{2.97}  $   device, Christians et al. observed losses in   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF with a rapid initial burn-in and biexponential decay in PCE under continuous illumination (Figure 3d). Further investigation revealed that   $  TiO_{2}  $   promoted irreversible cation redistribution in mixed-cation-mixed-halide perovskites, causing serious instability of devices.  $  ^{47}  $   Another effect reported in mixed halide perovskite was that   $  TiO_{2}  $   promoted halide ion segregation under pulsed laser excitation.  $  ^{48}  $   Again, these effects are detrimental to PSCs' performance and long-term stability. These findings highlight the predominance of the light-induced interfacial degradation pathway in   $  TiO_{2}  $ /perovskite PSCs, analogous to those observed at the NiO/perovskite interface induced by heat.

The insights into the cause of photodegradation reveal that the oxygen vacancies at the   $  TiO_{2}  $   (  $  Ti^{3+}, V_{o}  $  , Figure 3e) surface created during UV exposure act as deep trap states capturing electrons injected from perovskite.  $  ^{45,49}  $   The accumulation of these vacancies at the   $  TiO_{2}/perovskite  $   interface under electrical bias facilitates charge recombination and changes carrier injection and recombination dynamics at the interface.  $  ^{44,45}  $   Furthermore,   $  TiO_{2}  $   serves as an electron acceptor for   $  I^{-}  $  , oxidizing   $  I^{-}  $  chemisorbed on   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface to   $  I_{2}  $   (Figure 3e).  $  ^{49}  $   Thus, the perovskite structure is destabilized and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   decomposes to   $  CH_{3}NH_{2}  $   and HI, accumulating   $  PbI_{2}  $   at the interface (Figure 3e).  $  ^{2,49}  $   Such electrochemical reactions have serious implications on device stability, leading to a loss in device parameters and unfavorable hysteresis. Unlike   $  TiO_{2}  $   or ZnO,   $  SnO_{2}  $   is hailed as an alternative for its high performance as well as good photostability.  $  ^{2,47,56,57}  $   For example,   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL was reported to have achieved an impressive PCE of 25.2%.  $  ^{58}  $   Moreover, encapsulated   $  SnO_{2}/(FAPbI_{3})_{0.875}(MAPbBr_{3})_{0.125}(CsPbI_{3})_{0.1}  $   mixed halide perovskite devices showed excellent operational stability with 95% of initial PCE retained over 1000 h at MPPT while the PCE of the   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based device abruptly deteriorated and retained only 25% of its initial PCE after 1000 h of light exposure (Figure 5a).  $  ^{57}  $   Although   $  SnO_{2}  $   is considered a more robust ETL under illumination, we note that high temperatures can still induce interfacial reactions in these systems.  $  ^{19,22}  $   In particular, our work showed that the contact of   $  SnO_{2}  $   with   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   triggered interfacial degradation and altered the reaction pathway, thereby lowering the thermal stability of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   (Figure 5b).  $  ^{20}  $   Such interaction may still be significant to adversely affect the device performance at high temperatures. Additionally,   $  SnO_{2}  $   was also shown to promote halide segregation in mixed halide perovskites during pulsed laser excitation; however, the process was slower compared to   $  TiO_{2}  $ .  $  ^{48}  $   Therefore, photo and thermally induced degradation at the   $  SnO_{2}  $ /perovskite interface is worthy of further study to improve device performance and longevity.

Mitigation Strategies. Minimization of interfacial reactions between the metal oxide transport layer and perovskite through interface engineering is crucial for achieving efficient and stable PSCs. In this section, we summarize recent developments in interface modification strategies. Specifically, we focus on the use of interlayers including two-dimensional (2D) perovskites, metal oxide engineering-doping and bilayers,

and complex metal oxides to guide the rational design and optimization of interfaces for stable PSCs. Introducing interlayers, either inorganic or organic materials between the metal oxide and perovskite, has been demonstrated to be a promising approach to suppress degradation through interface passivation.  $  ^{15,26,44,55,59-61}  $   For example, Gao et al. reported the beneficial effects of modifying the   $  NiO_{x}/MAPbI_{3}  $   interface with KSCN, which helped impede the chemical reactions at the interface by passivating the surface defects on both   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  .  $  ^{59}  $   As a consequence of this double passivation effect, the unencapsulated device exhibited a high   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.14 V with PCE of 21.2% and enhanced stability over 400 h of continuous illumination.  $  ^{59}  $   To solve the UV light-induced photocatalysis and formation of oxygen vacancies in   $  TiO_{2}  $ , Zhang et al. modified the   $  TiO_{2}/Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.81}MA_{0.14}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $   interface using dopamine. The authors proposed that the hydroxyl groups in dopamine substantially reduced oxygen vacancies and suppressed deep trap states within   $  TiO_{2}  $ . In addition, the terminal amino groups in dopamine also passivated the uncoordinated Pb atoms and decreased the Pb–I/Br antisite defects and/or cation vacancies in perovskite at the interface. Thus, dopamine modification improved charge extraction efficiency at the   $  TiO_{2}/perovskite  $   interface while impeding charge accumulation and charge recombination. Consequently, the device delivered a PCE of 21% with negligible hysteresis and retained 80% of its initial performance after 1200 h of operation under constant full-sun illumination in the   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere.  $  ^{62}  $   Likewise, modification of ZnO with MAI was shown to be effective in removing hydroxyl groups from its surface by the formation of a thin layer of   $  ZnI_{2}  $ . Such a passivating layer suppressed the chemical activity of ZnO and resulted in improved thermal stability of the ZnO (MAI)/MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   device with 60% of the initial PCE maintained on heating at 65 °C for 12 h under   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere. Moreover, MAI-modified ZnO/  $  Cs_{0.12}FA_{0.88}PbI_{3}  $   also displayed improved photostability by retaining 82% of the initial PCE under continuous illumination using a metal halide lamp (50 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) light at 65 °C for 2100 h.  $  ^{63}  $   The incorporation of Eudoped WO  $  _{x}  $   between SnO  $  _{2}  $   and FAMAPb(BrI)  $  _{3}  $   led to remarkable improvement in PCE and long-term photostability of the device.  $  ^{64}  $   Eu-doped WO  $  _{x}  $   was argued to be efficient in minimizing interface traps/defects and hence the interfacial charge accumulation and recombination. Moreover, it improved crystallization of the perovskite film with the formation of larger grains and also suppressed UV-induced degradation of perovskite.  $  ^{64}  $   Furthermore, cross-linking of self-

The lower defect density slowed the formation of interfacial vacancies arising from ion migration that can serve as photogenerated charge recombination sites as well as nucleation sites for developing interfacial delamination cracks.  $  ^{66}  $   This work highlights the facile and efficient approach of using SAMs as device interlayer to impede irreversible degradation of perovskite layer and provide mechanical reinforcement at the metal oxide/perovskite interface. Given the merits of tailoring the interfaces using interlayers to simultaneously improve performance and long-term stability, further research efforts should be pursued to fully exploit their advantages and to understand the passivation mechanisms.

Benefiting from the excellent optoelectronic properties, ambient stability, and compatibility of the 2D perovskites with 3D perovskites, several works have successfully employed 2D perovskites to modify the perovskite/organic HTL interface to achieve stable PSCs.  $  ^{69-71}  $   However, research efforts in modifying the metal oxide/perovskite interface using this strategy are currently limited. By introducing phenethylammonium iodide (PEAI) on the   $  NiO_{x}  $   surface, Liu et al. demonstrated stable   $  NiO_{x}/PEA_{2}PbI_{4}/MAPbI_{3}  $   devices with PCE of 20.3% and better moisture and photostability when aged at elevated temperature in air.  $  ^{72}  $   The quasi 2D   $  PEA_{2}PbI_{4}  $   perovskite was formed at the interface through the coordination of bulky   $  PEA^{+}  $  organic cation with   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   and amine groups with Ni ions. The anchoring of amine groups on the   $  NiO_{x}  $   surface was proposed to reduce defects and thus improve interface contact properties.  $  ^{72}  $   Besides modifying the interface using organic–inorganic 2D-perovskites, Yang et al. showed the potential of inorganic 2D-  $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   perovskite nanosheet as interface passivation agent for realizing high performance and stable devices. The   $  TiO_{2}/2D-Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}/CsPbBrI_{2}  $   heterostructure achieved a PCE of 16.7% and significant enhancement in air, UV, and thermal stability over the   $  TiO_{2}/CsPbI_{2}Br  $   only device.  $  ^{73}  $   Therefore, integrated metal oxide/2D/3D perovskite design may offer a promising route to achieve targeted efficiency and stability in PSCs. Considering the versatile tunability of 2D perovskites, a plethora of opportunities exist for the design and optimization of metal oxide/3D perovskite interfaces.

Another way to optimize interfacial contact for efficient charge transport and mitigation of interfacial charge recombination or chemical reaction is to dope the metal oxides using elements such as Cl, S, Mg, Al, Ru, or F.  $  ^{24,52,74-78}  $   For example, doping   $  TiO_{2}  $   with Cl atoms was shown to reduce charge trap density at the interface, resulting in hysteresis-free devices with PCE of 20.1%. The device retained 90% of this initial value after 500 h of continuous illumination at MPPT.  $  ^{75}  $   In another study, doping ZnO with sulfur was proposed to efficiently decrease the oxygen vacancies and surface hydroxyl groups, thus impeding chemical reactions with the perovskite. The sulfur doped   $  ZnO/MAPbI_{3}  $   device displayed a PCE of 19.7%, and 90% was retained for 960 h under continuous 1 sun

illumination at 45 °C and MPPT.  $  ^{77}  $   Similarly, Ru incorporation in SnO  $  _{2}  $   was shown to reduce charge recombination at the interface. Notably, the hysteresis-free device showed improvement in   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF resulting in PCE of 22%. Further, the device retained 97% of its initial PCE over 2000 h under continuous illumination at MPPT.  $  ^{78}  $   These encouraging results should inspire researchers to explore this strategy by carefully choosing the dopant and optimizing their concentration to effectively improve the performance and longevity of PSCs.

Another interesting approach derives from making bilayer metal oxide charge extraction layers.  $  ^{57,79,80}  $   Because   $  SnO_{2}  $   displays better photostability, it makes sense to use it in contact with the perovskite in the bilayer configuration with other ETL materials.  $  ^{57,80}  $   For instance, Abuhelaiqa et al. showed stability enhancement when a   $  TiO_{2}/SnO_{2}  $   bilayer (Figure 5a, blue circles) was used; PCE of 19.8% was obtained with 97% retained for 1000 h at MPPT.  $  ^{57}  $   In a different approach, an ETL layer composed of nanoparticles with ZnO cores surrounded by   $  SnO_{2}  $   shells was shown to improve device stability with 90% of the initial PCE retained over 160 h. This work combined the benefits of excellent electronic properties of both ZnO and   $  SnO_{2}  $   materials while preventing the chemical reaction between ZnO and perovskite by eliminating their direct contact.  $  ^{81}  $   Because AZO is proven to be an efficient ETL with better performance and thermal stability than ZnO (Figure 4b left),  $  ^{52}  $   it is considered a promising material choice for the bilayer ETL configuration. When employed with ZnO, the bilayer ZnO/AZO/MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   device showed enhancement in thermal stability when heated at 100 °C. The device also maintained 91% of its initial PCE over 1000 h when stored in the dark under ambient conditions.  $  ^{82}  $   In pursuit of new metal oxide transport layers, a recent study explored the feasibility of Sn-doped   $  In_{2}O_{3}/In_{2}O_{3}  $   bilayer configuration as a top ETL in a p-i-n architecture. The authors showed that the   $  NiO_{x}/FAMAPbI_{3}/Sn-In_{2}O_{3}/In_{2}O_{3}  $   device can deliver PCE of 20.7% with negligible hysteresis. Importantly, this device exhibited excellent thermal stability and photostability over 2000 h of operation.  $  ^{83}  $   This work also showed the potential of developing all oxide-based HTL and ETL p-i-n devices. However, without mitigating the detrimental interactions at the   $  NiO_{x}/perovskite  $   interface, because   $  NiO_{x}  $   is particularly notorious for triggering interfacial degradation, any efforts to boost the operational stability of such PSCs will be futile. Furthermore, we note that electrochemical reactions between indium tin oxide and perovskite were reported to cause irreversible atomic mixing, forming   $  InI_{3}  $   at the interface as well as inducing morphological changes in the perovskite, presenting a serious problem to the operational stability.  $  ^{84,85}  $   Therefore, a careful choice of the metal oxides that are compatible with the adjacent perovskite layer will be of tremendous importance in designing bilayer transport layers to combine their respective advantages for practical use.

An effective strategy that avoids the complexity of additional interfaces or processing is to search for suitable alternative oxide transport layer materials. In this respect, complex metal oxides with a distinct crystal structure have advantages over binary oxides. Binary oxides undergo surface phase changes easily from redox reactions and change their composition. The crystal structure of complex oxides provides structural robustness and minimizes significant chemical changes. The extensive phase and compositional spaces of complex oxides further enable tailoring the crystal structure and composition and offer the versatility to tune optical and electrical properties.

It is no exaggeration that the predominance of interfacial reactions at metal oxide/perovskite interfaces is the main barrier to further improvements in PCE and the stability of PSCs. Therefore, progress toward the rational design of high-performance and stable PSCs using metal oxide transport layers necessitates better in situ characterizations and understanding of interfacial degradation processes accelerated by these oxides under light, heat, and combinations thereof. This should lead to a concerted effort for the design of target passivation materials. We also believe that integrating high-throughput density functional theory (DFT) calculations and machine learning with experimental validation will accelerate new materials discovery from the vast compositional spaces and design of efficient fabrication processes, revealing mechanistic insight into the device instability and degradation pathways and creating efficient interfacial modification strategies, all of which are vital for rapid development of PSCs for real-world deployment.  $  ^{95-97}  $   Another critical issue related to stability is the nonuniform testing criteria: each group performs its tests under different conditions and empirically evaluates device stability based on testing parameters they decide. Moreover, there is a lack of consistency and transparency in reporting the stability measurement conditions. Such variability in testing and reporting across the different research laboratories (see Table 1) poses an outstanding challenge to directly compare performance and assess the validity of mitigation strategies. To address these inconsistencies, the PSC research community should adopt unified stability testing and reporting protocols as outlined in the work of Khenkin et al.  $  ^{98}  $   It is also necessary to develop new protocols for accelerated aging tests under realistic operation conditions and to effectively predict the lifetime of PSCs. Achieving the targeted operational lifetime of 20–30 years will require us to overcome all barriers related to both intrinsic and extrinsic instabilities through a synergistic experimental and theoretical approach. When these concerns are addressed, PSC technology will be on track to generate sustainable and clean energy to meet the terawatt challenge.
