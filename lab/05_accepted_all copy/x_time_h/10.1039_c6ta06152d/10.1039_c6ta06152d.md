# Enhanced crystallization and stability of perovskites by a cross-linkable fullerene for high-performance solar cells

Meng Li,  $  ^{a}  $   Yi-Hsiang Chao,  $  ^{b}  $   Tin Kang,  $  ^{b}  $   Zhao-Kui Wang,  $  ^{*a}  $   Ying-Guo Yang,  $  ^{c}  $   Shang-Lei Feng,  $  ^{c}  $   Yun Hu,  $  ^{a}  $   Xing-Yu Gao,  $  ^{c}  $   Liang-Sheng Liao,  $  ^{*a}  $   and Chain-Shu Hsu  $  ^{*b}  $

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) still suffer from the issues of poor reproducibility and inferior stability despite of their considerable increase in efficiency. Here, we demonstrate planar PSCs with improved device characteristics and stability simultaneously by introducing a cross-linkable [6,6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C_{61}} $ -butyric styryl dendron ester (PCBSD) into  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_x\mathrm{Cl}_{3 - x} $  perovskites. The cross-linkable merit of C-PCBSD can enhance the crystallization of perovskites and address the issue of low electron extraction efficiency. In addition, the anti-solvent network of C-PCBSD facilitates the sequential solution process and avoids to be washed by the solvent used in upper layer. The C-PCBSD network could resist the moisture incursion to prevent the interfaces from erosion, and to passivate the voids or pinholes generated in the bulk active layer. As a result, an outstanding PSC with the power conversion efficiency of  $ 17.21\% $  is achieved with evidently improved cell stability.

# Introduction

In recent years, organometal halide perovskites based solar cells have rapidly become a focal point of the photovoltaic community due to their incredible achievement in the power conversion efficiency (PCE) over 20%. $ ^{1-8} $  Despite the high efficiency, many fundamental questions still remain unclear, which need to be addressed at both the material and device levels. For examples, unbalanced electron and hole diffusion lengths in the perovskites would hamper further improvement of PCEs in PSCs. $ ^{9} $  Uncontrolled film morphology associated with bad crystalline quality of the perovskites would result in low efficiency and poor reproducibility of the device performance. $ ^{10} $  In addition, the inferior stability may delay the progress of PSCs towards commercialization since the most high-efficiency PSCs based on present deposition techniques exhibited short device lifetime. $ ^{11,12} $  Some efforts on device structure, additive and interface engineering have been carried out to address these issues. For example, perovskites and fullerene derivatives were blended to balance the charge extraction, $ ^{13,14} $  reduce the hysteresis and recombination loss, $ ^{15} $  and improve the device fill factor. $ ^{16} $  Alkyl phosphonic acid  $ \omega $ -ammonium chlorides was used to crosslinking

the perovskite crystals, resulting in considerable increase in both the device efficiency and stability.  $  ^{[17]}  $   It should be noted that chlorobenzene (CB) or o-dichlorobenzene (ODCB), which were used as the solvent of the PCBM or Spiro-OMeTAD, may have the potential risk for washing out the fullerene derivatives in the perovskite absorbing layer. Therefore, developing solvent-resisted and facile film processing methodologies, which may resolve the intrinsic issues such as the charge extraction efficiency, the crystallinity and the stability in PSCs, is an efficient route to achieve high performance PSCs with long-term stability.

[6,6]-phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric styryl dendron ester (PCBSD) is a PCBM-based n-type material with a dendron containing two styryl groups functionalized as thermal cross-linkers.  $  ^{18}  $   The anti-solvent cross-linked PCBSD (C-PCBSD) has been utilized successfully in polymer solar cells with not only superior device characteristics but also remarkable long-term stability.  $  ^{18,19}  $   Preparation of perovskite film based on the one-step deposition technique often suffers from uncontrollable active layer with various morphologies, which invariably contain defects such as voids or pinholes in the bulk perovskite films.  $  ^{20}  $   Herein, we report planar structure perovskite solar cells

C-PCBSD can act as the electron acceptor in the composites of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}:C-PCBSD  $   to solve the problem of low electron extraction. # Experimental

# Materials

Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS, Clevios PVP AI 4083) was purchased from Heraeus (Germany). Methylamine (CH₃NH₂) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Lead chloride (PbCl₂, 99.99%), HI (99%), N, N-Dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.9%) were obtained from Alfa Aesar Ltd. Phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PC₆₁BM) and Bphen were purchased from Nichem Fine Technology Co. Ltd. (Taiwan). Cross-linkable [6,6]-phenyl-C₆₁-butyric styryl dendron ester (PCBSD) was synthesized according to our previous report.¹⁸

# Sample Preparation

The perovskite precursor solution containing C-PCBSD was prepared as follows. CH3NH3I (256 mg), PCBSD (varied mass) and 149 mg PbCl₂ were dissolved in 1 ml DMF (1 mL) mixed solvent with stirring overnight at 60 °C in the glove box. The mixture was kept stirred at 60 °C for 8 h and filtered through filter 0.45 (μm) before usage. The pre-patterned ITO-coated glass substrate with a sheet resistance of ~15Ω/sq was coated with PEDOT:PSS under 4500 rpm/40 s followed by annealing at 140 °C for 10 min in the air. And then, the sample was moved to the glove box for further layers deposition. Perovskite precursor solution was spin coated onto the PEDOT:PSS at 4000 rpm/40 s in the glove box. The annealing was carried out by heating from 60 to 100 °C at a ramp rate of 10 °C/10 min and then kept at 100 °C in the nitrogen glove box. PC₆₁BM (20 mg/ml in chlorobenzene) and Bphen were coated subsequently with room temperature annealing. Finally, the device was moved out of the glove box and transferred to a vacuum chamber under 2×10⁻⁶ Torr for Ag electrode evaporation. The active area of each device is 7.25 mm².

# Characterization

J-V characteristics of perovskite solar cells under 1 sun illumination were obtained using a programmable Keithley 2400 source meter under Air Mass (AM 1.5G) solar irradiation at   $  100 \, mW/cm^{2}  $   (Newport, Class AAA solar simulator, 94023A-U). The field-emission scanning electron microscope (SEM) images were obtained from a Quanta 200 FEG. X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurement was conducted using PANalytical (Empyrean) equipment. The grazing incidence X-ray diffraction (GIXRD) measurements were performed at the BL14B1 beam line of the Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF) using X-ray with a wavelength of   $  0.6887 \, \AA  $  . Two-dimensional (2D) GIXRD patterns were acquired by a Mar 3450 mounted vertically at a distance   $  \sim442.745 \, mm  $   from the sample with a grazing incidence angle of   $  0.2^\circ  $   and an exposure time of 10 sec. The 2D GIXRD patterns were analyzed using the FIT2D software and displayed in scattering vector q coordinates. The absorbance of the perovskite films were measured with an UV/Vis spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer Lambda 750). The transmission IR spectra were recorded from a Bruker Vertex 80v FTIR spectrometer in the air. The incident-photon-to-current efficiency (IPCE) measurement was performed using a system combining a xenon lamp, a monochromator, a chopper and a lock-in amplifier together with a calibrated silicon photodetector. The electrical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was obtained using an IM6e Electrochemical Workstation (ZAHNER, Germany) with alternating current amplitude of 10 mV. Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) measurements were acquired using Edinburgh Instruments FLS920 fluorescence spectrometer with a 532 nm pulsed laser as excitation source at room temperature. UPS analysis measurements using an unfiltered He I (21.2 eV) gas discharge lamp and a hemispherical analyzer.

It suggests that cross-linkable PCBSD played more important role than PCBM in improving the film quality of the perovskite active layer.

# Roles of cross-linkable PCBSD

To deeply understand the underlying mechanism of improved device performance in cross-linkable PSCs, photoluminescence (PL) spectra of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $  ,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $  : PCBM, and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $  : C-PCBSD films were characterized as shown in Figure 4a. The PL intensity of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $  : C-PCBSD and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $  : PCBM composite films are much weaker than the pristine   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $   films. It indicates that efficient electron extraction takes place in the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $  : C-PCBSD composite film rather than in the pristine   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $   films, which confirms the role of C-PCBSD as the “electron acceptor”.  $  ^{15}  $   However,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $  : C-PCBSD and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $  : PCBM composite films are not sufficient for charge separation of exciton. As a result, additional layer of PCBM film was deposited on the top of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $  : C-PCBSD composite films. The similar result coincides with Wu and co-workers’ report recently.  $  ^{16}  $

Figure 4b shows the Nyquist plots of both devices in the dark at an applied voltage of  $ 0.95\mathrm{V} $ . From the 2D GIXRD profiles, the diffraction spots in   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $ : C-PCBSD composite film (Figure 4e) is more sharper and weaker than those in   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $   film. We ascribed the high-quality crystallization to one of the main factors on improved device performance.  $  ^{31-35}  $

In all, cross-linkable PCBSD molecules in the active films can fill the pinholes and vacancies between perovskite grains, which will result in improved film coverage and more uniform perovskite films. It indicates that the improved crystallinity of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $  : PCBSD was mainly ascribed to the cross-linked blends by C-PCBSD.

# Photovoltaic Stability

In addition, degradation of the perovskite solar cells is mainly associated with the decomposition of the perovskite active layer due to the corrosion by moisture that can intrude into the perovskite active layer through the degraded top electrode, $ ^{36,37} $  and the leakage current caused by the formation of hot spots in bulk or at the interface. $ ^{38} $  Actually, cross-linkable PCBSD can produce a robust and adhesive film network with sufficient solvent resistance. $ ^{18,19} $  Therefore, C-PCBSD based PSCs are expected to demonstrate improved cell stability. Obviously, C-PCBSD included device presents a better stability than the pristine  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_x\mathrm{Cl}_{3 - x} $  based one. # Conclusions

We have demonstrated planar PSCs with improved device performance and stability simultaneously based on a one-step solution-processable strategy by blending cross-linkable PCBSD and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_x\mathrm{Cl}_{3 - x} $ . The anti-solvent network of C-PCBSD facilitates the sequential solution process and avoids to be washed by the solvent used in upper layer. The C-PCBSD network could resist the moisture incursion to prevent the interfaces from erosion, and to passivate the voids or pinholes generated in the bulk acitive layer. The cross-linkable merit of C-PCBSD can address the issue of low electron extraction efficiency, enhance the crystallization of perovskites and improve the stability of the PSCs. The promising approach provides a simple route in the fabrication of highly efficient and stable planar bulk heterojunction perovskite solar cells.
