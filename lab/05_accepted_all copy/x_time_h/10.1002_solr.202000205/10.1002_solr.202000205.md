# Abstract

A highly effective interface engineering approach employing a multifunctional molecule, 5-amino-2,4,6-triiodoisophthalic acid (ATPA) to anchor on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_3} $  simultaneously by reacting with dangling hydroxyl group on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  surface and passivating the defects of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_3} $  films. In addition, the introduction of ATPA results in cascade energy level alignment between the perovskite and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  electron-transporting layer (ETL) to improve the electron extraction property. Based on the ATPA modified  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  substrates, the optimized  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_3} $  perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) deliver the highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) of over  $ 18\% $  with suppressed hysteresis. Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) have been regarded as a promising candidate for next-generation photovoltaics (PVs) due to their various unique properties, including high absorption coefficients, large charge carrier mobilities, long electron/hole diffusion lengths, facile solution processability, and low fabrication cost.  $  ^{[1-4]}  $   However, the volatile nature of organic cations such as methylammonium (MA  $  ^{+}  $  ), formamidinium (FA  $  ^{+}  $  ), et al. in these perovskites causes some stability issues under the light, photo and thermal stresses.  $  ^{[5-7]}  $   Alternatively, inorganic cations such as Cs  $  ^{+}  $   ions have been reported that can potentially replace organic cations to address these stability issues.  $  ^{[8-12]}  $   For example, CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   based PVSCs demonstrated outstanding thermal stability than the organic-inorganic hybrid PVSCs.  $  ^{[13-16]}  $

Although the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the state-of-the-art inorganic PVSCs has achieved significant progress,  $  ^{[17]}  $  it is still far below those reported for the organic-inorganic hybrid counterparts, which has a certified PCE of 25.2%.  $  ^{[18]}  $  The main reasons for the low PCE in inorganic PVSCs are their relatively low open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) and fill factor (FF).  $  ^{[14, 19-20]}  $  Recently, several strategies have been reported on improving the  $  V_{OC}  $  and FF of  $  CsPbI_{3}  $  based PVSCs, including vacuum thermal co-evaporation,  $  ^{[21-22]}  $  solvent-controlled growth,  $  ^{[23]}  $  dimension engineering,  $  ^{[24]}  $  B-site doping/alloying,  $  ^{[25-26]}  $  additive loading,  $  ^{[27-28]}  $  et al. However, the  $  V_{OC}  $  deficit and FF loss are mainly caused by the defects in bulk and surface/grain boundaries of inorganic perovskites and the mismatch of energy bands between perovskite and charge-transporting layer (CTL).  $  ^{[29-37]}  $  Therefore, it would be ideal if novel dual-functional materials can be applied to simultaneously passivate perovskite surface and promote better energy alignment to improve PVSC performance.

In this work, we report the application of a novel dual-functional interfacial material: 5-amino-2,4,6-triiodoisophthalic acid (ATPA, chemical structure shown in Figure S1) to interact with both  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  perovskite and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  electron-transporting layer (ETL) to achieve better energy tuning and surface passivation. ATPA is a commercially available compound which not only can facilitate charge extraction and recombination by forming cascade energy alignment between perovskite and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL, but also can passivate the defects on the surface of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $ . The carboxylic acid and amine groups on ATPA engenders strong interactions with  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and perovskite to facilitate efficient electron transfer from perovskite to planar  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL. Moreover, the iodide groups in ATPA could provide a I-rich growth condition, which is benefiting for the formation  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  perovskites with suppressed intrinsic defects.[38-40] As a result, the  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  and FF of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  PVSCs could be simultaneously improved, leading to a high PCE over  $ 18.12\% $  with a  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of  $ 1.11\mathrm{V} $  and a FF of  $ 81.86\% $ . Moreover, the non-encapsulated  $ \mathrm{TiO_2/ATPA} $ -based device showed good photostability with  $ 93\% $  of its initial PCE retained under continuous 1 sun illumination for  $ 300\mathrm{h} $  in a nitrogen filled glovebox.

ATPA was selected as an interfacial material to modify the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite interface because it contains both carboxylic acid and amine groups, which allows chemical interactions with both perovskite and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  films. In order to determine the effects of ATPA on the morphology and electrical properties of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film, a careful analysis of the topography, surface potential and energy level was conducted using atomic force microscopy (AFM), Kelvin probe force microscope (KPFM), and ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS), respectively, as shown in Figure 1.

However, the average surface potential of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film increased significantly after the ATPA treatment (Figure 1c and 1d), implying the reduction of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  work function.[41] The ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurements were also conducted to characterize the surface energy levels of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  before and after ATPA treatment. The conduction band minimum (CBM), which can be calculated from the valence band maximum (VBM) extracted from UPS results (Figure 1e) and the bandgap  $ (E_{\mathrm{g}}) $  obtained by UV-Vis measurements (Figure S2), was -7.39 eV and -6.91 eV, respectively before and after the ATPA treatment. Since larger  $ \Delta E $  would lead to a bigger energy loss in charge transfer, the introduction of ATPA creates a desirable energy alignment for more efficient electron transfer from  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_3} $  to  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer with a smaller energy loss.[42-43]

To further explore the effect of ATPA on the defect states of the perovskite, temperature dependent steady-state PL of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films on   $  TiO_{2}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}/ATPA  $   were investigated at temperatures between 160 and 350 K (Figure 2e and 2f). In order to explore the interaction mechanism of   $  TiO_{2}/ATPA  $   and   $  ATPA/CsPbI_{3}  $  , the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was used to examine the chemical bonding states at the interface of   $  TiO_{2}/ATPA  $   (Figure 3a) and   $  ATPA/CsPbI_{3}  $   (Figure 3b). For the pristine   $  TiO_{2}  $   thin film, the O 1s core level could be deconvoluted into two peaks centered at 530.3 eV and 532.6 eV, respectively. The former peak (530.3 eV) was assigned to be the   $  O^{2-}  $   anion bonded with the   $  Ti^{4+}  $   cation (Ti-O) in the stoichiometric   $  TiO_{2}  $   structure, and the latter one (532.6 eV) represented the hydroxyl groups on the surface of   $  TiO_{2}  $   film (Ti-O-H).  $  ^{[49]}  $   After   $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate was treated with ATPA, the peak intensity of Ti-O-H was significantly reduced and a new peak appeared at 532.0 eV, indicating the formation of C-O-Ti bonds.  $  ^{[50]}  $   These results implied that the esterification between the hydroxyl

Furthermore, the peaks of   $  Ti\ 2p_{1/2}  $   (459.2 eV) and   $  Ti\ 2p_{3/2}  $   (464.9 eV) changed little before and after the ATPA treatment (Figure S7),  $  ^{[49, 51]}  $   indicating negligible influence of the reaction between   $  TiO_{2}  $   and ATPA on the valence state of Ti. As shown in Figure 3b, the Pb 4f peaks of the ATPA/  $  CsPbI_{3}  $   sample shifted to lower binding energy compared to that of the reference sample, which indicated there is interaction between ATPA and lead ion (  $  Pb^{2+}  $  ) via coordination bonding.  $  ^{[31]}  $   The state of Cs remained unchanged (Figure S8), indicating that ATPA had no interaction on the   $  Cs^{+}  $  ion. Therefore, ATPA should offer two types of chemical linkages to anchor the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   layer on top and   $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate simultaneously as illustrated in Figure 4b. It has been reported that the surface of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer is rich in dangling bonds such as hydroxyl groups, which might form deep trap states to induce charge recombination.  $  ^{[49, 52]}  $   The carboxylic acid group on ATPA would react with hydroxyl groups on   $  TiO_{2}  $   to result in reduced surface defects.  $  ^{[53]}  $   Furthermore, the hydroxyl groups might create stronger surface dipoles due to their higher electronegativity, the different molecular geometries of ATPA might affect the orientation of dipoles formed on   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface to lead to a more delocalized electron (cloud) distribution, which eventually weakened the surface dipoles. This meant the surface potential of   $  TiO_{2}  $   film could increase after ATPA treatment which is consistent with the KPFM results discussed above. Moreover, the carboxyl and amine groups on ATPA were also expected to passivate the defects of the perovskite layer, reducing charge recombination induced by these trap states.  $  ^{[54]}  $

Since the ATPA treatment significantly enhanced the performance of devices, the behaviors of charge carriers recombination and transport in the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   devices were also investigated to understand how did ATPA affect the device performance. The light-intensity dependent   $  V_{OC}  $   was depicted in Figure 5a. The PVSC based on   $  TiO_{2}/ATPA  $   showed smaller slope (1.26   $  k_{B}T/e  $  ) compared to that based on   $  TiO_{2}  $   (1.71   $  k_{B}T/e  $  ) (  $  k_{B}  $   is the Boltzmann constant and T is the absolute temperature), indicating that the trap-assisted recombination was effectively inhibited by the ATPA because of the reduced trap states.  $  ^{[49,59]}  $   Meanwhile, the transient photovoltage (TPV) decay curve of the PVSC based on   $  TiO_{2}/ATPA  $   showed longer lifetime than that based on   $  TiO_{2}  $   (Figure S14), indicating obviously suppressed charge-carrier recombination. In addition, the larger recombination resistance of PVSC based on   $  TiO_{2}/ATPA  $   revealed by electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements in Figure 5c also confirmed this suppressed charger recombination.  $  ^{[60]}  $   Figure 5b presented the light-intensity dependent   $  J_{SC}  $   (  $  J\propto I^{a}  $  , J is the current density, I is the light intensity and   $  \alpha  $   is the power factor). The device based on   $  TiO_{2}  $   exhibited an   $  \alpha  $   value of 0.91, whereas the device based on   $  TiO_{2}/ATPA  $   showed a larger   $  \alpha  $   value of 0.99. This result validated that the charge carriers loss induced by trap-assisted recombination was obviously reduced in   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PVSC with the incorporation of ATPA.  $  ^{[61]}  $   The dark current results in Figure 5d suggested that the ATPA treatment could effectively reduce the leakage current in   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PVSC, which is beneficial for larger   $  V_{OC}  $  . The faster photocurrent decay response of PVSC based on   $  TiO_{2}/ATPA  $   showed better charge extraction efficiency than that based on   $  TiO_{2}  $   (Figure S15) due to the better aligned energy level.

In conclusion, our work showed that ATPA could act as a multifunctional chemical linker between   $  TiO_{2}  $   and   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   layer to optimize the   $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface. The better aligned energy levels enabled more efficient charge extraction from   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   to   $  TiO_{2}  $  . Moreover, the passivation of ATPA on   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   surface led to fewer traps and suppressed the interfacial trap-assisted recombination. As a result, the optimized   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PVSC delivered a high PCE of 18.12% with
