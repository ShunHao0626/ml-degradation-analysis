# Temperature dependence of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   films by quasi-vapor deposition technique and impact on photovoltaic performance and stability of perovskite solar cells

Vijendar Reddy P, Suresh Maniyarasu, Reshma Dileep K, Ramya Krishna Battula, P. Uday Bhaskar, Easwaramoorthi Ramasamy, Ganapathy Veerappan*

Centre for Solar Energy Materials, International Advanced Research Centre for Powder Metallurgy and New Materials (ARCI), Balapur, Hyderabad 500005, Ind.

# ARTICLEINFO

Perovskite solar cells (PSC) have been a major focus in the photovoltaic community because of their high efficiency within a decade. The performance and stability of PSCs are highly dependent on the perovskite layer deposition and film quality. However, the solution processing techniques are still lacking to produce high-quality perovskite films for large scale device fabrication. In this work, a high-quality and uniform MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskite films are developed by quasi-vapor deposition (QVD) at low temperature, low working pressure, with short reaction time and without post annealing. The substrate temperature is varied from 70 °C to 130 °C during the deposition in order to study the film uniformity, morphology and its influence on PSCs. MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  films deposited at 100 °C showed uniform film with grain size of 375 nm and the PSCs prepared with the film exhibited the highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 15.6%. Simultaneously, PSC prepared with the solution process exhibited PCE of 12.0% with poor ambient stability than the QVD processed devices. The results will provide a new pathway for developing high quality and large scale device with high performance and stability.

# 1. Introduction

In recent times, organic-inorganic halide perovskites (OIHP) are considered to be an important absorber material for next generation solar cells. Within a short span of time, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSC) elevated from 3.8% (2009) to 25.5% (2019) [1,2], owing to the superior opto-electronic properties such as direct and tunable bandgap, high absorption coefficient, high charge carrier mobility and long diffusion length [3–5]. The photovoltaic performance and stability of PSCs are highly dependent on film morphology and crystallinity [6], which is in turn reliant on the deposition techniques. OIHPs are fabricated by various techniques such as solution process [7,8], thermal evaporation [9,10], vapor assisted solution process [11,12], doctor blading [13], slot die coating [14], hybrid chemical vapor deposition [15,16] and spray coating [17]. Among them, drop-casting and vapor processing method are mostly used to deposit high quality perovskite absorber films, which has achieved the highest efficiency so far [18]. Methylammonium lead

Reaction kinetics in solution process occurs at a faster rate due to the rapid reaction between metal halide (PbI $ _2 $ ) and methylammonium iodide (MAI) resulting in the formation of rough, porous and partially converted perovskite films [21]. Although, solution-based methods are simple and of low-cost, reproducibility, unconverted PbI $ _2 $ , film uniformity and large area fabrication are few bottlenecks. To overcome the drawbacks of solution-based methods, Liu et al. for the first time reported mixed halide composition of perovskite CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  by a dual-source vapor deposition technique, where PbCl $ _2 $  and MAI are simultaneously deposited on to the TiO $ _2 $  coated FTO substrate under high vacuum. PSCs fabricated by VASP exhibited uniform films with large grain size [11], but the whole process requires a controlled atmosphere (glove box), which hinders the scaling up of PSCs. To evade the complicated and expensive glove box setup, Leyden qi et al. made a high-quality perovskite film using hybrid chemical vapor deposition (HCVD). Here, they used multi-zone tubular furnace in which one zone is maintained at a higher temperature to evaporate MAI and the other zone is maintained at a lower temperature; where   $  PbCl_{2}  $   deposited substrates were placed. The films deposited by this process exhibited not only high efficiency but also retained almost same efficiency for 1100 h when stored under dry   $  N_{2}  $   [15]. Several groups focused on HCVD deposition method and varied the sample preparation conditions such as gas flow, pressure, temperature and position of the substrate and source to obtain defect and impurity free perovskite films. Very recently, Ng et al. [16] prepared a high performance PSC (17.6%) made with HCVD. However, using high temperatures of 150–175 °C for 2 h limits the process for flexible PSC fabrication. Peng et al. [22] made a high quality   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   based PSC, where the films were developed by HCVD using one-zone tubular furnace at 2 mtorr of vacuum with a reaction time of 3 h. Even though, they have employed low deposition temperature, the temperature at the source and substrate cannot be maintained independently, which is a constraint in one-zone tubular furnace. Also, further annealing is required to remove the excess MAI deposited on top of the film. In general, HCVD process requires high deposition temperatures and post annealing for perovskite film formation. In this work, we developed high quality   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films employing quasi-vapor deposition (QVD) technique at low temperature, low-pressure with no post annealing of the films. To improve the film quality and morphology, we varied the substrate temperature (70 °C, 100 °C and 130 °C) during deposition by keeping a vacuum of 1 mbar and reaction time of 60 min constant. PSCs were fabricated in each of these conditions and the effect of film uniformity, morphology, grain size variation due to substrate temperature and their influence on photovoltaic performance was evaluated. Among all, the device fabricated with the film deposited at 100 °C exhibited the highest PCE of 15.6% with an open circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.07 V and a current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 22.2 mA/cm². Simultaneously, PSC with solution processing method were fabricated for comparison, which exhibited the PCE of 12%. The QVD devices almost exhibited 80% of their initial PCE for 1200 h, while the solution processed devices retained only 60% of their PCE for 1200 h; after which they stopped functioning.

# 2. Experiments

# 2.1. Synthesis of methylammonium iodide (MAI)

MAI was synthesized as per our previously reported article   $  [23]  $  . Methylamine (40 wt% in methanol, sigma Aldrich) and hydroiodic acid (HI) (57 wt% in water, Aldrich) was mixed together in a round bottom flask, kept under stirring for 2 h at 0 °C (ice bath). The excess amount of HI acid was removed using a rotary evaporator at 50 °C and obtained a whitish MAI powder. The resultant powder was rinsed with diethyl ether multiple times to remove any unreacted HI. The resultant MAI salt was kept to be dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C overnight.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

One side edge of the fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass substrate (8  $ \Omega/\square $ ) was etched using zinc dust and hydrochloric acid. The etched substrates were ultra sonicated in soap water, distilled water, acetone and IPA for 15 min each. Two different concentration of compact- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  solution was made by mixing titanium diisopropoxide bisacetylacetonate and 1-butanol, which was then spin coated onto the substrates and sintered at  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 30 min [24]. Mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  layer comprising of  $ 20\mathrm{nm} $   $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  paste dispersed in ethanol in 1:3.5 ratio (by weight) is spin coated onto the compact  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  followed by sintering at  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C}/30\mathrm{min} $  to remove the binders. The prepared  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  were immersed in an aqueous  $ \mathrm{TiCl}_4 $  solution (40 mM in deionized water) and kept in furnace at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 60 min, later the substrates were annealed at  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 30 min [24].  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films are deposited by QVD technique; spin coating of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  followed by sublimation of MAI in a multi-zone furnace. 1 M  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  solution in dimethylformamide (DMF) was drop casted onto the preheated ( $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) substrates. The films were annealed at  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 30 min to ensure

The   $  PbI_{2}  $   coated substrates was placed in a multi-zone tubular quartz furnace; where the substrate temperature was altered from 70 °C to 130 °C. MAI powder was transferred into the furnace in a crucible and placed in an up-stream high temperature zone of 150 °C. Spiro-OMeTAD was spin coated on top of the perovskite films and followed by thermal evaporation of Au at a vacuum of   $  10^{-6}  $   mbar.

# 2.3. Characterization techniques

The crystallographic studies of the electrodes were analyzed using an X-ray diffractometer (D8 Advanced Bruker) with a monochromatic  $ \mathrm{Cu - K_{\alpha}} $  radiation ( $ \lambda = 1.5406\AA $ ). The optical characterization was carried out by Cary 5000 UV-Vis-INR spectrophotometer over the range of  $ 300 - 800\mathrm{nm} $ , the bandgap of the perovskite film was calculated using the obtained data. The surface morphology of the perovskite films was analyzed using a field emission scanning electron microscopy (ZEISS GeminiSEM 500), grain size distribution was analyzed using imageJ software. The photovoltaic parameters and the stability data were measured using AAA solar simulator (Oriel instruments, AM 1.5) in ambient conditions. The contact angle measurements were carried out using a drop shape analyzer DSA 100 (Kruss GmbH, Germany).

# 3. Results and discussion

Fig. 1 represents the schematic diagram of device fabrication via low vacuum (1 mbar) QVD setup and solution process. Here, the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film acts as a framework, as well as provides kinetically stable nucleation sites for  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film formation [11]. The  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  films when exposed to MAI vapor starts intercalating and reacting with the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  octahedron to form a polycrystalline perovskite film [11]. The crystallographic orientations of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  favor the film growth in such a way that the specific surface and interface energy is minimized [11]. The crystallinity of solution processed and the vapor processed perovskite films are characterized using X-ray diffractometer (XRD). The XRD spectrum (Fig. 2a) exhibits two major diffraction peaks at  $ 14.2^{\circ} $ ,  $ 28.4^{\circ} $  corresponding to (110) and (220) lattice planes of tetragonal  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  crystal structure. The spectra also consist of other orientations of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  such as (112) at  $ 20.0^{\circ} $ , (211) at  $ 23.5^{\circ} $ , (310) at  $ 32.1^{\circ} $  and (314) at  $ 43.2^{\circ} $  [15]. The additional peaks are related to the FTO substrate. Solution processed perovskite films exhibits an additional peak at  $ 12.6^{\circ} $ , suggesting the presence of unconverted  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , whereas the vapor processed films ( $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ,  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and  $ 130^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) completely converted into perovskite without leaving any residual  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . The reaction kinetics in the solution process is very quick, which leaves unconverted  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  at the surface [7,26]. The perovskite films prepared by QVD process ensure the formation of high-quality  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  crystalline film. The optical property of the perovskite films was studied using a UV-Vis-Spectroscopy and the results are shown in Fig. 2b. The QVD processed films exhibited higher absorbance, when compared with the solution processed perovskite film due to large grain size of the perovskite film. The  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  processed films exhibited lower absorbance compared to the

100 and 130 °C processed films. The variation in processing temperatures and its effect on band gap energy were also analyzed from the absorbance data. Tauc's plot was used to calculate the band gap energy. The band gap energy was consistently 1.58 eV for all vapor processed film and 1.54 eV for solution processed films. The optical properties of the vapor processed films are superior when compared to the solution processed films. The electrical properties of QVD processed MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  film was indirectly deduced from the current-voltage characteristics measured in dark condition at forward bias (Fig. S4). The onset potential required for the current to flow in QVD processed perovskite film is about 100 mV higher than that of solution processed film in a similar experimental configuration. This may attributed to better stoichiometry and high crystallinity which eventually results in less leakage pathways (i.e., defects) for the current to flow. The observation of relatively less current density at any given forward bias potential for QVD processed perovskite film further confirms superior electrical property.

Perovskite films processed by solution process tend to degrade faster as they are exposed to the solvent medium in the MAI precursor solution for a longer period; when compared with the QVD process [11]. The absence of solvent medium, longer reaction duration and low processing temperatures collectively contributed in yielding high quality perovskite films in QVD process when compared to the solution processed films.  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films processed by QVD exhibited complete conversion and high crystallinity rendering a high-quality perovskite films; which is a prime requirement for good photovoltaic performance. As the substrate temperature increased from  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  to  $ 130^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , the intensity of major peaks also improved, due to high crystallinity and larger grain size, which can be also observed from the FE-SEM (Fig. 3) results. Surface morphology and uniformity of perovskite film influences the photovoltaic performance of the PSCs. Perovskite films with incomplete surface coverage and pinholes, exhibits lower photovoltaic performance due to low-resistance shunting pathways [10,27]. Grain size distribution was calculated from the surface morphology images, and was found to increase with temperature. Solution processed films (Fig. 3a) exhibited an average grain size of  $ 162\mathrm{nm} $  and the vapor deposited films exhibited  $ 190\mathrm{nm} $ ,  $ 372\mathrm{nm} $  and  $ 375\mathrm{nm} $  for  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ,  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and  $ 130^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , respectively (Fig. 3b-d) [11]. QVD processed  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films yielded large grains in comparison with the solution processed film, which can be attributed to the volume expansion during the reaction and intercalation of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  with MAI [11]. The film deposited at  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  consisted of pin holes and small irregular shaped grains. The grain growth is dependent on the substrate temperature as the substrate temperature increases to  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , the film grows densely with large grains and full surface coverage, without pinholes. The grains in a solution processed perovskite films are found to be highly nonuniform and lack interconnection between the grains giving rise to amorphous intergranular spaces, which acts as initiation points for degradation of perovskite films [28]. The amorphous layer formation at the grain boundary in solution processed films are due to the nonstoichiometric composition and their segregation as non-stoichiometric excess residuals at the grain boundary [28]. These films when compared to the QVD processed films have a thicker amorphous layer at the grain boundary hence tend to degrade at a faster rate. In QVD processed films, the grains are large, highly uniform and well connected, reducing the intergranular space available for moisture attack. During intercalation of MAI into the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  lattice, the grains are rearranged in such a way to facilitate minimum grain boundary length resulting in reduced grain boundary energy [28]. The FE-SEM images of solution processed perovskite film showcases unevenly grown grains due to the uncontrolled Ostwald ripening, indicating a thermodynamically unstable film. In case of vapor deposited films, ripening or coarsening doesn't take place in the post stages of the film growth, suggesting a thermodynamically stable perovskite film formation even at lower temperatures [11].

# 3.1. Photovoltaic performance

In order to study the impact of MAPbI $ _3 $  deposition technique on photovoltaic performance, PSCs were fabricated by QVD and solution process with n-i-p configuration comprising of FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $ /m-TiO $ _2 $ /MAPbI $ _3 $ /Spiro-OMeTAD/Au. The I-V measurements were carried out under AM 1.5G illumination (100 mW/cm $ ^2 $ ) and are shown in Fig. 4. as well the results are tabulated in Table 1. The devices fabricated at 100 °C showed the highest PCE of 15.6% with a  $ V_{OC} $  of 1.07 V and a  $ J_{SC} $  of 22.2 mA/cm $ ^2 $ . The champion device exhibited an efficiency of 16.8% with a  $ V_{OC} $  of 1.06 V and a  $ J_{SC} $  of 24.2 mA/cm $ ^2 $  (Fig. S2). Uniform dense films with well-connected large grains provided a hassle-free pathway for the charge transport that led to enhanced performance of the QVD films processed at an optimum temperature of 100 °C. PSCs made with the films deposited at 70 °C showed low  $ V_{OC} $  and  $ J_{SC} $ , due to smaller grains leading to charge entrapping at the numerous grain boundaries. Although, the films deposited at 130 °C showed larger grain size and better uniformity with complete perovskite conversion, the photovoltaic performance was lesser than the films processed at 100 °C. This can be attributed to the faster rate of perovskite crystallization at a higher temperature, from a few

nucleation sites giving rise to large grains which result in inefficient charge transfer [29,30]. The solution processed films showed an efficiency of  $ 12\% $  with a  $ V_{OC} $  of  $ 1.0\mathrm{V} $  and a  $ J_{SC} $  of  $ 18.4\mathrm{mA} / \mathrm{cm}^2 $  (Fig. 4). And the champion solution processed device showed an efficiency of  $ 13.5\% $  with a  $ V_{OC} $  of  $ 1.08\mathrm{V} $  and a  $ J_{SC} $  of  $ 18.9\mathrm{mA} / \mathrm{cm}^2 $  (Fig. S2). The lower performance can be owed to the incomplete conversion of the precursor materials into the perovskite phase as seen in the XRD pattern in Fig. 2(a). The inefficient light harvesting by  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  along with the non-uniform film and irregular grain size led to deprived charge generation and transfer; which is reflected in its inferior performance and the reaction mechanism happens during the spin coating process itself. So, the smaller processing time leads to incomplete perovskite conversion, resulting in lower performance [31]. On the other hand, the gradual and complete crystallization of the perovskite grains minimizes the unwanted phases in the QVD films [32].

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, we developed a uniform and high-quality perovskite absorber film using QVD method at a vacuum of 1 mbar, reaction time of 60 min with no additional post annealing treatment. The perovskite films prepared by QVD method showed a uniform film with a large grain size (  $  \sim  $  375 nm). The effect of substrate temperature on the crystallinity of the film and device performance are also studied. The device fabricated with the best-optimized condition (100°C) showed a PCE of 15.6% with promising stability. The device retained 60% of its initial efficiency for more than 3000 h under ambient conditions, whereas, the device prepared by the solution processing method retained 60% of its efficiency for only 1200 h. Further optimization of film thickness may enhance device performance and stability. This deposition system could provide an answer for large-scale deposition of perovskite solar cells at a lower cost.
