# An Efficient and Thickness Insensitive Cathode Interface Material for High Performance Inverted Perovskite Solar Cells with 17.27 % Efficiency

Sen Peng  $  ^{a}  $  , Jingsheng Miao  $  ^{b*}  $  , Imran Murtaza  $  ^{a,d}  $  , Liang Zhao  $  ^{a}  $  , Zhao Hu  $  ^{b}  $  , Ming Liu  $  ^{b}  $  , Tingbin Yang  $  ^{c*}  $  , Yongye Liang  $  ^{c}  $  , Hong Meng  $  ^{a,b*}  $  , Wei Huang  $  ^{a}  $

In inverted planar heterojunction (PHJ) perovskite solar cells (PSCs), the interface layer between the electron transporting layer and cathode plays a very important role to achieve high power efficiency. Herein, we present the synthesis of a new water/alcohol soluble small molecular cationic compound based on naphthalene diimide functionalized pyridinium salt, N,N'-Bis(1-n-hexylpyridinium-4-ylmethyl)-1,4,5,8-naphthalenetetracarboxydiimide (PN6). PN6 exhibits a large band gap (2.95 eV), high electron mobility   $  (7.1 \times 10^{-5} \text{ cm}^{2}/\text{Vs})  $  , deep LUMO energy level (-4.07 eV) and can easily form highly transparent thin films. By introducing it as cathode interface layer in PHJ PSCs, PN6 can effectively decrease the work function of the cathode, boosting all the photovoltaic parameters of the fabricated devices. By using PN6 in ITO/NiO  $  _{x}  $  /Perovskite/PC61BM/PN6/Ag device structure, a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 17.27 % is obtained. Moreover, it is observed that the performance of PHJ PSCs with PN6 interface layer is insensitive to the interlayer thickness and even at a thickness of 30 nm; the PCE of the device can still reach up to 15.37 %, which makes it compatible with large-area roll-to-roll processing technology. These results demonstrate that the naphthalene diimide functionalized pyridinium salt can be a new category of water/alcohol soluble cathode interface material for high performance PHJ PSCs.

# Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells have attracted tremendous attention in the past seven years primarily due to their potential to cost-effectively generate renewable energy  $  ^{1-5}  $  . The organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials can form nearly defect free crystalline thin films which preserve broad optical absorbance  $  ^{6}  $  , low exciton binding energy  $  ^{7}  $   and long range charge transport  $  ^{6,8-10}  $  . Those unique properties of the perovskite semiconductor materials helped to improve the photovoltaic efficiency of the PSCs from the initial 3.8%  $  ^{11}  $   to the record 22.1%  $  ^{12}  $  .

The perovskite semiconductors can be adopted in several types of solar cell architectures which include perovskite-sensitized solar cells  $  ^{11}  $  , meso-superstructured solar cells  $  ^{13}  $   and p-i-n type planar heterojunction (PHJ) solar cells  $  ^{14}  $  . Despite the lower performance of PHJ PSCs than those based on other architectures, they are still considered to gain the most promising commercialization due to their relatively simple device architecture and potential for being fabricated by roll-to-roll processing at low-temperature

with low cost  $  ^{15}  $  . The commonly used architecture for PHJ PSCs is ITO/PEDOT: PSS or   $  NiO_{x}/perovskite  $   layer/PC61BM/cathode   $  {}^{16,17}  $  . For this commonly used PHJ PSCs architecture, there is a contact barrier between high work function metal and PC61BM  $  ^{18,19}  $  , which leads to poor electron extraction and deteriorate the performance of the PHJ PSCs  $  ^{19}  $  . Therefore, this issue needs to be addressed while fabricating PHJ PSCs. Several classes of materials have been used as cathode interlayers to enhance electron extraction properties of PHJ PSCs, including metal salts such as   $  LiF^{20}  $  , n-type metal oxide semiconductors such as   $  ZnO^{19,21}  $  ,   $  TiO_{x}^{2,22}  $  ,   $  SnO^{23}  $   etc. and n-type organic small molecular semiconductors such as   $  BCP^{14,24}  $  ,   $  C_{60}^{25}  $  ,   $  Bphen^{26}  $  ,   $  PCBC^{27}  $   etc. In addition to those materials, some of the water/alcohol-soluble organic materials have been successfully used as cathode interlayer due to their effective cathode modification properties and environmentally friendly solution processing. Water/alcohol-soluble property of organic interface materials is important for future industrial applications and can offer orthogonal solvent processability to prevent the solvent erosion problem during multilayer device fabrication  $  ^{28}  $  . Generally, to achieve these goals, specific functional group (ammonium  $  ^{29}  $  , ethylene oxide  $  ^{30}  $  , pyridinium  $  ^{31}  $  , phosphonate  $  ^{32}  $  , etc.) is proposed. Despite the successful application of water/alcohol soluble organic materials as cathode interlayer materials, they are generally very thickness-sensitive (usually less than 10 nm). For example, the optimized thickness of the PFN  $  ^{33}  $  , PN4N  $  ^{28}  $   and   $  C_{60}  $  -bis surfactant  $  ^{34}  $   is about 5 nm. The thickness-sensitive problem excludes their compatibility with large-area roll-to-roll processing technology, which is required for low-cost PHJ perovskite solar cells. Perylene diimides as the core with amino N-oxide as the terminal substituent (PDINO)  $  ^{35,36}  $  , and water/alcohol-soluble conjugated polymer PNDIT-F3N-Br  $  ^{37}  $   are the few thickness insensitive cathode interlayer materials that have been reported in fabricating PHJ PSCs. Nevertheless, the previous one has absorption between 400 nm to 650 nm  $  ^{38}  $  , which is a major drawback as for interface material, in order to provide best light harvesting conditions for the active layer, avoiding light absorption in the visible region is needed. Although some papers have reported water/alcohol-soluble small molecular interface materials based on pyridinium salt in organic solar cells  $  ^{31, 42}  $  , there are no reported thickness insensitive cathode interface small molecular materials based on pyridine functionalized group in PHJ PSCs and access to conjugated high electron mobility interface materials based on pyridinium salt remains a challenge.

Herein, we design and synthesize a novel n-type wide band gap water/alcohol-soluble small molecule N,N'-Bis(1-n-hexylpyridin-1-ium-4-ylmethyl)-1,4,5,8-naphthalenetetracarboxydiimide (PN6). Most importantly, we found that PN6 is not only an excellent cathode interface layer but also a good electron transporting material. Moreover, the performance of PHJ PSCs with PN6 interface layer is insensitive to the interlayer thickness, as the PCEs of the PHJ PSCs still could be reached up to 15.37%, even when the thickness of the PN6 was 30 nm.

# Experimental Section

# Materials:

PC61BM was purchased from Solenne BV. Dimethyl sulfoxide(DMSO),   $  \gamma  $  -butyrolactone, CsI, Formamidine Hydroiodide (FAI) and were purchased from TCI and used as received. Lead(II) iodide(PbI  $  _{2}  $  ), Lead(II) chloride(PbCl  $  _{2}  $  ), NiNO  $  _{3}  $  , 6H2O, hydroiodic acid(99.99%) and methylamine were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used as received Methlammonium iodide (MAI) was synthesized in our labs according to the literature  $  ^{46}  $  .

# Solar cell fabrication and testing

Indium tin oxide (ITO) was used as the substrate and was cleaned before oxygen-plasma treatment for 15 min. Afterward, a   $  NiO_{x}  $   layer was spin-coated onto the ITO substrate according to the published procedure  $  ^{47}  $  . To prepare the perovskite precursor solution, CsI, FAI, MAI with   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  PbCl_{2}  $   at a molar ratio of 0.1:0.1:0.8:0.9:0.1 were dissolved in   $  \gamma  $  -butyrolactone and DMSO with a volume ratio of 7:3 and stirred at 60 °C for 12 hours. Then, the

perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated onto the ITO/NiO  $  _{x}  $  substrate at 1000 rpm for 20 s and 5000 rpm for 50s and washed with toluene after 20 s at 5000 rpm during the second spin-coated step. The substrates were thermally annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. PC61BM layer (PC61BM, 10mg/ml in chloroform) was spin-coated onto the perovskite layer at 2000 rpm for 30 s. The PN6 thin films of 7 nm to 30 nm were deposited on to the PC61BM layer by spin-coating from 2 mg/ml to 12 mg/ml methanol solutions. For the film thicker than 20 nm, the thickness of PN6 was determined by the surface profiler, while the thickness of the film less than 20 nm was determined by the AFM. Finally, a 90 nm Ag film was evaporated through a shadow mask in a vacuum chamber under a pressure of  $  1\times10^{-4}  $  pa. The active area of the device was 4.5 mm  $  ^{2}  $ . The J-V characteristics were measured by a Keithley 2400 source-measurement unit under AM 1.5G (100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $ ) spectrum from a solar simulator (Newport, Oriel AM 1.5G, 100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $ ). The light intensity was calibrated using an NREL-certified monocrystalline Si diode coupled to a KG3 filter. The incident photon-to-current efficiency spectra were measured by a solar cell photodetector responsivity measurement system (Enlitech, Inc.).

# Results and Discussion

PN6 was synthesized by quaternization reaction processed in a nitrogen protected condition  $  ^{48}  $  . The synthesis procedure and details can be found in the Supporting Information. The normalized UV-vis absorption spectra of dilute PN6 solution (  $  \sim10^{-5}  $   M) in methanol are displayed in Figure 2b, together with the spectrum of solid thin films deposited by vacuum evaporation on a quartz plate. As shown in Figure 2b, PN6 shows an absorption band in solution (320 nm - 390 nm) due to   $  \pi-\pi^{*}  $   transition of the conjugated backbone. In contrast, the solid thin film shows obvious red-shift in comparison with PN6 in diluted solution, suggesting more ordered structure in the solid state. The absorption band of PN6 solid state is 320 nm - 420 nm, so the optical bandgap is 2.95 eV as calculated from the onset of the absorption. The wide bandgap of PN6 favors the absorption of visible light by perovskite layer. As shown in Figure 2 c, the absorption properties of ITO/NiO  $  _{x}  $  /Perovskite/PC61BM/PN6 (30 nm) are almost the same with the pristine one. The cyclic voltammogram (CV) of PN6 is displayed in Figure 2a. The LUMO of PN6 is -4.07 eV after calibration using ferrocene as reference, which is close to the LUMO level of the PC61BM (-4.2 eV) and thus makes it beneficial for electron transport from PC61BM to the PN6, and then to Ag. The HOMO of the PN6 is estimated to be -7.02 eV. In order to investigate the electron transporting properties of the PN6, electron-only devices were fabricated. The electron mobility was measured by using SCLC method  $  ^{49}  $  . Figure 2d shows the J-V characteristics of electron-only device based on the architecture of ITO/ZnO/PN6/Ag. The electron mobility of the PN6 film is found to be   $  7.1\times10^{-3}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $  /V.s.

The film morphology of the PN6 atop the PC61BM layer was studied by atomic force microscopy (AFM). As shown in Figure 3a, the perovskite/PC61BM thin films show very rough surface morphology with  $ 5.74\mathrm{nm} $  root-mean-square (RMS) roughness. Therefore, in order to uniformly cover the PC61BM layer, a thin interface layer at least  $ 6\mathrm{nm} $  is needed. After depositing  $ 7\mathrm{nm} $  PN6 onto the PC61BM, the surface becomes much smoother with  $ 3.39\mathrm{nm} $  RMS roughness (Figure 3b), suggesting that the surface of the PC61BM film is uniformly and fully covered by PN6 layer.

To investigate the effect of PN6 interface layer on the PHJ PSCs performance, Perovskite solar cells with PHJ architecture of ITO/NiO $ _{x} $ /Perovskite/PC61BM/PN6/Ag (Figure 1) were fabricated and the detailed fabrication process is provided in experimental section. Here, NiO $ _{x} $  was used as hole transporting layer (HTL) instead of conventional HTL PEDOT:PSS due to its better energy level alignment with perovskite $ ^{16,47} $ . The perovskite thin film was prepared through the solvent-engineering technology which is reported by Seok's group $ ^{50} $ . The morphology of the deposited perovskite layer on the NiO $ _{x} $  is shown in Figure S1(SEM image), which reveals a very uniform and dense perovskite layer with a grain size of 300 nm - 600 nm. For comparison, the control device without PN6 was also fabricated and tested. The current density-voltage (J-V) curves of the devices under 100 mW/cm $ ^{2} $  AM 1.5G illumination are shown in Figure 3a and corresponding performance of the devices is summarized in Table 1.

For control device without PN6, the maximum PCE obtained is 8.73 % with open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.08 V, short circuit current (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 18.36 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and fill factor (FF) of 43.96 %. Note that control device shows an S-shaped J-V curve in Figure 4a, which has been reported by many groups when they fabricated PHJ PSCs based on PC61BM/Ag architecture  $  ^{18, 35}  $  . Usually, S-shaped J-V curves result from carrier accumulation inside the devices  $  ^{51}  $  . In comparison, the device with PN6 as cathode interface layer shows significant improvement of device parameters, showing the maximum PCE of 17.27 % with higher   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.11 V, enhanced   $  J_{sc}  $   of 20.53 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and increased FF of 76.07 %. Meanwhile, the series resistance decreases from 340.9   $  \Omega  $  cm  $  ^{2}  $   to 4.0   $  \Omega  $  cm  $  ^{2}  $  . The decrease of the series resistance and the improvement of the   $  J_{sc}  $ , FF indicate enhanced carrier extraction from PC61BM to Ag cathode layer after inserting PN6 interface layer between the PC61BM and Ag cathode. Additionally, devices with PN6 show negligible J-V hysteresis as shown in Figure 4b. The enhancement in   $  J_{sc}  $   after inserting PN6 is consistent with incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency spectrum shown in Figure 4c, the calculated   $  J_{sc}  $   value of the control device is 18.43 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $ , which is in good agreement with the measured   $  J_{sc}  $   value of 18.36 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   in Figure 4a. As revealed by Figure 4c, the EQE values in the entire photo response range between 300 nm and 750 nm are significantly enhanced after inserting PN6. As a result, the calculated   $  J_{sc}  $   is 19.47 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $ , in good agreement with the measured   $  J_{sc}  $   value of 20.53 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $ .

To evaluate power output reliability of the fabricated PHJ PSCs with PN6, a stabilized power output was measured under a constant voltage bias of 0.92 V near the maximum power output point and is shown in Figure 4d. The device with PN6 showed very stable power output and a steady-state PCE of  $ 17.27\% $  is obtained. These results demonstrate that PN6 is a promising cathode interface layer for PHJ PSCs.

To understand why the PHJ PSCs with PN6 exhibit better device performance than the control devices and to get insight into the electronic properties of PN6 interface layer, ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS)  $  ^{52}  $  was applied. UPS was used to probe the work functions of pure Ag and PN6 modified Ag. Figure 5 displays the results of UPS measurements for bare Ag and PN6/Ag. For bare Ag, the work function is 4.7 eV, while after spin coating a thin layer (7 nm) of PN6 on the Ag electrode, the work function of Ag is decreased to 4.05 eV. Moreover, the UPS results indicate that the work function of PN6/Ag remains almost constant with different thicknesses of PN6 as shown in the Supporting Information Figure S3. The

As we discussed earlier, in roll-to-roll processing the main disadvantage of the application of interface layer is that the performance of the devices is highly sensitive to the thickness of the interface layer. Generally, increasing the thickness of the interface layer will inevitably increase the bulk resistance of the interface layer and adversely affect the device performance  $  ^{53}  $  . Providing the relative high electron mobility of PN6, we further investigated the dependence of the PHJ PSCs performance by varying the thickness of PN6 interface layer from 7 nm to 30 nm using different concentrations of the PN6 solution. The performance of the PHJ PSCs with different interface layer thickness is summarized in Table 2 and the corresponding J-V curves were shown in Figure 6a.

As shown in Table 2 and Figure 6a, the performance of devices is not much sensitive to the thickness of PN6. The optimized PN6 thickness of 7 nm yields a device with a PCE of 17.27 %. When the PN6 thickness is increased to 30 nm, the device shows some drop in   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF, with the PCE of 15.37 %, which is still much higher than that of Ag only device. In contrast, the device (Supporting Information Table S1 Figure S2) with 20 nm thickness of PFN as interface layer shows PCE of only 1.47 %. Figure 6b shows the EQE spectra of PHJ PSCs with different PN6 interface layer thicknesses to verify the differences in   $  J_{sc}  $  . Interestingly, all devices showed almost the same EQE response from 300 nm to 600 nm, but lower EQE response for devices with thicker PN6 from 600 nm to 800 nm. Thus, the lower EQE response leads to drop in   $  J_{sc}  $   for devices with thicker PN6 due to the higher recombination possibility in the long wavelength region  $  ^{54}  $  .

As we have shown before, the electron mobility of the device with PN6 interface layer is   $  7.6 \times 10^{-5}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $  /V.s, which is ten times higher than that of the devices with evaporated interface material BCP (  $  5.5 \times 10^{-6}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $  /V.s)  $  ^{65}  $  , meanwhile the LUMO level of PN6 (-4.06 eV) is much close to the LUMO level of PC61BM (-4.2 eV), which facilitates electron transfer through PN6 to the cathode. For some thickness insensitive interface materials, the electron transport properties can be enhanced by self-doping effect. To check whether PN6 possesses this self-doping property, electron spin resonance spectroscopy (ESR) was carried out. As shown in FigureS4, a weak ESR signal is observed for PN6, indicating the effect of self-doping in PN6. Thus the EPS result provides direct evidence of self-doping in PN6, which leads to enhanced electron transport properties of PN6.

Ambient stability of PSCs is very important for their practical application. To investigate the ambient stability of devices with and without PN6 interface layer, the normalized PCE, as a function of time, was recorded (Figure 7). The PHJ PSCs were un-encapsulated and stored in ambient atmosphere with humidity of  $ 40\% $ . As shown in Figure 7, compared with the device without PN6 interlayer, the stability of the device with PN6 interlayer is obviously improved. For the device with PN6 interlayer, the PCE can maintain  $ 90\% $  of its initial value after storing for about  $ 200\mathrm{h} $ . In contrast, PCE of the device without PN6 interlayer drops to  $ 63\% $  of its initial value after storing for the same time. These results demonstrate that PN6 interlayer can block the penetration of moisture to some extent, which is beneficial for improving the device stability.

# Conclusions

In summary, we have developed a new pyridine-functionalized NDI core small molecule as an efficient interface layer to enhance the performance of PHJ PSCs. Thus, the specific properties and very simple synthesis processes of the PN6 make it potential candidate as cathode interface layer for solution processed PHJ PSCs and can be applied in a roll-to-roll process.
