# ABSTRACT

Self-assembly of reduced graphene oxide, zinc [5,10,15,20-tetrakis(2,6-dichlorophenyl)porphyrin] complex and CuO nanoparticles was performed through  $ \pi-\pi $  stacking and hydrogen bonding using Calotropis procera plant to have a bio-nanocomposite (ZnPor/rGO/CuO). This green organic/inorganic ternary hybrid was constructed using bottom-up approach to have multiple electron transfer and characterized by SEM, TEM, XRD, EDX, elemental analysis, UV-Vis spectroscopy, and Cyclic voltammetry (CV). The bio-nanocomposite (ZnPor/rGO/CuO) possesses suitable energy level, high hole mobility, and excellent thermal stability. The obtained bio-nanocomposite was used as inorganic hole-transporting material (HTM) in carbon-based Perovskite Solar Cells (C-PSC). 9.8 is the best powder conversion efficiency of system in comparison with Spiro-OMeTAD as a commercial HTM. Its PCE is about  $ 59\% $  more than of PCE of control device (HTM free). This significant increase in PCE was due to the higher hole-extraction rate at carbon counter-electrode, in the presence of bio nanocomposite as a HTM confirming by Mott-Schottky analysis and the electrical conductivity.

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs), the third generation of solar cells, are practical and high-efficiency   $  [6–8]  $  . In commercial market, organic components such as spiro-OMeTAD, PTAA, and PCBM were used for hole or electron transport   $  [9]  $  . However, these organic chemicals have critical drawbacks such hard and expensive synthesis, poor crystallinity, possible, low mobility, and degradation under environmental influences. Therefore, replacing them with nanocomposites preparing natural and cheap can attract so much attentions   $  [10]  $  . Phthalocyanines and porphyrins could be successfully incorporated as the hole-transporting materials (HTM), a modifying layer for interfacial engineering, and additives to enhance the crystallinity of the perovskite layer in PSCs   $  [11–14]  $  . Graphene oxide due to its topping mobility, absorbance, conductivity, mechanical flexibility, transparency, and specific surface area has attracted numerous researchers in the field of in solar cells and energy storage   $  [15]  $  . Carbon materials can solve the problem of the expensive and non-stability of the commonly used organic hole transport materials limiting the commercialization of PSCs   $  [16,17]  $  . On the other hand, transition metal oxides are also good candidates for HTMs due to the appropriate optical transmittance, excellent stability, and hole mobility. Among them, CuO as a p-type semiconductor has the valence band of 5.4 eV matching well with   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   and it could be an ideal HTM for PSCs   $  [17,18]  $  .

Here, a bio-nanocomposite including CuO, graphene oxide, and porphyrins utilizing natural plants was synthesized through self-assembly approach. It was used as the first bio-nanocomposite HTMs for PSCs to have a cost effective, environmentally friendly, and economical PSCs.

# 3. Experimental

# 3.1. Synthesis of 5,10,15,20-tetrakis(2,6-dichlorophenyl)porphyrin and its zinc complex.

50 mL of pyrrole 0.1 M and 50 ml 2,5 dichlorobenzaldehyde were added to 400 mL of dried dichloromethane for 10 min under   $  N_{2}  $  . After that, 0.2 mL   $  BF_{3}.OEt_{3}  $   was added and stirred at room temperature. Purple color was obtained when 0.99 g (3.7 mmol) of p-chloranil was added and refluxed within 1 h and Por ligand was gained. 0.025 g of ligand in 3.5 mL chloroform was added to saturated zinc acetate dihydrate salt in 1 mL of methanol and refluxed for 12 h. After concentrating, the residue was soxhleted by chloroform and methanol, respectively.

# 3.2. Synthesis of graphene oxide

Synthesis based on the modified Hummers' method was done. Briefly, 5 g of graphite powder were mechanical dispersed in 200 mL   $  H_{2}SO_{4}  $   (95–97%) in a 1000 mL volumetric flask and then 5 g   $  NaNO_{3}  $   was added to this solution and kept under an ice bath (0–10 °C) for 3 h with continuous stirring.

Next, 15 g KMnO  $  _{4}  $  was slowly added to keep the reaction temperature lower than 10 °C and stirred for 2 h. The ice bath was then removed and the reaction mixture was transferred to a glass reactor followed by slow addition of 900 mL distilled water.

The reaction was heated to  $ 50^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 12\mathrm{h} $ . Then,  $ 5\mathrm{mL} $   $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2 $  (30%) was added and the resulting brown precipitate was washed with  $ 200~\mathrm{mL} $  HCl (10%) and distilled water several times and freezes dried at  $ -50^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 24\mathrm{h} $ .

# 3.3. Green synthesis of CuO nanoparticles

2 g of the C. procera(CP) in 40 mL distilled water was boiled for 10 min. Then, KOH (0.01 M) was added dropwise until pH = 9.   $  \mathrm{Cu(NO_{3})_{2}}  $  .   $  3\mathrm{H_{2}O}  $   (0.1 mM) was added to 40 mL the mixture of CP/water (1/1 ratio). After 10 h, the black resultant was heated at 800 °C for 3 h to have green copper oxide nanoparticles.

# 3.4. Procedure of ZnPor/rGO/CuO bio nanocomposite using C. procera plant

0.01 g of GO nanosheet in 20 mL CP solution and 0.01 g of CuO nanoparticles in 20 mL CP solution was separately dispersed for 30 min and mixed and stirred for 1 h. Then, 200   $  \mu  $  L of chloroform solution of ZnPor (1 mM) was injected in it under vigorous stirring for 10 h. The obtained nanobiocomposite was dried at 70 °C.

# 4.1. Electrochemical measurements

Electrochemical redox potentials for ZnPor/rGO/CuO bio nanocomposite (0.01 g/l) were obtained using CV having a three-electrode cells in dry acetonitrile containing KCl (0.1 M) as supporting electrolyte with 0.1 V/s scan rate (Fig. 8). Pt sheet, carbon and Ag/AgCl electrodes were utilized as counter, working and reference electrodes, respectively. It should be noted that the energy levels of Ag/AgCl in saturated KCl is 4.7 eV; the HOMO levels were estimated from the CV data, HOMO = -(Eoxd + 4.725) eV, where Eoxd is the onset potential of oxidation waves [30]. Holes transferred from the perovskite are achieved using HOMO levels of the HTM estimating values of -5.25 eV from the first oxidation peaks in the CV curves (Fig. 9). On the other hand, the mix-ion perovskite valence band (CH₃NH₃PbI₃ = MAPbI₃) is -5.43 eV and obtained results were shown that the holes could transport from the perovskite into nanocomposite very well.

The type of semiconductors made was obtained by electrochemical impedance studies and determination of capacitance at different electrical potentials by Matte-Schottky method. First, impedance measurement was performed at different bias potentials and then Mott–Schottky (MS) analysis (  $  (1/C^{2}  $   versus voltage)) was considered to calculate the density of charge carriers (concentration of charge carriers) and the flat band potential (  $  V_{FB}  $  ) according to M–S equation [31,32]:

$$

Self-assembled of ZnPor/rGO/CuO bio nanocomposite was fabricated using C. procera plantthroughbottom-up manner.
