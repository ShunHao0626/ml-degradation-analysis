ABSTRACT: Fullerene derivatives, especially [6,6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM), have been widely applied as electron transport layers of inverted planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, the solution-processed PCBM capping layer suffers from limited surface wetting which hinders the improvement in efficiency and scalability of PSCs. Herein, we develop a facile hybrid solvent strategy that enables very fast wetting of PCBM capping layer atop of the perovskite surface, leading to improved interfacial contact and electron transport. The significantly enhanced wettability of the PCBM solution fulfilled through blending isopropyl alcohol (IPA) into the commonly used chlorobenzene (CB) is attributed to the reduced surface tension while remaining viscosity. As a result, the electron mobility and electric conductivity of the PCBM capping layer increase by around two times, and the PSC devices exhibit the highest power-conversion-efficiency (PCE) of  $ 19.92\% $ , which is improved by  $ \sim 18\% $  relative to that of the control device ( $ 16.78\% $ ). Importantly, this strategy is also applicable for other alcohols (ethanol and methanol) and CB blends. Moreover, the fast wetting approach enables us to deposit the PCBM capping layer using a facile drop-casting method, affording comparable PCEs to those obtained by the conventional spin-coating method, which is not achievable by using the conventional single solvent. This fast wetting PCBM capping layer also contributes to efficiency improvement of large-area ( $ 1\mathrm{cm}^2 $ ) devices. These advances hold great potential for other scalable deposition methods such as blade-coating and slot-die coating.

# 1. Introduction

Metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have gained wide attention due to the excellent photovoltaic properties of perovskite light absorber materials, including large absorption coefficients, small exciton binding energies, high carrier mobilities, tunable bandgaps, and long carrier recombination lifetime.  $  ^{1-3}  $   During the past few years, vigorous efforts on optimizing the composition and morphology of the perovskite absorber layer, as well as engineering the device structure and interfaces have pushed the power conversion efficiency (PCE) exceeding 25%.  $  ^{4-10}  $

In planar heterojunction structure devices, the perovskite light absorber layer such as   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   is sandwiched between the hole transport layer (HTL) and electron transport layer (ETL). In particular, the inverted (p-i-n) structure with HTL deposited on the bottom transparent electrode and ETL located on the perovskite film is advantageous in terms of the low-temperature solution process compatible with flexible substrates and negligible hysteresis of current-voltage response.  $  ^{6,11-13}  $   Carbon materials, including low-dimensional nanocarbons and three-dimensional carbon blacks, have been applied as charge transport layers of PSC and shown broad prospect of enhancing both efficiency and stability of devices.  $  ^{14-15}  $   In particular, for the state-of-the-art inverted PSCs, fullerene derivatives especially phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) as the representative zero-dimensional nanocarbons are the most commonly used ETLs because of their advantages such as strong electron-accepting ability and high solubility in nonpolar solvents (toluene, chlorobenzene, ortho-dichlorobenzene).  $  ^{16-18}  $   Besides, PCBM can passivate perovskite for suppressing hysteresis and improving device stability by blocking the interdiffusion of metal and halide anions.  $  ^{18-20}  $   However, due to the limited surface wetting, the solution-processed PCBM capping layer generally exhibits unideal morphology and electronic properties, leading to shunting pathways and charge recombination at the PCBM/cathode interface.  $  ^{5}  $   To address this problem,

Noteworthy, these studies focus on using other functional molecules to modulate the electron transport property of PCBM layer, thus the effectiveness of such additives depend sensitively on the molecular structures of the additives.  $  ^{44}  $  Besides, some of these additives require complex synthesis procedures, and this is unfavorable for low-cost device fabrication. Hence, it is highly desired to develop simpler additives such as solvents capable of improving the surface wetting of PCBM layer.

Chlorobenzene (CB) is the most commonly used solvent for fabricating PCBM ETL with high solubility and orthogonal to the polar solvents used for fabricating the perovskite layer under PCBM layer. An intriguing question is whether blending another solvent additive with different polarity into CB would improve the surface wetting of PCBM layer. Herein, we develop a facile hybrid solvent strategy by blending isopropyl alcohol (IPA) into CB, leading to very fast wetting of PCBM layer and consequently improved interfacial contact and electron transport of PSC devices. IPA is a poor solvent for PCBM but it can significantly improve the surface wetting of PCBM layer through reducing surface tension while remaining viscosity, with negligible influence on the bottom perovskite layer. As a result, the PCBM ETL using IPA:CB hybrid solvent exhibited simultaneous improvements in morphology and electron transport properties, leading to dramatic efficiency enhancement of the inverted PSC devices. # 2. Results and Discussion

IPA is blended with CB with different volume ratios (0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.25, abbreviated as xIPA:CB, where x represents the volume ratio of IPA:CB). Figure 1 illustrates the intriguingly fast wetting of IPA:CB hybrid solvent on  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite film. Both IPA and CB exhibited slow spreadability, and the contact angle remained  $ >10^{\circ} $  within 1s after the drop touched the perovskite film spin-coated on FTO (Figure 1a,b and Supporting Information Figure S1a,b). However, the contact angle of 0.15IPA:CB hybrid solvent become very small within 0.2s due to its fast wetting on perovskite film (Figure 1c and Supporting Information Figure S1c). After optimization, we find that, when the IPA:CB volume ratio  $ >0.10 $ ,  $ 2\mu \mathrm{L} $  of IPA:CB hybrid solvent can cover the entire substrate ( $ 1.5\times 1.5~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ ) within 2s. However, for pure CB or IPA, more than  $ 30~\mu \mathrm{L} $  solvent is required to cover the same substrate. Hence, the consumption of solvent (and fullerene material) is reduced by a factor of  $ >15 $ . More details of wetting process can be found in the Supporting Information videos, where  $ 2\mu \mathrm{L} $  of 0.15IPA:CB hybrid solvent, pure CB, and pure IPA were dropped onto perovskite films.

To understand the origin of the improved wettability upon IPA adding, we measured the surface tension of different solvents. The measured surface tensions  $ (\gamma) $  of pure CB and IPA in an ambient environment (temperature:  $ 20^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) were  $ 33.75\mathrm{mN / m} $  and  $ 21.00\mathrm{mN / m} $ , respectively (Figure 1d, see also Supporting Information Table S1). When IPA was introduced into CB, the surface tension of the IPA:CB hybrid solvent ( $ \gamma_{\mathrm{IPA:CB}} $ ) decreased significantly relative to that of pure CB ( $ \gamma_{\mathrm{CB}} $ ). Owing to the remarkably improved wetting of the IPA:CB hybrid solvent, PCBM solution in IPA:CB hybrid solvent also exhibited significantly improved wetting compared with the control sample dissolved in pure CB (Figure 2a,b). This is confirmed further by investigating the evolution of contact angles of PCBM solutions on the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film. The contact angle of the PCBM solution in pure CB decreased from  $ 19.5^{\circ} $  to  $ 2.6^{\circ} $  within 2.7 s (Figure 2c). However, when PCBM was dissolved in the 0.15IPA:CB hybrid solvent, the PCBM solution dropped onto the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film spread immediately within 0.2 s and the contact angle was too small to be measured accurately (Figure 2c). This result confirms the fast wetting of PCBM layer induced by IPA addition.

We then evaluated the effect of IPA addition on the interfacial charge transport dynamics by measuring the steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) spectra. According to the comparison of the steady-state PL spectra, after PCBM layer capping, the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite film exhibited a 75.6 % PL quenching. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, we developed a facile hybrid solvent strategy by blending IPA into CB, leading to very fast wetting of PCBM layer through reducing surface tension while remaining viscosity. The resulting PCBM capping layer on  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite was featured with less microscale aggregation, increased electric conductivity, improved electron mobility, and facilitated interfacial electron transport. These factors directly led to  $ \mathrm{J_{sc}} $  and FF improvements in solar cells, consequently the highest PCE was dramatically improved from  $ 16.78\% $  to  $ 19.92\% $ . Such an efficiency enhancement was also verified by using other alcohol solvents and replacing PCBM with another fullerene derivative, confirming the universality of the hybrid solvent strategy. The improved morphology and smoothness of the PCBM capping layer further enabled its fabrication using a facile drop-casting method as well as efficiency improvement of large-area ( $ 1\mathrm{cm}^2 $ ) devices. The hybrid solvent strategy is facile, effective in improving efficiency and stability of PSC device, and universal for other alcohols and fullerene derivative, promising its potential for other scalable deposition methods such as blade-coating and slot-die coating.

# Device Fabrication.

The patterned FTO glass was cleaned by sequential ultrasonic treatment in deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol and then dried at 60°C. The FTO substrate was then treated in an ultraviolet–ozone chamber for 20 min. A NiO $  _{x}  $  compact layer was spin-coated on the FTO at 6000 rpm for 30 s and then heated at 280°C for 40 min. (The method to prepare NiO $  _{x}  $  precursor was the same as previously reported: 0.1 mmol Ni(Ac) $  _{2}  $ ·4H $  _{2}  $ O was dissolved in 10 mL of ethanol and 60 μL of ethanolamine, stirring for 4 hours at 70°C before use). Next, the perovskite precursor solution (CH $  _{3}  $ NH $  _{3}  $ I and lead (II) iodide (PbI $  _{2}  $ ) with a molar ratio of 1.1:1 were dissolved in DMF and DMSO with a volume ratio of 7:3) was then spin-coated onto FTO/NiO $  _{x}  $  substrate at 4000 rpm for 40 s. During the last 20 s of the spin-coating step, CB (80 μL) was dropped on the spinning substrate. Then the as-spun films were transferred on a hotplate annealed at 100°C for 10 min. After cooling down to room temperature, PCBM solutions with different IPA:CB solvent ratios (0-0.25) were dropped on the MAPbI $  _{3}  $  film and spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 30 s to obtained different PCBM ETLs. Then a cathode buffer layer bathocuproine (BCP) was spin-coated on PCBM ETL with a solution of 0.5 mg/ml in IPA. Finally, the devices were finished by thermally evaporating silver (Ag 100 nm) in a vacuum under a base pressure of about 10 $  ^{-5}  $  Torr. The device working area was 0.1 cm $  ^{2}  $  and 1 cm $  ^{2}  $  for small and large area devices, respectively.
