# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have gained excellent research interest recently thanks to their improvement in the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.0% [1]. Perovskite (PVK) refers to a class of oxides with the chemical formula of ABX $ _3 $ , where A is a sign for the organics (NH $ _2 $ CH=NH $ _2 $ +{FA} $ ^+ $ ), CH $ _3 $ CH $ _2 $ NH $ _3 $ +{MA+}), B is a sign for the metallic (Pb $ ^{2+} $ , Sn $ ^{2+} $ ), and X symbol for halides (Cl $ ^- $ , Br $ ^- $ , or I $ ^- $ ) [2]. These semiconducting materials have unique properties, such as adjustable bandgap, high absorption coefficient, long charge diffusion length, and high charge carrier mobility [3–5]. Many methods have been used to acquire a high-quality PVK crystalline layer with less unwanted pinholes and impurities [6]. Deposition of the PVK layer is accomplished using one or a two-step solution deposition process, chemical vapor deposition, evaporation technique, flash infrared

Hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have introduced as a good revolution photovoltaic to meet the green world energy demands. The crystallinity improvement of PSCs is a practical approach to improve the efficacy of PSCs. Double cation formamidinium (FA)/methylammonium (MA) PSCs were fabricated by using a simple sequential deposition process. The mesoporous titanium dioxide   $  \left(\mathrm{TiO}_{2}\right)  $   layers with 200 nm thickness were used as the electron transport layer (ETL). It was observed that with changing the FA/MA precursor temperature could achieve more desirable perovskite films for photovoltaic applications. Our measurements show that with pre-heating of FA/MA solution at 55 °C the defects on the perovskite layer are well passivated. Besides, the perovskite with better crystallinity and smooth morphology, and the defect-free surface was achieved. It is delightful that perovskite with 55 °C reveals stronger photon absorption and better charge collection. Using the pre-heating approach, a redshift in edge absorption of the perovskite films was observed, indicating a better light-harvesting phenomenon in the corresponding perovskite films. Through optimization of the pre-heating temperature of FA/MA solution, a champion PCE of 13.77% was yielded for PSCs higher than of 10.05% one for pre-heating. Also, modified mixed cation devices revealed higher stability behavior in atmospheric conditions than the reference devices.

Alternatively, the mixed cation/halide kind perovskite is more durable against exposure to harsh conditions than a conventional   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   [18]. PSCs with HTLs based on ambient-fabrication double cation PVK outside the glove box have been successfully prepared [19–21]. Among all n-type semiconductors,   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticles (NPs) are widely employed as an ETL to provide useful charge collection and play a vital role in transporting the photo-generated electrons to the FTO substrate [23]. However,   $  TiO_{2}  $   films suffer from high charge recombination and poor stability for corresponding single cation   $  MAPI_{3}  $   based PSCs [24–26]. Recently, to address this issue and further performance improvement of PSCs, researchers have used different materials into the   $  TiO_{2}  $   films to retard the charge recombination and increase the stability of PSCs [27–30]. Besides, using PVKs with mixed cations and halides has emerged as an effective strategy to stabilize PSCs.

In this work, the impact of FM/AM precursor temperature on the performance of mixed cation PSCs was systematically examined, where all devices were fabricated under ambient air conditions. Results show at an optimized temperature, due to the passivation of the grain boundary, the recombination rate is reduced in the PSCs. As a result, the best performing device composed of mixed-cation PVK as an absorber layer yields a cumulative PCE of 13.77%, which is better than that of the control device (10.01%). Moreover, the stability of the optimized device is improved by retaining 80% of its initial PCE, while the PCE of control PSC completely drops after 400 h storage in ambient conditions (relative humidity   $  \sim  $   25%-40%).

# 2. Experimental part

# 2.1. Materials synthesis

MACl, MA bromide (MABr), and FM iodide (FAI) were synthesized as stated in the literature [6]. Briefly, MACl and MABr were synthesized employing a mixture of methylamine in ethanol (40%, Alfa Aesar) and hydriodic acid (HI, 67%, Alfa Aesar), hydrochloric acid (HCl, 41%, Alfa Aesar), and hydro-bromic acid (HBr, 35%, Sigma-Aldrich), respectively, then mixed at 0 °C for 2 h. A mixture of formamidine acetate (98%, Sigma-Aldrich) with a double molar of HI was used to prepare FAI. The final products were gained via a heating at 55 °C. The yellowish powders were rinsed successively with chlorobenzene (CB) and recrystallized by ethanol four times. Consequently, the white products of MABr, MACl,

and FAI were collected via drying in a furnace for 12 h.

# 2.2. Solution preparation

For preparing lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ , Merck) solution, 600 mg of PbI $ _2 $  and 15 mg of lead bromide (PbBr $ _2 $ , 99.9%, Alfa Aesar) were inserted into DMF:DMSO (19:1) solvent, followed by and stirring at 65 °C for 12 h. FAI/MABr/MACl precursor was synthesized as reported in reference [13], Briefly, 60 mg of FAI was poured into 1 ml of isopropanol (IPA, 99%, Merck) and 6 mg of each MACl and MABr were added to FAI/IPA solution. After that, FAI/MABr/MACl precursors were heated at various temperatures before deposition. For preparation HTL, the Spiro-OMeTAD solution was prepared by inserting 17.5 μl bis (trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (99.95%, Alfa Aesar) in ACN (99.9%, Alfa Aesar) (520 mg/ml), and 28.8 μl 4-tertbutylpyridine (Alfa Aesar, 97%) to 60 mM Spiro-OMeTAD in CB.

# 2.3. Solar cells fabrication

The cleaning fluorine-tin oxide (FTO, 15  $ \Omega $ /sq, Solaronix) glasses were done as a general method in the literature [2]. A compact TiO $ _{2} $  (c-TiO $ _{2} $ ) layer was spun on the FTO glass at 2000 rpm for 30 s employing a mixture of titanium (IV) isopropoxide (TTIP, 97%, Sigma-Aldrich) in ethanol and annealed at 500  $ ^{\circ} $ C for 25 min. The mp-TiO $ _{2} $  layer was deposited on FTO/c-TiO $ _{2} $  substrates employing a paste TiO $ _{2} $  in ethanol at 4000 rpm for 60 s, followed by sintering at 440  $ ^{\circ} $ C for 40 min. The PVK film was prepared with a two-step deposition process [6], as shown in Fig. 1a. In the first, the PbI $ _{2} $  solution was spin coated at 1500 rpm for 30 s and dried in room temperature for 1 min, followed by heating at same temperature of double cation solution. Then the pre-heated double cation solution at different temperatures were spin coated at 1300 rpm for 30 s on top of the heated PbI $ _{2} $  film with same temperature. The films were annealed at 150  $ ^{\circ} $ C for 15 min to complete perovskite layer formation. After that, the HTL mixture was spin-coated at 4000 rpm for 30 s on top of the perovskite layer to form the HTL layer. Finally, a 120 nm Au electrode was evaporated on the HTL (see Fig. 1b).

# 3. Results and discussion

As stated in the experimental part, the mixed cation perovskite solar cells were designed via the deposition of FA/MA on mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ETL through the sequential deposition process. A yellow transparent lead iodide layer was spin-coated from a  $  PbI_{2}/PbBr_{2}/DMSO/DMF  $  mixture onto the FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}/mp-TiO_{2}  $  photoelectrode. The FAI/MABr/MACl precursor in isopropanol solvent was deposited on the pre-deposited  $  PbI_{2}  $  layer to fabricate mixed FAMAPb(IBr)  $  _{3}  $  PVK films. This configuration has been engineered by changing the FA/MA precursor temperature to achieve more desirable PVK films. The precursor temperature of FA/MA in PSCs was controlled at RT, 40 °C, 55 °C, and 70 °C for preparing PVK films. To study the changes between the mixed cation PSCs prepared at different temperatures, morphological, crystallographic, and optical characterizations were conducted. Furthermore, the overall performance of the fabricated devices was evaluated through J-V measurements.

The optical absorbance of photoactive PVK films has been measured and compared through UV–vis spectra (Fig. 2), and their corresponding energy band-gap (  $  E_{g}  $  ) estimated using Tauc plot analysis [31–33]. As seen in Fig. 2a, by increasing the temperature beyond RT, absorption of the PVK film was improved. Besides, it can be seen that the absorption of PVK film deposited at 55 °C is stronger than that of other films in the range of 600–800 nm, more absorbing sunlight is favorable for high efficient PSCs. This may be due to the more significant light-harvesting or, the smaller light scattering of the PVK layer spin-coated at 55 °C.

Tauc plots (Fig. 2b) reveal a slight redshift in the value of   $  E_{g}  $   of PVK film from 1.61 eV to about 1.58 eV by increasing the temperature from RT to 55 °C. Accordingly, absorption edges of the PVK films change from 770 nm to 784 nm, giving rise to the short-circuit photocurrent density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of the corresponding PSCs. This redshift was also obtained in steady-state PL spectra of our devices (Fig. 3a), corroborating with the corresponding UV–vis spectra. However, increase in temperature above 55 °C lead to lower crystallinity of the PVK layer and hence an inferior absorption spectrum. The observed red-shift with increasing the FA/MA precursor temperature due to suppuration of   $  PbI_{2}  $   and δ-  $  FAPbI_{3}  $   peaks as previously were reported with Mohseni et al. [20] and Binek et al. [34] (Fig. 3b).

As shown in Fig. 3a, the highest PL emission peak is obtained in the PVK layer spin-coated at 55 °C, which implies that the non-radiative charge recombination in PVK is drastically reduced compared to other devices [35]. It shows that the PL peak of PVKs is centered at 750–765 nm, which is consistent with previous reports [2,3]. The optimized temperature of FA/MA facilitates charge injection processes in PVK film because of better crystallinity and suppressed grain boundaries. However, when the FA/MA temperature increases to 70 °C the PL intensity reduces again, which reduces the crystallinity and increases crystal defects [36]. These findings confirm the improved charge injection by the photoactive PVK film, leading to improved PSCs performance. Moreover, it is worth noting that the PVK at 55 °C shows a more intense PL peak than other films, signifying fewer defects, and better morphological property [37]. XRD was employed to elucidate the effect of FA/MA solution temperature on the crystalline properties of PVK films and their corresponding plots are demonstrated in Fig. 3b. As shown, the prominent diffraction peaks are located at 14.04°, 24.32°, and 28.15°, which are for the preferred (001), (111), and (002) crystal planes, respectively [13]. The surface morphologies of spin-coated mixed cation PVK films at the RT and 55 °C are shown in Fig. 4. These results agree well with the XRD pattern. Such high-crystalline PVK films acquired by increasing FA/MA temperature are beneficial for fully air-processed PSCs. It is well-known that humidity can permit grain boundaries to decompose the PVK layer. Therefore, magnifying the PVK crystal size is an efficient strategy to reduce the instability of an ambient-processed PVK [38].

In addition to high PCE, the stability of air-processed PSCs is essential to the commercial applications. Devices must be capable of with-standing in atmospheric conditions. Thus, the stability of the unencapsulated references and optimized devices was studied in ambient humidity (25–40%), and the normalized PCE versus time is depicted in Fig. 7. Not surprisingly, the optimized PSC showed more excellent air stability. The PCE of the 55 °C-based devices maintains 80% of the original PCE after 16 days (400 h), whereas, the reference devices completely degrade under same conditions. The higher crystallinity of the PVK (Fig. 3b) and larger particle size could be more efficiently hinder erosion by humidity and greatly improve the device stability based on mixed cation PVK films. As shown in Fig. 7b, the XRD patterns of RT and 55 °C perovskite films were monitored after 240 h. It was found that RT-based films fast degrade to δ-FAPbI₃ and PbI₂ phases, simultaneously with a reduction peaks intensity of α-FAPbI₃. By contrast, after 240 h ambient air exposing of 55 °C-based perovskite films, no α-to-δ transition was observed, and just a slight intensity reduction of XRD peaks was observed. It indicates improved humidity resistance of the 55 °C-based perovskite layers as compared to RT-based films.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, the sequential deposition method was successfully used in ambient air to fabricate stable PSCs. The reference devices fabricated at RT suffered from the recombination process due to the presence of small particle size separated with more boundaries. These issues led to poor PCE (10%) and insufficient air stability (degraded after 400 h) in the reference device. To overcome these obstacles, it was observed that the recombination process and degradation of air-processed PSCs can be deactivated by changing the FA/MA solution temperature. It was found that fabricating PSCs at 55 °C of FA/MA solution causes multiple benefits in the fabrication process. Engineered devices have larger PVK grain size with better orientation in (001) plane. Also, it was observed that the devices fabricated at 55 °C showed a redshift in the absorption edge of the PVK film, which increases the Jsc of PSCs accordingly. Almost no instability behavior was observed in mixed cation PSCs fabricated at 55 °C after 400 h of storage at room temperature with 25%–40% RH. These findings will provide a new approach to improve performance and reduce the instability of mixed cation PSCs constructed in air conditions.
