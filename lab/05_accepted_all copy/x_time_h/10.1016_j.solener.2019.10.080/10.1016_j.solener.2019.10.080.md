# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic perovskite materials show outstanding optoelectronic properties, making them become excellent candidates for solar cell applications (Docampo and Bein, 2016; Cho et al., 2018; Malinkiewicz et al., 2014). Less than a decade of research and development, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PCEs) has increased from  $ 3.83\% $  to over  $ 23\% $  (Jeon et al., 2018). The great success of perovskite solar technology is mainly attributed to materials design, interfacial functionalization, and new device architecture. Therein, great efforts have been made to develop efficient hole transport materials (HTMs) (Saliba et al., 2016; Yu and Sun, 2015), because the conventional HTM spiro-OMeTAD needs tedious synthetic steps and sophisticated purification processes, leading to very high material costs. To develop alternative low-cost HTMs for PSCs, different building blocks such as inorganic (Kim et al., 2015; Murugadoss et al., 2017), polymer (Jo et al., 2016; Kim et al., 2018; Gawlinska et al., 2017) and small organic molecules (SOM) (Rodríguez-Seco et al., 2018)

# ABSTRACT

N-annulated perylene based materials show outstanding and tunable optical and physical properties, making them suitable to be charge transport materials for optoelectronic applications. However, this type of materials has so far not been well studied in solar cells. Here, we develop a new hole transport material (HTM), namely S5, based on perylene building block terms, for organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs). We have systematically studied the influences of the film thickness of S5 on their photovoltaic performance, and a low concentration of S5 with a thinner HTM film is favorable for obtaining higher solar cell efficiency. S5 shows excellent energy alignment with perovskite as well as high-quality thin film formation, and the PSCs based on S5 as HTMs show remarkable power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 14.90% with a much higher short-circuit photocurrent than that for conventional HTM spiro-OMeTAD (PCE = 13.01%). We conclude that the superior photocurrent for S5 is mainly attributed to the enhanced interfacial hole transfer kinetics as well as the high hole conductivity. In addition, we have investigated the stability of N-annulated perylene derivative as HTMs in PSCs devices, showing that the unencapsulated devices based on S5 demonstrate outstanding stability by remaining 85% of initial PCEs in ambient condition with a relative humidity of ~30–45% for 500 h, while for devices with spiro-OMeTAD the cell efficiency degrade to 57% of initial performance at the same conditions.

Organic materials, especially small molecules, have shown great advantages over their inorganic counterparts, such as cheap and facile synthesis, high-quality thin film formation, versatile structures as well as tunable properties. So far many types of building blocks in organic structures have been investigated and used in PSCs, such as carbazole (Xu et al., 2014; Magomedov et al., 2017; Leijtens et al., 2012; Magomedov et al., 2018; Wang et al.; Sheibani et al., 2019), thiophene (Li et al., 2014), triphenylamine (Park et al., 2016), triazines (Do et al., 2014), acridine (Qin et al., 2014), spiro (Zhang et al., 2016), TTF-1 (Liu et al., 2014). Several new HTMs have been reported to exhibit superior photovoltaic performance than spiro-OMeTAD, however, most of them show poor photostability or environmental unfriendly synthesis due to the use of toxic compounds like hydrazine (Binek et al., 2016; Ono et al., 2015). Therefore, developing alternative organic HTMs with both high efficiency and stability becomes one of the major challenges for the scalable application of PSCs. N-annulated perylene based molecules have electron-rich properties, and the  $ \pi-\pi $  interactions of molecular packing are favorable to have good charge transporting features (Zhang et al., 2017; Guo et al., 2017). In addition, the facile synthesis of such perylene molecules is the key to develop low-cost HTMs. However, so far little attention has been paid in applying perylene building blocks in HTMs for efficient PSCs (Li et al., 2018). Moreover, little work has been carried out to thoroughly investigate the stability of perylene based materials in optoelectronic devices. In this work, we applied a new type of perylene  $ \pi $ -conjugated structure, a kind of carbazole derivative, into HTMs for PSCs. Our key design strategy in this work is that peripheral methoxy groups are incorporated in order to obtain suitable energy levels and thermal property, due to their favorable intramolecular interaction with core perylene. Materials cost and stability have become two of the most important parameters for HTMs to be compatible with the industrial application. Very recently, Sellinger et al. and Zhang et al. independently demonstrated that oxygen engineering position on HTM structures plays crucial roles in the stability of PSCs (Zhang et al., 2018; Schloemer et al., 2019). Thus, in contrast to the former perylene core structure (Li et al., 2018), the new S5 molecule with subtle modification can be seen as a promising candidate for improving the stability of PSCs. In addition, S5 demonstrates promising thermal and environmental stability by demonstrating that the unencapsulated devices based on S5 remain over  $ 85\% $  of initial performance after  $ 500\mathrm{h} $  when the devices are exposed in ambient condition with high humidity of  $ 40\% $ .

# 2.1. Synthesis of S5

The synthetic routes of S5 are outlined with details in the supporting information (SI), and it only needs four steps to obtain the target products. Specifically, the first step was purified by column chromatography, and the remaining three steps were performed by crystallization to obtain the product with high yield. The purification of S5 with only one column chromatography greatly reduced the final material cost which is critical for practical application. # 2.2. Photophysical and electrochemical measurements

Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements of S5 is performed to determine the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels. Specifically, three-electrode system is used in CV measurements. A scan rate of 50 mV/s was applied during CV in dichloromethane solution containing tetrabutylammonium hexafluorophosphate (TBAPF  $  _{6}  $  ) as a supporting electrode and ferrocene was used as an internal standard. The HOMO energy of S5 is estimated at 0.58 V vs. NHE, as extracted from the first oxidation-reduction peaks, which is about 80 mV more positive than that for spiro-OMeTAD at the same condition, as shown in Fig. S3. As mentioned above, the large   $  \pi  $  -conjugated system with strong electron-donating of bis(4-methoxyphenyl)amine in S5 greatly increases its HOMO energy level. Although the HOMO of S5 and spiro-OMeTAD compounds have a big difference in solution, it shows a negligible difference in   $  V_{OC}  $   in solid state devices. According to the previous reports, there is no clear correlation between   $  V_{OC}  $   of devices and the HOMO energy level of HTM because in this case   $  V_{OC}  $   is probably mostly determined by surface recombination at the perovskite-HTM interface (Belisle et al., 2016; Shi et al., 2015; Dänekamp et al., 2019). This fast charge transfer properties in CVs demonstrates that S5 is a good charge transporter. In addition, S5 shows excellent electrochemical reversibility during redox reaction, which means that S5 has very good electrochemical stability, which is the key for the stable charge extraction processes in PSCs (Kou et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2017c). The lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) levels of S5 were estimated to be -2.69 by adding optical bandgap to HOMO energy levels, which is notably higher than the conduction band of perovskite, that can efficiently block electron transfer from the perovskite to counter metal electrode and thus reduce the charge recombination losses. Such good energy configuration between S5 and perovskite absorber is crucial to diminish the losses at the HTM/perovskite interfaces.

# 2.3. Photovoltaic performances

In order to clarify the potential application of S5, we have to utilize this new structure on PSCs device architecture. The well-studied champion HTM, spiro-OMeTAD, was also assessed as a reference. Fig. 2 shows typical cross-sectional FESEM images of the perovskite devices with a configuration of FTO/TiO  $  _{2}  $  compact layer/mesoporous TiO  $  _{2}  $ /CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $ /HTM/Au. The FESEM images demonstrate S5 films are compact and more homogenous than spiro-OMeTAD to prevent direct contact between perovskite and metal electrode. FESEM images display the formation of voids at the spiro-OMeTAD layer (Figs. S6 and S7), which is probably created from moisture-induced degradation (Hawash

et al., 2015; Hu et al., 2017). Unlike, no pinhole or other defects were observed for S5 based devices which are possibly originate from a better  $ \pi $ -stacking spatial configuration, that is essential for having suitable contact to other layers. The device results confirm the suggestion that the large  $ \pi-\pi $  stacking with planar structure is critical for preparing good film morphology with long term stability (Zhang et al., 2017; Rojas et al., 2018; Li et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2011; Sfyri et al., 2016; Zhang et al., 2016, 2018).

The photocurrent voltage (J-V) curves and the incident photon to current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra of the devices are shown in Fig. 3a-b. The corresponding photovoltaic parameters are also listed in Table 1. Firstly, we studied the influence of HTM thicknesses on their photovoltaic performance. As shown in Table 1, we found that the thickness of HTMs plays a crucial role in PSCs performance. For example, at high concentration of 0.06 mM (e.g. 72.3 mg of spiro-OMeTAD, 45 mg of S5 in 1 mL), the devices with spiro-OMeTAD display the PCE of 14.61%, with an open circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 0.98 V, a short circuit photocurrent density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 23.70 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and a fill factor (FF) of 0.63. In contrast, S5-based devices exhibit a lower PCE of 13.75%, with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 0.97 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.95 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and an FF of 0.62. Interestingly, when a low concentration of 0.04 mM is applied, the devices with S5 show impressive PCE 14.90%, with   $  V_{OC}  $   of 0.99 V,   $  J_{sc}  $   of 24.43 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and FF of 0.62. # 2.4. Stability test

We first analyze the thermal properties of S5 by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA). The decomposition temperature   $  \left(\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{d}}\right)  $   is   $  \sim425^{\circ}C  $   (Fig. S5), which indicates that the high thermal stability is favorable for application in photovoltaic devices. Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) of S5 demonstrated high glass transition temperature   $  \left(\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{g}}\right)  $   at   $  131^{\circ}C  $   and melting point   $  \left(\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{m}}\right)  $   at   $  207^{\circ}C  $   (Fig. S5), showing that the amorphous state is beneficial for thin film morphological stability by reducing the formation of grain boundaries in thin films (Leijtens et al., 2012; Xu et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2017d). The higher Tg of S5 than spiro-OMeTAD (Qin et al., 2017), indicates S5 has superior thermal stability than spiro-OMeTAD.

To further evaluate the stability of PSC devices, we have studied the stability of unencapsulated devices in ambient condition with a relative humidity of 40% for 500 h. In the case of the devices with S5 (0.04 mM), the PCEs remained over 85% of initial performance. However, in the case of spiro-OMeTAD, the PCE dropped significantly after 24 h and remained only 50% of initial PCEs. We also tested the stability of PSCs with HTMs with high concentration of 0.06 mM, and similar degradation behavior is observed. # 3. Conclusion

In this study, we developed a new type of HTM, S5, with an extended  $ \pi $ -conjugate structure based on the perylene core structure. S5 demonstrates excellent physical and electrochemical properties for HTMs in PSCs. With an optimal thickness, the devices based on S5 show a high PCE of  $ 14.90\% $  which is much higher than the conventional spiro-OMeTAD  $ (13.01\%) $ . The devices with S5 show remarkable photocurrent over  $ 24\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  and high IPCE over  $ 90\% $ . From the morphological and electrochemical characterization, we found that both the compact and uniform thin film formation as well as the fast interfacial charge transfer contribute to the high photocurrent.
