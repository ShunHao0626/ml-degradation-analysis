# Soft Template-Controlled Growth of High-Quality CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  Films for Efficient and Stable Solar Cells

Cheng Liu, Yi Yang, Xin Xia, Yong Ding,* Zulqarnain Arain, Shijie An, Xuepeng Liu, Roldán Carmona Cristina, Songyuan Dai,* and Mohammad Khaja Nazeeruddin*

The unfavorable morphology and inefficient utilization of phase transition reversibility have limited the high-temperature-processed inorganic perovskite films in both efficiency and stability. Here, a simple soft template-controlled growth (STCG) method is reported by introducing (adamantan-1-yl)methanammonium to control the nucleation and growth rate of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   crystals, which gives rise to pinhole-free   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film with a grain size on a micrometer scale. The STCG-based   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite solar cell exhibits a power conversion efficiency of 16.04% with significantly reduced defect densities and charge recombination. More importantly, an all-inorganic solar cell with the architecture fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/NiO  $  _{x}  $  /STCG-  $  CsPbI_{3}  $  /ZnO/indium-doped tin oxide (ITO) is successfully fabricated to demonstrate its real advantage in thermal stability. By suppressing the inductive effect of defects during the phase transition and utilizing the unique reversibility of the phase transition for the high-temperature-processed   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film, the all-inorganic solar cell retains 90% of its initial efficiency after 3000 h of continuous light soaking and heating.

The regulation of the nucleation and growth rates has been identified as an efficient strategy for enhanced film coverage, which confirms that sufficient nucleation is expected to occur before the rapid growth onset.  $  ^{[26,52,53]}  $   Recently, a solvent-controlled growth (SCG) method was introduced to enhance the mass transport and diffusion, which successfully optimized the monomer concentration at a positive supersaturation region for a higher nucleation ratio.  $  ^{[21]}  $   With the SCG method, the precursor films became more continuous, and the number of pinholes in the high-temperature-processed black-phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films was significantly reduced. The polymer polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) has also been reported to induce   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   nucleation during film formation and fill voids within high-temperature processed perovskite films.  $  ^{[23]}  $   A PVP-induced   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film showed a dense grained uniform morphology with a crystal size of   $  \approx100  $   nm. In addition, using small micelles in the precursor solution to form more nucleation sites by partially replacing   $  Pb^{2+}  $   with   $  Ca^{2+}  $   in the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   precursor has been demonstrated, and more uniform films with larger grains pushed the efficiency to 13.3%.  $  ^{[22]}  $   Even so, there is still a lack of an effective and facile route for high-temperature processing to fabricate actual compact and black-phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films with micrometer-scale crystalline grains, which can be truly competitive with organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite materials.  $  ^{[27,54,55]}  $

To demonstrate the formation mechanism, the growth process of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films with the STCG method was investigated stage by stage. With the heating of the precursor film up to 100 °C, Figure S1 in the Supporting Information shows that the grain size of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   decreases from   $  \approx  $  150 nm (without STCG) to   $  \approx  $  30 nm (with STCG) and that the thickness of both films is   $  \approx  $  400 nm. The number of grains in the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film fabricated using the STCG method at 100 °C is nearly 125-fold that the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film fabricated without the STCG method. This result indicates that the nucleation rate of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   at the initial stage of film formation increased by nearly two orders of magnitude with the addition of ADMA.

Sufficient nucleation sites are certainly required for the complete coverage over the underlying substrate during the subsequent high-temperature annealing process.  $  ^{[26]}  $   To understand the phenomenon of the nucleation boom, the interaction between ADMA and the perovskite was first simulated using density functional theory (DFT) calculations. As shown in Figure S2a in the Supporting Information, the calculated equilibrium lattice constants for the bulk perovskite are a = b = c = 6.39 Å, which is in good agreement with previous theoretical results.  $  ^{[56]}  $   Both the Cs- and ADMA-adsorbed systems are evaluated for comparison (Figure 2a and Table S1, Supporting Information), with a Cs vacancy-based hollow built at the surface of the crystal simulation model as a reference (Figure S2b, Supporting Information). In the Cs-adsorbed system, the Cs atom has the lowest adsorption energy of -3.89 eV at the crystal surface. By contrast, the adsorption energy in the ADMA-adsorbed system increases to -4.85 eV for superficial ADMA, indicating that it is easier for ADMA than Cs cations to be adsorbed at the crystal surface during the self-assembly process of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   from the nonperovskite phase to the cubic phase. Bader charge analysis was further performed to estimate the electron transfer nature and electronic interactions. As shown in Figure 2b,c, yellow and cyan isosurfaces represent charge accumulation (i.e., a gain of electron density) and depletion (i.e., a loss of electron density) in the system, respectively. There is a charge transfer of 1.02 e  $  ^{-}  $   from the ammonium group of ADMA to the I atoms of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   crystal structure (Figure 2b), whereas a smaller charge transfer of 0.87 e  $  ^{-}  $   is found when Cs replaces the position of ADMA (Figure 2c). In addition, charge transfer is also observed between other amino molecules and the I atoms of the perovskite crystal structure.  $  ^{[57,58]}  $   Thus, the larger adsorption energy of ADMA mainly results from its stronger charge

transfer ability to the I atoms of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   and illustrates the tight interaction between ADMA and the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   crystal.

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) measurements (Figure S3a, Supporting Information) were further carried out to confirm the interaction of ADMA with the perovskite. The FTIR peak located at   $  \approx1308~cm^{-1}  $   represents the C-N symmetric stretching of the methylamine group of ADMA. The symmetric stretching peak shifts to a higher wavenumber (  $  1317~cm^{-1}  $  ) when an I atom is bound to N by the strong covalent bond in adamantylammonium iodide (ADMAI). Coincidentally, after mixing ADMA with   $  CsPbI_{3}  $ , the symmetric stretching peak redshifts to a wavenumber of   $  1314~cm^{-1}  $ , which is exactly located between the peak of ADMA and ADMAI. The increase in the C-N stretching wavenumber might be due to the decreased spatial symmetry of the methylamine group after its strong interaction with the I atoms of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   and the stretching vibration became more intense.  $  ^{[30]}  $   In addition, strong X-ray diffraction (XRD) peaks (Figure S3b, Supporting Information) were observed in the STCG-free   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   spun film without annealing, indicating the rapid crystallization of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film at room temperature without thermal annealing.  $  ^{[30]}  $   By contrast, the nearly amorphous   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   spun film prepared with the STCG method demonstrates that the crystallization of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   was suppressed by forming the intermediate phase with the added ADMA, which was well matched with the DFT and FTIR results. Moreover, dynamic light scattering measurements on the   $  CsPbI_{3}/DMF  $   solutions with different amounts of ADMA were employed (Figure S3c, Supporting Information). The radius of   $  CsPbI_{3}-DMF  $   is measured to be 1277 nm, which is in the same range as that in the literature.  $  ^{[59]}  $   Upon increasing the molar ratio of ADMA from 0.5% to 5%, the radius of the   $  CsPbI_{3}-DMF  $   particles in the ADMA-  $  CsPbI_{3}-DMF  $   solution decreases from 530 to 169 nm. According to a previous study, a reduction in colloid size is an efficient way to reduce grain sizes, and it is expected that ADMA could act similarly as the soft template to generate a strong steric effect on the subsequent ultrafast nucleation process.  $  ^{[30,59,60]}  $

We speculate that there are two factors affecting the effectiveness of the template molecule for the STCG method: 1) the steric effect of the spacer group linked with methylammonium and 2) the interaction of the methylammonium group with the perovskite. To verify this postulation, two additional experiments were designed. First, since the size of the adamantane group is large enough, the spacer group size was gradually decreased by adopting cyclohexanemethylamine (CHMA) and butylamine (BA) as the template molecules. The surface morphology of the STCG-CsPbI $ _3 $  films fabricated with different template molecules is displayed in Figure S4 in the Supporting Information and the detailed information is summarized in Figure 2d. With decreasing spacer group size, the grain sizes of the CHMA- and BA-based CsPbI $ _3 $  films at 100 °C increase to ≈80 and ≈100 nm, respectively, compared to the 30 nm grain size of the ADMA-based film. This result indicates that the number of nucleation sites is reduced when a smaller template molecule is employed. As we expected, more pinholes appear on the CHMA- and BA-based CsPbI $ _3 $  films than on the ADMA-based film after annealing at 350 °C. # 2.2. Characterizations of the   $  \gamma  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   Film

The final perovskite films exhibit typical diffraction peaks of high-temperature-processed  $ \gamma $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  (Figure 3a). $ ^{[19]} $  The STCG method strongly strengthens the intensities of the characteristic  $ \gamma $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  peaks near 14.4° and 29.0°, indicating that the STCG-CsPbI $ _3 $  film has preferred crystal orientation at the (110) and (220) planes. The increased crystallization and crystal preferential orientation could facilitate charge transport and suppress charge recombination. $ ^{[61,62]} $  In addition, UV-vis absorption spectra (Figure 3b) show that the CsPbI $ _3 $  films fabricated both with and without the STCG method have the desired absorption edge above 715 nm, nearly covering the full visible-light region. To quantify the electron trap-state density, the space-charge-limited currents (SCLCs) of electron-only devices with the structure FTO/TiO $ _2 $ /CsPbI $ _3 $ /phenyl-C $ _{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM)/Ag were verified as a function of the voltage in the dark. Figure 3c shows the current-voltage (I-V) curves of electron-only devices, and the trap-state density (N $ _t $ ) is determined by the trap-filled limit voltage (V $ _{TFL} $ ). $ ^{[45,63]} $  Upon decreasing V $ _{TFL} $  from 1.18 to 0.67 V, the trap densities of the CsPbI $ _3 $  films fabricated by using the STCG method decrease from 9.2 × 10 $  ^{15}  $  to 5.2 × 10 $  ^{15}  $  cm $  ^{-3}  $ . The decreased trap densities are mainly attributed to the pinhole elimination and crystallinity promotion of the CsPbI $ _3 $  films. Moreover, time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) and steady-state photoluminescence (PL) measurements are conducted to investigate the photophysical properties of CsPbI $ _3 $  films. As shown in Figure 3d, the PL decay for the CsPbI3 film prepared without the STCG method exhibits a lifetime ( $  \tau_{ave}  $ ) of 0.9 ns, and the precise fitting method is described in the Experimental Section. In contrast, the STCG-based CsPbI $ _3 $  film shows a longer  $  \tau_{ave} = 4.0  $  ns, demonstrating that the STCG method provides less recombination loss in the CsPbI $ _3 $  film. $ ^{[64]} $  The corresponding steady-state PL spectra (Figure 3e) demonstrate that the PL intensity of the CsPbI $ _3 $  film prepared with the STCG method on glass is increased by 1.5-fold without an obvious shift in the emission peak. This increase provides strong evidence that the CsPbI $ _3 $  film prepared with the STCG method exhibits fewer defects than the film prepared without the STCG method. To observe these defects in situ, confocal PL mapping measurements were carried out on the CsPbI $ _3 $  films (Figure 3f). It can be observed that some ultralow PL intensity areas (black region) are unevenly distributed in the CsPbI $ _3 $  films prepared without the STCG method. Combined with surface SEM images (Figure 1b), this result leads us to ascribe the PL loss in these locations mainly to the pinholes and gaps formed during the rapid film growth. In contrast, the in situ PL intensity map of the STCG-based CsPbI $ _3 $  film exhibits not only a more uniform emission distribution but also a higher absolute PL intensity, which is consistent with the average PL data in Figure 3e. # 2.3. CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  -Based Solar Cells and Device Performance

Figure S10 in the Supporting Information displays cross-sectional SEM images of the fabricated solar device with the architecture   $  FTO/TiO_{2}/CsPbI_{3}/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au  $  . # 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, we reported a feasible STCG method to improve the nucleation rate of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   by nearly two orders of magnitude and regulate the growth rate during the annealing temperature increase. As a result, a pinhole-free   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film with micrometer-scale grains is obtained, and the device based on this high-quality and less-defect   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film exhibits a high efficiency of over 16.04%. By suppressing the inductive effect of defects on the phase transition and utilizing the unique reversibility of the phase transition for high-temperature-processed   $  CsPbI_{3}  $ , an all-inorganic solar cell with the structure   $  FTO/NiO_{x}/STCG-CsPbI_{3}/ZnO/ITO  $   retains 90% of its initial efficiency after 3000 h of continuous light soaking and heating. The present study points to a new direction for precisely controlling the crystallization process of perovskite films even under high temperature conditions and shows a new way to effectively improve the long-term stability of PSCs.
