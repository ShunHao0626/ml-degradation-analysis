# ABSTRACT

Solar cells based on organo-metal hybrid perovskites have exhibited promising commercialization potential owing to their high efficiency and low cost manufacturing. However, the poor out-door operational stability of perovskite solar cells restricted their practical application, and moisture permeation and organic compounds volatilization are realized as the main factors accelerating performance degradation. Herein, we developed a composite encapsulation, by sequentially depositing a compact   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   layer and a hydrophobic FDTS layer on the completed device, to efficiently circumvent vapor permeability. Thus, the stability of the encapsulated perovskite solar cells was systematically investigated at simulated operational condition. It was found that the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite was prone to decay into solid   $  PbI_{2}  $   and organic vapor at high temperature or light illumination, and the decomposition was reversible in a well encapsulated environment resulting in reversible performance degradation and recovery. The enhanced thermal stability was ascribed to the competition between the perovskite decomposition and reverse synthesis. The as prepared high-quality, multilayered encapsulation scheme demonstrated superior sealing property, and no obvious performance decline was observed when the device was stored under ambient air, continuous light illumination, double 85 condition (85 °C, 85% humidity) or even water immersion, respectively. Therefore, this work paves the way for scalable and robust encapsulation strategy feasible to hybrid perovskite optoelectronics in a reproducible manner.

Keywords: perovskite solar cells, atomic layer deposition, stability, encapsulation, perovskite decomposition.

# Introduction

Organo-metal hybrid perovskites are emerging materials in photovoltaic cells due to their promising photo-physical features, i.e., direct and tunable band-gaps, high hole/electron transport mobility, cost-effective solution processability and certain flexibility.  $  ^{1-6}  $   The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the perovskite solar cells have been developed rushly from 3.8%  $  ^{7}  $   to 25.2%  $  ^{8}  $   in the last decade. However, to maintain the high efficiency under out-door operational condition for a long period is one of the remaining obstacles for their practical application of the perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{9}  $

It has been reported that the perovskite decomposition is one of the key factors for fast performance degradation of solar cells based on perovskites.  $  ^{10-14}  $   Due to the ionic character, the perovskite materials are very sensitive to moisture and oxygen,  $  ^{10,11}  $   which can penetrate through porous charge transport layer (CTL), such as   $  PC_{61}BM  $   or Spiro-OMeTAD etc., and reach the perovskite layer. Even at a very low concentration level, the presence of moisture and oxygen has a tremendous effect on the perovskite stability.  $  ^{15}  $   In particular, the photoexcited electrons can transfer from perovskite layer to molecular oxygen and form superoxide (  $  O_{2}^{-}  $  ) and the Lewis base water molecules are likely to coordinate with perovskite, which deprotonate the methylammonium (MA) cation in turn result in fragmentation of perovskite component.  $  ^{13,14}  $   In addition, constant illumination, high energy UV light and heat released from the solar energy, which are unavoidable in practical environment, can boost the local ion migration, structural dynamics or hydrogen bonding damage between MA and iodide, therefore accelerating the perovskite decomposition.  $  ^{16-22}  $   And also, photocatalytic metal oxides in terms of   $  TiO_{2}  $  , ZnO, etc., are commonly employed as charge transport layers in perovskite solar cells, which can

Many efforts have been made to improve the operational stability of perovskite solar cells, for examples, boosting the intrinsic phase stability by turning the perovskite dimensions or the crystal tolerant factor via composition engineering, $ ^{25-29} $  reducing ionic defects via chemically bonding with extra strongly coordinated elements or ligands, modifying the perovskite crystallization to enlarge the grain size and reduce crystal boundaries, $ ^{30-34} $  or employing water resistant or UV light absorbing charge transport materials to prevent the moisture and oxygen penetration and high energy light damage, $ ^{35-45} $  but the improvement is still limited. Encapsulation, which is aim to efficiently circumvent the moisture penetration by applying extra glass cover slip, $ ^{46} $  compact metal oxides $ ^{47-49} $  or hydrophobic organic matters $ ^{50-52} $  on the complete device, thus retarded the perovskite decomposition. However, rigorous industrial requirements exist for photovoltaic device to qualify the outdoor testing under real operation conditions. A simple encapsulation technology is not feasible to combine both high compactness to block vapor penetration and superior hydrophobicity to enhance water resistance, it's necessary to rationally design an efficient and reproducible encapsulation technology to fully improve device stability at various application conditions.

By employing an efficient encapsulation on the completed device, the humidity and oxygen penetration and volatilization of organic compounds are circumvented (Figure 1b), and hence the perovskite decomposition is substantially suppressed. To design an efficient encapsulation on perovskite solar cells, the encapsulating materials and the deposition method must be selected rationally. Noted that   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   is frequently employed as encapsulating materials in food industrials to prevent the oxygen penetration owing to its outstanding compactness and homogeneity,  $  ^{54}  $   but the hydrophilic   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   with a water contact angle of   $  40.8^{\circ}  $   (Figure 2b) is particularly vulnerable when directly exposed to high humidity.  $  ^{47}  $   Perfluoroalkyl trichlorosilane (FDTS), a super hydrophobic material consisting of long chain fluoroalkyl (Figure 2a) with large water contact angle up to   $  147.2^{\circ}  $   (Figure 2c), good film-processing ability and high thermal stability  $  ^{55-57}  $  , is selected to combine with the   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   to further boost the moisture resistance of the encapsulation layer. As aforementioned, the high temperature and light illumination accelerated the decomposition of CH₃NH₃PbI₃ to solid PbI₂, and volatile CH₃NH₂ and HI. Without any encapsulation, the volatile species can escape from the device via pin-holes in charge transport layer, leading to a rapid and irreversible perovskite decomposing and performance degradation.⁹ In case of encapsulated devices, only a small diffraction peak of PbI₂ in the XRD pattern and no obvious yellow phase in the image was observed after heating at 140 °C for 30 minutes as shown in Figure 4d, and the PbI₂ peak in XRD pattern was completely disappeared after relaxation, suggesting an enhanced thermal stability and reversible perovskite decomposition. Indeed, the extra encapsulation on the device cannot enhance the thermal stability of the intrinsic perovskite. The reason for the enhanced performance stability was ascribed to the reversibly chemical perovskite decomposition process. Specifically, the heating has no doubt to induce the thermal decomposition of perovskite, but in the meantime the decomposed compounds can react and transform back to CH₃NH₃PbI₃ as indicated in Figure 1. The competition between the decomposition and synthesis of perovskite materials renders significantly enhanced thermal stability.
