# Interfacial Defect Passivation and Stress Release via Multi-Active-Site Ligand Anchoring Enables Efficient and Stable Methylammonium-Free Perovskite Solar Cells

Baibai Liu, Huan Bi, Dongmei He, Le Bai, Wenqi Wang, Hongkuan Yuan, Qunliang Song, Pengyu Su, Zhigang Zang, Tingwei Zhou,  $  ^{*}  $   and Jiangzhao Chen  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Interfacial trap-assisted non-radiative recombination and residual stress impede the further increase of power conversion efficiency (PCE) and stability of the methylammonium-free (MA-free) perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, we report an interfacial defect passivation and stress release strategy through employing the multi-active-site Lewis base ligand (i.e., (5-mercapto-1,3,4-thiadiazol-2-ylthio)acetic acid (MTDAA)) to modify the surface and grain boundaries (GBs) of MA-free perovskite films. Both experimental and theoretical results confirm strong chemical interactions between multiple active sites in the MTDAA molecule and undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   at the surface or GBs of perovskite films. It is demonstrated theoretically that multi-active-site adsorption is more favorable thermodynamically as compared to single-active-site adsorption, regardless of   $  PbI_{2}  $   termination and formamidinium iodide (FAI) termination types. MTDAA modification results in much reduced defect

density, increased carrier lifetime, and almost thoroughly released interfacial residual stress. Upon MTDAA passivation, the PCE is boosted from 20.26% to 21.92%. The unencapsulated device modified by MTDAA maintains 99% of its initial PCE after aging under the relative humidity range of 10–20% for 1776 h, and 91% after aging at 60 °C for 1032 h.

Since the ground-breaking report on all-solid-state perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with a power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to 9.7%,  $  ^{1}  $  a record certified PCE up to 25.5% was achieved based on single-junction PSCs,  $  ^{2}  $  which is comparable to the PCEs of silicon solar cells. Recently, the monolithic perovskite/silicon tandem solar cell has demonstrated an impressive PCE of 29.15%.  $  ^{3}  $  Although high PCEs have been obtained for both single-junction and tandem solar cells, their large-scale deployment is still restricted by the poor long-term operational stability. The stability of PSCs is closely related to their functional layers and interfaces. Among them, perovskite compositions play a key role in the fabrication of high-efficiency and stable PSCs. The perovskite absorber materials generally use the chemical formula of ABX  $  _{3}  $ , where A stands for a monovalent cation (e.g., methylammonium (MA  $  ^{+}  $ ), formamidinium (FA  $  ^{+}  $ ), Cs  $  ^{+}  $ , and Rb  $  ^{+}  $ ), B represents a divalent metal cation (e.g., Pb  $  ^{2+}  $  and Sn  $  ^{2+}  $ ), and X is representative of a monovalent anion (e.g., halide anions).  $  ^{4}  $  Presently, most PSCs with efficiencies over 23% were constructed based on MA-containing perovskite compositions, such as MAFA-based perovskites,  $  ^{5-9}  $  CsMAFA-based

MA-free perovskites   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   and   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   are obtained when   $  MA^{+}  $  in   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   is substituted by the   $  FA^{+}  $  and   $  Cs^{+}  $ , respectively. Although pure   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   possesses an optimal bandgap of 1.48 eV and thus a wide spectral response along with a high short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ), it is subjected to a spontaneous phase transition from   $  \alpha  $   black phase to   $  \delta  $   yellow phase due to an inapposite Goldschmidt tolerance factor, induced by the slightly larger ionic radius of   $  FA^{+}  $   (2.53 Å) as compared to   $  MA^{+}  $   (2.17 Å), which is not beneficial for the device performance.  $  ^{[17,18]}  $   In contrast, pure   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   still faces a big challenge of phase instability owing to a smaller Goldschmidt tolerance factor, slightly off the ideal value, resulting from the smaller ionic radius of   $  Cs^{+}  $   (1.81 Å) than   $  MA^{+}  $ .  $  ^{[19,20]}  $   Furthermore, the wide bandgap of 1.73 eV for pure   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   would narrow the absorption spectrum and thus lower   $  J_{SC}  $ .  $  ^{[20]}  $   Fortunately, the incorporation of a proper amount of   $  Cs^{+}  $  in   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   has been extensively certified to be able to stabilize the black phase of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   due to entropic gains and increased phase transition energy from the   $  \alpha  $   to the   $  \delta  $   phase.  $  ^{[21,22]}  $     $  Rb^{+}  $  was further employed to partially take the place of CsFA-based MA-free perovskites, which contributed to the improved film quality and accordingly increased device performance.  $  ^{[13]}  $   In addition, the incorporation of a proper amount of Br in CsFA-based perovskites can further increase the phase stability and quality of perovskite films.  $  ^{[22]}  $   Devices based on the wide-bandgap mixed Cs and FA–lead–mixed I and Br (denoted as CsFAPbIBr) perovskites containing a large amount of Cs (10–20 mol%) and Br (10–20 mol%) have certified excellent stability but much lower PCE (usually below 21.5%) as compared to the state-of-the-art MA-containing PSCs.  $  ^{[16,22-27]}  $   This indicates that the introduction of a large amount of Cs and Br enlarges the bandgap and thus reduces the current density. Nevertheless, it is not desirable to increase stability at the cost of sacrificing efficiency. In the past two years, narrow-bandgap MA-free perovskites with a small amount of Cs and Br (e.g.,   $  FA_{0.95}Cs_{0.05}PbI_{3}  $   and   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $  ) have been attempted, achieving PCEs over 22%.  $  ^{[18,28]}  $   This suggests that fabrication of narrow-bandgap MA-free perovskites through reducing the amount of Cs and Br is key to increasing the current density and thus PCE. However, there is still a large efficiency space between MA-free PSCs and MA-containing PSCs. Additionally, the current stability of MA-free PSCs is still far from meeting the commercial application standard (IEC 61215 stability standard) for international photovoltaics.  $  ^{[29]}  $   It is well known that the PCE and stability losses are primarily determined by the bulk and interfacial non-radiative recombination in PSCs.  $  ^{[4,30]}  $   Therefore, it is highly desirable to reduce the non-radiative recombination losses of the bulk and the interface in PSCs for the purpose of simultaneous realization of high PCE and excellent stability.

In the past few years, tremendous efforts have been devoted to improving the quality of MA-free perovskite films and accordingly suppressing bulk non-radiative recombination, such as composition engineering,  $  ^{13,21}  $   precursor solvent engineering,  $  ^{31,32}  $   additive engineering,  $  ^{16,18,24}  $   etc. In view of the soft ionic nature of organic inorganic hybrid perovskites used in PSCs, a large amount of defects would unavoidably be produced within the bulk and at the surface/interface of perovskite films during high-temperature annealing and fast crystallization.  $  ^{4,30,33-35}  $   Most bulk defects are one-dimensional point defects that are generally shallow-level defects, whereas most defects at the interface (e.g., grain boundary (GB), surface, precipitate, etc.) are deep-level defects which are detrimental to device performance.  $  ^{3,30,33-35}  $   It was reported that the interfaces in polycrystalline films exhibited charge trap density 1–2 orders

of magnitude greater than that at the film interior.  $  ^{36}  $   Accordingly, interfacial non-radiative recombination dominates in non-radiative recombination.  $  ^{37}  $   The charge carriers trapped by interfacial deep-level defects and ions from film bulk moving to the surface would build up at interfaces and thus result in charge accumulation, which leads to band bending, energy band realignment, interfacial non-radiative recombination, and finally deteriorating device performance.  $  ^{30}  $   Additionally, interfacial defects would lead to interfacial stress and thus interfacial non-radiative recombination, finally destroying device performance.  $  ^{38,39}  $   Therefore, rational interface engineering is crucial to minimize the interfacial non-radiative recombination losses. In the past few years, however, the work on modulating interfacial non-radiative recombination of MA-free PSCs via interface engineering has been very limited.  $  ^{28}  $

In the whole field of PSCs, diverse molecules have been explored to passivate and heal interface defects so far, such as Lewis acid molecules,  $  ^{40}  $   Lewis base molecules incorporating N, O, S, and P electron donors,  $  ^{41,42}  $   salts (e.g., organic ammonium salts, inorganic metal halide salts, ionic liquids, inner salts, etc.),  $  ^{43,44}  $   2D perovskites,  $  ^{45,46}  $   quantum dots (QDs),  $  ^{47}  $   etc. Among them, Lewis base molecules have been verified to be very effective to passivate interfacial defects through the coordination interaction between the electron donor atom and under-coordinated Pb (halide vacancies) defects at the interface. However, most passivating molecules contain only one active site (either N, O, or S electron donor) which can interact with undercoordinated Pb.  $  ^{41,48-50}  $   Even if a few reported molecules (like polymers) have more than one active site, it is difficult for them to participate simultaneously in passivating defects due to the large steric hindrance.  $  ^{51-53}  $   To maximize the potentials of Lewis base defect passivators, it is imperative to develop multi-active-site passivating molecules through rational design and modulation to minimize the interfacial non-radiative recombination losses.

In this work, we develop a novel interfacial defect passivation strategy where a Lewis base molecule (i.e., (S-mercapto-1,3,4-thiadiazol-2-ylthio)acetic acid (MTDAA)) containing multiple active sites (simultaneously containing N, O, and S electron donors) is employed to modify the perovskite film/SpiroOMeTAD interface. The strong chemical interaction between multiple active sites in the MTDAA molecule and perovskite film is confirmed both experimentally and theoretically. Density functional theory results show that the MTDAA molecule lying horizontally on the surface of the perovskite film is most thermodynamically favorable as compared to other modes of chemical action. The average carrier lifetime is significantly enhanced from 468.34 to 629.62 ns after MTDAA modification, which is ascribed to the chemical interaction between multiple active sites in the MTDAA molecule and undercoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  defects at the surface or GBs of perovskite films. In addition, interfacial residual stress is released after MTDAA modification. Therefore, the PCE is much increased from  $ 20.26\% $  for the control device to  $ 21.92\% $  for the device with MTDAA, due to improvement of open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ ) and fill factor (FF) along with slightly enhanced  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ . Moreover, MTDAA modification results in improved long-term operational stability. Reduced trap-assisted non-radiative recombination and released residual stress contribute to the increased PCE and stability. These results strongly suggest that it is of key importance to effectively passivate interfacial defects via rationally developing Lewis base molecules with multiple active sites.

MACl additive was introduced into the precursor solution, ultraviolet–visible (UV–vis) absorption spectra and X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the perovskite films without and with MACl were measured to study whether   $  MA^{+}  $  was incorporated into the final perovskite lattices. As presented in Figure S2a,b in the Supporting Information, the positions of the main diffraction peaks (i.e., (110), (220), and (310)) were not shifted after introduction of MACl as additive. As illustrated in Figure S2c,d, the absorption cutoff edges and bandgaps of the perovskite films without and with MACl are the same. Both UV–vis absorption and X-ray diffraction results proved that   $  MA^{+}  $  was not incorporated into perovskite lattices, which was in good accordance with previous reports.  $  ^{58-60}  $   In order to sufficiently heal the defects at the surface and GBs of MA-free perovskite films, MTDAA with multiple active sites was employed to modify MA-free perovskite films. The modified perovskite films were prepared by spin-coating a solution of MTDAA in isopropanol (IPA) onto the as-prepared perovskite films. As presented in Figure 1a, multiple active sites (e.g., O, S1, S2, S3, N1, and N2) could interact with perovskite films via coordination reactions between O, S1, S2, S3, and N1 acting as Lewis base and/or N2 and undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   defects acting as Lewis acid. It is expected that the multi-active-site MTDAA molecule is capable of effectively passivating undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   defects and other positively charged defects. The interaction modes and strength will be analyzed and discussed by combining experimental and theoretical results in detail below. For the sake of convenient description, henceforth, “control” means the perovskite films or devices without MTDAA modification, while “target” means the perovskite films or devices modified by the optimal concentration of MTDAA.

First, the presence of the MTDAA molecule was identified by the appearance of S element as exhibited in the energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) in Figure S3 in the Supporting Information. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was carried out to investigate the chemical interaction between the MTDAA molecule and perovskite. As exhibited in the Pb 4f spectra in Figure 1b, the peaks of Pb   $  4f_{7/2}  $   (138.05 eV) and Pb   $  4f_{5/2}  $   (142.90 eV) in the control perovskite film were shifted to higher binding energies of 138.39 and 143.29 eV after MTDAA modification, respectively, which was indicative of strong chemical interaction between the MTDAA molecule and the perovskite composition, as we expected. The peaks at 401.33 eV from N1 and 400.30 eV from N2 in MTDAA were shifted to 401.09 and 399.49 eV in the MTDAA-modified sample, respectively, as presented in Figure 1c.  $  ^{61}  $   This suggested that N1 and N2 in the MTDAA molecule also can generate coordination bonds with undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $  . As illustrated by the O 1s spectra in Figure 1d, the spectrum of the modified sample was deconvoluted into three peaks of 531.36, 532.17, and 533.76 eV, which were attributed to the Pb–O, C=O, and C–O–H, respectively.  $  ^{62,63}  $   The formation of the Pb–O bond and shift of the O 1s peak in C=O from 532.45 to 532.17 eV confirmed the interaction between O=C in the MTDAA molecule and undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $  . This coordination bond was formed via providing a lone electron pair to undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   by oxygen from C=O of the MTDAA molecule. The same peak position for O 1s in C–O–H in the MTDAA and modified perovskite film shows that there is no chemical interaction between O in C–O–H and undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $  . It was seen clearly that three S 2p peaks occurred in both the MTDAA molecule and MTDAA-modified

film (Figure 1e). The binding energies of S1 (C–S), S2 on the thiadiazole ring, and S3 (C=S) were reduced from 165.81, 164.54, and 162.42 eV to 165.57, 164.34, and 162.12 eV, respectively. This suggests that all three S active sites can interact with perovskite films, but S3 corresponding to C=S seems to have the strongest interaction, and S2 seems to have the weakest chemical interaction, which will be further discussed on the basis of theoretical calculations. The strong interaction between S and Pb in perovskite composition has been extensively certified in the past.  $  ^{50,64}  $   Subsequently, Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy also confirmed the existence of MTDAA molecules in the final perovskite films, due to the appearance of some new peaks in the MTDAA-modified films compared with the control film, as illustrated in Figure S4a in the Supporting Information. The full FTIR spectra of the MTDAA and perovskite films without or with MTDAA are exhibited in Figure S4b. The tensile vibration peak of C=S (1119.9 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ),  $  ^{65}  $   vibration peak of N–N=C (1265.9 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ),  $  ^{66}  $   and stretching vibration peak of C–N (1293.7 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  )  $  ^{65}  $   in the MTDAA molecule were shifted to 1117.5, 1226.2, and 1291.0 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   in the perovskite film within MTDAA, respectively. In summary, XPS and FTIR results indicate that multiple active sites in MTDAA can chemically interact with undercoordinated Pb  $  ^{2+}  $   in perovskite composition.

It was interesting that bright spots at the GBs can be distinctly observed in all perovskite films. TRPL spectra were fitted by the double-exponential function equation   $  I(t)=I_{0}+A_{1}\exp(-t/\tau_{1})+A_{2}\exp(-t/\tau_{2})  $  ,  $  ^{43,45}  $   where   $  A_{1}  $   and   $  A_{2}  $   are the amplitudes of the fast and slow decay process, respectively.   $  \tau_{1}  $   represents the fast decay time constant and   $  \tau_{2}  $   the slow time constant, respectively. It was widely reported that   $  \tau_{1}  $   is related to the trap-assisted non-radiative recombination and   $  \tau_{2}  $   is related to the radiative recombination in the bulk of perovskite films.  $  ^{43,45}  $   The average carrier lifetime (  $  \tau_{ave}  $  ) was obtained by using the equation   $  \tau_{\mathrm{ave}}=(A_{1}\tau_{1}^{2}+A_{2}\tau_{2}^{2})/(A_{1}\tau_{1}+A_{2}\tau_{2})  $  .  $  ^{43,45}  $   The glass/perovskite/MTDAA sample exhibited significantly enhanced carrier lifetimes (  $  \tau_{ave}=629.62  $   ns,   $  \tau_{1}=19.16  $   ns, and   $  \tau_{2}=632.93  $   ns) compared to the glass/perovskite sample (  $  \tau_{ave}=468.34  $   ns,   $  \tau_{1}=152.80  $   ns, and   $  \tau_{2}=488.84  $   ns). Moreover, the average carrier lifetime was improved from 7.41 ns for the control device to 20.62 ns for the target device.  $  ^{70}  $   These results show that MTDAA interface modification can passivate defects at the surface and GBs of perovskite films and inhibit non-radiative recombination. Confocal PL mapping spectra in Figure 2e,f also indicated that MTDAA modification

led to remarkably improved carrier lifetimes, which was in perfect accordance with SSPL results (see Figure 2c). The space-charge-limited current (SCLC) curves were recorded to quantitatively calculate the defect densities of perovskite films. Typical current–voltage (I-V) curves in the dark are illustrated in Figure 2g. The Ohmic region at low bias and the trap-filled limited region at intermediate bias region are clearly observed from dark I-V curves. The defect density was calculated by the equation   $  n_{\mathrm{t}} = (2\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}V_{\mathrm{TFL}})/(eL^{2})  $  , where   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum dielectric constant,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the dielectric constant of the perovskite,   $  V_{TFL}  $   is the trap-filled limiting voltage obtained through fitting dark I-V curves, e is the elementary charge, and L is the thickness film of the perovskite. The defect density of the control perovskite film was estimated to be   $  9.25 \times 10^{15} \, cm^{-3}  $  , which was much higher than   $  6.37 \times 10^{15} \, cm^{-3}  $   of the target perovskite film. Obtaining the above information at the atomic level is laborious using available experimental techniques. In consideration of the complexity of the   $  \mathrm{Rb}_{0.02}(\mathrm{FA}_{0.95}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.05})_{0.98}\mathrm{PbI}_{2.91}\mathrm{Br}_{0.03}\mathrm{Cl}_{0.06}  $   perovskite composition used in this work, herein we chose   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   with a cubic structure as the model to construct the heterojunctions of the   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   (001) surface with the iodine-defect-adsorbing MTDAA ligands. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), light-intensity-dependent   $  V_{OC}  $  , transient photovoltage (TPV), and transient photocurrent (TPC) measurements were employed to uncover the effect of MTDAA passivation on interfacial charge transfer and recombination. PL and EIS results indicate that MTDAA treatment can effectively restrain interfacial non-radiative recombination losses by interfacial defect passivation. The ideality factor (m) was considered as a powerful tool in PSCs which can credibly estimate the recombination.  $  ^{4}  $   The target device exhibited a lower m of 1.07 compared with the control device (1.81), which shows that the surface non-radiative recombination is significantly weakened, as exhibited in Figure 4b. In short, the improvement of interfacial charge transfer was confirmed by EIS, TPC, and   $  V_{bi}  $   results, and diminished interfacial non-radiative recombination was demonstrated by the EIS, m, and TPV results after MTDAA modification.

In summary, we have developed a novel interfacial defect passivation and stress release strategy which is achieved by employing a multi-active-site Lewis base molecule (MTDAA) to modify the surface and grain boundaries (GBs) of MA-free perovskite films. Both experimental and theoretical results show that multiple active sites in MTDAA can chemically interact with the undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   defects at the surface or GBs of perovskite films. Moreover, density functional theory results suggest that multi-active-site adsorption is more favorable thermodynamically than single-active-site adsorption, which can maximize the defect passivation effect of MTDAA. MTDAA modification much reduces defect density, significantly increases the average carrier lifetime from 468.34 to 629.62 ns, and almost thoroughly releases interfacial residual stress. Consequently, the MTDAA-modified device exhibits an efficiency enhancement from 20.26% to 21.92% compared with the reference device. Concomitantly, a very small voltage deficit of 0.36 V is demonstrated for the target device with MTDAA, which represents the lowest energy losses ever reported for MA-free PSCs. Through MTDAA modification, the light, thermal, and moisture stability of the devices is increased. The unencapsulated device modified by MTDAA sustains 99% of its original efficiency after being stored under a relative humidity range of 10–20% for 1776 h, 91% after heating at 60 °C for 1032 h, and 47% after aging under one sun illumination for 1000 h. This work highlights the importance of multi-active-site molecules in passivating interfacial defects and releasing interfacial stress. We strongly believe that it is indispensable to minimize the nonradiative recombination losses at the surface and GBs of perovskite films and completely release interfacial stress through developing novel multi-active-site defect passivation molecule for the purpose of pushing the efficiency and stability toward meeting the commercial application standard.
