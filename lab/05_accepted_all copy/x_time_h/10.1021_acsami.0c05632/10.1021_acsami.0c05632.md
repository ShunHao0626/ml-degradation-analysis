# Abstract

Achieving high efficiency and long-term device stability is a vital issue for the commercialization of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PeSCs). In this work, phenyl ethyl-ammonium iodide (PEAI)-induced bilateral interface engineering was developed to improve the device efficiency and stability of methylammonium lead triiodide  $ (\mathrm{MAPbI}_3) $ -based PeSCs. Introducing PEAI onto poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) layer modifies the surface properties of the PEDOT:PSS and facilitates the formation of a high-quality perovskite active layer with enlarged grains on the PEDOT:PSS. The  $ \mathrm{PEA^{+}} $  in the PEAI-PEDOT:PSS also alters the work function of the PEDOT:PSS, leading to a reduction in the energy difference between the PEDOT:PSS and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite layer, which decreases energy loss during charge transfer. Additionally, depositing PEAI onto three dimensional (3D) perovskite yields a two dimensional/three dimensional (2D/3D) stacked structure for the perovskite active layer. Because the two dimensional (2D) top layer acts as a capping layer to prevent water penetration, the stability of the perovskite active layer is significantly enhanced. A PeSC device fabricated based on this combination exhibits enhanced power conversion efficiency and extended device lifetime compared to a pristine PeSC. Under high-humidity conditions  $ (75\pm 5\%) $ , the PEAI-treated PeSC retains  $ 88\% $  of its initial power conversion efficiency (PCE) after  $ 100\mathrm{h} $ . In contrast, a pristine PeSC device loses over  $ 99\% $  of its initial PCE after only  $ 25\mathrm{h} $  under the same conditions.

Keywords: PEAI; bilateral interface engineering; high efficiency; long-term stability; perovskite solar cells.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PeSCs) have attracted significant attention as promising candidates for next-generation thin film photovoltaics based on the advantages of high carrier mobility, small exciton binding energies, and easy fabrication   $  {}^{1-6}  $  . Although remarkable progress in PeSC technology has been achieved, further enhancements of device power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term stability are still required for commercialization   $  {}^{7-10}  $  .

One reasonable approach to enhancing the PCE of PeSCs is to incorporate effective functional layers, such as a hole transport layer (HTL) and electron transport layer, in the device architecture   $  {}^{11-12}  $  . Such layers typically improve interfacial adhesion between the perovskite active layer and electrodes, which could accelerate charge carrier transportation and lower energy barriers at interfaces, leading to the enhancement of device efficiency   $  {}^{13}  $  . Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) is one of the most widely used functional materials based on numerous advantages, including low-temperature solution processability, transparency, good conductivity, and excellent thermal stability   $  {}^{14-16}  $  . However, the work function (WF) of PEDOT:PSS films is not well matched to the valence band maximum of perovskite (methylammonium lead triiodide,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  ,   $  (MAPbI_{3})  $  ) active layers, leading to significant open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) loss during charge migration   $  {}^{17-19}  $  . Furthermore, obtaining high-quality perovskite films with large crystal grains, high coverage degrees, uniformity, and smooth and pinhole-free morphologies on PEDOT:PSS layers remains a major obstacle to the fabrication of high-efficiency PeSCs   $  {}^{20-23}  $  .

Many research groups have attempted to enhance the properties of PEDOT:PSS to improve device efficiency   $  {}^{24-25}  $  . Lee et al.   $  {}^{26}  $   reported the enhancement of the   $  V_{oc}  $   and PCE of PeSCs by

The introduction of a self-organized perfluorinated ionomer into PEDOT:PSS alters its surface characteristics and WF, leading to reduced barrier energy at interfaces. Additionally, Hu et al.  $  ^{27}  $  introduced a sodium salt (NaCl) into PEDOT:PSS and observed an increase in device PCE of more than 18%. Although these works have successfully demonstrated remarkable improvements in device efficiency, PeSC devices still exhibit relatively poor stability in ambient air conditions.

The long-term stability of PeSCs is another critical issue that must be resolved for practical device application   $  {}^{28-33}  $  . Many previous papers have reported that the instability of PeSCs typically stems from two main factors. One is water and impurity permeation through the PEDOT:PSS layer and the other is instability of the perovskite itself. Because conventional PEDOT:PSS is highly hygroscopic, it destroys the underlying perovskite layer in humid environments, resulting in poor device stability   $  {}^{34-36}  $  . Ma et al.   $  {}^{37}  $   reported that the use of Nafion-treated PEDOT:PSS can protect the perovskite active layer in a device from the permeation of water through the PEDOT:PSS. Because the hydrophobic Nafion was positioned at the top of the PEDOT:PSS after coating, it could shield the PEDOT:PSS and suppress water permeation. Additionally, Xue et al. suggested dopamine-treated PEDOT:PSS as a viable alternative for improving the stability of PeSCs. They demonstrated that the hydroxyl and amino groups in dopamine could interact with uncoordinated Pb atoms in a perovskite active layer and to decrease the charge recombination rate at the trap sites generated by uncoordinated Pb atoms   $  {}^{38}  $  . # 2.2 Device fabrication

# 2.2.1 PEAI-induced bottom coating

In our fabrication process, a PEDOT:PSS aqueous solution was first spin-coated onto an ITO-coated glass at 4500 rpm for 40 s. In the traditional process (Figure S1), the obtained PEDOT:PSS film would be heated for 20 min. However, for PEAI-induced bottom coating (Figure 1a), after spinning the PEDOT:PSS film, a PEAI solution was spin-coated onto PEDOT:PSS film at 3000 rpm for 30 s, then heated at 145 °C for 20 min and transferred into a glove box. Perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated on the PEDOT:PSS layer first at 1200 rpm for 5 s then at 4500 rpm for 50 s. After beginning of the second step for 5 s, 200 μL chlorobenzene (CB) as anti-solvent was quickly dripped onto the center of substrate. 6,6-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methylester (PC $ _{61} $ BM) was then deposited onto the perovskite precursor film. Next, the ITO-coated glass was transferred into a vacuum oven and annealed at 120 °C for 10 min under a low pressure of  $ -8 \times 10^{4} $  pa. Subsequently, the PC $ _{61} $ BM layer was washed off using CB and a new PC $ _{61} $ BM film was deposited onto the perovskite film. Finally, 100 nm of aluminum (Al) electrode was evaporated onto the film under a low pressure of approximately  $ 5 \times 10^{-7} $  Torr. The devices fabricated via traditional and bottom coating were marked as MAPbI $ _{3} $  and PEAI-MAPbI $ _{3} $ , respectively.

Figure 4a presents the dependence of   $  J_{sc}  $   on the logarithmic form of incident light intensity (I) based on   $  J_{sc} \propto I^{\alpha}  $  . It is clear that the slope of the PEAI-MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -based devices with a gradient value of 0.84 is greater than the value of 0.83 of the pristine device, implying decreased bimolecular recombination under short-circuit conditions  $  ^{6}  $  . Additionally, the dependence of   $  V_{oc}  $   on I in logarithm form (Log(I)) is presented in Figure 4b, which fits the equation of   $  V_{oc} = \varepsilon kT\ln(I) / e + (a constant)  $  , where   $  \varepsilon  $   is the ideal factor, k is the Boltzmann constant, T is the absolute temperature, and e is the elementary charge  $  ^{59}  $  . The   $  \varepsilon  $   values were calculated to be 1.28 and 1.10 for the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  - and PEAI-MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -based devices, respectively. The reduced   $  \varepsilon  $   value also indicates the effective inhibition of trap-assisted charge recombination and the high mobility of charge carriers  $  ^{21}  $  .

# 3.2 Top coating PEAI treatment

In the top coating process, PEAI was deposited onto the perovskite film, as shown in Figure 1b. It was previously reported that 2D perovskites exhibit improved water resistance and extend the device lifetime of PeSCs compared to 3D perovskites. # 4. Conclusion

In summary, we have developed a simple and effective method to fabricate highly efficient and stable  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based PeSCs. By introducing PEAI onto PEDOT:PSS layers and perovskite active layers both separately and combined, we have successfully demonstrated significant enhancements of the device efficiency and stability of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based PeSCs. The deposition of PEAI on PEDOT:PSS facilitates the easy growth of a high-quality perovskite active layer on PEAI-PEDOT:PSS and reduces the energy difference between PEDOT:PSS and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite layers by altering the WF of the PEDOT:PSS, resulting in improved device efficiency. Furthermore, depositing PEAI onto 3D perovskite generates a 2D perovskite surface on the 3D perovskite and enhances the water resistance of the perovskite active layer. PeSCs prepared with combination coating exhibited PCE enhancement from  $ 15.32\% $  to  $ 18.44\% $  and extended device lifetime. Under a high-humidity conditions of  $ 75 \pm 5\% $  RH, a PEAI-treated PeSC retained  $ 88\% $  of its initial PCE after  $ 100\mathrm{h} $ , whereas a pristine PeSC device lost over  $ 99\% $  of its initial PCE after only  $ 25\mathrm{h} $  under the same conditions. Because the application of PEAI to PEDOT:PSS and perovskite is simple and effective, we believe that the PEAI treatment method developed in this work will provide a new approach to the construction of highly efficient and stable PeSCs.
