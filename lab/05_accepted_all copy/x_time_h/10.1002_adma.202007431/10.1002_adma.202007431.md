# Capturing Mobile Lithium Ions in a Molecular Hole Transporter Enhances the Thermal Stability of Perovskite Solar Cells

Seul-Gi Kim, Thi Huong Le, Thybault de Monfreid, Fabrice Goubard, Thanh-Tuân Bui,* and Nam-Gyu Park*

A thermally stable perovskite solar cell (PSC) based on a new molecular hole transporter (MHT) of 1,3-bis(5-(4-(bis(4-methoxyphenyl) amino)phenyl) thieno[3,2-b]thiophen-2-yl)-5-octyl-4H-thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6(5H)-dione (coded HL38) is reported. Hole mobility of  $ 1.36 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~cm}^2 \mathrm{~V}^{-1} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} $  and glass transition temperature of  $ 92.2^{\circ} \mathrm{C} $  are determined for the HL38 doped with lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide and 4-tert-butylpyridine as additives. Interface engineering with 2-(2-aminoethyl)thiophene hydroiodide (2-TEAI) between the perovskite and the HL38 improves the power conversion efficiency (PCE) from  $ 19.60\% $  (untreated) to  $ 21.98\% $ , and this champion PCE is even higher than that of the additive-containing  $ 2,2',7,7' $ -tetrakis(N,N-di- $ p $ -methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-MeOTAD)-based device ( $ 21.15\% $ ). Thermal stability testing at  $ 85^{\circ} \mathrm{C} $  for over  $ 1000 \mathrm{~h} $  shows that the HL38-based PSC retains  $ 85.9\% $  of the initial PCE, while the spiro-MeOTAD-based PSC degrades unrecoverably from  $ 21.1\% $  to  $ 5.8\% $ . Time-of-flight secondary-ion mass spectrometry studies combined with Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy reveal that HL38 shows lower lithium ion diffusivity than spiro-MeOTAD due to a strong complexation of the  $ \mathrm{Li^{+}} $  with HL38, which is responsible for the higher degree of thermal stability. This work delivers an important message that capturing mobile  $ \mathrm{Li^{+}} $  in a hole-transporting layer is critical in designing novel MHTs for improving the thermal stability of PSCs. In addition, it also highlights the impact of interface design on non-conventional MHTs.

The milestone report of solid-state perovskite solar cells (PSCs) in 2012 demonstrating PCE of 9.7% and stability for 500 h in an ambient condition,  $  ^{[1]}  $  following two seminal works on the liquid redox electrolyte-based perovskite-sensitized solar cells published in 2009  $  ^{[2]}  $  and 2011,  $  ^{[3]}  $  has opened up a new paradigm of emerging photovoltaics. Although huge progress has been achieved in term of the photovoltaic performance with

significant improvement in both environmental and thermal stabilities of the devices.  $  ^{[11-14]}  $   Dopant-free MHT has been also suggested to be one of solutions to solve the thermal instability. However, PCE is sacrificed due to the lowered conductivity in the absence of dopants and furthermore PCE of about 20% is still inferior to that of the conventional devices using additive (  $  >23\%  $  ).  $  ^{[15-17]}  $   Facing up to this issue, development of thermally stable MHTs even in the presence of dopants which effectively impede LiTFSI migration is of major interest. In addition, a new MHT may differently interact with the perovskite's surface due to different molecular structure as compared to the well-known spiro-MeOTAD. Thus, interface engineering at the perovskite/new MHT has to be carefully considered by meticulously selecting a suitable molecular interfacial modifier. When designing interfacial materials, bifunctional property is in general required in order to induce chemical interaction with both perovskite and MHT. For instance, n-hexyl trimethyl ammonium bromide was suggested for P3HT  $  ^{[18]}  $   and phenylethylammonium iodide for spiro-MeOTAD.  $  ^{[19]}  $   For designing the highly conductive p-type small molecules, donor–acceptor

Energy levels of frontier orbitals (HOMO and LUMO) are also readily tunable for having appropriate energy alignment with the perovskite absorber. In addition, D-A conjugated molecules with planar structure could enhance intermolecular interaction in solid-state thin film and thus improve charge carrier mobility. The combination of adequate donor, acceptor, and  $ \pi $ -spacer units is an important factor in designing D-A molecules as efficient MHT in PSCs. It is well known that, even the frontier orbitals are delocalized over the whole molecules; the HOMO and LUMO are mainly localized on the donor and acceptor units, respectively. Electron donor moieties have to be chosen to adjust HOMO levels being compatible to the valence band of perovskite layer. Typical examples of electron donors are benzodithiophene, $ ^{[20]} $  dithienothiophene, $ ^{[21]} $  carbazole, $ ^{[22]} $  dithienosilole, $ ^{[23]} $  dithieno[3,2-b:2',3'-d]pyrrole, $ ^{[24,25]} $  and triarylamine. $ ^{[26]} $  The choice of acceptor units would have an impact on the exciton dissociation and prevent the back transfer of electron to the conductance band of perovskite. A huge number of acceptor units have been employed to synthesize conjugated semiconducting materials, especially for bulk heterojunction organic solar cells. For example, 1,4-diketopyrrolopyrrole, $ ^{[27-29]} $  2,1,3-benzothiadiazole, $ ^{[30-33]} $  isoindigo, $ ^{[34-36]} $  thienoisoindoledione, $ ^{[37,38]} $  and bithiopheneimide and their derivatives $ ^{[39-41]} $  are widely used because of their strong electron-withdrawing characteristics. In particular, imide-functionalized aromatic groups such as thienoisoindoledione, thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6-dione, $ ^{[42-46]} $  and bithiopheneimide are attractive acceptor units to be used as a central acceptor unit in D-A molecules. Especially, thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6-dione is one of the promising acceptor units due to its geometry and small aromatic resonance energy promoting stable quinoidal character. $ ^{[40]} $  This characteristic is important to obtain stable state between ground state and excited state to achieve favorable properties such as high backbone coplanarity, enhance electron delocalization, and reduce bandgap of target molecules. In fact,  $ N $ -alkylation of imide backbone will improve the solubility and the solid-state packing without disrupting close intermolecular  $ \pi-\pi $  stacking which is required for efficient carrier transport. Especially, when the  $ \pi $ -bridge is a thiophene or its derivatives, the intramolecular S-O interactions involving the C=O groups of the imide moiety and the S atoms of the  $ \pi $ -bridge can occur as configuration "locks" to enhance  $ \pi $ -system coplanarity and thus enhance its charge mobility. $ ^{[47-49]} $  From this point of view, designing rationally planar-rigid fully conjugated molecules would be an effective way to construct MHT by balancing between improving  $ \pi-\pi $  stacking effect that is beneficial for  $ \pi $ -electron delocalization and solubility for solution processing. While thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6(5H)-dione has been widely employed in preparing organic photovoltaic materials, it has been hardly used in designing HTM for PSC. A very recent example of polymeric HTM based on thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6(5H)-dione has just been reported. $ ^{[50]} $

Herein, we report a thermally stable MHT in PSCs. A new D-π-A-π-D small molecule, 1,3-bis(5-(4-(bis(4-methoxyphenyl)amino)phenyl)thieno[3,2-b]thiophen-2-yl)-5-octyl-4H-thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6(5H)-dione, coded HL38, is synthesized based on triphenylamine and thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6-dione as donor and

acceptor units, respectively, in combination with thieno[3,2-b] thiophene as  $ \pi $ -linker. Optical and electrical properties of HL38 are investigated and compared with those of the conventional MHT (spiro-MeOTAD). Photovoltaic performance of PSCs employing HL38 in a n-i-p solar cell structure is studied, which is also compared with that of the spiro-MeOTAD-based PSCs. Moreover, interface engineering between HL38 and perovskite is carried out by post-treatment with a 2-TEAI. The choice of 2-TEAI as interfacial modifier is reasoned by the expected strong interaction between the thiophene ring in 2-TEAI with the HL38. The thermal stability test has been performed at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for over  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $ . The systematic studies reveal that photovoltaic performance for HL38 is higher than that for spiro-MeOTAD. In addition, the thermal stability of the HL38-based PSC is much better than that for the spiro-MeOTAD-based one  $ (\mathrm{PCE}_{1032\mathrm{h}} / \mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{initial}} = 0.86 $  for HL38 vs 0.27 for spiro-MeOTAD). It has been elucidated that high  $ T_{g} $  of HL38 with additive and the strong interaction of the mobile  $ \mathrm{Li^{+}} $  with the 5-octylthieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6-dione (octyl-TPD) moiety in HL38 is responsible for the significantly improved thermal stability.

Figure 1a shows the molecular structure of HL38 having two triphenylamine moieties as donor unit and thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6-dione as acceptor unit and two thieno[3,2-b]thiophene units linking donor and acceptor units. The synthesis of HL38 is schematically depicted in Figure 1b. Briefly, 4-bromo-N,N-bis(4-methoxyphenyl)aniline is treated with triisopropyl (5-(tributylstannyl)thieno[3,2-b]thiophen-2-yl)silane  $  ^{[51]}  $  under Pd-catalyzed coupling reaction followed by the deprotection of triisopropylsilyl group by tetra-n-butylammonium fluoride giving rise to an intermediate-1 in good yield (90% for two steps). The intermediate-1 thus reacts with n-BuLi, followed by adding of tributyltin chloride and the freshly formed product is immediately subjected to a Stille coupling reaction with 1,3-dibromo-5-octyl-4H-thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6(5H)-dione  $  ^{[52]}  $  providing the final target HL38 (overall 42% yield for two steps). The detailed synthetic procedure and structural analytical data (proton nuclear magnetic resonance  $  [^{1}H-NMR]  $ ,  $  {}^{13}C-NMR  $ ,  $  {}^{13}C  $  DEPT NMR, and HRMS of HL38) are given in Figures S1–S4, Supporting Information. The optical gap of HL38 estimated at the absorption edge (580 nm) is 2.14 eV. In solution, HL38 exhibits fluorescent property with the emission band centered at 603 nm with the Stokes shift of 92 nm. Carrier lifetime and steady-state PL intensity increase as concentration of 2-TEAI increases from 0 to 15  $ \times $  10 $ ^{-3} $  M (Figure S12a,b, Supporting Information). PL decay in TRPL is fitted to mono- or bi-exponential decay function including two distinct components corresponding to trap-mediated non-radiative recombination ( $ \tau_{1} $ , fast decay process) and radiative recombination ( $ \tau_{2} $ , slow decay process) $ ^{[72]} $  and the fitted results are listed in Table S2, Supporting Information. The amplitude of fast decay process gradually decreases from 63.2% (control) to 10.9% (6  $ \times $  10 $ ^{-3} $  M of 2-TEAI). The decay curves can be fitted to mono-exponential decay function for 10 and 15  $ \times $  10 $ ^{-3} $  M because most of fast decay component disappears as shown in Figure S12a, Supporting Information, which indicates that the surface traps are effectively passivated by higher concentration of 2-TEAI. Post-treatment with 10  $ \times $  10 $ ^{-3} $  M of 2-TEAI increases  $ \tau_{2} $  significantly from ≈211 to ≈736 ns, indicative of prolonged carrier lifetime by post-treatment with 2-TEAI. Further increase in concentration to 15  $ \times $  10 $ ^{-3} $  M would not alter the lifetime (≈743 ns for 15  $ \times $  10 $ ^{-3} $  M of 2-TEAI), which implies that optimal concentration is around 10  $ \times $  10 $ ^{-3} $  M. UPS measurement shows that the post-treatment with 10  $ \times $  10 $ ^{-3} $  M of 2-TEAI shifts  $ E_{\text{VB}} $  of FA $ _{0.9} $ Cs $ _{0.1} $ PbI $ _{2.8} $ Br $ _{0.2} $  perovskite upward from -5.95 to -5.67 eV and  $ E_{\text{CB}} $  from -4.39 to -4.11 eV (see Figure S12c,d, Supporting Information). Optical bandgap and absorbance are hardly changed by the post-treatment (Figure S12e, Supporting Information). The energy levels of materials participating in PSC are depicted in Figure 3e, where difference in energy between  $ E_{\text{VB}} $

of perovskite and HL38 decreases from 1.04 to 0.54 eV after introducing   $  10 \times 10^{-3}  $   M of 2-TEAI, which is beneficial not only for extraction of carriers but also increasing   $  V_{oc}  $   by maintaining high quasi-Fermi-level splitting.  $  ^{[73-75]}  $   As a consequence of the interface engineering with   $  10 \times 10^{-3}  $   M of 2-TEAI, PCE is enhanced from 19.60% to 21.98% mainly due to increased   $  V_{oc}  $   (from 1.037 to 1.123 V) and FF (from 0.8047 to 0.8272) as shown in Figure 3f. We investigate the effect of concentration on photovoltaic parameters, where PCE increases gradually as concentration increase from   $  0 \times 10^{-3}  $   M (control) to   $  10 \times 10^{-3}  $   M, maximized at   $  10 \times 10^{-3}  $   M and slightly decreases for further increase in concentration to   $  15 \times 10^{-3}  $   M (Figure 3g). This improvement is attributed to mainly the   $  V_{oc}  $   enhancement with concentration. It is interesting to see that   $  J_{sc}  $   is hardly influenced by post-treatment and the concentration, which is probably due to the unchanged absorbance as confirmed in Figure S12e, Supporting Information. The champion PCE of the HL38-based device is higher than that of the one with spiro-MeOTAD (21.15% in Figure S13, Supporting Information) even after interface engineering with 2-TEAI.

To explore thermal stability of the HL38-based PSCs, differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) is first studied on the HL38 doped with additives of LITFSI and tBP to determine the   $  T_{g}  $   because   $  T_{g}  $   could be affected by additives, impurity, and morphology.  $  ^{[6,7,76]}  $   We have measured DSC for two types of MHTs: a dried-powder obtained by evaporating solvent from the MHT solution and a recovered-powder obtained by collecting it from the MHT film deposited on a glass substrate and then dried under high vacuum for 36 h. The latter is investigated to study the thermal property of the MHTs in a real device system. The DSC results for the dried-powder (  $  T_{g}-1  $  ), the recovered-powder without additive (  $  T_{g}-2  $  ), and the recovered-powder with tBP (  $  T_{g}-3  $  ) are shown in Figure S14, Supporting Information, and those for the recovered-powder with tBP and LiTFSI (  $  T_{g}-4  $  ) are shown in Figure 4a.   $  T_{g}  $  s of spiro-MeOTAD are determined to be 127.1, 98.3, 72.8, and 62.3 °C, while those of HL38 are measured to be 131.7, 119.0, 107.1, and 92.2 °C for   $  T_{g}-1  $ ,   $  T_{g}-2  $ ,   $  T_{g}-3  $ , and   $  T_{g}-4  $ , respectively.   $  T_{g}  $   is lower for the film form (  $  T_{g}-2  $  ) than for the dried-powder (  $  T_{g}-1  $ ). It is noted in the recovered-powder that an inclusion of additive of tBP and LiTFSI has less influence on   $  T_{g}  $   of HL38 (from 119.0 to 92.2 °C) as compared to the more decrease in   $  T_{g}  $   from 98.3 to 62.3 °C for spiro-MeOTAD. Moreover, no obvious crystallization temperature (  $  T_{c}  $ ) and melting point (  $  T_{m}  $ ) are observed for the undoped/doped HL38, as compared to the distinct   $  T_{c}  $   and   $  T_{m}  $   for both the doped and undoped spiro-MeOTAD (Figure 3a and Figure S10, Supporting Information). This implies that HL38 is thermally stable regardless of doping. Thermogravimetric analysis for HL38 in Figure S15, Supporting Information, shows that decomposition starts from 339 °C (5 wt% loss) and is maximized kinetically at 465 °C. As a result, the HL38-based PSC maintains 85.9% of its initial PCE (from 21.9% to 18.8%) after aging for 1032 h at 85 °C, whereas a significant degradation in PCE from 21.1% to 5.8% is observed for the spiro-MeOTAD-based device (Figure 4f) (see also statistical stability data in Figure S18 and Table S3, Supporting Information). We have also investigated thermal stability of the doped (additive-containing) HL38 devices without 2-TEAI post-treatment to clearly show the role of HL38 on enhancing the thermal stability, together with that of the additive-free HL38 devices with and without 2-TEAI post-treatment (see Figure S19, Supporting Information). The additive-free HL38 device shows a PCE as low as 10.57% before 2-TEAI post-treatment, while a higher PCE of 15.91% is obtained after 2-TEAI post-treatment. As expected, the PCE of the additive-free HL38 (with 2-TEAI treatment) device maintains 89.0% of its initial PCE after 648 h at 85 °C, which might be due to the absence of the additives and/or intrinsic thermal property of HL38. For the doped HL38 device without 2-TEAI post-treatment, the PCE maintains substantially 87.3% of its initial PCE from 19.63% to 17.15% after aging for 648 h at 85 °C, which strongly indicates that the HL38 play decisive role in thermal stability of PSCs. In Table S4, Supporting Information, the thermal stability of the HL38-based PSC is compared with other hole-transporting materials, where the HL38-based PSC shows comparable or even better PCE and thermal stability at test condition of 85 °C for over 1000 h. This work highlights the importance of complexation of LiTFSI with MHT in order to improve thermal stability by protecting Li ion from diffusion from MHT into perovskite layer.

We have demonstrated thermally stable PSC using a D-  $  \pi  $  -A-  $  \pi  $  -D MHT (HL38) based on thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6-dione (acceptor) and triphenylamine (donor) linked by thieno[3,2-b] thiophene as   $  \pi  $  -linker. Commonly used MHTs containing additives of LiTFSI and tBP, such as spiro-MeOTAD, have shown low thermal stability test due to high degree of Li ion diffusivity from MHT to perovskite layer during high temperature. However, we discovered that the Li ion diffusion was effectively suppressed in the HL38-based hole-transport layer thanks to the chemical interaction between the Li ion and the carbonyl group and/or thiophene in HL38. As a result, the HL38-based PSC showed excellent thermal stability maintaining   $  \approx86\%  $   of initial PCE after the 1032 h aging at 85 °C, while the unrecoverable degradation was observed from the spiro-MeOTAD. Regarding photovoltaic performance, it is worth to emphasize that interface engineering is highly required when applying a newly synthesized MHTs to PSCs. An appropriate functional group of the interface modifier is critical in achieving higher performance. Interface engineering with 2-TEAI containing thiophene group was found to improve PCE of the HL38-based PSC because of accelerating charge extraction and adequate band alignment for controlling charge transfer and recombination. This work highlights the importance of suppressing diffusion of Li ion in hole-transporting materials for improving thermal stability and an impact of molecularly interface design on the non-conventional MHTs.
