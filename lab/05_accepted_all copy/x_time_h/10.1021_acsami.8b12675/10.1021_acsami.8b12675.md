# Naphthodiperylenetetraimide-based Polymer as Electron-Transporting Material for Efficient Inverted Perovskite Solar Cells

Kui Jiang, $ ^{\dagger \P} $  Fei Wu, $ ^{*\dagger \P} $  Linna Zhu $ ^{* \dagger} $  and He Yan $ ^{* \ddagger} $

$  ^{\dagger}  $  Chongqing Key Laboratory for Advanced Materials and Technologies of Clean Energy, Faculty of Materials & Energy, Southwest University, Chongqing 400715, P.R.

$  ^{\dagger}  $  Department of Chemistry and Energy Institute, The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, Clear Water Bay, Hong Kong.

ABSTRACT: An n-type conjugated polymer NDP-V (poly(naphthodiperylenetetraimide-vinylene)) with a backbone of alternating naphthodiperylenetetraimide and vinylene is successfully used as an efficient electron transport layer (ETL) material in inverted planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs). It was found that device based on NDP-V exhibit maximum power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 16.54 %, while a maximum PCE of 15.27 % is obtained based on fullerene derivative [6,6]-phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (  $  PC_{61}BM  $  ). The interfacial effect induced by NDP-V is studied using atomic force microscopy (AFM) images, and NDP-V ensures a good selective contact between perovskite material and the metal electrode. # INTRODUCTION

In the past few years, the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved remarkable development, and perovskite solar cells have become a strong competitor among the next-generation solution-processed solar cells. Perovskite materials possess strong light harvesting ability, absorb in a wide range, long charge carrier diffusion lengths, low cost and facile preparation.  $  ^{1-5}  $   These merits make the organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite a shining star in the field of the opto-electronic materials. With the cooperation and interdisciplinary research in chemistry, materials and physics, to date, power conversion efficiency (PCE) exceeding 23 % has just been achieved, which is comparable to the commercially available silicon-based solar cells.  $  ^{6-10}  $   Such a rapid evolvement of PSCs is unprecedented, making PSCs the focus of the energy conversion community

Generally, three types of device structures in PSCs are mostly studied at present, that are, the mesoporous structure, planar structure and the inverted planar structure. The former two structures are known as the n-i-p device, in which metal oxides (TiO $ _2 $ , SnO $ _2 $ ) are usually employed as the electron transporting layer (ETL). $ ^{11-13} $  However, high sintering temperature of the metal oxides greatly limit the potential large-scale production and flexible application. While the inverted planar architecture (denoted as the p-i-n structure) can avoid the high temperature procedure, because both HTL and ETL are organic materials that are solution-processable. $ ^{14-16} $  Thus the inverted PSCs are promising in low-cost production and the flexible applications in future. In inverted PSCs, the electron transporting layer usually adopts organic materials like fullerene and its derivatives, as derived from the acceptor materials in organic solar cells. $ ^{17,18} $  Till now, the development of electron transport materials still lag behind the exploration on hole transport materials for the n-i-p devices. $ ^{19} $  Fullerene and its derivatives (PC $ _{61} $ BM and C $ _{60} $ ) are the

A qualified electron transporting material should have matched energy levels with perovskite materials, high electron mobility, and good solubility. Recently, organic molecules such as hexaazatrinaphtho[2,3-c][1,2,5]thiadiazole (HATNT), naphthalene imide (NDI) and hexaazatrinaphthylene (HATNA) derivatives were developed as ETMs and gained successes. $ ^{21-24} $  Apart from the abovementioned materials, perylenediimide (PDIs), with a rigid planar  $ \pi $ -conjugated structure and high electron affinity, has been well-known as a famous building block for n-type organic semiconductors. $ ^{25-27} $  The rigid and planar  $ \pi $ -skeleton facilitate ordered molecular packing, which is beneficial for charge transporting. NDP-V is a PDI-based conjugated polymer, and its backbone is composed of naphthodiperylenetetraimide and vinylene units (structure shown in Figure 1b). It was previously demonstrated as an excellent non-fullerene electron acceptor in high-performance all-polymer solar cells.  $  ^{34}  $   In NDP-V, the large polycyclic NDP units reduces the chain flexibility compared to common conjugated polymers containing PDI unit, moreover, the large rigid   $  \pi  $  -conjugation is beneficial to improve molecular packing. Its LUMO level was as deep as -4.03 eV, which was lower than that of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   and comparable with that of PCBM (Figure 1c). The electron mobility of NDP-V was calculated to be about   $  2.5 \times 10^{-3}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $  V  $  ^{-1}  $  s  $  ^{-1}  $  , as determined from the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method, and the value was comparable to that of PC  $  _{61}  $  BM (  $  2 \times 10^{-3}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $  ) measured by the same method.  $  ^{34}  $   Additionally, NDP-V exhibited adequate solubility in common organic solvents. Therefore, NDP-V has great potential to be applied in inverted PSCs for electron extraction and electron transporting.

Herein, NDP-V was developed as a non-fullerene electron transporting material in the p-i-n type perovskite solar cells. Its performance as ETL was evaluated, and was compared to that of the commonly used PC $ _{61} $ BM ETL. The device structure is ITO/P3CT-Na/perovskite/NDP-V/Rhodamine 101/LiF/Ag, as shown in Figure 1a. P3CT-Na is the hole transporting material, while Rhodamine 101/LiF works as the buffer layer. As a result, the device with the NDP-V ETL shows the maximum efficiency of 16.54 %, with a short-circuit current density ( $ J_{\text{sc}} $ ) of 21.41 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , an open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{\text{oc}} $ ) of 1043 mV, and a fill factor (FF) of 0.74. The performance of NDP-V is higher than the most popular PCBM ETL (15.27 %) under the same testing conditions. # EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

Materials: In this work, all major materials were purchased from commercial suppliers and used without further purification, including P3CT (Rieke Metals),   $  PbI_{2}  $   (p-OLED, >99.99 %), MAI (p-OLED, ≥99.5 %), PCBM (p-OLED, ≥99 %), C60 (p-OLED), BCP (p-OLED), Rhodamine 101 (Sigma-Aldrich), DMF (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.8 %), DMSO (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.8 %) and CB (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.8 %).

Device Fabrication: ITO-coated glass was sequentially washed by sonication using deionized water, ethanol and acetone. The hole transporting layer P3CT-Na was formed on ITO substrates by spin coating at 4000 rpm for 60 s followed by annealing at 140 °C for 30 min.  $  ^{35}  $   Then the samples were transferred into a   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glovebox. A   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   precursor solution (1.4 M in DMF:DMSO mixed solution with a v/v of 4:1) was spin-coated in a two-step program at 400 and 5000 rpm for 3 and 30 s, respectively. During the second step, 200 μL of chlorobenzene was dropped on the spinning substrate at 10 s after the start-up. Next, the as-spun perovskite layer was annealed on a hot plate at 60 °C for 1 min and at 80 °C for 2 min to drive off solvent and form the perovskite phase. Then, PCBM or NDP-V solution in chlorobenzene were spin-coated onto the perovskite layer. To fabricate the ITO/P3CT-Na/Perovskite/ETL/Rhodamine 101/LiF/Ag device, Rhodamine 101 was deposited by spin coating Rhodamine 101 solution (0.05 wt% in isopropanol) at 1000 rpm for 40 s onto the ITO/P3CT-Na/Perovskite/ETL substrate.  $  ^{36}  $   Afterwards, 1 nm thick LiF was thermally evaporated, and an approximately 100 nm thick Ag counter electrode was deposited on top to finish the device fabrication. The Rhodamine 101 and LiF layers work as the buffer layers between ETL and Ag electrode. To fabricate ITO/P3CT-Na/Perovskite/NDP-V interlayer/C60/BCP/Ag device, C60 (20 nm) and BCP (8 nm) were evaporated under high vacuum on top of the ITO/P3CT-Na/Perovskite/NDP-V substrate. Finally, a 100 nm thick Ag electrode was deposited through a shadow mask. The active area of our device is 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $

It has been widely recognized that the leading explanation for J-V hysteresis in PSCs is ion migration within perovskite.  $  ^{[18,22]}  $   Therefore, in our work, the ion migration causing hysteresis has been effectively suppressed. This work demonstrates the great potential of the PDI derivatives to replace the fullerene-based ETLs in inverted PSCs.
