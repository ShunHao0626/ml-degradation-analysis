# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are a promising photovoltaic technology for low-cost solar energy conversion due to their low-temperature solution process and high power conversion efficiencies (PCEs). Since an early report in 2009 [1], great efforts have been made so far in boosting the advancement of PSCs, and abundant achievements have been obtained [2–12]. The impressive PCE with a current record of 25.2% has been reached [12]. At the same time, researchers have demonstrated progress in improving the thermal and light stability of PSCs by using more stable precursors and robust hole/electron transport layers, etc. [2–6] All these achievements excited us and made us believe that the promising photovoltaic technology would become feasible for practical applications in the near future.

# ABSTRACT

Mixed tin (Sn)-lead (Pb) iodide perovskite is considered the most promising low-bandgap photovoltaic material for pursuing both low toxicity and high performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, their performance is still lagging behind the full Pb-based analogues. In PSCs, the hole transport layer (HTL) is one of the most important fundamentals to affect the device performance. Herein, the   $  (\mathrm{FASnI}_{3})_{0.6}(\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3})_{0.4}  $   PSCs with an inverted structure were successfully fabricated, where a bilayer structure of CuI/PEDOT:PSS was employed as HTL. It is observed that the CuI/PEDOT:PSS film has a smooth morphology with good interfacial contact and the introduced CuI can partly affect the crystallization behavior of   $  (\mathrm{FASnI}_{3})_{0.6}(\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3})_{0.4}  $   to facilitate high-quality perovskite film growth. Simultaneously, the cascading energy alignment in the device together with the high mobility of CuI provided the efficient hole extraction and transport from perovskite to anode. Besides, the photovoltaic performance was observed to be significantly dependent on the concentration of CuI that determines its thickness, as evidenced by the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy measurement. Consequently, by using a double-layer CuI/PEDOT:PSS as HTL with the CuI concentration of 10 mg ml  $  ^{-1}  $  , the fabricated PSCs based on 60% Sn perovskite lead to an improved champion power conversion efficiency of   $  \sim15.75\%  $   with negligible J-V hysteresis. Importantly, the double HTL of CuI/PEDOT:PSS makes the PSCs show improved stability, which is a critical characteristic for the advancement of PSCs.

optimization of the perovskite film morphology, use of a reducing agent and modification of the device structure, etc. [16-26] Note that interface engineering has always been an important strategy to improve the device performance in PSCs, and so far it remains an appealing means and a worthwhile challenge that applies to the research of Sn-based ones.

Recently, Sn-based PSCs with an inverted p-i-n planar structure have showed promising device performance   $  [21,25,26]  $  . Typically, the absorber layer of the photoactive material is sandwiched between poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene): poly-(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) as a hole transport layer (HTL) and fullerene as an electron transport layer (ETL). As for inverted device structure, the HTL not only plays a dramatic role in charge extraction, transport and suppressing the recombination between the interfaces, but also provides a protective barrier for the perovskite layer or transparent electrode to achieve high device stability. Although PEDOT:PSS is an efficient and commonly used HTL material in inverted PSCs   $  [27–29]  $  , it cannot be regarded as an ideal hole transport conductor owing to its inefficient electron-blocking capability. Besides, PEDOT:PSS has shown drawback towards long-term stability resulting from its hygroscopicity and acidity, based on the research of organic photovoltaic   $  [30–34]  $  . It is well known that inorganic semiconductors have much higher charge carrier mobility and superior stability compared to the organic materials, and there have been several reports about using CuI, CuO or NiO semiconductor as an alternative to PEDOT:PSS in inverted planar PSCs   $  [34–36]  $  . The PCEs of best-performing PSCs with these inorganic semiconductors were comparable to the performance of devices based on PEDOT:PSS   $  [34]  $  . Among these inorganic p-type semiconductors, CuI is a particularly promising material and have attracted great attention in PSCs due to its broad band gap (3.1 eV), appropriate energy levels, high hole mobility, low cost, good environmental stability and solution processibility   $  [31–34]  $  . Despite these advantages, CuI is not perfect because the corresponding devices are vulnerable to the charge recombination at the interface between perovskite and CuI. Therefore, great attempts still would be needed to avoid the adverse effect arising from CuI.

In this work, we have successfully employed a bilayer structure consisting of a combination of CuI and PEDOT:PSS as HTL to fabricate the  $ 60\% $  Sn-based inverted PSCs. The photovoltaic performance of devices with CuI/PEDOT:PSS as HTL in comparison with either CuI or PEDOT:PSS single layer was recorded. The insertion of an CuI layer between ITO (indium tin oxide) and PEDOT:PSS not only can facilitate the hole extraction and transport from perovskite to ITO electrode but can partly benefit the formation of dense perovskite film with good crystallization, leading to the higher  $ J_{sc} $  and FF values of resulting PSCs. Meanwhile, the PEDOT:PSS can mitigate the  $ V_{oc} $  loss of interfacial charge recombination arising from CuI. Besides, we observed the device performance strongly depends on the CuI thickness determined by solution concentration, which is closely related to the interfacial charge transport and recombination of devices. Finally, the partial Sn-based PSCs with CuI/PEDOT:PSS structure showed a promising PCE of  $ 15.75\% $  with negligible  $ J - V $  hysteresis. Moreover, these devices also exhibited good reproducibility and efficient improvement in stability.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Materials

Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly-(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT: PSS) (Clevious P VP AI 4083), PbI $ _2 $  (>99.99%), CH $ _3 $ NH $ _2 $ I (MAI, ≥99.5%), HC(NH $ _2 $ ) $ _2 $ I (FAI, ≥99.5%), C $ _{60} $  (99.9%), and BCP (99.9%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Co. (PLT). The SnI $ _2 $  (beads, -10 mesh, 99.99% trace metals basis) were purchased from Aldrich and SnF $ _2 $  (99%) was obtained from Acros. The CuI (>99.998% metal basis) was purchased from Alfa. Ultradry solvent of N, N-dimethyl formamide (DMF, >99.9%) and Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, >99.9%) were obtained from J&K. All the chemicals and solvents were kept in the

glove-box before starting our experiment.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

The pre-patterned ITO substrates were cleaned with detergent, deionized water, acetone, and 2-propanol in sequence. CuI was dissolved in acetonitrile and the concentration was 10 mg ml  $  ^{-1}  $  . Then, the CuI films were prepared by dropping CuI solution during the spin-coating at 4000 rpm for 30 s in a glove-box, and baked on the hot plate at 100 °C for 10 min. The PEDOT:PSS solution was filtered and spin-coated on ITO/CuI substrates at 4000 rpm for 50 s and the films were dried at 140 °C for 20 min. The FASnI  $  _{3}  $   precursor solution was prepared by dissolving 372 mg of SnI  $  _{2}  $   and 172 mg of FAI with 10 mol % (15.6 mg) of SnF  $  _{2}  $   in 440 μL DMF and 60 μL DMSO. The MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   precursor solution was prepared by dissolving 461 mg PbI  $  _{2}  $   and 159 mg MAI in 630 μL DMF and 70 μL DMSO. The (FASnI  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{0.6}  $  (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{0.4}  $   precursor solution was obtained by mixing stoichiometric amounts of FASnI  $  _{3}  $   and MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite precursors and was kept for 30 min before spin coating. After that, the (FASnI  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{0.6}  $  (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{0.4}  $   precursors were spin-coated onto PEDOT:PSS at 5000 rpm for 60 s. Toluene was dropped onto the spinning substrate during the spin-coating. The perovskite films were annealed at 100 °C for 7 min in a glove box. Finally, C  $  _{60}  $   (20 nm)/BCP (5 nm)/Cu (60 nm) were sequentially deposited by thermal evaporation through a shadow mask with an active area of 0.10 cm  $  ^{2}  $   or 1 cm  $  ^{2}  $  .

# 2.3. Device characterization

The current-voltage characteristics of solar cells were measured by a computer-controlled Keithley 2400 source meter measurement system with an AM1.5G filter at a calibrated intensity 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  illumination, as determined by a standard silicon reference cell (91150V Oriel Instruments). IPCE spectra was measured in air under short-circuit conditions using a commercial IPCE setup (Crowntech QTest Station 1000AD), which was equipped with a 100W Xe arc lamp, filter wheel, and monochromator. Monochromated light was chopped at a frequency of 80 Hz and photocurrents were measured using a lock-in amplifier. The setup was calibrated against a certified silicon reference diode. For transient photocurrent and photovoltage measurements, the system is similar to that reported previously [41].

# 2.4. Thin film characterization

Film thicknesses were measured using a Veeco Dektak 150 surface profilometer. UV–Vis–NIR spectra were obtained using a Shimadzu UV-2550 spectrophotometer. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were recorded on Rigaku SmartLab X-ray diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda = 1.5418 \AA  $  ) at room temperature. The data were collected with a 0.01° step size (2θ). A field emission scanning electron microscope (Hitachi SU8020) was used to acquire SEM images. A Dimension FastScan from BRUKER was used to acquire AFM images. For steady-state PL, a 532 nm cw laser beam at 110 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  was used as a source of excitation. The PL signal was detected by a Horiba Symphony-II CCD detector. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) was performed using a multi-technique surface analysis XPS/UPS system (VG Scienta R3000).

We can conclude that the  $ \mathrm{E_v} $  of PEDOT:PSS is much higher than the work function of ITO  $ (4.8\mathrm{eV}) $  and would create a high energy barrier between the ITO electrode and PEDOT:PSS [33]. The  $ \mathrm{E_v} $  of  $ 5.34\mathrm{eV} $  for CuI layer is more beneficial for improving the energy level alignment at the interface of ITO and PEDOT:PSS, where it can form an Ohmic contact and facilitate the hole transportation and collection. However, the  $ \mathrm{E_v} $  position of CuI also means there may be more energy loss in the open circuit voltage of devices [37]. In addition, the higher conduction band  $ (\mathrm{E_c}) $  of CuI layer  $ (-2.24\mathrm{eV}) $  ensures it could block electrons from the perovskite effectively while the electron blocking capability of PEDOT: PSS is inadequate [33,34]. Unfortunately, it is difficult for us to know the exact band edge position of  $ (\mathrm{FASnI}_3)_{0.6}(\mathrm{MAPbI}_3)_{0.4} $  perovskite, as has been reported. The facile oxidation of  $ \mathrm{Sn^{2 + }} $  to  $ \mathrm{Sn^{4 + }} $  could easily alter the electronic property of surface and affect the accuracy of UPS measurement [26].

To understand the effect of interfacial layers on device performance, the morphologies of the different HTLs were observed by atomic force microscopy (AFM). The surface topographies of CuI, PEDOT:PSS, PEDOT:PSS/CuI and CuI/PEDOT:PSS were determined by AFM as shown in Fig. 3a-(d). The root mean square (RMS) roughness of CuI layer was  $ 7.81\mathrm{nm} $  that may be due to the formation of large CuI crystalline grains, while PEDOT:PSS film exhibited a smooth surface with the RMS roughness of only  $ 1.01\mathrm{nm} $ . The flat surface of PEDOT:PSS layer can ensure there is a good interface contact and it must be beneficial in reducing the charge recombination derived from interfacial trap states over the photophysical process of PSC [39]. In this case, the difference of surface morphological structure combined with that of energy level of  $ \mathrm{E_v} $  between CuI and PEDOT:PSS nicely explain the variation in  $ V_{oc} $  values in the corresponding PSCs. In addition, when PEDOT:PSS was covered with CuI layer, the surface morphology was similar to that of single CuI layer and the RMS roughness can reach up to  $ 8.58\mathrm{nm} $ , which is most likely to cause decrease to the  $ V_{oc} $  value and shunt resistance of devices originating from the interfacial leakage current. Noticeably, CuI/PEDOT:PSS film exhibited a blend morphology featured with CuI and PEDOT:PSS, and the RMS roughness was as low as  $ 2.04\mathrm{nm} $ . It might be due to the fact that the PEDOT:PSS filled the interstices of CuI crystalline grains well, so the device can form a relatively good interface contact to minimize the  $ V_{oc} $  loss caused by charge recombination.

Therefore, the high-quality perovskite film derived from CuI-included HTLs is also responsible for the improvement in   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF values by promoting the charge dissociation, transport, and diffusion length.

The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) measurement was conducted to explore the effect of CuI inserted layer on the extraction of photo-generated carriers from the perovskite absorber. Fig. 6 shows the PL spectra of  $ (\mathrm{FASnI}_3)_{0.6}(\mathrm{MAPbI}_3)_{0.4} $  films on the following different substrates: glass/CuI, glass/PEDOT:PSS, glass/PEDOT:PSS/CuI and glass/CuI/PEDOT:PSS. The PL signal at  $ 996~\mathrm{nm} $  is assigned to the emission from  $ (\mathrm{FASnI}_3)_{0.6}(\mathrm{MAPbI}_3)_{0.4} $  [26]. Obviously, CuI-introduced HTL showed more efficient PL quenching than the alone PEDOT:PSS film obtained from the steady-state PL, especially in the case of CuI/-PEDOT:PSS double HTL. Based on that the PL quenching is originated from the carrier extraction across the interface between  $ (\mathrm{FASnI}_3)_{0.6}(\mathrm{MAPbI}_3)_{0.4} $  and HTLs [6,31]. The lower PL intensity means that the carrier extraction is the most efficient for CuI/PEDOT:PSS HTL, which ascribes to both the high mobility of CuI and the substantially improved energy level alignment between ITO electrode and PEDOT:PSS. This result is coincident with the higher  $ J_{sc} $  and FF values of the CuI/PEDOT:PSS-based devices.

The long-term stability of solar cells is a critical parameter for the practical application, especially in the case of Sn-based PSCs. The widely used PEDOT:PSS shows the nature of acidity and hydrophilicity, which is unfavorable for the device stability   $  [31-34]  $  . However, the environmentally stable and hydrophobic CuI would be more favorable in terms of long-term stability of solar cells. We tracked the device performance in the glove-box of   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere without encapsulation. As clearly seen in Fig. 11, the PSC device based on CuI/PEDOT:PSS HTL have shown better durability compared to that based on single-layer PEDOT:PSS. After the storage of 600 h, the CuI/PEDOT:PSS based device still maintained 95% of the initial efficiency, which was higher than the PEDOT:PSS-based one. The improved stability of PSC devices with a CuI/PEDOT:PSS structure might be interpreted by that the inserted CuI layer not only protected ITO from the corrosion of PEDOT:PSS but weakened the hygroscopicity of PEDOT:PSS to some extent   $  [31-34]  $  .

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we have successfully employed a bilayer structure of CuI/PEDOT:PSS as HTL to fabricate the  $ 60\% $  Sn-based PSCs with an inverted structure. The CuI/PEDOT:PSS film has a smooth morphology and the introduced CuI can partly affect the crystallization behavior of  $ (\mathrm{FASnI}_3)_{0.6}(\mathrm{MAPbI}_3)_{0.4} $  to yield compact perovskite films with good crystallization and preferred orientation. Moreover, the ITO/CuI/PEDOT:PSS can form a desired band alignment, and that together with the high mobility of CuI facilitate the hole extraction and transport from

The CuI concentration was also observed to be a crucial factor to determine the device performance, as evidenced by the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy measurement. With the CuI of 10 mg ml  $  ^{-1}  $  , the partial Sn-based PSCs consisting of a bistratal CuI/PEDOT:PSS delivered the champion efficiency of 15.75%. Importantly, the inverted Sn-based PSCs exhibited improved durability. This work demonstrates a double hole-transport layer strategy toward tin-lead mixed PSCs with excellent photovoltaic performance and device stability.
