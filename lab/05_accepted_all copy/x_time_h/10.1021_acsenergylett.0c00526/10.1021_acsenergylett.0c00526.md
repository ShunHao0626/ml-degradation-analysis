# ABSTRACT

Maintaining the stability of tin halide perovskites is a major challenge in developing lead-free perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Adding extra   $  SnX_{2}  $   (X=F, Cl, Br) in the precursor solution to inhibit   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation is an essential strategy to improve the device efficiency and stability. However,   $  SnX_{2}  $   on the surface of perovskite grains tends to prohibit charge transfer across perovskite films. Here, we report a coadditive engineering approach by introducing antioxidant gallic acid (GA) together with   $  SnCl_{2}  $   to improve the performance of tin-based PSCs. The   $  SnCl_{2}-GA  $   complex can not only protect the perovskite grains but also more effectively conduct electrons across it, leading to highly stable and efficient PSCs. The unencapsulated devices can maintain   $  \sim80\%  $   of its initial efficiency after 1000 h storage in ambient air with a relative humidity of 20%, which is the best air stability that can be achieved in tin-based PSCs until now.

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted much attention due to their rapidly improved power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 3.8% to 25.2% in the past decade. $ ^{1-6} $  Generally, PSCs reported so far are mainly based on perovskite materials in a formula of ABX $ _3 $ , where A is a monovalent organic or inorganic cation (e.g., CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3^+ $  (MA $ ^+ $ ), CH(NH $ _2 $ ) $ _2^+ $  (FA $ ^+ $ ), Cs $ ^+ $ ), B is an octahedrally coordinated divalent metal cation (e.g., Pb $ ^{2+} $  or Sn $ ^{2+} $ ) and X is a halide anion (Cl $ ^- $ , Br $ ^- $ , I $ ^- $ ). $ ^{7-8} $  Although organolead halide perovskites are employed in almost all of the top-performing PSCs due to their excellent optoelectronic properties, the toxicity of lead may prohibit the commercialization of the PSCs. $ ^{9-12} $  Therefore, many environmentally friendly cations, such as tin, $ ^{13-14} $  germanium, $ ^{15-16} $  bismuth, $ ^{17} $  and copper, $ ^{18} $  have been explored as alternatives to lead. Specifically, tin is the most promising candidate since tin halide perovskite shows a suitable bandgap (E $ _g $ ) of 1.2–1.4 eV and a high charge mobility. $ ^{19} $  In 2014, Snaith's group first reported MASnI $ _3 $  solar cell with a record efficiency of 6.4%. $ ^{20} $  Kanatzidis's group engineered the bandgap of MASnI $ _3 $  through chemical substitution of I with Br to obtain MASnI $ _{3-x} $ Br $ _x $  and realized a PCE of 5.73%. $ ^{21} $  A recent study reported FASnI $ _3 $ -based PSCs with a PCE of 9.0%, which contains trace amount of layered tin perovskite in 3D FASnI $ _3 $  perovskite. $ ^{22} $  Besides relatively low efficiency, poor long-term stability is a more serious problem for tin-based PSCs in practical applications.

The limited efficiency and poor stability of tin-based PSCs are originated from the chemical instability of   $  Sn^{2+}  $  , which tends to convert into more thermodynamically stable   $  Sn^{4+}  $  .  $  ^{23-25}  $   Consequently, the concentrations of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   vacancies in tin-based perovskites are increased, leading to high levels of self p-doping and high densities of trap states. To mitigate   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation, the introduction of extra   $  SnX_{2}  $   (X=F, Cl, Br) additive becomes a prevailing method in the deposition of tin-based perovskite films.  $  ^{26-27}  $     $  SnX_{2}  $   acts as both a reducing agent to prevent   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation and a tin compensator to supply the missing   $  Sn^{2+}  $  .  $  ^{19}  $   However, excess   $  SnX_{2}  $   presents as a second phase

Thus, the secondary additive plays an important role on the efficiency and stability of tin-based PSCs.

In this work, we introduced antioxidant gallic acid (GA) as the secondary additive to assist the fabrication of   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite films containing excess   $  SnCl_{2}  $  . GA can restrict   $  SnCl_{2}  $   aggregation through the formation of a   $  SnCl_{2}  $  -GA complex that envelops the perovskite grain surface. As a result, the inner perovskite is protected from oxidation and less defects are generated in the films. The resultant tin-based PSCs with an inverted planar architecture show PCE up to 9.03%. In comparison with other additives, we find that GA can decrease the conduction band level and enhance electron transfer in the SnCl $ _2 $ -additive complex covering the surface of FASnI $ _3 $  perovskite grains, which benefit the improved efficiency in our devices.

PSCs based on   $  FASnI_{3}  $   with an inverted structure shown in Figure 1a were prepared by solution process. GA was introduced in the active layer as an additive to form a   $  SnCl_{2}-GA  $   complex that can envelop   $  FASnI_{3}  $   grains. As a strong chelating agent and antioxidant, GA can not only modulate the surface morphology of   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite film but also retard   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation. To evaluate the antioxidant effect of GA on tin components, we prepared   $  FASnI_{3}  $   precursor solutions with different additive compositions (w/o additives; with   $  SnCl_{2}  $  ; and with   $  SnCl_{2}  $   and GA) and stored them in air for comparison. All the solutions showed the similar yellow color initially, as shown in Figure S1 in the Supporting Information. After being exposed to air for 4 min, the solution without additives quickly turned red as a result of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation to   $  Sn^{4+}  $ , whereas the color of the solution containing   $  SnCl_{2}  $   did not change too much. The solution with both GA and   $  SnCl_{2}  $   had no change in its color. With the increase of exposure time, all the solutions suffered from discoloration. Notably, the rate of discoloration of the solution containing both   $  SnCl_{2}  $   and GA was apparently slower compared to the solution with only   $  SnCl_{2}  $   additive. This result suggests the potential of GA as a secondary antioxidant to defer   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation in   $  FASnI_{3}  $   precursor solution. The antioxidant property of GA lies in the reducing hydroxyl groups (-OH) attached to the aromatic ring. When the sample with GA additive is exposed to air, -OH can effectively scavenge oxygen by donating hydrogen atoms and electrons, and finally GA is transferred to quinones derivatives, as illustrated in Figure 1b.  $  ^{35}  $   Hence, GA can continuously scavenge oxygen and provide efficient protection of tin-based perovskite against oxidative damage.

The perovskite films were fabricated via a one-step spin coating method from precursors of   $  FASnI_{3}  $   in a mixture DMF/DMSO solution with 7 mol% excess   $  SnCl_{2}  $   and different amounts of GA additive (Figure 1c), followed by thermal annealing at 70 °C. Due to the Lewis acidity of   $  SnCl_{2}  $  , it is inclined to accept lone pairs from donors (such as O atoms in GA) and coordinate with GA. Since GA and   $  SnCl_{2}  $   both possess melting points higher than 200 °C, we consider that GA may interact with   $  SnCl_{2}  $   and form a   $  SnCl_{2}-GA  $   complex in the final perovskite films, as schematically shown in Figure 1d. Therefore, GA can modulate the surface morphology of   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite films in the presence of   $  SnCl_{2}  $  .

To illustrate the effect of GA on film morphology, we compare the scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of FASnI $ _3 $  films with SnCl $ _2 $  and GA additives (FASnI $ _3 $ :SnCl $ _2 $ :GA=1:0.07:x, x=0, 0.01,0.02 or 0.03). For the perovskite film deposited from the solution with only SnCl $ _2 $  additive, excess SnCl $ _2 $  tends to aggregate on the film surface and form many needle-like objects, as shown in Figure 1c and Figure 1e. Upon addition of 1mol % GA to the above solution, the fabricated FASnI $ _3 $  film becomes much smoother with the aggregates almost eliminated (Figure 1c, f). However, with increasing addition of GA, the perovskite grain size decreases slightly while the distance between individual grains becomes larger (Figure 1g, h). We speculate that excess GA might impede FASnI $ _3 $  crystallization and growth by interacting with Sn $ ^{2+} $  in the perovskite component and ultimately remain at the grain boundaries and surfaces. $ ^{36} $

PSCs with a device structure of ITO/NiO $ _{x} $ /FASnI $ _{3} $ /PCBM/BCP/Ag were fabricated and the thickness of each layers were characterized by cross-sectional SEM as shown in Figure 2a. It is worth noting that SnCl $ _{2} $  is used as the basic additive with its amount optimized to be 7 mol%, as shown in Figure S7. Figure 2b shows the J–V curves of the best performing PSCs with the introduction of 0, 0.5, 1, 2, and 3 mol% GA. Notably, the device with the GA addition of 1 mol% demonstrates the best performance. It is obvious that EQE value is increased by GA additive in all the wavelength region. The integrated J $ _{sc} $  for the GA incorporated device is 19.82 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , closely matching the measured value from the J–V curve. The PCE statistical histogram of 20 devices w/o and with  $ 1\% $  GA is presented in Figure 2d, which verifies the reproducibility of the overall PCE improvement by the GA additive.

The statistical photovoltaic parameters for all conditions are shown in Table 1. The devices with 1 mol% GA addition exhibit an average PCE of 8.32%, which is much higher than that of the control devices (2.82%). This significant improvement in PCE after the addition of GA is due to the obvious increase in   $  V_{oc}  $   from 0.28V to 0.61V and FF from 53.9% to 69.4%. The higher   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF can be attributed to the improved surface morphology of the perovskite film, as observed from the SEM images in Figure 1e-h. Generally, Voc loss can be caused by the non-radiative recombination at trap states within the perovskite bulk or at interfaces.  $  ^{37}  $   Since GA acts as a strong antioxidant distributed on the surface of perovskite grains, GA can protect the perovskite from oxidation and inhibit the generation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   vacancy defects within the perovskite grain. Moreover,   $  SnCl_{2}  $   aggregates could also affect charge collection by lowering the contact area with the Ag electrode. By coupling   $  SnCl_{2}  $   with GA, the   $  FASnI_{3}/PCBM  $   interface is smoothened and   $  SnCl_{2}  $   aggregation is reduced. So the addition of 1 mol% GA can increase   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF of the devices. However, with further increase of the GA concentration to more than 2%, the device PCE and   $  J_{sc}  $   are significantly decreased, which can be attributed to the decreased crystallization of the perovskite film induced by GA. The performance of devices with only GA additive was also investigated. The inferior results shown in Figure S9 indicate that   $  SnCl_{2}  $   is needed as a moderator for optimal performance.

The TRPL spectra of the films are presented in Figure 3b. In summary, GA additive together with excess   $  SnCl_{2}  $   has been introduced into   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite films to improve the device photovoltaic performance for the first time. This coadditive engineering approach enables capping of the perovskite grains with an amorphous   $  SnCl_{2}-GA  $   complex and leads to suppressed   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation and defect densities in the perovskite films. Consequently, the performance of the resultant PSCs is substantially enhanced. We also notice that the large bandgap of   $  SnCl_{2}  $   prohibits the transfer of both electrons and holes from   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite to charge transport layers while the barrier height for electron transfer is dramatically decreased by formation of complex with GA.
