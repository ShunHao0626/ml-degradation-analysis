# New Strategy for Two-Step Sequential Deposition: Incorporation of Hydrophilic Fullerene in Second Precursor for High-Performance p-i-n Planar Perovskite Solar Cells

Guiying Xu, Rongming Xue, Weijie Chen, Jingwen Zhang, Moyao Zhang, Haiyang Chen, Chaohua Cui, Hongkun Li, Yaowen Li,* and Yongfang Li*

# 1. Introduction

In p-i-n planar perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) based on methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI $ _3 $ ) perovskite, high-quality MAPbI $ _3 $  film, perfect interfacial band alignment and efficient charge extracting ability are critical for high photovoltaic performance. In this work, a hydrophilic fullerene derivative [6,6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid-(3,4,5-tris(2-(2-(2-methoxyethoxy)ethoxy)ethoxy)phenyl)methanol ester (PCBB-OEG) is introduced as additive in the methylammonium iodide precursor solution in the preparation of MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite film by two-step sequential deposition method, and obtained a top-down gradient distribution with an ultrathin top layer of PCBB-OEG. Meanwhile, a high-quality perovskite film with high crystallinity, less trap-states, and dense-grained uniform morphology can well grow on both hydrophilic (poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)/poly(styrenesulfonic acid)) and hydrophobic (polytriarylamine, PTAA) hole transport layers. When the PCBB-OEG-containing perovskite film (pero-0.1) is prepared in a p-i-n planar pero-SC with the configuration of ITO/PTAA/pero-0.1/[6,6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester/Al, the device delivers a promising power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 20.2\% $  without hysteresis, which is one of the few PCE over  $ 20\% $  for the p-i-n planar pero-SCs. Importantly, the pero-0.1-based device shows an excellent stability that can retain  $ 98.4\% $  of its initial PCE after being exposed for  $ 300\mathrm{h} $  under ambient atmosphere with a high humidity, and the flexible pero-SCs based on pero-0.1 also demonstrate a promising PCE of  $ 18.1\% $ .

The two-step deposition method represents a more efficient and controllable solution process,  $  ^{[16]}  $   in which methylammonium iodide (MAI) layer and   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer are deposited from their precursors separately. In order to address these issues, the strategies such as volume expansion-adjustable   $  PbI_{2}\cdot xMAI  $   (x = 0.1–0.3) precursor,  $  ^{[18]}  $   amorphous   $  PbI_{2}  $   film,  $  ^{[19]}  $   and bath immersion  $  ^{[20]}  $   were used to accelerate the conversion of   $  PbI_{2}  $ . Unfortunately, the limited PCEs in the range of 14–18% indicate that the quality of perovskite film as well as the band alignment need to be further improved.

Recently, several methods were developed to improve the quality of the perovskite films. Among them, incorporating an additive, such as inorganic or ammonium salts,  $  ^{[21,22]}  $   organic molecules,  $  ^{[23]}  $   and ionic liquids,  $  ^{[24,25]}  $   in the precursor solution (in N,N-dimethylformamide, DMF or dimethyl sulfoxide, DMSO solvent) is demonstrated as one of the most effective strategies. It can be used to provide homogenous nucleation, slow-down the crystallization rate, and change the surface energy for forming a uniform perovskite film with enlarged crystals and controllable crystal growth directions. However, there are few reports of applying these additives in the second step precursor of the two-step deposition method. This is probably because the additives are not soluble in the second precursor isopropanol (IPA) solvent. Table S1 (Supporting Information) summarized the reported top 20 PCEs of the planar pero-SCs for the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   system. It can be found that the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film by combining antisolvent and other treatments leads the state-of-the-art devices with the highest PCEs around 20% in p-i-n planar pero-SCs for the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   system.  $  ^{[13,14]}  $   Unfortunately, the antisolvent method needs more stringent processing and toxic solvent, which are unfavorable for large-area solar cell fabrication and device reproducibility.  $  ^{[29]}  $   Nevertheless, we can conclude that all previous studies only focusing on one method of additive, antisolvent, and solvent annealing could not meet the multiple demands, including morphological, crystallization, trap-states, and band alignment properties, for realizing super high-performance perovskite thin films. Therefore, it is very challenging and pressing to explore a facile and universal method to simultaneously meet above demands.

In this work, we synthesized a hydrophilic fullerene derivative [6,6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid-(3,4,5-tris(2-(2-(2-methoxy-ethoxy)ethoxy)ethoxy)phenyl)methanol ester (PCBB-OEG) (as shown in Figure 1a) and first used it as an additive of second precursor solution containing MAI (in IPA solvent) in the two-step deposition method. We found that the PCBB-OEG additive in the second precursor can effectively diffuse into the pre-deposited  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ :MAI film forming a top-down PCBB-OEG gradient distribution, which can not only act as a soft-template to assist the growth of high-quality  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  crystals but also can benefit band alignment in the device. More importantly, this new two-step deposition strategy provides a facile way with high

reproducibility and post-treatments free such as antisolvent, solvent annealing and additional interface engineering. In order to achieve fully low-temperature fabrication process and high-performance pero-SC, we chose p-i-n planar device structure with commercial poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)/poly(styrenesulfonic acid) (PEDOT:PSS) or polytriarylamine (PTAA) as hole transport layers (HTLs) and PCBM as electron transport layer (ETL), respectively. The pero-SCs based on the PCBB-OEG-containing MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  with PTAA HTL showed a high PCE of 20.2% with negligible hysteresis and excellent device stability under ambient atmosphere with a high humidity. As shown in Table S1 (Supporting Information), for the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  system, 20.2% is comparable with the highest reported PCEs of p-i-n (20.3%) and n-i-p (20.23%) planar pero-SCs to date. This photoactive layer structure was also prepared on flexible substrates and the flexible pero-SCs also demonstrated a promising PCE of 18.1%.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Preparation of the Fullerene Derivative-Containing Perovskite Films by Two-Step Method

The synthetic route of hydrophilic PCBB-OEG is shown in Figure 1a. PCBB-OEG was synthesized by the reaction between fullerene acid (PCBA) and (3,4,5-tris(2-(2-(2-methoxyethoxy) ethoxy)ethoxy)phenyl)methanol (compound 3) in ortho-dichlorobenzene (o-DCB) using 4-dimethylamino-pyridine (DMAP), p-toluenesulfonamide (p-TSA), and diisopropylcarbinol (DIPC) as catalysts, delivering a 51.2% yield. PCBB-OEG shows high thermal stability with decomposition temperature of ≈349.3 °C (5% weight loss) from thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA, Figure S1, Supporting Information) and amorphous nature from DSC measurement (no  $  T_g  $  was detected as shown in Figure 1b). These results suggest that the hydrophilic PCBB-OEG is beneficial for enduring high temperature and reducing aggregation at elevated temperature.[30] Detailed synthetic procedure and characterization are described in the Experimental Section and Figures S2–S4 (Supporting Information), respectively,

The electronic energy levels of PCBB-OEG were measured by electrochemical cyclic voltammetry with  $ \mathrm{Ag / Ag^{+}} $  as reference electrode and  $ \mathrm{Fc / Fc^{+}} $  couple as an internal reference.[31] As shown in Figure 1c, PCBB-OEG shows a similar onset reduction potential  $ (E_{\mathrm{red}}) $  compared to that of PCBM, resulting in a comparable the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy level of PCBB-OEG and PCBM. Moreover, UV-vis absorption spectrum of PCBB-OEG film is nearly the same as that of PCBM (Figure S5, Supporting Information), indicating similar HOMO energy levels  $ (E_{\mathrm{HOMO}}) $  of the two fullerene derivatives. Thus, it appears that the oligo(ethylene glycol) (OEG) group attached to PCBB-OEG has little effect on the  $ E_{\mathrm{HOMO}} $  and  $ E_{\mathrm{LUMO}} $ . This result is further confirmed by the almost same oxidation/re-reduction reversibility of PCBB-OEG in the cyclic voltammograms (Figure 1c). In order to demonstrate the feasibility of using PCBB-OEG as an additive in the MAI precursor (IPA solution), we tested the solubility of PCBB-OEG and PCBM in IPA solvent, respectively.

We first investigate the composition of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film fabricated by the modified two-step sequential deposition method from the PCBB-OEG added second precursor solution, and the processing steps are illustrated in Figure 2a. In nitrogen glovebox, a  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  layer was first prepared by spin-coating  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ :MAI (1.3 M:0.3 M) precursor solution on the bottom PTAA/ITO electrode. Then the MAI precursor solution in IPA containing different concentration of PCBB-OEG (0.01, 0.1, and  $ 1\mathrm{wt}\% $  ) was spin-coated on top of the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ :MAI film. After that, the samples were treated by thermal annealing immediately in nitrogen atmosphere to form perovskite films. Here, for the first time, a fullerene derivative (hydrophilic PCBB-OEG) was introduced into the MAI precursor solution in IPA, and the amount of PCBB-OEG in the perovskite film was controlled by regulating the concentration of PCBB-OEG in the MAI precursor solution. In the following, we define the samples as pero-0.01, pero-0.1, and pero-1 for the perovskite films prepared by spin-coating the MAI precursor solutions with the concentration of PCBB-OEG at 0.01, 0.1, and  $ 1\mathrm{wt}\% $ , respectively. And we define the perovskite film without PCBB-OEG as the sample of pero-pristine.

To validate incorporation of PCBB-OEG in perovskite film, time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectroscopy (ToF-SIMS) measurement was performed to monitor the depth distribution of   $  O^{-}  $  and   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  ions in the perovskite film. The spatial distribution of   $  O^{-}  $  and   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  ions is associated with the distribution of PCBB-OEG molecules and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ , respectively. It should be noted that   $  O_{2}  $   was incorporated in the perovskite film during the sample preparation and transfer for test, thus the weak   $  O^{-}  $  signals from PCBB-OEG molecule in the pero-0.01 and pero-0.1 perovskite films are hard to be distinguished in mass spectroscopy. Therefore, we used pero-1 with higher concentration of PCBB-OEG for analysis. As shown in Figure 2b, the   $  O^{-}  $  depth profile of the pero-1 film exhibited a continuous and gradual decrease with a significant spatial overlap on the   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  profile, in which the extremely high intensity of the   $  O^{-}  $  signal at the top region of pero-1 film is originated from the absorbing   $  O_{2}  $   as confirmed by the pristine perovskite film (Figure S6a, Supporting Information). The dramatically increased intensity of   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  after a short initial sputtering time (0–1.8 s) reveals that there is a several nanometer PCBB-OEG film coated on top of the perovskite film. Similar result with slightly increased intensity of   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  was also obtained in pero-0.1 film, as shown in Figure S6b (Supporting Information), indicating an even thinner PCBB-OEG film formed on top of the perovskite layer.

# 2.2. X-Ray Diffraction (XRD) Patterns and Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) Morphology of the Perovskite Films

We then studied the effect of PCBB-OEG concentration on the growth of perovskite film. The crystal quality and morphologies of the perovskite film were investigated by XRD analysis. Figure 2c shows the XRD patterns of pero-pristine, pero-0.01, pero-0.1, and pero-1 grown on PTAA. All the samples exhibit distinctive diffraction peaks at 14.2°, 28.5°, and 32.0°, assigned to the (110), (220), and (310) lattice planes of the cubic perovskite structure, respectively,  $  ^{[9]}  $  and show almost same absorption spectra (Figure S5, Supporting Information). These results indicate that the blended PCBB-OEG in the MAI precursor solution has little effect on the formation of perovskite crystal structure. In addition, there is no impurity peak in the XRD patterns of all the samples with or without PCBB-OEG except the peak of excess  $  PbI_{2}  $  at 12.6°, indicating that there is no

crystal structure change in the perovskite films when incorporating PCBB-OEG.  $  ^{[32]}  $   The weaker intensity of   $  PbI_{2}  $   peak in pero-0.1 film suggests lower   $  PbI_{2}  $   content in the perovskite photoactive layer, which is beneficial for improving the perovskite photovoltaic performance due to its passivation effect at grain boundaries that reduces charge recombination.  $  ^{[33]}  $   One striking change of the XRD patterns is the intensities of the (110) and (220) peaks. With increasing the concentration of PCBB-OEG from 0.01 wt% in pero-0.01 to 0.1 wt% in pero-0.1, the intensities of both (110) and (220) diffraction peaks enhanced, suggesting the crystallinity of the resulting perovskite films were improved.  $  ^{[34]}  $   Whereas the intensities of (110) and (220) diffraction peaks were dramatically decreased with further increasing the concentration of PCBB-OEG for pero-1. Meanwhile, the intensity ratios of (110)/(310) and (220)/(310) peaks showed the similar tendency (see Figure 2d)  $  ^{[6]}  $   as that of the (110) and (220) peak intensities, indicating a strong grain orientation in pero-0.1 film. We speculate that a small amount of PCBB-OEG (from 0.01 to 0.1 wt%) in the MAI precursor could play a soft-template for the growth of the perovskite films. However, further increasing the content of PCBB-OEG from 0.1 to 1 wt% makes the (110) and (220) peak intensities of pero-1 decrease significantly (see Figure 2c,d), due probably to the excess PCBB-OEG hindered the formation of perovskite crystal as that reported for   $  \alpha  $  -bis-PCBM-doped perovskite film.  $  ^{[28]}  $

# 2.3. Photovoltaic Performance of the p-i-n Planar Pero-SCs

We constructed p-i-n planar pero-SCs using pero-0.1 as photoactive layer with a device configuration of ITO/PTAA or PEDOT:PSS/pero-0.1/PCBM/Al, as illustrated in Figure 4a. We used pero-0.1 in the pero-SCs because pero-0.1 showed the best crystalline structure and morphology. Figure 4b provides the corresponding energy-level diagram. We can see that the perovskite films with PCBB-OEG as additive do not change the overall energetic landscape, but the top-down fullerene gradient distribution will certainly benefit the electron transfer process of the devices.

The surface potentials of pero-0.1 and the control peropristine films were measured by Kelvin probe force microscope (KPFM), and the observed variation in the surface potential is

proportional to the work function.  $  ^{[40]}  $   As shown in Figure 4c, the average surface potential (-114 mV) of pero-0.1 film is upshifted by   $  \approx  $  190 mV in comparison to that (  $  \approx  $  303 mV) of the pero-pristine film,  $  ^{[41]}  $   that is, a lower work function (lowered by   $  \approx  $  190 meV) at the surface of pero-0.1 film than that of peropristine film. This indicates a reduced potential barrier for electron transport between pero-0.1 and PCBM. It is also worth noticing that the formation of ultrathin hydrophilic PCBB-OEG (with a strong polar OEG moiety) layer on top of the pero-0.1 film can produce additional surface dipole, which can lower the cathode work function, thus resulting in an improved band alignment.  $  ^{[42]}  $   In addition, the dipole moment of PCBB-OEG perpendicularly point toward planar perovskite film,  $  ^{[43]}  $   which can further enhance the built-in electric field of device.  $  ^{[44]}  $

Figure 4d shows the cross-section SEM image of the pero-SC based on pero-0.1. It can be seen that all functional layers can be clearly distinguished, where the uniform perovskite crystals form a condensed packing with a thickness of  $ \approx 400~\mathrm{nm} $ . In contrast, the cross-sectional SEM image of the control device without PCBB-OEG additive (see Figure S7, Supporting Information) displays an inhomogeneous  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer with more grain boundaries. The result is in good agreement with the corresponding top-view SEM images (Figure 3a,c).

In order to further look into the large enhancement of PCE for the pero-SCs based on pero-0.1, we investigated the charge dynamics by measuring steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL). # 2.5. Stability of the p-i-n Planar Pero-SCs

Stability of perovskite is a critical issue for the application of pero-SCs. Recently, tremendous efforts have been devoted to improve the stability of the perovskite solar cells. For example, both high-quality electron-beam-evaporated   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL  $  ^{[56]}  $   and bilayer spiro-OMeTAD/PbS HTL  $  ^{[57]}  $   have been demonstrated to dramatically enhance the stability of pero-SCs. Here, we evaluated the device stability in an ambient environment by fabricating the pero-SCs with the device structure of ITO/PTAA/pero-pristine or pero-0.1/PCBM/  $  C_{60}  $  /BCP/Cu. It is noted that inert Cu top cathode and   $  C_{60}  $  /BCP bilayer ETL were used instead of Al, which can effectively restrain reactivity between cathode/perovskite film.  $  ^{[58]}  $   The unencapsulated devices were stored under ambient atmosphere with a 70–80% relative humidity (RH). As shown in Figure 8a, after 300 h exposure, the pero-0.1-based device retained almost 98.4% of the initial PCEs, while the PCE of the pero-pristine based device degraded to 88.1% of its initial value. The promising stability of pero-0.1-based device reveals that the perovskite film doped with 0.1 wt% PCBB-OEG exhibits a much stronger resistance to water and oxygen in ambient atmosphere than that of the pristine sample.

Chemical decomposition in a humid environment is the main degradation pathway for the MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite materials. In order to study the effect of humid environment on the stability of the PCBB-OEG-doped perovskite films, we prepared pero-0.01, pero-0.1, pero-1, and control pero-pristine on PTAA without coating PCBM, and exposed them to ambient atmosphere. As shown in Figure 8b, the color of the control peropristine film changed from black to yellow after storage in the 70–80% RH for 14 d, indicating almost complete decomposition of MAPbI $ _3 $  in the pero-pristine sample. For the pero-0.01 film, there are some black regions left after 14 d, which indicates that the stability of the pero-0.01 film was improved a little in comparison with that of the pero-pristine sample. Interestingly, there is almost no obvious color change after 14 d for the pero-0.1 and pero-1 films, reviewing that their stability was greatly improved by doping higher concentrations of PCBB-OEG in the perovskite films. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, we have synthesized a hydrophilic fullerene derivative PCBB-OEG by attaching three oligo(ethylene glycol) moieties on a stable fullerene skeletons of[6,6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $ . And PCBB-OEG was used as an additive in the second-MAI precursor solution for the two-step fabrication of pero-SCs. We have demonstrated that the PCBB-OEG additive in the second precursor can effectively diffuse into the pre-deposited  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ :MAI film, and act as a soft-template to assist the growth of high-quality  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  crystals. Importantly, the method can be used to prepare high-quality perovskite films on both hydrophobic PTAA and hydrophilic PEDOT:PSS HTLs. This strategy allows us to simultaneously address several major challenges, including constructing a top-down gradient distribution fullerene derivative in perovskite film, improving electronic coupling and band alignment at interface, reducing trap-states of perovskite grain boundaries, as well as eliminating the hysteresis behavior and improving stability of pero-SCs. As a result, the p-i-n planar pero-SCs based on pero-0.1 fabricated with this strategy and with PTAA HTL showed significantly enhanced photovoltaic performance, delivering a PCE of  $ 20.2\% $  for rigid device on glass and a PCE of  $ 18.1\% $  for the flexible pero-SCs, which is one of the highest PCE among the planar pero-SCs to date. Moreover, this two-step sequential deposition strategy improves the reproducibility of the device performance, and avoids the use of toxic antisolvent, additional interface engineering, and high annealing temperature, thus opens a new avenue to the scalable fabrication of the pero-SCs on glass and the flexible pero-SCs in the near future.

Device Fabrication: ITO glass substrates were sequentially washed with isopropanol, acetone, distilled water, and ethanol for 20 min, then dried with N₂. The flexible PET/Ag-mesh/PH1000 substrate was prepared according to a recently reported technology.[9] 2 mg mL⁻¹ of PTAA containing 1 wt% F4-TCNQ was spin-coated on the ITO in nitrogen-filled glovebox and then annealed at 100 °C for 10 min to obtain PTAA HTL. PEDOT:PSS (30 nm) was spin-coated on the clean patterned ITO glass substrate in ambient atmosphere and annealed on a hot plate at 150 °C for 30 min to obtain PEDOT:PSS HTL. Then, both the HTL-coated substrates were kept in a nitrogen-filled glovebox for the deposition of perovskite films. The MAPbI₃ photoactive layer was then deposited by a sequential two-step deposition method. For the control pero-pristine film, 35 μL of the mixed precursor containing PbI₂ (1.3 M) and MAI (0.3 M) in DMSO and DMF (1:9) was first dropped onto the HTL substrate by spin-coating at 6000 rpm for 15 s, then 40 μL of MAI in its IPA solution (35 mg mL⁻¹) was cast on top of it at 4000 rpm for 45 s. For the PCBB-OEG-doped perovskite films, the PbI₂:MAI film was first prepared according to the control pero-pristine film. Then, PCBB-OEG was added in the MAI precursor with three different weight ratios (PCBB-OEG vs MAI = 0.01, 0.1, and 1 wt%), respectively, and the doped second precursor (PCBB-OEG/MAI in IPA) was spin-coated on top of PbI₂:MAI film with the same condition as pero-pristine film. After that, the substrate was annealed at 100 °C for 30 min to form a MAPbI₃ film in nitrogen-filled glovebox. The PCBM ETL was then spin-coated on perovskite films at 2000 rpm for 30 s with a concentration of 20 mg mL⁻¹ in chlorobenzene. Finally, Al (100 nm) were deposited on PCBM as electrode by vacuum evaporation under 1 × 10⁻⁶ mbar.
