# Multifunctional Ion-Lock Interface Layer Achieved by Solid–Solid Contact Approach for Stabilizing Perovskite Solar Cells

Zhi-Wen Gao, Yong Wang, Zhengyan Jiang, Bihua Hu, Baomin Xu,  $  ^{*}  $   and Wallace C. H. Choy  $  ^{*}  $

It has been a hindering issue in perovskite solar cells that the interfaces between the perovskite and charge transport layers show significantly high concentrations of defects with an amount about 100 times more than inside the bulk perovskite layer. The issue causes substantial reduction in both the efficiency and stability of the devices. Herein, a solid–solid contact approach is demonstrated to realize a multifunctional ion-lock layer with strong chemical interaction to the perovskite layer. The multifunctional ion-lock layer remarkably suppresses the interface defects and tunes the work function, contributing to promoting the carrier extraction, increasing the open-circuit voltage, and enlarging the photocurrent. In addition, the multifunctional ion-lock layer successfully locks ions from movement and thus improves the stability of the devices. Finally, with a multifunctional ion-lock layer, the perovskite solar cells deliver an efficiency of up to 23.13% along with desirable long-term operational, storage, and humidity stability. Consequently, the work offers guidelines for establishing defect-suppressed interfaces between perovskites and hole transport layers.

# 1. Introduction

Metal halide perovskites (MHPs) have emerged as revolutionary semiconductors because of their excellent photophysical properties including high absorption coefficients, long carrier

ditions including electric fields, moisture, oxygen, heat, and illumination.  $  ^{[8-13]}  $   For example, the high deliquescent lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) in the hole transport layer based on 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene can easily absorb water to accelerate the hydrolysis of the underneath perovskite layer.  $  ^{[14-21]}  $   Moreover, abundant defects at interfaces will inherently favor ion migration arising from trap-induced fields, resulting in the deterioration of both organic CTLs and perovskite layers.  $  ^{[22,23]}  $   Hence, it is necessary to tailor a special interface layout to make outstanding improvements in defects passivation and ions permeation, unlocking the full potential of PSCs.  $  ^{[24]}  $   The majority of currently reported works focus on the surface traps passivation by organic-based solution post-treatment method. These works typically pay more attention to the chemical passivation of surface defects/traps but are rarely involved in the suppression of ion migration between different layers.  $  ^{[14,15,25-28]}  $   Moreover, the solution post-treatment method strongly relies on the solvent (usually chlorobenzene or isopropanol) that will dissolve and destroy the perovskite and cause an insurmountable constraint to the development of more efficient interface layers.

In this study, we propose a solid–solid contact (SSC) strategy to form a thin sulfonated tetrafluoroethylene-based fluoropolymer-copolymer (Nafion) multifunctional ion-lock layer, which not only reduces the surface defects of perovskite but also suppresses serious ion migration at interfaces. The

More efforts are needed to make devices more stable while not compromising the performance of PSCs.

# 2. Results and Discussion

Figure 1a shows the chemical structure of Nafion composed of perfluorovinyl ether groups terminated with sulfonate groups onto a tetrafluoroethylene backbone, which features with abundant fluorine (-F) atoms. Considering the solvent-induced degradation concerns of forming Nafion film from its water-based solution on perovskite active layer of PSCs, an SSC strategy is successfully developed to achieve an ultra-thin Nafion layer on the surface of  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.92}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.08}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite (denoted as  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.92}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.08}\mathrm{PbI}_3/\mathrm{Nafion} $ ) (Figure 1b). The Nafion film is firstly formed on the Si/SiO $ _2 $  substrate and then stacked on the surface of  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.92}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.08}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film with heating at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 2\mathrm{min} $ .

Through the solid-state contact transfer, an ultra-thin Nafion film forms on the surface of perovskite, confirmed by F 1s X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) signal on the surface of  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.92}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.08}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  (Figure 1d). Based on these Nafion-modified perovskites as light absorber layers, we fabricate PSCs with a configuration of glass/ITO/electron-transport layer/perovskite/hole-transport layer/Au. All  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.92}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.08}\mathrm{PbI}_3/ $  Nafion-based PSCs except for excessive Nafion modification exhibit improved device efficiency compared with the  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.92}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.08}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  ones (Figure S1 and Table S1, Supporting Information). Notedly, the device based on perovskite film covered with blank  $ \mathrm{Si}/\mathrm{SiO}_2 $  with the same heating condition has also been fabricated and studied to exclude the effect from the  $ \mathrm{Si}/\mathrm{SiO}_2 $  substrate. Our results show that the process with blank  $ \mathrm{Si}/\mathrm{SiO}_2 $  makes no difference to the device performance of the control PSCs without any Nafion. Considering that the Nafion at  $ 0.5\mathrm{wt.\%} $  concentration provides the optimal photovoltaic (PV) performance, we then choose the case of  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.92}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.08}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  modified by  $ 0.5\mathrm{wt.\%} $  Nafion for detailed investigations on the effect of Nafion on the perovskite film and device performance.

Structural, spectral, and surface chemical state characterizations show that the introduction of the Nafion multifunctional ion-lock layer can strongly interact chemically with   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $   but shows few effects on the morphology and bandgap of   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $  . The X-ray diffraction (XRD) results indicate that the introduction of Nafion layer exhibits a neglected effect on the crystalline of   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $   (Figure S2, Supporting Information).  $  ^{[29]}  $   Both   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $   and   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion  $   films show dense morphologies with similar size, as confirmed by scanning electron microscope (SEM) results (Figure 1c). Additionally,   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $   and   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion  $   films show similar bandgap (  $  \approx1.53~eV  $  ), as proved by the UV–Vis results (Figure S3, Supporting Information). Then, XPS is conducted to characterize the top surface chemical state to determine the interaction between Nafion and   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $  . Compared to   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $ , the peaks related to Pb 4f and Cs 3d in the   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion  $   show 0.3 eV and 0.6 eV shift to high binding energy, respectively, while the peak assigned to F 1s from Nafion shows 0.6 eV shift to low binding energy (Figure 1d). The binding energy shift of Pb 4f, Cs 3d, and F 1s core levels confirm the strong interaction among the abundant higher electronegativity halogen (F) of Nafion and lower electronegativity Cs and Pb in   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $  .  $  ^{[30]}  $   This strong interaction could passivate uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $ , which induces deep trap states that severely degrade both PCE and long-term stability of PSCs.

We further study the photoluminescence (PL) and transient absorption (TA) of   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $   with Nafion layer on the surface. The PL intensity of   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion  $   film is much stronger than that of pure   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $   film (Figure S4a, Supporting Information). In addition, the time-resolved PL (TRPL) spectra in Figure S4b (Supporting Information) show that the PL lifetime (  $  \tau  $  ) of   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion  $   (178.58 ns) is much longer than that of   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $   (112.12 ns) (Table S2, Supporting Information). The enhanced PL intensity and improved PL lifetime indicate that we successfully passivate the surface defects and thus significantly suppress non-radiative recombination in   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion  $   arising from the introduction of Nafion, attributing to decreased trap densities.

Transient absorption spectra (TAS) measurement was conducted to explore the ultrafast charge transfer process and recombination mechanism in perovskite films. Figure 2a,b show the contour maps of TAS and femtosecond TA spectroscopy in the range of 2 ps to 800 ps of   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $   and   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion  $   films, respectively. Compared to   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $ ,   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion  $   film shows a much slower decay ratio, in which   $  \Delta A  $   decreases to 16.7% at 800 ps for pristine   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $ , while a higher ratio of 40.4% for   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion  $   is achieved during the same time. Figure 2c shows the transition dynamics of decay time. By using the biexponential kinetic model as described in the experimental section to fit the TAS results of pristine   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $   film at 778 nm, we obtain a short time constant of 7.2 ps and a long time constant of 353.2 ps. On the contrary, a much slower decay kinetic process with a short time constant of 14.5 ps and a long time constant of 565.8 ps are fitted for   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion  $   film. Figure 2d further simply exhibits the possible ultrafast exciton trapping and recombination process. Upon being excited with a high-energy photon, the generated electron will transit to the conduction band, which rapidly decays to the conduction band edge to form a free carrier. The formed free carrier can recombine directly leading to radiative recombination followed by photon emission or is trapped by trap states from defects into non-radiative recombination without emission. The process of cooling for hot exciton occurs in a very short time. Therefore, the fitted short lifetime belongs to the trapping of excitons and the long lifetime can be assigned to band edge exciton recombination.  $  ^{[31-33]}  $   The longer lifetime indicates a lower density of surface defect states after being passivated by Nafion, which is direct evidence that the non-radiative recombination is suppressed in   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion  $   film compared to pristine   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $   film.

Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) results confirm that the introduction of Nafion is beneficial to the extraction of charge carriers. The UPS and valence band spectra results are shown in Figure S5 (Supporting Information) and the energy level alignment at the top interface is shown in Figure S6 (Supporting Information). Compared with   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $   film, the work function of   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion  $   film increases from 4.24 to 4.39 eV. The increased work function can enhance the built-in electric field to promote carrier transport and benefit to promote PV high performance.  $  ^{[34]}  $

Benefiting from the advantages of the introduced Nafion interface layer,   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion-based  $   device with the configuration of glass/ITO/  $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite/Nafion/SpiroOMeTAD/Au (Figure S7, Supporting Information) shows much-enhanced PV performance compared with the device without Nafion interface layer. The champion   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion based  $   device achieves a PCE of 23.13%, with an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) value of 1.135 V, a fill factor (FF) of 80.9%, and a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 25.19 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   (Figure 3a; Table S3, Supporting Information), which is much higher than that of   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}  $   based device (20.69%) with a   $  V_{oc}  $   value of 1.052 V, a FF of 80.1%, and a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 24.55 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  . Moreover, the   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion-based  $   devices also show lower hysteresis (Figure S8, Supporting Information) and good reproducibility (Figure 3b). # 3. Conclusion

We have developed an effective solid–solid contact strategy to construct a multifunctional ion-lock layer on the surface of perovskite. The introduction of Nafion ion-lock layer passivates surface traps of perovskite and increases the built-in electric field at the interface, contributing to an improved   $  J_{sc}  $   and   $  V_{oc}  $  . Moreover, the hydrophobic and ion obstruction of Nafion interlayer can lock   $  Cs^{+}  $  and   $  Pb^{+}  $  from ion migration, resulting in improved stability of PSCs. Finally, the   $  FA_{0.92}Cs_{0.08}PbI_{3}/Nafion-based  $   devices achieve a remarkably improved efficiency of 23.13% as well as improved humidity, thermal and operational stability. We anticipate that constructing ion-lock by SSC strategy will not only be a step forwards for developing more efficient perovskite photovoltaics but also enlighten to fabricate perovskite heterojunction devices with other materials in other research areas.

After cooling down, a Spiro-OMeTAD solution with consists of 72.3 mg Spiro-OMeTAD, 28.8   $  \mu  $  L of 4-tert-butyl pyridine, 27.8   $  \mu  $  L of FK209 Co (III) TFSI solution (300 mg of FK209 in 1 mL of ACN), and 17.5   $  \mu  $  L of Li-TFSI solution (520 mg of Li-TSFI in 1 mL of ACN) in 1 mL CB was spin-coated on perovskite films at 4000 rpm for 25 s. Finally, 80 nm of Au electrode was thermally evaporated under a high vacuum using an E-beam thermal evaporation system.
