# Cobalt Chloride Hexahydrate Assisted in Reducing Energy Loss in Perovskite Solar Cells with Record Open-Circuit Voltage of 1.20 V

Pengyang Wang, Bingbing Chen, Renjie Li, Sanlong Wang, Ningyu Ren, Yucheng Li, Sayantan Mazumdar, Biao Shi, Ying Zhao, and Xiaodan Zhang*

ACCESS

ABSTRACT:   $  SnO_{2}  $   is widely used and one of the most efficient electron transport layers in perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However,   $  SnO_{2}  $   films often contain detrimental defects and may also have mismatches in energy level alignment with perovskite films, thus limiting the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ). Managing the defects and band structure are critical to reduce energy loss in PSCs. Herein, cobalt chloride hexahydrate (  $  CoCl_{2}\cdot6H_{2}O  $  ) is introduced into a   $  SnO_{2}  $   film, which shows favorable energy level alignment and better charge extraction. Correspondingly, an enhanced   $  V_{OC}  $   up to 1.20 V was achieved along with an efficiency of 23.82%, which is the record open-circuit voltage at the optical band gap of 1.54 eV in planar structure PSCs. Moreover, the target devices show enhanced stability, which retains 83.5% of their initial efficiencies after 200 h under continuous irradiation. The doping method provides an effective strategy for reducing energy loss to further enhance the efficiency of PSCs.

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted tremendous attention in the past decade; the power conversion efficiency (PCE) has increased from 3.8%  $  ^{1}  $  to 25.5%.  $  ^{2-4}  $  The architecture of PSCs is commonly based on mesoporous  $  TiO_{2}  $  as the electron transport layer (ETL),  $  ^{5,6}  $  which needs a high temperature above 400 °C to burn out the organic components in the precursor paste,  $  ^{7}  $  limiting the scope of real-life applications.  $  ^{8}  $  Therefore, low-temperature processed ETLs are important and have achieved rapid progress, such as  $  SnO_{2}  $ ,  $  ^{9,10}  $   $  TiO_{2}  $ ,  $  ^{11-14}  $   $  BaSO_{3}  $ ,  $  ^{15,16}  $  and ZnO.  $  ^{17}  $  Among them,  $  SnO_{2}  $  ETL is widely used and one of the most efficient ETLs in PSCs.  $  ^{18-20}  $   $  SnO_{2}  $  exhibits higher electron mobility, which results in reduced charge accumulation at the interface between ETL and perovskite and thus caused the suppressed hysteresis. Very recently, a record certified efficiency up to 24.35% has been achieved based on  $  SnO_{2}  $ .  $  ^{21}  $

In spite of these beneficial properties, solution processed   $  SnO_{2}  $   still has some disadvantages. First, energy level alignment between perovskite and ETL is also essential to device voltage deficits.  $  ^{22-24}  $   Furthermore, according to a previous report, there are many hydroxyl groups on the surface of the metal oxide, which would cause nonradiative recombination at the interface between perovskite and ETL. Hence, rational management of the energy level and defects are critical for enhancing the performance of PSCs. Considerable progress has been made in this field. For example, Sargent et al. found

Hence, it is necessary to develop a new passivation method to achieve the full potential of PSCs.

In the present work, we devised a simple and effective method by introducing cobalt chloride hexahydrate   $  \left(\mathrm{CoCl}_{2}\cdot6\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}\right)  $   into   $  SnO_{2}  $   precursor solution to passivate the defects in the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film and also the bottom of the perovskite films for suppressing interfacial charge recombination, which was inspired by the preparation of   $  \left[\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{6}\right]^{2+}  $   by reaction with   $  Co^{2+}  $   and   $  NH_{3}\cdot H_{2}O  $  . The presence of   $  CoCl_{2}\cdot6H_{2}O  $   also adjusts the energy level alignment at the perovskite/ETL interface which contributes to electron extraction, thus demonstrating a record voltage of 1.20 V and improved fill factor (FF). Consequently, a champion PCE of 23.82% was achieved; in contrast, the relevant reference devices provided a PCE of 22.25% and   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.16 V.

The commercially purchased   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloidal dispersion contains potassium hydroxide (KOH) stabilizer,  $  ^{32}  $   which also has the potential to cause instability. We developed a simple method to stabilize the diluted   $  SnO_{2}  $   dispersion by using   $  CoCl_{2}\cdot6H_{2}O  $   and   $  NH_{3}\cdot H_{2}O  $  . It can be seen in Figure S1, when the   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloidal dispersion was diluted by the   $  CoCl_{2}\cdot6H_{2}O  $   aqueous solution, an opaque complex with visible aggregations was obtained. However, the solution turns to be transparent without any precipitate with the help of   $  NH_{3}\cdot H_{2}O  $  , forming pink coordination compound   $  [\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{6}]^{2+}  $  . As can be seen in Figure 1a, after 30 days, the colorless and transparent diluted

pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   solution turns into a milky white solution with aggregations.  $  ^{53}  $   However, the wine red colored   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   solution remains clear and transparent without any sediment. In order to characterize the change of particle size before and after aging, dynamic light scattering (DLS) was carried out. As shown in Figure S2, the average hydrodynamic radius of aged   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   solution was increased from 8.11 to 36.44 nm and 10.16 to 12.57 nm, respectively. The higher surface zeta potential of   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   (-31.1 mV) than that of pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   (-14.1 mV) (Figure S3) is responsible for the enhanced stability. Higher zeta potential indicates stronger electrostatic repulsion between particles.

As per the previous report, hydroxyl groups exist on the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   and can bind to metal sites.  $  ^{[27]}  $   There are two main active sites on the surface of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   crystal, which could interact with water to form a hydroxyl group, a more hydroxylated surface when treated with UV-ozone. The defect sites (hydroxyl group) would lead to a trap state and cause nonradiative recombination. We speculate that the   $  Co^{2+}  $   from the   $  CoCl_{2}  $   could interact with the hydroxyl groups (  $  OH^{-}  $  ) on the surface of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film, because the hydroxyl groups are basic and tend to dissociate as -OH. So we conducted X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) to confirm this inference. As shown in Figure 1b, the pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   film shows a distinct peak at 532 eV and that substantiates the presence of   $  OH^{-}  $  . The peak located at 530.3 eV originates from the saturated oxygen (  $  O_{2}^{-}  $  ) in   $  SnO_{2}  $  . However, the   $  OH^{-}  $  peak is greatly suppressed in the   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   film (Figure 1c), which confirmed our hypothesis.

To investigate the effect of   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   ETL on device performance, we fabricated PSCs with the structure of ITO/ETL/perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au (Figure S11 and Figure 3b), and the thickness of the perovskite film is about 700 nm. Further characterizations were conducted to well understand the ETL/perovskite interface. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) was performed to examine the band structure of   $  SnO_{2}  $   with and without   $  CoCl_{2}\cdot6H_{2}O  $  . The band structure is shown in Figure 3c and Figure S12, and a decreased work function was observed and the conduction bands of pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $  ,   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $  , and the perovskite film were determined to be -4.38 eV, -4.20 eV, and -4.16 eV, respectively. Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) was also conducted to probe the effect of   $  CoCl_{2}\cdot6H_{2}O  $   on the surface potential and work function of   $  SnO_{2}  $   (Figure S13).  $  ^{35}  $   The surface of   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   exhibits a higher average surface potential (544 mV) than that of pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   film (150 mV) (Figure 3d), which is consistent with the results of UPS. The conduction band of   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   is closer to the conduction band of perovskite.

The transfer of electrons to   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   is accompanied by an energy loss of 0.04 eV, which is much smaller than pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   with an energy loss of 0.22 eV. A favorable energy level alignment was formed, and that has been proven to be beneficial for carrier extraction and enhancement of   $  V_{OC}  $  .  $  ^{36}  $

In addition to enhanced devices performance, our devices also show better stability than reference devices (Figure 3i). The  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2\mathrm{-CoCl}_2 $ -based PSCs can retain  $ 83.5\% $  of their initial

efficiency after 200 h under continuous irradiation. In contrast, the reference devices only retain 74.2% of their initial efficiency. We also compared the thermal stability of   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $  -based PSCs, as it is shown in Figure S18. The   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $  -based PSCs retain 79.6% of their initial efficiency after 100 h at 60 °C in ambient air (relative humidity >50%). In the same conditions, the   $  SnO_{2}  $  -based PSCs only maintain 72.3% of their initial efficiency. This may due to the larger grain size with less grain boundaries which act as an additional protection against oxygen and moisture.

To quantitatively evaluate the trap density in perovskite films based on different ETLs, we measured the space charge limited current (SCLC) of electron-only devices with the structure of ITO/ETL/perovskite/PCBM/Au. As shown in Figure 4c, the trap-filled limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ) of devices based on pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   are 0.51 and 0.39 V, respectively. According to the equation:   $  N_{t}=2\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}V_{TFL}/eL^{2}  $  ,³⁸ the trap densities of perovskite film deposited on pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   ETL are   $  2.26\times10^{15}~cm^{-3}  $   and   $  1.73\times10^{15}~cm^{-3}  $  . The lower trap densities benefit from larger grain sizes along with less grain boundaries,³⁹ which is consistent with PL and TRPL results.

The impact of ETL on the built-in potential   $  (V_{\mathrm{bi}})  $   alteration in the full device was confirmed by capacitance–voltage   $  (C-V)  $   measurements with Mott–Schottky analysis. As shown in Figure 4d, the PSCs with   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   shows much higher   $  V_{bi}  $   of 1.30 V than that of 0.95 V in devices based on pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $ , which reveals that the enhancement of   $  V_{OC}  $   is mainly due to the improvement of   $  V_{bi}  $   at the interface.  $  ^{40}  $

We further elucidate the effect of   $  CoCl_{2}  $   on the defects of   $  SnO_{2}  $   film and recombination dynamics, and transient photovoltage (TPV) and transient photocurrent (TPC) decay were measured. As shown in Figure 4e, the carrier recombination lifetime of device with   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   increased from 27.9 to 168.2   $  \mu  $  s, confirming the suppressed charge recombination at the ETL and perovskite interface.  $  ^{41}  $   For the transport time constants (  $  \tau_{t}  $  ) from TPC (Figure 4f), PSCs based on   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   exhibited a smaller charge extraction time of 0.75   $  \mu  $  s compared to that of the pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $  -based PSCs (1.37   $  \mu  $  s), indicating enhanced charge extraction.

In conclusion, our work provided a simple and effective strategy on modifying   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL by using   $  CoCl_{2}\cdot6H_{2}O  $  , leading to a favorable energy level alignment and that enhances charge extraction and a significant suppressed interfacial recombination; thus, a dramatic improvement of   $  V_{OC}  $   and performance of PSCs occurred. PSCs based on this strategy manifested a record voltage of 1.20 V (a minimum energy loss of 0.34 V) at the optical band gap of 1.54 eV in the planar structure PSCs along with a champion PCE of 23.82%. Remarkably, the PSCs based on   $  SnO_{2}-CoCl_{2}  $   show enhanced stability, which retain 83.5% of their initial efficiency after 200 h under continuous irradiation and retain 79.6% of their initial efficiency after 100 h at 60 °C in ambient air (relative humidity >50%). These findings demonstrate crucial interface design insights to reduce energy loss and further push the PCE of PSCs comparable to that of mesoporous structures.
