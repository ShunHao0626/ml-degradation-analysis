# 1. Introduction

The increasing awareness in our current society regarding the environmental issues precisely the hazardous effects as a result of the combustion and rapid decline of fossils fuels has gained attention towards the safe energy and other alternatives. Amongst these renewable sources, the use of solar energy is undeniably and the most promising approach to fulfill the current and future needs. Nowadays, the renewable energy sources are a means to resolve the issues of the rapid growth of energy demands and also to resolve the environmental pollution rise due to the combustions of fossil fuels   $  [1]  $  . The photovoltaic devices are considered to be a promising renewable alternatives to fossil fuels, which convert solar energy directly to electrical energy   $  [2–5]  $  . State of the art, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are the one of the most investigated material recently due to their long diffusion length, high absorption coefficient, high charge carrier mobility, and abroad spectral absorption range, which made them promising light-absorbing materials. As a hole transporting material (HTM), N2,N2,N2',N2',N7,N7,N7',N7'-octakis (4-methoxyphenyl) spiro [fluorene-9,9'-xanthene]-2,2',7,7'-tetraamine (X60) in mesoscopic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been widely utilized for substitution of the 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis (N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spiro-bi-fluorene (spiroOMeTAD). In this study, we have introduced an ionic liquid N-butyl-N'-(4-pyridylheptyl) imidazolium bis(trifluoromethane) sulfonamide (BuPyIm-TFSI) as a p-dopant to increase the hole conductivity and stability of the X60 based perovskite solar cells. Moreover, the device based on X60: BuPyIm-TFSI composite HTM at ambient conditions with humidity of ~40% exhibited good PSCs performance with the long-term stability of 840 h. Hence, the use of BuPyIm-TFSI as a p-dopant for X60 played a significant role in enhancing the electrical properties, stability and efficiency of PSCs.

Generally, PSCs with configuration (n-i-p) combine with a p-type hole-transporting materials (HTMs), sandwiched between the perovskite film and the cathode material, in order to enhance the efficiency and stability [1]. # 2.2. Device fabrication

Hydrochloric acid (HCl, 2 M) and Zn powder for etching fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)-coated glass (Pilkington TEC 15) were utilized. Thereafter, the substrates were cleaned using detergent solution, deionized water, and ethanol. The FTO-coated glass was put under oxygen plasma for 30 min to remove organic residues. On cleaned FTO glasses, roughly of 30–40 nm compact-  $  TiO_{2}  $  was deposited as blocking layer (BL), then for 30 min the blocking layer was sintered in the air at 500 °C. Through dissolving in anhydrous ethanol at a ratio of 1:5.5 per weight a mesoporous-  $  TiO_{2}  $  layer prepared from diluted mesoporous-  $  TiO_{2}  $  paste (Dyesol 18NR-T) was deposited by spin-coating at 5000 rpm for 30 s. After that, the layer was sintered in the air at 500 °C for 30 min. The perovskite films were prepared by two-steps spin-coating at 1000 rpm for 10 s and continuously for 5000 rpm for 20 s onto the (mesoporous-  $  (TiO_{2})/BL-(TiO_{2})/FTO  $  ) substrates from mixed-cation perovskite precursor solution. After 15 s from the start of the second step, 200 mL of chlorobenzene was dropped onto the substrates, then the substrates were heated for 60 min on a hot plate at 100 °C and left to cool to room temperature. At 2000 rpm on the top perovskite layers for 30 s and via solution process, X60: BuPyIm-TFSI with different concentrations were spin-coated. 80 mg of X60 was dissolved in 1 mL chlorobenzene (CB) to prepare X60 (80 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ). # 3. Characterizations

The UV–Vis spectra were measured on Agilent 8453

spectrophotometer (Model: HP 8435, China). SEM cross-section image was carried out by HR-SEM, implemented with FEI (Field Emission Instruments: Nova Nano SEM 450, USA). For cyclic voltammetry (CV), a potentiostat (PGSTAT30, Metrohm) was used to characterize the electrochemical properties, with three-electrode standard configurations consisting of Ag/AgCl as reference and platinum wire as counter electrode. As supporting electrolyte solution, tetramethylammonium hexafluorophosphate [Bu4N] [PF6] (0.1 M) was dissolved in dichloromethane. As reference, the potential of the materials were measured vs the standard ferrocenium/ferrocene. The CV measurements were implemented at a scan rate of 0.1 V s  $  ^{-1}  $  . The CV curves obtained from the forward and backward scan measurements, the average values of the redox potentials were estimated and calculated. The conductivity was measured by using linear current-voltage curves, with a Keithley 2400 source meter; a two-point probe setup was used. In dark conditions and at different applied bias voltage, the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was measured through an impedance/gain-phase analyzer (Zahner Model: Zennium, Serial No. 40037, German), with survey frequency range from   $  10^{6}  $   to 0.1 Hz for the electrochemical workstation.

# 4.4. Photovoltaic performances with X60: BuPyIm-TFSI

The performance of the fabricated PSCs mesoscopic structure of the X60 without and with different concentrations of the BuPyIm-TFSI were tested. The optimization of the concentration to achieve a better device performance was carried out by investigating the influence of the concentration of p-type dopant of HTMs on device performance. # 4.6. The stability of PSCs with X60: BuPyIm-TFSI as HTM

The addition of BuPyIm-TFSI to the X60 resulted in the generation of more charge carriers, with subsequent increase in the efficiency and stability of the PSC by regenerating the charges on the HTM layer. The stability was investigated by the distribution of the device parameters with time testing of the stability measurements of the PSC devices under dark, encapsulated under an ambient environment, humidity level of  $ \sim 40\% $  and encapsulated and preserved in the dark. Interestingly, the PSC with BuPyIm-TFSI doped X60 crystallization enhanced the device performance and showed excellent long-term stability and preserved more than  $ 93\% $  of its initial PCE, and the PSC with X60 doped with TBP, Li-TFSI, and FK209 preserved only  $ 70\% $  of its initial PCE as shown Fig. 11(a). Fig. 11(b) shows the stability of the other photovoltaic parameters for PSCs based on X60: BuPyIm-TFSI, and X60: FK209 as HTMs. Stability tests reveal that the BuPyIm-TFSI has a remarkable positive impact on the long-term stability of perovskite solar cells.

For water penetration testing of X60 without, with TBP, Li-TFSI, and FK209, and with BuPyIm-TFSI doped X60 (4.85 mM), PSCs testing was carried out in Fig. 12 with the angles of 84.9° for X60 without, 82.5° for the X60 with TBP, Li-TFSI, and FK209, and 86.00° for the BuPyIm-TFSI doped X60, respectively. The hydrophobic properties of the BuPyIm-TFSI to the water can stop water penetration into the perovskite layer, which prevents the effect of water on the device, and this can extraordinarily improve the stability for PSCs.

# 5. Conclusion

We successfully introduced X60: BuPyIm-TFSI as a hole-transporting material on mesoporous PSCs. The bulk conductivity was increased by adding the BuPyIm-TFSI to the X60 as p-dopants. UV–Vis spectrum proved that the oxidation occurred by adding of the BuPyIm-TFSI to the X60, and the oxidation of X60 occurs via proton of BuPyIm-TFSI transfer to X60, which increases the bulk conductivity. The X60 with BuPyIm-TFSI as a p-dopant based PSCs yielded a high overall performance over 14.65% with excellent long-term durability with a humidity of ~40% under an ambient environment. Accordingly, the hole transporting materials (HTM) based on X60 doped BuPyIm-TFSI based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) lead to the enhancement of the device performance and demonstrated an increased lifetime of the device. To end with, this work elucidated that the X60 doped BuPyIm-TFSI can work effectively as HTMs layer for fabricating perovskite-based solar cells with a high-performance. Furthermore, the improvement of solar cells can be achieved by utilizing a new device structure or by mixing other materials to perovskite layer or by optimizing the HTM thickness by manipulating the HTM and p-dopant concentrations.
