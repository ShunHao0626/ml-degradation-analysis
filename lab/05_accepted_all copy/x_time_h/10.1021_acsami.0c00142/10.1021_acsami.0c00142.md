# 1. INTRODUCTION

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite, as an emerging photoelectric material with solution processability, has many appealing attributes, such as high light-extinction coefficient, moderate bandgap, ultralong carrier lifetime and high defect tolerance. Over the past years, rapid progress in power conversion efficiency (PCE) has been realized by perovskite solar cells (PSCs), increasing from 9.7% in 2012 to 25.2% in 2019.  $  ^{1-4}  $   The above increase demonstrates great potential in the future photovoltaic market. Electron transport layer (ETL) is a key component in PSCs for its critical purpose of separating and extracting photogenerated electrons.  $  ^{5-7}  $   Currently, an ETL material routinely used in PSCs are   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanocrystallites.  $  ^{8-10}  $   However,   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based ETLs usually require a high-temperature (above 450 °C) annealing procedure, which is not compatible with flexible and tandem devices in future applications.  $  ^{11-12}  $   In addition, the relatively low electron mobility and ultraviolet (UV) photonic instability of   $  TiO_{2}  $   material remain obstacles for further increases in PCE and stability of PSCs.  $  ^{8,13-15}  $

As shown in Figure 1, the colorless solution becomes a pale yellow after   $  O_{3}  $   infusion, indicating the formation of new chemical bonds after the reaction between   $  Nb(C_{2}H_{5}O)_{5}  $   and   $  O_{3}  $  . Due to a weak bond force between   $  Nb^{5+}  $   and   $  -OC_{2}H_{5}  $  , the   $  Nb(C_{2}H_{5}O)_{5}  $   solution is prone to substitution with   $  H_{2}O  $   under ambient conditions, which generates   $  Nb(OH)_{5}  $   precipitate.  $  ^{25}  $   Similar to   $  H_{2}O  $   molecules, the O atoms in   $  O_{3}  $   molecules have several pairs of lone pair electrons and demonstrate a stronger coordination ability than the   $  -OC_{2}H_{5}  $   group with a larger molecular volume. Thus, before the solution is hydrolyzed, the introduced   $  O_{3}  $   can substitute   $  -OC_{2}H_{5}  $   and coordinate with   $  Nb^{5+}  $  . To further study the reaction degree between the   $  O_{3}  $   and niobium ethanol, we adjusted the   $  O_{3}  $   bubble duration. The UV-visible absorption characterization results of the solution are shown in Figure 1b. Within 60 s, the absorption edges gradually redshift with the increased   $  O_{3}  $   bubbling duration, indicating a gradual replacement of   $  -OC_{2}H_{5}  $   by   $  O_{3}  $   (Figure 1c), which can be explained by the ligand-to-metal charge transition (LMCT) theory.  $  ^{29}  $   By comparison to   $  C_{2}H_{5}O^{-}  $  , the electron-donating ability of   $  O_{3}  $   is stronger as it contains more lone pair electrons. Therefore, in the case of the solution infused with   $  O_{3}  $  , less energy is required for charge transition that in turn causes a red shift in the absorption spectrum. However, after further infusion, the absorption edge did not shift anymore, and the 90 s curve coincides with the 60 s curve, indicating that the reaction becomes saturated within 60 s.

To explore the hydrolysis resistance property of the solution after reacting with   $  O_{3}  $  , we first tested the hydrolytic stability of the   $  NbO_{x}  $   precursor solutions. It can be seen that in less than 15 s, a large amount of white precipitate is formed on the substrate from the solution without   $  O_{3}  $   infusion, indicating the rapid hydrolysis characteristic of the Nb salt solution. Figure 3b shows that the  $ \mathrm{O_3@NbO_x} $  film exhibits a high dark current, indicating that reducing the OH amount in the low-temperature  $ \mathrm{NbO_x} $  film can effectively increase the electrical conductivity of the film. The improved transmittance and electrical conductivity of the  $ \mathrm{O_3@NbO_x} $  film will increase the light use of the device and the photogenerated electron transport efficiency in the device.

We further used ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) and UV–vis spectrophotometry to explore the energy level structure of the ETLs. Figure 3c shows the UPS spectra and absorption spectra (insert) of the   $  NbO_{x}  $   and   $  O_{3}@NbO_{x}  $   films. The energy level calculation results are shown in Figure 3d, in which   $  NbO_{x}  $   and   $  O_{3}@NbO_{x}  $   show conduction bands (CBMs) of -4.23 eV and -4.34 eV, respectively. Both films show good energy level matching with both the FTO (CBM: -4.4 eV) and the mixed structure perovskite (CBM: -4.17 eV) we reported previously;  $  ^{28,34}  $   this indicates that both   $  NbO_{x}  $   ETLs have suitable energy level positions for electron transfer in perovskite solar cells, which is consistent with previous reports.  $  ^{30,35-36}  $
