# ABSTRACT

Bulk and interface carrier nonradiative recombination losses impede the further improvement of power conversion efficiency (PCE) and stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). It is highly necessary to develop multifunctional strategy to minimize surface and interface nonradiative recombination losses. Herein, we report a bulk and interface defect passivation strategy via the synergistic effect of anions and cations, where multifunctional potassium sulphate  $ (\mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{SO}_4) $  is incorporated at  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2/ $  perovskite interface. We find that  $ \mathrm{K}^+ $  ions in  $ \mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{SO}_4 $  diffuse into perovskite layer and suppress the formation of bulk defects in perovskite films, and the  $ \mathrm{SO}_4^{2-} $  ions remain located at interface via the strong chemical interaction with  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  layer and perovskite layer, respectively. Through this synergistic modification strategy, effective defect passivation and improved energy band alignment are achieved simultaneously. These beneficial effects are translated into an efficiency increase from  $ 19.45\% $  to  $ 21.18\% $  with a low voltage deficit of  $ 0.53\mathrm{V} $  mainly as a result of boosted open-circuit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{oc}}) $  after  $ \mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{SO}_4 $  modification. In addition, the  $ \mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{SO}_4 $  modification contributes to ameliorated stability. The present work provides a route to minimize bulk and interface nonradiative recombination losses for the simultaneous realization of PCE and stability enhancement by rational anion and cation synergistic engineering.

# 1. Introduction

In recent years, widespread attention has been focused on perovskite solar cells (PSCs) development due to excellent optoelectronic properties of metal halide perovskites, such as solution-processible property, low cost, long intrinsic carrier diffusion length, small exciton binding energy, tunable bandgap, high molar absorption coefficient, etc   $  [1-5]  $  . In less than decade since the successful preparation of all-solid-state PSCs   $  [6,7]  $  , the PCE has been rapidly boosted to presently certified record efficiency up to 25.5%   $  [2]  $  .

$ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  is one of the most classical electron transport layers for all-solid-state solar cells. Zhang et al. [8] used hydrothermal method to prepare anatase  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanopyramid arrays for directional charge extraction in PSCs, and achieved the highest PCE for PSCs consisting of 1D electron transport materials. And many PSCs with a PCE above  $ 23\% $  have been prepared using mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  as the electron transport layer (ETL) [9-12]. However, mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -based PSCs still suffer from high annealing temperature usually over  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for ETL layer, poor UV stability, and serious hysteresis [1,13]. In contrast, the preferences for  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ -based PSCs have mushroom growth due to  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  with high electron mobility, deep conduction band, excellent UV stability and low-temperature preparation [2,14-16]. Recently, a certified PCE of  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ -based PSCs rises to  $ 25.5\% $  [2], which suggests that  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ -based planar PSCs is highly promising. However, there is still large room for PCE enhancement toward limit theoretical efficiency. In addition, the poor operational stability faced by PSCs is widely recognized to be one critical factor impeding its large-scale practical deployment [17]. The above-mentioned losses in efficiency and stability are mainly due to the non-radiative recombination losses which are derived from defects at the surface and interface [18-20]. Thus, it is a potentially effective way for the further improvement of PCE and stability by means of minimizing nonradiative recombination losses.

As we know, high-quality perovskite film is one of the necessary conditions to achieve higher efficient and stable PSCs. And so far, a variety of strategies in

improving perovskite film quality have been implemented, mainly including solvent engineering [21,22], perovskite composition engineering [23], additive engineering [24], and substrate engineering [25]. Among them, substrate engineering is an efficient method in improving perovskite film quality via modulating nucleation and growth process [14,15,26]. Except for crystallization modulation, it is highly desirable for the development of appropriate additive molecules to passivate the defects within perovskite films. Potassium (K $  ^{+}  $ ) ion has been certified to improve efficiency and inhibit hysteresis due to their effectiveness in passivating the defects and suppressing ion migration in perovskite films [27–29]. Apart from perovskite film quality, SnO $  _{2}  $ /perovskite interface is also essential for fabricating high-efficiency and stable PSC. The following critical issues at SnO $  _{2}  $ /perovskite interface should be addressed urgently. First, imperfect energy level alignment and inappropriate energy offset would result in interfacial energy losses [14]. Second, interfacial defects between SnO $  _{2}  $  and perovskite films would lead to interfacial charge nonradiative recombination losses [14,26,30,31]. The different levels of oxygen vacancy in SnO $  _{2}  $  ETL would hinder electron extraction and transport [14,26,30]. Oxygen vacancy defects in SnO $  _{2}  $  ETL can be feasibly and effectively passivated through incorporating interface modifiers which can coordinate with Sn $  ^{4+}  $  [14,26,30]. Anionic and cationic defects would produce inevitably at the surface and grain boundary (GB) of perovskite films during fast crystallization and high temperature annealing. Organic or inorganic salts containing anion and cation simultaneously have been widely certified to passivate the uncoordinated defects of cationic and anionic [14,26,32,33].

So far, most reported interface-modified molecules in PSCs are frequently used to either improve perovskite crystallization, modulate energy band alignment, or passivate interface defects. It is still a great challenge to accomplish the above three functions simultaneously. # 2. Experimental

# 2.1.Materials

$ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  colloidal solution (15 wt% in water) was purchased from Alfa Aesar. Methylammonium bromide (MABr,  $ 99.9\% $ ), lead (II) bromide  $ (\mathrm{PbBr}_2, 99.9\%) $ , cesium iodide (CsI,  $ 99.99\% $ ), bis(trifluoromethane) sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI,  $ 99\% $ ) and tert-butylpyridine (tBP,  $ 99\% $ ) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Formamidine hydroiodide (FAI,  $ 99.9\% $ ), lead (II) iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2, 99.99\%) $  and Spiro-OMeTAD ( $ 99.86\% $ ) were purchased from Advanced Election Technology CO. Ltd. Potassium sulfate  $ (\mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{SO}_4, 99\%) $ , chlorobenzene (CB,  $ >99.9\% $  purity), N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF,  $ 99.8\% $ ), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO,  $ 99.8\% $ ) and acetonitrile (ACN,  $ 99.8\% $ ), were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. All the chemicals were used without further purification.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

FTO glass substrates were ultrasonicated with deionized water, alcohol, acetone and isopropyl alcohol for 20 min, respectively. Then, the dried FTO was treated with UV-ozone for 30 min. 0.3 mL of   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloidal solution were diluted in 1.2 mL of ultra-pure water and   $  K_{2}SO_{4}  $   powder was dissolved in ultra-pure water at a concentration of 1.5, 3, 5 and 8 mg/mL respectively.   $  SnO_{2}  $   diluent is spin-coated on FTO substrate at 3000 rpm for 30 s, followed by the annealing at 150 °C for 30 min. And then the different concentrations of   $  K_{2}SO_{4}  $   solutions were spin-coated onto the cooled   $  SnO_{2}  $   films at 4000 rpm for 30 s, followed by the annealing at 120 °C for 10 min. For the better surface wetting of perovskite precursor, the substrate was treated with UV-ozone for 11 min. The CsFAMA mixed perovskite precursor was prepared by dissolving 172 mg FAI, 22.4 mg MABr, 507.2 mg   $  PbI_{2}  $   and 80.8 mg   $  PbBr_{2}  $   in a mixture solvent of DMF/DMSO (4:1, by volume), and then a 52.6 μL CsI solution (pre-dissolved as a 1.5 M stock DMSO solution) was added to obtain the required perovskite precursor solution with appropriate excess lead halide. The CsFAMA perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated on   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}/K_{2}SO_{4}  $   substrates at 1000 rpm for 10 s and 6000 rpm for 20 s, and 200 μL chlorobenzene was rapidly

drop-coated at the last 6th seconds and immediately annealed at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 60 min. After cooling to room temperature, the hole transporting layer was spin-coated on the perovskite films at 3000 rpm for 30 s using spiro-OMeTAD solution, which consisted of 57.84 mg Spiro-OMeTAD, 28  $ \mu $ L lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulphonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) (260 mg/mL stock acetonitrile solution), 24  $ \mu $ L 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP) and 0.8 mL chlorobenzene. Finally, 100 nm of Ag film was thermally evaporated under a vacuum of  $ 5\times 10^{-5} $  Pa. The effective area of the devices was 0.11 cm $ ^{2} $ .

# 2.3. Characterization

XPS and UPS were measured (Thermo Fisher Escalab 250Xi spectrometer) using a monochromatized Al source. XPS was calibrated using the peak position of C1s and UPS was calibrated using the work function of Au. The FTIR spectra were collected in the transmittance mode on the Nicolet iS50 Infrared Fourier spectrometer instrument. The optical absorption and transmission spectra of pre-prepared films were measured by means of Shimadzu UV-3600 Spectrophotometer. Crystallographic information for the perovskite films was obtained by X-ray diffraction spectra (XRD, PA Nalytical Empyrean Xpert Materials Research diffractometer equipped with Cu Kα as the radiation source (  $  \lambda = 1.5406 \AA  $  ). Morphological and cross-sectional information for the perovskite films was performed on a field-emission SEM (JEOL JSM-7800 F). The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra were performed on the perovskite films by a fluorescence spectrophotometer (FLS1000, Edinburgh Instruments Ltd. which equipped with a pulse laser diode with a wavelength of 450 nm). Transient photocurrent (TPC) and transient photovoltage (TPV) measurements were performed using a system excited by a 532 nm (1000 Hz, 6 ns) pulsed laser. Recording photocurrent or photovoltage decay process used a 1 GHz Agilent digital oscilloscope (DSO-X3102A) with a 50 Ω or 1 MΩ sampling resistor. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) and Mott-Schottky measurements (1000 Hz) were performed on Chenhua electrochemical workstation (CHI 760E) under dark conditions. The current density-voltage (J-V) characteristics of the devices were measured in ambient air (the relative humidity was 40–50%) by an AM 1.5G solar simulator (Newport, 2612A).

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated a bulk and interface defect passivation strategy via the synergistic effect of anion and cation, where multifunctional  $ \mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{SO}_4 $  is incorporated at  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2/ $  perovskite interface. We find that  $ \mathrm{K}^+ $  ions in  $ \mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{SO}_4 $  diffuse into perovskite layer and suppress the formation of bulk defects in perovskite films, and the  $ \mathrm{SO}_4^{2-} $  ions are anchored at interface via the strong chemical interaction with  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  layer and perovskite layer, respectively. After interface modification, effective defect passivation and ameliorated energy band alignment are achieved simultaneously. The PCE of devices is boosted from  $ 19.45\% $  to  $ 21.18\% $  with a low voltage deficit of 0.53 V mainly as a result of boosted  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  after  $ \mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{SO}_4 $  modification. As high as  $ 85\% $  of its initial efficiency value is maintained for the modified PSCs by storing in ambient condition with a  $ 25\% $  relative humidity for  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $ . The present work provides a route to minimize bulk and interface nonradiative recombination losses for the simultaneous realization of PCE and stability enhancement by rational anion and cation synergistic engineering.
