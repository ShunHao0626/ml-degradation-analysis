# Reduced Graphene Oxide/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  Composite as a Desirable Candidate to Modify the Electron Transport Layer of Mesoscopic Perovskite Solar Cells

Adam K. Kadhim, Mohammad R. Mohammad, Atheer I. Abd Ali, and Mustafa K. A. Mohammed*

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Perovskite (PVK) materials as a promising candidate for next-generation photovoltaic devices have been widely developed by scientists to solve global warming concerns. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been raised up to 25%, a competitive value with silicon-based solar cells. Low shelf stability is one of the first concerns of PSCs and should be considered by researchers' attempts. In this regard, many attempts have been reported for designing graphene-based composite nanoparticles (NPs) for use in the PSCs. Here, to boost the PCE and shelf stability of triple cation PSCs, the electron transport layer (ETL) was modified with novel reduced graphene oxide (rGO) combined with bismuth oxide (  $  Bi_{2}O_{3}  $  ), which was prepared with a simple mixing of rGO and   $  Bi_{2}O_{3}  $  . Different volume ratios of   $  rGO/Bi_{2}O_{3}  $   were added to the mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   precursor and used as a modified ETL in the PSCs. It was found that   $  rGO/Bi_{2}O_{3}  $   composite improves the conductivity of the ETL and boosts the electron extraction to the FTO electrode. Besides this, the   $  rGO/Bi_{2}O_{3}  $   composite improved the ETL/PVK interface and consequently reduced charge recombination in this interface. The PCE of the PSCs reached 17.21% after the ETL modification process compared with 13.41% for unmodified PSCs. Finally, a more stable behavior in modified PSCs was observed, which shows that the resistance of the modified PVK layer to composition degradation is higher than that of unmodified layers.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Nowadays, renewable energy has become an important topic for many nations in the world in order to minimize the impact of pollution and to replace the traditional fossil fuels.  $  ^{1-3}  $   Among renewable sources, the solar source has drawn significant interest from the solar cell community for processing and performance.  $  ^{4}  $   Different solar cells have been developed in recent decades, for instance, dye-sensitized solar cells,  $  ^{5}  $   organic solar cells,  $  ^{6}  $   thin-film solar cells,  $  ^{7}  $   quantum dots solar cells,  $  ^{8}  $   silicon solar cells,  $  ^{9}  $   and perovskite solar cells (PSCs).  $  ^{10,11}  $   Presently, PSCs based on organometal halides have been the fastest-growing photovoltaic (PV) due to their unique optoelectronic characteristics and the low manufacturing cost of such PVs.  $  ^{12}  $   The certified efficiency of PSC has increased from 3.9% up to 25%, which makes it a promising candidate for competing with silicon solar cells.  $  ^{13}  $   The rapid improvement in the performance of PSCs is mainly assigned to the superior merits of the perovskite absorber material, including fewer recombination processes, high light-harvesting ability in the visible range, long carrier diffusion path, and adjustable bandgap energy.  $  ^{14-17}  $

Most importantly, the high quality of each layer in the PSCs is known to be an important factor, especially the electron transport layer (ETL), PVK photoactive layer, and hole transport layer (HTL).  $  ^{18}  $   The interfacial properties of these layers need to be optimized to allow for the induced electrons and holes in the PVK absorber film.  $  ^{19}  $   Also, the recombination rate at both PVK–photoelectrode interfaces needs to be suppressed to acquire a fast charge extraction.  $  ^{20}  $   To minimize

Moreover, the modified PSCs revealed highly desirable ambient stability compared with the control PSCs.

# 2. EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS

2.1. Materials Synthesis. The rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  composites were prepared by a simple mixing method. First, both rGO (purity  $ \sim $  98%,  $ \sigma \sim $  600 S/m, Sigma-Aldrich) and Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  (99.5%, 20–30 nm, Merck) were separately dispersed in ethanol (C $ _2 $ H $ _6 $ O, Merck) with a concentration of 0.5 mg/mL via sonication for 1 h. Lately, 5% Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  solution was poured into rGO solution in the volume ratio and stirred for 24 h at 70  $ ^{\circ} $ C. The rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  nanocomposites were collected by centrifugation and washed with distilled water and C $ _2 $ H $ _6 $ O three times. Finally, the precipitate was dried at 60  $ ^{\circ} $ C with an oven for 12 h.

2.2. Device Fabrication. To clean the F-doped   $  SnO_{2}  $   (FTO, 15   $  \Omega/sq  $  , Solaronix) substrates, they were sonicated with distilled water, acetone,   $  C_{2}H_{6}O  $  , and isopropanol (IPA, 99.8%, Merck) sequentially for 10 min. Then, the substrates were dried and treated with UV–ozone for 15 min. The hole-blocking   $  TiO_{2}  $   (c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) layers were fabricated with spin-coating of a middle acid solution of titanium isopropoxide (TTIP, 98%, EXIR) with a speed of 4000 rpm for 30 s. Then, the pure mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   (mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) or rGO/  $  Bi_{2}O_{3}  $   modified mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   layers were spin-coated above c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   layers with a speed of 4000 rpm for 30 s. The precursor of pure mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   was prepared with diluting of   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste in   $  C_{2}H_{6}O  $   (1:5 wt %). To prepare modified mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   precursors, different amounts of rGO/  $  Bi_{2}O_{3}  $   solutions (2%, 4%, and 6%) in 1,2-dichlorobenzene (DCB, 99%, Merck) (1 mg/mL) were added to the diluted   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste and stirred for 2 h. Both c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   and mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   layers were baked at 500 °C for 1 h. The PVK film was fabricated as follows. First, a mixed solution of   $  PbI_{2}  $   (600 mg, Alfa Aesar) and lead bromide (15 mg, Merck) in DMF (950   $  \mu  $  L) contains 10% CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   in a volume ratio was spin-coated on FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}  $ /mp-  $  TiO^{2-}  $   substrates at 1500 rpm for 30 s, followed by annealing at 70 °C for 1 min. Then, a mixture of formamidinium iodide (FAI),

methylammonium bromide (MABr), and methylammonium chloride (MACl) solution (60 mg/6 mg/6 mg) in IPA was poured on the PbI  $  _{2}  $  layer, followed by spin coating at 1300 rpm for 30 s. To complete PVK film fabrication, they were annealed at 150 °C for 15 min. The HTL mixture was obtained by adding 17.5  $  \mu  $  L of bis-(trifluoromethane) sulfonimide lithium salt (99.95%, Merck) in acetonitrile (Sigma-Aldrich, 520 mg/mL) and 28.8  $  \mu  $  L of 4-tertbutylpyridine (Merck, 97%) to 60 mM Spiro-OMeTAD in DCB. The HTL solution was deposited at 3000 rpm for 40 s on top of the PVK film to develop the HTL film. Then, a 100 nm Au contact layer was sputtered on the HTL.

2.3. Characterization. The top-view morphology of the PVK films on a substrate of FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $ /mp-TiO $ _2 $  was monitored with an FESEM device (TESCAN, Mira 3), and rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  micromorphologies were investigated using a scanning electron microscope device (SEM, KYKY-EM3200). X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the PVK layers and rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  were collected with an X-ray diffraction spectrometer (Bruker, D8 Advance). The optical characteristics of PVK layers on FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $ /mp-TiO $ _2 $  substrates were investigated using a UV–vis spectrophotometer (Analytic Jena, Specord 250) in the wavelength range of 200–900 nm. The photoluminescence (PL) spectra of PV layers on pure and modified mp-TiO $ _2 $  substrates were monitored by employing a PL system (Teifsanje, FL-Ar-2015). The Raman spectrum of rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  was measured using Raman spectroscopy (HORIBA, HR550) ( $ \lambda $  = 532 nm). The photocurrent–voltage ( $ J-V $ ) measurements of the PSCs were evaluated under a calibrated intensity of 100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $  using a Keithley Model 2400 instrument. The active area of 8 mm $ ^2 $  was used for all PSCs to measure  $ J-V $  characteristics. To measure the incident photon to conversion electron (IPCE) spectra of perovskite solar cells, a Newport IPCE device was employed.

To probe the effects of rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  on the properties of PVK films, a series of measurements was conducted (Figure 3). Figure 3a illustrates the absorbance behavior of the PVK layers spin-coated on various ETLs. As shown, an increment in the amount of the rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  additive to 4% would result in a notable improvement in the UV–vis absorption of the corresponding PVK layer within the visible range. However, further increment in the rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  amount would lead to the decrement of the absorption spectrum of the PVK layer. As the results showed, the rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  additive did not change the absorption edge of the PVK layer. As reported in previous studies, the high UV–vis absorption spectrum is mainly attributed to the enlarged crystallite size of the PVK. $ ^{42} $  Moreover, the Tauc curves in Figure 3b reveal shifting in the value of the bandgap energy ( $ E_g $ ) of the PVK layer from 1.6 to 1.59 eV after rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  was added to the mp-TiO $ _2 $  solution. This shift was also detected in the steady-state PL spectrum of the PVK deposited on the rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ -doped mp-TiO $ _2 $  layer (Figure 3c), which agrees well with the corresponding UV–vis results. As clearly shown, the addition of rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  induces the suppression of the PL intensity on the order of 0%, 2%, and 4%, which indicates a more efficient charge transfer in the corresponding PVK film with fewer recombination processes. $ ^{15} $  Also, we noted that the overdose adding of rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  would lead to intensifying the PL signal again, confirming the negative role of using a high amount of this additive. The stability measurement brings one to the satisfaction that the stability enhancement of modified devices is due to the grain boundary passivation and interface improvement caused by the addition of rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  additives. As demonstrated by Castro-Méndez et al., $ ^{44} $  the grain boundaries in a PVK layer and unsuitable TiO $ _2 $ /PVK interfaces are responsible for the instability of

PSCs. A PVK layer in moisture undergoes a decomposition process starting from the surface, and the speed of decomposition increases through grain boundaries. rGO/  $  Bi_{2}O_{3}  $  additives are, therefore, introduced as an efficient approach to tackle the stability issue of the perovskite solar cells.

# 4. CONCLUSION

In brief, we report a novel rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  additive to fabricate high-performance mesoporous PSCs. This approach is based on preparing a nanocomposite structure of rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  and incorporating it into mp-TiO $ _2 $  to obtain electron acceptor/transport-mediator ETLs. Compared with control PSCs, rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ -based PSCs reveal better charge injection and lower recombination processes, resulting in higher  $ J_{sc} $  and PCE. Besides this, higher conductivity of the rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ -doped TiO $ _2 $  additive than pure TiO $ _2 $  may facilitate the transfer of the photogenerated electrons from PVK film to the FTO substrate. Our results propose that rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  could serve as an excellent additive in the PSCs, enhancing the PV parameters of the PSCs. Notably, a champion PCE of 17.2% with high stability is realized based on rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  additive. With the further increment in the rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  amount, PV parameters were reduced due to the lower transparency of the mp-TiO $ _2 $  ETL. The enhanced stability of the rGO/Bi $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ -modified PSCs was due to the stabilization of PVK film with larger grain size and higher crystallinity, which in turn results in the more stabilized PSCs.
