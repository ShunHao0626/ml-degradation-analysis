ABSTRACT: All-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite is emerging to be an alternative light-harvesting material in solar cells owing to the enhanced stability and comparable photovoltaic performance compared to organic–inorganic hybrid perovskites. However, the desirable black phase   $  \alpha-CsPbI_{3}  $   is not stable at room temperature and degrade rapidly to a non-perovskite yellow phase   $  \delta-CsPbI_{3}  $  . Herein, we introduce a compositional engineering approach via incorporating   $  Bi^{3+}  $   in   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   to stabilize the   $  \alpha  $  -phase at room temperature. Fully inorganic solar cells based on the Bi-incorporated   $  \alpha-CsPb_{1-x}Bi_{x}I_{3}  $   compounds demonstrate a high PCE of 13.21% at an optimal condition (incorporation of 4 mol%   $  Bi^{3+}  $  ) and maintain 68% of the initial PCE for 168 h under ambient conditions without encapsulation. This is the first attempt that partial substitution of “B”-site of the perovskite to stabilize the   $  \alpha-CsPbI_{3}  $  , which paves the way for further developments of such perovskites and other optoelectronic devices.

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite materials have attracted tremendous research interesting to their intriguing optical characteristics as well as promising application in next-generation opto-electronic devices.  $  ^{1-6}  $   Among the various hybrid halide perovskites,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (  $  MAPbI_{3}  $  ) and   $  \mathrm{HC(NH_{2})_{2}PbI_{3}}  $   (  $  FAPbI_{3}  $  ) have been frequently studied and have achieved power conversion efficiencies (PCE) exceeding 20% in solar cells.  $  ^{7-15}  $   However, due to the hygroscopicity and thermally unstable nature of organic cation   $  MA^{+}  $ ,   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   is thermal instable and vulnerable to moisture.  $  ^{16-20}  $   Even for the more thermostable   $  FAPbI_{3}  $ , the presence of hygroscopic   $  FA^{+}  $  also makes it suffer from the moisture stability issue.  $  ^{8,21-23}  $   In order to improve the stability and photovoltaic performance of the devices, a series of Cs-incorporated system have been developed,  $  ^{24-34}  $   such as   $  Cs_{x}MA_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $ ,  $  ^{24}  $     $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $ ,  $  ^{27,28,30}  $     $  FA_{0.83}Cs_{0.17}Pb(I_{1-x}Br_{x})_{3}  $ ,  $  ^{31}  $   and   $  Cs_{x}(MA_{0.17}FA_{0.83})_{1-x}Pb(I_{0.83}Br_{0.17})_{3}  $ .  $  ^{29,32}  $   However, these Cs-incorporated system still face big challenge for the long-term stability due to the remain of organic components.

Recently, all-inorganic cesium lead halide perovskites (CsPbX $ _3 $ ) are emerging to be alternative light-harvesting materials in solar cells and have exhibited excellent ability to resist moisture and heat.  $ ^{35-39} $  Nevertheless, CsPbBr $ _3 $  has a very large band-gap of 2.3 eV, which is unable to absorb light with long-range wavelength and usually results in low PCE of the solar cells.  $ ^{38-40} $  Compared to CsPbBr $ _3 $ , black phase  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  (Figure 1a) has a more suitable band-gap of 1.73 eV for solar cells. However, the  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  is not stable and degrade rapidly to a non-perovskite yellow phase  $ \delta $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  (Figure 1a) at room temperature.  $ ^{41-45} $  The undesirable phase transition is owing to the size of cesium cation is too small to support the PbI $ _6 $  polyhedral frame in the cubic perovskite structure.  $ ^{27,44,46} $  This has been confirmed by recent works that partial substitution of Cs $ ^+ $  by larger cations like MA $ ^+ $  and FA $ ^+ $ ,  $ ^{24,27,28} $  or partial substitution of I $ ^- $  ion by smaller Br $ ^- $  ion could stabilize the perovskite phase.  $ ^{37,43} $  On the other hand, Snaith et al. proved

that  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  could be stabilized at room temperature under inert atmosphere by adding a small amount of hydroiodic acid (HI) in the precursor solution and a preliminary PCE of 2.9% was obtained. $ ^{44} $  Luo et al. further developed this method by a sequential isopropanol (IPA) treatment process. $ ^{45} $  The  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  could be further stabilized at room temperature under ambient air for several days, and the champion solar cell achieved a PCE of 4.13%. The HI induced microstrain in the crystal lattice was believed to be the major reason for the stabilization of  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  at room temperature. Hence, we hypothesized that by partially substituting Pb $ ^{2+} $  (1.19 Å) with a smaller cation also could cause the lattice distortion to produce microstrain in the crystal lattice and stabilize the  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  at room temperature. Recently, other and our group's parallel studies proved that the incorporation of Bi $ ^{3+} $  (1.03 Å) into MAPbBr $ _3 $  and CsPbBr $ _3 $  is capable of narrowing the bandgap and improving the electrical conductivity. $ ^{47,48} $  Therefore, it is anticipated that the incorporation of Bi $ ^{3+} $  would further stabilize the  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  phase at room temperature and the solar cells based on the Bi-incorporated CsPbI $ _3 $  film would exhibit improved PCE and stability.

Herein, we attempt to stabilize the  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  at room temperature by partial substitution of “B”-site of the perovskite with Bi $ ^{3+} $  for the first time. By tuning the stoichiometric ratio of Bi $ ^{3+} $ , Bi-incorporated  $ \alpha $ -CsPb $ _{1-x} $ Bi $ _x $ I $ _3 $  compounds with excellent stability as well as improved light absorption and electrical conductivity are obtained. Fully inorganic CsPb $ _{1-x} $ Bi $ _x $ I $ _3 $  solar cells in planar-architecture with CuI as the hole transport material demonstrate remarkable photovoltaic performances. At an optimal condition (incorporation of 4 mol% Bi $ ^{3+} $ ), the solar cell achieve a high PCE of 13.21% which is far exceed the devices based on the controlled  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  (8.07%). The optimal solar cell shows remarkable stability for 168 h under ambient conditions at 25 °C with relative humidity (RH) of 55%, maintaining 68% of the initial PCE without encapsulation.

In summary, we have proved that the incorporation of   $  Bi^{3+}  $   in   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   could stabilize the black   $  \alpha  $  -phase at room temperature for the first time. The Bi-doped   $  \alpha  $  -CsPb  $  _{1-x}  $  Bi  $  _{x}  $  I  $  _{3}  $   (0≤x<0.1) compounds exhibit superior photoelectric properties and moisture stability than the pure   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  . We also found suitable control of the Bi ratios in the precursor solution is critical for the complete transformation from   $  \delta  $  -phase to   $  \alpha  $  -phase and the final device performance. At an optimal condition (doping 4 mol% Bi),   $  CsPb_{0.96}Bi_{0.04}I_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells with CuI as the hole transport material demonstrated a high PCE of 13.21%. To the best of our knowledge, this is the highest PCE for fully inorganic perovskite solar cells so far. The optimal solar cell also showed remarkable moisture stability for maintaining 68% of the initial PCE without encapsulation during 168 h under ambient conditions. Although the efficiencies of our solar cells are still lower than that of the prevailing hybrid halide perovskite solar cells, this work demonstrated the possibility to build a fully inorganic perovskite solar cell with high efficiency. The successful stabilization of   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   at room temperature by partially substituting “B”-site of the perovskite may also helpful to the research of other optoelectronic devices such as photodetectors, light-emitting diodes, and lasers based on the all-inorganic halide perovskites.

All-inorganic perovskite solar cells fabrication: Firstly, a fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) conducting glass (sheet resistance of 8 ohm/square) was ultrasonically cleaned by detergent, deionized water, and acetone for 20 min sequentially, and then treated by a UV/O  $  _{3}  $  cleaner for 15 min.
