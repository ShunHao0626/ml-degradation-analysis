# Designing Perylene Diimide/Fullerene Hybrid as Effective Electron Transporting Material in Inverted Perovskite Solar Cells with Enhanced Efficiency and Stability

Zhenghui Luo, Fei Wu, Teng Zhang, Xuan Zeng, Yiqun Xiao, Tao Liu, Cheng Zhong, Xinhui Lu, Linna Zhu,* Shihe Yang,* and Chuluo Yang*

Abstract: Electron transport materials (ETM) play an important role in the improvement of efficiency and stability for inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs). This work reports an efficient ETM, named PDI- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $ , by the combination of perylene diimide (PDI) and fullerene. Compared to the traditional PCBM, this strategy endow  $ \mathrm{PDI - C_{60}} $  with slightly shallower energy level and higher electron mobility. As a result, the device based on  $ \mathrm{PDI - C_{60}} $  as electron transport layer (ETL) achieves high power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 18.6\% $ , which is significantly higher than those of the control devices of PCBM  $ (16.6\%) $  and PDI  $ (13.8\%) $ . The high PCE of the  $ \mathrm{PDI - C_{60}} $ -based device can be attributed to the more matching energy level with the perovskite, more efficient charge extraction, transport and reduced recombination rate. To the best of our knowledge, the PCE of  $ 18.6\% $  is the highest value in the PSCs using PDI derivatives as ETLs. Moreover, the device with  $ \mathrm{PDI - C_{60}} $  as ETL exhibits better device stability due to the stronger hydrophobic properties of  $ \mathrm{PDI - C_{60}} $ . The strategy using the PDI/fullerene hybrid provides insights for future molecular design of the efficient ETM for the inverted PSCs.

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted extensive attention due to multiple advantages, such as large absorption coefficient, wide absorption range, negligible hysteric behavior, high carrier mobility, long carrier diffusion lifetime and length.  $  ^{[1]}  $   Among the photovoltaic solar cells, PSCs are the fastest growing class, with certified power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) from 3.8% in 2009 to 23.3% in 2018.  $  ^{[2]}  $   Generally, PSCs have two different device architectures,

including the inverted p-i-n and the conventional n-i-p configurations.  $  ^{[3]}  $   For the n-i-p configuration, the perovskite layer is sandwiched between a top hole transporting layer (HTL) and a bottom ETL.  $  ^{[4]}  $   However, the applied hygroscopic dopants in the n-i-p configuration affect the stability of the device, and the high temperature calcined metal oxide increased the cost of device preparation.  $  ^{[5]}  $

The p-i-n devices, also known as inverted PSCs, with a bottom HTL and an ETL on top of perovskite layer, can avoid using hygroscopic dopants and the high temperature calcination process compared to the conventional PSCs.  $  ^{[6]}  $   Besides, the inverted PSCs tend to exhibit less hysteresis effect.  $  ^{[7]}  $   All the merits make inverted PSCs promising candidate for future commercialization fabrication. However, the ETMs limit the further development of p-i-n devices.  $  ^{[8]}  $   Currently, fullerene derivatives (for example, [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PC  $  _{61}  $  BM)) have been widely employed in inverted PSCs owing to their low-temperature solution processability, high electron mobility, proper energy level and effective passivating perovskite trap-state ability.  $  ^{[9]}  $   Nevertheless, the photochemical instability of PCBM limits its further improvements in device performance and stability.  $  ^{[10]}  $   Apart from fullerene derivatives, some large n-type planar π-conjugated organic molecules have been also introduced as ETMs in the p-i-n devices, including PDI derivatives,  $  ^{5a, [11]}  $   hexaazatrinaphthylene (HATNA) derivatives,  $  ^{[12]}  $   and ITIC analogues.  $  ^{[13]}  $   Among these π-conjugated organic materials, PDI derivatives have received widespread attention due to the large conductivity and high mobility.  $  ^{[14]}  $   Moreover, PDI derivatives have been proved to offer good isolation for MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   thin films, passivate surface trap and prevent the perovskite decomposition.  $  ^{5a, 6d, [15]}  $   However, the device performance of PDIs as ETL is not desirable in PSCs.

At the current stage, both PDI-based small molecules and fullerene derivatives display some merits and limitations in the application for perovskite solar cells. The device efficiencies by using PDI derivatives as ETL are not high, but their device stability is good; on the contrary, the fullerene derivative-based devices give decent PCEs, but unsatisfactory device stability. In this context, we skillfully incorporate of PDI with fullerene to yield PDI/fullerene hybrid, namely PDI- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  (Scheme 1), which is expected to combine the advantages of both structures. In comparison with PCBM,  $ \mathrm{PDI - C_{60}} $  exhibits improved electron mobility and molecular ordering due to the introduction of large planar  $ \pi $ -conjugated structure of PDI unit. Additionally, some atoms or atomic groups with lone pair electrons in PDI, such as  $ \mathbf{N},^{6d} $  and  $ \mathbf{C} = \mathbf{O} $  provide lone pair electrons to form coordination bonds with under-coordinated Pb atoms, $ ^{9f} $  which is favorable for enhancing the device stability. The synthetic route of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  is displayed in Scheme 1. PDI underwent electrophilic substitution reaction to give the brominated PDI, followed by Suzuki-coupling with 4-formylphenylboronic acid to obtain PDI-CHO. The target product PDI- $  C_{60}  $  was efficiently synthesized by refluxing N-methyl glycine,  $  C_{60}  $ -fullerene and PDI-CHO in toluene. The target product PDI- $  C_{60}  $  was characterized by  $  ^{1}  $ H-NMR,  $  ^{13}  $ C-NMR, MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry and elemental analysis (EA) (see Supporting Information). PDI- $  C_{60}  $  exhibits good solubility in normal solvents due to the flexible alkyl chains, which makes it applicable to the solution-processed PSCs. As shown in Figure S1, PDI- $  C_{60}  $  shows quite good thermal stability, and its decomposition temperature is close to 400 °C.

The LUMO level of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  was investigated by the electrochemical cyclic voltammetry (CV) (Figure 1a). The LUMO level of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  estimated from the onset reduction potentials is -3.96 eV, which is slightly higher than that of PCBM (-4.0 eV). $  ^{52}  $  The favorable LUMO level of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  is beneficial to minimize energy loss for high-performance invert PSCs. Based on the optical bandgap of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  (2.02 eV) in film (Figure 1b), the HOMO value is calculated to be -5.98 eV, indicating the good hole blocking ability. Neat PDI- $  C_{60}  $  film shows broader and redshifted absorption spectra with respect to it solution one, indicative of strong intermolecular aggregations, which may contribute to the electron mobility. Consequently, we employ space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method to assess the electron mobility of PCBM and PDI- $  C_{60}  $  (Figure S2). The electron mobility of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  is  $  8.76 \times 10^{-4} \text{ cm}^2 \text{ V}^{-1} \text{ s}^{-1}  $ . The slightly higher electron mobility of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  over PCBM ( $  6.01 \times 10^{-4} \text{ cm}^2 \text{ V}^{-1} \text{ s}^{-1}  $ ) facilitates for the electron transport. Additionally, the grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) experiments were performed to investigate the molecular packing and crystallinity of PDI- $  C_{60}  $ , PCBM and PDI neat films (Figure S3). The two ETMs can cover on the perovskite layer well, and the root-mean-square (RMS) roughness are 9.81 nm and  $ 8.77\mathrm{nm} $  for  $ \mathrm{PDI - C_{60}} $  and PCBM, respectively. The cross-sectional scanning electron microscope (SEM) image (Figure S4c) indicate the excellent contact between ETL and perovskite interface. In addition, the perovskite layer that expands across the entire thickness confirms large coverage on the substrate.

As a result, the optimized PSCs based on  $ \mathrm{PDI - C_{60}} $  as ETL gave a PCE of  $ 18.6\% $  with a  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of  $ 1061~\mathrm{mV} $ , a  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  of  $ 22.1~\mathrm{mA} $   $ \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $ , and a FF of 0.792. To the best of our knowledge, the PCE of  $ 18.6\% $  is the highest value in the PSCs using PDI derivatives as ETLs (Table S2). For comparison, PCBM-based device delivers a lower PCE of  $ 16.6\% $  with a  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of  $ 1027~\mathrm{mV} $ , a  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  of  $ 21.0~\mathrm{mA} $   $ \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $ , and a FF of 0.767 under the identical condition. In addition, PDI-based device gives the lowest PCE of  $ 13.8\% $ , which mainly results from the low FF ( $ 62.6\% $ ). The low FF in the PDI-based device is related to the poor film morphology of PDI. Compared with the PCBM-based control devices,  $ \mathrm{PDI - C_{60}} $ -based devices exhibit the improved  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ ,  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  and FF simultaneously. The higher  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  may be related to the more suitable energy levels of  $ \mathrm{PDI - C_{60}} $ , which is conducive to achieving a lower energy loss; the enhanced  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  and FF may result from the more favorable morphology and effective electron extraction ability of the  $ \mathrm{PDI - C_{60}} $ -based device. As shown in Figure 2b,  $ \mathrm{PDI - C_{60}} $ -based device shows higher photoresponses in the wavelength range of  $ 400 - 800~\mathrm{nm} $  relative to that of PCBM-based device of. The integrated  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  from the EQE curves are 21.1, 20.2 and 20.1 mA  $ \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $  for the devices based on  $ \mathrm{PDI - C_{60}} $ , PDI and PCBM as ETLs, respectively, coincide well with those from the  $ J - V $  curves.

As shown in Figure 2c, the hysteresis of the device based on PDI-  $  C_{60}  $  as ETL was investigated via scanning the J-V curves in forward and reverse direction. The J-V curves overlap fairly well in two different scanning directions, and the forward scanning gives an efficiency of 18.4% (Table S3), which is quite close to the reverse scanning value (18.6%). The negligible hysteresis suggests that ion migration can be efficiently suppressed. The stabilized PCE and  $  J_{SC}  $  under a voltage bias of 0.90 V are shown in Figure 2d. A stabilized power output of 18.2% and a stable photocurrent density of 20.23 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  within 200 s are recorded for the PDI-  $  C_{60}  $ -based device.

Steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectroscopy were employed to survey the charge extraction properties of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  as an ETL. As shown in Figure 3a, the perovskite thin film displays strong and broad emission in the

wavelength of 700-850 nm when excited at 480 nm, while the PL emission are effectively quenched after coating the PCBM or PDI- $  C_{60}  $  on the perovskite film. The higher quenching efficiency reveals that PDI- $  C_{60}  $  can suppress electron-hole recombination effectively. In addition, the PL decay time of bare perovskite device was calculated to be about 125.1 ns, upon depositing PCBM or PDI- $  C_{60}  $  on the perovskite layer, the PL decay time significantly decrease to 24.1 ns and 8.36 ns, respectively. Notably, the faster PL decay implies the more efficient charge extraction in PDI- $  C_{60}  $ -based film, which could be a reason for the higher  $  J_{SC}  $  and FF in the PDI- $  C_{60}  $ -based device relative to that of the device based on PCBM.

To investigate the charge transport properties of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  as ETL, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was performed. As shown in Figure S5, EIS was measured at an applied voltage of 0.90 V under dark, and the illustration describes an equivalent circuit model consisting of a series resistance ( $  R_s  $ ) and a recombination resistance ( $  R_{rec}  $ ) at the ETL/perovskite interface, $ ^{[16]} $  Apparently, PDI- $  C_{60}  $ -based device exhibits larger  $  R_{rec}  $  relative to that of PCBM-based device, indicating that the more reduced recombination rate occurred in the PDI- $  C_{60}  $ -based device, $ ^{[17]} $  and thus leaded to a higher FF.

The device stability of PCBM and PDI- $  C_{60}  $  as ETLs was evaluated under ambient conditions with 35-40% humidity without encapsulation (Figure 4a). It is observed that the PDI- $  C_{60}  $ -based device still remained about 75% of its original peak value after 500 hours, while PCBM-based device kept only 50% of the original performance. We performed a series of contact angle measurements of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  and  $  PC_{61}BM  $  films to investigate the influence of different chemical structures on surface properties (Figure 4b). A slightly larger water contact angle of 100.4° was recorded for the PDI- $  C_{60}  $  film surface relative to that of PCBM surface (93.3°), which revealed an increased hydrophobic tendency caused by the PDI unit. The ethylene glycol contact angles of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  and  $  PC_{61}BM  $  films displayed a similar trend to that of the water contact angles. In addition, as shown in Table S4, the surface energy ( $  \gamma^{tot}  $ ) of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  and  $  PC_{61}BM  $  were calculated to be 27.0 and 32.1 mN/m, respectively. The lower  $  \gamma^{tot}  $  and stronger hydrophobic properties of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  over  $  PC_{61}BM  $  result in better device stability.

In order to further investigate the other potential applications of PDI- $  C_{60}  $ , we employed the PDI- $  C_{60}  $  as the interlayer in the invert PSCs with the structure of ITO/P3CT-Na/perovskite/interlayer/ $  C_{60}  $ /BCP/Ag. We optimized the concentration of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  from 0.6 to 2 mg/mL (Figure S7a, Table S5 and Table S6). The champion PCEs of the device based on PDI- $  C_{60}  $  as interlayer were recorded at the concentration of 1.0 mg/MI, and the corresponding thickness is about 4.8 nm. Apparently, we find that devices with PDI- $  C_{60}  $  as interlayer are more efficient than devices without the interlayer. The device without PDI- $  C_{60}  $  achieves a PCE of 17.5% with a  $  V_{OC}  $  of 1068 mV, a  $  J_{SC}  $  of 21.2 mA cm $  ^{-2}  $ , and a FF of 0.775. Significantly, when adding PDI- $  C_{60}  $  as interlayer, a higher PCE of 20.2% was recorded, with a  $  V_{OC}  $  of 1102 mV, a  $  J_{SC}  $  of 22.5 mA cm $  ^{-2}  $ , and a FF of 0.816. It is worth mentioned that the PCE over 20% and the FF of 0.816 is among the highest values reported for PSCs so far. These results indicate that the introduction of PDI- $  C_{60}  $  improved the electron transport and passivate surface trap, thereby increasing  $  V_{OC}  $ ,  $  J_{SC}  $  and FF simultaneously. The calculated  $  J_{SC}  $  values from the EQE spectra are 21.3 and 20.3 mA cm $  ^{-2}  $  for the device with and without PDI- $  C_{60}  $  (Figure S7b), respectively. In addition, the J-V curves do not show any difference in two different scanning directions, indicating the little hysteresis in the PSCs (Figure S7c and Table S7). As shown in Figure S7d, the statistical distribution range of PCE is quite narrow (18.9-20.2%), revealing good reproducibility of the invert cells.

In summary, we develop a novel PDI/fullerene hybrid (PDI- $ C_{60} $ ) as an efficient ETM. Compared with PCBM, this strategy endowed PDI- $ C_{60} $  with slightly shallower HOMO and LUMO values, higher electron mobility and stronger hydrophobic capacity. As a result, the device based on PDI- $ C_{60} $  as ETL achieves an excellent PCE of 18.6%. The improved PCE should be attributed the enhanced FF,  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  and  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  simultaneously. The more efficient charge extraction, transport and reduced recombination rate account for higher FF and  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  as indicated by PL, TRPL and EIS measurements. Moreover, the device with PDI- $ C_{60} $  as ETL exhibits better device stability due to its strong hydrophobic capacity and good isolation for the MAPbI3 film. In addition, PDI- $ C_{60} $  was also employed as the interlayer, and the device with PDI- $ C_{60} $  as interlayer gave a high PCE of 20.2% with a FF as high as 0.816. The success of the PDI/fullerene hybrid strategy shine a light on the molecular design of the efficient ETL for the invert PSCs.
