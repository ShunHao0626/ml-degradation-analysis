# Abstract:

Inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted tremendous attention recently but the energy levels between the perovskite absorber and conventional hole transport layers (HTL) are mismatch, resulting in the lower open-circuit voltages ( $ V_{oc} $ ) than that of regular PSCs. Herein, a gradient heterojunction (GHJ) based on poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene:polystyrenesulphonate) (PEDOT:PSS)/PEDOT:PSS- $ \mathrm{VO}_{\mathrm{x}} $  was constructed in situ by low-temperature annealing and used as HTL of the inverted PSCs. This GHJ structure fabricated

conveniently by doping a small amount of triisopropoxyvanadium oxide isopropyl alcohol solution into the PEDOT:PSS solution during spin-coating can efficiently facilitate charge separation and improve charge extraction efficiency, leading to significantly improved PSC performance with  $  V_{oc}  $  up to 1.02 V and power conversion efficiency (PCE) to 18.0%. More impressively, owing to the more hydrophobic surface and lower acidity than the PEDOT:PSS layer after the formation of high work function  $  \mathrm{VO}_{\mathrm{x}}  $  mainly on the surface of HTL, the GHJ-based PSCs show excellent long-term stability, which retain over 80% or 70% of their initial PCEs after exposure to full spectrum illumination in  $  \mathrm{N}_2  $  for 750 h or in air for 175 h, respectively. These results illustrate the significant advantages of the in situ formed  $  \mathrm{VO}_{\mathrm{x}}  $ -modified HTLs in gradient structures using organic  $  \mathrm{VO}_{\mathrm{x}}  $  precursors, providing important clues in constructing GHJ for inverted PSCs with high efficiency and stability.

# Introduction

With power conversion efficiency (PCE) exceeding 23%, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been recognized as the one of the most promising photovoltaic devices.  $  ^{[1, 2]}  $   Varies PSC structures have been developed, including planar heterojunction, tandem architecture and mesoporous scaffold.  $  ^{[3-5]}  $   Among them, inverted planar p-i-n structure has gradually gained a growing interest, owing to mainly the excellent steady-state output originated from its negligible hysteresis and low temperature fabrication.  $  ^{[6, 7]}  $   The PCEs of inverted PSCs have boomed from 3.9% to 21.5%, largely due to the continuous efforts in optimizing device structures by using various electrode buffer layers.  $  ^{[8, 9]}  $   Hole transport layer (HTL) is a key component of PSCs which plays a critical role in efficiently extracting photogenerated holes and preventing the charge recombination at interface.  $  ^{[10]}  $   Specifically, PCE is determined by open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ), short-circuit current

density ( $ J_{sc} $ ) and fill factor (FF); all of which are closely related to the hole extraction efficiency of HTL. $ ^{[11]} $

Owing to the excellent solution processibility, good film morphology and high conductivity, poly(ethylenedioxythiophene): poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) is widely adopted as the HTL of inverted PSCs.  $  ^{[12, 13]}  $   However, the hydrophilicity and acidity of PEDOT:PSS may result in deprived stability of the devices.  $  ^{[13]}  $   Moreover, the work function of PEDOT:PSS (  $  \sim 4.9-5.1  $   eV) is lower than that of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   layer (  $  \sim 5.5  $   eV), leading to an energy loss and a lower   $  V_{oc}  $   (normally < 1.0 V) compared to PSCs with mesoporous scaffold.  $  ^{[14-16]}  $   Many efforts have been made to address the aforementioned issues of PEDOT:PSS HTLs.  $  ^{[15, 17-23]}  $   Xue et al prepared a dopamine copolymerized PEDOT:PSS with a deep work function (  $  \sim 5.33  $   eV) to relief the imperfect energy level between perovskite and HTL of the inverted PSCs, resulting in 14% higher in PCE than the control devices.  $  ^{[15]}  $   Chen et al modified PEDOT:PSS using dimethylformamide to improve the conductivity and reduce surface roughness, which improves PCE from 11.8% to 16.7%.  $  ^{[22]}  $   Sun et al developed a facile and simple method to promote the hole extraction and conductivity by adding sodium chloride into PEDOT:PSS for the increased PCE from 15.1% to 17.1%.  $  ^{[23]}  $   Bian et al attempted to use VO  $  _{x}  $   nanoparticles to tune the work function of PEDOT:PSS, leading to increased   $  V_{oc}  $   and PCE up to 1.05 V and 17.5%, respectively  $  ^{[6]}  $  . Yan et al also use VO  $  _{x}  $   nanoparticles to fabricate double-decked VO  $  _{x}  $ /PEDOT:PSS HTL to enhance the charge transfer rate and suppress the charge recombination for the increased PCE from 13.9% to 15.9%; the relatively lower device performance should be due to the high surface roughness induced by the heavy nanoparticles aggregating effects using this direct VO  $  _{x}  $   nanoparticles modification strategy.  $  ^{[24-25]}  $

Here, we constructed, for the first time, a gradient heterojunction (GHJ) using PEDOT:PSS/PEDOT:PSS-  $  VO_{x}  $  to suppress the nonradiative recombination and improve the hole extraction for the high-performance inverted PSCs. The GHJ was successfully constructed by doping triisopropoxyvanadium oxide (  $  C_{9}H_{21}O_{4}V  $  ) into the PEDOT:PSS solution for spin-coating followed by a low-temperature thermal treatment. We found that the organic  $  VO_{x}  $  precursor in PEDOT:PSS will form in situ a gradient heterostructure after the low temperature annealing and the GHJ can facilitate charge separation and improve charge extraction efficiency  $  ^{[26-28]}  $  due to the introduction of high work function  $  VO_{x}  $ , leading to a  $  V_{oc}  $  up to 1.02 V and a PCE to 18.0%, respectively. These values are 44% higher in PCE than conventional planar heterojunction (PHJ) devices based on PEDOT:PSS and are among the best results reported for the PEDOT:PSS/MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ -based inverted PSCs. More interestingly, with a more hydrophobic surface and lower acidity than PEDOT:PSS layer, the GHJ PSCs show good stability with only 20% or 30% PCE loss after exposure to full spectrum illumination in  $  N_{2}  $  atmosphere for 750 h or in air for 175 h, respectively. These results are promising, highlighting the significant advantages of the  $  VO_{x}  $ -modified HTL via GHJ design strategy for high-performance inverted PSCs.

# Results and Discussion

A facile two-step process was developed to prepare the GHJ PSCs based on PEDOT:PSS and   $  VO_{x}  $   as shown in Figure 1a. Firstly, PEDOT:PSS was spin-coated onto the ITO substrates followed by a   $  120^{\circ}C  $   annealing for 20 min. In the second step, PEDOT:PSS solution doped with 6.9 mg/mL of   $  C_{9}H_{21}O_{4}V  $   was deposited onto the PEDOT:PSS film surface followed by thermal annealing at   $  150^{\circ}C  $   for 20 min. It should be noted that   $  C_{9}H_{21}O_{4}V  $   is an organic   $  VO_{x}  $   precursor, which has a different electrostatistic feature in comparison to that of PEDOT:PSS and may act as

an important fact to form in situ the GHJ structure of PEDOT:PSS/PEDOT:PSS-  $  VO_{x}  $  . The GHJ structure with gradient energy level is highly attractive in simultaneously supporting effective hole extraction and preventing the charge recombination at interfaces for high device performance of inverted PSCs (Figure 1b).

To experimentally confirm the formation of the  $ \mathrm{VO_x} $  and GHJ structure, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were carried out. The XPS spectrum, corresponding to vanadium in two different oxidation states, can be well fitted by a Gaussian function. The presence of  $ \mathrm{V}^{4+} $  ions in  $ \mathrm{VO_x} $  with oxygen vacancies could enhance the  $ \mathrm{VO_x} $  film conductivity [29, 30]. From Figure 2a, the atomic concentration ratio of  $ \mathrm{V}^{5+} $  to  $ \mathrm{V}^{4+} $  is calculated to be about 3.08:1, indicating that  $ 24.5\% $   $ \mathrm{V}^{4+} $  is present in  $ \mathrm{VO_x} $  layer. From the atomic concentration ratio of  $ \mathrm{V}^{5+} $  to  $ \mathrm{V}^{4+} $ , it is also estimated that the value of x in  $ \mathrm{VO_x} $  is about 2.38. To confirm the  $ \mathrm{VO_x} $  doping of PEDOT:PSS in a gradient distribution, we used XPS characterization with argon ion etching. As shown in Figure 2b, the V 2p signal decreased gradually after the argon time increasing, revealing V element is in gradient distribution.

Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements were performed to evaluate the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) levels of the surface of PHJ base on PEDOT:PSS and GHJ layer (Figure S1). The HOMO levels of PHJ layer was calculated to be -4.97 eV. Owing to the presence of   $  VO_{x}  $   in PEDOT:PSS, which has a higher work function (  $  \sim 5.60  $   eV),  $  ^{[25]}  $   the HOMO level of GHJ surface was deep and estimated to be   $  \sim 5.54  $   eV. The transmittance spectra of the GHJ layer on indium tin oxide (ITO) substrates is shown in Figure S2, it reveals that the GHJ layer slightly change the transmittance of the ITO/PEDOT:PSS substrate.

The smaller MAPbI $ _3 $  gain size on GHJ layer should be due to a higher nucleation center density with the introduction of VO $ _x $ , since the increased density of the nucleation site might lead to the formation of smaller grain size of MAPbI $ _3 $  film.

The charge transport and extraction, which are significantly influenced by electrode buffer layers (ETL or HTL), are crucial for the final performance of PSCs. Therefore, to elucidate the effect of GHJ layer on charge dynamics, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film coated bare ITO, PHJ and GHJ layer were measured with excitation at  $ 450~\mathrm{nm} $  (Figure 4a and b). From Figure 4a, it can be found that the PL is quenched obviously for the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film coated on GHJ layer. Furthermore, the TRPL measurements show the similar results. The stretched exponential decay lifetimes of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films coated on ITO, PHJ and GHJ surface were 16.3, 9.5, and 4.3 ns, respectively. The

To understand the charge recombination losses, light-intensity (I) dependent  $ J-V $  measurements were also carried out. The dependence of  $ V_{oc} $  on light-intensity in PHJ or GHJ PSCs is shown in Figure 4c. Slope deviation from unity kT/q indicates the occurrence of trap-assisted Shockley-Read-Hall recombination in PSCs (k is the Boltzmann constant, T is absolute temperature and q is elementary charge), which is regarded as the dominant recombination pathway in PSCs.[33] The slope for the PHJ device (1.76 kT/q) is much larger than that for GHJ device (1.02 kT/q). This result indicates that trap-assisted nonradiative recombination was more suppressed in the GHJ PSCs, leading to a higher PCE. The variation in  $ J_{sc} $  as light-intensity variation is depicted in Figure 4d. The dependence of  $ J_{sc} $  upon I can be used an a low as  $ J_{sc} \propto (\mathrm{I})^{\alpha} $ . A device with no space charge effects will have a power law relationship with  $ \alpha $  close to 1. The slopes ( $ \alpha $ ) for PHJ and GHJ PSCs are 1.13 and 1.04 respectively, indicates that the GHJ PSCs have less space charge effect and lower charge losses.[34-37] It also suggested that the favorable matching energy level between GHJ layer and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film enables the holes to be extracted from  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer faster without charges accumulation. Moreover, the GHJ device showed 1 order of lower dark current density compared to the PHJ device (Figure S8), indicates that the current leakage can be dramatically suppressed with a GHJ layer modification. Finally, we evaluated the long-term stability of GHJ PSCs under different environmental atmosphere. Figure 5a compares the stability of the PHJ and GHJ PSCs without encapsulation under full spectrum illumination in  $ \mathrm{N}_2 $  atmosphere. The PCE of PHJ PSCs reduces to  $ 50\% $  of the initial efficiency within  $ 750\mathrm{h} $ . However, the PCE of the GHJ PSCs retained over  $ 82\% $  under the same condition, showing excellent stability. Meanwhile we also monitored the stability under ambient conditions (humidity of  $ 30\sim 50\% $ ; temperature at  $ 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) (Figure 5b). After exposure to air for  $ 175\mathrm{h} $ , the GHJ PSCs dropped to over  $ 70\% $  of initial PCE, while the PHJ PSCs reduced to nearly zero. Further improvement of the PSCs stability could be ascribed to a more hydrophobic surface of GHJ surface ( $ 32.9^{\circ} $ ) and lower acidity ( $ \mathrm{pH} $  increased from 2.19 to 4.14) as  $ \mathrm{VO_x} $  doping in PEDOT:PSS solution.

# Conclusions

In summary, a gradient heterojunction structure based on PEDOT:PSS/PEDOT:PSS-  $  VO_{x}  $  was facilely fabricated in a two-step procedure and used, for the first time, in the inverted PSCs. Owing to the in situ formation of the high work function  $  VO_{x}  $ , the newly established GHJ can facilitate charge separation and improve charge extraction efficiency, leading to significantly improved PSC performance with  $  V_{oc}  $  up to 1.02 V and PCE to 18.0%, which are 44% higher in PCE than conventional PHJ devices based on PEDOT:PSS. More importantly, with a more hydrophobic surface and lower acidity, the GHJ PSCs show excellent long-term stability, which retain over 70% of their initial PCE after exposure to full spectrum illumination in air for 175 h. This GHJ strategy using organic  $  VO_{x}  $  precursors could provide important guidelines for the

Highlights:

1. Gradient heterojunction (GHJ) was facilely constructed by organic  $ \mathrm{VO}_{\mathrm{x}} $  precursors and used, for the first time, in the inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs).

2. This in situ formed GHJ structure can efficiently facilitate charge separation and improve charge extraction efficiency for significantly improved PSC performance with  $  V_{oc}  $  up to 1.02 V and PCE to 18.0%.

3. With increased hydrophobic surface and reduced acidity, the GHJ PSCs show excellent long-term stability.
