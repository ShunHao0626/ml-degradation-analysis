# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) have recently become a hot research field for photovoltaic applications due to their excellent optoelectronic properties such as strong broadband light-harvesting ability, long exciton diffusion lengths, little exciton binding energy, high charge-carrier mobility and ambipolar charge-transporting characteristics   $  [1-6]  $  . The ambipolar semiconducting capacities further enable variable device architectures, ranging from mesoscopic to planar structures with n-i-p or p-i-n architectures   $  [7-12]  $  . The mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based pero-SCs with power conversion efficiencies (PCE) surpassing 20% on laboratory-type cells   $  [9]  $   are already promising as a close competitor to crystalline silicon solar cells. However, the high-temperature annealing is adopted for   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based pero-SCs which complicate the fabrication of these devices

# ABSTRACT

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) have attracted tremendous attention recently because of their excellent photovoltaic performance. Simultaneously achieving high performance and long-term stability mainly relies on improving the quality of the perovskite film and overcoming the interface losses through interface engineering. Here, we demonstrate a high performance ITO/CuSCN-based planar heterojunction pero-SCs using solution-processable perylene–diimide derivative PDINO as cathode buffer layer. The device showed a high open-circuit voltage   $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $   of 1.05 V, a hysteresis-free photovoltaic response and a maximum power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 16.4%. The high performance can be ascribed to the proper choice of the anode and cathode interface materials. In comparison with the commonly used PEDOT:PSS anode buffer layer, the CuSCN anode buffer layer can increase the built-in potential and decrease the interfacial energy barrier with   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , which results in higher   $  V_{oc}  $  . The thickness-insensitive PDINO cathode buffer layer is beneficial to higher short circuit current density. The results indicate that the combination of the CuSCN anode buffer layer and PDINO cathode buffer layer is an effective way to get high photovoltaic performance of the p-i-n planar pero-SCs.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1.Materials

Methylamine solution (40 wt% in ethanol), hydriodic acid (57 wt% in   $  H_{2}O  $  ) and lead (II) iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  , 99.999%) were purchased from Alfa or Sigma-Aldrich. Phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) was obtained from Solamer Materials Inc. Methylammonium iodide (MAI) was synthesized according to the literature by stoichiometrically reacting methylamine with hydriodic acid [23]. The perovskite precursor was prepared by mixing MAI and   $  PbI_{2}  $   in a molar ratio of 1:1 in anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%, Alfa), and the final concentration of the perovskite was controlled to approximately 40 wt%. The solution was stirred overnight at room temperature and filtered with 0.45   $  \mu  $  m PVDF filters before spin-coating. The molecular structure of PDINO is shown in Fig. 1c, and its synthesis and characterization was reported elsewhere [36].

# 2.2. Solar cell device fabrication

First, the patterned ITO substrates were sequentially ultrasonic cleaned with detergent, deionized water, acetone and isopropanol. On cleaned ITO substrate, PEDOT:PSS aqueous solution filtered through a 0.45   $  \mu  $  m PVDF filters was spin-coated at 4,000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at 150°C in air for 15 min. The CuSCN was potentiostatically electrodeposited for 50 s from an aqueous solution of 12 mmol L  $  ^{-1}  $   copper sulfate (CuSO  $  _{4}  $  ), 12 mmol L  $  ^{-1}  $   ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) and 12 mmol L  $  ^{-1}  $

potassium thiocyanate (KSCN) at a fixed potential of -0.3 V versus Ag/AgCl (3 mol L  $  ^{-1}  $  KCl) [31]. After drying with nitrogen, The as-prepared perovskite precursor solution was spin coated onto the ITO/PEDOT:PSS or ITO/CuSCN substrate at a speed of 5000 r.p.m for 30 s. During the last 4.5 s of the spinning process, chlorobenzene was quickly added to induce the fast crystallization. Then the substrate was dried on a hot plate at 100 °C for 10 min. The PCBM (20 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  in chlorobenzene) and PDINO (1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  in methanol) were then sequentially deposited by spin coating at 1,500 rpm for 30 s and 3,000 rpm for 30 s, respectively. Finally, a 100 nm Al electrode was deposited on PDINO layer under a high vacuum through a shadow mask by thermal evaporation with an active area of 0.05 cm  $  ^{2}  $ .

# 2.3. Characterizations and measurements

The current density–voltage (J-V) characteristics were measured by a computer-controlled Keithley 2450 Source–Measure Unit. The light source was Oriel Sol3A Class AAA Solar Simulator (model, Newport 94,023A) with a 450 W xenon lamp and an air mass (AM) 1.5 filter. The light intensity was calibrated to   $  100 \, mW cm^{-2}  $   by a reference cell (Newport Oriel 91150 V). The EQE spectra were recorded with an Enli Technology (Taiwan) EQE measurement system (QE-R), and the light intensity at each wavelength was calibrated with a standard single-crystal Si photovoltaic cell. The thickness of the film was measured by a Profilometer (Ambios Tech. XP-2). The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was performed in order to investigate the morphology of the perovskite films prepared on top of PEDOT:PSS or CuSCN. Top-view and cross-section images was characterized by HITACHI s-4800 (Hitachi Limited, Japan) using an InLens detector operating at an accelerating voltage of 10 kV. The atomic force microscopy (AFM) images were obtained by utilizing SPA-400 SPM (Seiko Instrument, Inc.). The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were recorded on a D/MAX-2000 X-ray diffractometer with monochromated Cu Kα irradiation (  $  \lambda = 1.5418 \, \AA  $  ). The electrodeposition of the CuSCN layer and the EIS measurements were performed on a Zennium electrochemical workstation (Zahner Instrument, Inc.).

# 3. Results and discussion

The device structure of the p-i-n PHJ pero-SCs is shown in Fig. 1a and further confirmed by cross-section SEM image as illustrated in Fig. 1e. The CuSCN layer is electro-deposited on a prepared indium tin oxide (ITO)-coated glass as a HTL and tight underlying layer for   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   layer to fabricate the PHJ pero-SCs. Different thickness of CuSCN layer can be obtained by systematically varying the electrodeposition time, and the optimized thickness of CuSCN layer is 55 nm (Fig. S1 in Supplementary Information (SI)). As presented in Fig. 1d, the ITO electrode can be almost fully covered with the CuSCN layer which is expected to efficiently block contact between the ITO and the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   layer. The photo-harvesting layer,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , is then introduced using a one-step fast deposition-crystallization method [31] with an optimized thickness of 300 nm (Fig. S2 in SI). The electron transport layer consists of PCBM and PDINO layer beneath the Al cathode. The corresponding molecular structure of PDINO is shown in Fig. 1c. The optimized thickness for PCBM, PDINO, and Al is 20, 15, and 100 nm, respectively.

As seen from the energy levels diagram in Fig. 1b, the energy levels of CuSCN,  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ , PCBM and PDINO are well aligned and suggest efficient charge extraction. The work function of CuSCN as determined by Kelvin Probe is  $ -5.3\mathrm{eV} $ , which is slightly higher than the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level of  $ -5.4\mathrm{eV} $  of the perovskite layer and indicates a direct and efficient hole transfer from the perovskite to CuSCN. Photocurrent hysteresis effect is often observed for the pero-SCs with the   $  TiO_{2}  $   electron selective contact [37]. This hysteresis may be due to either the charge traps of the low quality perovskite film, ferroelectric properties of perovskite material, the electromigration of ions in the perovskite layers, and the trap states on the surface [38]. Here we also use forward and reverse voltage scan directions to measure J-V curves and the results are displayed in Fig. 6a and b. After fabricated with the PDINO layer, the J-V curves of devices are measured in forward (from negative to positive bias) and reverse (from positive to negative bias) modes under the illumination of AM 1.5 G, 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  . No obvious photocurrent hysteresis is observed by changing the scan directions. The photovoltaic performance without hysteresis could be attributed to that the PCBM passivate charge trap states at the interfaces and

grain boundaries of perovskite, thus results in an effective impact on the suppression in the hysteresis, which agrees well with our and other groups' observations   $  [39]  $  .

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) is further carried out to study the inner   $  R_{s}  $   of the PHJ pero-SCs. We can obtain the detailed electrical properties of the interfaces by the EIS analysis rather than by direct current measurements. The   $  R_{s}  $   is composed of the charge-transfer resistance (  $  R_{CT}  $  ) at the interfaces between different layers and the sheet resistance (  $  R_{sheet}  $  ) of the electrodes. Fig. 6c displays the Nyquist plots of the PHJ pero-SCs with PEDOT:PSS HTL (PEDOT:PSS-based device) and CuSCN HTL (CuSCN-based device) under dark at the same applied voltage, 0.70 V to build an equivalent resistor-capacitor (  $  R_{C}  $  ) circuit [40]. From the Nyquist plots, the semicircle indicates the accuracy of the   $  R_{C}  $   circuit model and confirms each layer is relatively homogeneous along with the transport pathways without having discernible multiple interfacial boundaries [34]. As shown in Fig. 6c the   $  R_{CT}  $   values are 562 and 10 Ω for the PEDOT:PSS-based device and CuSCN-based device, respectively. The smaller   $  R_{CT}  $   of CuSCN-based device is mainly attributed to the high electrical conductivity of the CuSCN [30], which demonstrates there are sufficiently transported and less lost energy of the holes when the hole transport process in the pero-SCs. Therefore, high   $  J_{sc}  $   and PCEs are obtained from the CuSCN-based device. In addition, the   $  R_{CT}  $   at the PEDOT:PSS/CH₃NH₃PbI₃ interface is higher than that at the CuSCN/CH₃NH₃PbI₃ interface may be attributed to the different quality of CH₃NH₃PbI₃ film. As shown in Figs. S3 and Fig. S4 in SI, the CH₃NH₃PbI₃ film on the PEDOT:PSS film exhibits more and larger voids than that on the CuSCN film, which may result in larger   $  R_{CT}  $  .

# 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we have demonstrated that high performance PHJ pero-SCs, with a PCE of  $ 16.4\% $  and a higher  $ V_{oc} $  of  $ 1.05\mathrm{V} $  without hysteresis effect, can be fabricated with a low temperature processed electrodeposited CuSCN HTL in combination with a solution-processed PCBM/PDINO ETL. In comparison with the traditional PEDOT:PSS HTL, CuSCN HTL can increase the built-in potential and decrease the interfacial energy barrier. The solution-processbale and thickness-insensitive PDINO ETL can achieve conformal covering and decrease the contact leakage. Moreover, the photovoltaic performance of the CuSCN-based pero-SCs have good reproducibility and higher stability. All these results indicate that the combination of CuSCN HTL and PDINO ETL is an effective way to improve the photovoltaic performance of the PHJ pero-SCs.
