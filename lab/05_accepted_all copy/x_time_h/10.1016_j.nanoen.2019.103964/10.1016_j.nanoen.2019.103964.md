# Abstract

For commercialization of perovskite solar cells (PSCs), the device stability issues attributed to degradation of perovskite material, photo and thermal instability, and photocurrent density-voltage  $ (J - V) $  hysteresis behavior should be solved. Furthermore, most of presented high efficiency PSCs utilize an antisolvent-strategy to assist perovskite crystal growth with large grain-size and excellent coverage. To solve such issues, we developed fully-ambient-air and antisolvent-free processes for the fabrication of PSCs with perovskite-based composites (i.e.,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x\mathrm{-Cu}: \mathrm{NiO} $ ,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x\mathrm{-Cu}: \mathrm{NiO} $ -carbon-graphite and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x\mathrm{-Al}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $ ). Such perovskite composites based cells with interface engineering yielded high power-conversion-efficiency (PCE) of  $ 18.6\% $  and fill factor (FF) of  $ 78.3\% $  with excellent reproducibility. More importantly, the target cells showed hysteresis-free behavior and dramatic enhancement in air-, photo- and thermal-stability. Compared to the pristine cells, the target device showed remarkable long-term stability with retaining almost  $ 100\% $  of the initial values of  $ V_{oc} $  and  $ J_{sc} $ , and  $ \sim 94\% $  of FF and PCE over 280 days.

Keywords: perovskite solar cells,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x\mathrm{-Cu}: \mathrm{NiO} $ -carbon-graphite composites, ambient-air- and antisolvent-free-processed, hysteresis-free, stability

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on organic-inorganic halide compounds have recently achieved an astounding record-high power-conversion-efficiency (PCE) over  $ 23\% $ , which have been endowed with superb potential for the next-generation imperative photovoltaic (PV) applications towards highly efficient, low-cost, easy-to-produce, lightweight, wearable, building-integrated-semitransparent and tandem solar modules [1-6]. However, PSCs have been struggling with their anomalous photocurrent density-voltage  $ (J - V) $  hysteresis behavior and poor operational stability problems caused by internal factors which relate to interface contacts and interactions, i.e., the chemical incompatibility of the perovskite layer with charge transport layers and electrodes, ion migration and diffusion of electrode materials (i.e., Au and Ag), as well as external factors, such as moisture, oxygen, heat and ultraviolet light. In order to determine device efficiency accuracy, device reproducibility and operation life, the  $ J - V $  hysteresis and stability have to be addressed based on the aboved factors [7-13]. In this regards, two commendable strategies have been made to improve the PCE, reproducibility and stability with suppressing  $ J - V $  hysteresis behavior: (1) developing perovskite composites with organic [14-23] or inorganic materials [24-32] and (2) exploring novel and stable device configurations with interface engineering [5,25-26,33-37]. By combining with experimental and theoretical studies, the perovskite composites with organic or inorganic materials can significantly enhance the intrinsic

More importantly, the perovskite composites with inorganic materials, such as NiO nanostructures [24-27] and carbon-based nanomaterials [29-31,38], are potential for future commercialization.

However, most of presented high efficiency PSCs have utilized an antisolvent-strategy to assist the growth of perovskite crystals having large grain-size, excellent coverage and high uniformity [39-40]. The halogenated antisolvents such as toluene and chlorobenzene are highly toxic and detrimental to environment, and the accurate control of dosing parameters (i.e., antisolvents volume, deposition time and position) is difficult, which have become challenges for future large-scale production [41-52]. More importantly, the antisolvent-processed perovskite film based cells with  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and Spiro-OMeTAD as electron-transport-layer (ETL) and hole-transport-layer (HTL), respectively, show poor operational stability under continuous illumination, attributed to several factors: interfacial collapse,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  photocatalysis effect, charge accumulation at  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite interface, [43-44] and  $ J - V $  hysteresis behavior and degradation of pervoskite molecules attributed to ions migration, [45] and formation of AuI or AgI because of deiffusion of Au or Ag into perovskite.[ 46-47] Thus, several strategies have been explored for solving such barriers, such as substitute of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  with other stable materials, [48-49] use of doped  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  materials, [50-51] and interface engineering with inserting stable interfacial materials

# 2. Materials and Experimental

# 2.1 Materials and solutions preparation

# 2.1.1 Synthesis of methylammonium iodide (CH₃NH₃I)

The  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  (methylammonium iodide, MAI) was prepared following the procedure reported in previous works [24-26]. In brief,  $ 30~\mathrm{mL} $  hydroiodic acid (57 wt% in water, YAKURI) was mixed with  $ 27.92~\mathrm{mL} $  methylamine (40 wt% in water, Sigma-Aldrich) under continuous stirring in an ice bath for  $ 1\mathrm{h} $ , and then the well-mixed solution was evaporated in a rotary evaporation system (SB-1200) to remove the solvents and yield crystal MAI. The as-synthesized MAI was re-dissolved in ethyl alcohol and precipitated with diethyl ether for several times. Finally, the white solid MAI was dried for  $ 24\mathrm{h} $  in a vacuum oven at  $ 600^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ .

# 2.1.2 Synthesis of   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanostructures and Cu doped NiO nanoparticles (Cu:NiO NPs)

For electron transport layer of   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanostructures, 0.86 mmol Tin (IV) chloride pentahydrate (  $  SnCl_{4}\cdot5H_{2}O  $  , Sigma Aldrich) was dissolved in 10 ml of isopropanol (IPA, Sigma Aldrich) under continuous stirring and heating at 60 °C until yielding to a transparent and clear solution, and followed by filtering with a 0.2 μm syringe filter prior to spin-coating. # 2.2 Device Fabrication

All the fabrication processes of the PSCs were carried out under antisolvent-free and ambient-air processes (45-50 % humidity and 25-30 °C). FTO substrates were sequentially cleaned by using detergent, diluted water, ethanol and acetone. As-prepared SnO₂ precursor was spin-coated on FTO substrates at 4000 rpm for 40 s; then, Al₂O₃ solution and Cu:NiO NPs solutions were spin-coated sequentially on FTO/SnO₂ substrates at 4000 rpm for 40 s; next, C-G-Cu:NiO composite solution were drop-casting at 6000 rpm on FTO/SnO₂/Al₂O₃/Cu:NiO substrates; after each step was followed by annealing at 250 °C for 10 min. The pre-heated MAPbI₃-xClₓ-Cu:NiO mixed solution at 130 °C was then spin-coated on the FTO/SnO₂/Al₂O₃/Cu:NiO/(C-G-Cu:NiO composite) at 1500 rpm for 30 s, followed by annealing

A representative PSC configuration consists of an organic-inorganic halide perovskite film sandwiched between TCO/ETL and HTL/metal, thereby yielding TCO/ETL/perovskite and perovskite/HTL/metal interfaces, respectively. Thus, the photoinduced holes and electrons have to transport across the interfaces in the device, leading to charge loss and interfacial degradation because of possible interfacial defects and recombination, and chemical incompatibility between perovskite and contact materials. Moreover, interfacial recombination at the perovskite/charge transport layers often causes non-radiative recombination loss in a cell, leading to large  $  V_{oc}  $  loss that is considerably lower than the radiative limit of quasi-Fermi level splitting  $  V_{oc}  $  (QFLS- $  V_{oc}  $ ). Therefore, such issues extremely affect devices performance. In the previous work, it was reported that the MAPbI $  _{3-x}  $ Cl $  _{x}  $ -NiO composites and Al $  _{2}  $ O $  _{3}  $ /NiO interfacial engineering in a cell of FTO/c-TiO $  _{2}  $ /mp-TiO $  _{2}  $ /Al $  _{2}  $ O $  _{3}  $ /NiO/MAPbI $  _{3-x}  $ Cl $  _{x}  $ -NiO/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au configuration resulted in a  $  PCE  $  of 18.14%, with sustaining its air-stability retaining over 210 days under ambient conditions [25-26].
