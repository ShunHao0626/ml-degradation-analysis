# ABSTRACT

Nonradiative recombination at perovskite/charge transport layer interfaces is caused by surface defects and instability, and it is known to limit the long-term development of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). To overcome this issue, the three-dimensional/two-dimensional (3D/2D) perovskite heterojunction has emerged as a possible solution to improve the stabilities and efficiency of PSCs. Herein, we employ a simple one-step method to prepare n-i-p-structured PSCs using a 3D/2D perovskite heterojunction as the absorption layer. For this purpose, the large and non-centrosymmetric 4,4-difluorocyclohexylammonium (DFCHA  $  ^{+}  $  ) cation, which has been confirmed to be a valid organic spacer in 2D Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) phase perovskites, is employed as an organic ligand for post-treatment of the surfaces of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  films. The presence of an ultrathin 2D RP phase perovskite was confirmed on the surface, and the 3D/2D perovskite heterojunction was successfully constructed. Benefitting from surface post-treatment, the density of the surface trap states was reduced with effective passivation. In addition, nonradiative recombination was suppressed, and the interface bands were aligned. As a result, the optimal device achieved a power conversion efficiency of 21.93% with a remarkable open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.14 V, a current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 23.71 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , and a fill factor of 0.82. Furthermore, owing to the hydrophobicity of the DFCHA  $  ^{+}  $  cation, the unencapsulated device was able to maintain an initial efficiency of 82.3% after storage for 500 h at a relative humidity of ~45%. We believe that this post-treatment strategy has wide application potential in the field of photovoltaic devices.

# I. INTRODUCTION

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites have gained significant attention in the field of photovoltaics owing to their large photon absorption coefficients, high defect tolerances, low binding energies, high charge carrier mobilities, and long diffusion lengths.  $  ^{1-3}  $   In the past ten years, three-dimensional (3D) perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have undergone unprecedented rapid development, boosting the power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 3.8%  $  ^{4}  $   to 25.7%  $  ^{5}  $   under standard 1 sun AM1.5G illumination, which is approaching the Shockley–Queisser limit. However, defects on the surfaces of the

3D perovskite films lead to insufficient charge extraction between the perovskite thin films and the charge transfer layer (CTL), thereby limiting the device performances of PSCs. Moreover, the commercialization of 3D PSCs is hindered by an unsatisfactory stability, $ ^{6} $  and so low-dimensional perovskites have attracted growing attention owing to their superior environmental and long-term stabilities, especially in the case of two-dimensional (2D) Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) phase perovskites. The general formula of the 2D RP perovskites is R $ _{2} $ A $ _{n-1} $ B $ _{n} $ X $ _{3n+1} $ , where R is the large cation of the optional organic barrier layer [such as  $ n $ -butylamine (BA $ ^{+} $ ) or phenylethylamine (PEA $ ^{+} $ )],  $ n $  represents the number of inorganic layers, A

Although 2D RP perovskites tend to exhibit superior stabilities, the performances of devices based on pure 2D RP phase perovskite absorbers are unsatisfactory because of their charge transport anisotropies, large bandgaps, and exciton binding energies.  $  ^{10-12}  $   To overcome this drawback, the construction of 3D/2D heterojunction absorbers shows great potential for improving device performances and stabilities.  $  ^{13-15}  $   In such devices, the growth of an ultrathin 2D RP perovskite on a 3D perovskite surface is able to effectively prevent water infiltration while also acting as a passivation layer to reduce the surface defect density.  $  ^{16,17}  $   For example, Liu et al. prepared a 3D/2D heterojunction using pentafluoro-phenylethylamine (FEA  $  ^{+}  $  ). Benefitting from the enhanced hole extraction and improved surface hydrophobicity of this device, its efficiency exceeded 22%, and an initial efficiency of 90% was retained even after 1000 h of operation.  $  ^{18}  $   In addition, Jeong et al. built a 3D/2D heterojunction absorption layer based on cyclohexylamine, and they found that the PCE increased from 20.41% to 23.91% through the effective passivation of anion defects by ammonium ions (  $  -NH_{3}^{+}  $  ).  $  ^{19}  $   Furthermore, Liu et al. developed a gradient 2D/3D heterojunction engineering strategy to improve the efficiency and stability of PSCs by defect passivation in the bulk and the interface. They found that a PCE of 22.54% could be achieved, and all stability parameters improved significantly.  $  ^{20}  $   These results indicate that the performances of devices based on 3D/2D heterostructures are highly dependent on the structures and performances of the large cations present in the interlayer. Thus, to enhance the photovoltaic performance of devices simply and effectively, and to provide a superior selectivity for the development of PSCs, three potential strategies could be considered, namely, the development of large organic cations as hydrophobic layers, optimization of the related interface engineering, and the construction of 3D/2D heterojunctions as absorption layers.

4,4-Difluorocyclohexylammonium iodide (DFCHAI) is a non-centrosymmetric material that has been reported to react with   $  PbI_{2}  $   to form layered 2D RP phase (DFCHA)  $  _{2}  $  PbI  $  _{4}  $   perovskite products; these products exhibit good ferroelectric properties in addition to controllable spontaneous polarisation.  $  ^{[21]}  $   Although the physical mechanism of the ferroelectric photovoltaic effect (FEPV) and ferroelectricity in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   are controversial, the fluorine (-F) and ammonium (  $  -NH_{3}^{+}  $  ) moieties present in DFCHAI have been demonstrated to improve the photovoltaic performances and stabilities of PSCs.  $  ^{[22,23]}  $   Thus, in the current study, DFCHAI is selected as the passivating agent for treatment of the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite thin film. More specifically, by reacting the PbI  $  _{2}  $   residue with DFCHAI, an ultrathin 2D RP perovskite (DFCHA)  $  _{2}  $  PbI  $  _{4}  $   is formed on the top of the 3D perovskite as a capping layer to produce a 3D/2D heterojunction. The ability of the 2D perovskite to effectively passivate the surface defects of the 3D perovskite is evaluated, in addition to its role in inhibiting nonradiative recombination, and that in improving the interface band alignment. Based on this strategy, the optimal device achieves a PCE of 21.93%, which is the latest record

for polycrystalline MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -based n-i-p-structured PSCs reported to date, and is relatively close to the current record for polycrystalline MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -based PSCs (22.15%).  $  ^{24}  $   Furthermore, the ability of the 2D RP perovskite in preventing moisture diffusion into the perovskite layer to slow decomposition of the perovskite is examined. Finally, the photovoltaic parameters of the n-i-p-structured PSCs are determined, and the effects of post-treatment on the device stability are investigated.

# II. RESULT AND DISCUSSION

Following the preparation of a 3D perovskite film of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  via a one-step spin-coating anti-solvent-assisted deposition method, the film was treated according to the process outlined in Fig. 1(a). To explore the crystallization process taking place on the surfaces of the perovskite films after DFCHAI treatment, the XRD patterns of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films treated with different concentrations of DFCHAI (0, 2, and  $ 10\mathrm{mgml}^{-1} $ ) were collected, along with that of the pure 2D perovskite  $ (\mathrm{DFCHA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $  film [Fig. 1(b)]. The films and devices based on the pure 3D  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  are referred to as the "control," whereas the films treated with  $ 2\mathrm{mgml}^{-1} $  DFCHAI are labeled "DFCHAI-treated," and any other post-treated specimens are identified accordingly. Initially, sharp and clear diffraction peaks with similar intensities were observed for the treated and untreated perovskite films, reflecting the typical cubic phase crystal structure of the 3D  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite. However, at a higher DFCHAI concentration of  $ 10\mathrm{mgml}^{-1} $ , new peaks appeared at 6.36, 12.58, and  $ 25.05^{\circ} $  (solid circles), which were attributed to the 2D RP perovskite,  $ (\mathrm{DFCHA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $ . Furthermore, the intensity of the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  peak at  $ 12.77^{\circ} $  was significantly reduced after DFCHAI treatment [Fig. 1(c)], thereby suggesting that DFCHAI reacts with  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  at the surface during the post-treatment process, and that the product is a 2D RP-phase perovskite, namely,  $ (\mathrm{DFCHA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $ . In the case of the  $ 2\mathrm{mgml}^{-1} $  DFCHAI-treated film, no obvious low-dimensional characteristic peak could be observed, perhaps due to the x rays penetrating the ultrathin 2D RP perovskite film to produce only signals originating from the bulk phase. To verify this consideration, the GIXRD patterns of the  $ 2\mathrm{mgml}^{-1} $  DFCHAI-treated perovskite film were collected using incident angles of 2 and  $ 5^{\circ} $  [Figs. S1 and Fig. 1(d)]. As expected, weak peaks corresponding to the 2D perovskite appeared at 6.36, 12.58, and  $ 25.05^{\circ} $  (solid circles), thereby confirming the formation of a 3D/2D heterojunction. In addition, the crystalline peak at  $ 14.30^{\circ} $  became more intense when the incident angle was  $ 5^{\circ} $ , indicating an improved crystallinity of the perovskite close to the substrate, as expected.

To gain further insight into the effects of surface post-treatment on morphological engineering, scanning electron microscopy and atomic force microscopy (AFM) images were collected for the untreated and DFCHAI-treated  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films. As shown in Fig. S2, the crystal size remained relatively constant after post-processing, as evidenced by the fact that the XRD peak intensity did not change after post-treatment. However, the surface morphology was slightly different, owing to the generation of a 2D phase on the surface of the 3D phase. Moreover, the local grain boundary appeared to be slightly fuzzy, probably due to defect filled by the generated 2D RP perovskite, i.e.,  $ (\mathrm{DFCHA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $ . Furthermore, the PSC based on the 3D/2D heterojunction maintained an initial PCE of 82.3% after ageing for 500 h, which is significantly higher than the initial value of 52.7% achieved for the control device under the same conditions. Previous research has found that the degradation of perovskites is initiated at highly active and diffusive defect sites, such as those found on film surfaces and at grain boundaries.  $  ^{32}  $   Therefore, such enhancement of the long-term stability suggests that   $  (\mathrm{DFCHA})_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{4}  $   can passivate the defects on the surface and retard perovskite degradation.  $  ^{33,34}  $   As shown in Fig. S8, the water contact angles of the untreated and DFCHAI-treated perovskite films were measured, and as expected, the perovskite films displayed higher contact angles after post-treatment, thereby confirming their improved stability under humid conditions. This notable improvement in moisture resistance can be attributed to the hydrophobicity of the large   $  DFCHA^{+}  $  cation, although the presence of F can also effectively protect PSCs from corrosion by water vapor.

# III. CONCLUSION

In summary, we successfully introduced the large fluorinated 4,4-difluorocyclohexylammonium (DFCHA  $  ^{+}  $  ) cation into MA-based PSCs using a post-treatment method. The ultrathin two-dimensional (2D) Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) phase perovskite (DFCHAI)  $  _{2}  $  PbI  $  _{4}  $  was generated on the surface of the three-dimensional (3D) perovskite by reacting with PbI  $  _{2}  $ , and as a result, a 3D/2D heterojunction was constructed as an absorption layer. Benefitting from the passivation of surface defects by the 2D RP perovskite, nonradiative recombination was effectively suppressed in this structure. In addition, the generated 2D perovskite improved the energy-band alignment of the perovskite/HTL interface, promoting the separation and extraction of charges. Moreover, the large fluorinated cations enhanced the hydrophobic properties of the perovskite films to give significantly improved stabilities over long-term usage and under humid conditions. As a result, the MA-based PSCs with an n-i-p structure afforded an optimal PCE of 21.93% after post-treatment with DFCHAI, giving an outstanding open-circuit voltage
