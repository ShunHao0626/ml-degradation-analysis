# 1. Introduction

In comparison to several solar cells like standard silicon ones, one can express that the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are the most important solar cells because of considerably diminishing the reducing fabrication costs and having high power conversion productivity [1,2]. Many manufacturing approaches and new perovskite compounds have been developed to make high-efficiency solar cells [3,4]. For  $ \mathrm{Cs_xFA_{1 - x}Pb} $   $ (\mathrm{I}_{1 - x}\mathrm{Br}_y)_3 $  PSCs, it is evident that with increase open-circuit voltage  $ (\mathrm{V_{OC}}) $  and fill factor (FF), there is a great potential for achieving the improvement in room temperature is evident [5-11]. The significant role of the active layer in the improvement of power conversion efficiency (PCE (%)) is not deniable. However, the interface layer between the electron transport layer (ETL) and active layer should be notable because this layer can enhance stability and performance of PSC. Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on ZnO have strikingly succeeded recently and researchers are attempting to develop the next generation of affordable and high-power conversion efficiency (PCE (%)) of PSC based on ZnO. The aim of this study is to investigate the effect of reduced graphene oxide (rGO) sheet on the PCE (%) enhancement of nickel (Ni)-doped ZnO and silver (Ag)-doped ZnO at 5 wt% as electron-transport layers (ETLs) in planar PSCs. According to results, with a comparison between Ni-doped ZnO and Ag-doped ZnO ETLs, it is obtained that Ag doping at 5 wt% is helpful in charge carrier separation and, consequently, a reduction in the recombination rate by creating defect states for ZnO-based PSCs is obtained. According to J-V characteristics, the electrochemical impedance (EIS) and Mott-Schottky (MS), using rGO sheet interlayer significantly impacts the electrical properties in un-doped and -doped ZnO-based PSCs. According to the results, the rGO sheet can passivate un-doped ZnO and doped-ZnO ETLs as well as can assist in quickly charge-carrier extraction from the perovskite layer and decrease the recombination in PSCs.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Synthesis materials

One of the low-cost yet most useful and advanced techniques used to produce various materials including nano-scaled materials is combustion synthesis (CS) [61]. ZnO and Ni-doped and Ag-doped ZnO (NPs) were initially synthesized via the CS method.

5gr high-purity zinc nitrate and stoichiometric ratio of different fuels including glucose and glycine were added to 20cc deionized (DI) water with pH levels around 5.6 separately. For synthesizing Ni-doped ZnO5 (wt%) NPs and Ag-doped ZnO5 (wt%) NPs, 5 wt% of nickel nitrate and silver nitrate were added. Moreover, in order to create a green gel for Ni-doped ZnO and yellow gel for Ag-doped ZnO NPs, they were heated at 80 °C for one-half hour. To combustion of NPs, the formed gels were placed in 900 W microwave oven for 1 min and 1 h heating at 500 °C for removing the residual carbon.

The modified hummers method was used to prepare graphene oxide (GO), and rGO was subsequently extracted from GO through a thermal reduction process   $  [58]  $  .

# 2.2. Fabrication of the PSCs

In the present work, a Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) coated glass (of   $  1.4 \times 1.4 \, cm^{2}  $  ) was applied. Furthermore, the FTO-coated glass was etched using a combined solution made of 2 M hydrochloric acid and zinc metal powder for FTO electrode preparation. For rinse the substrate, which was then cleaned through an ultrasonic bath using deionized (DI) water and acetone for 10 min each, respectively. In the next step, followed by washing it using isopropyl alcohol and ethanol through an ultrasonic bath. Finally, the FTO was dried in an oven at the temperature of   $  90 \, ^{\circ}C  $   for 30 min.

# 2.3. Preparation of Ni-doped ZnO/rGO and Ag-doped ZnO/rGO bilayers ETLs

un-doped ZnO, Ni-doped ZnO 5 wt% and Ag-doped ZnO 5 wt% NPs were sonicated for 2 h, in DI water. Moreover, three dispersions were heated up to 50 °C via a hot-plate magnetic stirrer, and stirred for 24 h at 1500 rpm. The prepared dispersions were then filtered (by 0.22 μm, hydrophilic filter). Un-doped ZnO, Ni-doped ZnO 5 wt% and Ag-doped ZnO 5 wt% dispersions were deposited on substrates (FTO). The optimum results were achieved at the rate of 3000 rpm for 30 s. The obtained samples were annealed in an oven for 1 h at 90 °C.

In the following, 1 mg of rGO per 1 ml of solvent was dispersed in Dimethylformamide (DMF), which followed by 2 h sonication, and then, was positioned on a hot plate stirrer for 24 h with 1500 rpm at room temperature and 0.45   $  \mu  $  m PTFE filter was used to filtration, and the spin coating technique was then used to deposition on 3 films (un-doped ZnO, Ni-doped ZnO 5 wt% and Ag-doped ZnO 5 wt% in 25 s at 2500 rpm, which followed by annealing rGO at 150 °C for 1 h. 3.1.7. Two point probe electrical conductivity

Electrical conductivity (EC) analysis was also carried out on all three NPs (un-doped ZnO NPs, Ni-doped ZnO 5 wt% and Ag-doped ZnO 5 wt% and the highest EC was detected in Ag-doped ZnO NPs. The electrical conductivity of the solid materials is commonly measured through two methods, including 2- and 4-probe procedures. In the 2-probe method, the material is held between two copper electrodes and subjected to a DC electrical current. Then, the induced voltage between the mentioned electrodes will be measured by the same instrument, and considered as the electrical conductivity of the material. Such a method provides more accurate EC measurements, as eliminates the contact resistance between two electrodes [79]. In this study, the 2-probe method was applied to the synthesized powders. 0.3 gr of each powder was initially weighed and poured into a polycarbonate tube equipped with two Au-coated copper electrodes at both ends. NPs were externally pressed, and the electrical current was applied to it. The induced voltage was then measured using an electrometer (Solid state 610 C, Keiyhley, Instruments, USA). Two-point probe electrical conductivity of un-doped ZnO, Ni-doped ZnO 5 wt% and Ag-doped ZnO 5 wt% NPs were calculated to be 4.45E-10, 5.84E-10, and 6.16E-09 S/cm, respectively in Table3. We ascribe this decreasing electrical conductivity in Ni-doped ZnO NPs to the famous Burstein-Moss effect. According to the Burstein-Moss, effect, the lowest conduction band states are blocked by the enhanced Ni doping and significant deep traps are created in Ni-doped ZnO NPs at 5 wt% leading to reduced mobility of electron. According to Table3, free electron density increased by creating shallow traps, which led to an increase in the electrical conductivity of Ag-doped ZnO NPs.

# 4. Conclusions

In this present work, the effect of the rGO sheet was investigated in order to promote the performance of Ni-doped and Ag-doped ZnO ETLs in PSCs. First of all, extensive analyses of nano-micro structural and optical properties of Ni-doped ZnO and Ag-doped ZnO NPs were carried out. The bandgap of Ni-doped ZnO increased. According to this result, the wide bandgap of Ni-doped ZnO can be related to the Burstein-Moss effect. This implies that increased carrier concentration of Ni doping and blocking the lowest electronic states in the conduction band lead to the increased bandgap. According to the results, a reduction in bandgap happens for Ag-doped ZnO 5 wt%. The bandgap reduction resulting from appearing defect states (shallow traps) into the conduction band is interpreted. As the results of two-point probe electrical conductivity shows Ag-doped ZnO at 5 wt% NPs have the least resistivity. Ag-doped ZnO improved the charge carrier separation and, consequently, a reduction in the recombination rate, leading to the facilitation of photo-excited carriers. According to (PL), (J-V) characteristics and (EIS) results, with a comparison between Ni and Ag doping ETLs, we achieved that ZnO modification by Ag doping is more helpful in the passivation of defects in ZnO in the planar PSCs. Adding the rGO sheet to Ni-doped ZnO and Ag-doped ZnO at 5 wt% ETLs will suppress passivation, the electron recombination, and interfacial contact between the perovskite active layer and FTO will be reduced, resulting in an enhanced   $  V_{OC}  $  ,   $  J_{SC}  $  , and PCE in the planar PSCs. PCE and stability of Ag-ZnO/rGO bilayer-based planar PSC are indicating to greatly passivation and charge-carrier extraction at ZnO/perovskite interface, in so doing meaningfully improving the performance and stability of the ZnO-based planar PSC.
