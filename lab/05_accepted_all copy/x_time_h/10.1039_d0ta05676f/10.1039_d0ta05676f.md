# Long-term Stable and Highly Efficient Perovskite Solar Cells with Formamidinium Chloride (FACI) Additive

Shuai You,  $  ^{b,1}  $   Xiaoyan Xi,  $  ^{a,1}  $   Xin Zhang,  $  ^{a}  $   Hanying Wang,  $  ^{a}  $   Pingqiang Gao,  $  ^{a}  $   Xiangrong Ma,  $  ^{a}  $   Shiqing Bi,  $  ^{a,*}  $  , Jianqi Zhang  $  ^{c,*}  $  , Huiqiong Zhou  $  ^{c,*}  $   Zhixiang Wei  $  ^{c}  $

Despite the power conversion efficiency (PCE) in organometal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has achieved 25.2%, the control of the crystallization process and its impact on film quality is still one of the main challenges. Here, a new perovskite growth method using Formamidinium Chloride (FACI) as additive is introduced to enlarge crystal grain size and improve the quality of perovskite film. Strikingly, the champion cell conversion efficiency of   $  FA_{0.85}MA_{0.15}PbI_{x}Br_{3-x}  $  -based and   $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.85}MA_{0.10}Pb(I_{0.97}Br_{0.03})_{3}  $  -based PSC devices gets to 21.02% and 22.56%, respectively. The hysteresis index is found to decrease from 0.101 to 0.005, which is attributed to suppressed ion migration and surface charge trapping. Besides, the better film quality by FACI significantly increases the environmental stability of the perovskite films, yielding devices that maintain 90% of their initial efficiency after 1200 h in ambient air condition, whereas the pristine devices without FACI only keep 50% of their initial efficiency. Our solar cells processed with additives also receive a retardation on degradation with a considerably enhanced environmental stability. This work offers a promising route through crystallization management to achieve high performance and stable PSCs.

# Introduction

In the last couple of years, the breakthrough of 25.2% PCE in organometal halide perovskite solar cell has attracted significant attention  $  ^{[1-5]}  $  . This remarkable progress is due to its superior excellent light absorption (  $  \approx 10^{5}  $   cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ), low exciton binding energies (20-30 meV)  $  ^{[6,7]}  $  , high charge carrier mobility  $  ^{[8,9]}  $  , long carrier diffusion distance (>1   $  \mu  $  m), and non-excitonic charge generation with low recombination rates  $  ^{[10-12]}  $  . Besides, with the possibility of low-cost solution processing to reduce manufacturing cost, PSCs have already been viewed as a serious contender for the next-generation photovoltaic (PV) development. However, it is noted that solution-processed polycrystalline perovskite film suffer from poor surface coverage, defects at grain boundaries, instability of perovskite

film and reproducibility. These problems have been identified as a critical roadblock to the commercial viability of perovskite photovoltaic technology. Therefore, many efforts have been used to improve these issues, such as additives engineering, optimization of spin procedure, modifying deposition approaches, surface treatments, varying of the precursor concentration and using mixed solvents and so on, leading to advances in understanding the defect chemistry and crystallization for perovskites.

Among the above methods, the application of additives engineering has been proven with great versatility to modulate the perovskite film formation and resultant photovoltaic behaviors. Additives, such as organic polymers (PMMA)  $  ^{[13]}  $  , inorganic salts (KCl, NH  $  _{4}  $  Cl)  $  ^{[14,15]}  $  , organic small molecules (PCBM, DIO, Thiazole)  $  ^{[16-18]}  $  , inorganic acids (HI, HCl)  $  ^{[19-22]}  $  , ionic liquid  $  ^{[23]}  $  , water  $  ^{[24]}  $  , or organic halides (MACl, MAI)  $  ^{[25-27]}  $  , can affect perovskite film crystallization and film formation, defect passivation in the bulk and at the surface/interface, or interface tuning of structure and crystallization kinetics, enabling improvements in perovskite stability, optoelectronic properties, and photovoltaic performance. For example, by using a small organic molecule (PCBM), the constituted perovskite-PCBM solar cell represented highly dense light-absorbing layer, large grains and fewer grain boundaries. PbI  $  _{2}  $  (99.99%, TCI), PbBr(98%, TCI), HC(NH  $  _{2}  $  )  $  _{2}  $  I (purity scale: 99.8%, Dyesol) and CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  I (purity scale: 99.8%, Dyesol) were used as received. Spiro-OMeTAD (99.5%) was purchased from Xi'an polymer Light Technology Inc. Dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 98%) and chlorobenzene (99.8%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich.

# Device fabrication

To start, FTO patterned glass substrates were cleaned by soap-scrubbing and sonicated sequentially in ultra-pure water, acetone and isopropanol and finally dried on 80 °C for 20 min before use. TiO₂ compact layers was deposited on FTO conducting glass substrate via hydrolysis of TiCl₄ at 70 °C, forming the electron-selective contact. Spiro-OMeTAD solution was prepared by dissolving 72.3 mg of Spiro-OMeTAD in 1 mL of chlorobenzene, and then mixed with 28.8μl 4-tert-butylpyridine and 17.5μl of a stock Li⁺ solution (which contained 520 mg/mL bis-trifluoromethylsulfonylamine lithium salt in acetonitrile). For solar cells, The FAMA-based perovskite precursor was prepared by dissolving 1.4M mixture of metal lead salts which are composed of 0.85 PbI₂ and 0.15 PbBr₂, and 1.35M organic cation which are composed of 0.85 FAI and 0.15 MABr in a co-solvent of DMSO/DMF (4:1, by volume). FACI, FABr and FAI were added into the precursor solution with different mass concentrations. After heating at 60 °C for two hours with sufficient stirring, the perovskite precursor solutions were then spun-coat onto the FTO/TiO₂ substrates at 1000 rpm for 10 s and then at 5000 rpm for 30 s. During the last 25 s of the second spin-coating step 90 μL anti-solvents of ethyl acetate was dropped on the substrates . The films were then annealed at 100 °C for 45 min, and after cooling down, the hole extraction layer comprising of Spiro-OMeTAD was spun-coat atop the annealed perovskite films at 5000 rpm for 30 s. For Cs₀.₀₅FA₀.₈₅MA₀.₁₀Pb(I₀.₉₇Br₀.₀₃)₃-based perovskite, Perovskite precursor solution was prepared by dissolving a mixture of PbI₂ (1.61mmol, 742.0 mg), FAI (1.31 mmol, 224.4 mg), MABr (0.15 mmol, 16.2 mg), and CsI(0.08 mmol, 19.8 mg) Cs₀.₀₅FA₀.₈₅MA₀.₁₀Pb(I₀.₉₇Br₀.₀₃)₃ with 5% excess of PbI₂ in 1mL of mixed solution of DMF and DMSO [DMF (v):DMSO (v) = 4:1] under mild heating condition at ~70 °C to assist dissolving. The perovskite precursor solution was deposited on the freshly prepared FTO/TiO₂ substrate, and a two-step spin-coating method was applied. The first step was carried out at 2000 rpm with an acceleration rate of 200 rpm/s for 10 s. The second step followed at 6000 rpm with an acceleration rate of 2000 rpm/s for 30 s. CB (100 μL) was slowly dripped at the 15 s before the second step end. After this, the substrate was annealed at 120

°C for 20 min. Finally, 80 nm of Au top contact was thermally evaporated through shadow masks under high vacuum (<1×10 $  ^{-5}  $  Pa). The area of the solar cells is 0.04 cm $  ^{2}  $ .

# Characterization

Current-density versus voltage (J-V) characteristics of FAMA-based PSCs were recorded by a Keithley 2400 Sourcemeter under nitrogen environment and the scan rate is  $ 0.1\mathrm{V / s} $ .  $ \mathrm{FA_{0.85}MA_{0.15}PbI_xBr_{3 - x}} $  solar cells were tested under irradiation by using a solar simulator calibrated by a certified reference Si cell. Single carrier devices were tested in dark in the charge injection regime. Incident photon to current efficiency (EQE) of  $ \mathrm{FA_{0.85}MA_{0.15}PbI_xBr_{3 - x}} $  solar cells was measured by a Newport external quantum measurement system (Model 66920).

The morphology of as-prepared TiO2 and perovskite films were characterized by a field emission scanning electron microscope (Hitachi-SU8220). Transmittance and absorbance spectra were obtained by utilizing a UV-vis spectrometer (PE Lambda 650/850/950 UV-vis spectrophotometer). Crystallinity and phase identification of the samples were performed using an X-ray diffractometer (Philips X'Pert) equipped with Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda = 1.5419 \AA  $  ). Steady-state PL spectra were measured by a HORIBA Fluorolog-III spectrofluoroemeter. PL lifetime was determined by a customized spectrometer system (Fluorolog-III, HORIBA Jobin-Yvon Inc) mounted with a diode laser (  $  \lambda = 532  $  nm) as the excitation source with the detector (model R-928) operated at a voltage of 950 V and with peak preset = 3000 counts. The decay dynamics were analyzed by Data Analysis Software. GIWAXS measurements were obtained on a Xenocs Xeuss SAXS/WAXS beamline system based on an X-ray wavelength of 0.6887 Å.

# Conclusions

To summarize, we demonstrate a facile and highly efficient process strategy using FACI additives to regulate crystal growth orientation result in high quality perovskite films with enlarge crystal grain sizes. In the best condition, FAMA-based PSCs processed with  $ 3\mathrm{mg / mL} $  FACI produce an enhanced PCE of  $ 22.56\% $  with  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  based planar architecture. Based on a variety of experimental analyses, we identify the interactions of additives with the iodine anions in perovskite precursor solutions via the halogen bonding which can lead to a modified crystallization in FAMA-based perovskite films with improved film morphology. We found that the carrier transport becomes enhanced in the additive-processed perovskite films, with which charge extraction becomes expedited with reduced recombination losses. In consequence, the device efficiency receive a considerable boost showing inhibited hysteresis. With the demonstrated additive strategy, we simultaneously achieve a favorable retardation on the degradation of PCE in our solar cells. The gain of environmental stability is primarily related to the morphological mediation of perovskite films with pinhole-free and compact features.
