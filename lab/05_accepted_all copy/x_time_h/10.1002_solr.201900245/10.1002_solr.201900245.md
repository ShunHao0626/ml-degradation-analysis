Sn-based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are promising alternatives to replacing toxic Pb-based PSCs, which have shown a rapid rise in photovoltaic applications in the past 1 year. However, the reported Sn-based PSCs are often fabricated with a small aperture area (typically   $  0.02–0.1 \, cm^{2}  $  ) because forming homogeneous pinhole-free continuous films over a large surface area is still challenging. Herein, a post-deposition vapor annealing (PDVA) process assisted by methylammonium chloride vapor is presented that enables the fabrication of stable, homogeneous pinhole-free   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite absorber films with low crystal defects and low surface recombination over a relatively large area up to   $  1.02 \, cm^{2}  $  . Inverted planar solar cells fabricated with a   $  1.02 \, cm^{2}  $   aperture area show a maximum power conversion efficiency of 6.33% with high reproducibility and stability. The shelf-lifetime stability test shows that the PSCs retain 90% of their performance for more than 1000 h when stored in a   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glove box and under dark conditions. The preliminary light-soaking stability tests under continuous illumination and maximum power-tracking conditions are relatively promising. This study marks an important step toward the up scaling of Sn-based PSCs.

# 2. Results and Discussion

We have prepared FASnI $ _3 $  films from a precursor solution containing 1.0 M SnI $ _2 $  and formamidinium iodide (FAI) with 10 mol% SnF $ _2 $  and 3 mol% EDAI $ _2 $  as the additives by a one-step antisolvent method and annealed at 100 °C for 10 min (in a N $ _2 $  glove box). For simplicity, hereafter, we refer to the perovskite film prepared by this method as “normal annealing (NA).” Note that our FASnI $ _3 $  perovskite films were prepared from a precursor solution containing 3 mol% EDAI $ _2 $  in contrast to the reported work of Diau et al. $ ^{[19]} $  First, the PSCs were fabricated with an inverted planar structure and a small aperture area of 0.09 cm $ ^2 $  for each unit cell (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The detailed fabrication methods of the full PSCs are shown in Figure 1a and described in the Experimental Section. The best-performing PSC showed the highest PCE of 8.35% after 120 h of storage in a N $ _2 $  glove box (Table ST1, Supporting Information, shows the photovoltaic parameters). However, we have observed that the photovoltaic performances of PSCs fabricated within the same substrate largely vary (Table ST1, Supporting Information). For instance, on the same substrate, only one unit cell shows the highest PCE, whereas the other unit cells show a much lower PCE. Unit cells numbered 1, 2, and 4 showed PCEs of 4.54%, 7.3%, and 6.79%, respectively, with very different figures of merits, such as open-circuit voltage (V $ _{OC} $ ) and fill factor (FF) (Table ST1, Supporting Information). Interestingly, the corresponding film showed nonuniform and nonhomogenous coverage with numerous pinholes over the large surface area, which are the sources of scattering of the photovoltaic parameters of the resultant PSCs (unit cells) within the same substrate. To overcome this problem, we have introduced the PDVA process assisted by MACl vapor. For the fabrication of FASnI $ _3 $  films by the PDVA process, after spin coating the FASnI $ _3 $  (from the same precursor as stated earlier) on the poly (3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)-poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT: PSS)-coated substrate, the sample was transferred to a closed vessel and set face up on top of a ring where a small amount of MACl powder was placed and heated at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $  (see Figure 1a). We have observed the film's surface morphology, which showed a pinhole-free, homogeneous smooth surface of FASnI $ _3 $  films with large uniform grains over the whole surface area (Figure 1c) with an average grain size of  $ 679\mathrm{nm} $ , which was higher than the NA-processed films ( $ 127\mathrm{nm} $ ) (Figure S2, Supporting Information). Fewer pinholes and larger grains in the FASnI $ _3 $  film (Figure 1c) result in reduced grain boundary defects with wire-shaped structures along the grain boundary (probably excess of MACl along the grain boundary and on the surface of the grain).[26] Additionally, the PDVA-processed films showed a low root mean square (r.m.s) surface roughness of  $ 17\mathrm{nm} $ , whereas, for the NA-processed FASnI $ _3 $  films, the surface r.m.s. roughness of  $ 42\mathrm{nm} $  was observed (Figure S3, Supporting Information). The reduced surface roughness provided by the PDVA process indicates that dense and smooth films can be formed, which can suppress the charge recombination and allow us to fabricate large-area and high-performance PSCs.

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurement is performed to evaluate the effect of NA and PDVA processes on FASnI $ _3 $  films and shown in Figure 2. The atomic ratio of Sn:I was observed at 1:2.6 (Table ST2, Supporting Information) for PDVA-processed films, which is close to the expected ratio of 1:3 for FASnI $ _3 $ . We compared the XPS peaks of C 1s, N 1s, O 1s, and Cl 2p for NA- and PDVA-processed FASnI $ _3 $  films. From elemental analysis, we observed a relatively sizeable peak assigned to Cl 2p at 198 eV for the PDVA-processed film, which can be attributed to Cl induced from the MACl vapor with 1.3 at% (Table ST3, Supporting Information). A 5.4 at% of O was observed for the PDVA-processed film, which is half (10.8 at%) of the film fabricated by the NA process. The relatively lower amount of O on the surface might reduce the formation of superoxide species from oxygen and might contribute to enhanced carrier lifetime. $ ^{[27]} $  However, to confirm this in FASnI $ _3 $  films, further studies must be conducted. Moreover, minutely reduced atomic percentage of Sn $ ^{4+} $  for the FASnI $ _3 $  film prepared by the PDVA process as compared with the NA-processed FASnI $ _3 $  film was observed (Figure S4, Supporting Information). To further understand the influence of the PDVA process on the optoelectronic properties and charge dynamics of the FASnI $ _3 $  film, we have performed photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL), as shown in Figure 3a,b. FASnI $ _3 $  films fabricated by both NA and PDVA processes showed PL peaks at 850 nm. However, the PL intensity of the FASnI $ _3 $  films

fabricated by the PDVA process was significantly enhanced (in average eight times) compared with the films prepared by the NA process. In addition, the PL decay curves, fitted with a single exponential function, revealed that the decay time (and therefore, the carrier lifetime) is, in average, 57% longer for the PDVA-processed FASnI  $  _{3}  $  films as compared with the NA-processed film (2.7 ns). This increased carrier lifetime of the FASnI  $  _{3}  $  films is attributed to the improved crystallinity and grain sizes, which is in accordance with the scanning electron microscope (SEM) observations (Figure 1c). The improved crystallinity provided by the PDVA process contributes to reducing the nonradiative recombination centers in FASnI  $  _{3}  $  films.

For better understanding the structural properties, we have performed an X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis of the   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films prepared by both processes, as shown in Figure 4a. The   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films prepared by both fabrication processes show the characteristic peaks (100), (111), (200), (201), and (220) of an orthorhombic lattice structure. Interestingly,   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films prepared by the PDVA process show that the (100) and (200) peaks have, in average,   $  \approx5  $   times higher intensity, which confirms the improvement in crystal quality. Additionally, the peak at 14.1° shows a narrower full width at half maxima (0.071) for the PDVA process as compared with NA-processed   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films (0.088) (Table ST4, Supporting Information). To quantitatively evaluate crystal quality, we have also calculated the crystallite size by Scherrer formula.  $  ^{[28]}  $   The average crystallite sizes of NA- and PDVA-processed   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films were 95.0 and 117.7 nm, respectively, which also support the improvement of film quality by the PDVA process. Figure 4b represents the absorption spectra of the   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films prepared by both processes. For few instances, it has been reported that the absorption band below the optical bandgap originates from the defect center.  $  ^{[4,29]}  $   Interestingly,   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films prepared by the NA process indeed showed absorption in the 850–900 nm region, whereas, for the PDVA-processed   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films, the latter is almost absent. From this result, we can conclude that the PDVA-processed   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films contain low density of defects compared with the NA-processed one. This result is consistent with the PL and TRPL measurements, as shown in Figure 3a,b. The reduction of defects owing to the PDVA process can effectively reduce the density of recombination centers and might result in the higher photovoltaic performances PSCs.

With these favorable optoelectronic properties provided by the PDVA process, we have fabricated the inverted planar PSCs (see structure in Figure S1a, Supporting Information) with an aperture area of   $  0.09 \, cm^2  $  . The current density–voltage   $  (J-V)  $   curves of the best performance   $  FASnI_3  $   solar cells fabricated by the PDVA process and measured under 1 sun illumination in reverse bias scan (from 0.6 to -0.1 V) and the corresponding photovoltaic performance parameters are summarized in Table ST5, Supporting Information. Fabricated within the same substrate, an even distribution of photovoltaic performance was observed for each unit cell with the best PCE of 7.31%. All the photovoltaic parameters of each unit cell, such as short-circuit current   $  (J_{\mathrm{sc}})  $  , open-circuit voltage   $  (V_{\mathrm{oc}})  $  , and FF, were in line with the best-performing PSC. # 3. Conclusion

In this work, we have shown that, by introducing the PDVA process, highly crystalline, pinhole-free, homogeneous smooth-surface   $  FASnI_{3}  $   films can be fabricated over a large surface area. The PDVA process results in the Sn-based perovskite absorber film with high crystallinity, low crystal defects, and low surface recombination, which eventually enables us to fabricate PSCs with an aperture area of   $  1.02\ cm^{2}  $   and PCE of 6.33%. Moreover, due to high-quality film morphology and improved crystallinity, the best PSC showed a relatively stable shelf lifetime up to 1000 h with minor degradation. Preliminary, light-soaking stability tests under continuous illumination at MPPT conditions for the champion PSC look promising as well. This work highlights the potential of Sn-based PSCs for further up scaling and might bring nontoxic PSCs one step closer to applications.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: We have used all the chemicals as received without any further purification, including   $  SnI_{2}  $   (99.99%, Sigma-Aldrich), FAI (>98%, Tokyo Chemical Industry Co., Japan),   $  SnF_{2}  $   (>99%, Sigma-Aldrich),   $  EDAI_{2}  $   (Merck), PEDOT:PSS (Clevious P VP Al 4083), and C60 (99.5%, Lumtec Co., Taiwan). Bathocuproine (BCP) and super-dehydrated solvents of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), chlorobenzene, methanol, and ethanol were purchased from Wako Co., Japan.

Perovskite Film Fabrication: The precursor solution for the FASnI $ _3 $  perovskite film was prepared by mixing an equimolar ratio of (1 mmol) SnI $ _2 $  and FAI with 10 mol% SnF $ _2 $  and 3 mol% EDAI $ _2 $  in the DMSO solvent. The perovskite film was deposited by spin coating the precursor solution at 5000 rpm for 75 s. During spin coating, chlorobenzene was dripped on the substrate after 30 s.

Solar Cell Fabrication: PSCs were fabricated on the patterned indium tin oxide (ITO)-coated glass substrates. The substrates were ultrasonically cleaned with detergent, deionized water, acetone, and ethanol, respectively, for 15 min each. The cleaned substrates were treated with UV/ozone radiation for 30 min. The PEDOT:PSS layer was deposited on top of the ITO substrate by spin coating at 4000 rpm for 30 s. The PEDOT:PSS films were then annealed at 150 °C for 30 min and cooled down to room temperature. After cooling at room temperature, the substrates were transferred to a N₂ glove box, and the FASnI₃ films were deposited. Subsequently, C₆₀ (50 nm) and BCP (7 nm) were deposited by thermal evaporation. Finally, a 60 nm-thick Ag layer was vacuum deposited through a shadow mask with an active area of either 0.09 or 1.02 cm². The PSCs were sealed, by a cavity glass, using UV curable glue on top of the front active area of ITO.

Characterization: The optical absorption spectra were measured by a Shimadzu UV/Vis 3600 spectrophotometer. The morphology of the films and structure of devices were observed by SEM (JSM-6500F field emission under an acceleration voltage of 5 kV). XRD was measured by a Rigaku RINT-2500 powder X-ray diffractometer using Cu Kα radiation. XPS was performed on the film deposited on the glass substrate using PHI Quantera SXM (ULVAC-PHI). XPS measurement was carried out at ultrahigh vacuum as high as   $  5 \times 10^{-11}  $   mbar. X-ray from the monochromatic Al Kα source (hν = 1486.7 eV) was used to excite the samples, and electrons were detected at   $  45^{\circ}  $   take-off angle. Multipack software was used for curve fitting. Steady-state PL and TRPL were measured by a Hamarnatsu C12132 fluorescence lifetime spectrometer using a 1.5 ns pulsed laser (a frequency of 15 kHz), at an excitation wavelength of λ = 532 nm, and an excitation power of 0.1 mW. For PL and TRPL measurements, the relevant films were deposited on glass substrates and encapsulated in a glove box but were measured in ambient conditions. The J-V curves were measured using a solar simulator with standard air mass 1.5 sunlight (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , WXS-155S-10: Wacom Denso Co., Japan) under ambient conditions. The
