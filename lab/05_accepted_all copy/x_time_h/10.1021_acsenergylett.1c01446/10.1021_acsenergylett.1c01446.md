# Amorphous TiO  $  _{2}  $  Coatings Stabilize Perovskite Solar Cells

Seongrok Seo, Sooeun Shin, Eunsoo Kim, Seonghwa Jeong, Nam-Gyu Park,  $  ^{*}  $   and Hyunjung Shin  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Significant progress has been achieved in improving the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) for a decade, but the long-term stability is still underdeveloped. In a regular PSC structure, 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) with Li additives and metal electrodes are widely adopted, which leads to facile degradation under device operating conditions because of ion migration. Herein, we show an interface-engineered stabilization approach to prevent ion migration in PSCs enabled by amorphous  $ (a) $ - $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  capable of hole transfer grown by atomic layer deposition (ALD). This layer prevents ion migration of Li additives with consequent aggregation as well as metal electrode diffusion into the perovskite layer. Furthermore, the combined layers of Spiro-OMeTAD/a- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  unprecedentedly promote device efficiency, which is further verified with other organic hole transport layers. Finally, the operational stability of the  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $ -PSC is substantially improved in comparison to that of the control PSC.

While a new generation of halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has exhibited great improvement in the power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to 25.5% since their first demonstration a decade,  $  ^{1-6}  $  the long-term stability is still underdeveloped and remains a challenging obstacle for successful commercialization. A prototypical halide perovskite for a photovoltaic device is MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  (MA = methylammonium, CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $  ). Later, compositionally engineered FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  (FA = formamidinium, CH(NH  $  _{2}  $  )  $  _{2}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $  ) realized by substituting cations for MA and Cs or anions for Br was applied to enhance both the device performance and stability. This enhancement is mainly attributed to the excellent optoelectronic properties of FA-based perovskites and better structural tolerance to phase transition.  $  ^{7-9}  $  However, this composition still has multiple stability problems stemming from the volatility of MA molecules under high temperature and phase segregation under light or electrical biases.  $  ^{10-12}  $  FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  has recently drawn massive attention because of its more desirable band gap (1.45–1.51 eV) and ability to produce the best-performing devices,  $  ^{13-15}  $  which demands further the stability engineering of PSCs based on FAPbI  $  _{3}  $ .

Device constituent layers, such as charge transport layers (CTLs), metal electrodes, and their interfaces, are also identified as important origins of degradation, especially under operating conditions. More stable HTL alternatives such as poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA) $ ^{21,22} $  and inorganic CuSCN $ ^{23} $  have definitely enabled better stability PSCs under high-temperature (60–85  $ ^{\circ} $ C) operating conditions because of their higher thermal stability and absence of Li salts. They are still not utilized for fabricating the highest performing devices. The alternative HTLs of fluorine-terminated Spiro-OMeTAD have recently been reported to considerably improve the stability of highly efficient PSCs under high temperature (60  $ ^{\circ} $ C) $ ^{24} $  or humidity $ ^{25} $  conditions because of the high glass transition temperature (>100  $ ^{\circ} $ C). However, these HTLs are still doped with Li salt and should be further examined under more severe conditions and endowed with longer-term stability. Kerner et al. proposed electrochemically driven reaction of Au occurs in the presence of released iodide from the perovskite layer. $ ^{26} $  The spacer, Spiro-OMeTAD, cannot prevent this reaction completely with just increased threshold of the reaction. Domanski et al. developed the approach of inserting a Cr layer between the HTL and Au electrode. $ ^{19} $  They demonstrated that PSCs can be stabilized under operating conditions at a high temperature of 70  $ ^{\circ} $ C by inhibiting the migration of Au, at the expense of the initial performance.

The state-of-the-art stability outcome in FA-based PSCs utilizing Spiro-OMeTAD is retaining 90% of the initial efficiency (24%) for 400 h under operating conditions, which still requires further stability improvement.  $  ^{27}  $   As a result, we believe that there is further room to increase the stability of highly efficient PSCs based on Spiro-OMeTAD by suppressing degradation processes, for example, Au migration and pinhole formation under stress.

Herein, we propose stabilization of highly efficient regular structured  $ n $ - $ i $ - $ p $  PSCs using amorphous  $ (a) $ -TiO $ _2 $  grown by atomic layer deposition (ALD). The ALD layer is placed between Spiro-OMeTAD and Au electrodes on top of the perovskite. We found that  $ a $ -TiO $ _2 $  can be used for hole transfer when combined with organic HTLs because of the presence of hole traps in the gap. We observed that the photovoltaic performance of PSCs is slightly improved by the addition of TiO $ _2 $  coatings. Moreover, the environmental stability of perovskite films and devices is significantly improved by preventing Li and Au migration. Finally, the operational stability of the TiO $ _2 $ -PSCs is almost 3 times better than that of the control PSCs.

ALD is a powerful method to prepare various metal oxides with uniform, pinhole-free, and dense film quality because of the sequential self-limiting chemical reactions at relatively low temperature (RT to 200 °C), which is hard to achieve with other thin-film growth methods.  $  ^{28-31}  $   ALD-grown thin films have already been demonstrated to be suitable for the fabrication of efficient and stable PSCs as CTLs and passivation layers.  $  ^{5,32-34}  $   Recently, several research groups including ours have attempted to protect the perovskite layer by overlying ALD-grown ETLs on the perovskite/PCBM heterojunction, such as   $  TiO_{2}  $  ,  $  ^{35}  $     $  SnO_{2}  $  ,  $  ^{36}  $   and ZnO.  $  ^{37}  $   The stability of inverted-type p-i-n PSCs was dramatically improved by these layers, as they provide an excellent barrier against ion migration and atmospheric moisture as well as oxygen ingress. However, stability engineering based on ALD-grown HTLs for n-i-p PSCs is rarely approached because of the degradation of Spiro-OMeTAD during the ALD process, even at temperatures as low as approximately 60 °C, as well as the lack of HTL ALD

synthesis chemistry.  $  ^{38}  $   HTL candidates such as NiO,   $  CuO_{x}  $  ,   $  MoO_{x}  $  , and   $  VO_{x}  $   often require a strong oxidizer, i.e., ozone, as a precursor and/or a high process temperature.  $  ^{39-41}  $   We expect that passivation of the HTL would be effective in improving the stability of n-i-p PSCs when the overlying ALD-grown films on perovskites are deposited without any damage. In this work, we used low-temperature (  $  \sim60\ ^{\circ}C  $  )-deposited a-  $  TiO_{2}  $   as a leaky dielectric layer grown on Spiro-OMeTAD layer by ALD (Figure 1a), which can transfer holes effectively within a certain thickness, different from crystalline   $  TiO_{2}  $   with a strong hole-blocking property.

Leaky dielectric   $  a-TiO_{2}  $   has already been investigated as a photoanode to stabilize Si photoelectrochemical cells.  $  ^{42,43}  $   The authors reported the hole-transfer channel of ALD-grown   $  a-TiO_{2}  $   on Si for the oxygen evolution reaction. Unlike electron transport via the conduction band of   $  TiO_{2}  $ , the transfer of holes through the defect states, alternative to the valence band, has been suggested.   $  a-TiO_{2}  $   has been demonstrated to have the actual position of defects at 0.94 eV below from the Fermi level by UPS/XPS measurement.  $  ^{44}  $   The peak has   $  \sim0.88  $   eV in full width at half-maximum (FWHM). The resultant defect bands are positioned from 5.20 to 6.04 eV and centered at 5.64 eV (vs   $  E_{vac}  $  ). It is enough for holes to be transferred from adjacent layers of Si (VB: 5.2 eV) and/or Spiro-OMeTAD (VB: 5.2 eV). In addition,   $  TiO_{2}  $   deposited by ALD with various deposition variables has produced similar defect bands.  $  ^{45}  $   It is further verified that the more   $  Ti^{3+}  $   chemical states in the films, the greater the area of the defect band produced, which means the defect bands primarily originate from   $  Ti^{3+}  $   defects.  $  ^{46}  $   Likewise, our ALD-  $  TiO_{2}  $   films display considerable   $  Ti^{3+}  $   states when as-prepared, and they decreased after annealing at 400 °C in ambient as evidenced by XPS Ti   $  2p_{3/2}  $   spectra (Figure S1).

We speculated that the hole-transfer mechanism of  $ a $ -TiO $ _2 $  can be applied to PSCs. The environmental stability of perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD layers against moisture, light illumination, and heat depending on the   $  a-TiO_{2}  $   ALD was investigated (Figures S10–S12). The samples were stored in a humidity-controlled box with a relative humidity of   $  85 \pm 5\%  $   for 100 h (Figure S10). Bare perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD films exhibit a severe phase transition to the   $  \delta  $  -phase of FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  , evidenced by the appearance of an XRD peak at approximately   $  11.7^{\circ}  $   and by the complete color change from black to transparent. In contrast, the   $  a-TiO_{2}  $  -deposited films show higher resistance. The perovskite films were also tested against AM 1.5G light illumination for 200 h (Figure S11). No noticeable difference in XRD patterns is observed for both samples. However, the absorption capability of the   $  a-TiO_{2}  $  -deposited films is retained slightly better than that of the control. Such a slight degradation originates from the use of charge extraction layers such as FTO and Spiro-OMeTAD, as degradation will be severe under light illumination when one kind of charges of electrons or holes is accumulated, which accelerates ion migrations.  $  ^{55}  $

The thermal stability of Spiro-OMeTAD with Li additives leads to degradation of PSCs because of Li ion migration, while   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   is known to have high thermal stability. SEM, rather than XRD, was used to detect the morphological changes of Spiro-OMeTAD-coated perovskite films (Figure S12). As a reference, the film stored at room temperature shows typical grains of the underlying perovskite surface with tiny pinholes in the Spiro-OMeTAD film. The presence of small pinholes with a size of less than approximately 10 nm has also been reported to be observed in conventionally coated Spiro-OMeTAD by electron microscopy.  $  ^{56}  $   The pinholes are considered a driver

facilitating the diffusion of Li ions, eventually degrading PSCs. The films stored at 85 °C for 24 h show noticeable changes in the morphology, appearing as brighter contrast small dots. The dots are the resultant substance of Li oxidation under high temperature, moisture, and oxygen. It is further confirmed that the spots are highly originated from Li-related components. The Spiro-OMeTAD thin film did not produce these spots after 24 h of aging at 85 °C if they are prepared without containing any Li-TFSI as additives (Figure S12d). In contrast, substantial suppression of bright dots is found in ALD-grown TiO  $  _{2}  $  -coated films stored at the same high temperature because of the retardation of ion migration via pore filling and the presence of an additional barrier on the surface. With a-TiO  $  _{2}  $   ALD on the film, the degradation of the perovskite and Spiro-OMeTAD layers is significantly retarded. The improvement in the environmental stability originates from the barrier property of ALD-grown a-TiO  $  _{2}  $   preventing the diffusion of ions and moisture molecules.

Stability of the PSCs under a high temperature of 70 °C for 80 h was achieved. As shown in Figure 4a,b, the control PSCs display much lower thermal stability than the a-TiO  $  _{2}  $  device. We chose the stability test at 70 °C because the temperature is usually reached on a hot, sunny day in realistic operating conditions.  $  ^{57}  $  An improved thermal tolerance is observed in the a-TiO  $  _{2}  $ -based PSCs. Regardless of TiO  $  _{2}  $  coating, both devices suffer from thermal degradation. Even though  $  V_{oc}  $  barely changes in the control, FF and  $  J_{sc}  $  can be observed to visibly decrease. The a-TiO  $  _{2}  $ -PSC displays less reduction in both  $  J_{sc}  $  and FF. This can be understood as the a-TiO  $  _{2}  $ -PSC series resistance being maintained, whereas the control device shunt resistance is somewhat more preserved. Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (TOF-SIMS) results of both PSCs aged at 70 °C for 80 h are shown in Figure 4c. Au is distinctly diffused from the control PSC into the perovskite layer, especially at the perovskite/SnO  $  _{2}  $  interface, while the TiO  $  _{2}  $  device suppressed it. In the perovskite layer, the diffused Au is considered a charge recombination center, leading to the reduction in  $  J_{sc}  $  of the control device. The operational stability of the control and   $  a-TiO_{2}-PSCs  $   was tested under continuous AM 1.5G light illumination and a constant bias of 0.85 V without a UV cutoff filter (Figure 5). The   $  a-TiO_{2}-PSCs  $   retained 96.5% of their initial PCE for 120 h, while the control PSCs lost 20% of their efficiency within 120 h. The improvement in the stability primarily comes from the ALD-grown   $  a-TiO_{2}  $   layer retarding the diffusion of ions such as Li and Au. CHMAI surface treatment was not applied in this test. PSCs without surface treatment provide approximately 18–19% of PCE. The detailed evolution of the J-V curve during the aging period is shown in Figure S13. The role of CHMAI treatment in the stabilization of PSCs is beyond the scope of this work. Even though the conventionally prepared Spiro-OMeTAD has too poor stability to be adopted in a stable PSC architecture, the ion diffusion blocking has been demonstrated to be capable of improving the stability of PSCs without sacrificing the performance.

Amorphous   $  TiO_{2}  $   films grown by ALD have been successfully prepared for hole charge transfer when combined with organic HTLs. We found that   $  a-TiO_{2}  $   coatings on organic HTLs improved the performance of PSCs. More importantly, ALD-grown   $  a-TiO_{2}  $   films on perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD layers considerably retarded the degradation of the film under various environmental stresses, such as heat, moisture, and illumination. The ALD-grown   $  a-TiO_{2}  $   coating was also revealed to be capable of preventing the diffusion of materials such as Li ions and Au, which led to improved device stability at a high temperature of 70 °C. Furthermore, the   $  a-TiO_{2}-PSC  $   is almost 3 times more stable than the control PSC under operating conditions. This method offers a novel approach of coating of a passivation layer on polymeric HTLs to improve the stability and performance of n-i-p PSCs.
