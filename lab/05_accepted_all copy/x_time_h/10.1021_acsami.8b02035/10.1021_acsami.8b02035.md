# Abstract:

Significant efforts have been devoted to enhancing both the performance and long-term stability of lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) to promote its practical application. In this context, a self-assembled monolayer composed of dye molecule is demonstrated for the first time to be efficient in passivating the surface of hole transport layer (HTL),   $  NiO_{x}  $  , in the p-i-n PSCs through multiple functions, including the minimization of energy level offset, reducing surface trap states, and enhancing wetting between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and perovskite layers coupled with increasing perovskite crystallinity. Consequently, the dye monolayer has sufficiently improved the hole extraction efficiency and suppressed the charge recombination, validated by steady and transient photoluminescence (PL) measurements and the electrochemical impedance analysis. Concurrently, a mixed layer of   $  BaSnO_{3}  $   nanoparticles and PCBM (BSO/PCBM) was exploited as an efficient electron transport layer (ETL), resulting in superior electron transport properties and correspondingly excellent device stability. By incorporating these bifacial modifications, the device performance of the inverted PSC was propelled to 16.2%, compared with 14.0% for that without any interfacial and compositional engineering. Benefiting from the excellent crystallinity of the perovskite through dye passivation and the blocking of moisture, oxygen and ion migration by using the hybrid BSO/PCBM layer, over 90% of the initial PCE has been preserved for the device after exposure to ambient air for 650 hours.

Key words: dye molecule; Barium stannate; surface passivation; long-term stability; charge recombination.

# Introduction:

Organometal–halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs), with power conversion efficiency (PCE) remarkably rocketing from 3.8% to 22.7%,  $  ^{1-3}  $  have gained worldwide recognition as one of the most promising candidates in the photovoltaic field. Generally, PSCs structures are divided into two categories, including a conventional n-i-p configuration  $  ^{4-5}  $  and an inverted p-i-n one.  $  ^{6-7}  $  The latter resulting in PCE exceeding 20%  $  ^{8}  $  has been attracting increasing attention due to the compatibility of low-temperature processing and negligible hysteresis behavior.  $  ^{9-10}  $  Charge transport materials (CTM) and their interfaces with the perovskite film play significant role in determining the photovoltaic performance and stability of the as-prepared device. Though a few organic semiconductors have been successfully and widely used as contact materials in p-i-n PSCs, such as poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) poly(styrenesulphonate) (PEDOT:PSS) as hole transport material (HTM) and [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl (PCBM) as electron transport material (ETM), the device performance is still inferior compared to their counterpart in n-i-p configuration.  $  ^{8,11}  $  PEDOT:PSS has been reported to suffer from serious long-term stability issues due to its high acidity and hygroscopicity.  $  ^{12}  $  Great efforts have been devoted to exploring more chemically stable and cost-effective contact material substitutes. Among all, nickel oxide (NiO  $  _{x}  $ ) is the most investigated HTM candidate, standing out owing to its high mobility and superior stability.  $  ^{13-14}  $  However, it has been reported that the defects usually exist on the NiO  $  _{x}  $  surface leading to poor interface properties between NiO  $  _{x}  $  and perovskite layer, thus not only deteriorating the crystallinity of perovskite layer but also hindering the carrier transfer at the interface to a large extent.  $  ^{15-17}  $  Interfacial engineering by passivating with a self-assembled monolayer (SAM) has been developed as an effective and simple solution to circumvent these problems.  $  ^{18-19}  $  Especially, the application of SAM on ETM has been extensively applied for ETM,  $  ^{20-22}  $  while it is still rare for HTM. Qin Wang and colleagues explored a range of organic molecules to modify the NiO  $  _{x}  $  surface in an inverted PSC, and found that para-substituted benzoic (R-BA) acid is beneficial for the perovskite crystallinity through chemical interaction and further facilitates the interface charge transfer by improving the adhesion

Apart from these, it is reported that the solubility and viscosity of PCBM solution are very limited, resulting in defective film morphology and unsatisfactory film coverage on perovskite surface.  $  ^{26}  $  It is also worth mentioning that PCBM is non-ideal for blocking the oxygen and moisture into the perovskite layer  $  ^{27}  $  or hindering the ion diffusion from perovskite film to react with the silver electrode  $  ^{28}  $ . With two carboxylate radicals (-COO-) and two carboxyl groups (-COOH) in one molecule, the N719 dye molecule has the ability to strongly attach to the solution-processed  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  by reacting with the hydroxyl groups on the surface. Through this, N719 dye molecule is expected to effectively passivate the defects on the surface of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ . # Experimental:

# 2.1 Materials

2,2',7,7'-Tetrakis-(N,N-di-4-methoxyphenylamino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) was purchased from Borun Chemicals Co., Ltd. (Ningbo, China). N719 dye and methylammonium iodide  $ \mathrm{(CH_3NH_3I} $ , MAI) were purchased from Dyesol Ltd (Australia). All the other materials, unless specified, were purchased from either Alfa Aesar or Sigma-Aldrich, and used as received without further purification.

# 2.2 Fabrication of Solar cells

Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO, sheet resistance  $ \sim 10\Omega /\square $  ) substrates were ultrasonically cleaned with detergent, deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol for  $ 15\mathrm{min} $  each in sequence. All substrates were further treated with oxygen plasma for  $ 5\mathrm{min} $  before device fabrication.

Next,   $  NiO_{x}  $   HTL was fabricated by spin-coating a solution of nickel(II) nitrate hexahydrate (  $  Ni(NO_{3})_{2}\cdot6H_{2}O  $  ) in anhydrous ethanol (0.1 M) at 4000 rpm for 30 s. This deposition was repeated for twice. Before the second spin-coating, the film was dried at 80 °C for 15 min. Afterwards, the film was sintered at 350 °C for 30 min. For the interfacial passivation by dye molecule, a solution of N719 dye in anhydrous ethanol (0.5 mM) was dropped onto the   $  NiO_{x}  $   substrates and spin-coating at 2000 rpm for 30 s after waiting for 5 min.

Then, the substrates were annealed on a hotplate at   $  80\ °C  $   for 5 min to remove the solvent and enable the chemical coupling between N719 dye and   $  NiO_{x}  $  . To wash away the loosely adsorbed N719 molecules, pure ethanol was dripped onto the film and spun at 2000 rpm for 30 s for 2 times.

The precursor solution composed of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   and lead iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  ) (molar ratio of 1:1; 1.35 M) in a mixed solvent of N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) and dimethyl sulphoxide (DMSO) (volume ratio of 7:3) was deposited onto the HTLs by spin-coating at 5000 rpm for 60 s. Meanwhile, chlorobenzene was quickly dropped onto the center of the substrate 10 s after the spin-coating commences. The as-deposited film was then heated at 100 °C for 15 min for crystallization.

The BSO nanoparticles were synthesized and calcined according to the reported method:  $  ^{33}  $     $  BaCl_{2}\cdot2H_{2}O  $   (10 mmol),   $  SnCl_{4}\cdot5H_{2}O  $   (10 mmol) and critic acid (7.5 mmol) were dissolved in hydrogen peroxide aqueous solution (  $  H_{2}O_{2}  $  , 170 ml, 30%, OCI) by constant stirring. Then, 120 ml of aqueous   $  NH_{4}OH  $   (25%) was added to the solution for reaction. White precipitates were instantly formed by reaction and the solution was further stirred for 12 h under ambient condition. Afterwards, the white precipitates were obtained by centrifugation at 4000 rpm for 3 min and washed by distilled water. Finally, the white precipitate was freeze-dried for 24 h and calcined at 900 °C for 2 h in air. BSO nanoparticles were homogenously dispersed in chlorobenzene (  $  \sim  $  10 mg/ml) with ultrasonic. PCBM (10 mg/ml) in chlorobenzene was then dissolved in the BSO dispersion solution and spin-coated onto the perovskite layer at 1500 rpm for 40 s. For pure PCBM ETL, a solution of PCBM in chlorobenzene (20 mg/ml) was spin-coated onto the perovskite layer at 1500 rpm for 40 s.

Finally, silver with a thickness of about  $ 120\mathrm{nm} $  was thermally evaporated onto the ETLs to fabricate a top electrode.

We performed the interfacial passivation on the NiO $ _{x} $  side by innovatively using a uniform self-assembled dye molecule layer (noted as NiO $ _{x} $  (m) in the following). As demonstrated in the inset of Figure 1a, the functional groups of the dye molecules could react with the hydroxyl group or interact with the charged defects on the surface of solution-processed NiO $ _{x} $ . Such a strategy is anticipated to result in multiple benefits for the device performance, including: i) to serve as a “connection bridge” to improve the adhesion between the NiO $ _{x} $  film and the perovskite layer, and better facilitate the charge transfer by passivating the surface trap states; ii) to modify the work function of NiO $ _{x} $  through the dipole moments; iii) to change the perovskite crystallinity and morphology through chemical interactions by means of different terminated functional groups.

To enable efficient charge collection, the energy level alignment between the perovskite and each CTL is vital. The valence band (VB) edge of   $  NiO_{x}  $   (-5.19 eV),   $  NiO_{x}  $   (m) (-5.31 eV) and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   (-5.43 eV) are determined by photoemission yield spectroscopy analysis (PYSA) shown in Figure S2. It is notable that the extracted VB values for   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   are very close to those marked in the reported works.  $  ^{[16,23]}  $   After dye molecule modification, the valence band maximum of   $  NiO_{x}  $   was downward shifted by   $  \sim0.1  $   eV, which would be beneficial for reducing the interfacial energy loss, thus improving the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) yield.  $  ^{[34]}  $   Based on this, a BSO/PCBM hybrid ETL was further designed for efficient electron transport and improving the cell stability simultaneously. There is negligible difference in the conduction band (CB) edge between BSO and PCBM, both locating at   $  \sim3.9  $   eV.  $  ^{[35-36]}  $   This corresponds to a minimal potential energy loss at the interface with perovskite whose CB edge is   $  \sim3.88  $   eV.

Beyond this, excellent wettability is desirable for the contact material to obtain high quality perovskite films, as it straightforwardly determines how the perovskite crystallizes and grows on its surface. It is found that the dye modification largely improved the wettability of NiO $ _{x} $  HTLs, as the contact angle (CA) with perovskite precursor solution is reduced from 19.1° for pristine NiO $ _{x} $  (Figure 2a) to 14.2° for modified NiO $ _{x} $  (Figure 2b). Lower CA would allow the perovskite precursor solution to spread on the substrate easily and homogenously, thus being beneficial for the perovskite crystallization. This is further validated by the morphology of the as-prepared perovskite layer shown in Figure 2c and Figure 2d. The crystal-size distribution of perovskite based on different HTLs are illustrated in Figure S6 (a) and (b). Better uniformity and larger crystal size with an average value of ~290 nm were achieved based on the dye modified NiO $ _{x} $  (m). Nevertheless, perovskite based on pure NiO $ _{x} $  has an average diameter of ~185 nm. The crystallization variation is further confirmed by XRD patterns of the two perovskite films illustrated in Figure S7. The phenomena mentioned above might be induced by the coordinating effects between the -COO $ ^{-} $  group in N719 and MA $ ^{+} $  or Pb $ ^{2+} $  ions in MAPbI $ _{3} $ . $ ^{21} $

We found that both   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  NiO_{x}  $   (m) HTLs resulted in obviously eliminated PL peak intensity of perovskite layer compared to that based on bare glass, also known as quenching effect. Long-term stability is another critical challenge for PSCs in realizing the practical application. Non-encapsulated device would readily react with moisture and oxygen under ambient condition. Additionally, iodide ion migration to Ag electrode will form an insulating film that will result in high series resistance and therefore severe current loss.  $  ^{47}  $   This is adequately resolved by using our bifacial hybrid contact, as seen from the stability behavior performed under ambient condition (temperature   $  \sim20^{\circ}C  $  , humidity   $  \sim30\%  $  ). Figure 5a-d presents the evolution of all the photovoltaic metrics as a function of storage time under ambient condition for the devices with and without modifications at the perovskite/contact material interface. Both N719 modification and BSO/PCBM hybrid film pose positive effects on lifting the stabilities of the PCSs. In detail, PSC based on   $  NiO_{x}  $   (m) and BSO/PCBM solely can preserve about 50% and 80% of its initial PCE respectively in ambient air after about 650 hours (approximately 27 days), while the reference device without any modification degraded to only around 30%. The degradation is mainly contributed by the decrease of   $  J_{SC}  $   and FF, while Voc shows negligible reduction (over 90% of the initial   $  V_{OC}  $   for all devices). We think the stability enhancement by N719 modification could be ascribed to: i) the improved crystallization and thus palliate water invasion or penetration on the macro- or microscale dimensions;  $  ^{48}  $   ii) the elimination of hydroxyl groups by reaction.  $  ^{37}  $   On the ETL side, the incorporation of inorganic BSO into PCBM resulted in a denser film compared with pure PCBM ETL that could effectively block the penetration of the moisture and oxygen. Additionally, with more compact crystal structure and atomic stacking, ion migration (such as I  $  ^{-}  $  ) from the perovskite to the Ag contact could be prohibited.  $  ^{47,49}  $

# Conclusion:

In summary, we developed a new approach to effectively passivate the   $  NiO_{x}  $   surface by using a self-assembled dye molecule monolayer, which enhanced the contact between HTL and the perovskite layer, suppressed interfacial recombination and improved the hole extraction properties. We further developed an efficient BSO/PCBM hybrid ETL to replace the pure PCBM, which enhanced the coverage on the perovskite and lead to improved electron transport properties. Combining both passivating strategies dramatically advanced the p-i-n PSC performance from approximately 14% to 16.2%. Importantly, the well crystallized and compact perovskite layer induced by the dye modification and the uniform inorganic/organic BSO/PCBM blocking layer also dramatically improved the stability of the devices. The as-prepared device retained over 90% of its initial PCE after over 600-hour storage under ambient condition. Especially, the BSO/PCBM ETL could not only prevent the invasion of the ambient moisture and oxygen, but the ion migration from the perovskite to the contact material as well. This work therefore provides important insight to improve the contact material properties and interfacial passivation for further advancing the perovskite photovoltaic technology and other optoelectronic applications.
