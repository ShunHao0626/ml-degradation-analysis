# Abstract

We report an inverted perovskite solar cell (PSC) based on a solution-processed CuSCN thin film for hole transporting layer. Methylammonium lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{MAPbI}_3) $  perovskite film is formed on the CuSCN layer, which shows hysteresis-less current-voltage characteristics. Post-treatment of the CuSCN film with KSCN improves power conversion efficiency (PCE) from  $ 11.9\% $  to  $ 14.9\% $  due to main increment of photocurrent density from 16.71 to 19.20  $ \mathrm{mA/cm^2} $ . From the test with various salts of KX (X = SCN, I and Br) and MSCN (M = K and Na), we have found that thiocyanate anion plays more critical role in improving photovoltaic parameters. Photoluminescence (PL) is more quenched and PL decay time is faster after post-treatment with KSCN, which is indicative of better charge separation and responsible for the improved photocurrent density. In addition interface recombination, estimated by impedance spectroscopy, is suppressed by the post-treatment with KSCN, which is related to the improved open-circuit voltage. PSCs with KSCN-treated CuSCN demonstrates better photo- and humidity-stability as compared to those with untreated CuSCN, which is likely to correlate with improved interface.

# Introduction

Perovskite solar cell (PSC) receives great attention due to its superb photovoltaic performance. Since the first report on  $ 9.7\% $  -efficient, 500 h-stable PSC based on methylammonium lead iodide  $ \mathrm{(MAPbI_3)} $  in 2012 [1], following two important works on perovskite-sensitized liquid junction solar cells in 2009 [2] and 2011 [3], there is a preponderance of research works on PSC. As a result, ca. 10,000 peer-reviewed articles were published as of September 2018. This clearly indicates that PSC is regarded as a disruptive technology in photovoltaics. The best power conversion efficiency (PCE) of over  $ 22\% $  was achieved based on a normal mesoscopic perovskite solar cell employing a polymeric hole transporting material (HTM) [4,5]. The origins of such a superb photovoltaic performance are related to high absorption coefficient, high carrier mobility and low exciton binding energy. In fabricating PSCs, either normal structure or inverted structure is available. Normal

structure is composed of the perovskite film deposited on an electron transporting material (ETM) formed on TCO (transparent conductive oxide) substrate, while inverted structure comprises the perovskite film deposited on HTM formed on TCO. For the inverted PSCs, organic poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) has been commonly used as a HTM [6,7]. Inorganic HTMs have been also studied for the inverted structure, where CuI [8] and CuSCN [9] were firstly studied as HTMs and then PbS quantum dots [10],  $ \mathrm{CuInS}_2 $  quantum dots [11],  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  [12], CuS [13],  $ \mathrm{CuO_x} $  [14],  $ \mathrm{MoO_3} $  [15] and  $ \mathrm{WO_3} $  [16] were applied to the inverted PSCs. Among them, CuSCN is a good candidate because of high transparency in the visible light, inexpensive (more than 100 times cheaper than Spiro-MeOTAD [17]) and high hole mobility of  $ 0.01 - 0.1~\mathrm{cm}^2 /\mathrm{Vs} $  [18,19]. The PCE exceeding  $ 20\% $  was reported by using a CuSCN thin film in the normal structure, where the fast carrier extraction and collection was confirmed [20]. Moreover, 1000-h stability under one sun illumination and at  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  was demonstrated by insertion of a conductive graphene oxide spacer layer between the CuSCN layer and the Au electrode. However, relatively low PCE was reported when the CuSCN was used in the inverted structure. Ye et al. reported the CuSCN-based inverted PSCs with the PCEs ranging from about  $ 11\% $  to  $ 16\% $  depending on preparation procedure of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film [21]. The CuSCN layer was prepared via electro-deposition of the aqueous precursor solution containing coper sulfate  $ (\mathrm{CuSO}_4) $ , ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) and potassium thiocyanate (KSCN). Spin-coating method was reported to fabricate the CuSCN film for the inverted PSC, which showed a PCE of  $ 10.8\% $  with negligible hysteresis [22]. Aqueous ammonia based CuSCN film showed much better photovoltaic performance than diethyl sulfide based one because of better hole mobility and less defect density [23]. Nevertheless, the CuSCN-based PSCs were reported to be unstable, which is related to interface between CuSCN and perovskite layer [24].

We report here effect of post-treatment of the CuSCN layer with KSCN in the inverted PSCs on photovoltaic performance and stability. A solution-processed bare CuSCN film shows PCE of  $ 11.9\% $  which is significantly improved to  $ 14.9\% $  by surface modification of CuSCN film with KSCN. In addition, the KSCN post-treatment enhances stability. Effect of post-treatment

# Experimental Details

Materials: Copper thiocyanate (99%), diethyl sulfide (98%), potassium thiocyanate (99%), sodium thiocyanate (99.99%), potassium iodide (99%), potassium bromide (99%), lead iodide (99.9985%), N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.9%), diethyl ether (99.7%), isopropyl alcohol (IPA, 99.7%), chlorobenzene (99.8%), and hydroiodic acid (57 wt% in water) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. [6,6]-Phenyl C61 butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM, 99.5%) was purchased from Fine Chemicals Industry. Bathocuproine (BCP, 99.0%) and methylamine (40 wt% in water) were purchased from Tokyo Chemical Industry (TCI). All the chemicals were used as-received without further purification. Indium tin oxide (ITO)-coated glass with a sheet resistance of ≤10 Ω/sq was used as a transparent electrode.

Synthesis of methylammonium iodide (MAI). MAI was synthesized by reacting  $ 30~\mathrm{mL} $  of hydroiodic acid with  $ 27.8~\mathrm{mL} $  of methylamine at  $ 0^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , where hydroiodic acid was slowly dropped into a round-bottom flask filled with methylamine. After stirring for  $ 2\mathrm{h} $ , dark yellow precipitate was recovered by evaporating the solvent at  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  using rotary evaporator. The solid was washed with diethyl ether and recrystallized from ethanol. Finally, white precipitate was dried under vacuum at  $ 65^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 24\mathrm{h} $  and then stored in glove box filled with argon.

Device fabrication. ITO-coated glass was cleaned with detergent, wished with water and ethanol, rinsed with acetone and then dried with the dry air flow. Prior to deposition of a thin CuSCN on the ITO-coated substrate, the ITO substrate was treated with ultraviolet-ozone (UVO) for 20 min. The coating solution for the CuSCN film was prepared by dissolving CuSCN in diethyl sulfide (30 mg/mL), which was spin-coated on the ITO substrate at 2000 rpm for 30 s. The CuSCN film was obtained by annealing at 65 °C for 5 min. The CuSCN

film was further treated with KSCN by spin-coating the KSCN solution (2 mg/mL in IPA) at 4000 rpm for 30 s and annealing at 85 °C for 4 min. Methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) film was formed on the bare CuSCN film or the KSCN-treated CuSCN film by spin-coating the 1.3 M precursor solution containing MAI (0.159 g), PbI  $  _{2}  $   (0.461 g), DMSO (78 μL) and DMF (0.691 mL) at 4000 rpm for 25 s, where 500 μL of diethyl ether was dripped on the rotating substrate to remove DMF and induce adduct film. The transparent adduct film was annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. 50 μL of the PCBM solution in chlorobenzene (20 mg/mL) was spin-coated on the top of perovskite layer at 2000 rpm for 30 s. Subsequently, a BCP layer was formed by spin-coating the BCP solution in IPA (0.5 mg/mL) at 5000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, the devices were completed by deposition of a ca. 160 nm-thick Ag electrode using thermal evaporator onto BCP under ca. 3.8×10  $  ^{-7}  $   torr at evaporation rate of 0.3 Å/s.

Characterizations. Photocurrent density-voltage (J-V) curves were obtained by a solar simulator with 450-W Xenon lamp (Newport 6279 NS) and a Keithley 2400 source meter. The devices were illuminated under AM 1.5G one sun (100 mW/cm²) which was adjusted by the NREL-calibrated silicon reference solar cell with a KG-5 filter. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) was measured using a EQE system quipped with the 75-W Xenon lamp (USHIO) as a white light source, where the monochromatic beam was generated by chopping the white light. The electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) of the perovskite solar cells were measured with an electrochemical workstation (Autolab, Eco-Chemie) in a frequency range of 0.1 Hz ~1 MHz in the dark. The impedance data was analyzed by Z-view software. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images were observed with a field-emission scanning electron microscopy (JSM7000F). The UV-vis spectra were obtained using a UV-vis spectrophotometer (Lambda 45, Perkin Elmer). Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra were measured by using a fluorescence spectrometer (QuantaurusTau C11367-12, Hamamatsu) with excitation of a 464-nm laser (PLP-10, Humamatsu) pulsed at a frequency of 10 MHz for steady-state PL and 200 KHz for TRPL. TRPL data were recorded by a time-correlated single-photon counting (TCSPC) technique. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were obtained by Bruker AXS (D8 Advance,

In order to understand whether potassium cation (  $  K^{+}  $  ) or thiocyanate anion (  $  SCN^{-}  $  ) influences photovoltaic performance, other chemicals with different cation or anion are investigated. Firstly, potassium salts are investigated to see any anion effect using KSCN, KI and KBr. Figure 3 shows that photovoltaic performance is not affected by the post-treatment with both KI and KBr but much improved by the post-treatment with KSCN. This indicates that thiocyanate anion plays critical role in improving photovoltaic performance. It was reported that excess   $  SCN^{-}  $  could increase hole conductivity of CuSCN film [25,26]. Thus, KSCN post-treatment in our study might improve hole conductivity by excess   $  SCN^{-}  $ , which might be responsible for the improvement.

Figure 7 shows surface morphology of perovskite films formed on the bare CuSCN film and the KSCN-treated CuSCN film. It is interesting to see that perovskite film morphology is different, which indicates importance of substrate in forming perovskite film. Size distribution is homogeneous in the perovskite film formed on the KSCN-treated CuSCN film (Figure 7(a)) as compared to inhomogeneous size distribution observed in the perovskite film deposited on the bare CuSCN film (Figure 7(b)). Grain size distribution in perovskite film was reported to affect photovoltaic property, where inhomogeneous size distribution led to non-radiative recombination and thereby low photovoltaic performance [27]. Therefore, homogeneous perovskite grain size induced by the KSCN post-treatment might contribute in part to the enhanced photovoltaic performance. In addition, difference in grain size distribution underlines that interface between CuSCN and perovskite might be different. Absorbance is hardly affected, which again evidences that the improved  $ \mathrm{J_{sc}} $  is closely related to the improved conductivity of CuSCN and/or better interface formed on the KSCN-treated CuSCN film.

To get better insight into the charge transport phenomena, carrier mobility is estimated for the CuSCN films before and after the KSCN post-treatment by using space charge limited current (SCLC) method based on the Mott-Gurney SCLC equation [29-32]. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) is measured for characterizing recombination resistance ( $ \mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{rec}} $ ) in an intermediate frequency region. Equivalent circuit with two RC components is constructed for fitting the data according to the previous reports [34-36]. Fit result shows that the resistance for the PSC based on the KSCN-treated CuSCN is determined to be  $ 1.49\times 10^{6}\Omega $  that is nearly 20 times higher than the resistance of  $ 0.68\times 10^{5}\Omega $  for the untreated CuSCN. # Conclusion

We demonstrated how to improve PCE and stability in an inverted perovskite solar cell (PSC) based on the CuSCN hole transporting layer. Post-treatment of the CuSCN film with SCN  $  ^{-}  $  ion was found to improve significantly photovoltaic property and stability because better electronic property of the CuSCN film was realized without sacrificing optical transmittance. In this work, stability of PSC was found to be strongly dependent on the interface of heterojunction. This work can give important insight into importance of interface between perovskite and inorganic hole transporting layer in terms of carrier injection and long-term stability as well.
