# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials have recently gained substantial attention for their long diffusion length   $  [1]  $  , broad absorption range   $  [2]  $  , low-cost processing   $  [3]  $  , and ease of fabrication   $  [4]  $  . These excellent features make them the most potential materials for the fabrication of high-performance photovoltaic devices. Power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has made great progress over these few years   $  [5–17]  $  .

In the past few years, ZnO nanorod arrays (NAs) have been widely used as electron transport layer because of their excellent properties such as fast electron mobility, high light absorption and providing direct channels for electron extraction. However, owing to the charge recombination and instability of ZnO/perovskite bilayer, the stability of PSCs based on ZnO nanorod arrays as electron transport layer is limited. The high-power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term environmental stability are two critical factors for the development of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, blends of polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) and polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) have been added to improve the quality of perovskite film, stabilize perovskite crystal structure, and enhance the efficiency and air stability of PSCs. The device modified by 1.5 wt%PVP/0.5 wt%PVA shows a 28.3% increasement of PCE compared to that of the pristine one. Most importantly, the unencapsulated device retains over 82.2% of its initial efficiency even after 30 days in an atmospheric environment with 70% relative humidity. Furthermore, the effect mechanisms of PVP/PVA blends on the properties of   $  Cs_{0.2}FA_{0.3}MA_{0.5}Pb(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_{3}  $   film and the performance of corresponding solar cell were clarified. For one thing, the interactions between O atoms of C=O in PVP and the incomplete coordinated Pb in   $  Cs_{0.2}FA_{0.3}MA_{0.5}Pb(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_{3}  $   passivate the defects in perovskite. For another thing, the hydrogen bonds between O atoms of OH in PVA, and H atoms of MA (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}^{-}  $  ) and FA (  $  CH(NH_{2})_{2}^{+}  $  ) in   $  Cs_{0.2}FA_{0.3}MA_{0.5}Pb(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_{3}  $   stabilize the perovskite structure. Such synergistic effect between PVP/PVA blends and   $  Cs_{0.2}FA_{0.3}MA_{0.5}Pb(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_{3}  $   perovskite effectively optimized the quality of perovskite film and stabilized perovskite film, ultimately improving the device performance. Our study provides a useful way to simultaneously improve the efficiency and stability of lead halide PSCs, and supplies a profitable reference for their commercialization.

Up to date, much work has been done to improve the tolerance of perovskite materials to humidity. Liu's group have introduced the J71-polymer into perovskite solar cell, which makes the device maintain 90% of its initial PCE after exposure to the ambient environment for two weeks [30]. Fakharuddin et al. [31] added PVP polymer into MAPbI $ _3 $ , attaining an improved stability at ambient with relative humidity between 50–70%. By introducing PVP into the perovskite precursor solution, Xiong et al. [32] realized highly compact and uniform perovskite films, and obtained optimized device stability in ambient air with relative humidity over 60%. Sun et al. [33] adopted PVA polymer as additive into perovskite and the unencapsulated devices held improved stability under 90% relative humidity. These studies show that the stability of PSCs can be modified by PVP or PVA single additive. Furthermore, the PSC with 1.5 wt%PVP/0.5 wt%PVA blends maintains more than 82.2% of its initial PCE after 30 days in the air, which is much better than those of the unmodified device and the single polymer modified device.

# 2.2. Preparation of ZnO@ZnSe NAs

The ZnO@ZnSe NAs were fabricated by a successive ion layer absorption and reaction (SILAR) method. Sodium selenosulfate   $  \left(\mathrm{Na}_{2}\mathrm{SeSO}_{3}\right)  $   solution was prepared by refluxing selenium (Se) and hydrazine hydrate in a sodium sulfite   $  \left(\mathrm{Na}_{2}\mathrm{SO}_{3}\right)  $   aqueous solution. The ZnO NAs film was alternately dipped into   $  \mathrm{Zn(CH_{3}COO)_{2}\cdot2H_{2}O}  $   aqueous solution and   $  Na_{2}SeSO_{3}  $   aqueous solution, and then rinsed with deionized water and dried with   $  N_{2}  $  . Finally, ZnO NAs were coated with ZnSe layer by using several SILAR cycles.

# 2.3. Perovskite solar cell fabrication in ambient atmosphere

The complete scheme of perovskite solar cell fabrication by two-step sequential deposition method in ambient atmosphere is depicted in Fig. 1 (a). Perovskite precursor solution were prepared by dissolving 0.461 g PbI $ _2 $ , 0.065 g PbBr $ _2 $ , 0.061 g CsI, 0.061 g FAI in N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF)/dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) mixed solvents with a certain volume ratio of 9:1 under stirring at 70 °C. Then different mass ratios of PVP to PVA were added into the above precursor solution, and the solution was spin-coated on the ZnO@ZnSe NAs films at 3000 r/min and annealed at 70 °C for half an hour. Afterwards, perovskite layers were formed by dipping these slides into MAI in isopropanol (IPA) for a certain time and then annealed at 100 °C for 2 min. After that, 50 μL spiro-MeOTAD solution was spin-coated on the perovskite films at 3000 r/min. Finally, 80 nm Au film was deposited by thermal evaporation. The active area of the device is 0.08 cm $ ^{2} $ .

# 2.4. Characterization

Morphologies of samples were characterized by field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM) (Hitachi, SU8010). Crystal structures of the samples were studied by X-ray diffractometer (XRD) (PANalytical, X'Pert PRO). UV–vis absorption and Photoluminescence (PL) investigations of the samples were recorded by UV–vis spectrophotometer (MAPADA, UV-6100pc) and fluorophotometer (HORIBA, FL3-211-P), respectively. Time resolved photoluminescence spectra (TRPL) were measured by fluorophotometer (HORIBA, FL3-211-P). Photocurrent density-voltage (J-V) curves and hysteresis effects of the cells were measured by electrochemistry workstation (ChenHua, CHI660A) under an irradiation of simulated solar light (AM 1.5 G, 100 mW/cm²). Monochromatic incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) of the cells was obtained via electrochemistry workstation (Zahner, CIPMS-2) under a constant light illumination of 100 mW/cm². Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra of the samples were measured by infrared spectrometer (JASCO, FT-IR-6100).

In order to further analyze the recombination behavior of perovskite films without and with  $ 1.5\% \mathrm{PVP} / 0.5\% \mathrm{PVA} $  blends, we measured the time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra, as shown in Fig. 6, where CsFAMA stands for  $ \mathrm{Cs_{0.2}FA_{0.3}MA_{0.5}Pb(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_3} $ . The TRPL curves were fitted with the following bi-exponential decay function [40]:

$$
\mathrm{y} = \mathrm{A} _ {1} \exp (- t / \tau_ {1}) + \mathrm{A} _ {2} \exp (- t / \tau_ {2}),
$$

where  $  \mathrm{A}_1  $  and  $  \mathrm{A}_2  $  are the relative amplitudes, and  $  \tau_1  $  and  $  \tau_2  $  are fast decay component and slow decay component, respectively. And we calculated the average carrier lifetimes  $  (\tau_{\mathrm{ave}})  $  of perovskite films by the following formula [41]:

$$
\tau_ {\text { ave }} = \Sigma A _ {i} \tau_ {i} ^ {2} / \Sigma A _ {i} \tau_ {i}.
$$

The fitting parameters are shown in Table 1. Result shows that the perovskite film modified with  $ 1.5\% \mathrm{PVP} / 0.5\% \mathrm{PVA} $  exhibits longer  $ \tau_{\mathrm{ave}} $  (43.24 ns) than the unmodified film (4.68 ns), indicating that the addition of  $ 1.5\% \mathrm{PVP} / 0.5\% \mathrm{PVA} $  blends suppressed the defects controlled non-radiative recombination of perovskite film, which can be attributed to the enlarged grain size with less defects [42]. This result is consistent with the SEM and PL results in Figs. 2 and 5 (b).

As mentioned before, air stability is another key factor for the development of PSCs. Environmental stability of the PSCs without encapsulation was investigated by monitoring the PCE evolution of solar cells during exposure to an ambient environment (RH   $  \sim  $   70%, 25   $  {}^{\circ}  $  C) for 30 days, as shown in Fig. 9. It is easy to see that PCE of the device without any polymer addition was dramatically reduced to 22.5% of its initial PCE after 9 days, indicating the rapid decomposition of perovskite. However, the device with 2 wt% PVP retained more than 62.4% of its initial PCE even after 30 days. Under the same conditions, the device modified by 1 wt% PVA can keep more than 70.1% of its initial PCE after 30 days. Especially, the device with 1.5 wt%PVP/0.5 wt% PVA blends maintained more than 82.2% of its initial PCE after 30 days, demonstrating its better environmental stability. The improved air stability of the devices with PVP/PVA blends can be ascribed to the excellent hygroscopicity of PVP/PVA blends, and the strong interactions between perovskite and PVP/PVA blends make perovskite structure stable, thus inhibiting film decomposition. The PVP and PVA molecules on the surface of perovskite film can absorb moisture efficiently to form a compact moisture barrier around perovskite crystal grains and prevent moisture from entering the perovskite film [24].

# 4. Conclusions

We have demonstrated the synchronous improvement of the efficiency and air stability of PSCs via the modification of  $ \mathrm{Cs_{0.2}FA_{0.3}} $ .  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.5}\mathrm{Pb(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_3} $  perovskite by PVP/PVA blends. The perovskite solar cell with  $ 1.5\mathrm{wt}\% \mathrm{PVP} / 0.5\mathrm{wt}\% \mathrm{PVA} $  modification exhibits an efficiency enhancement of  $ 28.3\% $  compared with the unmodified device. Besides, PCE of the unsealed cell with  $ 1.5\mathrm{wt}\% \mathrm{PVP} / 0.5\mathrm{wt}\% \mathrm{PVA} $  blends could still maintain more than  $ 82.2\% $  of its initial value after 30 days in high humidity environment, which clearly indicated its better air stability.
