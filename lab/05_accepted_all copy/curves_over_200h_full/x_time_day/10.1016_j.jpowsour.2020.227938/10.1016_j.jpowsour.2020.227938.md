# GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT

# ABSTRACT

Hole-transporting materials (HTMs) play a vital role of transporting holes from the perovskite layer to the counter electrode in perovskite solar cells (PSCs). A novel HTM BTPA-8 is feasibly synthesized by incorporating four dimethoxytriphenylamine leaflets and anthracene-based central bridge. BTPA-8 exhibits a suitable band alignment with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  ( $ \mathrm{MA} = \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3 $ ) or  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.85}\mathrm{MA}_{0.15}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  ( $ \mathrm{FA} = \mathrm{HC}(\mathrm{NH}_2)_2 $ ), high hole mobility, and high thermal stability. The best  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.85}\mathrm{MA}_{0.15}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  device based on BTPA-8 exhibits a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 17.99\% $  in the reverse scan with an aperture area of  $ 0.09~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ . Under the same condition, a PCE of  $ 18.92\% $  is achieved by the solar cell based on the standard spiro-OMeTAD (2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene). A comparable PCE of  $ 12.31\% $  is also obtained for BTPA-8 based  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  device compared with spiro-OMeTAD (13.25%) with an aperture area  $ >1~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ . BTPA-8 based PSCs exhibit better long-term stability than spiro-OMeTAD due to its high hydrophobicity. A lower synthesis cost of BTPA-8 than that of spiro-OMeTAD along with the elevated long-term stability makes it promising for application in PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

In recent years, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted worldwide interest with its rapidly increasing power conversion efficiency (PCE) initiated from 3.8% in 2009 [1] to the current reported maximum value of 25.2% [2]. Cost, efficiency, and stability are the three main restriction factors to its large-scale application. Besides various perovskite materials, hole transporting materials (HTMs) are of great significance in determining the device performance, which are responsible for transporting the photogenerated holes and blocking the electrons from the perovskite material to the Au electrode. In recent years, a wide number of HTMs, ranging from conducting polymers [3–6] via inorganic compounds [7–10] to small molecules [11–14], have been synthesized and investigated in PSCs. Among them, the ideal small molecular HTMs should possess well matched highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels, good solubility in common solvents, appropriate hydrophobic character, superior thermal stability, and good hole mobility. As we all know, spiro-OMeTAD (2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene) is used as the typical HTM in PSCs [15–17]. However, the device stability and the high cost have constrained its large scale application [18]. Particularly its synthesis involves 6 reaction steps with harsh reaction conditions like the use of corrosive reagents (Br₂), quite low temperature (-78.8 °C), and highly sensitive reagents (n-butyllithium or Grignard reagent). Recently, many efforts have been devoted to exploring alternative HTMs to replace spiro-OMeTAD by lowering the cost of the precursor chemicals including raw materials and reagents to improve the reaction yields, as well as to shorten the reaction steps. More and more small HTMs with low cost have been synthesized and investigated in PSCs, such as triphenylamine (TPA)-core derivatives [19,20], a difluorinated phenyl derivative [21], azomethine derivatives [22], a pentacene derivative [23], carbazole derivatives [24], spiro [fluorene-9,9'-xanthene]derivatives [25,26], a silafluorene derivative [27], and so on.

Besides, anthracene derivatives as semiconductors have shown excellent potential for application in organic electronic devices such as organic field-effect transistors (OFET) [28,29]. However, anthracene unit based HTMs have not been employed in PSCs to date. Moreover, it is of significantly practical value to develop novel HTMs using anthracene unit due to their facile synthesis and low cost as shown in Table 1. In this study, for the first time, we designed and synthesized an anthracene-containing small molecular HTM, BTPA-8, as shown in Fig. 1. This HTM consists of four methoxy-substituted TPA as the branches and the anthracene units as the central core. Interestingly, the four TPA branches appear to be the four leaflets of a lucky clover. We investigate its photochemical properties, electrochemical properties, environmental stability, hole conductivity, hole mobility as well as the device performance of  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.85}\mathrm{MA}_{0.15}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  or  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  ( $ \mathrm{FA} = \mathrm{HC(NH_2)_2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{MA} = \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3 $ ) PSCs with BTPA-8 as HTM in comparison to similar devices using spiro-OMeTAD as HTM.

# 3.2. UV-vis absorption and electrochemical property

The UV–vis absorption spectrum of BTPA-8 was measured in   $  CH_{2}Cl_{2}  $   as shown in Fig. 2a. BTPA-8 showed one major absorption peak at 302 nm with the corresponding molar extinction coefficient of   $  7.55 \times 10^{4} M^{-1} cm^{-1}  $  . Fig. 2b shows the energy diagram for a PSC. The HOMO level for BTPA-8 was calculated from its first oxidation peak shown in the electrochemical measurement (see the Supporting Information, Fig. S4) by the following equation:   $  E_{HOMO} = -4.5 - E_{ox}  $   to be -5.28 eV. All the

optical and electrochemical data are summarized in Table 2. This evidences that BTPA-8 has a matched energy level for favorable hole transfer from  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  or  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.85}\mathrm{MA}_{0.15}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  in PSCs [31]. Importantly, the electrons in perovskite materials would be prohibited from transferring to the Au electrode due to the insertion of BTPA-8 which has a much higher lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level, as obtained from absorption and luminescence spectra, than the perovskite material. DFT calculations (B3LYP functional, 6-311G (d,p) basis set, Gaussian 09 software) indicate that, as shown in Fig. 2b, the electron density of the HOMO is mainly located at two of the four branches and the electron density of the LUMO is localized at the central anthracene unit. The UV-vis absorption peak for BTPA-8 is mainly attributed to this significant charge-transfer (CT) character of the HOMO to LUMO transition.

# 3.4. Device performance

The current density–voltage (J-V) characteristics of   $  FA_{0.85}MA_{0.15}PbI_{3}  $   PSC devices with BTPA-8 or spiro-OMeTAD were measured under AM1.5G 1 sun (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) intensity with aperture area of 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $   (Fig. 3). The photovoltaic performances of the devices with BTPA-8 obtained immediately after fabrication are listed in Table 4 and the PSC device parameter distribution (PCE,   $  J_{sc}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $  , and FF) for a batch of 20 PSCs is shown in Fig. S8. In the reverse scan, BTPA-8 yielded a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 17.99% with a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 24.21 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , an open-circuit photovoltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.04 V, a fill factor (FF) of 0.71. Under the same condition, spiro-OMeTAD yields a PCE of 18.92% with a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 24.24 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.07 V, a FF of 0.73. The slightly lower   $  J_{sc}  $   of PSC based on BTPA-8 was in good agreement with the lower integrated   $  J_{sc}  $   value as seen from the IPCE curve, which also agreed with its relatively lower conductivity compared with that of spiro-OMeTAD (  $  2.82 \times 10^{-4}  $   S cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ) [35]. The BTPA-8 and spiro-OMeTAD based PSCs exhibit a stabilized PCE of 17.83% and 18.85%, respectively. By comparison, the BTPA-8 based PSC showed a more stable behaviour over 200 s under one sun illumination.

# 3.6. Stability

The glass transition temperature  $ (T_{\mathrm{g}}) $ , which has been shown to have quantitative relations with the stability of the noncrystalline (amorphous) phase for HTMs, is certainly crucial for PSC solar cell device performance and stability [48,49]. A higher  $ T_{\mathrm{g}} $  is generally required for organic HTMs in PSCs. It is clearly observed from Fig. 5a that the  $ T_{\mathrm{g}} $  value for BTPA-8 is  $ 121^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , which is comparable to that for spiro-OMeTAD  $ (125^{\circ}\mathrm{C}) $  [50]. From the point of HTMs themselves, thermal stability should be a vital parameter representing the sensitivity to the air. As shown in Fig. 5b, BTPA-8 shows the decomposed

temperature above 300 °C in ambient air, which indicates that BTPA-8 has an excellent thermal stability. Besides, the long-term stability is the biggest determinant for the commercialization of PSC devices. Generally, hydrophobicity is effective to improve the long-term stability of PSCs [51]. As shown in Fig. 5c, the water contact angles of BTPA-8 is 81°, which is significantly higher than that of spiro-OMeTAD (70°) [35]. HTMs with higher water contact angles tend to show better protection from humidity for the solar cell devices [52,53]. After 10 days, BTPA-8 based PSC device showed 7% reduction of its initial PCE while spiro-OMeTAD exhibited about 26% reduction under the humidity of ~30% in the air. It can be concluded that, compared with spiro-OMeTAD, BTPA-8 exhibited a better long-term stability.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, a novel “lucky clover” hole-transport material named as BTPA-8 with anthracene unit as a conjugated core and four methoxy-substituted triphenylamine units as branches was synthesized and successfully employed in PSCs. BTPA-8 exhibits a suitable band alignment with MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   or FA  $  _{0.85}  $  MA  $  _{0.15}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $  , a comparable glass transition temperature to that of spiro-OMeTAD, high hole mobility, and high thermal stability. Thus, a comparable device performance is obtained for BTPA-8 compared with spiro-OMeTAD whether in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   or FA  $  _{0.85}  $  MA  $  _{0.15}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $   PSCs. The best FA  $  _{0.85}  $  MA  $  _{0.15}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $   device based on BTPA-8 exhibited a PCE of 17.99% in the reverse scan with an aperture area of 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  . More hydrophobic BTPA-8 based PSC exhibits better environmental stability than spiro-OMeTAD. Lower synthesis cost and better stability of its PSCs, with comparable performances, than spiro-OMeTAD make BTPA-8 promising as a HTM in the PSCs.
