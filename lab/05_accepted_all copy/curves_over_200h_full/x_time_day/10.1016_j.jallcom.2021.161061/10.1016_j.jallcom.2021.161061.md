# 1. Introduction

With ease in the fabrication process, high efficiency and low cost, the organo-metal halide PSCs has soared from 3.8% photo conversion efficiency (PCE) to 25.6% (certified 25.2%) in a short span of time. Organic-inorganic halide perovskites   $  \left(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{PbI}_{3}\right)  $   has high-absorption coefficient, tuneable bandgap over a range of 1.48–2.3 eV, longer diffusion length, ambipolar in nature and high tolerance to crystal defects; which has created a scope to utilize this material in optoelectronic applications [1–7]. # ABSTRACT

One dimensional (1-D)   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanorod (NR) scaffold layers are considered to be a superior electron transporting layer (ETL) for perovskite solar cells (PSC) as compared to   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticles (NP) in relation to morphological and electronic properties. Rutile phase porous   $  TiO_{2}-NR  $   has been grown with different lengths and porosity via the hydrothermal method. Porosity and length of the rods are controlled by varying the reaction time and concentration of the precursor solution. Change in the morphology and their effect on the photovoltaic and charge transport properties are investigated deeply through scanning electron microscopy, current density voltage (J-V) curves and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). The synthesized   $  TiO_{2}-NR  $   and   $  NP-TiO_{2}  $   electrodes are used to fabricate ambient processed PSCs. Compared to the conventional   $  NP-TiO_{2}  $   based PSC device, the device is prepared with the 350 nm porous   $  TiO_{2}-NR  $   has shown improved power conversion efficiency (14.0%). Such enhancement in photovoltaic performance arises from uniform infiltration of perovskite into the scaffold layer, which enhances the light collection efficiency and increases electron-transport property. Furthermore, the NR based PSC devices have retained 80% of its initial performance even after 50 days of being stored in ambient conditions (relative humidity (RH) - 60%). These results further emphasize the use of 1-D NR as ETL for ambient processed PSC with improved stability.

Despite the faster electron transport, ZnO nanorod based PSCs were inferior to those of   $  TiO_{2}  $  -NR based solar cells and had to be studied extensively to understand the reason [22]. Several reports explaining the effect of   $  TiO_{2}  $  -NR and its physical properties on the performance of both PSC and DSSC are scrutinized [25–29]. Whereas, the ambient processed perovskite film exhibits better crystallinity and grain size, at 25% relative humidity conditions [30]. Fabrication under the presence of small humidity promotes the nucleation sites, which favours crystal growth [31–33].

In this work, we have established a correlation between the lengths of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR and porosity of the electrode for the ambient processed PSCs. The porosity of the electrode plays a vital role in perovskite infiltration and sensitization. The inter-pore distance between the two  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR determine the porosity of the photoelectrode, and the porosity varied as the growth of the nanorod length increases. In this work, we had precise control over the length, porosity and the morphology of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR from compact film to nanostructured film. Nanostructures of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR were controlled by varying the concentration of the titania precursor and the growth time. PCE for nanorod based PSCs enhanced with variation in the length and porosity of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR. All the devices are reported in this work have been prepared by a two-step sequential deposition process in ambient atmosphere.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Preparation of FTO substrate and   $  TiO_{2}  $   seed layer film

Commercially available FTO substrates (  $  \sim  $  15   $  \Omega/\Box  $  ) were patterned and cleaned with the standard procedure (i.e.) cleaned with detergent, DI water, acetone and ethanol followed each step for 10 min in ultrasonication. Two different concentration (0.15 M & 0.3 M) of compact layer   $  TiO_{2}  $   solution was prepared using TTIP (titanium isopropoxide and butanol and coated on to the cleaned FTO substrates. The substrates were treated in hotplate at 100 °C/10 min and then annealed at 500 °C for 30 min

# 2.2. Growth of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  -NR over the seed  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer

Vertically aligned  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR was grown by hydrothermal method by our previously reported method with small modifications [17].

$ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanorod precursor solution was prepared by mixing  $ 25\mathrm{ml} $  of hydrochloric acid (HCl) into  $ 25\mathrm{ml} $  of DI water (1:1 ratio) and allowed to stir at room temperature for  $ 10\mathrm{min} $ . Then,  $ 0.5\mathrm{ml} $  or  $ 0.7\mathrm{ml} $  of titanium diisopropoxide bis-acetylactonate was added into the mixture and kept it for stirring at room temperature for  $ 5\mathrm{min} $ . The seed layer coated FTO substrate was placed in the autoclave at an angle of  $ 45^{\circ} $  with the conducting side facing up, which was then sealed and heated at a constant temperature of  $ 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 1.5, 3.0 and  $ 4.5\mathrm{h} $  to obtain the desired length of 350, 500 and  $ 700\mathrm{nm} $ , respectively. After the hydrothermal process, the reactor was kept in the furnace until it reaches room temperature. Then, the substrates were taken out and cleaned using DI water and ethanol, subsequently annealed at  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} / 30\mathrm{min} $ . As grown  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NR samples were further treated with  $ \mathrm{TiCl_4} $  treatment at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} / 1\mathrm{h} $ .

# 2.3. Photovoltaic device fabrication

Methylammonium iodide (MAI) was synthesized by treating hydroiodic acid and methylamine solution under certain processing conditions as reported in our previous publication   $  [34]  $  . Freshly prepared   $  PbI_{2}  $   and MAI solutions are used for the device fabrication process. The active layer was deposited using two-step sequential technique. At first,   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer was formed with spin coating method over the   $  TiO_{2}  $  -NR film and annealed at 70 °C; then the MAI dissolved in IPA solution was dropped and spin-coated over the   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer with a loading time of 20 s and annealed at 100 °C for 20 min. The spiro-OMeTAD solution was prepared by mixing 72 mg of spiro-OMeTAD in 1 ml of chlorobenzene, in order to improve the conductivity of the solution, additives such as 37.5 μl of 4-tert-butylpyridine and 20.5 μl of bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (520 mg in ml acetonitrile) were added. The prepared HTM solution was spin-coated over the perovskite layer and finally top contact (Au) was deposited by thermal evaporation. For comparison, NP-  $  TiO_{2}  $   devices were also prepared with similar experimental conditions.

# 2.4. Characterization

The structure of the prepared   $  TiO_{2}  $  -NR was analyzed using Field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) (Hitachi S3400N) and High-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM) (Tecnai G2–20T 200 kV). The crystallinity and phase structure of   $  TiO_{2}  $  -NR was identified with X-ray diffraction (XRD). The fabricated device was studied with Newport Solar Simulator under AM 1.5G spectral current distribution. The output illumination was adjusted to 100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and analysis of current-voltage (J-V) characteristics of the device was performed with Keithley source meter. Bentham PVE300-XY analyzed the IPCE performance of the devices and impedance studies were done in dark using electrochemical workstation (PGSTAT302N, AUTOLAB). Impedance spectra were recorded at DC bias extended from 0.8 V to 1 V superimposed with 10 mV AC perturbation in the frequency range from 100 mHz to 100 kHz. (Fig. S3). The devices were stored under dark at ambient conditions with a relative humidity of 60 ± 5% in order to study the stability. The stability test data was collected by measuring the photovoltaic properties of the exposed devices on alternative days.

The PSCs prepared with  $ 350\mathrm{nm} $   $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR exhibited better photovoltaic performance (14.0%), when compared with the conventional NP- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  (12.0%), which is of similar ETL thickness. The similar thickness of NP- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  based device exhibited less value of  $ Jsc $  as compared with NR- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , which was attributed to the densely packed of  $ 20\mathrm{nm} $   $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  particles inhibiting the loading of perovskite. The reduction in performance might be attributed to the poorer infiltration of perovskite in the densely packed  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NR, as seen from SEM images. The perovskite capping layer on top of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR promoted charge carrier recombination resulted in reduced  $ V_{OC} $  and  $ FF $  which affects the device performance. The photovoltaic device performance strongly depends on the length and porosity of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR. The notable increase in the efficiency of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR based cells is mainly because of the enhanced  $ J_{SC} $  and the  $ FF $  [26]. The increase in the  $ FF $  and  $ J_{SC} $  for the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR based device is observed because of the uniform perovskite sensitization, which infiltrated deep into the ETL, leading to a decrease in the series resistance. The high crystallinity of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR provides a low resistance to the flow of electrons in the ETL. However, resistance is directly proportional to the length of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR, with optimization in this porous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR, improvement in the electrical parameter and high fill factor was achieved. On the other hand, loading of perovskite is high with improved porosity in the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -NR based ETL, which is another reason for the high efficiency. The diffusion length of electrons in the  $ \mathrm{CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $  perovskite is about  $ 200\mathrm{nm} $ , so the thickness of the ETL should also be equivalent; to prevent hindering of the electron transport from the  $ \mathrm{CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $  to the FTO [4,15]. # 4. Conclusion

We have fabricated ambient processed PSC by utilizing a porous   $  TiO_{2}-NR  $   as ETL, which clearly showed an enhanced photovoltaic performance due to the improvement of higher photocurrent (Jsc), compared to conventional   $  NP-TiO_{2}  $   as ETL. Hydrothermal method was successfully applied to grow 1-D NR with different length and porosity by varying the growth time and titania precursor concentration. The devices fabricated with 350 nm   $  TiO_{2}-NR  $   showed the best PCE of 14.0%, due to the large pores which helps uniform sensitization of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   and reduced recombination of charge carriers due to the 1-D nanostructures. EIS characterization further supports the enhanced photovoltaic behavior of the nanorod ETLs which clearly dictates a better lifetime and lower series resistance for devices employing nanorods as ETL. We have also observed that the device prepared on 1-D structure showed better durability because of its high crystallinity as compared to mesoporous scaffold electrodes. Shorter length, large pores and well-aligned   $  TiO_{2}  $   NR films could be a potential ETL electrode to improve the performance and stability of PSCs.
