# Enhanced photoelectric performance of inverted   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite solar cells with zwitterion modified ZnO cathode interlayer

Lijun Chen  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Zhipeng Yin  $  ^{a}  $  , Sikai Mei  $  ^{a}  $  , Xunwen Xiao  $  ^{b,**}  $  , Hai-Qiao Wang  $  ^{a,*}  $

# HIGHLIGHTS

- Zwitterionic molecules TPPPS are adopted to modify  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $  cathode interlayer in inverted  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs for the first time.

- TPPPS molecules passivate ZnO surface and  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br / ZnO} $  interface, promoting electron extraction from perovskite to cathode.

- Ideal energy level alignment and lowered charge recombination at cathode contact deliver  $  \mathrm{V_{OC}}  $  of 1.25 V and PCE of 14.62%.

- Superior shelf-stability in ambient air and light stability under illumination are demonstrated for optimal devices.

All-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are attracting more attention due to potential superior stability than organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs. Electrode Interface plays an important role in achieving efficient performance and high stability of the devices. Here, zwitterionic molecules 3-triphenylphosphaniumylpropane-1-sulfonate are adopted to modify ZnO cathode interlayer in inverted  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  PSCs. By which, defects on ZnO surface and at the interface of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br / ZnO} $  are passivated. More efficient electron extraction from perovskite to cathode is achieved due to enhanced charge transfer and suppressed charge recombination at the interface. Simultaneously, desirable energy level alignment at the cathode contact is formed. Consequently, superior optoelectronic device performance and stability are accomplished. Hugely improved champion efficiency of  $ 14.62\% $  is achieved with  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $  up to  $ 1.25\mathrm{V} $  and FF of  $ 76.83\% $ , compared with the control device (PCE of  $ 12.03\% $ ,  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $  of  $ 1.168\mathrm{V} $  and FF of  $ 66.58\% $ ). The optimized device remains  $ \sim 80\% $  of its initial efficiency for 32 days under illumination and for 18 days stored in ambient air.

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are considered promising photovoltaic technology of next generation for their low cost and high efficiency. But they are still confronted with the big challenge of long-term stability under light or thermal stresses [1]. All-inorganic Cs-based PSCs have attracted tremendous attention recently due to potential superior device stability [2,3]. Among those all-inorganic perovskites  $ (\mathrm{CsPbX}_3,\mathrm{X} = \mathrm{I} $  and/or Br),  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  as light absorber possesses balanced absorption and phase stability for achieving efficient and stable PSC [4]. According to Shockley-Queisser analysis, the theoretical maximum open-circuit voltage  $ (\mathrm{V_{OC}}) $  is as high as 1.615

V, and short-circuit current density  $ (\mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{SC}}) $  is about  $ 17\mathrm{mA} / \mathrm{cm}^2 $  [5]. In the past few years, a series of strategies have been adopted to improve the photovoltaic performance and stability. But the state-of-the-art efficiency of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  is still low compared to other efficient PSCs [6], implying plenty room of improvement for these solar cells.

Several research groups have successively reported the power conversion efficiency (PCE) beyond 16% for normal structure (n-i-p)   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs [7–12]. However, inverted (p-i-n)   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with high efficiency are rarely reported. Although they possess advantages in suppressing hysteresis, device fabrication and multijunction device application [13]. As far as we can find in literature, efficient inverted   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC (FTO/NiOx/  $  CsPbI_{2}Br/ZnO@C60/Ag  $  ) was firstly reported

by Mai et al. [14], in which promising optoelectronic performance (with PCE of 13.74%) and thermal stability were demonstrated [14,15]. After that, Chen et al. [16] obtained a efficiency of 14.8% by using Li and Mg doped NiOx as hole-transporting layer (HTL) and Nb doped C–TiO₂ as electron-transporting layer (ETL). It should be noticed that, they all need high-temperature sintering/annealing to prepare the HTL and/or ETL (400 °C and 250 °C for NiOx and C–TiO₂ respectively). Recently, we reported inverted CsPbI₂Br PSCs based on solution-processed P3CT HTL and fullerene ETL [17], in which high-temperature annealing of the interlayers is eliminated. It is beneficial for large-scale, flexible and printing manufacturing [18]. However, it is still confronted with large energy loss due to energy barrier between absorber and cathode by imperfect energy level of fullerenes [17], resulting in charge accumulation/recombination and energy loss [19].

Considering appropriate conduction and valence band of ZnO   $  [20,21]  $  , it would be suitable to serve as ETL in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs to extract electrons and simultaneously block holes at cathode contact. Apart from that, inorganic metal oxides possess advantages of superior stability, extraordinary carrier mobility, high transmittance and low fabrication cost   $  [1,22]  $  . However, the imperfect surface of metal oxides like defects and agglomeration of nanocrystals could result in depressed efficiency and stability of devices   $  [23,24]  $  . Surface modification by introducing chemicals containing oxygen, sulfur or metal ions to alleviate this issue metal oxides has been reported in organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs   $  [24–26]  $  . Passivation of perovskite/ZnO interface is demonstrated effective way to improve electrode contact and thus device performance   $  [27–30]  $  .

Herein, we report inverted all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs, which utilize zwitterion TPPPS modified ZnO as cathode interlayer and P3CT as anode interlayer. Modified by TPPPS, the surface defects of ZnO are passivated and the charge recombination at contact interface is depressed. Simultaneously, more suitable energy band alignment for charge transport is formed. The optimized PSCs achieve a champion efficiency of 14.62% with the highest   $  V_{OC}  $   up to 1.25 V, whereas the control device exhibits a PCE of only 12.03%. Apart from that, superior shelf-storage, light and thermal stability is achieved for unencapsulated devices. It retains ~80% of its initial PCE under continuous illumination for 32 days, while the control device maintains 36.7% of its initial PCE under the same conditions.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

1,3-propane sultone and triphenyl phosphine were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Zinc acetate dihydrate  $ (\mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2\cdot 2H_2O}) $  potassium hydroxide (KOH), methanol and trifluoroethanol were manufactured from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co. Lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2) $ , Lead bromide  $ (\mathrm{PbBr}_2) $ , cesium iodide (CsI) and fullerene (C60) were ordered from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and Poly [3-(4-carboxylbutyl)thiophene] (P3CT) were purchased separately from Alfa Aesar and Rieke, America. All the above chemical reagents were used without any further purification.

Synthesis of TPPPS: The zwitterion 3-triphenylphosphoniumylpropane-1-sulfonate (TPPPS) was synthesized according to the literature method [31]. Equimolar ratios of 1,3-propane sultone and triphenyl phosphine were stirred and refluxed in toluene for  $ 24\mathrm{h} $ , then the white precipitate was collected by separating, filtering and drying, the desired zwitterion without further purification. The product was assessed by NMR spectroscopy.

Synthesis of ZnO:TPPPS NPs: The ZnO nanoparticles were synthesized according to our published literatures [26]. In details, the methanol solution (100 ml) in round-bottom flask was first preheated to 60 °C, and   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}\cdot2H_{2}O}  $   (2.40 g) was added into it until dissolved. Then, the KOH solution (1.18g KOH in 52 ml methanol) was dropped into the round-bottom flask at a constant rate, transparent ZnO solution

was obtained after stirring and reacting for 2.5 h at 60 °C. At last, the NPs were centrifuged and cleaned three times, and they were dispersed in trifluoroethanol to obtain a transparent solution for further used. The zwitterionic modified ZnO NPs (ZnO:TPPPS) were acquired by adding certain amount of TPPPS (5%) into the ZnO NPs.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

The patterned ITO glass were sequentially cleaned with deionized water, detergent, acetone and isopropanol and then dried under the   $  N_{2}  $   flow. The substrates were treated by oxygen plasma 10 min for further cleaning before spreading the P3CT solution. The P3CT HTL film and   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film were fabricated by our previous approach [17]. The prepared ZnO or ZnO:TPPPS solution with 6 mg/ml was spin-coated on the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film at 2000 rpm for 30 s, and annealed at 100 °C for 5 min. Finally, 20 nm C60 and 100 nm Ag were respectively deposited by the thermal evaporation under high vacuum. The effective working area of device was 9 mm  $  ^{2}  $  .

# 2.3. Measurements and characterization

The J-V characteristics were recorded with a Keithley 2400 source meter under AM 1.5G spectrum, and they were measured in a glove box under forward scan from -0.2 V to 1.3 V with dwell time of 50 ms (reverse scan from 1.3 V to -0.2 V).   $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR spectra were measured at 293 K from a Bruker 400 MHz with tetramethylsilane (TMS) as reference. EQE was conducted by Newport EQE from 300 nm to 800 nm in ambient atmosphere. XPS and UPS were analyzed by Kratos AXIS ULTRA DALD system, the XPS value was calibrated according the standard value of C 1s peak at 284.8 eV, UPS was carried out by using He I (21.2 eV) radiation. The steady state PL spectra and time-resolved PL were recorded respectively with a fluorescence spectrophotometer (F-4600, Japan) and PL spectrometer (FLS980, Britain). EIS was measured by using Solartron 1260A impedance analyzer under dark condition from 1.0 Hz to 1.0 MHz. XRD was investigated by a X-ray diffractometer (Bruker AXS D8 Advance, Germany) with Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda = 0.154  $   nm). FTIR spectroscopy was conducted by IR spectrometer instrument (Thermo, Nicolet 6700). UV–vis spectra was analyzed on the GS54T spectrophotometer (Shanghai Lengguang Technology Co, China). The TPC and TPV decays were recorded by electrochemical workstation (Zahner, Germany). The top-view and cross-sectional SEM images were recorded by using field emission scanning electron microscope (Hitachi, S-4800, Japan). EDX was performed by FEI-SEM (FEI Quanta FEG250, USA) to study the elements distribution. The confocal PL mappings were investigated by confocal microRaman spectrometer (Renishaw inVia Reflex).

Stability Measurements: The light stability of devices was conducted under illumination of white light LED (spectra region: 430–890 nm, Suzhou D&R instruments Co., Ltd.) in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere, and the efficiency was recorded with a Keithley 2400 source meter under AM 1.5G spectrum every two days. The shelf-storage air stability was tested by keeping the device in air with 25% humidity, the efficiency was recorded every two days.

Considering the suitable  $  E_{CBM}  $  and deep  $  E_{VBM}  $  levels of ZnO and ZnO:TPPPS, both of them can serve as ETL with good electron-transporting and hole-blocking ability, facilitating electron extraction and reducing charge recombination, thus enhancing  $  V_{OC}  $  [35].

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was conducted to study the surface electronic states of ZnO with or without TPPPS. Fig. 3a presents the wide-scan XPS spectra. A shift in the Zn 2p binding energy suggests that chemical interaction or electron transfer occurs by TPPPS modification (Fig. 3b) [36,37]. Fig. 3c shows the O 1s core level spectra of ZnO and ZnO:TPPPS. The peaks are assigned to Zn-O-Zn (530.1 eV) and Zn-OH (531.9 eV) respectively. The reduced peak intensity of Zn-OH (531.9 eV) suggests replacing of hydrogen bond by coordination of TPPPS on ZnO and implies defect passivation [24]. The existence of TPPPS in the film is further confirmed by the S (2p) and P (2p) core levels observed in XPS (Fig. 3d and e), and verified by the vibrations peaks of P-C, S=O and C-C bound in fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra (Fig. 3f). X-ray diffraction (XRD) spectra certify high quality crystallization of the interfacial layer (Fig. S5) [26]. High transmittance (>85%) in the whole spectrum range offers promising ability as interfacial layer in devices. In addition, the conductivity of ZnO and ZnO:TPPPS film was analyzed by J-V curves of the device with structure of ITO/ETLs/Ag. Slightly enhanced conductivity was confirmed for ZnO after modification. Furthermore, the influence on the charge transport property was also investigated by space-charge-limited-current (SCLC) analysis with electron-only devices. Higher electron mobility (4.90*10 $  ^{-4}  $  cm $  ^{2}  $  v $  ^{-1}  $  s $  ^{-1}  $ ) was determined for ZnO:TPPPS compared to ZnO (3.69*10 $  ^{-4}  $  cm $  ^{2}  $  v $  ^{-1}  $  s $  ^{-1}  $ ), as shown in Figure S5, probably due to interstitial effect and reasonable conductivity of TPPPS molecule. The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) of ZnO with/without TPPPS are conducted. Lowered defect PL intensity (Fig. S6) at 550 nm was recorded after TPPPS modification, indicating the passivation of defects on ZnO.

The fast decay lifetime  $ \tau 1 $  and slow decay lifetime  $ \tau 2 $  stand for the surface recombination and bulk recombination, respectively. To further study the recombination mechanisms of device, the ideality factor (n) of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite solar cells is estimated by fitting   $  V_{OC}  $   as a function of light intensity,   $  V_{OC} = nkT/qln(I)  $   [39]. As shown in Fig. 4c, the lowered n value (from 1.74 to 1.53) suggests reduced trap-assisted recombination, which is consistent with the PL measurements. Dark current density-voltage (J-V) curves were measured to investigate the diode behavior of the control and modified   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs (Fig. 4d). Lower dark current density with higher rectification ratio was obtained for ZnO:TPPPS device, suggesting facilitated charge dissociation instead of charge recombination due to defects in device [40], and explaining the enhanced   $  V_{OC}  $   and FF of the optimal device. This is verified again by the transient photovoltage (TPV) measurement (Fig. 4e) [41], with doubled TPV lifetime (2.52   $  \mu  $  s) of ZnO:TPPPS PSC compared with control device (1.19   $  \mu  $  s). Further, lower series resistance (  $  R_{s}  $  ) and much higher recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) were determined for ZnO:TPPPS device by impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurement under dark condition (Fig. 4f) [42].

# 4. Conclusions

We demonstrated efficient and stable inverted all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with zwitterionic TPPPS modified ZnO cathode interlayer. The TPPPS passivated the defects on ZnO surface and at the perovskite/ZnO interface, provided desirable energy level alignment at cathode contact for device, thus suppressed charge recombination in device and improved parameters of   $  V_{OC}  $   and FF. A champion efficiency of 14.62% is obtained with hugely enhanced   $  V_{OC}  $   up to 1.25 V and FF of 76.83%, compared with the control device (PCE of 12%,   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.168 V and FF of 66.58%). Moreover, superior device stability was also demonstrated for TPPPS modified   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. It maintained ~80% of initial efficiency for over one month under light illumination, and 18 days stored in air. This work provides effective way to passivate metal oxides in perovskite-based optoelectronic devices and promote their photoelectric performance and stability.
