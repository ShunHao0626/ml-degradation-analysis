# 1. Introduction

Organic/inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have gained significant attention as a promising photovoltaic technology owing to potential low-cost manufacturability, excellent optoelectronic properties such as high absorption coefficient, long charge carrier diffusion length, defect tolerance and high power conversion efficiency (PCE)   $  [1-5]  $  . PSCs have shown a rapid increase in the PCE with present certified PCE of 25.5%, already surpassing the multi-crystalline silicon and thin film-based photovoltaic technologies   $  [6]  $  .

Two architectures of PSC devices have been pursued namely normal (n-i-p) and inverted (p-i-n) structures [7]. Although the normal n-i-p structure has demonstrated higher PCE, requirements of high-temperature sintering of electron transport layer (ETL)  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  inhibits the fabrication of flexible devices for roll-to-roll

# ABSTRACT

[6, 6]-phenyl  $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $  butyric acid methyl ester  $ (\mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM}) $  is a common fullerene derivative widely employed as an electron transport layer in inverted perovskite solar cells. However, major challenges such as interfacial recombination, current leakage and low mobility results in poor photovoltaic performance and stability for  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $ -based devices. To overcome these issues, we demonstrated, for the first time, doping of the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  with fullerene  $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  to tailor the properties of the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $ . This doping approach helped to realize superior power conversion efficiency of perovskite solar cell with  $ 17.46\% $  versus pristine device with  $ 14.20\% $ . The  $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  doped  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  reinforced the surface morphology of the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  layer with reduced roughness and better coverage, up-shift of Fermi Level, higher electrical conductivity, higher maximum charge carrier generation rate, and higher charge collection probability. All these improvements contributed to reduced charge leakage at the interface, reduced charge recombination and promoted efficient charge transfer, transport, and collection. Furthermore, the enhanced hydrophobicity of the  $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  doped  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  enabled superior ambient device stability compared to the pristine device. This work facilitates a simple and effective route to tailor perovskite solar cell performance towards higher efficiency and better stability.

The inverted p-i-n structure provides a low-temperature fabrication alternative that is ideal for roll-to-roll manufacturing of flexible PSCs while maintaining high PCEs   $  [1,7]  $  .   $  [6, 6]  $  -phenyl   $  C_{61}  $   butyric acid methyl ester (  $  PC_{61}BM  $  ) is the most common ETL in inverted p-i-n structure PSCs due to its strong electron transport properties and relatively high electron affinity   $  [1,2,11–14]  $  . However, it has several limitations such as poor electron mobility, difficulty in forming high-quality films, current leakage, and extreme nonradiative recombination at the perovskite/  $  PC_{61}BM  $   interface due to small relative permittivity of   $  PC_{61}BM  $   that causes a large electron capture region. These limitations compromise the PCE of the inverted PSCs   $  [1,2]  $  .

$  PC_{61}BM  $  ETL include carbon nanodots [15], carbon quantum dots [2], cobalt selenide (CoSe) [16], graphdiyne [17], reduced graphene oxide [18], dual doping graphdiyne [19], oleamide [20], polystyrene [21], conjugated n-type polymer such as poly[(9,9-dioctyluorene)-2,7-diyl-alt-(4,7-bis(3-hexylthien-5-yl)-2,1,3-benzothiadiazole)-2',2''-diyl] (F8TBT) [1], bathocuproine (BCP) [22], poly(9,9-dioctylfluorene) (PFO) and poly(9,9-dioctylfluorene-cobenzo-thiadiazole)(F8BT) [23], n-type dopant 1,3-dimethyl-2-phenyl-2,3-dihydro-1H-benzomidazole(DMBI) derivatives [24], a small molecule 3,9-bis(2-methylene-(3-(1,1-dicyanomethylene)-indanone)-5,5,11,11-tetrakis (4-hexylphenyl)-dithieno[2,3-d:2',3'-d']-sindaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b']-dithiophene (ITIC) [25] PCBM and its dumb-belled dimer (d-PCBM) [26],  $  C_{60}  $ -diphenylmethanofullerene-oligoether ( $  C_{60}  $ -DPM-OE) molecules [27]. Another approach that has been implemented is the use of multiple layers such as fullerene  $  C_{60}  $  as an additional layer to form a  $  PC_{61}BM/C_{60}  $  bilayer [28], or lithium fluoride (LiF) and  $  C_{60}  $  as two additional layers by thermal evaporator to form a triple cathode buffer layer  $  PC_{61}BM/C_{60}/LiF  $  [29], or  $  C_{60}/BCP  $  to replace  $  PC_{61}BM  $  [30,31].

In this work, we introduced a successful facile strategy to enhance the performance of inverted PSCs in terms of PCE and stability by modifying  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  with fullerene  $ (C_{60}) $  as a direct doping material. It was observed that the doping of the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  ETL tailored the morphology of  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  layer with reduced roughness, enhanced electrical conductivity of ETL, and promoted up-shift of Fermi Level and increased electrical conductivity and maximum charge carrier generation rate. The incorporation of doped  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  ETL resulted in a reduced nonradiative recombination at the perovskite/  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  interface, facilitating efficient charge transfer, transport and collection in inverted PSCs. An optimal volume ratio  $ (\mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM}:\mathrm{C}_{60}) $  of 1:0.07 delivered an enhancement in PCE of the inverted PSC from  $ 14.20\% $  for pristine (without  $ C_{60} $ ) to  $ 17.46\% $  for doped  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $ . In addition, the doped  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $ -based PSCs exhibited a superior device stability in ambient air compared to pristine devices, which can be related to the increased hydrophobicity of the doped  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  layer. The results of this work further extend the scope of doping as a simple and efficient method to enhance PSC performance.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1.Materials

Methylammonium iodide (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I) was obtained from Great-Cell Solar, and PbI $ _2 $  (99.99%) was purchased from Alfa Aesar [6, 6]-phenyl C $ _{61} $ butyric acid methyl ester (PC $ _{61} $ BM) and Fullerene C $ _{60} $  were acquired from Nano-C. Chlorobenzene (99%), and toluene were obtained from Acros Organics, Anhydrous dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (99%),  $ \gamma $ -butyrolactone (99%), acetone, and Rhodamine 101 (Rh) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Anhydrous isopropyl alcohol (IPA) was acquired from Fisher Scientific. Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly (styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS, Clevios™ P VP AI 4083) was ordered from Heraeus. All materials were used as received without any modification.

# 2.2. Preparation pristine   $  PC_{61}BM  $   ETL and   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   ETL

The pristine PC $ _{61} $ BM ETL (20 mg/ml) in chlorobenzene was kept at 70 °C overnight with continuous stirring. 2 mg of C $ _{60} $  was dissolved in 1 ml of chlorobenzene with continuous stirring at 70 °C overnight. Then the various volume ratios of PC $ _{61} $ BM and C $ _{60} $  were mixed before spin coating to prepare the treated ETL of PC $ _{61} $ BM:C $ _{60} $  (1:0), (1:0.04), (1:0.05), (1:0.07), (1:0.1), and (1:0.2).

# 2.3. Device fabrication

Zn powders and 2 M HCl were used to pattern fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) coated glass substrates with an area of  $ 1.5 \times 1.5 \, \text{cm}^2 $ . After cleaning the etched FTO substrates with detergent, deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol for 30 min each, the FTO substrates were dried with a nitrogen ( $ \mathrm{N}_2 $ ) gun. UV– $ \mathrm{O}_3 $  was used to further clean the pre-cleaned and dry FTO substrates for 30 min. PEDOT:PSS was filtered through a  $ 0.45 \, \mu\text{m} $  syringe filter before being spin-coated for 60 s on pre-treated FTO substrates at a speed of 4500 rpm. The FTO/PEDOT:PSS films were annealed in an atmospheric environment at  $ 140^{\circ}\text{C} $  for 10 min after spin coating.

Then, the FTO/PEDOT:PSS substrates were directly moved to a   $  N_{2}  $   filled glove box after cooling down. The perovskite precursor solution was prepared by dissolving   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (209 mg) and   $  PbI_{2}  $   (581 mg) in a 1 ml (3:7 vol ratio) mixed solvent of DMSO and -butyrolactone and keeping it at 70 °C overnight with continuous stirring. The freshly prepared perovskite precursor was filtered and held at 70 °C before being spin-coated in two steps; at a speed of 750 rpm for 20 s and 4000 rpm for 60 s. After the 40s of spinning of the second step, the antisolvent toluene (160 μl) was dropped, followed by annealing at 80 °C for 10 min.

After that, the pristine   $  PC_{61}BM  $   or   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (with various volume ratios) ETL was spin-coated on the perovskite film for 40 s and a speed of 2000 rpm then annealed at 80 °C for 5 min. The thickness of the   $  PC_{61}BM  $   ETL and   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   ETL layers was   $  \approx40  $   nm. Then, the buffer film of Rhodamine 101 (0.5 mg/ml in anhydrous isopropyl alcohol) was spin-coated for 40 s at speed of 4000 rpm to act as an interfacial layer between the ETL and the top silver (Ag) electrode. Finally, a shadow mask with an active area of 0.12 cm  $  ^{2}  $   was used to evaporate an approximately 80 nm of Ag electrode under a high vacuum of 9 × 10  $  ^{-6}  $   torr.

# 2.4. Characterization

A Veeco Dektak 150 alpha step surface profiler and Agilent 5500 scanning probe microscopy were used to measure the film thickness. An Agilent 8453 UV–visible Spectroscopy System was used to measure the absorbance of the films. The VCA 2000 camera contact angle device was used to calculate the contact angle of the films. The atomic force microscopy (AFM) images were taken using an Agilent 5500 scanning probe microscope. For Kelvin Probe Force Microscopy (KPFM) measurements, a Budget Sensors Multi 75-EG tip with platinum/iridium conductive coating was used. In addition, conductive atomic force microscopy (C-AFM) measurements were used to determine the average surface current of FTO/PC  $  _{61}  $  BM and FTO/PC  $  _{61}  $  BM:C  $  _{60}  $  films.

The current density-voltage (J-V) characteristic curves of PSC devices in an atmospheric setting without encapsulation were measured using a Xenon arc lamp (AM 1.5 filter, Newport), which was calibrated using a standardized silicon solar cell from NREL (S1133-14). An Agilent 4155C semiconductor parameter analyzer was used to characterize the PSC devices at the same conditions; sweeping from 0 to  $ 1.2\mathrm{V} $  in the forward scan and 1.2 to  $ 0\mathrm{V} $  in the reverse scan with a scan rate of  $ 0.5\mathrm{Vs}^{-1} $ , a step of  $ 0.01\mathrm{V} $ , and a hold time of  $ 0.5\mathrm{s} $ . A Xenon lamp (Newport, Model 67005) was used to measure the external quantum efficiency (EQE) and corresponding integrated current density of PSC devices. The monochromator (Newport, Model 74125) produced the output light which was focused on the PSC devices.

The work functions of the different ETLs were calculated from the surface potential images measured by Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) to further elucidate the impact of  $ C_{60} $  doping to  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  ETL, as seen in Fig. 2 (a). Equation (1) was used to determine the work functions of the samples [33]:

$$
\Delta V _ {\mathrm{CPD}} = \frac {\varphi_ {\text {tip}} - \varphi_ {\text {sample}}}{- \mathbf {e}}
$$

(1)

where  $ \Delta V_{\mathrm{CPD}} $  is the contact potential difference between the tip and the sample,  $ \varphi_{\mathrm{tip}} $  and  $ \varphi_{\mathrm{sample}} $  are the work functions of the tip and the sample, and  $ e $  is the elementary charge.

It was observed that the   $  C_{60}  $   doping led to a decrease in the work function of   $  PC_{61}BM  $ , where the estimated work functions of   $  PC_{61}BM  $ ,   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (1:0.05), and   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (1:0.07) are 4.93 eV, 4.91 eV, and 4.81 eV, respectively. This has been depicted in Fig. 2 (b) which shows the change of the Fermi level with respect to the HOMO energy level of the films with and without   $  C_{60}  $   doping. The work function is known as the energy level difference between the   $  E_{F}  $   and the vacuum energy level; hence, an increase in the work function causes the   $  E_{F}  $   to shift away from the conduction band or the LUMO energy level. The Fermi level of   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (1:0.07) film is shifted toward the LUMO energy level compared to the pristine   $  PC_{61}BM  $   film. This up-shift in the Fermi level suggests an increase in the number of electrons of the device with   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (1:0.07), which leads to an increase in photocurrent of the solar cell device [34,35]. Furthermore, the up-shift in the Fermi level of an ETL can lead to higher open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of the solar cell. In addition, the potential energy loss at the perovskite/ETL interface can be greatly reduced by lowering the work function of the   $  PC_{61}BM  $   after adding   $  C_{60}  $ . This will also increase the built-in potential and electron extraction, leading to a higher   $  V_{oc}  $ , fill factor (FF), and short circuit current (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of the PSCs based on the   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   ETL [36–38].

However, when the volume ratio of   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   was further increased to (1:0.1), the work function increased to 4.93 eV. Increasing the work function indicated that the   $  E_{F}  $   had moved down from the LUMO energy level, as seen in Fig. 2 (b). This also explained why the work-function of (1:0.1) concentration is not closer to the LUMO of perovskite, but in fact further. The down-shift of   $  E_{F}  $   was expected to decrease the density of electrons on the conduction band or LUMO, which may lead to a lower   $  J_{sc}  $  . In addition, the   $  V_{oc}  $  , and FF were decreased due to the down-shift of   $  E_{F}  $   that caused a reduced electron transfer and extraction. This led to a poor efficiency in the PSC devices with   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (1:0.1) relative to the best PSC device with   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (1:0.07).

To further evaluate the origin of the enhanced   $  J_{sc}  $   of the fabricated PSC device after doping   $  PC_{61}BM  $   with   $  C_{60}  $ , the average surface currents of pristine   $  PC_{61}BM  $   film and   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (1:0.07) film deposited on FTO/glass substrate were measured by conductive atomic force microscopy (C-AFM) measurements as shown in Fig. 2(c). It was found that the average currents of pristine   $  PC_{61}BM  $   film and   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (1:0.07) film are 200 pA and 600 pA, respectively. This result suggests enhancement in the electrical conductivity of   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (1:0.07) film compared to the pristine   $  PC_{61}BM  $ . Improving the electrical conductivity of the ETL promotes a faster photogenerated carrier separation that leads to enhance the   $  J_{sc}  $   of the solar cell devices [2,17].

To examine the effect of adding  $  C_{60}  $  to the  $  PC_{61}BM  $  ETL on the performance of the PSC devices, current density-voltage (J-V) characteristic curves were measured under AM 1.5G irradiation

(100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) as displayed in Fig. 3. The (J-V) characteristic curves of the PSC devices with various volume ratios of PC  $  _{61}  $  BM:C  $  _{60}  $   (1:0), (1:0.04), (1:0.05), (1:0.07), (1:0.1), and (1:0.2) are shown in Fig. 3(a). Table 1 summarizes the corresponding PSC parameters.

It was found that the PCEs of the PSC devices based on   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   ETL (1:0.04), (1:0.05), and (1:0.07) were increased compared to the pristine cells. However, by further increasing the ratios of   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   ETL to (1:0.1) and (1:0.2), the PCEs of the PSC devices were decreased. The changes of PCEs in the PSC devices with various volume ratios of   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   are shown in Fig. S3. This showed that PCEs increase with a higher amount of   $  C_{60}  $ . The maximum PCE was achieved at a   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   volume ratio of 1:0.07, which delivered an optimal PCE of 17.46% at a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 0.97 V,   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.97 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $ , and FF of 0.78. In contrast, the control PSC devices with   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (1:0) ETL exhibited a PCE of 14.20% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 0.95 V,   $  J_{sc}  $   of 19.54 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $ , and FF of 0.77. These results showed a PCE improvement of 23% with the   $  C_{60}  $   doping of the   $  PC_{61}BM  $ .

To evaluate hysteresis in these devices, the  $ J - V $  characteristic curves of the PSC devices with pristine  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $ , and with the optimal ratio of  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM}:C_{60} $  (1:0.07) were measured under AM 1.5G irradiation  $ (100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2}) $  in both forward and reverse scan directions as shown in Fig. 3 (b) and the corresponding PSC parameters are tabulated in Table S1. The control device with the pristine  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  delivered PCE of  $ 13.22\% $  and  $ 14.20\% $  under forward and reverse scans, respectively, which resulted in hysteresis of  $ 6.90\% $ . In contrast, the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM}:C_{60} $  (1:0.07)-based device yielded lower hysteresis of  $ 3.89\% $  resulting from PCEs of  $ 16.78\% $  and  $ 17.46\% $  under forward and reverse scans, respectively. We suggest the reason is the improved surface morphology of the Perovskite/  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM}:C_{60} $  film compared to the Perovskite/  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $  film supported by the AFM results.

In order to support the enhancement in   $  J_{sc}  $   observed in the J-V measurements, external quantum efficiency (EQE) curves and the integrated   $  J_{sc}  $   of the PSC devices with pristine   $  PC_{61}BM  $   ETL and with   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (1:0.07) ETL were measured as shown in Fig. 3 (c). It can be seen that the EQE curves for the two devices showed a broad band in the region of 350–850 nm. The integrated   $  J_{sc}  $   values of PSC devices with pristine   $  PC_{61}BM  $   and   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (1:0.07) are 19.70 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and 21.96 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , respectively, which is very close to the   $  J_{sc}  $   values from J-V results. The higher   $  J_{sc}  $   values obtained from the EQE measurements for the   $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (1:0.07) compared to pristine   $  PC_{61}BM  $   further validates the performance enhancement induced by the   $  C_{60}  $   doping. The UV–Vis absorption spectra of the perovskite/  $  PC_{61}BM  $   and Perovskite/  $  PC_{61}BM:C_{60}  $   (different volume ratios) deposited on FTO/glass substrate in the band of 500–800 nm are shown in Fig. 3 (d). It is seen that the absorbance slightly increased after doping   $  PC_{61}BM  $   with   $  C_{60}  $  . This result suggests that the absorption is not the main origin of the   $  J_{sc}  $   improvement even though is slightly higher.

The statistical data of photovoltaic parameters of 20 independently fabricated PSCs based on pristine PC $ _{61} $ BM and PC $ _{61} $ BM:C $ _{60} $  (1:0.07) are displayed in box charts as presented in Fig. 4 and the values are tabulated in Table S2. It can be observed that all the photovoltaic parameters of the PC $ _{61} $ BM:C $ _{60} $  ETL-based devices are superior to that of the PSCs based on pristine PC $ _{61} $ BM. # 4. Conclusion

In summary, we demonstrated doping of   $  C_{60}  $   into the   $  PC_{61}BM  $   electron transport layer for the first time as a successful strategy to enhance power conversion efficiency and stability of inverted perovskite solar cells. The   $  C_{60}  $   doping primarily promotes (1) reinforcement of the surface morphology of the   $  PC_{61}BM  $   layer with reduced roughness and better coverage, promoting reduced charge leakage and decrease charge carriers recombination at the interface with perovskite and the cathode, (2) up-shift of Fermi Level,

promoting efficient charge transfer and collection at the interface and (3) higher electrical conductivity of the electron transport layer, facilitating efficient charge transport, and (4) higher maximum charge carrier generation rate  $ (G_{\mathrm{max}}) $ , and higher charge collection probability. As a result, a superior perovskite solar cell performance was achieved, where an optimized volume ratio of  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM:C}_{60} $  demonstrated power conversion efficiency of  $ 17.46\% $  versus the pristine  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM} $ -based device with  $ 14.20\% $ . Furthermore, the unencapsulated  $ C_{60} $ -doped solar cell exhibited superior ambient stability compared to the pristine device, owing to the
