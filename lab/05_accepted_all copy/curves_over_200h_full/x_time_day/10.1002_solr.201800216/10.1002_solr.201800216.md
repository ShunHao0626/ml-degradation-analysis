# Synergy of Hydrophobic Surface Capping and Lattice Contraction for Stable and High-Efficiency Inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   Perovskite Solar Cells

Haoran Wang, Hui Bian, Zhiwen Jin,  $  ^{*}  $   Lei Liang, Dongliang Bai, Qian Wang,  $  ^{*}  $   and Shengzhong F. Liu  $  ^{*}  $

$ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  has been recognized as a promising material for photovoltaic applications due to its excellent optoelectronic properties and compositional stability. Unfortunately, its desired perovskite phase is not stable in humid environments as it is spontaneously transformed into a yellow non-perovskite phase. Herein, we present our strategy to use phenylethylammonium chlorine (PEACl) treatment to significantly improve the moisture-resistance of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film without compromising its high solar cell efficiency. It is found that: 1) small-sized hydrophobic aromatic group  $ \mathrm{PEA}^+ $  forms in the edge-on orientation on the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  surface and 2) smaller halide  $ \mathrm{Cl^-} $  is doped into the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  lattice during post-annealing, leading to a smaller lattice structure with beneficial crystallization quality. Compared with the reference sample without the PEACl treatment, the present device achieves a comparable powerconversion efficiency of  $ 14.05\% $  and much improved moisture resistance.

In the present work, a surface treatment using phenylethylammonium chlorine (PEACl) is demonstrated for the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   absorber film. It was found that the PEA  $  ^{+}  $  adsorbed onto the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film surface forming a hydrophobic capping layer. Meanwhile, Cl  $  ^{-}  $  ions doped into the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   lattice lead to lattice shrinkage and improved crystallinity. Finally, the PSCs show highly reproducible PCEs up to 14.05% with greatly enhanced moisture resistance.

The crystal-structure changes of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   due to different PEAX (X=Cl, Br, and I) treatments are illustrated in Figure 1a. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Figure 2) images were obtained to study the morphology resulting from post-treatment with PEAX. Apparently, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film shows a uniform and compact surface (Figure 2a). We observed no changes on the surfaces of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films for treatments using different amounts of PEA1 (Figure 2b–d). When a larger amount of PEAX was used, the water contact angle increased dramatically, from 63.5° to 101.5°. Such a phenomenon indicates that the   $  PEA^{+}  $   (with its hydrophobic terminal groups) was modified on the surface of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film. Simultaneously, we compared SEM images of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films treated with PEABr (Figure 2e) and PEACl (Figure 2f), and both films exhibited an enlarged water contact angle. The larger water contact angle means better hydrophobicity and excellent moisture-resistance for the device. Interestingly, it seems that the crystallization of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film begins to improve when PEACl is used. It is proved that the   $  Cs^{+}  $   in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   crystal lattice is highly stable and would not cation exchange with organic cations to form a 2D perovskite layer as hybrid ones. For the purpose of clearly identifying the effects of PEAX treatment on the films, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were carried out. Figure 3a shows the XPS spectra for each element at the surface. After PEACl treatment, Cl was clearly observed. To confirm whether the post-treatment with PEAX is useful to the crystal structure, we conducted X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements and photoelectric performance characterizations. As shown in Figure 3b, in agreement with there being no overall structural change, the halide ions doped into the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film formed a cubic perovskite structure.  $  ^{[54]}  $   Meanwhile, the series of XRD patterns indicates that there is no obvious change in the XRD peak intensities for different PEAX treatments, except for PEACl. This phenomenon proved that the   $  Cl^{-}  $  doping could enhance the crystallization of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film. The XRD peaks at   $  29.6^{\circ}  $  , shown in the magnification portion, were assigned to the (200) planes of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   (Figure 3c).  $  ^{[55]}  $   In the case of substitution, the XRD peak shifted to higher   $  2\theta  $  , indicating the incorporation of smaller halide ions into the lattice sites of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  . For the PEAI treatment, a monotonic shift of the XRD peaks to lower angles with increasing PEAI content indicates an expansion of the crystalline lattice, while for PEABr and PEACl, the XRD peaks shifted to higher angles, resulting in shrinkage of the crystalline lattice and enhanced stability.  $  ^{[56]}  $

Optical absorption measurements (Figures 3d and e) were also carried out on the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  films with different PEAX processing; After PEAI treatment, the positions of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film absorption edges exhibited a slight red-shift caused by the induced I-enriched  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_{2 + x}\mathrm{Br}_{1 - x} $  layer. For the PEABr or PEACl treatments, the positions of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film absorption edges exhibited a slight blue-shift caused by the induced Br- or  $ \mathrm{Cl^-} $  enriched  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_{2 - x}\mathrm{Br}_{1 + x} $  or  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_{2 - x}\mathrm{Br}_{1 - y}\mathrm{Cl}_{x + y} $  layer.[57] This phenomenon is in good agreement with the above EQE results. Meanwhile, the characteristic multiple absorption peaks of the 2D films were not found in the absorption spectra which in good consisted with the cross-section SEM results. To characterize the interface and band alignment around the graded heterojunction, valence band (VB) XPS spectra (Figure 3f and g) were estimated. The VB energy can be derived from the intersection of the linear portion of the spectra near the Fermi edge,[58] revealing the shift of the surface energy levels after PEAI treatment.[59,60] Meanwhile, the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film obtained after PEABr or PEACl treatment was nearly unchanged, proving that the surface shifted or that the unchanged VB of the active layer above the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film/PTAA interface could not hinder the whole collection.

Images of the above fabricated   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films at different durations of exposure to air with different values of relative humidity (RH%) are shown in Figure 4a. All of the films were nearly unchanged when stored at lower RH% (25–35%) for 1 month, and no decay of their PCE was detected. After exposing the films to higher RH% (40–50%), the pristine and PEAI-0.5 films quickly turned yellow, and the PEAI-1 and PEAI-1.5 films slowly turned yellow owing to their thicker   $  PEA^{+}  $  layers. As we know, more groups of   $  PEA^{+}  $  on the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   surface will lead larger water contact angle, better hydrophobicity and excellent moisture-resistance for the

device. However, in the   $  CsPb(Br_{x}I_{1-x})_{3}  $   systems, the compounds with 0.2 < x < 0.4 are most attractive for solar cells. When x is <0.2, the insulating   $  \delta  $   phase is thermodynamically preferred at room temperature.  $  ^{[23]}  $   Although the PEA-1.5 sample have best hydrophobic surface, its surface with much I-enriched   $  CsPbI_{2+x}Br_{1-x}  $   layer and could easily transfer into insulating   $  \delta  $   phase at room temperature, especially in humidity environment.

In summary, the moisture resistance of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC is designed through an effective PEACl interface treatment between the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film and the HTL. Compared with the reference device, the optimized device achieved an undiminished PCE of 14.05% and dramatically improved stability in humid conditions. Further study found that the PEACl plays the beneficial roles of: 1) assisting the formation of a hydrophobic layer on the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   surface and 2) controlling the crystallization of the film and lattice shrinkage induced by the   $  Cl^{-}  $  doping effect. We believe our findings provide an effective strategy to address the main barrier to the practical application of inorganic perovskite devices, which may also be applicable to other inorganic perovskite-based optoelectronic devices beyond photovoltaics.

# Experimental Section

Precursor Solution Preparation: CsPbI $ _2 $ Br precursor solution was prepared by dissolving CsI:PbBr $ _2 $ :PbI $ _2 $  (1:0.5:0.5) in DMF/DMSO (v/v of 19:1) with a concentration of 0.5 M, and stirring at 50°C until completely dissolved. For the PEAX solution, different amounts of dry PEAX salts were added directly to isopropanol, and the heterogeneous mixture was stirred at room temperature for 20 min. HTL solution was prepared by dissolving PTAA (32 mg), a sulfonyl imide (Li-TFSI, 22 μL) solution (520 mg Li-TFSI in 1 mL acetonitrile), and tert-butylpyridine (TBP, 36 μL) in 1 mL of chlorobenzene solution. All of the solutions used were filtered through a 0.4-μm-pore PTFE filter and stored in a dry nitrogen atmosphere.

Preparation of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -blocking layer: A  $ 25 \times 25 \mathrm{~mm}^2 $  piece of FTO-coated glass was washed sequentially with detergent, deionized water, acetone,

and isopropanol with ultrasonication for 10 min each. The coated glass was then dried with   $  N_{2}  $   and treated in   $  O_{2}  $   plasma. The clean substrate was immersed in a 40 mM   $  TiCl_{4}  $   aqueous solution for 30 min at 70°C and washed with distilled water and ethanol, followed by annealing at 200°C for 30 min in air to form a compact n-type blocking layer of   $  TiO_{2}  $  .

Growth of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   Film:   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution and substrate were both heated to 100°C before the solution was spin-coated onto the substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s, followed by sintering of the films at 300°C for 10 min. Then, the prepared PEAX solution was dropped onto the prepared film and spin-dried at 2000 rpm for 30 s in turn, followed by film annealing at 150°C for 10 min.

Assembly of Solar Cells: An HTL film was prepared by spin-coating the HTL solution onto the above film at 4000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, a 70-nm-thick Au electrode was thermally evaporated to complete the device fabrication. Bare devices without any encapsulation were stored with exposure to the ambient environment (in air at different RH% values at 25 °C).

Film Characterization: The film-surface morphology was characterized by FESEM (Jeol SU-8020). XRD patterns of the samples were obtained using a Bruker D8 GADDS Diffractometer. The XPS measurements were performed in a VG ESCALAB MK2 system with monochromatized Al Kα radiation. Absorption spectra were collected using a Shimadzu UV-3600 double beam spectrometer. Contact angles were measured on an OCA20 instrument (Data Physics).

Device Measurement: J-V curves were measured at 25°C under AM1.5G (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) illumination. A black cardboard mask with a window area of 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $   was clipped onto the glass side to define the active area of the cell. The reverse-scan mode (from   $  V_{OC}  $   to   $  I_{SC}  $  ) and forward-scan mode (from   $  I_{SC}  $   to   $  V_{OC}  $  ) were used at a scan rate of 30 mV s  $  ^{-1}  $  . The spectral response was taken by an EQE measurement system (QEX10, PV Measurement) equipped with a monochromator, a lock-in amplifier, a Xe lamp, and a current-voltage amplifier. Prior to irradiation, the spectral response and light intensity were calibrated using a mono-silicon detector.
