# Introduction

In the past few years, hybrid organic-inorganic lead halide perovskites have received tremendous attention due to their superb optoelectronic properties like strong light absorption, $ ^{1} $  large charge carrier mobility $ ^{2} $  and ultra-long diffusion length of carriers. $ ^{2,3} $  More impressive is the rapid boosting of the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs), with a certified record of  $ 22.7\%^{4} $  obtained so far. In spite of high PCEs, the long-term stability of the widely used three dimensional (3D) perovskites, especially in the presence of moisture, remains a bottleneck problem hindering the large-scale commercialization of PSCs. Recent years, the emerging two-dimensional (2D) layered hybrid perovskites provide an effective strategy to mitigate the stability issue. This new category of layered perovskites has demonstrated better stability than the 3D ones due to their bulkier organic components that are more hydrophobic

In this structure, the bottom 3D perovskite layer ensures efficient light absorption and charge generation while the thin 2D capping perovskite layer serves as a moisture barrier by limiting water diffusion and increasing surface hydrophobicity.  $  ^{8-12}  $   Apart from improving the stability as a moisture barrier, the 2D capping layer was demonstrated to have an impact on the photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{12,13}  $   The capping layer, formed by the reaction between   $  PbI_{2}  $   and organic long chain amine iodides (such as butylammonium iodide, i.e. BAI), was reported to play a role in passivation of the cationic and halide vacancies on the surface or grain boundaries of 3D perovskites, by which the non-radiative recombination could be suppressed efficiently.  $  ^{14}  $   Phenylethylammonium iodide (PEAI) was commonly used in fabricating 2D perovskite. As one example, it was claimed by Bai et al. that the 2D capping layer played a role in modifying the interface energy level and reducing interface charge recombination, by which an ultrahigh open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.17 V was yielded.  $  ^{15}  $   Chen et al. also illustrated a higher   $  V_{oc}  $   that was induced by the larger Fermi-level splitting in the 2D-3D perovskite film under light illumination.  $  ^{16}  $   Although the 2D perovskite as a capping layer can reduce the surface defects of 3D perovskite, such a thin layer itself may probably introduce additional interfacial defects in a 2D/3D stacking structure, which is thought to be able to trigger trap-assisted non-radiative recombination or ion migration. From the perspective of reducing interfacial defects, in this work we conduct a systematic investigation into the 2D/3D stacking perovskite structure, which is well characterized by means of scanning electronic microscopy (SEM), X-ray diffraction (XRD), Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectrum, steady-state photoluminescence and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). It is first found that PEAI-derived 2D perovskite as capping layer in a 2D/3D stacking structure has limited role in reducing interfacial defects, while there is only slight improvement in terms of the photoluminescence lifetime as well as the devices' photovoltaic performance when compared to pristine 3D perovskite. Then, we demonstrate that bromine (Br) doping of the 2D perovskite capping layer is an efficient strategy to passivate interfacial defects robustly, by which the photoluminescence lifetime is enhanced notably while the interfacial charge recombination is suppressed a lot. As a result, the PCE is enhanced from  $ 18.01\% $  (3D perovskite) to  $ 20.07\% $  (Br doped 2D/3D perovskite) along with improved stability.

The 2D perovskite capping layer is prepared by the reaction between PEAI (or PEAI and a small amount of PEABr, i.e. the Br doped sample) and the residual PbI2 of 3D perovskite. Hence, we prolonged the storing time of the 3D perovskite film in air (four days, humidity was about  $ 70\% $ ) so that adequate  $ \mathrm{PbI_2} $  could be generated through decomposition of the 3D perovskite underneath. # Conclusions

In summary, this work is focused on the 2D/3D stacking structure, where the 2D perovskite is formed by the reaction between PEAI (or Br doped PEAI) and the residual  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  of 3D perovskite. The existence of 2D perovskite capping layer is demonstrated by means of SEM, XRD, FTIR spectrum, steady-state PL and XPS. Moreover, through TRPL and EIS measurement, it is found that PEAI-derived 2D perovskite as capping layer in a 2D/3D stacking structure has limited role in reducing interfacial defects, which is unable to achieve notable improvement in terms of the
