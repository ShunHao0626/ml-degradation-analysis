# Efficient Passivation of Hybrid Perovskite Solar Cells Using Organic Dyes with —COOH Functional Group

Xing Li, Chun-Chao Chen, Molang Cai, Xin Hua, Fengxian Xie, Xiao Liu, Jianli Hua,  $  ^{*}  $   Yi-Tao Long, He Tian, and Liyuan Han  $  ^{*}  $

Organic–inorganic halide perovskites are efficient absorbers for solar cells. Nevertheless, the trap states at the surfaces and grain boundaries are a detrimental factor compromising the device performance. Here, an organic dye (AQ310) is employed as passivator to reduce the trap states of the perovskites and promote better stability. The results demonstrate that the trap states of perovskite are minimized by the presence of AQ310's –COOH group and the formation of coordination with under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions. The resulting carrier recombination time is prolonged and verified by the photoluminescence and open-circuit voltage decay measurements. Consequently, the best average power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 19.43% is achieved for the perovskite solar cell (PSC) with AQ310 passivation, as compared with a low average PCE of 17.98% for the PSC without AQ310 passivation.

Organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs), with a relatively simple and low-cost engineering, have recently become one of the most fast-growing research fields in the new generation solar cells.  $  ^{[1-4]}  $   The highest certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) has exceeded 22% in just a few years due to a series of advanced optimizations in terms of material,  $  ^{[5-8]}  $   interface  $  ^{[9-12]}  $   and fabrication technologies.  $  ^{[13-17]}  $   As one of the most important components, perovskite materials have many excellent photoelectric properties, such as low exciton binding energy, strong light-harvesting capacity, and high charge carrier mobility.  $  ^{[18-22]}  $   However, the perovskite materials based on the current solution processing methods are made of polycrystalline morphology, which can easily beget all kinds of trap states

These trap states at the surface and grain boundaries are most likely induced by ions migration, oxidization of   $  I^{-}  $  or evaporation of methylammonium iodide (MAI), which are mainly manifested as under-coordinated metal cations or halide anions.  $  ^{[27-29]}  $   So far, a variety of passivation materials (also known as passivator) have been added into perovskite films to

induce defect passivation through forming coordination with under-coordinated metal cations or halide anions. For instance, phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM), as a Lewis acid could passivate the trap states by forming coordination with halide ions and thus eliminate the notorious photocurrent hysteresis.[28,30] On the contrary, Snaith and co-workers demonstrated that Lewis base molecules, such as thiophene or pyridine, could heal the trap states by forming coordination with under-coordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  ions in perovskite films.[31] Since these initial results of defect passivation by reducing the uncoordinated ions in perovskite layer is proven to be effective, a defect passivator in perovskite layer should have more room for improvement. For example, a well-designed passivator in the perovskite layer should take the defect coordination and device air stability into consideration simultaneously. Thus, more effort is required to understand how to choose a suitable passivator in the perovskite layer.

Among the large selection of functional groups, carboxyl (-COOH) has been effectively used in other photovoltaic device as an indispensable anchoring group due to the strong coordination with metal oxide.  $  ^{[32]}  $   In the case of perovskite films, -COOH is also found to have the interaction with perovskite films.  $  ^{[33,34]}  $   Small molecules such as amino acids,  $  ^{[35]}  $   acetate acids  $  ^{[8]}  $   were used to crosslink the perovskite boundaries or assist the crystallization process. However, the effect of charge recombination which is critical for the device performance is rarely mentioned in the previous work. It may because of small molecules were used as small amount of additives in the perovskite film which randomly distributed among the crystal boundaries. As the defects of perovskite film mainly locate at the top surface,  $  ^{[28]}  $   the controlling of stereochemical configuration is required for the film surface passivation.

In the past two decades, many attentions are paid to the research of organic dyes in dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs) for their advantages of flexible stereochemical configuration, simple synthetic method, and good stability. Herein, we employed a large molecular organic dye (AQ310)  $  ^{[36]}  $  as passivator in the one-step antisolvent solution processing method. The dye molecule AQ310 with -COOH functional group mainly distribute on the top surface of perovskite film, which can reduce the trap states of under-coordinated  $  Pb^{2+}  $  ions by forming coordination and neutralizing charges with -COOH group, resulting in the prolonged carrier recombination time and enhanced  $  V_{oc}  $ . Furthermore, the hydrophobic alkyl chains on AQ310 can also act as a moisture protection barrier and improve the device stability. Consequently, a high average PCE of 19.43% was achieved for the PSC with AQ310 passivation, compared with a low average PCE of 17.98% for the device without AQ310 passivation. As there are already tens of thousands of organic dyes for DSSCs with different stereochemical configuration, it undoubtedly provides great backup materials for the defects passivation of perovskites and interface modification of the related devices.

In order to uncover the reasons for the enhancement of photovoltaic performance using AQ310 passivation, we carried out a series of verification measurements and characterization. By comparing the top-view SEM images (Figures S2a and S2b, Supporting Information) of the perovskite films without/with AQ310 passivation, the morphology and grain size of the perovskite films were not significantly affected by AQ310 passivation. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns indicated that the crystal structures are the same with the dominant lattice reflection (111) for above two perovskite films (Figure S2c, Supporting Information). Moreover, their absorption spectra were also no difference as shown in Figure S2d in the Supporting Information. Those results illustrated that the enhancement of photovoltaic performance should be related to reduced trap states rather than the crystal properties and light harvesting capacity of perovskite films. Furthermore, we investigated the photoluminescence (PL) characteristics of perovskite films without/with AQ310 passivation. The steady-state PL spectra of films deposited on the glass (without   $  TiO_{2}  $  ) were shown in Figure 4a. The PL intensity of the AQ310-coating perovskite film on the glass substrates is increased by nearly 25% compare with pristine perovskite film, indicates less quench obtained by AQ310 passivized perovskite film. The passivation effect is further analyzed by time-resolved PL decay profiles as displayed in Figure 4b, which was also obtained by detecting the emission at 780 nm with the exciting light at 470 nm through the film side. The decay can be well fitted with biexponential components. The fast decay process (  $  \tau_{1}  $  ) is attributed mainly to bimolecular recombination of photo-generated free carriers, and the slow decay process (  $  \tau_{2}  $  ) caused by trap-assisted recombination. The   $  \tau_{1}  $   is not obviously changed by the recombination in the perovskite film, while   $  \tau_{2}  $   is mainly associated with trap-assisted recombination.  $  ^{[37,38]}  $   The perovskite film with AQ310 passivation exhibits slower decay (  $  \tau_{2}=183.8  $   ns) than that of pristine perovskite film (  $  \tau_{2}=155.6  $   ns). The enhanced PL lifetime indicates the reduced recombination in the film, and it is associated with the AQ310 passivation on the perovskite surface. It is essential for longer exciton/carrier diffusion lengths to allow large film thicknesses for light harvesting.

To further address the trap-assisted recombination in the bulk or at the interfaces, we investigated steady-state PL spectra on glass/perovskite and glass/perovskite/hole transport material (HTM). As shown in Figure S3a in the Supporting Information, the glass/perovskite passivated by AQ310 displayed a blue-shifted PL peak (777 nm) compared with that of pristine perovskite (781 nm), which indicates that the AQ310 can passivate the trap states near to the surface or along grain boundaries.  $  ^{[28]}  $   When the perovskite film forms a contact with HTM in Figure S3b in the Supporting Information, PL quenching indicates the electron transfer at the perovskite/HTM interface. When coating the AQ310 on the perovskite layer, the PL quenching was stronger compared with that of pristine perovskite, indicating that charge transfer from perovskite to HTM is enhanced by the AQ310 passivation. Additionally, the open-circuit voltage decay under the open-circuit condition was used to characterize the complete devices without/with AQ310 passivation (Figure 5a), which demonstrated that the charge recombination lifetime (  $  \tau_{i}  $  ) of device with AQ310 passivation (166   $  \mu  $  s) was substantially longer than that of the pristine (62   $  \mu  $  s). As a result, it thus can suggest that the charge recombination was effectively suppressed by the AQ310 passivation.

Last but not least, another organic dye (AQ) as reference was also applied to PSCs to identify the universal applicability of organic dye with -COOH group as passivator. AQ has the similar molecular structure with AQ310, but less hydrophobic alkyl chains, $ ^{[41]} $  and the AQ-treated PSC also exhibited a better PCE of 18.5% for forward scan and 19.1% for reverse scan (Figure S5, Supporting Information). As a result, organic dyes with -COOH group can be effective passivators for organic-inorganic halide perovskites.

In conclusion, we have proposed a strategy for improving defect passivation in perovskite films using an organic dye (AQ310) as passivator. The AQ310 passivation can reduce the trap states of under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions by forming coordination with -COOH group, which prolongs the carrier recombination time and thus leading to the enhancement of   $  V_{OC}  $  . By employing this strategy, we have achieved an average efficiency of 19.43% for AQ310-passivated PSCs, as compared with a low average efficiency of 17.98% for pristine PSC. Meanwhile, a reference organic dye (AQ), showing the similar molecular structure as AQ310 but less hydrophobic alkyl chains, is also introduced to PSC, demonstrating that organic dyes with -COOH group is commonly suitable for the use as passivator in the perovskites. Those results indicated that further performance enhancement could be expected by carefully optimizing the molecular structure of organic dye, such as tuning coordination or hydrophobic functional groups.
