# Polyelectrolyte-Doped   $  SnO_{2}  $   as a Tunable Electron Transport Layer for High-Efficiency and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Xiangping Huang, Jianhui Du, Xing Guo, Zhenhua Lin,  $  ^{*}  $   Jing Ma, Jie Su, Liping Feng, Chunfu Zhang, Jincheng Zhang, Jingjing Chang,  $  ^{*}  $   and Yue Hao

The charge transport layer is crucial to the performance and stability of the perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Compared with other conventional metal oxide electron transport materials,   $  SnO_{2}  $   has a deeper conduction band and higher electron mobility, and can efficiently serve as an electron transport layer to facilitate charge extraction and transfer. Herein, an optimized low-temperature solution-processed   $  SnO_{2}  $   electron transport layer is achieved by doping poly-ethylenimine polyelectrolyte into   $  SnO_{2}  $   for the first time in the PSCs. It is found that the performance of all aspects of the doped   $  SnO_{2}  $   film is improved over that of the pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   film. The better energy level alignment, larger built-in field, enhanced electron transfer/extraction, and reduced charge recombination all contribute to the improved device performance. Finally, a PSC with a power conversion efficiency of 20.61% is successfully prepared under low temperature below 150 °C. Moreover, the stability of the doped   $  SnO_{2}  $  -based device is also greatly improved.

There are many ETL materials, such as the n-type metal oxides (TiO $ _x $ , ZnO, and SnO $ _2 $ ), and organic small molecules and polymers such as PCBM, PNDI, and so on. In a typical PSC with normal structure, a TiO $ _2 $  film is usually used as ETL. $ ^{[12,15,16]} $  However, TiO $ _2 $  has a low electron mobility (0.01–4 cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ ), and its morphology and crystallinity control requires a complicated multistep process, including high-

temperature annealing. In contrast, ZnO is relatively easy to crystallize in most solution processes and readily available in a variety of forms.  $  ^{[17-19]}  $   Moreover, ZnO crystals have high electron mobility of 110–138 cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $  , which is several orders of magnitude higher than that of TiO  $  _{2}  $  . However, ZnO films face the challenge that severe device instability would occur due to the surface hydroxyl, oxygen vacancies, and solvent residues caused deprotonation when being used as an ETL for PSCs.  $  ^{[13,20]}  $   Compared with TiO  $  _{2}  $   and ZnO, SnO  $  _{2}  $   has faster electron mobility, wider band gap, and favorable energy band alignment.  $  ^{[21,22]}  $   Moreover, SnO  $  _{2}  $   exhibits better UV stability than TiO  $  _{2}  $  .  $  ^{[23]}  $   Therefore, SnO  $  _{2}  $   films show the potential for PSCs with planar structures to solve the long-term air stability and hysteresis issues.  $  ^{[21,22,24]}  $

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have rapidly become a hot research area in photovoltaic field due to its low production cost, easy fabrication process, and high power conversion efficiency (PCE). The PCE has skyrocketed from 3.8% to a certified value of 24.2% in less than 10 years.  $  ^{[1-5]}  $   Even though most PSCs have excellent performance, many planar PSCs still suffer serious problems of photocurrent lag and output instability.  $  ^{[6]}  $   These problems are mainly

However, the device efficiency based on low-temperature-processed   $  SnO_{2}  $   is very sensitive to   $  SnO_{2}  $   thickness or charge transfer ability.  $  ^{[25,26]}  $   To achieve rapid charge transfer, the thickness of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL for high performance PSC needs to be less than 30 nm. This thin and dense film is difficult to manufacture by spin-coating techniques,  $  ^{[25,27-29]}  $   mainly because of the aggregation of nanoparticles in the ink.  $  ^{[30]}  $   In addition, the performance of PSCs still suffers from surface trap states present at the   $  SnO_{2}/perovskite  $   interface  $  ^{[31]}  $   which are responsible for charge recombination.  $  ^{[32,33]}  $   In view of the aforementioned problems, it is valuable to explore effective modification methods for   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer. Previously, several methods have been reported to improve the film properties of   $  SnO_{2}  $ , such as fullerene modification, metal

To investigate the charge transfer and recombination processes, we measured the transient photocurrent and photovoltage of the PSCs devices based on pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}  $ :PEIE ETLs measured under open-circuit conditions. Compared with pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $ -based devices (0.16 ms), the charge recombination lifetime of   $  SnO_{2}  $ :PEIE-based devices was increased to 0.24 ms. This indicates that the   $  SnO_{2}  $ :PEIE-based device effectively suppressed the charge recombination process and reduced the trap density.

To further investigate the improvement of   $  V_{oc}  $  , the capacitance–voltage (C-V) measurements with the   $  1/C^{2}-V  $   plots of devices based on different   $  SnO_{2}  $   films were tested at different bias voltages, as shown in Figure 5c. The measurement results showed that the built-in voltage (  $  V_{bi}  $  ) of the cell with   $  SnO_{2}  $ :PEIE ETL was 0.91 V, larger than the cell with pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   (0.85 V) ETL. The increase in   $  V_{bi}  $   is related to the increased drive capability of photogenerated charge transfer and extraction. This result is consistent with the   $  V_{oc}  $   values extracted from the J-V measurements.  $  ^{[40]}  $

Finally, to test the stability of the PSCs, we routinely test unencapsulated devices in a drying cabinet with an  $ \mathrm{RH}\% $  of  $ \approx 40\% $ . The measurement results of stability were shown in Figure 5d. The device based on  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ :PEIE ETL maintained its initial value of  $ 82.1\% $  (PCE = 16.89%) after 70 days of storage, indicating significant PCE stability. Under the same storage conditions, the PCE value of the device based on pristine  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ETL

decreased rapidly, and the device completely lost PCE after 50 days. In addition, the light stability of the device was also investigated. As shown in Figure S6, Supporting Information, the device based on SnO $ _2 $ :PEIE ETL exhibited better light stability. The improvement of the perovskite/ETL interface and the perovskite film quality is the main reason for the improvement of device stability, $ ^{[52]} $  which is consistent with the characterization results described earlier. The results of device stability test indicate that PSCs based on SnO $ _2 $ :PEIE ETL exhibited better stability and potential commercial potential.

# 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, we proposed a simple, low-cost method to dope the   $  SnO_{2}  $   with PEIE polyelectrolyte to produce a high-quality ETL. The perovskite layer was prepared by a modified two-step method, and the device exhibited a PCE of 20.61%. Better quality films, enhanced charge transport and extraction capabilities, and reduced charge recombination are all reasons for device performance improvement. In addition, devices based on   $  SnO_{2}  $ :PEIE ETL have better stability. This simple and cost-effective approach can promote the development of perovskite-based photovoltaic devices and contribute to the further development of commercialization.
