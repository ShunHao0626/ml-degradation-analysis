# ABSTRACT

The quality of perovskite film plays an important role on the performance and stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, dense and uniform perovskite  $ (\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3) $  films are fabricated by combining hot-casting technique (HCT) and methylamine  $ (\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_2) $  gas treatment (MGT) under ambient conditions with a relative humidity of about  $ 60\% $ . These high quality  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  films are further used to fabricate planar heterojunction (PHJ) PSCs with a structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/ $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3/\mathrm{PCBM}/\mathrm{Ag} $ , showing a power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to  $ 14.2\% $  with negligible hysteresis. More importantly, these PHJ-PSCs show excellent stability, and over  $ 80\% $  of their original efficiencies could be maintained even stored in air (humidity  $ \sim 45\% $ ) for 20 days without encapsulation. Meanwhile, the PCE up to  $ 13.6\% $  could be achieved for PHJ-PSCs fully fabricated in air including electron transport layer PCBM. The researches provide an ease-of-fabrication method to fully fabricate highly efficient and stable PHJ-PSCs under ambient conditions with high humidity, which would potentially accelerate the commercialization of PHJ-PSCs.

Keywords: Hot-casting; Methylamine gas; Humidity; Perovskite solar cells; Planar heterojunction

# 1. Introduction

In recent years, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted tremendous attentions due to their high efficiency and low cost. Since methylammonium lead triiodide  $ (\mathrm{MAPbI}_3) $  perovskite was initially used as an active layer in dye sensitized solar cells and got a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 3.8\% $  in 2009 [1], the PSCs have been studied extensively in photovoltaic devices due to their excellent properties, such as long range visible light absorption, low trap-state density and long carrier diffusion for both electrons and holes [2-5]. To date, the certified PCE of PSCs has rocketed to a record as high as  $ 22.1\% $  [6], meeting the requirements of commercialized thin film photovoltaic technology.

Since the performance of PSCs has a strong dependence on the quality of perovskite films, quiet a number of researches have been done to help improve morphologies and crystallinity of perovskite films. A variety of methods, such as doctor blading [7], sequential deposition [8], vapor deposition [9], anti-solvent method [10] and two-step spin coating [11], have been put forward to produce high quality perovskite films. Nevertheless, the prepared films invariably contain defects, such as voids and pinholes, and the humidity and oxygen content must be strictly controlled, which are incompatible with the commercialization of PSCs. Meanwhile, the PSCs photovoltaic devices face the trouble of performance degradation in ambient conditions when exposing to humidity and light. Hence, it is indispensable to develop a feasible method to prepare outstanding perovskite films in air and sequentially fabricate high-efficiency and air-stable photovoltaic devices.

The interaction between  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_2 $  (MA) gas at room temperature has been observed [12], which could convert porous, rough  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite films into fully dense, high-quality films within a few seconds. However, they still have some limitations that the films need to be prepared in glove box. Besides, the heated or preheated substrate also has a great influence on the morphology of the perovskite films and corresponding devices [13,14]. In this work, it was found that spin-coating on the heated substrate not only avoids the influence of moisture in air on the formation of perovskite films, but also increases the coverage of

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

All materials were used directly without any purification, including   $  PbI_{2}  $   (99%, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.),   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (99%, Jingge, Wuhan), N,N-dimethylformamide (99.8%, J&K Scientific), PEDOT:PSS (Baytron, PVP AI 4083), Fullerene derivative [6, 6]-phenyl C61-butyric acid methyl ester (  $  PC_{61}BM  $  , Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.), chlorobenzene (99.8%, J&K Scientific), and methylamine ethanol solution (30%, Sinopharm).

# 2.2. Device Fabrication

Fig. 1 presents the architecture of PSCs and a schematic diagram of perovskite films prepared by in-situ hot-casting technique (HCT) and MGT. The patterned indium tin oxide (ITO) substrate was ultrasonically cleaned in acetone, detergents/ $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $ , distilled water, and isopropyl alcohol for 20 min sequentially, and was then dried by clean  $ \mathrm{N}_2 $  flow and treated by UV-ozone for 20 min. The PEDOT:PSS solution was spin-coated at a speed of 3000 rpm for 30 s, followed by a post-annealing at  $ 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 15 min. The perovskite precursor solution with a concentration of 450 mg/ml (550 mg/ml for anti-solvent method) was prepared by mixing  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  in DMF (the mole ratio  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I}:\mathrm{PbI}_2 = 1:1 $ ). It was vigorously stirred at 65 for 12 hrs and filtered with a  $ 0.22\mu \mathrm{m} $  PVDF filter before the use. The deposition of perovskite active layer was performed in air ambient (relative humidity of  $ 60\% $  and the room temperature of 20). The PEDOT:PSS-coated ITO substrate was heated on heating spin-coater for two minutes to ensure the substrate reached the corresponding temperature, and then the mixed precursor solution was spin-coated at 3000 rpm for 30 s, followed by a heat-treatment at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 10 min in air. After cooling to room temperature, the raw  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $

perovskite films were simply placed in the   $  CH_{3}NH_{2}  $   gas environment for 2 s at room temperature [12,15,16]. In an instant, the perovskite films would react with   $  CH_{3}NH_{2}  $   to form   $  MAPbI_{3}\cdot xCH_{3}NH_{2}  $ , which is colorless and transparent in liquid form and would spread over the PEDOT:PSS-coated substrate. Removed to the ambient quickly,   $  CH_{3}NH_{2}  $   molecules would depart from the intermediate, and perovskite films turned to dark brown and became shiny. It is necessary to point out that the room temperature and methylamine alcohol solution should be controlled at   $  \sim20\ ^{\circ}C  $   to prevent too rapid volatilization of methylamine gas from the intermediate, which has an adverse effect on morphology of the films. Preparation process of perovskite films is divided into spin-coating at room temperature without methylamine gas treatment (RT-W/O) and with methylamine gas treatment (RT-MGT), in-situ hot-casting without methylamine gas treatment (HCT-W/O) and with methylamine gas treatment (HCT-MGT). The fabrication of perovskite films with anti-solvent method in glove box refers to our previous work [17]. Subsequently, the PCBM solution with a concentration of 20 mg/mL was spin-coated onto the perovskite layer at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, a 100 nm Ag electrode was deposited via thermal evaporation with a mask, resulting in an active area of   $  0.09\ cm^{2}  $ .

# 2.3. Characterization

The UV-vis spectrophotometer (Puxi, T9, China) and X-ray diffraction (XRD, Rigaku D, Max 2500, Japan) were used to characterize the absorption properties and crystallographic properties of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films, respectively. Scanning electron microscope (FEI Helios Nanolab 600i SEM, America) was employed to characterize the morphology of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  thin films. Current density-voltage (J-V) characteristics of PHJ-PSCs were measured by a digital Source Meter (Keithley, model 2420) from  $ -0.2\mathrm{V} $  to  $ +1.2\mathrm{V} $  with a  $ 50~\mathrm{ms} $  scanning delay in both reverse- and forward-scan modes. The measurement was carried out by a Xenon-lamp-based solar simulator (Newport 91160s, AM 1.5 G) with a light intensity of  $ 100\mathrm{mW/cm^2} $ , which was calibrated by a standard silicon solar cell.

# 3. Results and discussion

# 3.1. Film analysis

The porous, rough  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite films could turn into compact and high-quality films under the HCT and MGT processes, supported by SEM images. Fig. 2a and Fig. 2b show the SEM images of raw perovskite films fabricated by one step spin-coating without and with heating substrate, respectively. It is obvious that both  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films do not cover the substrate, and the former one is composed of large-size dendrite-like crystals. These morphologies are similar to the previous reports using DMF solvent without anti-solvent treatment [18]. As using MGT, the prepared perovskite films via HCT can become very compact and smooth (Fig. 2d). On the contrary, the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films fabricated by normal spin-coating without heating treatment are discontinuous and the coverage is poor as well (Fig. 2c). It indicates that the MGT not only has dramatically changed the morphology of perovskite films, but also the substrate heating temperature in the process of spin coating has a great influence on the final film morphology. Therefore, the effect of substrate heating temperatures (room temperature,  $ 40^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ,  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ,  $ 80^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) on the morphology of perovskite films have been studied (Fig. S1). They results show that the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  morphology varies greatly with the different substrate heating temperature. With the

# 3.3. Devices performance

The PHJ-PSCs with a simple structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/MAPbI $ _3 $ /PCBM/Ag are fabricated, as shown in Fig. 1. The PHJ-PSCs show poor performance for MAPbI $ _3 $  films without MGT (Fig. S3a), which is attributed to MAPbI $ _3 $  films with poor light absorption and surface morphology. The performances of PHJ-PSCs can be dramatically improved with the MAPbI $ _3 $  films treated by MGT. Meanwhile, different substrate heating temperatures have a great influence on device performances, as shown in Fig. S4 and Tab. S1. With the increase of substrate heating temperatures, short-circuit current ( $ J_{sc} $ ) gradually increase firstly and then decrease, reaching the summit at 60 °C, which are consistent with the absorption results. As the substrate temperature is 80 °C or 100 °C, the  $ J_{sc} $  and fill factor ( $ FF $ ) decrease dramatically, leading to the reduction of efficiency. This can be attributed to the apparent deterioration of MAPbI $ _3 $  film and a significant increase of defects at higher temperatures, leading to pronounced carrier recombination [13]. # 3.3 Stability in air

The stability of PHJ-PSCs prepared by HCT&MGT has been improved significantly. Fig. 4 shows the change trends of photovoltaic parameters of PHJ-PSCs over time. It is worth noting that all of devices are placed in dark condition without encapsulation and continuously exposed to air (25 °C and 45% relative humidity). The performances of PHJ-PSCs prepared in glove box with anti-solvent method decay markedly within 10 days, which mainly come from attenuation of FF and   $  J_{sc}  $  . Both FF and   $  J_{sc}  $   experience severe decay, from ~70% to 40% and from ~19 to 10 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   respectively, resulting from the attenuation of the interfaces at MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /PEDOT:PSS and MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /PCBM [21]. After storage for 20 days, the   $  V_{oc}  $   and   $  J_{sc}  $   deteriorate rapidly and the device begins to form a yellow substance that should be the PbI  $  _{2}  $   originating from the decomposition of the perovskite. Additionally, silver electrode turns to be light yellow resulting from the migration of iodide ions of perovskite to the silver electrode and the reaction with silver [22]. It is interesting that the photovoltaics can be stably stored in air for 35 days before the efficiency dying out. Herein, it should be noted that the devices in this experiment show better stability comparing to PHJ-PSCs.

It should be noted that, in order to avoid the influence of residual methylamine molecules, the perovskite films with MGT are followed by annealing treatment in some other reports [16,24-26]. However, the annealing process was not processed in our case. The mechanism of the residual methylamine delaying the attenuation of perovskite solar cell devices may have the following four aspects: (1) The residual methylamine reacts with the perovskite to form an intermediate, thus inhibiting the deterioration of the perovskite layer, which may occur mainly in the perovskite grains [27]; (2) At the grain boundaries (GBs), superfluous methylamine exists mainly in molecular form since the accumulation of amorphous perovskite or impurities. As the erosion of perovskite caused by moisture takes place at GBs [28], superfluous MA would bound permeated moisture, thus inhibiting the influence of moisture on perovskite. Meanwhile, with the constant slow volatilization of superfluous methylamine, the permeation and erosion of moisture for perovskite would be inhibited, and the permeated moisture can be taken away; (3) The mechanism of the perovskite decomposition is shown in equation as follows [21,29-30]. It is responsible for suppressing non-radiative recombination and improving hole and electron extraction at GBs, resulting in a slight increase in  $ J_{sc} $  in the short run (20 days) [31]. # 4. Conclusion

In summary, high-quality, density and uniform perovskite films are fabricated in air with HCT&MGT. In one-step spin-coating process, the effect of humidity in the air on the formation of perovskite film is avoided by HCT. Rough and tough perovskite films prepared in air with HCT could be converted into smooth and compact films through MGT. The results of SEM, UV-vis and XRD show that the perovskite films prepared by this method have good surface morphology, absorbance and crystallinity. Applying the films to fabricate PHJ-PSCs, the  $ PCE $  of the cells based on the simple structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /PCBM/Ag is up to 14.24% and the average  $ PCE $  is 13.17%. This consequence is comparable with the performance of the device fabricated by anti-solvent method in glove box. Meanwhile, the devices prepared by HCT&MGT are much more stable than those prepared in the

This work not only proposes a new method for preparing high-quality perovskite films, but also provides an important route for developing large-area perovskite films and PHJ-PSCs under environmental conditions.

# Highlights

(1) High-quality   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite film is fabricated under ambient condition with a humidity of   $  \sim60\%  $  .

(2) Simple planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells (PHJ-PSCs) fabricated in air exhibit a power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to 14.2%.

(3) The PHJ-PSCs fabricated in air show excellent stability without encapsulation.

(4) It provides a route to fully fabricate highly efficient and stable PHJ-PSCs under ambient condition with high humidity.
