# Chemical Doping Engineering by Utilizing Trilacunary Keggin Polyoxometalates as a Dopant for High Performance Perovskite Solar Cell

Xiao Fan,  $  ^{a}  $   Jian Zhang,  $  ^{*a}  $   Yulin Yang,  $  ^{*a}  $   Debin Xia,  $  ^{a}  $   Yayu Dong,  $  ^{a}  $   Lele Qiu,  $  ^{a}  $   JiaQi Wang,  $  ^{a}  $   Wei Cao,  $  ^{a}  $   Boyuan Hu,  $  ^{a}  $   Wei Wang  $  ^{a}  $   and Ruiqing Fan  $  ^{*a}  $

Chemical doping engineering is an effective strategy to modify the hole transport layer (HTL) and achieve high-efficiency perovskite solar cells (PSCs). In this work, we synthesize an infrequent trilacunary Keggin type polyoxometalate  $ \mathrm{Na_{10}[Zn_2(H_2O)_6(WO_2)_2(BiW_9O_{33})_2]} $  ( $ \mathrm{BiW_9 - Zn} $ ) and apply it as an additive to enhance the hole mobility and electrical conductivity of Spiro-OMeTAD based HTL. Thanks to its strong electron-accepting properties of polyoxometalate molecules, as-synthesized  $ \mathrm{BiW_9 - Zn} $  can directly oxidize Spiro-OMeTAD in the inert atmosphere and avoid boring long-term oxidation process. Therefore, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of optimal PSCs with doping  $ \mathrm{BiW_9 - Zn} $  are enhanced from  $ 17.58\% $  (without doping) to  $ 19.56\% $  with the significant improved fill factor and open-circuit voltage. In addition, the assembly repeatability and long-term stability of PSCs are also improved. This work not only demonstrates the potential of using polyoxometalates (POMs) as low-cost, efficient and highly flexible chemical dopant for HTL, more importantly pave a new route to enhance the performance of PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Nowadays, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted tremendous attention as a promising candidate for solar-energy conversion, owing to their excellent properties including low-cost, favorable photon absorption and high charge mobility.  $  ^{1-3}  $   Therefore, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has skyrocketed to a certified record efficiency of 25.5%.  $  ^{4}  $   Although significant progresses have been realized in both PCE of the various devices and understanding of the underlying physics of the material,  $  ^{5-6}  $   there are many outstanding issues caused by low-quality films still impede the commercialization of PSCs.  $  ^{7-9}  $   As a result, the researches focusing on further improving the morphology and stability of each functional layer turn to a new-coming hot spot in the field.  $  ^{10-12}  $

The hole transport layer (HTL) used for extracting holes from perovskite is one of the most important components for high-efficiency PSCs.  $  ^{13}  $   Currently, 2,2',7,7'-Tetrakis (N, N-p dimethoxy-phenylamino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) was widely used as commercialized HTL material due to good morphological stability and suitable HOMO energy level. However, Spiro-OMeTAD molecules itself suffer from the poor intrinsic hole mobility and conductivity, as well as necessary oxidation process.  $  ^{14-15}  $   Therefore, the lithium bis

(trifluoromethylsulfonnyl) (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP) are commonly applied as additives to enhance the electrochemical performance of Spiro-OMeTAD based HTLs.  $  ^{7,16}  $   However, both additives cannot directly oxidize Spiro-OMeTAD and hygroscopic properties of them inevitably attract moisture from the ambient, resulting in poor stability and low efficiency of PSCs. In an attempt to obtain more stable devices, chemical doping engineering has been performed on developing high-efficiency PSCs.  $  ^{17-18}  $   In recent years, many chemical dopants, such as 2,3,5,6-Tetrafluoro-7,7,8,8,-tetracyanoquinodimethane (F4-TCNQ), carbon, carbon nanotubes, metal-based complexes,   $  FeCl_{3}  $  , and CuI, have been reported as effective additives for the oxidization of Spiro-OMeTAD.  $  ^{19-21}  $   Nevertheless, these dopants exhibit many inherent drawbacks, such as complex synthesis progress and high cost, which seriously restrict further application.

Polyoxometalates (POMs) is a type of multi-metallic anionic nanocluster with a cage structure composed of heteroatoms and central atoms.  $  ^{22-23}  $   Generally, strong photocatalytic and electrocatalytic activities, as well as certain redox capabilities make POMs excellent candidates as chemical dopants. Besides, many works have reported that POMs also can improve the photoelectric properties of thin-film solar cells.  $  ^{24-25}  $   In our previous work, POMs (Keggin type) could oxidize SpiroOMeTAD due to its matched energy level. Owing to improving the transportation and inhibiting the recombination of carriers, the photoelectric properties efficiency of PSCs has been effectively enhanced.  $  ^{26}  $   Compared with conventional POMs, the lacunary Keggin type POMs are easily doped with transition metal elements and maintain their structural characteristics. # 2. Experimental Section

# 2.1. Synthesis of BiW$_9$-Zn

According to previous reports with modification,  $  ^{27-28}  $     $  Na_{9}[BiW_{9}O_{33}]  $   was selected as a starting material for the preparation of   $  BiW_{9}-Zn  $  . Briefly, 5.70 g (2 mmol)   $  Na_{9}[BiW_{9}O_{33}]  $   was dissolved in 30 ml of sodium acetate buffer solution (at 60 °C) with stirring. Next, 0.44 g   $  \mathrm{Zn(CH_{3}COO)_{2}}  $   (2 mmol) was dissolved in little water and dropwise added in pre-prepared solution. The mixture continued stirring subsequently for 1 h. At last, the resulting solution was filtered through a medium frit and cooled to room temperature. After a few days, white crystal was obtained by filtration.

# 2.2. Device Fabrication

At first, FTO glass was etched by hydrochloric acid and zinc powder, followed by cleaned with acetone and water respectively, and FTO glass was pre-treated with UV-ozone for 30 minutes. An Electronic transport layer (ETL) was deposited onto FTO glass by spin-coating the mixture of titanium (IV) isopropanol and hydrochloric acid at 4000 rpm for 20 s. Next, substrates were annealed at 500 °C for 30 minutes. The multiple cation halide perovskite solution was prepared by dissolving

PbI $ _2 $  (0.507 g), PbBr $ _2 $  (0.073 g), FAI (0.172 g) and MABr (0.022 g) in 1 mL of a 1:4 (v/v) mixture of DMSO and DMSO. An 85 μL CsI (1.5 mmol/ml) solution was added in pre-prepared solution, which was dissolved in 1 mL DMSO, yielding a nominal composition of Cs $ _{0.1} $ [(FA $ _{0.83} $ MA $ _{0.17} $ )] $ _{0.9} $ Pb(I $ _{0.83} $ Br $ _{0.17} $ ) $ _3 $ . The perovskite film was prepared by spin-coating on the ETL at 1500 rpm for 15 s and 3000 rpm for 20 s. During the spin-coating, 200 μL of chlorobenzene was deposited onto the perovskite film for 4 s. Afterward, the devices were annealed at 100 °C for 1h under heating. The Hole transport material (HTM) was deposited onto perovskite by spin-coating the mixture of Spiro-OMeTAD, TBP and Li-TFSI at 4000 rpm for 20 s. Finally, 80 nm of Au electrode was thermally evaporated under high vacuum (<5 x 10 $ ^{-4} $  Pa) to complete the device fabrication. To prepare the BiW $ _9 $ -Zn doped HTM solution, the pristine HTM solution was doped with different concentrations of BiW $ _9 $ -Zn, respectively.

# 2.3. Characterization of the materials

X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements and Scan electron microscopy (SEM) images were performed separately by Shimadzu XRD-6000 instrument and Hitachi new generation cold-field emission SEM. Thermo Gravimetric Analyzer (TGA) and Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC) were conducted on PerkinElmer STA 6000 simultaneous thermal Analyzer. ESCALAB 250Xi was used to carried out X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements. Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra was tested on a PerkinElmer Spectrum 100 FT-IR spectrometer. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was obtained using the Gamry electrochemical workstation. Ag/AgCl electrode as reference electrode, Pt slice as counter electrode and Pt plate as working electron in tetrabutylammonium hexafluorophosphate  $ (\mathrm{Bu}_4\mathrm{NPF}_6, 0.1\mathrm{M}) $  chlorobenzene solutions.

# 2.4. Electrochemical characterization

PerkinElmer 750 spectrophotometer was used to test UV/Vis absorption spectra. Electrochemical properties of samples and devices was recorded by an electrochemical workstation (Gamry INTERFACE 1000E). Steady-state PL measurements were performed by using a FLS 920 luminescence spectrometer. Photoluminescence performance was investigated by steady-state fluorescence spectrometer FLS920. Hole mobility was measured by the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method, which can be described by the following equation:

$$
J = (9 / 8) \mu \varepsilon_ {r} \varepsilon_ {o} \left(V ^ {2} / d ^ {3}\right)
$$

where  $  J  $  is the current density,  $  \mu  $  is the hole mobility,  $  \varepsilon_r  $  is the dielectric constant of the material (normally taken to approach 3 for organic semiconductors),  $  \varepsilon_o  $  is the vacuum permittivity ( $  8.85 \times 10^{-12} \mathrm{~F / m}  $ ),  $  V  $  is the applied bias, and  $  d  $  is the film thickness.

# Conclusions

In summary,  $ \mathrm{BiW_9 - Zn} $  was successfully prepared as a trilacunary Keggin type POMs and be used as a cost-effective dopant for Spiro-OMeTAD firstly. The photoelectric performances and the J-V measurements prove that the conductivity and hole mobility of hole carriers were significantly increased by introducing  $ \mathrm{BiW_9 - Zn} $  in HTL. In addition, the chemical doping of  $ \mathrm{BiW_9 - Zn} $  could also reduce the recombination of carriers and promotes the transport of holes in the interface. As a result, the optimal PSC with a high efficiency of  $ 19.56\% $  as well as enhanced  $ FF $  and  $ V_{oc} $  was obtained when employing  $ \mathrm{BiW_9 - Zn} $  (4mg/ml) doped HTL. More importantly, the repeatability and long-term stability of PSCs also have been improved. The PCE of PSCs prepared by HTM with  $ \mathrm{BiW_9 - Zn} $  still maintains about  $ 89\% $  after 30 days. This work not only demonstrates the potential of using POMs as low-cost, efficient and highly flexible chemical dopant for HTL, more importantly present a strategy to enhance performance of PSCs.
