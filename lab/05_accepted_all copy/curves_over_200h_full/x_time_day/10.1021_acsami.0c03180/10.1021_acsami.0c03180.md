# ABSTRACT

Numerous trap states and low conductivity of compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers are major obstacles for achieving high power conversion efficiency and high stability perovskite solar cells. Here we report an effective   $  Na_{2}S  $   doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer, which can improve the conductivity of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers, the contact of   $  TiO_{2}  $ /perovskite interface, and also the crystallinity of following perovskite layers. Comprehensive investigations demonstrate that Na cations would increase the conductivity of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers while S anions would change the wettability of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers, and improve the crystallinity of perovskite layers and passivate defects at the   $  TiO_{2}  $ /PVK interface. Synergetic effects of dopants lead to a champion efficiency as high as 21.25% in unencapsulated PSCs, with much improved stability. Our work provides new insights on anion dopants in   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers which is usually neglected in previous reports, and also proposes a simple approach to produce low-cost and high-performance electron transporting layers for high performance PSCs.

KEYWORDS: perovskite solar cells, additive, compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , electron extraction, crystallization

# INTRODUCTION

Organic-inorganic hybrid halide perovskite (PVK) materials have been considered as the most promising candidates for next generation solar cells,  $  ^{1-2}  $  due to their extraordinary optoelectronic properties and low production cost. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have reached 25.2% this year from the initial 3.8% in 2009,  $  ^{3}  $  thanks to tremendous efforts on composition engineering, interface engineering, and crystallization control. However, improving the quality of electron transport layers (ETLs) is still one of major challenges for high stability and high performance PSCs.  $  ^{4}  $

In normal structure PSCs, ETLs not only provide channels for electron extraction, but also have vital impacts on the interface defects and the crystallization process of following PVK layers. Surface modification and ion doping are the most frequently used strategies to improve the conductivity and passivate the surface defects of compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   (titanium dioxide) layers. Due to the ionic nature of PVK lattice, organic molecules containing various organic functional groups have been demonstrated as effective inter-layer for passivating defects at   $  TiO_{2}/PVK  $   interface. H. Ip et al inserted a thin layer of PCBM ([6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester) between compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer and PVK as an efficient charge transfer interlayer, resulting in the significantly improved morphology of PVK films, better interface trap states passivation and lower hysteresis.  $  ^{5}  $   Hou et al introduced dopamine self-assembled monolayer on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer and correspondingly improved carrier transport, attribute to effectively interface trap states passivation by hydrogen bonding

Meanwhile, sulfur is from the same group as oxygen, and might partly fill the oxygen vacancies in   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers. It is found that the doping of   $  Na_{2}S  $   into   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers not only improve the conductivity of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers, but also leads to better interface passivation and improved crystallinity of PVK. As a result, planar PSCs fabricated on   $  Na_{2}S  $   doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs achieved a boosted PCE of 21.25% (reverse scan) with a highest   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.221 V. The mechanism of   $  Na^{+}  $   and   $  S^{2-}  $   ions are also discussed in this work. This work not only provides a simple method to simultaneously improve the quality of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers and PVK layers, but also open an avenue for anion dopants in ETLs.

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was firstly used to investigate the existence and the chemical bonding states of   $  Na^{+}  $  ,   $  Cl^{-}  $   and   $  S^{2-}  $   ions in   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers. The binding energy scale was calibrated using the C 1s peak at 284.6 eV. Peaks located at 1071.4 eV, 162.8 eV and 198.3 eV were identified as that of Na 1s, S 2p and Cl 2p, respectively, as shown in Figure 1c and Figure 1d. Those peaks can also be clearly observed with no obvious position change after   $  Ar^{+}  $   etching, confirming that   $  Na^{+}  $  ,   $  S^{2-}  $   and   $  Cl^{-}  $   ions are introduced into the bulk of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers with the identical chemical states as those on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface. Ti 2p and O 2p peaks in the XPS spectra of C1, C2, and   $  D_{1}  $   samples are almost identical as shown in Figure 1e and Figure 1f, which indicates that the incorporation of   $  Na^{+}  $  ,   $  S^{2-}  $  , and   $  Cl^{-}  $   ions doesn’t change the chemical states of   $  Ti^{4+}  $   and   $  O^{2-}  $  . UPS measurements in Figure S1 reveal that the introduction of   $  Na_{2}S  $   doesn’t change the work function of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers (a variation of 0.06 eV is in the range of measurement errors). We can conclude that   $  Na^{+}  $  ,   $  S^{2-}  $  , and   $  Cl^{-}  $   ions are doped into the bulk of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers, and don’t affect the chemical states of the pristine   $  TiO_{2}  $ , indicating that those ions probably mainly locate at grain boundaries in   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers and   $  TiO_{2}/PVK  $   interfaces.

# CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we demonstrated efficient   $  Na_{2}S  $   doped ETLs prepared with a facial doping method by directly adding   $  Na_{2}S  $   into the   $  TiO_{2}  $   precursor solution to improve the device performance and stability of PSCs. Both   $  Na^{+}  $  and   $  S^{2-}  $   ions are successfully introduced into   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers and confirmed by XPS and TEM characterization. The incorporation of   $  Na^{+}  $  ions improves the conductivity and the electron extraction capability of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers, resulting in the improved FF and   $  J_{sc}  $   of corresponding PSCs. The incorporation of   $  S^{2-}  $   ions increases the hydrophobicity of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers and probably forms Pb-S bonds at   $  TiO_{2}/PVK  $   interfaces, resulting in the improved quality of   $  TiO_{2}/PVK  $   interfaces and the increased crystallinity of the PVK layer in terms of lower trap density and larger grain size. PSCs composed of 1%   $  Na_{2}S  $   doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers have successfully achieved PCE as high as 21.25% with an impressive high   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.221 V, and significantly improved stability with only 10% PCE drop after 90 days' storage in the air. This work would open new avenue on the anions doping in ETLs and provide insights into the modification of traditional   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers for the fabrication of high performance PSCs.

# Device Fabrication:

The FTO glass (sheet resistance = 7Ω sq  $  ^{-1}  $  ) was sequentially cleaned with detergent, deionized water, acetone and isopropanol in an ultrasonicator for 20 minutes, then dried with nitrogen gun and treated by UV-ozone for 15 min. Then the mixed TiO  $  _{2}  $  precursor solution was dropped onto the FTO substrates to form the c-TiO  $  _{2}  $  layers at 5000 rpm for 30s in the air. Samples were pre-annealed at 120 °C for 20 min on the hot plate, and then annealed in the muffle oven at 450 °C for 1 h. Samples were treated with UV-ozone for 15 min before PVK deposition process and then transferred into a glove box. PVK layers were deposited rapidly by a one-step spin coating process at 500 rpm for 5s and 5000 rpm for 50 s on FTO/c-TiO  $  _{2}  $  substrates. During the PVK layers spin-coating process, 50 μL of chlorobenzene (Alfa Aesar) was injected onto the spinning substrate constantly. All the samples were then annealed at 150 °C for 10 min with a cover plate. After several minutes, the Spiro-OMeTAD precursor was then deposited on PVK layers by spin coating at 5000 rpm for 50 s. At last, 80 nm-thick Au electrodes were deposited by the vacuum thermal evaporation.
