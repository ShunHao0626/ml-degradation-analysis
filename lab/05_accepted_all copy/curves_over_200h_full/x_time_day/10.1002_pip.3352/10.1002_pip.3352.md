# Enhanced fill factor for normal n-i-p planar heterojunction and mesoscopic perovskite solar cells using ruthenium-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   electron transporting layer

Sawanta S. Mali  $  ^{1}  $   | Jyoti V. Patil  $  ^{1,2}  $   | Pravin S. Shinde  $  ^{3}  $   | Chang Kook Hong  $  ^{1}  $

# Abstract

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) fabricated with a normal mesoscopic negative-intrinsic-positive   $  (n-i-p)  $   device structure have shown a great promise for further developments ever since their enhanced performance with high power conversion efficiency (PCE, 25.2%) and excellent reproducibility. The electron transporting layer (ETL) employed in such PSCs has been a critical component for improving their performance. The present work focuses on the synthesis of high-quality Ru-doped compact   $  TiO_{2}  $     $  (c-TiO_{2})  $   ETLs   $  (Ru:c-TiO_{2})  $   by a simple spin-coating technique. Further, the role of   $  Ru^{4+}  $   cation doping in   $  c-TiO_{2}  $   is discussed in detail. A systematic study revealed that the Ru-doping not only significantly influences the open-circuit voltage   $  (V_{oc})  $  , current density   $  (U_{sc})  $  , and fill factor (FF) but also suppresses the charge recombination in the perovskite devices. The PSCs prepared using   $  Ru-c-TiO_{2}  $   ETLs with optimum Rudoping content exhibited PCEs of 19.48% for planar and 20.87% for mesoscopic device architecture with enhanced photovoltage. Additionally, the fabricated PSC devices based on 1.5%   $  Ru:c-TiO_{2}  $   ETLs exhibited air stability over 200 days, which is much higher than that of a control device.

# KEYWORDS

doped electron transporting layers, n-i-p-type perovskite solar cells, stability

# 1 | INTRODUCTION

Mixed-halide mixed-cation-based hybrid perovskite solar cells (HPSCs) have received considerable interest due to their promising properties and ever exceeding 25.5% (for single-junction) and 29.1% (for Si/perovskite tandem configuration) power conversion efficiencies (PCEs).  $  ^{1-13}  $   In another study, methylammonium (MA)-free perovskite with an absorber layer of   $  Rb_{5}Cs_{10}FAPbI_{3}  $   (FA = formamidinium) exhibited a PCE of 18.16% with stability up to 1000 h.  $  ^{14}  $   Therefore, the mixed-halide materials that incorporate triple or quadrupole cations hold excellent promise and prospects for future developments on perovskite research.

Because the mesoscopic negative-intrinsic-positive   $  (n-i-p)  $   HPSCs exhibit greater efficiency and reproducibility,  $  ^{15,16}  $   researchers around

the world are giving a great deal of attention to the development of mesoscopic n-i-p-type device configuration to tweak and enhance the efficiency further. Among the various electron transporting layers (ETLs) employed so far, the compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   (c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) along with mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   (mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) as the ETLs showed the ultimate value in the efficiency. Also, it attracts considerable interest due to the multifunctionality of c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   toward planar as well as mesoscopic development. On the other hand, the c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   layer is prone to the defect trap levels that lead to charge recombination, consequently affecting the electron transport and hence the charge collection efficiency. To address the above issue and to further improve the overall performance, the mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   has been doped with metals such as Li, Sn, and Nb.  $  ^{17-20}  $   However, it has resulted in low electron mobility due to the porous nature of mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $  . Due to its mesoporous nature, the electron travel path length in  $ mp\text{-TiO}_2 $  is much slower than that in  $ c\text{-TiO}_2 $ . As a result,  $ mp\text{-TiO}_2 $  suffers from unwanted recombination losses resulting in a poor electron extraction from the perovskite absorber.

To avoid such a scenario, the   $  mp-TiO_{2}  $   could be replaced with   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   or   $  ZrO_{2}  $   layer as an insulating scaffold layer.  $  ^{14,21}  $   In another approach, metal-doped (Mg, Sn, Nb, Li, Ta, Al, and Ga) compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   has been used as a promising ETLs for HPSCs.  $  ^{19-26}  $   Besides, recently, Mg and Cs doped   $  ZnO^{27,28}  $   and   $  Cs:TiO_{2}  $    $  ^{29,30}  $   have been used as ETLs for inorganic PSCs to improve the device performance and stability of the unstable perovskite layer. The metal-doping in compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL has been shown to lower the series and charge transfer resistances, to facilitate the charge transport efficiently. The first report on Ru-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   suggested that the electronic properties of   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL improved significantly,  $  ^{31}  $   albeit at the cost of limited efficiency.  $  ^{32,33}  $   Besides, due to promising   $  H_{2}  $   production properties of Ru-doping, most of the previous reports for the Ru-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   have been focused on hydrogen generation studies using hierarchical nanostructures of Ru-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $  .  $  ^{34,35}  $   It is reported that the higher doping levels of Ru (>5%) can significantly affect the optical properties of   $  TiO_{2}  $ , and such Ru-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   can be used toward visible-light water splitting. Apart from the   $  H_{2}  $   production application, the lower doping levels of Ru in   $  TiO_{2}  $   were utilized in dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs)  $  ^{36,37}  $   as well as in PSCs.  $  ^{32,33}  $   However, these previous reports demonstrated limited efficiency and stability from such low-cost photovoltaics.

Herein, we report a systematic study investigating the role of Ru-doped  $ c\text{-TiO}_2 $  (hereafter  $ \mathrm{Ru:c - TiO_2} $ ) ETL in HPSCs. The motive of the current study is based on the fact that the Ti-site can be replaced with Ru easily to create additional oxygen vacancies due to the nearly equivalent ionic radii of  $ \mathrm{Ti}^{4+} $  (0.68 Å) and  $ \mathrm{Ru}^{4+} $  (0.62 Å). Therefore, it is expected that Ru-doping would help the adequate hole blocking as well as the recombination rate reduction. We optimized the Ru precursor concentration to tune the optoelectronic properties of Ru-doped  $ c\text{-TiO}_2 $  ETLs and used them in HPSCs. Based on the optimized condition at  $ 1.5\% $  Ru doping, we demonstrated  $ 20.87\% $  PCE with over 100 days of stability.

# 2 | RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

# 2.1 | Optoelectronic properties of Ru:c-TiO  $  _{2}  $

The precursor solution was prepared with different concentrations of   $  TiO_{2}  $   precursor solution with or without the addition of an appropriate amount of Ru from 1.0 M   $  RuCl_{3}\cdot xH_{2}O  $   stock solution. Figure 1A shows the digital photographs of different precursor solutions used for the deposition of the Ru:c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs. Figure 1B shows the photographs of transparent pristine and Ru-doped c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   films (up to 3%Ru). Such highly compact and transparent Ru:c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   films were obtained by spin-coating the Ru:c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   precursor upon annealing. Ru doping beyond 3% up to 10% resulted in light brown semi-transparent films.

The interface wettability between   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL and perovskite solution plays a key role in controlling the grain growth, uniformity, and the grain size of the perovskite thin films. Therefore, we performed the contact angle measurements for water and   $  PbI_{2} + DMF:DMSO(4:1~v/v)  $   solution onto a bare FTO substrate, pristine, and   $  Ru:c-TiO_{2}  $   ETLs, as shown in Figure 1C,D. The bare FTO substrate showed a water contact angle of   $  63.68^{\circ}  $ , which is decreased to   $  39.13^{\circ}  $   for   $  c-TiO_{2}  $   ETL, revealing its hydrophilic nature. On the other hand, the water contact angle increased to   $  69.44^{\circ}  $   for 1.5%   $  Ru:c-TiO_{2}  $   ETL, revealing a lower wettability. Furthermore, the   $  PbI_{2} + DMF:DMSO(4:1~v/v)  $   solvent contact angle of pristine   $  c-TiO_{2}  $   is improved from   $  16.76^{\circ}  $   to   $  21.83^{\circ}  $   for 1.5   $  Ru:c-TiO_{2}  $   ETL, indicating the lower wettability for   $  PbI_{2}/DMF:DMSO  $   solvents. From the water and   $  PbI_{2} + DMF:DMSO  $   solvent contact angle analyses, it is apparent that the Ru-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs are hydrophobic for water as well as   $  PbI_{2} + DMF:DMSO  $   solvents, which could facilitate uniform distribution of perovskite nuclei. Such a lower wettability of the Ru-  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs offers a lower surface tension dragging force (STDF), which enables the uniform distribution of nuclei compared to the pristine   $  c-TiO_{2}  $   ETLs in substantial grain growth.  $  ^{38-41}  $   This was further evidenced by surface roughness and grain size analyses using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and atomic force microscopy (AFM) in the following section.

The influence of Ru-doping on the optical transmittance and bandgap properties of Ru-modified   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs was investigated from the UV–vis spectra of pristine and Ru:c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs (Figure 1E). For the optical transmittance measurements, the pristine and Ru-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   electrodes are deposited onto the FTO-coated glass substrates. The optical properties revealed that all the ETLs with Ru-doping concentrations up to 1.5% exhibited over 90% transmittance. This demonstrates that such ETLs could easily transmit the visible solar spectrum that is beneficial for back illumination. However, the transmittance of Ru-  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs dropped to 80% for 2% Ru-doping and went on to decrease further 2% Ru, exhibiting higher absorption of visible light. Further increment in Ru-doping resulted in a darker precursor solution, and all such samples exhibited strong absorption in the UV band region. The absorption spectra showed a similar type of absorption edge around 343 nm (3.61 eV), which got shifted to 405 nm (3.05 eV) with Ru doping. The increase in the bandgap (  $  E_{g}  $  ) or the red-shifting of the   $  E_{g}  $   suggested a successful Ru doping (Figure S1). Figure S2A shows the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of pristine and Ru:c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs. The XRD patterns of c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs on FTO exhibited no signs of any   $  TiO_{2}  $   peaks, apparently due to minimal thickness (  $  \sim50  $   nm). Figure S1 shows the   $  E_{g}  $   values obtained from Tauc plots for c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   and Ru:c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs. The   $  E_{g}  $   value for pristine c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL is 3.61 eV, which is decreased to 3.05 eV at 10% Ru-doping. A minor band gap variation is seen up to 1% Ru-doping. At 1.5% Ru doping concentration, the bandgap decreased to slightly 3.49 eV. However, continuous bandgap decrement is noticed after 2% Ru doping. The c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs doped with 2.0%, 2.5%, 3.0%, and 5% Ru show the   $  E_{g}  $   values of 3.49, 3.48, 3.23, and 3.20 eV, respectively. However, the bandgap reached to the lowest value of 3.05 eV for 10% Ru-doping.

# 2.2 | Topographical and chemical composition studies

The surface topographies of pristine and Ru-doped  $ c $ -TiO $ _2 $  ETLs were studied using SEM. Figure S3 shows the SEM micrographs of bare FTO, pristine  $ c $ -TiO $ _2 $  and Ru: $ c $ -TiO $ _2 $  thin films. The quality and compactness of the  $ c $ -TiO $ _2 $  ETLs decide the PSC's performance. The top-view surface of the  $ c $ -TiO $ _2 $  and Ru: $ c $ -TiO $ _2 $  ETLs revealed the formation of a highly uniform Ru: $ c $ -TiO $ _2 $  layer on the FTO surface, which is favorable for good ohmic contacts. The cross-sectional SEM images (Figure S4) of both the ETLs exhibited the formation of the highly uniform layer ( $ \sim $ 50 nm thickness) due to the growth of compact nanoparticles with good interconnectivity. For knowing the surface chemical compositions of pristine and Ru-doped  $ c $ -TiO $ _2 $  ETLs, the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra were obtained (Figures S5 and S6). The survey spectrum reveals the presence of Ti, O, and Ru elements. The pristine  $ c $ -TiO $ _2 $  ETL exhibited a spin-orbit splitting ( $ \Delta $ E) of 5.77 eV between Ti2p doublet peaks (Figure S7B). In the case of Ru: $ c $ -TiO $ _2 $  ETL sample, a blue shift is observed, and the  $ \Delta $ E splitting is widened up to 5.89 eV, which is an indication of higher electron density and replacement of Ti sites by Ru atoms in Ru: $ c $ -TiO $ _2 $  ETL. $ ^{42} $  The Ru-doping reduces the Ti $  ^{4+}  $  transformation to Ti $  ^{3+}  $  via protonation to an adjacent site. The characteristic peak position of O1s in

c-TiO $ _2 $  is slightly blue-shifted in the Ru:c-TiO $ _2 $  ETL. This newly shifted O1s peak appears at 529.54 eV with a shoulder peak at 531.69 eV, suggesting the formation of the Ti-O-Ru bond in Ru:c-TiO $ _2 $  ETL. In the case of Ru3d analysis, it is hard to notice Ru bands because of overlapping binding energies. Usually, both C1s and Ru 3d core levels cover the 278-289 eV region (Figure S7C). As a result, a weak Ru 3d core level signal is observed for the 1.5% Ru:c-TiO $ _2 $  sample, which stems from minimal Ru-doping concentration. Relatively weak core level Ru peaks appear at 280.7 (3d $ _{5/2} $ ) and 284.6 eV (3d $ _{3/2} $ ), suggesting a Ru $ ^{4+} $  oxidation state. To trace Ru-doping, we further recorded the XPS spectrum of the 3% Ru:c-TiO $ _2 $  ETL (Figure S7D and Figure S8). For 3% Ru:c-TiO $ _2 $ , the spin-orbit splitting ( $ \Delta $ E) was further widened up to 5.89 eV for the octahedral coordinated Ti $ ^{4+} $  state. The Ru 3d core level peaks appear at 280.7 and 284.6 eV, suggesting the presence of Ru $ ^{4+} $  states. $ ^{43} $  Due to different oxidation states of Ru, such as Ru $ ^{2+} $ , Ru $ ^{3+} $ , Ru $ ^{4+} $ , and Ru $ ^{5+} $ , it is sometimes difficult to analyze these states. $ ^{44} $  However, from our XPS analysis of the Ru:c-TiO $ _2 $  ETLs, the presence of less oxidized metals reveals the presence of Ti $ ^{3+} $  species within the TiO $ _2 $  matrix. This facilitates the higher percentage of loosely bound Ti $ ^{3+} $ -O bonds over Ti $ ^{4+} $ -O stronger bonds, which enhances the charge transport properties. # 4 | CONCLUSIONS

In summary, the   $  Ru^{4+}  $   cation-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs have been successfully prepared and employed in planar as well as mesoscopic normal n-i-p-type perovskite devices. In fully oxidized Ru-doped   $  TiO_{2}  $ ,   $  Ru^{4+}  $ -substituted   $  Ti^{4+}  $   sites-based ETL provided excellent optoelectronic properties and resulted in over 20% PCE. The Ru-modified   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs produced unique optoelectronic properties, which helped improve the   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF values of the PSC device effectively. The optimization of the Ru-doping level enhanced the PCE from 15.33% to 20.87% with improved stability of over 200 days. This approach is promising in improving the efficiency with negligible hysteresis due to an effective charge balance. We believe that Ru-doping would be a promising approach to develop PSCs with over 20% efficiency. Our study revealed that the precise   $  Ru^{4+}  $ -doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   could also be served as a potential ETL for PSCs instead of   $  H_{2}  $   production.

# 5.2 | Methods

# 5.2.1 | Preparation of c-TiO  $  _{2}  $   and Ru:c-TiO  $  _{2}  $   ETL

Laser-patterned FTO-coated glass substrates (TEC-8, Pilkington) were ultrasonically cleaned in an alkaline aqueous solution, rinsed with deionized water, acetone, and ethanol and then treated with UV-ozone for 15 min. Nearly 40–50-nm-thick compact blocking   $  TiO_{2}  $   (c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) layers were deposited on the substrates by first spin coating the   $  TiO_{2}  $   precursor and then annealing the spin-coated precursor in the air at 450°C for 30 min. The precursor composition was as follows: 1 ml commercial titanium isopropoxide solution (Sigma-Aldrich) that was diluted in ethanol containing 12 M HCl. Ruthenium doping was achieved by using ruthenium(III) chloride hydrate (  $  RuCl_{3}\cdot xH_{2}O  $  ) precursor addition in the above solution, followed by the same procedure. A nearly 180 nm mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was composed of the 30-nm-sized particles and deposited by spin coating a commercial   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste (Dyesol DSL-30NR-T, Dyesol) diluted in ethanol (6:1 weight ratio) at 4000 rotations per minute (RPM) for 20 s with a ramp rate of 2000 RPM s  $  ^{-1}  $  . After drying at 125°C, the   $  TiO_{2}  $   films were gradually heated to 500°C, baked at this temperature for 15 min, and then cooled to room temperature. The mesoporous deposited film was again treated with   $  TiCl_{4}  $ , followed by sintering. The Li treatment was achieved by spin coating of 200 μl of LiTFSI in acetonitrile (10 mg ml  $  ^{-1}  $  ) at 3000 RPM followed by sintering at 450°C for 30 min in the air. When the furnace temperature cooled to 150°C, the Li-treated mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   electrodes were transferred in the glove box for perovskite deposition.

# 5.2.2 | Synthesis of the perovskite precursors and their solution

The FAI and MABr were synthesized as per our reports.  $  ^{53}  $   Briefly, methylamine solution and formamidine acetate powder were separately reacted with hydroiodic acid (57% in water, Aldrich) and hydrobromic acid (HBr) for the preparation of MABr and FAI. The reactions were carried out in a 250 ml round-bottomed flask at 0°C for 2 h with stirring. The precipitates were recovered by evaporating the solutions at 60°C for 2 h. The products were dissolved in ethanol or methanol, recrystallized using diethyl ether, and finally dried at 60°C in a vacuum oven for 24 h. For FAPbX/MAPbX, the precursors were synthesized by reacting FAI, MABr, PbI  $  _{2}  $  , and PbBr  $  _{2}  $   powder with the desired amount in DMF/DMSO (4:1 v/v%) solution. The desired volume of CsI in DMSO was added in the above solution to make Cs  $  _{0.05}  $  FA  $  _{0.81}  $  MA  $  _{0.14}  $  PbI  $  _{2.55}  $  Br  $  _{0.45}  $  . # 5.2.3 | Preparation of PTAA-based HTM

The hole transport material (HTM) was prepared as per our previous report.  $  ^{[53]}  $   In brief, 15 milligrams of a poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA, Sigma Aldrich) dissolved in 1.5 ml toluene (99.8%, Aldrich) with addition of 15   $  \mu  $  l bis(trifluoromethane) sulfonimide lithium salt (LiTFSI, 99.95%, Aldrich) (170 mg ml  $  ^{-1}  $  ) in acetonitrile and 7.5   $  \mu  $  l 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP, 96%, Aldrich). The prepared PTAA HTM solution was spin-coated on the FTO/Ru:c-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /perovskite electrode at 3000 RPM for 30 s. Then, the substrates were transferred to a vacuum chamber and evacuated to a pressure of 2 × 10  $  ^{-6}  $   mbar. For the counter electrode, 80-nm-thick Au contacts were deposited on the top of the HTM over layer by thermal evaporation (growth rate ~0.5 Å s  $  ^{-1}  $  ). The active area of this electrode was fixed at 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  . An active area was calculated as per the gold and laser-pattern cross-sectional area. The exact illumination to the active area was fixed by attaching a metal shadow mask from the back side during measurements.

# 5.2.4 | Characterizations

The top-surface and cross-sectional morphological images were recorded by a field-emission SEM (FESEM; S-4700, Hitachi). XRD measurements were carried out using a D/MAX Ultima III XRD spectrometer (Rigaku, Japan) with Cu Kα line of 1.5410 Å. The surface topographies of Ru:c-TiO₂ and perovskite thin films were analyzed using AFM (XE-100 Advanced Scanning Probe Microscope, Park Systems). The chemical states of the deposited samples were analyzed using an X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (XPS) (VG Multilab 2000-Thermo Scientific, USA, K-Alpha) with a multichannel detector, which can endure high photonic energies from 0.1 to 3 keV. The UPS measurements were carried out using a Thermo VG Scientific Sigma Probe, UK, UV Mono-chromatic He I (21.22 eV) gas, (Bias -5.0 V, Aperture size 8 mm adjustable) for UPS base UHV pressure at 1 × 10⁻⁹ mbar without surface treatment. The PL spectra were measured using a PL spectrometer (f = 0.5 m, Acton Research Co., Spectrograph 500i, USA) and an intensified CCD (PI-MAX3) (Princeton Instrument Co., IRY1024, USA). The DPSS (diode-pumped solid-state) laser (Ekspla) with a wavelength of 266 nm and a power of 310 mW was utilized as an excitation source for PL measurement. TRPL decay transients were measured at 750 ± 20 nm using excitation with a 470 nm light pulse at a frequency of 5 MHz from the Spectrophotometer F-7000. All PL and TRPL measurements were done in ambient conditions and excitation used from the perovskite layer.

# 5.2.5 | Photovoltaic studies

The cells were illuminated using a solar simulator at AM 1.5 G for 10 s, for which the light intensity was adjusted to 1 sun intensity (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) through the use of an NREL-calibrated Si solar cell with a KG-5 filter. The spectral response was taken by a spectral IPCE measurement system (K3100, McScience), which was equipped with a monochromator, a K240 XE 300 lamp source connected with K401 OLS XE300W lamp Power supply, and a K102 signal amplifier. Prior to the use of the light, the spectral response and the light intensity were calibrated using a Si-photodiode (Model: S1337-1010BQ) and InGaAs photodiode (model: G12180-050A) for 300–1100 nm and 1100–1400 nm calibration, respectively. Measurements were taken in EQE mode.

# 5.2.6 | Device stability

The device stability was tested in the air, and after each measurement, the devices were stored in the dark without any encapsulation.
