Lead-free tin perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have emerged as a promising candidate toward high-performance and eco-friendly photovoltaic technology with great potential for future application. However, tin PSCs with over 10% efficiency usually feature an organic hole transport layer (HTL) at the illumination side that may induce device degradation during long-term operation. Removing the unstable organic HTL is an important way to solve these stability issues, but the efficiency of HTL-free tin PSCs is still much lower than that of the completed cells. Herein, it is demonstrated that formamidinium tin iodide doped with heterogeneous ammonium salts can form an upward band-bending structure to selectively extract the hole in the HTL-free devices. By using this band-bending structure, a promising efficiency of over 10% is first achieved for the lead-free PSCs with a HTL-free structure. More importantly, the optimized cell is highly stable, keeping 95% and 90% of the initial efficiency after continuous light soaking for 40 days and 80 °C annealing for 300 h, respectively. This work paves a route toward the development of efficient, eco-friendly, and highly stable perovskite photovoltaics.

Perovskites solar cells (PSCs) emerging as a promising third-generation photovoltaic (PV) technology show a rapid growth of power conversion efficiency (PCE) in recent years,  $  ^{[1-6]}  $  but the

However, most of the high-performance tin PSCs with a PCE of over 10% are based on an inverted structure using organic poly(3,4ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) as the hole transport layer (HTL), which may cause the PCE loss during long-term operation because this organic material at the illumination side are unstable when exposed to UV light or thermal stress.  $  ^{[25-27]}  $   On the other hand, the commonly-used inorganic HTL, nickel oxide (NiO  $  _{x}  $  ), with a high density of Ni  $  ^{3+}  $   could accept the electrons from tin perovskites and accelerate the oxidation of Sn  $  ^{2+}  $   to Sn  $  ^{4+}  $  , resulting in a poor stability of NiO  $  _{x}  $  -based tin PSCs.  $  ^{[28,29]}  $   Until now, a suitable hole transport material with excellent stability for developing the highly efficient tin PSCs is still missing. Therefore, the HTL-free devices should be an important choice to solve the potential stability issues.

The HTL-free structure was first employed in the lead PSCs to avoid the use of typical 2,2,7,7-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9-bifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) with moisture-sensitive lithium salts that can accelerate the device degradation in air.  $  ^{[30,31]}  $   For example, researchers reported a regular HTL-free PSC with a structure of FTO/TiO  $  _{2}  $  /ZrO  $  _{2}  $  +perovskite/carbon electrode and achieved a certified PCE of 12.8% with a large improvement of air stability.  $  ^{[32,33]}  $   Besides, HTL-free structure was also applied to address the long-term

# 2. Results and Discussion

As shown in Figure 1a, the device configuration used for HTL-free tin PSCs was ITO/FASnI $ _3 $ -based perovskite/PCBM/BCP/Ag. Simply, a layer of FASnI $ _3 $  perovskite was deposited on ITO glass to study the impact of interface band alignment in HTL-free device, while methylammonium iodide (MAI) and ethylenediammonium diiodide (EDAI $ _2 $ ) were employed as additives to improve the crystallinity and passivate the surface defect states of tin perovskite films. We first used the scanning electron microscope (SEM) to study the impact of MAI and   $  EDAI_{2}  $   additives on the top-view morphology of   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskites (Figure S1, Supporting Information). We found that the pure   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite showed many pinholes with a low coverage on the ITO substrate, while the   $  FASnI_{3}  $   film with 5 mol% MAI additive alone exhibited an increased grain size and less pinholes.  $  ^{[39]}  $   By contrast, the   $  FASnI_{3}  $   film with 1 mol%   $  EDAI_{2}  $   alone exhibited a pinhole-free morphology but a decreased grain size, owing to the increase of the nucleation sites induced by the divalent A-site cations, which has been investigated by the previous studies.  $  ^{[40]}  $   Finally, a pinhole-free tin perovskite film with a large grain size of   $  \approx1\mu m  $   could be fabricated after the incorporation of both MA and EDA cations. Such compact morphology was further confirmed by the cross-section SEM analysis (Figure 1b), it can be found that a 170 nm-thick   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite layer with MA and EDA doping fully covered the smooth ITO glass. This compact   $  FASnI_{3}  $ -based layer contacting with ITO can facilitate effective charge separation and prevent the interface non-radiative recombination, which is quite important for achieving high-performance HTM-free devices.  $  ^{[41]}  $

To further investigate the crystallization of   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -based perovskite on different substrates, X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -based perovskite films coated on ITO and PEDOT:PSS were measured for comparison. A series of diffraction peaks at   $  2\theta=14.0^{\circ}  $  ,   $  24.4^{\circ}  $  ,   $  28.2^{\circ}  $  , and   $  31.7^{\circ}  $   were detected for both ITO/PEDOT:PSS/FASnI  $  _{3}  $  -based perovskite and ITO/FASnI  $  _{3}  $  -based perovskite samples, associated with the (100), (102), (200), and (122) crystal planes of the orthorhombic Sn perovskite phase, respectively (Figure 1c).  $  ^{[42]}  $   Besides, we also measured the UV–vis absorption spectrum of   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -based perovskite on ITO and PEDOT:PSS substrates (Figure 1d), it can be found that both two samples showed the similar absorption edge of   $  \approx900  $   nm. The above analysis indicates that removing the bottom PEDOT:PSS HTL has a negligible impact on the crystal orientation and absorption of tin perovskite layer. Furthermore, the bandgaps of the   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -based perovskite films were measured via the Tauc plot analysis (Figure S2, Supporting Information), we found that the incorporation of MAI and EDAI  $  _{2}  $   has a negligible impact on the bandgap of   $  FASnI_{3}  $   crystal. The bandgaps of pure   $  FASnI_{3}  $  ,   $  FASnI_{3}+MAI  $  ,   $  FASnI_{3}+EDAI_{2}  $  , and   $  FASnI_{3}+MAI+EDAI_{2}  $   samples were calculated to be 1.40 eV, 1.39 eV, 1.40 eV, and 1.39 eV, respectively.

Moreover, we also fabricated the HTL-free PSCs with a   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  -based perovskite layer to compare the device performance with HTL-free   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -based device. A compact   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  -based perovskite film with the grain size of 600–800 nm can be found in Figure S3, Supporting Information. Moreover, Figures S4 and S5, Supporting Information show the UV–vis absorption spectrum and Tauc plot of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  -based perovskite directly coated on ITO substrate, respectively. The   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  -based perovskite shows an absorption edge of about 790 nm and an optical bandgap of 1.61 eV.

To further reveal the energy-level alignment at the interface between perovskite layers and ITO electrode in HTL-free devices, we studied the energy-level diagram formed at the contact of  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $ -based perovskite/ITO or  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $ -based perovskite/ITO. The work-function of ITO and the valence band maximum (VBM) of  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $ -based perovskite

and   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  -based perovskite were measured to be -4.68 eV, -4.83 eV, and -5.61 eV via photoelectron spectroscopy, respectively (Figure 2a). Moreover, the valence band X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectrum of   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -based perovskite and   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  -based perovskite were measured (Figure 2b), from which we can derive the Fermi level of Sn perovskite and Pb perovskites to be 0.54 eV and 0.44 eV above the VBM positions, respectively. To understand the doping effect of MAI and   $  EDAI_{2}  $   on the band structure of   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskites, we also measured the valence band (Figure S6a, Supporting Information) and Fermi level (Figure S6b, Supporting Information) of the pure   $  FASnI_{3}  $ ,   $  FASnI_{3}  $  +MAI, and   $  FASnI_{3}  $  +  $  EDAI_{2}  $   perovskite films, and then drew the schematic band structure of corresponding samples in Figure S6c, Supporting Information. We found that MAI will cause a slightly downshift of the conduction and valence band position of   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite and the   $  EDAI_{2}  $   will cause a slightly upshift of the band position. However, the incorporation of both MA and EDA cations has a negligible impact on the band position and the Fermi level of   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite film.

Based on the defined VBM position and Fermi level, we can derive the energy-level diagram at the Sn perovskite/ITO and Pb perovskite/ITO interface, as can be seen in Figure 2c,d, respectively. In the case of FASnI $ _3 $ -based perovskite, the Fermi level of FASnI $ _3 $ -based perovskite  $ (-4.29\mathrm{eV}) $  is shallower than the work function of ITO electrode  $ (-4.68\mathrm{eV}) $ . After contacting, the electrons on the bottom surface of FASnI $ _3 $ -based perovskite would transfer to the ITO to create an equilibrium condition of Fermi level, then a hole-rich depletion layer would appear at the bottom of Sn perovskite layer, resulting in an upward band-bending structure at the contact area to selectively extract the photo-generated hole from FASnI $ _3 $  absorber to the ITO while block the transfer of electron or the back flow of from ITO. This upward band-bending effect can effectively suppress the electron-hole recombination at the interface between FASnI $ _3 $ -based perovskite and ITO glass, which was also consistent with the previous reports on HTL-free PSCs with the assistance of surface dipole to achieve a high PV performance.[43] In contrast, a downward band-bending structure is formed at the interface between Pb perovskite and ITO due to the much deeper Fermi level of FAPbI $ _3 $ -based perovskite materials  $ (-5.18\mathrm{eV}) $  than the ITO work function, as depicted in Figure 2d. Such downward band-bending may undesirably obstacle the hole collection process and accelerates the flow of electron to the electrode (n-type contact), leading a serious charge recombination in the inverted-type HTL-free PSCs.

The stability is another big issue to be addressed for tin PSCs. In this work, we tested the stability of the   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -based PSCs with and without HTL under both illumination and thermal-aging conditions. As can be seen in Figure 4a, under 1-sun illumination, the PSCs without HTL showed a promising stability, maintaining over 95% of their initial efficiency after being aged for 40 days in a   $  N_{2}  $   glovebox. By contrast, the PCE of PEDOT:PSS-based device decreased by 38% after the aging process. This phenomenon indicates that the PEDOT:PSS HTL is unstable and could cause the degradation of tin PSCs under illumination although the oxygen could be prevented completely. Furthermore, we found that the tin PSCs with a PEDOT:PSS HTL decayed to about 72% of its initial efficiency after 300-h aging at 80 °C while the HTL-free device was able to retain over 90% of the initial value at the same conditions.

Based on this result, we further revealed the thermal-induced degradation mechanism of   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -based perovskite on PEDOT:PSS substrates. We first studied the XRD pattern of the   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -based perovskite films coated on ITO/PEDOT:PSS or ITO before and after thermal aging in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere. According to Figure S9, Supporting Information, we found that the (100) main diffraction peaks of both two Sn perovskite samples showed the similar intensities after thermal annealing at 80 °C for about 300 h, indicating that the crystallinity of   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -based perovskite film was negligibly affected by the PEDOT:PSS HTL under thermal stress. On the other hand, we conducted the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) measurement to compare the PL quenching process at the PEDOT:PSS/Sn perovskite and ITO/Sn perovskite interfaces before and after annealing. As shown in Figure 4c,d, a similar PL quenching ratio was found in the fresh samples with (45%) or without (42%) a PEDOT:PSS HTL compared to the quartz-based control film, indicating a good charge extraction ability at both PEDOT:PSS/perovskite and ITO/perovskite interfaces. However, after annealing at 80 °C for 300 h, the PL quenching ratio at the PEDOT:PSS/Sn perovskite interface significantly

dropped to about 19% while the PL quenching ratio of ITO/Sn perovskite interface maintained the initial value. Therefore, we deduced that the large efficiency loss in PEDOT:PSS-containing tin PSCs is attributed to the reduction of hole-collection efficiency at the contact area under thermal stress, which may be induced by the chemical degradation of the PEDOT:PSS HTL at a high annealing temperature.  $  ^{[49]}  $

# 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, we developed an HTL-free mixed-cation tin PSCs with an upward band-bending structure at the contact between tin perovskite and ITO electrode to promote the hole-collection process and block the interface electron-hole recombination. As a result, the HTL-free device with an inverted structure showed a record PCE up to 10.11% and kept about 90% of the initial value after thermal aging at 80 °C for 300 h. More importantly, this work paves a new way to make large area tin PSCs in the future since it can relieve the increasing of series resistance due to HTLs when enlarging the device area, which pushes forward the large-scale production of lead-free tin PSCs toward the future commercialization.

Tin PSC Fabrication: ITO glass substrates were patterned and cleaned via sequential ultrasonication acetone and isopropanol for (10 min each). Then the substrates were treated with UV light and ozone for 10 min. To prepare the perovskite precursor solution, we dissolved FAI, MAI, EDAI $ _2 $ , SnI $ _2 $ , and SnF $ _2 $  at a molar ratio of 0.93:0.05:0.01:1:0.1 in solutions of DMSO with a total concentration of 0.9 M. The perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated onto ITO glass at 1000 rpm for 10 s and 5000 rpm for 50 s, chlorobenzene (120 μL) was dripped onto the perovskite film 30 s after the rotation speed was increased to 5000 rpm. The resulting films were annealed at 100 °C for 12 min. PCBM and BCP layers were sequentially spin-coated on Sn perovskites layer, 80 nm Ag electrode were sequentially evaporated on BCP layer under high vacuum of 10 $ ^{-5} $  Pa. For tin PSC with HTL, PEDOT:PSS solution was spin-coated onto the ITO substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s, and the coated substrate was then annealed at 150 °C for 10 min in air before the deposition of Sn perovskite layer.

Lead PSC Fabrication: ITO glasses were cleaned same way, then   $  PbI_{2}  $  , FAI,   $  PbBr_{2}  $  , MABr, CsI, MACl (20 mol%) were dissolved in DMF and DMSO (5.5:1 v/v) with a chemical formula of   $  Cs_{0.05}(FA_{0.92}MA_{0.08})_{0.95}Pb(I_{0.92}Br_{0.08})_{3}  $   and concentration of 1.4 M. The solution was stirred overnight. 110   $  \mu  $  L of perovskite solution was spin-coated on the ITO glass at 1000 and 5000 rpm for 10 and 30 s, respectively. 200   $  \mu  $  L EA was dripped onto the film about 20 s at second spin-coating step, then the perovskite film was annealed at 100 °C for 30 min. PCBM and BCP layers were sequentially spin-coated on perovskites layer. Finally, 80 nm top Ag electrode was thermally evaporated on BCP layer. For lead PSCs with HTL, 2 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   PTAA toluene solution was spin-coated onto the cleaned ITO at 5000 rpm for 30 s, and the substrate was then annealed at 100 °C for 10 min before deposition of the Pb perovskite layer.

UV–vis spectra were obtained with a Shimadzu UV–vis 3600 spectrophotometer. Photoelectron spectroscopies were measured by means of PESA (Rikenkeiki AC-3). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy was measured by PHI Quantera SXM (ULVAC-PHI) with X-ray source of Al Kα (mono). J-V curves were measured by using a solar simulator with standard air mass 1.5 sunlight (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , WXS-155S-10, Wacom Denso). The light intensity of the solar simulator was calibrated with a standard silicon solar cell.
