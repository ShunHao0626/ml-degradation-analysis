# GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT

# ABSTRACT

All-inorganic cesium lead halide perovskites (CsPbX $ _3 $ , X = Cl, I, Br) have attracted great attention for developing high-performance photovoltaics due to their superb thermal stability. However, the short electron-hole diffusion lengths ( $ L_{\text{D}s} $ ) in CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite are the primary limiting factor for further improvement of the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br solar cells. Herein, a simple yet effective method is reported to prolong the  $ L_{\text{D}s} $  in CsPbI $ _2 $ Br via codoping of Zn $ ^{2+} $  and acetate (Ac $ ^{-} $ ) ions (i.e., using Zn(Ac) $ _2 $  as dopant). It is demonstrated that Zn $ ^{2+} $  partially replaces Pb $ ^{2+} $  to form a more stable black phase, and Ac $ ^{-} $  coordinates with Pb $ ^{2+} $  to passivate the defects at the perovskite grain boundaries which reduces electron-hole recombination within the perovskite. As a result, the  $ L_{\text{D}s} $  of Zn(Ac) $ _2 $  doped CsPbI $ _2 $ Br are greater than 1  $ \mu $ m, which is the first report on all-inorganic perovskites. The champion PCE of the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br solar cells is drastically increased from 12.31% to 14.93% upon doping with Zn(Ac) $ _2 $ , stemming mainly from the increased open-circuit voltage and fill factor. Furthermore, the non-encapsulated devices with Zn(Ac) $ _2 $  doped CsPbI $ _2 $ Br show less than 10% efficiency loss after storage in dry air for 30 days.

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites, such as methylammonium lead triiodide (MAPbI $ _3 $ ) or formamidinium lead triiodide (FAPbI $ _3 $ ), have attracted much attention due to their unparalleled merits of wide light absorption, solution-processability, and low-cost fabrication [1–3]. In the past few years, power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of these perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have increased from 3.8% to 25.2%, which is close to the established photovoltaic (PV) technologies such as silicon and cadmium telluride [4,5]. Despite the rapid progress in PCE, the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites are prone to degradation at high temperatures (over 100 °C), owing to the volatility of the organic cations such as methylammonium ions (MA $ ^+ $ ) or formamidinium ions (FA $ ^+ $ ) [6]. Fortunately, the thermal stability of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials can be effectively improved via substituting the organic cations with cesium cations (Cs $ ^+ $ ), yielding inorganic cesium lead halide perovskites (CsPbX $ _3 $ ; X = I, Br, Cl, or mixed halides) [7,8].

CsPbX $ _3 $  have a problem on phase transition from cubic phase (black colored  $ \alpha $ -phase) to undesirable orthorhombic phase (yellow colored  $ \delta $ -phase) at room temperature and humid conditions, leading to reduced PCE and stability in PSCs [9–13]. This is because the cation radius of Cs $ ^+ $  is too small to hold [PbX $ _6 $ ] $ ^{4-} $  (X = Br, I) octahedra [8]. The phase stability of CsPbX $ _3 $  based perovskite is enhanced with increasing the proportion of Br in the total halide content; however, the PCEs of such PSCs are sacrificed in the meantime due to the widened bandgap [13]. Among the mixed-halide inorganic perovskites, CsPbI $ _2 $ Br that has a relatively narrow bandgap ( $ \sim $ 1.9 eV) and a good phase stability is a promising candidate after balancing the bandgap and phase stability. Nevertheless, it also suffers from a phase transition from black  $ \alpha $ -phase to yellow  $ \delta $ -phase under high humidity conditions [14]. Several strategies to improve the phase stability of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br have been proposed, including (i) treatment of the precursor PbI $ _2 $  using hydroiodic acid (HI) or dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) to form adducts of PbI $ _2 $ -HI [15] or PbI $ _2 $ -DMSO [16] in the precursor solution; (ii) partial replacement of Pb-site cations with smaller ions (Sn $ ^{2+} $  [17], Nb $ ^{5+} $  [18], Mn $ ^{2+} $  [14,19], In $ ^{3+} $  [20], Ge $ ^{2+} $  [21], Sr $ ^{2+} $  [22], Zn $ ^{2+} $  [23] or Eu $ ^{3+} $  [24]); (iii) passivation of the defects at CsPbI $ _2 $ Br grain boundaries with hydrophobic materials, e.g., phenethylamine (PEA) [25] or poly(3-hexylthiophene-2,5-diyl) (P3HT) [26]. Although the cubic phase stability in CsPbI $ _2 $ Br is improved using these strategies, the PCEs of these devices are still unsatisfactory, which mainly comes from the relatively low open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{OC} $ , typically < 1.25 V). To enhance the  $ V_{OC} $  and PCE, several approaches have been explored such as modification of device structure using an amino-functionalized polymer (PN4N) interlayer [27], and controlled perovskite growth with anti-solvent (chlorobenzene or isopropanol) and gradient thermal annealing (GTA) methods [28], achieving the  $ V_{OCs} $  of higher than 1.25 V and the PCEs of higher than 15%. However, the synthesis of interfacial materials is complicated and the anti-solvent method leads to poor reproducibility of the device performance. Therefore, it is urgently necessary to develop a simple, low-cost and highly reproducible approach for achieving stable and high-efficiency CsPbI $ _2 $ Br based PSCs.

In this work, we report a simple yet efficient method to fabricate high quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films with good phase stability by introducing a small amount of zinc acetate   $  (\mathrm{Zn}(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{COO})_{2}  $  , abbreviated as   $  \mathrm{Zn}(\mathrm{Ac})_{2}  $  ) into perovskite precursor solution. It is demonstrated that   $  Pb^{2+}  $   at the B-site is partially substituted by   $  Zn^{2+}  $  , and the defect sites of uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   at the perovskite grain boundaries are passivated by   $  Ac^{-}  $  group. Benefiting from the synergistic effect of   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $  , the   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films show long electron-hole diffusion lengths (  $  L_{D}s  $  ) greater than 1   $  \mu  $  m, allowing efficient charge transport and collection [29,30]. As a result, the PSCs based on   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   show outstanding performance compared with those based on pristine   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   owing to the striking improvement in   $  V_{OC}  $   and fill factor (FF). The best solar cells made with   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   give a champion PCE of 14.93%, which is much higher than that (12.31%) of

the solar cells with pristine   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  .

# 2.1. Synergistic effect of   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   on the crystal structure of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite

$ \mathrm{Zn^{2+}} $  effect. The absorbance spectra of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  films with and without various amounts of  $ \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2} $  are shown in Fig. S1. All the films exhibit almost overlapping spectra with absorption onset around 650 nm, which indicates that a small amount of  $ \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2} $  doping has a negligible effect on the absorption property of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $ . To evaluate the optical bandgaps of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  perovskites with and without  $ \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2} $  doping, we plotted  $ (Ahv)^2 $  as a function of  $ hv $ , where  $ A $  represents the absorption coefficient and  $ hv $  is the photon energy (Fig. S2). The bandgap of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  is gradually narrowed with increasing  $ \mathrm{Zn^{2+}} $  doping concentration from 0.5 to  $ 3\mathrm{mg / ml} $ , although the change is very small. The narrowing of bandgap is also verified by the redshift in the photoluminescence (PL) peak for  $ \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2} $  doped  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  film (Fig. S3). This indicates that  $ \mathrm{Zn^{2+}} $  ions are likely to substitute the  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2+}} $  sites and thereafter induce changes in the lattice parameter of the perovskite phase [24].

To testify this hypothesis, we measured the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films with and without   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doping. As illustrated in Fig. 1a, all the perovskite films exhibit two main diffraction peaks at   $  2\theta  $   of about   $  14.6^{\circ}  $   and   $  29.6^{\circ}  $ , corresponding to the (100) and (200) planes of the cubic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite phase, respectively [31]. This implies that the doping does not lead to the formation of other crystalline phases. Fig. 1b presents the corresponding magnified XRD patterns of perovskite film (100) plane. The (100) diffraction peaks gradually shift to higher angles with the increase of   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doping concentration, and their full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) values decrease initially then followed by a slight increment (Table S3). The d-spacing can be estimated using the Bragg's equation [18]. As the concentration of   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   increases from 0 to 1.5 mg/ml, the d-spacing decreases from 6.06 to 6.02 Å, which indicates the contraction of perovskite lattice volume [32]. Given that the ionic radius of   $  Zn^{2+}  $   (0.74 Å) is smaller than that of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   (1.19 Å), the contracted lattice volume is likely to result from the partial replacement of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   with   $  Zn^{2+}  $ , in agreement with what has been suggested by the absorption and PL measurements. To convince our speculation, the XRD patterns of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films doped with different concentrations of   $  ZnI_{2}  $   and   $  \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_{2}}  $   are shown in Fig. S4. The   $  ZnI_{2}  $   doping leads to a slight shift of the (100) peak toward a higher diffraction angle, while the   $  \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_{2}}  $   doping does not have a significant effect on the (100) peak position, which verifies our hypothesis on the origin of contracted crystal lattice. Based on the above analysis, it can be concluded that   $  Zn^{2+}  $   partially replaces   $  Pb^{2+}  $ , resulting in the lattice contraction and the stabilization of the black   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   structure via tuning of the tolerance factor.

# 2.3. Electron-hole  $  L_{D}  $ s of pristine and  $  Zn(Ac)_2  $  doped perovskites

To evaluate the effect of   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doping on the charge carrier extraction properties of the perovskite film, time-resolved PL (TRPL) measurements were performed on the pristine and   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films (Fig. 3a and b). The TRPL decay curves were fitted using a bi-exponential decay function, with the relevant key parameters summarized in Table 1 and Table S4. The average PL lifetime (  $  t_{ave}  $  ) of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film increases from 9.70 to 29.90 ns upon   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   incorporation, which suggests suppression of non-radiative recombination losses. This can be rationalized by considering the passivation of defects in   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , resulting from the   $  Pb^{2+}/Ac^{-}  $  interactions [36,37]. # 2.4. Trap-density in pristine and  $ \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2} $  doped CsPbI $ _2 $ Br

In order to assess the trap-density   $  (N_{\mathrm{I}})  $   in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films with and without   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doping, we performed dark J-V measurements on the hole-only devices based on these two perovskites. As shown in Fig. 3c, the dark J-V curves can be divided into three regions, namely the ohmic region, the trap-filled limit region and the trap-free space-charge limited current (SCLC) region [30]. The voltage between the ohmic and trap-filled regions is known as the trap-filled limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ), which is determined by the trap density (  $  N_{I}  $  ) [30]. Therefore, the   $  N_{I}  $   can be extracted from the dark J-V curves at   $  V = V_{TFL}  $  , according to the following equation:

$$
N _ {\mathrm{t}} = \frac {2 \varepsilon_ {0} \varepsilon_ {\mathrm{r}} V _ {\mathrm{TFL}}}{q d ^ {2}}
$$

# 2.5. Performance of PSCs based on pristine and   $  Zn(Ac)_{2}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $

Fig. 4a shows the schematic of the planar  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  PSCs with regular structure of ITO/ $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ / $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $ /PTAA/ $ \mathrm{MoO_3} $ /Au, and Fig. 4b displays the current density-voltage ( $ J-V $ ) characteristics of the PSCs based on pristine and  $ \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2} $  doped  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $ . The PSCs based on  $ \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2} $  doped  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  exhibit a champion PCE of  $ 14.93\% $ , which is significantly higher than that  $ (12.31\%) $  of the PSCs based on pristine  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $ . The PCE improvement is mainly contributed by the  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  and FF, arising from the reduced  $ N_{\mathrm{t}} $  and the prolonged electron and hole  $ L_{\mathrm{DS}} $  in the perovskite [39]. We also found that doping a suitable amount of  $ \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{ZnI_2} $  into  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  could enhance the  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  and FF, respectively (Figs. S9 and S10 and Tables S7 and S8). Further investigation reveals that  $ \mathrm{ZnI_2} $  doped  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  films show higher charge-carrier mobilities, and the  $ \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_2} $  doped  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  films exhibit lower  $ N_{\mathrm{s}} $  compared to undoped  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  films (Fig. S11 and Table S9). This suggests that proper  $ \mathrm{Zn^{2+}} $  and  $ \mathrm{Ac^-} $  doping can simultaneously increase charge-carrier mobility and decrease the trap-density in  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $ . As an aside, the PSC based on the  $ \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2} $  doped  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  shows relatively more serious hysteresis behavior than that based on the pristine  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  (Fig. S12 and Table S10), which might result from the severe ion migration of  $ \mathrm{Zn^{2+}} $  with smaller size [40].

To investigate the underlying mechanism of the improved   $  V_{OC}  $   for the PSCs with   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_2Br  $ , capacitance-voltage (  $  C^{-2}-V  $  ) measurements are employed to understand the change of built-in potential (  $  V_{bi}  $  ) in solar cells (Fig. S14). The   $  V_{bi}  $   is obtained by the formula of Mott-Schottky equation   $  C^{-2} = \frac{2(V_{\mathrm{Bi}}-V)}{S^2 q_{\mathrm{r},\varepsilon_0}N_A}  $ , where V, S, q,   $  \varepsilon_r  $ ,   $  \varepsilon_0  $   and   $  N_A  $   represent applied voltage, active area, elementary charge, relative permittivity, vacuum permittivity and charge-carrier concentration, respectively [28]. The   $  V_{bi}  $   of   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_2Br  $   PSCs is estimated to be 1.16 V, which is higher than that (1.09 V) of pristine   $  CsPbI_2Br  $   PSCs. The enhanced   $  V_{bi}  $   in the   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2}  $   doped PSCs means an increased driving force for separation and transportation of the photogenerated charge carriers as well as a more effective suppression of the electron-hole recombination at the depletion region of the device, which leads to an increase of   $  V_{OC}  $   in the   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2}  $   doped PSCs [41]. Furthermore, the increased   $  V_{OC}  $   can also be explained using the formula of   $  V_{OC} = \frac{mRT}{F} \ln\left(\frac{J_{\mathrm{SC}}}{J_0} - 1\right)  $ , where m, R, F,   $  J_{SC}  $   and   $  J_0  $   represent ideality factor, ideal gas constant, faraday constant, short-circuit current density and dark current density, respectively [42]. The   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_2Br  $   PSC exhibits a lower reverse leakage current than the pristine   $  CsPbI_2Br  $   PSC, as shown in the dark J-V curves (Fig. S15). Therefore, the lower   $  J_0  $   and higher   $  J_{SC}  $   lead to the increased   $  V_{OC}  $   in the   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_2Br  $   device.

For the  $ \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2} $  doped  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  PSCs, the lowered  $ J_{0} $  suggests a reduction of recombination loss due to passivation of defects, which is beneficial for improving device performance [43]. The types of recombination channels are further investigated by measuring  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  and  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  as a function of light intensity ( $ P_{\mathrm{light}} $ ). Fig. S16 presents the  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  versus  $ P_{\mathrm{light}} $  for the devices based on pristine and  $ \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2} $  doped  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $ .  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  has a logarithmic dependence on  $ P_{\mathrm{light}} $  with a slope of  $ nk_{\mathrm{B}}T / q $ , where  $ n $ ,  $ k_{\mathrm{B}} $  and  $ T $  are the ideality factor, Boltzmann constant and absolute temperature, respectively. The smaller slope (1.16  $ k_{\mathrm{B}}T / q $ ) for the  $ \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_2} $  doped

# 2.6. Stability of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films and solar cells with and without   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doping

To investigate the effects of   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doping on the phase stability of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , we exposed the pristine and   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films in humid air (40–50% relative humidity (RH), 25 °C) (Fig. 5a). The color of the pristine   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film quickly becomes transparent within an hour, indicating poor stability of the cubic phase against humidity. In contrast, the   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film remains the original color with a slight color fading on the edge within the same period. This indicates that doping   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   into   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   can enhance the phase stability of the films. Furthermore, long-term stability of the non-encapsulated devices with pristine and   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   is tested in dry air (15–20% RH, 25 °C), as shown in Fig. 5b. # 3. Experimental

# 3.1. Device fabrication

ITO glass substrates (CSG Holding Co., Ltd,  $ 10\Omega \mathrm{sq}^{-1} $ ) were first treated with ultra-sonication in detergent, acetone, isopropanol and ethanol, followed by treatment in ultraviolet-ozone for  $ 15\mathrm{min} $  after drying in  $ \mathrm{N}_2 $  stream.  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  colloidal solution (Alfa Aesar,  $ 15\mathrm{wt}\% $  in  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $ ) was diluted to  $ 2.5\mathrm{wt}\% $  by deionized (DI) water and mixed with  $ 0.5\mathrm{wt}\% $  ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA, TCI,  $ 98\% $ ) to enhance the electrical conductivity [46]. The prepared  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  colloidal solution was spin-coated onto the UVO-treated ITO substrates (3000 rpm for  $ 30~s $ ), followed by a thermal annealing at  $ 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $ .

The pristine   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution was prepared by dissolving 208 mg CsI (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.9%), 184 mg of   $  PbI_{2}  $   (TCI, 98%) and 148 mg of   $  PbBr_{2}  $   (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.999%) in 1 ml of N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) (Sigma-Aldrich, anhydrous, 99.8%) and DMSO (Sigma-Aldrich, anhydrous, 99.9%) (4:1, volume/volume), before stirring for 2 h (90 °C). For   $  Zn(Ac)_{2}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   samples, the precursor solution was prepared by dissolving 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, and 3 mg   $  Zn(Ac)_{2}  $   (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.999%) into 1 ml pristine perovskite solution, and then stirring for 2 h (at 60 °C). The perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated at 2500 rpm for 30 s (at 55 °C). Afterwards, the as-cast precursor films were annealed at 180 °C for 10 min. The hole-transport layer PTAA is prepared by dissolving 15 mg PTAA (Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.) and 0.75 mg DPI-TPFB (TCI) in 1 ml chlorobenzene (CB, Sigma-Aldrich, anhydrous, 99.8%) and spin-coated onto the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite thin films (3000 rpm, 30 s). Finally, all the above-prepared films were evaporated with a 5 nm thick   $  MoO_{3}  $   as interlayer as well as the Al electrode (120 nm) at base pressure of   $  9.0 \times 10^{-5}  $   Pa at growth rate of 0.1 and 1 Å/s, respectively. The photoactive area for the PSC devices was defined to be 0.04 cm² using a shadow mask. The hole-only devices have a structure of ITO/NiOₓ/Perovskite/PTAA/Al, which is fabricated by the process similar to PSCs except for NiOₓ. The layer of NiOₓ is prepared by dissolving 0.5 M Ni(NO₃)₂·6H₂O (Aladdin, 99.99%) in ethylene glycol with a small amount of ethylenediamine, and spin-coated onto the ITO substrate at 4000 rpm for 90 s, followed by thermal annealing at 300 °C for 1 h.

# 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, in setting out to prolong the electron-hole   $  L_{pS}  $   of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , we perform a systematic study on the effects of codoping   $  Zn^{2+}  $   and   $  Ac^{-}  $  into   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   (i.e., using   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   as dopant). By the effects of   $  Zn^{2+}  $   and   $  Ac^{-}  $ , we have achieved long electron/hole   $  L_{D}  $   over 1   $  \mu  $  m that is comparable to organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite. The   $  Zn^{2+}  $   partially replaces the position of   $  Pb^{2+}  $ , which is beneficial to enhance the crystallization of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film and to stabilize its cubic phase by increasing the tolerance factor. Additionally, the   $  Ac^{-}  $  can coordinate with uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   to passivate the defects at the perovskite grain boundaries. As a result, compact and uniform   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films are obtained upon   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   incorporation, and the resulting devices achieve a champion PCE of 14.93%. Moreover, these devices demonstrate better stability in comparison to the pristine   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   device. The   $  \mathrm{Zn(Ac)_{2}}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC maintains more than 90% of its initial PCE within 30 days in dry air, while the pristine   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC only retains 70% of its initial PCE under the same conditions.
