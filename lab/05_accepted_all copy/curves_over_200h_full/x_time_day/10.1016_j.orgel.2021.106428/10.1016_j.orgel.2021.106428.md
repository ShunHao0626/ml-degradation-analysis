# 1. Introduction

The organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials are fostering intensive interest and have marked as a milestone in the perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) recently for their advantages such as broad absorption spectra, tunable band gap, excellent bipolar charge transport, long charge diffusion length, and low cost solution processability   $  [1-4]  $  . The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PVSCs have been raised from 3.8% to over 25% most recently   $  [5]  $  , surpassing that of polycrystalline silicon solar cells and making the PVSCs potential to be a low cost technology for renewable energy production. The PVSC device structure can be majorly classified as conventional (n-i-p) and inverted (p-i-n)

This paper mainly discusses the structure-property relationship of two donor-acceptor-donor (D–π-A–π-D) type dopant-free hole transporting materials (HTMs) (TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ) comprising primarily of 2,4,6-trisubstituted pyridine as the acceptor core and 4,4'-dimethoxytriphenylamine as the peripheral donor groups and their use in p-i-n perovskite solar cells (PVSCs). Compared to inferior TPA-Py-PTZ, TPA-TPy has a superior hole extraction and hole transport at the HTM/perovskite interface. The pinhole-free, smooth and dense, fully covered and well-crystallized MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskite layer on TPA-TPy reduces the carrier recombination and substantially improves the short circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ), open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ), and the fill-factor (FF) of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  PVSCs. The PVSC employing TPA-TPy as HTM exhibits a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 15.33% with a  $  J_{sc}  $  of 23.69 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , a  $  V_{oc}  $  of 0.95 V, and a FF of 68.10%. Especially, both TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ PVSCs exhibit a better moisture stability than that of NiOx PVSCs. It is because of the hydrophobic nature of TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ, which enables the formation of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskite layer having a larger grain-size, a less grain boundary, and a less infiltration of moisture.

# 3.2. Energy levels determination

The energy level of the highest occupied molecular orbitals (  $  E_{HOMO}  $  ) of TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ and the work function of   $  NiO_{x}  $   (which is used as HTM in the standard device in the present work) was measured by photoelectron spectroscopy (PES) and the spectra are shown in Fig. S10.

The energy of the lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals  $ (E_{\mathrm{LUMO}}) $  was calculated by using the  $ E_{\mathrm{LUMO}} = E_{\mathrm{HOMO}} + E_{\mathrm{g}} $  where  $ E_{\mathrm{g}} $  is the optical bandgap energy determined from the thin film absorption onset wavelength. The relevant data are summarized in Table S1. The estimated  $ E_{\mathrm{HOMO}} $  are  $ -5.23 $  and  $ -5.34\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ E_{\mathrm{LUMO}} $  are  $ -2.85 $  and  $ -3.05\mathrm{eV} $  for TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ, respectively. The energy level alignment of the HTM and other materials are illustrated in Fig. S11.

Such results indicate that the HOMO energy level of TPA-TPy is higher than the work function  $ (-5.31\mathrm{eV}) $  of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  but TPA-Py-PTZ has a lower HOMO energy level than the work function of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ . In terms of such energy levels, we can infer that TPA-TPy should be a better hole extraction material than  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  regarding  $ \mathrm{MAPbI_3} $  perovskite, whereas TPA-Py-PTZ is not.

Nevertheless, since the PCE of three PVSCs is 6.41%, 15.33%, and 18.56% for TPA-Py-PTZ, TPA-TPy, and NiO  $  _{x}  $  HTM, respectively, in this study (see 3.6 Photovoltaic performances of inverted PVSCs), there must be some other factors more than just the HOMO energy level or work function of HTM influencing the PCE of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  PVSCs. On the other hand, the LUMO energy levels of both TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ are considerably higher than -3.93 eV, the LUMO energy level of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskite. The electron blocking from reaching the anode of inverted MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  PVSCs by TPA-TPy or TPA-Py-PTZ HTM should be effective.

# 3.4. Thermal property, hole mobility, and MAPbI$_{3}$ PL quenching studies

The results of thermogravimetric analyses (TGA) of TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ are shown in Fig. S13 and Table S1. Both molecules exhibit a relatively good thermal stability, a 15% weight loss at 411 °C and

391 °C for TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ, respectively. However, a 5% weight loss was observed at 250 °C and 184 °C for TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ, respectively. Such a 5% weight loss is attributed to the less thermally stable 4,4'-dimethoxytriphenylamine groups. More importantly, from differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) characterization (Fig. 2a and b), TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ show a glass transition temperature (  $  T_{g}  $  ) of 103 °C and 81 °C, respectively (Fig. 2a and b, Table S1). Such measured   $  T_{g}  $  s, one below and the other above 100 °C, are critical for the PCE of fabricated inverted MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   PVSCs, of which the thermal annealing process is conducted at ~100 °C. Such an annealing temperature may create a problem for TPA-Py-PTZ HTM in generating inhomogeneous thin film morphology, which deteriorates the interfacial contact with MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   layer. This is one of several other reasons that PCE of TPA-Py-PTZ-based PVSCs is much worse than TPA-TPy-based PVSCs.

The hole mobilities of TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ were measured by space charge limited current (SCLC) method using a hole only device with an architecture of FTO/PEDOT:PSS/HTM/Au. The current density-voltage  $ (J - V) $  curves of TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ are displayed in Fig. 3. By fitting the results to a space charge limited form, the hole mobilities were calculated using Mott-Gurney equation,  $ J = 98^{-1}\varepsilon \varepsilon_0\mu_{\mathrm{h}}V^2 L^{-3} $ , where  $ J $  is the current density,  $ \varepsilon $  is the relative permittivity of the material,  $ \varepsilon_0 $  is the permittivity of free space,  $ \mu_{\mathrm{h}} $  is the hole mobility,  $ V $  is the applied voltage, and  $ L $  is the thickness of the thin films.

The obtained hole mobility values for TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ are  $ 3.02 \times 10^{-4} $  and  $ 1.35 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~cm}^2 \mathrm{~V}^{-1} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} $ , respectively, which are somewhat lower than that of  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  ( $ 8.73 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~cm}^2 \mathrm{~V}^{-1} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} $ ) [17]. Such a hole mobility order is in parallel to the order of PCE of three HTM PVSCs (see 3.6 Photovoltaic performances of inverted MAPbI₃ PVSCs). Nevertheless, we infer that a longer π-conjugation structure of D-π-A-π-D enabling a higher hole mobility of TPA-TPy than TPA-Py-PTZ. This is one of several other reasons that PCE of TPA-Py-PTZ-based PVSCs is much worse than TPA-TPy-based PVSCs.

To gain insights into the hole extraction (and hence the interfacial contact) of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  by the three HTM, the steady-state photoluminescence (ssPL) studies were carried out. A sample of FTO/HTM/perovskite was used to measure the steady-state PL quenching. As a comparing standard, the pristine  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite on FTO exhibits an intense emission peak at  $ 780~\mathrm{nm} $ . A significantly quenched  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  PL has been observed when a layer of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ , TPA-TPy, or TPA-Py-PTZ is inserted in between FTO and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  (Fig. 4).

The PL quenching ratio of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  on  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ , TPA-TPy, and TPA-Py-PTZ is  $ 91\% $ ,  $ 91\% $ , and  $ 82\% $ , respectively. From such ssPL results, we can infer that the exciton dissociation of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer due to the proximity of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  or TPA-TPy is similarly efficient, whereas it is relatively less efficient for TPA-Py-PTZ. A better interfacial contact between TPA-TPy and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  (than TPA-Py-PTZ and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ ) may facilitate a stronger PL quenching, which is in accordance with a longer  $ \pi $ -conjugation and a more planar molecular shape of TPA-TPy, compared with TPA-Py-PTZ. Since the PL quenching of FTO/TPA-TPy/MAPbI₃ is virtually same as that of FTO/NiOₓ/MAPbI₃, it suggests that TPA-TPy should be a better choice (than TPA-Py-PTZ) for HTM as a replacement for NiOₓ in the inverted PVSCs.

# 3.7. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS)

To elucidate the interfacial charge extraction and recombination processes, the EIS studies have been carried out. Fig. 8 illustrates the Nyquist plots of PVSCs FTO/HTM/MAPbI $ _3 $ /PC $ _{61} $ BM/BCP/Ag, which were measured at bias voltage of 0.7 V. In theory, there are three arcs (or semicircles) in the Nyquist plots which can be fitted with an equivalent circuit shown in Fig. 8. As many other EIS studies, a semicircle in the relatively high frequency region, which is usually too small to be seen in the plots compared to two other semicircles, associates with the series resistance of the external wires in the circuit ( $ R_s $ ). Two other semicircles, which are readily visible in the Nyquist plots, are in the frequency range of 0.1 KHz to 100 KHz. One of the two semicircles, in the intermediate frequency region, associates with the resistant of charge recombination resistance ( $ R_{\text{rec}} $ ) in the photoactive layer. The other semicircle in the lower frequency region is due to the resistant of dielectric relaxation ( $ R_{\text{dt}} $ ), meaning the charge rearrangement from disordered distribution to an ordered distribution and the dipole generation in solar cells [22, 23]. The corresponding fitted parameters are listed in Table 3.

According to our results, both  $ \mathrm{R_{rec}} $  and  $ \mathrm{R_{dr}} $  of devices have a similar trend following an order of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x > TPA-TPy > TPA-Py-PTZ} $ . It reveals that the devices based on  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  and TPA-TPy not only have a reduced charge recombination and less defects in perovskite layers and at the

interfaces, but also have a retarded dielectric relaxation and a reduced charge accumulation, improving the charge separation of photogenerated perovskite excitons, which is in accordance with hysteresis results from the study. Accordingly, the worse   $  R_{rec}  $   of TPA-Py-PTZ device compared with that of   $  NiO_{x}  $   and TPA-TPy devices increases the rate of charge recombination which lowers the   $  V_{OC}  $  . Within the context, the worse   $  R_{dr}  $   of TPA-Py-PTZ device decreases the   $  J_{SC}  $   and the FF of the devices.

# 3.8. Stability test of MAPbI$_{3}$ PVSCs having NiO$_{x}$ or organic HTMs

Besides the PCE, a long-term stability of PVSC is another major issue when it comes to commercialization. Therefore, stabilities of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  PVSCs based on TPA-TPy or TPA-Py-PTZ HTM were tested under conditions of 40–60% relative humidity. The PVSCs were stored without encapsulation in the dark at room temperature for 40 days. The stability results of the devices measured at regular intervals are shown in Fig. 9 and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters are given in Tables S8–S10.

MAPbI $ _3 $  PVSCs based on TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ maintains  $ \sim 90\% $  of their initial PCE over 8 days of testing period. Apparently, the TPA-TPy device retains  $ \sim 50\% $  of its initial PCE over 40 days outperforming TPA-Py-PTZ ( $ \sim 25\% $ ) and NiO $ _x $  ( $ \sim 30\% $ ) devices. Interestingly, in the period of day 21 to day 28 of testing, the regular measurement was interrupted because of the COVID-19 pandemic and the laboratory lockdown. All the testing devices were kept in a moisture-proof cabinet (with  $ \sim 40\% $  relative humidity) without exposure to light or heat. On the day 29, the stability testing resumed. In terms of PCE, TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ devices remained unchanged between day 20 and 29, whereas the NiO $ _x $  device declined significantly (Fig. 9). Since the moisture is the only factor affecting the stability of PVSCs during the period of laboratory lockdown, a hydrophobicity study of NiO $ _x $ , TPA-TPy, TPA-Py-PTZ, and the MAPbI $ _3 $  on top of these HTMs should provide an insightful information about the moisture stability of the devices.

Accordingly, Fig. 10a displays (from left to right) the water contact angles on the surface of NiO $ _{x} $ , TPA-TPy, and TPA-Py-PTZ, respectively. The observed water contact angles of NiO $ _{x} $ , TPA-TPy, and TPA-Py-PTZ are 34.5°, 102.6°, and 96.2°, respectively. Clearly, the hydrophobicity of NiO $ _{x} $  is significantly inferior to that of TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ. This is consistent with the worst moisture stability of NiO $ _{x} $  devices compared with both TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ devices. The higher contact angle of TPA-TPy than TPA-Py-PTZ may be ascribed to the hydrophobic decyloxy substituent of TPA-TPy. More interestingly, the observed water contact angles of MAPbI $ _{3} $  on top of NiO $ _{x} $ , TPA-TPy, and TPA-Py-PTZ are 56.5°, 65.1°, and 58.8°, respectively (Fig. 10b). The smallest contact angle was observed for NiO $ _{x} $ /MAPbI $ _{3} $ .

To verify the contact angle results further, we have compared the time evolution (in the beginning 4–6 min) of decreasing water volume and contact angle (Fig. 11). From either the volume or the contact angle of water droplets, the slowest decreasing was observed for FTO/TPA-TPy or FTO/TPA-TPy/MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  . Due to the much more hygroscopic nature of NiO  $  _{x}  $   than organic TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ, the fast decreasing water volume or contact angle observed for FTO/NiO  $  _{x}  $   is quite natural. However, it is intriguing to observe that the HTM underneath will affect the hydrophilicity or hydrophobicity of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   layer above. According to the XRD and SEM results, we suggest that the larger crystalline (or grain) size and less grain boundary of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   crystallite on top of either TPA-TPy or TPA-Py-PTZ than NiO  $  _{x}  $   more effectively impede the moisture from infiltrating into MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   thin films, refrain the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   from moisture damaging, and promote the shelf-life of PVSCs. Similar to other studies reported in literature, the stability of PVSCs hinges on the degradation of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite which is essentially due to the moisture, light, and the thermal heating that associates with the incident light. For a rare opportunity, we have an isolation of PVSCs from the light exposure during the period of laboratory lockdown, but the moisture becomes a major and single factor detrimental to the stability

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, two D–A–D type dopant-free organic HTMs viz., TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ, consisting of 2,4,6-trisubstituted pyridine as the core electron acceptor unit and 4,4'-dimethoxytriphenylamine as peripheral electron donor units were designed, synthesized, and studied for their potentials in p-i-n (or inverted) MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  PVSCs. The extended π-conjugation (thiophenylene-vinylene-phenylene) of TPA-TPy provides excellent thermal stability to the HTM and exert remarkable influence on the device performance, including higher hole mobility, higher hole extracting and transporting ability, superior film morphology facilitating tighter MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  crystallite packing, and probably a better contact interface to MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  that facilitates the exciton dissociation, enhancing current density and reducing leakage current. Accordingly, the TPA-TPy-based MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  PVSCs exhibit a comparatively higher PCE of 15.33%, J  $  _{SC}  $  of 23.69 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , V  $  _{OC}  $  of 0.95 V, and FF of 68.10% than those of TPA-Py-PTZ-based ones. The hydrophobicity of both TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ provide a much superior MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  grown on top in terms of crystallinity or grain size compared with those of NiO  $  _{x}  $ -based devices. Consequently, we have observed an extended shelf-life for TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ than NiO  $  _{x}  $ -based MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  PVSCs. We have elucidated why TPA-TPy is superior to TPA-Py-PTZ and why both TPA-TPy and TPA-Py-PTZ HTMs provide MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  PVSCs an extended shelf-life than NiO  $  _{x}  $  HTM.
