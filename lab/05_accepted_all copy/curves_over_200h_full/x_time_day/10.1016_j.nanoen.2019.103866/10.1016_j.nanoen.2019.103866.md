# ABSTRACT

The surface defects and grain boundary defects of organometallic halide perovskite films are detrimental to both the efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Furthermore, the electrical conductivity, work function and surface morphology of the hole transport layer (HTL) can also affect the performance of PSCs significantly. Here, we first have developed a novel synergistic strategy that uses multiple functional EMIC (1-Ethyl-3-methylimidazolium chloride) ionic liquid to modify PEDOT:PSS (poly (3, 4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrene sulfonate) HTL, thereby obtaining the HTL with high electrical conductivity, low work function and smooth surface. Moreover, a novel S-acetylthiocholine chloride molecule which replaces expensive PCBM (phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester) is developed to effectively passivate the negative- and positive-charged ionic defects in hybrid perovskite. The synergistic strategy extends the carrier recombination lifetime and reduces the charge trap density. In addition, the current hysteresis of the inverted device was also effectively eliminated. As a result, the champion cell in small area shows  $ 20.06\% $  efficiency with no hysteresis, along with an efficiency  $ 18.77\% $  for inverted PSCs in an active area of  $ 1\mathrm{cm}^2 $ , both

# 1. Introduction

Organometallic halide perovskite semiconductors have a unique class of marvelous optoelectronics properties, such as broadband absorption, $ ^{[1]} $  long intrinsic carrier recombination lifetime, $ ^{[2]} $  and high charge carrier mobility, $ ^{[3]} $  achieving remarkable power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) with a simple and low-cost route. The history of PCE enhancement for perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has witnessed the value of reducing charge-recombination loss both inside the perovskite active layer and at the carrier transport layers. $ ^{[4-10]} $  PEDOT:PSS (poly (3, 4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrene sulfonate), owe to its advantages like high transparency, low cost of fabrication, and relatively low temperature (< 150 °C) processing in air, has been widely used in the inverted PSCs as an hole transport layer (HTL), $ ^{[11-15]} $  to form the device structure as ITO/PEDOT:PSS/perovskite/PC $ _{61} $ BM/C $ _{60} $ /BCP/Ag. However, the energy level of PEDOT: PSS (-5.12 eV) does not match well with CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  (-5.4 eV) perovskites, and thus the one-step inverted PSCs with PEDOT:PSS HTL generally exhibited relatively low open circuit voltages (Voc) less than 1 V. In addition, the transfer of charge inside the PEDOT:PSS layer is not as effective as needed due to its electrical inhomogeneity and large microstructure defects, including the gradient of the electrical conductivity near the surface area to bulk and

order-of-magnitude variations in film morphology.  $  ^{[16-18]}  $   Within the inverted PSCs, the inefficient process of hole transport can lead to an unbalanced carrier charge transport, which inevitably creates the accumulation of charge carrier and the increase of resistance, and ultimately result in a low fill factor (FF) and large leakage of current.  $  ^{[19-22]}  $   Furthermore, the electrical conductivity of PEDOT:PSS layer is   $  \approx0.2~S~cm^{-1}  $  , which is considerably lower than that of common metal cathode. Such inferior conductivity of PEDOT:PSS layer leads to the loss of energy in the process of charge carrier transport, thereby decreasing the photocurrent, FF and the PCEs of PSCs.  $  ^{[23-27]}  $   The doping strategy for PEDOT:PSS HTL has been demonstrated to be an effective approach to improve the hole transport layer in PSCs.  $  ^{[26-29]}  $   For instance, Gong et al reported that PEO (polyethylene oxide) doped PEDOT:PSS can efficiently improve the PCEs of PSCs from 11.43 % (undoped) to 16.52 % (0.50 vol% of PEO doping) by increasing the PEDOT:PSS film's conductivity.  $  ^{[26]}  $   Fung et al reported that the F4-TCNQ (2,3,5,6-tetrafluoro-7,7,8,8-tetracyanoquinodimethane) doped PEDOT:PSS films can exhibit an enhanced electrical conductivity and the low HOMO (the highest occupied molecular orbital) level that are beneficial to enhance the efficiency of PSCs, where the highest PCEs was increased from 13.30 % (undoped) to 17.22 % (0.30 wt% of F4-TCNQ doping).  $  ^{[27]}  $   On the other hands, due to good chemical stability, negligible vapor pressure, high conductivity, and low flammability, ionic liquids have also been applied to electron-transport layer in perovskite solar cells to enhance the efficiency via increasing the conductivity of electron-transport film.  $  ^{[30,31]}  $   However, few papers have been reported on the use of multiple functional ionic liquids to modify PEDOT:PSS in perovskite solar cells. 1-Ethyl-3-methylimidazolium chloride (EMIC) is a multiple functional, highly conductive imidazole halide ionic liquid which could bind to the positively charged under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions at a certain degree.  $  ^{[32-34]}  $   Here, we have found that the doping of EMIC with appropriate amounts can efficiently reduce the work function of the PEDOT:PSS HTL and improve the electronic conductivity and surface morphology of the PEDOT:PSS HTL in

inverted PSCs, and the conductivity enhancement mainly resulted from the insulating PSS's depletion and the highly conductive PEDOT domains's formation. The advanced electrical conductivity, low work function and smooth surface of EMIC-treated PEDOT:PSS HTL facilitated effective transportation of the holes, thereby large Voc, large FF and high Jsc were anticipated from the inverted PSCs integrated using EMIC-treated PEDOT:PSS HTL.

Furthermore, passivation of ion defects at the surface of perovskite film becomes critical.  $  ^{[35-38]}  $   These ion defects on the perovskite surface are most likely caused by the low thermal stability or low formation energy of the organic component-containing perovskite materials that easily evaporate from the surface during the thermal annealing.  $  ^{[39-41]}  $   Due to their energy disorder and reduced carrier concentration, these surface defects would significantly affect the open circuit voltage (Voc) of the cell devices, due to the pulling down the quasi-Fermi level splitting.  $  ^{[42,43]}  $   In addition, these defects can also lead to other device problems like related current hysteresis, ion migration, and device degradation in air.  $  ^{[41,44,45]}  $   Therefore, it is highly desirable to electronically passivate the defects at surface in the perovskite layer to promote the PCE, and to heal these defects to extend the durability of PSCs. To date, different passivation molecules have been reported as electron acceptors or donors that can interact with the charged defects of perovskites and then eliminate the associated defect-induced charge traps.  $  ^{[36,32-34]}  $   PCBM is a good electron transporting material that could accept an electron from the negative-charged Pb–I antisite defects   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  or under-coordinated halide ions, and thus passivate the halide-induced deep traps.  $  ^{[36,46,47]}  $   Furthermore, thiophene or pyridine usually perform as the electron donors, and they could bind to the positively charged under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions.  $  ^{[32-34]}  $   However, most of these passivation molecules could passivate only one type of defect, either positive or negative charged defects. The passivation of charge defects in perovskite should take the charge neutrality into consideration. Huang et. al. reported that choline chloride can efficiently passivate various

charged defects in perovskite.  $  ^{[45]}  $   But more passivation molecules need further exploration to replace expensive PCBM.

In this paper, we have developed a novel synergistic strategy that a multiple functional EMIC (1-Ethyl-3-methylimidazolium chloride) ionic liquid-treated PEDOT:PSS with the greatly enhanced electrical conductivity, low work function and morphology improvement, as the HTL for the inverted PSCs for the first time. With such a high quality HTL, a novel S-acetylthiocholine chloride molecule which replaces expensive PCBM is further developed to effectively passivate the negative- and positive-charged ionic defects in perovskite. This synergistic strategy extends the carrier recombination lifetime and reduces the charge trap density. Furthermore, the current hysteresis of the inverted device was also effectively eliminated. As a result, the best hysteresis-free PCE of 20.06 % with an open-circuit-voltage of 1.08 V was obtained in the inverted PSCs, with 19.7 % stabilized output efficiency at the MPP (maximum power point), which is the highest efficiency in the one-step PEDOT:PSS-based inverted PSCs so far. Moreover, the efficiency of the cell device with 1 cm $  ^{2}  $  active area has achieved 18.77%, which is also the highest efficiency reported so far for large-area PEDOT:PSS-based inverted PSCs. Compared to PCBM, the device passivated by S-acetylthiocholine chloride also has improved environmental stability (retaining 85% of initial PCE after 35 days storage without encapsulation in air with 60% humidity) and thermal stability (retaining 87% of initial PCE after 80°C for 24 h storage without encapsulation under inert atmosphere). These results demonstrate that the combination of highly electrical conductive, low work function and smooth surface of the EMIC-treated PEDOT:PSS HTL with passivation of the perovskite film surface defects by S-acetylthiocholine chloride molecule offers a simple and effective mean to significantly boost PCEs and stability of inverted PSCs.

# 2.2 Discussion on the passivation effects of different passivation molecules on the EMIC-treated PEDOT:PSS-based PSCs

The passivation of surface defects in the perovskite film is very critical for the efficient and stable PSCs. The perovskite passivation layer of the above cell structure with EMIC-treated PEDOT:PSS HTL is based on the fullerene derivative PCBM, which could accept an electron from the negative-charged Pb–I antisite defects   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  or under-coordinated halide ions, and only passivating one type of defect   $  [36,46,47]  $  , and this limits the further improvement of the perovskite cell efficiency. In the next study, we use a novel passivation molecule of S-acetylthiocholine chloride to replace the expensive PCBM, and compare with the reported choline chloride molecule. The results are shown in Figure 3(a-e). There are both negatively charged anionic and positively charged cationic defects in   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite, such as   $  MA^{+}  $  and   $  I^{-}  $  vacancies, respectively, while S-acetylthiocholine ions are expected to passivate   $  MA^{+}  $  vacancies by occupying cuboctahedral sites to compensate the   $  MA^{+}  $  loss on the perovskite film surfaces, as shown in Figure 3b. The loss of halide (  $  I^{-}  $  ) ions caused by the evaporation of MAI during thermal annealing requires additional halide (  $  Cl^{-}  $  ) ion compensation. In addition, the small amount of Cl addition has been broadly reported to enhance the charge-recombination lifetime in   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  .  $  ^{[38]}  $   Hence, S-acetylthiocholine chloride has an

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we have showed an efficient synergistic strategy of enhancing the PCEs and stability of PSCs through utilizing the multiple functions of EMIC-treated PEDOT:PSS as HTL, which possesses the high electrical conductivity, low work function and morphology

19

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

improvement, and employing S-acetylthiocholine chloride as the passivation layer to replace expensive PCBM, which can effectively passivate both the negative- and positive-charged defects at the surface of perovskite film. This synergistic strategy extends the carrier recombination lifetime and reduces the charge trap density. In addition, the current hysteresis of the inverted device is also effectively eliminated. As a result, the champion cell in small area shows 20.06% efficiency with no hysteresis, along with an efficiency 18.77% for inverted PSCs in an active area of   $  1 \, cm^2  $  , both of which are the highest efficiency in the one-step PEDOT:PSS-based inverted PSCs so far. Compared to PCBM, the devices passivated by S-acetylthiocholine chloride exhibit improved environmental stability (retaining 85% of initial PCE after 35 days storage without encapsulation in air with 60% humidity) and thermal stability (retaining 87% of initial PCE after 80°C for 24 h storage without encapsulation under inert atmosphere). The approach developed in this work, that is, modifying HTLs using multiple functional ionic liquid combining with passivating both negative-charged defects and positive-charged defects using quaternary ammonium halide salts with acyl sulfur functional group, could also be widely used in improving other type solar cells and electronic devices.
