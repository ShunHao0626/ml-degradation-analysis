# Alkali Metal Fluoride-Modified Tin Oxide for n–i–p Planar Perovskite Solar Cells

Chunyan Wang, Jihuai Wu,  $  ^{*}  $   Shibo Wang, Xuping Liu, Xiaobing Wang, Zhongliang Yan, Liqiang Chen, Xiao Liu, Guodong Li, Weihai Sun, and Zhang Lan

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: The practical applications of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are limited by further improvement of their stability and performance. Additive engineering and interface engineering are promising medicine to cure this stubborn disease. Herein, an alkali metal fluoride as an additive is introduced into the tin oxide   $  \left(\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\right)  $   electron transport layer (ETL). The formation of coordination bonds of   $  F^{-}  $   ions with the oxygen vacancy of   $  Sn^{4+}  $   ions decreases the trap-state density and improves the electron mobility; the hydrogen bond interaction between the F ion and amine group   $  \left(\mathrm{FA}^{+}\right)  $   of perovskite inhibits the diffusion of organic cations and promotes perovskite (PVK) stability. Meanwhile, the

alkali metal ions (  $  K^{+}  $  ,   $  Rb^{+}  $  , and   $  Cs^{+}  $  ) permeated into PVK fill the organic cation vacancies and ameliorate the crystal quality of PVK films. Consequently, a   $  SnO_{2}  $  -based planar PSC exhibits a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 20.24%, while the PSC modified by CsF achieves a PCE of 22.51%, accompanied by effective enhancement of stability and negligible hysteresis. The research results provide a typical example for low-cost and multifunctional additives in high-performance PSCs.

KEYWORDS: perovskite solar cells, additive engineering, electron transport layer, tin oxide, alkali metal fluoride

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have received widespread interest owing to their superb photovoltaic (PV) properties and rapidly increasing efficiency in a short period of time; at present, their power conversion efficiency (PCE) has reached 25.5%  $  ^{1-5}  $  . Nevertheless, the industrial development of PSCs is restricted by further improvement of its stability caused by the defects in the body and interface of PSCs.  $  ^{6-9}  $   Additive engineering and interface engineering are promising medicine to cure this stubborn disease.  $  ^{10-14}  $

As a viable candidate for the electron transport layer (ETL), tin oxide   $  \left(\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\right)  $   possesses multiple superiorities, such as deep conduction band, outstanding electron mobility, and low-temperature processing.  $  ^{9}  $   However, during the preparation process,   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanoparticles are prone to agglomeration and generate defects and oxygen vacancies in the film, causing serious nonradiative recombination, depreciating the performance of the device and having an obvious hysteresis.  $  ^{7-9}  $   To overcome the issues, many metal cations such as   $  Li^{+}  $  ,   $  {}^{15}Nb^{5+}  $  ,   $  {}^{16}Y^{3+}  $  ,   $  {}^{17,18}Na^{+}  $  ,   $  {}^{8}K^{+}  $  ,   $  {}^{19}  $   and   $  Cs^{+}{}^{20-22}  $   have been employed and various anions (such as   $  CH_{3}COO^{-}  $  ,   $  {}^{23}Cl^{-}  $  ,   $  {}^{24}F^{-}  $  ,   $  {}^{19,25}  $   etc.) have attempted to passivate the defects. Among them, fluoride ions have significant effects. For example, Zheng et al. employed KF into   $  SnO_{2}  $   and improved the overall electron mobility of inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC and open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) by adjusting its conduction band to better match with that of the

# 2. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

To carefully observe the surface morphology of   $  SnO_{2}  $   films, top-views scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images are recorded and shown in Figure S1a–d. The nanocrystals in the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film that participated in the test comparison have a similar average size. However, after introducing MF into   $  SnO_{2}  $ , the distribution of nano-  $  SnO_{2}  $   gradually tends to be uniform and dense, demonstrating that additives can improve the agglomeration properties of   $  SnO_{2}  $   in colloidal dispersion.  $  ^{26}  $   The difference morphology of   $  SnO_{2}  $   films is ascribed to the interaction between different MFs and Sn in   $  SnO_{2}  $ .

Based on   $  SnO_{2}  $   substrates modified with MF, the surface morphology of the PVK film is shown in Figure 1a–d. Previous research has confirmed that the white phases are   $  PbI_{2}  $ , and the black phases are perovskite materials.  $  ^{[27]}  $   The pinholes on the film surface are effectively reduced, which ultimately better the overall quality of the perovskite film. The grain sizes of the perovskite on   $  Cs:SnO_{2}  $   films are 1310 nm, which are obviously larger and more uniform than those of the PVK film on the pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   (980 nm) (Figure 1e–h), which is speculated as the influence of fluoride on the crystal nucleation and crystallization kinetics of PVK.  $  ^{[4,28,29]}  $

The cross-view SEM images of the PVK films grown on different   $  SnO_{2}  $   films are displayed in Figure 1i–l, the PVK films grown on the original   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL contain smaller crystal grains as well as more pinholes. On the contrary, the cross section of the PVK film based on   $  SnO_{2}  $   modified with alkali metal

fluoride has large vertically oriented crystal grains and fewer grain boundaries, which is beneficial for carrier transport and collection.  $  ^{30}  $   In addition, the roughness of the PVK films deposited on different ETLs was studied by atomic force microscopy (AFM). Figure 1m–p depicts that the root mean square (RMS) of the PVK films grown on   $  SnO_{2}  $  , K:  $  SnO_{2}  $  , Rb:  $  SnO_{2}  $  , and Cs:  $  SnO_{2}  $   shows a gradual decrease (from 45.6 to 36.4 nm), which indicates that the introduction of alkali metal fluoride makes better uniformity of the grain size of the PVK film. This perhaps is ascribed to the reduction in the surface roughness of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL and improvement in wettability after doping with alkali metal fluoride.  $  ^{10}  $

To verify the diffusion, energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) is applied to investigate the distribution of the alkali metal fluoride diffused into the perovskite, which is constructed by ITO/Cs:  $  SnO_{2}  $  /PVK/spiro-OMeTAD/Au. According to the aforementioned investigation, the possible passivation mechanism of   $  SnO_{2}  $   modified with alkali metal fluoride in the perovskite solar cell is suggested in Figure 2f. The added   $  F^{-}  $   ions form coordination bonds with   $  Sn^{4+}  $   in the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film, which reduces oxygen vacancy defects and improves the electron mobility.  $  ^{31}  $   Furthermore, the added   $  F^{-}  $   ions form hydrogen bonds (N–H…F) with the amine group (  $  FA^{+}  $  ), which effectively inhibit the diffusion of cations. Meanwhile, the alkali metal cations migrate into the PVK layer and occupy the organic cation vacancies (  $  FA^{+}  $  ) of PVK films,  $  ^{8}  $   which alleviates the cation vacancy defects and improves the crystal quality of the PVK film. Multiple functions of alkali metal fluoride additives, particularly in CsF, will dramatically improve the PV properties and stability of the devices, effectively suppressing hysteresis.  $  ^{42}  $

# 3. CONCLUSIONS

$ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  was chosen as the ETL and MFs were introduced as additives into the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ETL to modify  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ , perovskite, and the interface of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2/ $  perovskite. The morphology observation, XPS analysis, and photoelectrochemical characterization indicate that the  $ \mathrm{F}^- $  ion can not only coordinate with  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{4+} $  to reduce oxygen vacancy defects and improve the electron mobility but also form hydrogen bonds with the amine group  $ (\mathrm{FA}^{+}) $  in PVK to reduce the diffusion of organic cations and promote PVK stability. Meanwhile, the alkali metal cations  $ (\mathrm{K}^{+}, \mathrm{Rb}^{+}, \mathrm{Cs}^{+}) $  can permeate into perovskite and fill organic cation vacancies to decline cation defects and ameliorate the crystal quality of the perovskite film. Among them, CsF possesses the best effect owing to the larger radius and smaller electronegativity of the  $ \mathrm{Cs}^{+} $ ion. Consequently,  $ \mathrm{FA}_{1-x}\mathrm{MA}_x\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  planar PSC based on  $ \mathrm{Cs:SnO}_2 $  ( $ 3\mathrm{mg}\cdot\mathrm{mL}^{-1} $ ) reaches a PCE of  $ 22.51\% $ , along with improved stability and negligible hysteresis. We believe that the facile, low-cost, and multifunctional CsF additive has a bright prospect in highly effective PSCs.

# 4. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

4.1. Materials. Methylammonium iodide (MAI, ≥99.5%) and methylamine hydrochloride (MACl, 99.5%) were bought from Xi'an Bright Optoelectronics Technology Co., Ltd. SnO₂ aqueous colloidal precursor (tin(IV) oxide) was ordered from Alfa Aesar. Rubidium fluoride (RbF, 99.8%) and cesium fluoride (CsF, 99.99%) were bought from Aladdin. Potassium fluoride (KF, 99%) was ordered from Innochem. Formamidinium iodide (FAI) and lead iodide (PbI₂, 99.99%) were ordered from Advanced Electronic Technology Co., Ltd. Isopropanol (IPA, 99.5%), dimethylformamide (DMF, >99.9%), and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, >99.9%) were ordered from Sigma-Aldrich. All of the chemical materials were utilized as received with no further treatment.

4.2. Device Fabrication. The preparation method of the indium tin oxide (ITO) substrate was the same as previously reported.  $  ^{[31]}  $   Predetermined amount of MF (M = K, Rb, and Cs) was dissolved in 0.8 mL of deionized water with stirring for 10 min. The MF solution are mixed with 0.2 mL of   $  SnO_{2}  $   solution. The   $  MF:SnO_{2}  $   precursor solutions were spin-coated on the ITO at a speed of 3500 rpm for 25 s and annealed on a heating stage at 150 °C for 30 min to get the relevant ETL.   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution (0.6915 g of   $  PbI_{2}  $   dissolved in 900 μL of DMF and 100 μL of DMSO solution) was spin-coated on the ETL-coated substrate at a speed of 1500 rpm for 30 s and annealed afterward at 70 °C for 1 min. Next, the mixed solution (MAI/MACI/FAI = 0.00639:0.0090:0.0900 g dissolved in 1 mL IPA) was spin-coated on the   $  PbI_{2}  $   substrate at a speed of 2000 rpm for 30 s. The spin-coated film was preannealed at 30 °C for 5 min and later annealed at 150 °C for 15 min, which led to the deposition of the   $  FA_{1-x}MA_{x}PbI_{3}  $   layer on   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETLs. The hole transport material (spiro-OMeTAD) was spin-coated on the   $  FA_{1-x}MA_{x}PbI_{3}  $   substrate at 700 rpm for 5 s and 3500 rpm for 20 s. Finally, the thickness of 80 nm gold electrodes was vapor-deposited on the upper layer of HTL by thermal evaporation at an evaporation rate of 1 Å·s  $  ^{-1}  $  . Thus, an n-i-p planar PSC device with an active area of about 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $   was fabricated.

4.3. Characterization. X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS) were measured by a Thermo Fisher Scientific K-alpha+. Surface morphologies of perovskite films and ETL films and cross-sectional structures of these PSCs were characterized by FE-SEM (JSM-7610F Plus, Japan). The roughness of the perovskite films was measured by AFM (Multimode-8).   $  {}^{1}  $  H proton nuclear magnetic resonance (  $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR) was conducted by a 500 MHz nuclear magnetic resonance spectrometer (Bruker, Switzerland). The TRPL spectra and the steady-state PL were conducted on an Omni-l Monochromator. X-ray diffraction (XRD) spectra were tested by a SmartLa (Netherlands). UV–vis absorption spectra were conducted by a UV–vis spectrometer (PerkinElmer Lambda 950). The Electrochemical

Workstation (China) recorded impedance spectroscopy and the ZView program calculated and analyzed the data. The current density vs voltage   $  (J-V)  $   curves were plotted with a light intensity of 100 mW·cm  $  ^{-2}  $   (AM 1.5G) using a scan rate of 150 mV·s  $  ^{-1}  $   by Newport. Long-term stability tests were measured with an RH of 45% and a temperature of 25 °C. The incident light intensity was calibrated using NREL-calibrated silicon solar cells equipped with a KG-2 filter. In the unpackaged state, we characterized and measured the n-i-p planar PSC devices.
