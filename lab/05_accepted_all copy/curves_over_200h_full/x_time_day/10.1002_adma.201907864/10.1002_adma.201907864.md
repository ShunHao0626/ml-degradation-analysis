# Heavy Water Additive in Formamidinium: A Novel Approach to Enhance Perovskite Solar Cell Efficiency

Ankur Solanki, Mohammad Mahdi Tavakoli, Qiang Xu, Sai S. H. Dintakurti, Swee Sien Lim, Anirban Bagui, John V. Hanna, Jing Kong, and Tze Chien Sum*

Heavy water or deuterium oxide (  $  D_{2}O  $  ) comprises deuterium, a hydrogen isotope twice the mass of hydrogen. Contrary to the disadvantages of deuterated perovskites, such as shorter recombination lifetimes and lower/invariant efficiencies, the serendipitous effect of   $  D_{2}O  $   as a beneficial solvent additive for enhancing the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of triple-A cation (cesium (Cs)/methylammonium (MA)/formaminidium (FA)) perovskite solar cells from   $  \approx19.2\%  $   (reference) to 20.8% (using 1 vol%   $  D_{2}O  $  ) with higher stability is reported. Ultrafast optical spectroscopy confirms passivation of trap states, increased carrier recombination lifetimes, and enhanced charge carrier diffusion lengths in the deuterated samples. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy and solid-state NMR spectroscopy validate the   $  N-H_{2}  $   group as the preferential isotope exchange site. Furthermore, the NMR results reveal the induced alteration of the FA to MA ratio due to deuteration causes a widespread alteration to several dynamic processes that influence the photophysical properties. First-principles density functional theory calculations reveal a decrease in   $  PbI_{6}  $   phonon frequencies in the deuterated perovskite lattice. This stabilizes the   $  PbI_{6}  $   structures and weakens the electron–LO phonon (Fröhlich) coupling, yielding higher electron mobility. Importantly, these findings demonstrate that selective isotope exchange potentially opens new opportunities for tuning perovskite optoelectronic properties.

Within ten years of the first report, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite photovoltaics has improved remarkably from 3.8% to more than 25%.  $  ^{[1]}  $   Steep rise in perovskite photovoltaic research and the resulting performance improvements have thrown a tough challenge out to the inorganic counterparts. Perovskites are appealing due to their easier processing and excellent tunable optical, electrical and mechanical properties.  $  ^{[2-7]}  $   However, higher charge carrier trap density is still one of the PCE limiting factors in efficient devices. Recently, perovskite compositional engineering and control of thin film morphology are two main methodologies to suppress the traps and enhancing the PCE.  $  ^{[8]}  $   Beyond the various fabrication approaches, the solvent additive approach adapted from organic solar cells,  $  ^{[9]}  $   provides an effective and facile means for optimizing the morphology, suppressing the defects in perovskite film, and thus improving the PCE. Since the first report of high quality perovskite films with improved stability prepared under 30% relative humidity (RH), $ ^{[10]} $  incorporation of H $ _2 $ O as a solvent additive at different stages of device processing has captured the community's imagination. $ ^{[11,12]} $  Heavy water or deuterium oxide (D $ _2 $ O), an isotope of H $ _2 $ O, is widely used for various applications such as moderator in certain nuclear reactors; solvent for nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR); or as an isotope label in the study of reaction pathways involving proton movement and tunneling in biochemistry. In fact, D $ _2 $ O has never been used as a solvent additive in the perovskite research up to now. Furthermore, deuterated MAPbI $ _3 $  (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ ) perovskite was previously reported to yield shorter recombination lifetimes and lower or invariant PCEs compared to their non-deuterated counterparts. $ ^{[13,14]} $  Therefore, D $ _2 $ O is not expected to have any positive influence on perovskites, especially with deuteration occurring.

Herein, we reveal the serendipitous discovery of   $  D_{2}O  $   as an effective solvent additive for enhancing photovoltaic performance in triple A-cation based PSC devices. An optimum concentration of only 1 vol% of   $  D_{2}O  $   in the perovskite solution yields the highest PCE of 20.8% and stability compared to 19.2% in the reference PSC, i.e., cells fabricated without   $  D_{2}O  $  . Interestingly,   $  D_{2}O  $   also yields higher PCE compared to the addition of 1 vol% of   $  H_{2}O  $  . Incorporation of   $  D_{2}O  $   passivates the perovskite defects without any obvious topographical changes in the film, as proved by atomic force microscopy (AFM); while the increase in both charge carrier lifetime and diffusion lengths are confirmed by ultrafast optical spectroscopy. Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy confirms the presence of deuterium in the thin film, while NMR spectroscopy reveals that isotope exchange occurs at the   $  N-H_{2}  $   site and it also induces an alteration to the FA to MA ratio in perovskite thin film, which influences the charge carrier dynamics. DFT calculations indicate reduced   $  PbI_{6}  $   phonon frequencies, which helps with the stabilization of the   $  PbI_{6}  $   structure.

Triple-A cation perovskite films were fabricated using the antisolvent method from a solution of formamidinium iodide (FAI), methylammonium iodide (MAI), cesium iodide (CsI), lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ ) and lead bromide (PbBr $ _2 $ ); dissolved in a mixed solvent of dimethylformamide (DMF) and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) as detailed in the experimental section. The presence of a higher fraction of N–H groups in FA and their easier deuteration (i.e., proton deuteron exchange due to lower N–H bond energy (319 kJ mol $ ^{-1} $ ) as compared to C–H (413 kJ mol $ ^{-1} $ )) make FA the dominating perovskite in the appropriate host system for D $ _2 $ O additive. In this study, a small fraction of D $ _2 $ O was added to the pre-dissolved perovskite solution. Using the D $ _2 $ O-modified perovskite solution, we fabricated inverted PSCs with the structure of glass/indium tin oxide (ITO)/poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA)/perovskite/C $ _{60} $ /bathocuproine (BCP)/Ag as shown in Figure 1a. The current density–voltage (J–V) curves of the PSCs without and with optimum concentration of D $ _2 $ O are shown in Figure 1b and Figure S1b in the Supporting Information. Our photovoltaic (PV) results indicate that by increasing the amount of   $  D_{2}O  $   from 0 to 1 vol%, the PCE of the device gradually increased, mainly due to higher short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) and fill factor (FF). However, adding more   $  D_{2}O  $   from 1 to 4 vol% to the perovskite solution, the PCE dropped since all the PV parameters, especially the FF, decreased. As shown in Figure 1b, the highest PCE of 20.8% (  $  J_{SC} = 23.57 \, mA cm^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{OC} = 1.13 \, V  $  , and FF = 78%) was achieved with a concentration of 1 vol%   $  D_{2}O  $   as compared to 19.2% (  $  J_{SC} = 22.34 \, mA cm^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{OC} = 1.13 \, V  $  , and FF = 76%) for the reference cell without   $  D_{2}O  $  . On the other hand, the devices prepared with 1 vol%   $  H_{2}O  $   for comparison shows a relatively lower PCE of ≈19% (Table 1). Stabilized PCEs of 18.85% and 20.5% for the reference and 1 vol%   $  D_{2}O  $   devices, respectively, were obtained by tracking the maximum power point (MPP) over 60 s as shown in the inset of Figure 1b. The extracted   $  J_{sc}  $   values from the integration of the external quantum efficiency (EQE) with the solar spectrum (Figure 1c) are 21.2 and 22.3 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   for the reference and 1 vol%   $  D_{2}O  $   devices, respectively. They are in good agreement with the J-V results, suggesting that   $  D_{2}O  $   has a role in enhancing the current density of the PSCs.

Figure 1d and Figure S2 in the Supporting Information show the statistics of the PV parameters for PSCs with different concentrations of   $  D_{2}O  $   additive and compared with the 1 vol%   $  H_{2}O  $   additive. As shown, the average values of   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF and thus PCE are enhanced with the addition of   $  D_{2}O  $   by 1 vol%, whereas the average value of the   $  V_{oc}  $   remains almost invariant. Additionally, further increase of   $  D_{2}O  $   to 4 vol% results in all PV parameters being reduced drastically. Our PV results indicate that 1 vol% is the optimum value of   $  D_{2}O  $   in the PSCs. Moreover, the hysteresis index (HI (%) =   $  ((PCE_{backward} - PCE_{forward})/PCE_{backward}) \times 100  $  ) is also reduced from 1.75% in the reference to 1.24%, in the 1 vol%   $  D_{2}O  $   PSCs as shown in Figure 1e. Since defects were previously attributed as a likely origin for the J-V hysteresis in PSCs,  $  ^{[15]}  $   we posit that   $  D_{2}O  $   helps to reduce the defect density in these devices.  $  ^{[12]}  $   To investigate the effect of   $  D_{2}O  $   on the stability of PSCs, shelf-life stability was studied over 40 days under ambient conditions of 20% RH. As seen in Figure 1f, 1 vol%   $  D_{2}O  $   PSC shows better stability over time compared to the reference and 1 vol%   $  H_{2}O  $   added devices with only 22% PCE loss, indicating the role of   $  D_{2}O  $   in the device stability. Similarly, stronger PCE stability was also observed in   $  D_{2}O  $   added device while exposing to 1 Sun continuous illumination (Figure S2e, Supporting Information, devices were stored in glovebox without encapsulation). The addition of   $  D_{2}O  $   probably forms a stable byproduct within perovskite film, which leads to better stability in the device with 1 vol%   $  D_{2}O  $ .  $  ^{[16]}  $

The dissolution of the precursors in perovskite solution also depends on the polarity of the solvents and is crucial to controlling the defect density in a perovskite film. Addition of another polar solvent such as   $  D_{2}O  $   aids the complete dissolution of the precursors due to strong dipole–dipole interaction. Specifically, it coordinates well with lead halides to dissolve   $  PbI_{2}  $  ,   $  PbBr_{2}  $  , but is a poor solvent for FAI, MAI and CsI  $  ^{[16]}  $   compared to host solvents, analogous to   $  H_{2}O  $  .  $  ^{[17]}  $   Thus, it is likely that an excess of   $  D_{2}O  $   (>1%) causes an imbalance to the precursors' solubility, resulting in poor perovskite film quality and consequently lower device performance with reduced   $  V_{oc}  $  .  $  ^{[18,19]}  $   Noticeably,   $  D_{2}O  $   has

To investigate the effect of   $  D_{2}O  $   addition on the trap states, we estimated the trap density using the PL-based model developed in an earlier work.  $  ^{[21]}  $   Briefly, our model assumes an infinite number of trapping pathways within the perovskite film, each with a trap state density and a trapping rate. Figure 2c shows the photoexcited carrier density as a function of integrated PL intensity for the reference and   $  D_{2}O  $  -added perovskite films, and each data set can be well-fitted by considering only one trapping pathway. The results, summarized in Table 2, display a correlation between increasing PL lifetime with an associated decrease in trap density and thus trap-assisted recombination. One of the major sources of defects in perovskite films is the amorphous region present at the boundaries between perovskite grains.  $  ^{[22]}  $   We believe that   $  D_{2}O  $   ingression occurs readily at such locations, causing grain boundary healing that suppresses the defect density. However, the increase of trap density on addition of 1%   $  H_{2}O  $   indicates a different interaction mechanism compared to   $  D_{2}O  $  .

Using a similar approach in the literature,  $  ^{[23]}  $  the carrier diffusion lengths were extracted using TRPL measurements on bare perovskite films, films with  $  PC_{61}BM  $  electron extraction layer, and films with PTAA hole extraction layer (Figure S7, Supporting Information). Low charge carrier recombination is evidently reflected in the longer carrier diffusion lengths as shown in Figure 2d. From the results summarized in Table 2, the inclusion of  $  D_{2}O  $  results in a substantial improvement of charge diffusion length, which is most prominent in the sample with 1 vol%  $  D_{2}O  $  (hole diffusion length from 330 to 560 nm, while electron diffusion length from 80 to 350 nm), and falls off in samples with higher  $  D_{2}O  $  concentration in accordance to TRPL lifetime and trap density. The significant increase of the electron diffusion length in the deuterated perovskite film is attributed to the passivation of the deep electron traps.  $  ^{[24]}  $  Moreover, more balanced charge carrier diffusion lengths (in 1%  $  D_{2}O  $ ) compared to 1%  $  H_{2}O  $  additive thin films confirms  $  D_{2}O  $  as a viable additive for this perovskite system.

Apart from the longer diffusion lengths, the low recombination also influences the short circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) and fill factor (FF) of the   $  D_{2}O  $  -based PSCs, as observed from the charge carrier dynamics summarized in Table 2. The sharp decrease in trap-assisted recombination, observed in the 1 vol%   $  D_{2}O  $   perovskite film, accompanied with an increase in the   $  J_{SC}  $   and FF values. These findings agree well with the results of a recent work by Sherkar et al., through modeling of the PV parameters, demonstrated the correlation between charge recombination rate and the photovoltaic parameters, with the latter having a stronger dependence on the recombination.  $  ^{[25]}  $   Additionally, they found that interfacial trap-assisted

recombination is the dominant loss mechanism, limiting device performance.

The encouraging solar cell performance and TRPL studies raise an interesting question about the role of   $  D_{2}O  $   in the perovskite film. Its presence in the active layer is uncertain as the films were subjected to thermal annealing, performed at 100 °C for 1 h in the samples. Figure 3a shows the FTIR spectra measured in transmission mode on the films prepared from reference and   $  D_{2}O  $   (1 vol%) based perovskite solutions. The FTIR spectrum of the reference film is very similar to that of the   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite film.  $  ^{[26]}  $   The vibrational peaks at 1352 and 1713 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   represent the C–N bending and C=N stretching in the standard perovskite film, respectively.  $  ^{[27]}  $   The other strong peaks located at 3270 and 3406 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   originate from the symmetric and asymmetric N–H stretching vibration of   $  NH^{3+}  $   groups in the   $  FA^{+}  $  and   $  MA^{+}  $  cations. The appearance of a new vibrational band between 2337 and 2360 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   corresponds to deuterium, due to two possibilities: a) penetration and trapping of   $  D_{2}O  $   molecules in the perovskite lattice through sharing of the A-site cation with   $  FA^{+}  $ ,   $  MA^{+}  $ , and   $  Cs^{+}  $  and b) the formation of deuterated-FA-MA through the hydrogen–deuterium exchange from C–H and/or N–H groups. In the case of (a), analogous to hydrated perovskite,  $  ^{[28,29]}  $     $  D_{2}O  $   penetrates into the perovskite lattice during the processing and embeds inside the   $  PbI_{6}  $   octahedral cage, sharing the A-site   $  FA^{+}  $ ,   $  MA^{+}  $ , and   $  Cs^{+}  $  cations. While for the case of (b), proton exchange between   $  FA^{+}/MA^{+}  $  cations and   $  D_{2}O  $   can occur under the influence of a local field in the perovskite film  $  ^{[30]}  $   with N–H or C–H being the probable deuterium exchange sites. Nevertheless, the p-subshell in carbon is partially filled (C—1s  $  ^{2}  $ , 2s  $  ^{2}  $ , 2p  $  ^{2}  $   vs N—1s  $  ^{2}  $ , 2s  $  ^{2}  $ , 2p  $  ^{3}  $ ) and therefore has higher electron affinity (153.9 kJ mol  $  ^{-1}  $ ) than nitrogen (7 kJ mol  $  ^{-1}  $ ). The greater electron affinity of carbon results in the higher C–H bond energy (413 kJ mol  $  ^{-1}  $ ) as compared to N–H (391 kJ mol  $  ^{-1}  $ ).  $  ^{[31]}  $   Hence, deuterium exchange of hydrogen is expected to be easier with the N–H bond compared to the C–H bond in both organic cations. Note that the instability of the hexagonal phase (yellow phase) of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   makes it an inappropriate system though it has a higher fraction of the N–H groups compared to   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   and thus three cation perovskite remains as one of the more suitable host system for   $  D_{2}O  $   additive. Here, we believe that the deuteration exchange process initiates at the solution stage and reaches an equilibrium within a few seconds.  $  ^{[32]}  $

DFT calculations of the phonon dispersion curves and the IR spectra can provide a deeper understanding of the effects of deuteration on vibrational properties. Due to the higher fraction of FA compared to MA in the triple A-cation system, here the DFT calculations focused on the FA cation. The details of all calculations are given in the experimental section.

In our calculations, we only considered N–H as the active site for deuterium exchange (from our NMR results). Based on this configuration, a deuterium atom (D) has the same charge distribution as a hydrogen atom, with an extra neutron. Therefore, N–D and   $  N-D_{2}  $   have the same bond lengths and bond angles as of N–H and   $  N-H_{2}  $ , respectively.  $  ^{[38]}  $   Since deuterium is heavier than hydrogen, the energies of N–D and   $  N-D_{2}  $   bonds are higher and their vibrational energies are smaller than those of N–H and   $  N-H_{2}  $ . These results suggest that the N–D and   $  N-D_{2}  $   bonds are stronger than N–H and   $  N-H_{2}  $   ones. Our DFT calculations revealed that deuteration of FA gives rise to a decrease in the phonon frequencies of the   $  PbI_{6}  $   network. This in turn affects the carrier mobility and carrier scattering along the   $  PbI_{6}  $   network. Due to the polarization of Pb-I bonds, electron–LO phonon coupling (Fröhlich coupling) exists in hybrid lead halide perovskites.  $  ^{[39]}  $   Fröhlich coupling typically decreases the energy of the conduction band edge and increases the electron effective mass.  $  ^{[38-40]}  $   However, Fröhlich coupling is weakened in deuterated   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   as LO phonon frequencies are decreased, which helps to increase the carrier mobility. The change in phonon mode is further verified by Thz transmission measurements of the ref. (i.e., 0%   $  D_{2}O  $  ) and 1%   $  D_{2}O  $   added perovskite films coated on z-cut quartz substrates (Figure S11, Supporting Information). The higher intensity of the phonon peak at around 0.96 Hz in the deuterated sample compared to the reference sample confirms the increased distortion of the   $  PbI_{6}  $   octahedra cage, which decreases the charge carrier scattering and thus increases the mobility in the deuterated sample to give higher current density.  $  ^{[41]}  $   In addition, electron–hole Coulomb interaction is another property that determines the carrier mobility and thus its recombination lifetime is also dependent on perovskite's   $  PbI_{6}  $   framework.  $  ^{[14]}  $   Hence, the longer carrier lifetime together with the longer charge carrier diffusion lengths for the 1 vol%   $  D_{2}O  $   based samples can be attributed to higher charge carrier mobility as compared to the reference samples (see Table S in the Supporting Information). Any change in the perovskite crystal structure as the origin of higher mobility can be discounted, since XRD in Figure S6a in the Supporting Information shows a similar structure. Therefore, we attribute the suppression of carrier scattering as the likely origin of the enhanced charge carrier mobility and lifetime in deuterated perovskite film. In contrast to the works by Gong et al. and Chen et al. that focused on the isotope effect of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ , where MA has fully deuterated C–D or N–D bonds,  $  ^{[13]}  $   our findings emphasize the selective deuteration of the N–H bond and the organic A cation maybe a viable approach to improve perovskite solar cells and design other high efficiency perovskite optoelectronics such as perovskite LEDs and photodetectors.

In retrospect, we discovered the potential of   $  D_{2}O  $   as an additive to enhance the photovoltaic performance of PSC close to PCE of 21% with good stability and negligible J-V hysteresis. The solar cell device performance along with PL lifetimes significantly increases with increase in   $  D_{2}O  $   concentration, peaking at around the optimal 1 vol% concentration. Our results also showed that it is a better solvent additive candidate than   $  H_{2}O  $  . Transient kinetics revealed the reduced trap-assisted recombination as the dominating mechanism responsible for the rise in the fill factor. A significant improvement in charge carrier diffusion lengths in 1%   $  D_{2}O  $   added samples was also observed, as compared to the reference perovskite films. FTIR and NMR spectroscopies confirmed   $  N-H_{2}  $   as the preferential isotope exchange site on deuteration and alteration of the FA/MA composition ratio. DFT calculations proved the decrease in   $  PbI_{6}  $   phonon frequencies in deuterated-FA perovskite lattice.

This, in turn, helps to stabilize the   $  PbI_{6}  $   structure and weaken the electron–LO phonon (Fröhlich) coupling, thus reducing the charge carrier scattering. Our findings uncover a novel and facile approach to control and improve the optoelectronic properties of halide perovskite devices.

# Experimental Section

Device Fabrication: To prepare the solar cell devices, ITO glass substrates were cleaned through sonication in 2% Hellmanex water solution. Substrates were further cleaned thoroughly by DI-water, acetone, and isopropyl alcohol before conducting plasma treatment of the ITO surface for 15 min.

The   $  (\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3})_{0.87}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})_{0.13}  $   perovskite solution was prepared by mixing FAI (1.1 M, Greatcell),   $  PbI_{2}  $   (1.16 M, TCI), MABr (0.2 M, Greatcell) and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   (0.2 M, TCI) in the mixed solvent of DMF:DMSO (4:1). Then, Csl solution (1.5 M in DMSO) was prepared and 5% vol of this solution was added to the   $  (\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3})_{0.87}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})_{0.13}  $   perovskite solution to achieve a triple A-cation perovskite. Different concentration of   $  D_{2}O  $   by volume was added into the perovskite solution to achieve corresponding volume %   $  D_{2}O  $  -perovskite solution. Different perovskite solutions were coated onto PTAA layer using two-step spin-coating program as 1000 rpm for 10 s and 6000 rpm for 30 s. Antisolvent treatment was performed by dripping 100   $  \mu  $  L chlorobenzene on the spinning substrate at the 15th second of the second program. Films were subsequently annealed at 100°C for 60 min. Thin layers of   $  C_{60}  $   (  $  \approx  $  23 nm, ETL) and BCP (  $  \approx  $  8 nm, buffer layer) were thermally evaporated on these perovskite films under high vacuum. Finally,   $  \approx  $  80 nm silver (Ag) was deposited through a shadow mask as a back contact.

Characterizations: A source meter (Keithley model 2400, USA) was used to measure the J-V characteristics of these devices. A 450 W xenon lamp (Oriel, USA) was used to illuminate the devices for the light J-V measurements. The device was selected as   $  0.1cm^{2}  $   using a black shadow mask. The crystallographic X-ray diffraction patterns of thin films coated on glass substrates were obtained using Bruker AXS (D8 ADVANCE) X-ray diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation. The cross-sectional image of the device and topography image of the thin films were recorded using field-emissionscanning electron microscopy (FESEM, JEOL, JSM 7600F). UV-Vis absorption spectra of perovskite films were collected by Shimadzu UV3600 spectrophotometer.

Steady-state photoluminescence of the perovskite films measured with excitation of 600 nm pump wavelength. The excitation pump was generated from a Coherent OPerA Solo optical parametric amplifier pumped with a Coherent Libra regenerative amplifier (50 fs, 1 kHz, 800 nm). Steady-state PL was collected on an Acton Spectra Pro 2500i spectrometer coupled with a Princeton Instruments PIXIS 400B CCD camera. The FTIR spectra were collected in the transmission mode of Perkin-Elmer FTIR spectrometer coupled with DTGS detector. The spectra were measured under ambient conditions in the scan range of 8000–4000 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  using the slit width of 1 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  with 2.2 kHz as the scanning speed. The multinuclear solid-state MAS NMR data were acquired at 14.1 T using a wide bore Bruker Avance III-600 spectrometer. See the Supporting Information for more details of the NMR spectroscopy.

Transient Characterization: A streak camera (Optronics SC-10) was used to measure the time-resolved PL of the samples. The samples were excited by 600 nm pulses from a Coherent OPerA Solo optical parametric amplifier that was pumped by a Coherent Libra regenerative amplifier.
