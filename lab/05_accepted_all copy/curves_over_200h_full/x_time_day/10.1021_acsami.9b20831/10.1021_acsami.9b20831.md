# INTRODUCTION

In the past decade, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has rapidly increased from 3.8% to 25.2% by updating solar cell materials, innovating preparation processes and optimizing device structures et al, which can be compared with state-of-the-art silicon solar cells.  $  ^{1-4}  $   Unfortunately, the organic components (such as MA  $  ^{+}  $  , FA  $  ^{+}  $  ) in the organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs always suffer volatilization and/or decomposition under the attacks by high temperature, high humidity and oxygen, therefore the stability of hybrid PSCs free of encapsulation can not meet the demands in real conditions. Using inorganic Cs  $  ^{+}  $   to make a substitution with organic ions (e.g. MA  $  ^{+}  $  ) to prepare all-inorganic

CsPbI $ _x $ Br $ _{3-x} $  ( $ x = 0, 1, 2 $  or  $ 3 $ ) can remarkably improve the temperature-resistance of PSCs. $ ^{5-6} $  However, the CsPbI $ _x $ Br $ _{3-x} $  ( $ x > 0 $ ) perovskite is also easily degraded in a humid environment, therefore the tri-bromine cesium lead bromine (CsPbBr $ _3 $ ) is regarded as one the most promising inorganic light-harvesters because of the simple preparation in ambient atmosphere without humidity control, high charge carrier mobility and good stability in harsh environments.

In 2016, Liang et al.  $  ^{7}  $  prepared the typical  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  PSC prototype with a architecture of  $  FTO/c-TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}/CsPbBr_{3}/carbon  $  free of precious organic hole-transporting material (HTM) (e.g. spiro-MeOTAD and polytriarylamine) and expensive metal back electrodes (e.g. Au and Ag), but the maximal PCE of only 6.7% was far from the organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs. The low PCE output of  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  PSC is mainly caused by two aspects: (i) The large optical bandgap of  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  perovskite layer leads to a narrow spectral absorption; (ii) There is a serious charge recombination process taking place at the interface of device and the grain boundary of perovskite crystal. Although the substitution of bromide ions by iodide ions can undoubtedly broaden the spectral response range, the introduction of iodide species significantly drags the resistance toward moisture.  $  ^{8}  $  One of the solutions to this impasse is to reduce the charge carrier recombination of all-inorganic  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  PSCs. Theoretical studies have shown that the main defects causing non-radiative recombination is the uncoordinated ions of perovskite film, which seriously deteriorate the performance of PSCs.  $  ^{9}  $  Passivating the defects easily formed on light-absorbing layer surface is crucial for the performance improvement of PSCs. Ionic liquids (ILs) are regarded as one of the ideal passivation materials in photovoltaics due to their high ionic conductivity and good heat-resistance.  $  ^{15-18}  $   For instance, 1-alkyl-4-amino-1,2,4-triazolium,  $  ^{19}  $   1-hexyl-3-methylimidazolium chloride,  $  ^{20}  $   1-ethylpyridinium chloride,  $  ^{21}  $   methylammonium acetate,  $  ^{22}  $   and 1-methyl-3-imidazolium iodide  $  ^{23}  $   have been the favorite additives for defect passivation by adding them into perovskite precursor solution, causing a markedly increased PCE and the reduced charge recombination. However, the additive engineering of ILs as well as other chemical compounds in perovskite film requires complicated optimization process caused by blending system could undergo serious phase separation resulted from the thermodynamic driving force.  $  ^{24}  $

Inspired by the preliminary results and mentioned by the remaining problems, we launch here the surface imperfections passivation of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  by modifying an IL of 1-butyl-2, 3-dimethylimidazolium chloride ([BMMIm]Cl) with an electron rich nitrogen atom and an alkyl side-chain. These group features make [BMMIm]Cl have strong modification effect on passivating the positive charge ions existing on the  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  photoactive layer surface and the ability to enhance the humidity tolerance of devices. These results suggest that the interface modification of ILs provides new opportunities for defects passivation and interface energy level alignment improvement to prepare high-performance inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSC platforms.

In this work, the n-i-p devices possessing the architecture of FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}/CsPbBr_{3}/[BMMIm]Cl/carbon  $  configuration are fabricated to investigate the passivation effect of [BMMIm]Cl IL. During annealing process, the [BMMIm]Cl molecules (Figure S1) anchor to  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  perovskite surface due to the bonds formed between N atom containing lone pair of electrons and the uncoordinated positively charged ions (  $  Pb^{2+}  $  and  $  Cs^{+}  $ ), as shown in Figure 1a. Simultaneously, the hydrophobic alkyl chain in [BMMIm]Cl molecule is arranged along the perovskite surface, which provides hydrophobicity to increase the moisture-resistance of  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  perovskite film. To corroborate the mechanism of the [BMMIm]Cl modification on  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  perovskite film at molecular scale, the surface molecule binding of pristine and [BMMIm]Cl-modified perovskite films are measured by XPS, as shown in Figure S2. After deconvolution, two typical peaks are obtained in the high-resolution XPS spectra for Pb 4f and Cs 3d (Figure 1b, 1c), i.e., the Pb  $  4f_{7/2}  $  and Pb  $  4f_{5/2}  $  peaks, the Cs  $  3d_{5/2}  $  and Cs  $  3d_{3/2}  $  peaks, respectively. It is observed that the binding energy of Pb 4f and Cs 3d shifts 0.24 eV and 0.59 eV to lower energy in the spectra of [BMMIm]Cl-modified perovskite films in comparison with that of the pristine  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $ , respectively.
