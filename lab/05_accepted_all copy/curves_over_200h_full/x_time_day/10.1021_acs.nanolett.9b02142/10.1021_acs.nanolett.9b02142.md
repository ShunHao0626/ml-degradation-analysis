# Introduction

The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on formamidinium lead triiodide (FAPbI $ _3 $ ) has exceeded 24.0%. $ ^{1,2} $  In a PSC composed of a multilayered film consisting of electron/hole transporting layers and a halide perovskite (HP) layer, the efficiency is influenced by each of the constituents. Until now, however, the efficiency improvement has been mainly achieved by controlling the characteristics of the perovskite itself. $ ^{3-6} $  In the future, other aspects of solar cell materials, such as their long-term stability, should be considered for their eventual commercialization. Long-term exposure of multilayered PSCs, especially the HP thin films, to moisture, heat, and light can significantly reduce the PCE of the device. $ ^{7-12} $  Obviously, many studies have focused on the degradation of HP materials in humid atmosphere, their thermal decomposition at high temperatures, and their degradation under continuous light illumination. $ ^{7-10} $

So far, however, despite the importance of HP's inherent properties changes over time, very few reports have described the natural decay of PCE during the long-term storage of the device in the dark.  $  ^{13}  $   Obviously, the associated mechanisms are not easy to understand, because of the many interconnected factors, such as the degradation of the individual material and/or the interfacial affinity between layers in the PSCs over this period. However, it is important to examine how the characteristics of an HP, that are expected to be most sensitive to long-term chemical degradation, change over time. Further, to understand the intrinsic properties of HPs, it is crucial to establish a correlation between the migration of ions or defects and the resultant natural decay in the PCE of a PSC, particularly in dark conditions where decomposition due to external factors such as light, heat, and moisture is minimal. In fact, many researchers followed the traditional analysis methods using common instruments, such as femtosecond transient absorption spectroscopy,  $  ^{14,15}  $   photoluminescence spectroscopy,  $  ^{16,17}  $   time-resolved

However, more precise investigations are needed for the relationship between the charge transport and defects, in order to fabricate high-performance PSCs with long-term chemical stability.

Recently, we reported that the addition of a certain amount of triiodide ions (  $  I_{3}^{-}  $  ) to the PSCs reduces the concentration of defects in the perovskite and thus significantly improves their efficiency.  $  ^{2}  $   In addition to evaluating the relationship between the efficiency and the defects, investigations into the relationship between the concentration of these defects and natural decay of the PCE of PSCs during aging in dark condition can provide important clues for fabricating PSCs with long-term stability. In this study, we explored the effect of long-term chemical aging on the performance of FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -based high-performance PSCs for 200 days following their initial fabrication, by comparing two representative solar cells without (denoted as control-HP) and with added I  $  _{3}^{-}  $   (denoted as low-defect HP (LD-HP)). After aging the control-HP and LD-HP for 7 days (denoted as fresh-HP) and 200 days (denoted as aged-HP) in the dark, changes in the FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   crystalline phase, the photo-generated charge carrier density, and the amount and type of defect concentration and species were compared using grazing incidence wide angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS), deep-level transient spectroscopy (DLTS), high-resolution photoemission spectroscopy (HRPES), and scanning photoelectron microscopy (SPEM), respectively. We found that the initial defect concentration of HPs affects the structural transformation, defect migration, and the concentration of O  $  _{x}  $  . The addition of I  $  _{3}^{-}  $   hindered the degradation of the PSC and improved the efficiency, which is closely related to the nature of HP with lower initial defect concentration.

To investigate how the structural transformation of control-HP and LD-HP changes over time, we conducted GIWAXS experiments for fresh-HP and aged-HP samples. The GIWAXS is a very useful technique for studying nanostructured thin films, and it can provide very important information on the structural transformation caused by the degradation or others causes inside the HP layers. As shown in Figure 2, we can observe the representative diffraction rings assigned to  $ \delta $ -FAPbI $ _3 $ -[100] $ _h $ , PbI $ _2 $ -[001] $ _t $ ,  $ \alpha $ -FAPbI $ _3 $ -[100] $ _c $ ,  $ \alpha $ -FAPbI $ _3 $ -[200] $ _c $ , and  $ \alpha $ -FAPbI $ _3 $ -[210] $ _c $  for both fresh- and aged-HP samples. The GIWAXS ring patterns of control-HP and LD-HP do not show much difference even after aging. The fitted azimuthal circular average GIWAXS 1D full spectra are displayed in Figure S2(a and b), and magnified GIWAXS 1D spectra between 0.7 and 1.1 per Å on q $ _z $  are shown in Figure S2 (c and d). As shown in Figure S2, in comparison with the GIWAXS of the two fresh-HPs, the amount of  $ \alpha $ -FAPbI $ _3 $  phase in LD-HP is slightly higher than that of the control-HP, suggesting that the addition of I $ _3^- $  during the formation process of HP contributes to the complete formation of  $ \alpha $ -FAPbI $ _3 $  phase. After aging for 200 days, the amount of  $ \alpha $ -FAPbI $ _3 $  had been reduced in both samples; however, the reduction was more severe in control-HP relative to LD-HP. When we carefully examined the base-line of GIWAXS 1D full spectra in Figure S2 (a, b), control-HP shows a rise in baseline compared to fresh and aged conditions, but LD-HP does not. We thus recognized the presence of amorphous crystalline phases in the control HP but not in the LD-HP. Thus, to precisely determine the defect species and their migration to other positions in both samples during aging, we investigated charge trap levels by means of the activation energy (  $  E_{a}  $  ) and trap density (  $  N_{t}  $  ) determined under the transient temperature condition from 150 to 330 K through DLTS. The DLTS is a technique that can quantitatively deduce the absolute defect concentrations and electrically active defect concentrations and the carrier binding energy to the defect.  $  ^{22}  $   In DLTS, under varying temperatures, a peak is produced when the emission rate matches the rate window (RW) determined by the positions of   $  t_{1}  $   and   $  t_{2}  $  . By repeating the temperature scan with different settings of   $  t_{1}  $   and   $  t_{2}  $  , the system filters out RW, and so each   $  T_{max}  $   corresponds to the temperature at which the trap emits carriers at that RW. The magnitude of the peak   $  \Delta C  $   gives the concentration of deep states:   $  N_{t} = 2(N_{d} - N_{a}) \Delta C/C  $  , where   $  N_{d}  $   and   $  N_{a}  $   are the donor and acceptor densities (  $  cm^{-3}  $  ), respectively,   $  \Delta C  $   is the capacitance caused by the applied pulse (pF), and C is the equilibrium capacitance (pF). Figure 3(a, b) shows the DLTS signals obtained at RW = 0.82 s  $  ^{-1}  $   for control-HP and LD-HP during aging for 200 days. As we reported previously,  $  ^{1}  $   after the first day (as-deposited), the DLTS signals showed three defect locations for control-HP, which are assigned to peaks A1, A2, and A3. Meanwhile, LD-HP showed two significantly reduced defects of A2 and A3. After 7 days (fresh-HP), control-HP displayed reduced   $  N_{t}  $   for the same defect species (A1, A2, and A3) compared to that observed on the first day. This might be related to the gradual increase in device efficiency over an initial period of approximately one week after fabrication of the PSC. # Conclusions

In summary, we investigated the degradation of HP during aging for 200 days using two representative devices (control-HP and LD-HP) with different initial defect concentrations. At first, the efficiency difference between these devices was  $ 1.3\% $  and it increased to  $ 2.6\% $  after 200 days of aging. Thereafter, the rate of efficiency reduction was the same for both devices. To understand this behavior, the structural transformation of  $ \alpha $ -FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite was investigated using GIWAXS and DLTS to determine the defect concentrations and their migration, while the chemical species acting as impurities were analyzed using synchrotron-based SPEM and HRPES. Through these analyses, the initial defect concentration was found to affect the structural transformation and defect migration. In LD-HP, the defects were reduced by chemical treatment such as I $ _3^- $  addition, the device showed less efficiency reduction than that of control-HP, and the defect concentrations were directly related to the long-term
