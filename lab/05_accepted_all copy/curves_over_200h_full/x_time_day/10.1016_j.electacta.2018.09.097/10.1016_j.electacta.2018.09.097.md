# Abstract:

Three dimensional (3-D) flower-shaped  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ -TiO $ _2 $  nano-structure has been synthesized by electrospinning and incorporated on top of sol-gel ZnO ETL to fabricate highly efficient (highest efficiency:  $ 17.25\% $ ) triple-cation (methyl ammonium, formamidinium and rubidium cations) based perovskite solar cell (PSC). The flower-based PSCs demonstrate superior photovoltaic performance compared to control ZnO or one-dimensional (1-D) fiber-shaped nano-structure ETL based devices. Nanostructured ETLs passivate the interstitial trap sites in pristine ZnO by intercalation of metal atoms in host ZnO lattice matrix and increase the n-type conductivity of the (nano-structured) ETL films by reducing the functional groups on ZnO surface. The accumulated ions at the perovskite/ETL interface are also well-distributed and hence the accumulation capacitance is significantly reduced in nanostructured ETL based PSCs, due to the branch-structured ETL network. Moreover, the nano-flower based PSC demonstrates superior charge transfer property, compared to nano-fiber based PSC owing to enhanced material crystallinity and higher effective surface area of 3-D nano-flower network, with respect to 1-D nano-fiber structure. The photo-current hysteretic phenomena are also most suppressed in nano-flower based PSC, due to mitigated electrode polarization mechanism in it. Adding to the merits, PSCs incorporating nano-flower ETL demonstrate enhanced device stability compared to the control devices, retaining about  $ 92\% $  of its initial efficiency even after a month. The enhanced device stability with nano-flower based PSC is contributed by the lower hydrophilicity, lower extent of functional surface hydroxyl group and lower content of vacant interstitial trap sites of the respective ETL film.

Keywords:  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 - TiO_2} $  composite nano-flower, ZnO ETL, perovskite solar cell, low temperature, interstitial sites, accumulation capacitance, recombination lifetime, impedance spectroscopy

# 1. Introduction:

With the power conversion efficiency (PCE) surging up to over  $ 22\% $  [1] in less than a decade, organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are considered to be a potential game changer in the photovoltaic industry in recent years [2, 3]. Apart from the high PCE, their solution-processability in low-temperature fabrication route [4-14] makes them compatible with the mass-scale commercial production via roll-to-roll process using flexible substrates. Of late, PSCs incorporating ZnO electron transport layer (ETL) [5, 12-19] have shown immense potential for the roll-to-roll technology owing to their facile fabrication technique in low-temperature, solution-processed route. However, pristine ZnO film necessitates some form of surface modifications for attaining high PCE along with PSCs, since pristine ZnO film inhibits energetic disorder induced defect states within its forbidden energy gap [7, 20], which can lower the device performance of PSC by working as recombination sites for the photo-generated charge carriers [15, 21]. To date, the modification of ZnO ETL surface for PSCs has been reported by metal doping [21-25], by ultra-violet ozone treatment [26], by adding a secondary organic ETL film [19, 27, 28] or by building one-dimensional (1-D) [29-33] or two-dimensional (2-D) [34, 35] ZnO nanostructures on compact ZnO layer. In particular, building a nanostructured network on pristine ZnO ETL film is an interesting approach for attaining high PCE with PSCs, since the nanostructured ETLs provide higher material crystallinity, higher effective surface area, as well as lower number of grain boundaries compared to thin film ETLs [36, 37], which contribute to more efficient separation and extraction of charge carriers from photo-active perovskite material [37-39]. However, the 1-D and 2-D ZnO nanostructures [29-32, 34, 40] reported so far along with compact ZnO ETL render relatively low PCE (highest PCE:  $ 13.60\% $ ) [35] for PSCs. In this regard, building a three-dimensional (3-D) nanostructured network like nano-flower on top of pristine ZnO ETL film bids fair to be an intriguing prospect, since nano-flower ETL has been reported to enhance the PCE of dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs) by virtue of its (nano-flower's) lower surface trap density and enhanced crystallinity [41, 42].

To date, for PSCs, hydrothermally grown 3-D  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nano-flower ETL has been reported on top of compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film [38, 43]; however, the reported nano-flower ETL necessitates high temperature ( $ \sim 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) processing which makes it incompatible with roll-to-roll process [44]. Moreover, the reported hydrothermal process incorporates a secondary nano-structure etching step along with concentrated acid solution [38, 43], that impedes its facile processability. Besides, the time-consuming, sequential steps of hydrothermal process for the nano-flower fabrication [38, 43] make it less attractive for the mass-scale batch production, which can be substantially simplified via inexpensive nano-structure deposition technique like electro-spinning [37, 41, 42, 45]. In addition to these, the pristine  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  based nano-flower ETLs exhibit relatively low electron mobility ( $ \sim 0.1\mathrm{cm}^2\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1} $ ) [41], which limits the efficient charge transfer from the nano-structure network to the external electrodes. In this regard, a novel  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 - TiO_2} $  composite nano-flower network on top of compact ZnO

film is a promising prospect, since   $  SnO_{2}  $   demonstrates ~2-3 orders of higher electron mobility (  $  \sim10-125~cm^{2}~V^{-1}~s^{-1}  $  ) [41] compared to   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL. Another motivation behind the implementation of such novel 3D composite nano-flower is the fact that a dual   $  SnO_{2}-TiO_{2}  $   thin film ETL has been successfully implemented for PSCs [46] recently, which render enhanced PCE compared to pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   or   $  TiO_{2}  $   based devices due to the reduced surface defect density and enhanced n-type conductivity of the   $  SnO_{2}-TiO_{2}  $   dual ETL film [46]. In this work, we have synthesized  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ - $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  composite 3-D nano-flower by electro-spinning and incorporated it on top of compact ZnO ETL film to fabricate highly efficient [maximum PCE:  $ 17.25\% $ ], triple cation based  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.57}\mathrm{FA}_{0.38}\mathrm{Rb}_{0.05}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  (MA: methyl ammonium, FA: formamidinium, Rb: Rubidium) PSC. The photovoltaic performance of the nano-flower based PSC has been compared with  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ - $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  composite 1-D nano-fiber and conventional ZnO ETL based perovskite devices. The nano-flower based PSCs demonstrate enhanced PCE, compared to nano-fiber or pristine ZnO based devices. To comprehend the enhanced device performance with nano-flower ETL, the interstitial vacancy and the content of chemisorbed species of the ETL films have been quantitatively analysed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and their concomitant influence on the material work-function and hence the electron conductivity has been investigated with ultra-violet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurement. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) and Mott-Schottky characterization have been conducted to delve into the charge transfer characteristics and carrier recombination phenomena of the fabricated devices. We have also conducted a month-long, degradation study to comprehend the device stability of the PSCs. The enhanced device stability with nano-flower based ETL has been investigated with the surface wettability characterization of the ETL films and by the quantitative analysis of the functional groups prevailing on the respective ETL surfaces. It is worth mentioning that nucleation-aided restricted volume solvent annealing (RVSA) method [47] was used to fabricate the perovskite film on top of ZnO ETL in our work. This method inhibits thermal decomposition of perovskite film on basic ZnO surface [28, 48]. This can be attributed to the enhanced device stability of the reported PSCs, as compared to prior perovskite literature incorporating ZnO as ETL [28].

# 2.2 Synthesis of   $  SnO_{2}-TiO_{2}  $   composite nano-flower

The  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 - TiO_2} $  nano-flowers were prepared through electro-spinning method (Scheme-1(A)) using a large scale electro-spinning pilot plant employing 100 needles (Bioinicia, Fluidnatek, Spain). As a typical procedure, titanium (IV) n-butoxide (1.56 g), tin (II) chloride dehydrates (1.24 g) and PVAc (4 g) were added to a solution of DMF (25 ml), ethanol (8 ml) and acetic acid (4 ml). The resulting sol was contained in an airtight bottle and stirred for  $ 24\mathrm{h} $  before electro-spinning. The as-prepared solutions were filled in a syringe attached to a tube to be connected to the machine. The electro-spinning parameters were set as: applied voltage  $ 24\mathrm{kV} $ , injection rate  $ 12\mathrm{mL}\mathrm{h}^{-1} $ , the distance between the collector and tip of the needle assembly is  $ 16\mathrm{cm} $ , the relative humidity of  $ 40 - 45\% $ . Solid samples were collected from the collector and annealed at  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 3\mathrm{h} $  at a heating rate of  $ 0.5^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\min^{-1} $  to remove the polymeric components and to allow the nucleation and growth of  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 - TiO_2} $  nano-flowers.

# 2.3 Synthesis of   $  SnO_{2}-TiO_{2}  $   composite nano-fiber

The  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ - $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  nanofibers were prepared through electro-spinning method using the same electro-spinning pilot plant as for nano-flower (Scheme-1(B)). The polymeric solution contained similar components as nano-flower, except for ethanol; however the concentration of the components were different. First the stock solution of PVAc (11 gm) was prepared in DMF (89 ml) and stirred it continuously until the formation of clear solution. After that 4.5 ml of above stock solution was taken and mixed with 2 ml of titanium (IV) n-butoxide, then various wt% (1, 2 and 5%) of tin (II) chloride dehydrates and 0.5 ml glacial acetic acid were added in it. The resulting sol was contained in an airtight bottle and stirred for 24 h before electro-spinning. The subsequent nucleation and growth process of the nano-fibers from the as-prepared solutions were identical as nano-flowers.

# 2.4 Device Fabrication

At the beginning of the device fabrication, patterned ITO/glass substrates (Lumtec inc.) were washed using an ultrasonic bath, sequentially with alkaline (Hellmanex III) soap water, deionized (DI) water, acetone and isopropanol solvents. After the substrate cleaning, a thin layer of sol-gel ZnO film (annealing temperature:  $ 140^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) was deposited on the substrate in a similar manner as described in our earlier works [49, 50]. For nano-flower and nano-fiber based devices, a dispersion solution of the electro-spun nano-materials were prepared in anhydrous isopropanol (3 mg/ml) solvent. The as-synthesized nano-structures (by electrospinning) were ground into small pieces and incorporated into the dispersion solution. The dispersion solution was spin-coated on the compact ZnO film at 3000

The x-ray diffraction (XRD) characterization was performed with PANalytical Empyrean powder and thin-film XRD machines. # 3. Results and Discussion:

# 3.1 Photovoltaic Performance:

$ \mathrm{SnO_2 - TiO_2} $  composite nano-flower and nano-fiber networks were built on compact ZnO film to work as ETLs along with normal structured PSCs. Control devices were also fabricated based on pristine ZnO ETL film. For further referencing to this work, pristine ZnO,  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 - TiO_2} $  composite nano-flower and nano-fiber ETLs will be termed as ZnO, NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO ETL, respectively and the corresponding devices will be named as ZnO, NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO PSCs, respectively. The schematic presentation for the device structure of the fabricated PSCs has been shown in Fig. 1(A). An optimization study was conducted to find out the optimum spin-rate for the dispersion solution of NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO ETL along with the fabricated PSCs (Table S1-S2). As comprehended from Table S1-S2, the optimum spin-rate for both the NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO dispersion solutions is 3000 rpm, which demonstrates an average PCE of  $ 16.68\% $  and  $ 15.24\% $  for the NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO PSCs, respectively. The control ZnO PSCs show relatively low photovoltaic performance, with the average PCE being  $ 13.75\% $  (Table 1).

# 3.2 Material and Optical Characterization of ETL Films

We have conducted the powder XRD of the electro-spun  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 - TiO_2} $  composite nano-flower and nano-fiber to compare their material crystallinity [Fig. 2(A)-2(B)]. As comprehended from Fig. 2(A)-2(B), both nano-flower and nano-fiber exhibit identical XRD characteristic peaks; however, the nano-flower demonstrates higher material crystallinity, relative to the nano-fiber. We have also performed XPS elemental analysis of the ETL films to investigate the modification of pristine ZnO surface due to the nano-structured network. The XPS survey curves for both the NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO ETL films exhibit  $ \mathrm{Ti}_{2\mathrm{p}} $  and  $ \mathrm{Sn}_{3\mathrm{d}} $  peaks (Fig. S3), which ensure the presence of the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 - TiO_2} $  composite nano-structures on top of ZnO film. Corresponding high resolution XPS spectra for  $ \mathrm{Ti}_{2\mathrm{p}} $  and  $ \mathrm{Sn}_{3\mathrm{d}} $  for both NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO ETL films have been presented in Fig. S4(A)-(B) and Fig. S4(C)-(D), respectively. Fig. 3(A), 3(B) and 3(C) depict the high resolution  $ \mathrm{O}_{1\mathrm{s}} $  XPS spectra of ZnO, NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO films, respectively along with their respective de-convoluted Gaussian sub-peaks. The numeric values of the peak binding energies, peak width and elemental percentage of the ETL films are listed in Table S8-S10. As comprehended from Fig. 3(A)-3(C), all the three ETL films exhibit a main ( $ \mathrm{O}_{1\mathrm{s}} $  A) and a shoulder ( $ \mathrm{O}_{1\mathrm{s}} $  B) Gaussian sub-peak, while pristine ZnO ETL alone demonstrates an additional sub-peak ( $ \mathrm{O}_{1\mathrm{s}} $  C) at a binding energy of  $ 531.91\mathrm{eV} $ . The integrated area ratio of main to shoulder peak  $ [(S = \frac{S_1}{S_2}) $ , as marked in Fig. 3(A)-3(C)] is relatively low in NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO ETLs compared to ZnO (ZnO: 1.15, NFL-ZnO: 1.05, NFB-ZnO: 1.05), which indicates that the nano-structured ETLs demonstrate more pronounced  $ \mathrm{O}_{1\mathrm{s}} $  B shoulder peak [53] than ZnO ETL. Moreover, as observed from Fig. 3(A)-3(C), both NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO ETL exhibit a high energy shift in the peak positions of  $ \mathrm{O_{1s}} $  XPS spectra compared to ZnO ETL. Similar high energy shifts of the  $ \mathrm{Zn_{2p(1/2)}} $  and  $ \mathrm{Zn_{2p(3/2)}} $  XPS spectra for NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO ETL films are observed from the high resolution  $ \mathrm{Zn_{2p}} $  spectra (Fig. S5). Thus, the ZnO interstitial site-induced trap-states are more suppressed in NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO ETL film, which also conforms to the enhanced average  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $  values [56] in NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO PSCs, compared to ZnO devices (Table 1).

Referring back to the  $ \mathrm{O_{1s}} $  XPS spectra of the ETL films, the additional  $ \mathrm{O_{1s}} $  C Gaussian sub-peak in ZnO ETL [Fig. 3(A)] is associated with the chemisorbed species [hydroxyl (-OH), carbonate  $ (-\mathrm{CO}_3) $ , adsorbed  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}] $  on ZnO surface [57]. As calculated from Fig. 3(A), the integrated intensity area ratio  $ (S' = \frac{S_3}{S_1 + S_2 + S_3}) $  [58] for ZnO ETL is 0.296, while the respective values for NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO ETLs are theoretically null; since no  $ \mathrm{O_{1s}} $  C sub-peak can be de-convoluted from the corresponding high resolution  $ \mathrm{O_{1s}} $  XPS spectra for these two nano-structured ETLs. The reduced chemisorbed species are expected to increase the electron mobility [56] of NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO ETLs, compared to ZnO ETL. Nonetheless, to investigate how different carbon-related functional species vary on these ETL surfaces, we can refer to the de-convoluted Gaussian sub-peaks of the high resolution  $ \mathrm{C_{1s}} $  XPS spectra of the ETL films [Fig. 3(D)-3(F)]. All the three ETL films demonstrate three Gaussian sub-peaks, where,  $ \mathrm{C_{1s}} $  A,  $ \mathrm{C_{1s}} $  B and  $ \mathrm{C_{1s}} $  C spectra are associated with the bond between non-oxygenated  $ \mathfrak{sp}^3 $  hybridized carbon atoms (C-C), the bond between atomic carbon and hydroxyl group (C-OH) and the bond related to carboxylate group (O-C=O) [59, 60], respectively. From Table S8-S10, the content of surface hydroxyl groups is the lowest in NFL-ZnO ETL, followed by those of NFB-ZnO and ZnO ETL (ZnO:  $ 1.90\% $ , NFL-ZnO:  $ 1.29\% $ , NFB-ZnO:  $ 1.35\% $ ). Moreover, NFL-ZnO ETL also exhibits the lowest amount (ZnO:  $ 4.34\% $ , NFL-ZnO:  $ 4.21\% $ , NFB-ZnO:  $ 4.45\% $ ) of carboxylate ion related functional group of the three ETLs. Due to the reduced chemisorbed functional groups, the electron mobility and hence the n-type conductivity [15, 56] in NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO ETL are expected to be higher than ZnO ETL.

Moreover, as comprehended from the C-V curves of the fabricated PSCs, NFL-ZnO PSC demonstrates the lowest capacitance value in the accumulation capacitance  $ (\mathrm{C_S}) $  region, while ZnO device exhibits the highest value of the three. # 4. Conclusion

We have synthesized  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ - $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  composite 3-D nano-flower and 1-D nano-fiber by electro-spinning and implemented them along with sol-gel ZnO ETL to fabricate highly efficient (maximum PCE:  $ 17.25\% $ )  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.57}\mathrm{FA}_{0.38}\mathrm{Rb}_{0.05}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  PSCs via a low temperature fabrication route. Nano-flower based NFL-ZnO PSC and nano-fiber based NFB-ZnO PSC demonstrate about  $ 21\% $  and  $ 10\% $  higher average PCE, respectively, compared to the control ZnO PSC. The nano-structured ETLs passivate the interstitial trap states in pristine ZnO by intercalation of metal and oxygen atoms in host ZnO lattice matrix and increase the n-type conductivity of the (nano-structured) ETL films by reducing the functional groups on ZnO surface. Due to the branch-structured network, the accumulated ions at the perovskite/ETL interface are well-dispersed in NFL-ZnO and NFB-ZnO PSCs, which contribute to lower accumulation capacitance in them, compared to ZnO device. However, NFL-ZnO PSC demonstrates superior charge extraction property, compared to NFB-ZnO device because of the higher material crystallinity and larger effective surface area of 3-D nano-flower network, with respect to 1-D nano-fiber structure. NFL-ZnO PSC, thus, demonstrates the highest recombination resistance and lifetime, the lowest contact resistance at perovskite/ETL interface and overall the most enhanced photovoltaic performance of the three PSCs under study. The photo-current hysteretic phenomena are also most suppressed in NFL-ZnO PSC, due to mitigated electrode polarization mechanism in it. NFL-ZnO PSC also demonstrates superior device stability, compared to ZnO device, retaining  $ 92\% $  of its (NFL-ZnO PSC's) initial PCE after a month. The enhanced device stability with nano-structured ETL based PSCs has been found to be correlated with the lower hydrophilicity, lower extent of functional surface hydroxyl group and lower content of vacant interstitial trap sites of the respective ETL films. Therefore, the reported composite  $ (\mathrm{SnO}_2\text{-TiO}_2) $  nano-structured ETLs exhibit intriguing prospects towards the possible mass-scale production of efficient and stable perovskite solar cells in roll-to-roll process via low-temperature fabrication route.

# Highlights

- Electrospun  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ -TiO $ _2 $  3-D nanocomposite is incorporated as ETL in perovskite solar cells.

- The nano-structured ETLs passivate the interstitial trap states in underlying pristine Zn

• Accumulation capacitance at the perovskite/ETL interface is significantly low.

- Enhanced charge extraction property of the modified ETL layer due to low recombination.

• Superior device stability retaining 92% of its initial PCE even after a month.
