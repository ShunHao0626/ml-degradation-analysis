# Abstract

Aiming at fabricating simple, reproducible and scalable perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with least time consumption, novel  $ \mathrm{CoO_x} $  hole transport layer (HTL) was first proposed and introduced in this work. The  $ \mathrm{CoO_x} $  HTL thickness was minimized to about  $ 10\mathrm{nm} $  with complete coverage on FTO substrate (F doped  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ ) by DC magnetron sputtering. The compact ultrathin HTL could minimize the incident light loss caused by cobalt ion absorption and reduce the carrier transport loss by shortening the transport path. Copper was incorporated in  $ \mathrm{CoO_x} $  lattice to address the low conductivity of  $ \mathrm{CoO_x} $  film and the energy level mismatch between  $ \mathrm{CoO_x} $  and perovskite material. # Introduction

Renewable energy is a sticking point against the background of artificial intelligence and smart devices in 21  $  ^{st}  $  century. The diversity of power supply is the curtain in the future since fossil fuel is unsustainable and environmental-unfriendly. Among various technologies, photo-electricity conversion technology is a promising candidate because of the intrinsic maturity and vast applicability. One of the most impressive events in solar energy conversion territory is the emerging of alkali lead halide perovskite photovoltaics. In 2009, when the first perovskite based solar cell (PSC) was fabricated, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) was only 3.8%  $  ^{1}  $ . Up to date, the certified value was up to 22.1%  $  ^{2}  $ . The unprecedented performance developing speed made it the focus of world attention. The amazing breakthrough on the PCE of PSCs is ascribed to several fascinating properties of perovskite materials, such as widely tunable direct band gap  $  ^{3-12}  $ , large absorption coefficient  $  ^{13-16}  $ , non-polar carrier mobility  $  ^{16-20}  $ , long carrier diffusion lengths  $  ^{19, 21-24}  $  and low non-radiative recombination rate  $  ^{25-28}  $ . Combined with the advantages of low-temperature

As an emerging photovoltaic technology, the PCEs of PSCs were high enough for mass production. The major problems in front of mass production and commercialization are process simplicity, payback time and device stability. To minimize the cost of PSC, the process and energy consumption are supposed to be simple and low. PSC with mesoporous layer generally has a calcination process at about 500  $ \square^{30} $ , which is not only time consuming but also power demanding. The payback time of mesoporous structured PSC is hence long. In contrast, p-i-n structured PSC has lower process temperature. PEDOT:PSS and NiO $ _{x} $  hole transport layer (HTL) are generally calcined at 100-150  $ \square^{31} $  and 400  $ \square^{32} $  respectively. Besides, room temperature approaches were developed as well $ ^{33-34} $ , which would dramatically shorten the fabrication duration and cut the overall cost. On the other hand, the main concern towards the commercialization of PSC is the poor stability. Perovskite lattice is sensitive to external environment, ultraviolet light $ ^{35} $ , heat $ ^{36} $  and moisture $ ^{37} $  for instance. Since perovskite absorber is sandwiched by carrier transport layer, the property of carrier transport material has an influence on the device stability. Several works have shown that inorganic carrier transport layer have superior stability compared with organic semiconductors. $ ^{38-39} $  Triggered by this, a novel inorganic hole transport layer HTL: CoO $ _{x} $  was introduced to achieve stable PSCs with simple and fast process. The preparation of   $  CoO_{x}  $   precursor took 3 days and the   $  CoO_{x}  $   film fabrication took over 3 hours (calcine at 300 ☐: 2 h, calcine at 400 ☐: 1 h, ozone treatment: 18 min). Besides, the energy consumption was not low enough since a two-step annealing was used with a highest temperature of 400 ☐. In this report, robust   $  CoO_{x}  $   HTL was deposited with least time and power consumption which would dramatically decrease the cost and shorten the payback time.

There were several challenges in utilizing   $  CoO_{x}  $   as HTL of PSC. At first, the morphology of solution processed   $  CoO_{x}  $   film is undesirable. Second, compared to metal oxide counterparts like   $  NiO_{x}  $ , ZnO,   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}  $ , the transmittance of   $  CoO_{x}  $   is low. Third,   $  CoO_{x}  $   film is inferior in conductivity. Pristine   $  CoO_{x}  $   has a conductivity of about   $  10^{-4}~S\cdot cm^{-1}{}^{41-42}  $ , which is much lower than PEDOT:PSS (1-1000 S·cm  $  ^{-1}  $ )  $  ^{43}  $ . At last, there exists a mismatch of energy levels between   $  CoO_{x}  $   and perovskite.

To improve the film quality of  $ \mathrm{CoO_x} $ , magnetron sputtering was utilized in this work to overcome the poor coverage of solution processed  $ \mathrm{CoO_x} $  to FTO. In order to enhance the film transparency, the thickness was controlled. With a low film thickness, the resistance of  $ \mathrm{CoO_x} $  film decreases. Aiming at further reduce the carrier transport loss and tune the valence band (VB) position of  $ \mathrm{CoO_x} $ , copper was introduced by co-sputtering technique to form a binary metal oxide:  $ \mathrm{Co_{1 - y}Cu_yO_x} $ . A short calcination of 30 min at 300 °C was adopted, which allows us to complete the assemble process of a PSC within 100 min.

During our optimization of   $  Co_{1-y}Cu_{y}O_{x}  $   based PSC, a versatile methodology was proposed for the optimization of carrier transport materials (CTM) for planar heterojunction PSCs. At first, the surface roughness of CTM, which influences the crystallization process of perovskite, should be low enough. Secondly, the transmittance through the available spectrum of perovskite of CTM at the light illumination side should be as high as possible for transparency determines the incident light loss. Thirdly, the conductivity should be high so that the carrier transport loss could be minimize. At last, energy level match between carrier transport materials and perovskite is of significance, energy level determines the carrier separation driving force and basic energy loss.

Even though the precursor solution concentration was low, the transmittance between  $ 400\mathrm{nm} $  and  $ 500\mathrm{nm} $  was less than 0.6 which indicated a serious incident light loss of  $ \mathrm{CoO_x} $  based PSCs for the light extinction of cobalt ions. Field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) was thus conducted to check the morphology of  $ \mathrm{CoO_x} $  prepared with different precursor concentrations (Figure 1 C and D). When the precursor concentration was low, uncontinuous  $ \mathrm{CoO_x} $  dots attached on FTO grains was observed. While when the precursor concentration was high, numerous pin-holes formed on the film. The undesirable transmittance, morphology as well as device performance stimulated us to seek new deposition approaches of  $ \mathrm{CoO_x} $ .

To identify the valence state of elements in co-sputtered films, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was conducted. The results were illustrated in Figure 3. After deconvolution with Lorentzian-Gaussian distribution function, the eight peaks comprising Co 2p spectra were coded as i-viii. As it presents in table 2, peak i-iv and peak v-viii correspond to Co (II) and Co (III) respectively. It was apparent that peak v-viii were intensified while peak i-iv were weakened after the copper introduction, which revealed the increase of  $ \mathrm{Co}^{3+}/\mathrm{Co}^{2+} $  ratio. In Figure 3 B and D, the O 1s peaks coded as I and II were assigned to oxygen orbits combined with Co (II) and Co (III) respectively. The emergence of an extra peak in O 1s spectrum of copper-doped sample (coded as III) implied the combination of copper and oxygen. The intensity variation of O 1s peaks verified the change of  $ \mathrm{Co}^{3+}/\mathrm{Co}^{2+} $  ratio as well. The peak position shift of O 1s in (C) and Co 2p in (D) suggested the substitution of Co by Cu. Copper signal was detected in  $ \mathrm{Co}_{1-y}\mathrm{Cu}_y\mathrm{O}_x $  layer (Figure 3E), the peaks at  $ 952.8\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ 932.8\mathrm{eV} $  were assigned to the  $ \mathrm{Cu}2\mathrm{p}_{1/2} $  and  $ \mathrm{Cu}2\mathrm{p}_{3/2} $  orbits of cuprous ion  $ (\mathrm{Cu}^{+}) $ .  $ \mathrm{Cu}^{+} $  doping was first introduced to improve the performance of PSCs, as it was Cu or  $ \mathrm{Cu}^{2+} $  by solution methods in previous reports.[44-46] The substitution of Cobalt ions by  $ \mathrm{Cu}^{+} $  in  $ \mathrm{Cu_yCo_{1-y}O_x} $  film would generate free holes to maintain electrical neutrality, which would conspicuously enhance the hole concentration.

On the other hand, since smoother substrate surface would reduce the heterogeneous nucleation on HTL and thus reduce the undesirable crystal growth during the nucleation period, the quality of perovskite films on   $  CoO_{x}  $   would have better quality and even grain size  $  ^{48}  $  .

Although PSCs achieved excellent performance, the major concern is whether the cells were durable under ambient conditions. PEDOT:PSS, a widely used HTL for high performance p-i-n PSCs, effectively extracts holes and blocks electrons. However, its hydrophilic and acidic properties have adverse effect on device stability.  $  ^{52}  $   In contrast, inorganic metal oxides have remarkable stability, which was obviously beneficial to the actual application of PSCs. To verify this, we monitored the PCEs of the devices based on PEDOT:PSS and   $  Co_{0.817}Cu_{0.183}O_{1.19}  $   without encapsulation (the results were presented in Figure 10). The cells were exposed under a constant temperature and humidity atmosphere of   $  30\;^{\circ}C  $   and 60% relative humidity and were stored in dark. The PCE remained above 90% of the initial value after 12 days for   $  Co_{0.817}Cu_{0.183}O_{1.19}  $   based device. While the PCE of PEDOT:PSS based device dramatically degraded to less than 50% after 10 days. This was mainly attributed to the degradation of key photovoltaic parameters: FF and   $  J_{SC}  $   versus time. Possible mechanisms were the acidic and hydrophilic characteristics of PEDOT:PSS, which would degrade the FTO electrode and perovskite absorber layer. The advantage in stability of p-type metal oxide   $  Co_{0.817}Cu_{0.183}O_{1.19}  $   was believed of great significance to the practical application of PSCs.

A DC reactive magnetron sputtering system (Shenyang Tengao Vacuum Technology Co.Ltd., JSS-600) with Cobalt and Copper target (99.99% purity) was used to deposit   $  CoO_{x}  $   with or without copper doping. In our experiment, the angle between the target and the substrate was fixed at 37° and substrate rotation speed was 15 rpm during the deposition process. The base pressure of the deposition chamber was kept at   $  4 \times 10^{-4}  $   Pa and the working pressure was around 2 Pa. DC magnetron sputtering was conducted with a constant hybrid gas flow composed of 24 sccm Ar and 12 sccm   $  O_{2}  $  . By adjusting the work pressure and sputtering power, distinctive films could be obtained with certain thickness. Prior to the deposition process, the Co and Cu target was pre-sputtered for 10 min in order to remove the residual oxide layer. The sputtered films were calcined at 300 °C for 30 min.
