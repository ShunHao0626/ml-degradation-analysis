# Interface engineering of low temperature processed all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite solar cells toward PCE exceeding 14%

# ARTICLEINFO

All-inorganic perovskite   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   has received much attention recently due to its suitable bandgap and excellent thermal stability. Herein, we demonstrated a low temperature solution process to obtain high quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films and fabricate devices with a facile n-i-p structure (ITO/  $  SnO_{2}/CsPbI_{2}Br/Spiro-OMeTAD/MoO_{3}/Ag  $  ), in which   $  MoO_{3}  $   was introduced as interfacial layer that led to high efficient charge extraction and suppressed carrier recombination. As a result, the champion cells exhibited a relatively high power conversion efficiency of 14.05% and superb fill factor of 81.5%. More importantly, unencapsulated PSCs with   $  MoO_{3}  $   interfacial layers showed outstanding stabilities with retaining 80% of initial PCE with thermal treatment at 85 °C for 140 min in ambient air, 80% of initial PCE under continuous illumination for 120 min in ambient air, and 89% of initial PCE after being stored in   $  N_{2}  $   glove-box over 60 days. Meantime, it should be mentioned that all interlayers and active layer were processed at temperature below 160 °C, and hence, this fabrication technique is promising for flexible energy devices and future commercialization.

# 1. Introduction

Hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved tremendous progress in the past few years and the champion of the certified power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) has exceeded 23%, owing to their amazing photoelectric properties coupled with low-temperature solution processability   $  [1-8]  $  . More importantly, perovskites have been promised to speed up its commercialization for future sustainable energy. One of attractive achievements is that high performance perovskites can be employed in tandem devices with silicon or Cu(In, Ga)Se  $  _{2}  $   sub-cells, which has been widely reported and the tandem cells showed excellent PCEs   $  [9,10]  $  . However, the instability of all state-of-the-art cells based on organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites is an urgent issue for its further commercialization, and mainly induced by volatile and hygroscopic organic cations   $  [11-13]  $  . Recently, extensive works   $  [14-17]  $   have demonstrated that the improved long-term stability could be achieved by defect passivation and interface modification. With the progress of the improved long-term stability, inorganic perovskites have

Among all-inorganic halide perovskites (CsPbX $ _3 $ ) (X = Cl, Br, I), CsPbI $ _3 $  based PSCs show the highest PCEs with suitable band gap of 1.73 eV and high charge carrier mobility, which is similar to the organic-inorganic perovskites [24]. Recently, Wang et al. have reported that the highly stable PTABr treated CsPbI $ _3 $  based PSCs exhibited excellent PCEs up to 17.06% [25]. Despite its high photovoltaic performance, its stability deteriorates significantly when suffering from high-humidity or high-temperature attacking [22,26,27]. Contrarily, CsPbBr $ _3 $  based PSCs show superior stability but low device performance, which is mainly depended on its large bandgap of 2.3 eV [28]. [20,21,29-31]. However,  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  perovskites can only absorb the light with the wavelength below  $ 646~\mathrm{nm} $  due to its band gap of  $ 1.92\mathrm{eV} $ , which leads to a low current density for single cells ( $ < 16\mathrm{mA/cm}^2 $ ). Thus, in order to further improve device performance, enhancing open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $ ) and fill factor (FF) should be an effective approach. For example, Nam et al. discovered the formation of perovskite films through optimal annealing temperature and the device achieved an efficiency of  $ 10.7\% $  with a high  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  of  $ 1.23\mathrm{V} $  [31]. Yuan et al. demonstrated that  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  solution post-processing was a facile strategy to passivate the deep trap state of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film, resulting in a PCE of  $ 12.34\% $  with a high  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  of  $ 1.29\mathrm{V} $  [32]. Bai et al. have fabricated solar cells with optimal precursor solution temperature of  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  that exhibited the best PCE stabilized up to  $ 14.81\% $  with a  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  of  $ 1.22\mathrm{V} $  [20]. Despite these excellent results, achieving high  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  is a difficult way to obtain high device performance. Another novel approach is to promote the FF of device via interface passivation and interface modification. For instance, Liu et al. introduced the ZnO@C60 bilayer as electron transport layer to gain a PCE of up to  $ 13.3\% $  with a high FF of 0.77 [23]. Zhang et al. have fabricated efficient  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  solar cells with dimensionally graded heterojunction, resulting in an overall PCE of  $ 12.39\% $  with a high FF of 0.80 [33]. Moreover, Bian et al. have fabricated a graded bandgap structure with multiple treatments and achieved a high FF of 0.78 [34]. Although these works contributed to improving device performance, it is highly imperative and urgent to develop a facile route to balance between high efficiency and stability for PSC commercialization.

In this work, we proposed a low temperature route for desired   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film and demonstrated that both   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF could be improved significantly by introducing   $  MoO_{3}  $   layer to act as interfacial layer in solar cells with n-i-p architecture. Moreover,   $  MoO_{3}  $   layer could suppress interfacial trap-assisted recombination and enhance hole transport. As a result, high efficiency PSCs based on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films with PCE of 14.05% have been achieved via a sequential graded thermal annealing method and reached an ultra-high FF of 81.5%. In particular, the PCE of device with the modification of   $  MoO_{3}  $   showed excellent thermal stability and long-term stability without any encapsulation.

# 2. Result and discussion

The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskites were deposited via one step precursor solution to obtain high quality film. Compared to conventional single thermal annealing process, the optimized sequential graded thermal annealing process significantly enhanced the film quality, as shown in the SEM images and XRD spectra (Figs. S1 and S2). Hence, in this study, the optimized sequential graded thermal annealing route was used to obtain   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film. Meanwhile, the low-temperature, solution-processed   $  SnO_{2}  $   electron transport layer (ETL) was used to replace the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL to simplify the device fabrication process. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern of perovskite   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   deposited on   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL was shown in Fig. 1a. It is observed that the intense diffraction peaks appeared at 14.6°, 20.8°, and 29.5°, corresponding to the (100), (110) and (200) typical crystal planes of cubic phase, respectively, which suggested the good crystallinity of the resulting film. Furthermore, the surface morphology of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film deposited on   $  SnO_{2}  $   was examined using a high-resolution scanning electron microscope (SEM), as shown in Fig. 1b. It is clear that a fully covered surface morphology with dense and large crystallized grains (  $  \approx1\mu m  $  ) was observed for the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film. Moreover, no grain boundaries or intragranular defects could be observed. To further characterize the optical properties, the absorbance spectrum of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film was measured by UV-vis spectroscopy, as shown in Fig. 1c. The absorption spectrum showed an optical band-edge around   $  \approx653nm  $  , indicating an optical bandgap of 1.90 eV. Furthermore, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra of the perovskites on the ITO glass substrates and   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL were collected from the air side, and it is observed that the PL peak of perovskites on   $  SnO_{2}  $   (645 nm) was blue-shifted, compared with perovskites on ITO glass

(649 nm). The blue-shifted peak indicated that the high quality perovskite film with reduced tap states was obtained based on   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL [35]. Meantime, the pervoskite film based on   $  SnO_{2}  $   showed efficient PL quenching, indicating the effective electron charge transfer and extraction from perovskite to   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL. The similar results were revealed with the time-resolved PL, as shown in Fig. 1d. The carrier lifetimes were extracted from TR-PL curves and the values were listed in Table S1. It is notable that the carrier lifetime for the perovskite film on   $  SnO_{2}  $   substraite (1.27 ns) was significantly lower than that of the film on ITO glass (2.12 ns). This result further indicated that the photogenerated charge carrier could be efficiently transported to   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL, which would play an important role in optimizing the performance of PSCs based on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film.

To further verify the high quality of the perovksite   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, the n-i-p planar cell with a simple structure of ITO/  $  SnO_{2}/CsPbI_{2}Br  $  /Spiro-OMeTAD/  $  MoO_{3}/Ag  $   was fabricated, as showed in Fig. 2a. And the corresponding energy level diagrams of different components in PSCs were shown in Fig. 2b. The energy levels of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and   $  MoO_{3}  $   layer were measured with ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS), as shown in Fig. S3, and the valence band maximum (VBM) values can be obtained according to the formula of VBM = 21.22 - (  $  E_{cutoff} - E_{onset}  $  ). The VBM energies of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and   $  MoO_{3}  $   films were calculated to be 5.65 and 5.14 eV, respectively. The other energy level values are obtained from previous studies [31,36,37]. As an anode interfacial layer in organic solar cells,   $  MoO_{3}  $   is considered as an excellent choice to improve the hole injection, block the electrons, and reduce the charge carrier recombination [38,39]. In this work,   $  MoO_{3}  $   was introduced between Spiro-OMeTAD and Ag layer to reduce voltage loss and charge carrier recombination, and hence enhance   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF. It is clear that the energy level of   $  MoO_{3}  $   (valence band: -5.14 eV) aligns well with the Spiro-OMeTAD (HOMO: -5.2 eV) and the perovskites (valence band: -5.65 eV), which indicates that holes could efficiently transport from perovskites to anode. Meantime, the higher conduction band of   $  MoO_{3}  $   could form an electron-transport barrier to minimize possible charge recombination loss at the anode, resulting in the increased device performance (Fig. 2b).

To further investigate the quality of the perovskite   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film and effect of the   $  MoO_{3}  $   layer, the current density – voltage (J - V) characteristics of PSCs with and without   $  MoO_{3}  $   were presented in Fig. 3a. Meantime, the efficiency distribution performance of PSCs based on different conditions were shown in Fig. 3b and summarized in Table 1. Without   $  MoO_{3}  $   as interfacial layer, the PSCs exhibited lower   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF of 1.04 V and 73%, respectively. The low   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF were the main reasons for the limited PCE. It is obvious that the PSCs modified with   $  MoO_{3}  $   outperformed those without   $  MoO_{3}  $ , with significantly enhanced   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF of 1.10 V and 81%, respectively, leading to a higher average PCE of 13.5%. The   $  MoO_{3}  $   thickness was also optimized and found that 8 nm provided the best performance (Fig. S4). It is notable that the short circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of PSCs with   $  MoO_{3}  $   was slightly higher than the one without   $  MoO_{3}  $ . Similar results have been verified by incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra due to the efficient charge transfer and suppressed recombination for PSCs with   $  MoO_{3}  $ , as shown in Fig. 3c. The integrated current densities of the PSCs with or without   $  MoO_{3}  $   were 14.8 mA/cm² and 14.7 mA/cm², respectively. It is clear that the integrated current density matched well with those extracted from J - V measurements, which indicated the reliability of the devices. The corresponding steady state outputs of current density and steady-state efficiency at the maximum power point have been given in Fig. 3d. The stabilized PCE output for the PSC with   $  MoO_{3}  $   was located at 13.1% which was higher than that of conventional route of 11.0%. These results have further confirmed the reliability and validity of our measured device performance.

To investigate the charge transfer and recombination processes of PSCs with  $ \mathrm{MoO_3} $  layer, transient photocurrent (TPC) and transient photovoltage (TPV) measurements were carried out. As shown in Fig. 5a, the photocurrent decay lifetime of the PSCs with  $ \mathrm{MoO_3} $  layer  $ (0.68\mu s) $  was significantly lower than the conventional device  $ (1.01\mu s) $ , indicating that the device with  $ \mathrm{MoO_3} $  layer possessed more efficient charge transfer and extraction process. Meantime, the results of TPV measurement shown in Fig. 5b revealed the charge carrier recombination process. It is found that the charge recombination lifetime of the PSC with  $ \mathrm{MoO_3} $  layer  $ (112\mu s) $  was higher than that of conventional device  $ (44\mu s) $ , indicating the efficiently suppressed charge recombination. was a linear relationship between  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  and light intensity on a semi-logarithmic scale with a slop of  $  n k_{\mathrm{B}}T / q  $ , where  $  q  $  is elementary charge,  $  k_{\mathrm{B}}  $  is Boltzmann constant, and  $  T  $  is absolute temperature [42]. The slope of the PSCs with  $  \mathrm{MoO}_3  $  (1.66) was lower than that (2.21) extracted from the curve of PSCs without  $  \mathrm{MoO}_3  $ , which further verified that suppressed charge recombination appeared in PSCs with  $  \mathrm{MoO}_3  $  layer. Furthermore, the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were performed to investigate the charge carrier recombination in PSCs based on  $  \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br}  $  film. Herein, the EIS spectra were measured at an applied bias of  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  with a frequency range from 1 MHz to 100 Hz and 20 mV AC amplitude under working condition. The Nyquist plots were shown in Fig. 5d and the fitted parameters were listed in Table S2. It is clear that the PSCs with  $  \mathrm{MoO}_3  $  layer exhibited a higher recombination resistances ( $  R_{\mathrm{rec}}  $ ) value compared with conventional devices, indicating that the effectively suppressed charge recombination existed in PSCs with  $  \mathrm{MoO}_3  $  layer. As a result, the significant increase of  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  and PCE could be achieved due to the low voltage potential losses ( $  E_g - qV_{\mathrm{oc}}  $ ) and suppressed charge recombination. All these results are consistent with device characterization results as discussed above.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we introduced  $ \mathrm{MoO}_3 $  as interfacial layer to efficiently

improve the efficiency and stability of the PSCs based on all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film. Moreover, the PSCs based on   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL with   $  MoO_{3}  $   interfacial layer showed efficient charge transport, enhanced charge extraction and suppressed charge carrier recombination. As a result, the device performance, thermal stability, light-soaking stability and long-term stability were significantly enhanced. The champion cell had a relatively high average PCE of 13.5%, which represented a 21% improvement compared with the conventional device. Moreover, all the metal oxide transport layers and perovskite layer were processed at low temperature, and compatible with flexible substrates. These results provide a facile approach and important interface design insights toward the widespread application in high-efficiency and low-temperature all-inorganic PSCs for commercialization.

# 4.2. Film formation and device fabrication

The indium tin oxide (ITO) glass substrates   $  (10\ \Omega\ \mathrm{sq}^{-1})  $   were cleaned by ultrasonic treatment with detergent, deionized water, acetone and ethyl alcohol for 20 min and dried with nitrogen. Then, the ITO substrates were treated with UV-ozone for 20 min to remove organic residues.   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloid precursor was diluted to 5% with deionized water. A thin layer of   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanoparticle film was spin-coated at 4000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 150 °C for 30 min.   $  PbI_{2}:PbBr_{2}:CsI  $   (0.6 M: 0.6 M: 1.2 M) were dissolved in DMSO (1 mL) and stired all night. The prepared precursor solution was filtered with filter (0.22 μm). The precursor solution was spin-coated on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   substrate at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Then the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film annealed via sequential graded thermal annealing process at 50 °C for 3 min and 160 °C for 10 min. Then, the Spiro-OMeTAD film was spin-coated on the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskites film at 1000 rpm for 10 s and 4000 rpm for 30 s. The Spiro-OMeTAD solution contained 72.5 mg Spiro-OMeTAD, 18 μL of LiTFSI stock solution (520 mg/mL in acetonitrile) and 29 μL of FK209 solution (300 mg/mL in acetonitrile) and 29 μL of tBP. A thin film of   $  MoO_{3}  $   (8 nm) was thermally evaporated under vacuum with evaporation rate of 0.3 Å/s. Finally, 100 nm Ag electrodes were thermally evaporated to finish the device fabrication. All the devices had an effective area of 7.5 mm² defined by the metal shadow mask.

# 4.3. Materials and device characterization

All the devices were measured via using Keithley 2400 under

standard solar simulator with an intensity of   $  100 \, mW/cm^{2}  $  . The system was calibrated against a NREL certified silicon reference solar cell. All the measurements of the solar cells were performed under ambient atmosphere at room temperature without encapsulation. IPCE measurements were carried out on the SCS10-X150 systems (Zolix instrument. Co. Ltd). XRD measurements were performed by Bruker D8 Advance XRD. The UV-vis absorption spectra were measured with Perkin-Elmer Lambda 950 spectrophotometer. PL and TR-PL spectra were recorded via using the Pico Quant Fluotime 300 with a 510 nm ps pulsed laser. The morphology characterization of the films was measured by SEM (JSM-7800F). XPS measurements were performed by the Escalab 250Xi with a source of monochromatic Al-Ka (1486.6 eV). Transient photocurrent (TPC) measurement was performed with a system excited by a 532 nm (1000 Hz, 3.2 ns) pulse laser. Transient photovoltage (TPV) measurement was performed with the same system excited by a 405 nm (50 Hz, 20 ms) pulse laser. A digital oscilloscope (Tektronix, D4105) was used to record the photocurrent or photovoltage decay process with a sampling resistor of   $  50 \, \Omega  $   or   $  1 \, M\Omega  $  , respectively.
