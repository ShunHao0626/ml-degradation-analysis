# NbF  $  _{5}  $  : A Novel   $  \alpha  $  -Phase Stabilizer for FA-Based Perovskite Solar Cells with High Efficiency

Shihao Yuan, Fang Qian, Shaomin Yang, Yuan Cai, Qiang Wang, Jie Sun, Zhike Liu,* and Shengzhong (Frank) Liu*

The  $ \mathrm{HC(NH_2)_2^+(FA^+)} $  is a well-known substitute to  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3^+(\mathrm{MA}^+) $  for its capability to extend light utilization for improved power conversion efficiency for perovskite solar cells; unfortunately, the dark cubic phase ( $ \alpha $ -phase) can easily transition to the yellow orthorhombic phase ( $ \delta $ -phase) at room temperature, an issue that prevents its commercial application. In this report, an inorganic material  $ (\mathrm{NbF}_5) $  is developed to stabilize the desired  $ \alpha $ -phase perovskite material by incorporating  $ \mathrm{NbF}_5 $  additive into the perovskite films. It is found that the  $ \mathrm{NbF}_5 $  additive effectively suppresses the formation of the yellow  $ \delta $ -phase in the perovskite synthesis and aging process, thus enhancing the humidity and light-soaking stability of the perovskite film. As a result, the perovskite solar cells with the  $ \mathrm{NbF}_5 $  additive exhibit improved air stability by tenfold, retaining nearly  $ 80\% $  of their initial efficiency after aging in air for 50 d. In addition, under full-sun AM 1.5 G illumination of a xenon lamp without any UV-reduction, the perovskite solar cells with the  $ \mathrm{NbF}_5 $  additive also show fivefold improved illumination stability than the control devices without  $ \mathrm{NbF}_5 $ .

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskite materials with general formula   $  ABX_{3}  $  , where   $  A = CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}(MA^{+})  $  ,   $  HC(NH_{2})_{2}^{+}(FA^{+})  $  ,   $  B = Pb^{2+}  $  ,   $  Sn^{2+}  $  , and   $  X = I^{-}  $  ,   $  Br^{-}  $  ,   $  Cl^{-}  $  , have attracted a considerable amount of attention due to their high photovoltaic performance, in which the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on mixed cations (  $  FA_{0.85}MA_{0.15}PbI_{3}  $  ) represent the outstanding performance in terms of efficiency 22.1%.  $  ^{[1,2]}  $   Despite such prominence, the formamidinium (FA)-based perovskite is still battling against inherent phase-instability problems. Up to now, very few reports are conducted on finding effective phase stabilizer for FA-based perovskite materials.

The introduced more electronegative anions should greatly increase the electrostatic interaction with organic cations (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}/HC(NH_{2})_{2}^{+}  $  ) at grain boundaries and/or the surface so as to stabilize phase of perovskite materials.  $  ^{[9]}  $   This inspires us to focus on high electronegative ions doping on perovskite materials such as fluorine ions which can form chemical bonds with   $  HC(NH_{2})_{2}^{+}  $   through an enhanced electrostatic interaction.  $  ^{[10]}  $

To investigate the effect of   $  NbF_{5}  $   additive on the electronic property of perovskite film, the space charge–limited current technique is applied to evaluate quantitatively the electron trap-state density using a device structure of fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/TiO  $  _{2}  $  /perovskite(  $  NbF_{5}  $  )/[6,6]-phenyl C61 butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM)/Ag.  $  ^{[6,22]}  $   Figure 2a,b shows the typical dark current–voltage characteristics of the perovskite without and with   $  NbF_{5}  $   additive, respectively. There are two kink points on the two curves where the current quickly increases, corresponding to the trap-filled bias voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ). The trap-state density (  $  n_{trap}  $  ) is calculated using the equation of   $  n_{\mathrm{trap}} = (2\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}V_{\mathrm{TFL}})/(eL^{2})  $  , where   $  \varepsilon  $   and   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   are the relative dielectric constant of pervoskite and the vacuum permittivity, respectively; e is the elementary charge and L is the thickness of the perovskite film.  $  ^{[23,24]}  $   The trap densities   $  n_{trap}  $   are calculated to be   $  1.75 \times 10^{15}  $   and   $  0.8 \times 10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $   for the perovskite films without and with   $  NbF_{5}  $   additive, respectively. The defect density is decreased more than half in the perovskite film with   $  NbF_{5}  $  , which could be attributed to the passivation effect of F ions.  $  ^{[25]}  $

To investigate the influence of   $  NbF_{5}  $   additive on photogenerated charge carriers in the FA-based perovskite film, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra are measured. Compared to the perovskite film without   $  NbF_{5}  $   additive, the perovskite film with   $  NbF_{5}  $   exhibits a much higher PL intensity, suggesting that the nonradiative recombination rate within the perovskite film is significantly reduced.  $  ^{[26,27]}  $   In addition, the PL peak of perovskite film with   $  NbF_{5}  $   additive has a small blueshift (Figure 2c), which implies a lower trap density near the band-edge of perovskite film.  $  ^{[5]}  $   Thus, the reduced recombination loss and improved charge collection in the perovskite film with   $  NbF_{5}  $   is expected. The PL decay lifetimes is measured by time-resolved PL (TRPL) (Figure 2d; Table S1, Supporting Information) and fitted with a biexponential equation.  $  ^{[28]}  $   The perovskite film with   $  NbF_{5}  $   displays a longer carrier lifetime (109 ns) than that of the control film without   $  NbF_{5}  $   (50 ns). The prolonged lifetime of the PL decay may be ascribed to the decreased concentration of defects at grain boundaries and/or the surface, as well as the increased crystallinity of pervoskite film with   $  NbF_{5}  $  .

The effect of   $  NbF_{5}  $   additive on the surface composition and electronic states of FA-based perovskite is investigated by the XPS. The entire XPS spectra are shown in Figure S6 in the Supporting Information. The XPS spectrum of the perovskite film with   $  NbF_{5}  $   shows a peak located at 685 eV corresponding to F 1s (Figure 4a), and two peaks at 207.6 and 210.3 eV correspond to Nb   $  3d^{5/2}  $   and Nb   $  3d^{3/2}  $   transitions (Figure 4b), respectively, demonstrating the presence of   $  F^{-}  $  and   $  Nb^{5+}  $   ions in the perovskite film with   $  NbF_{5}  $   additive. Under ambient conditions, the   $  \alpha  $  -phase of FA-based perovskite is easy to undergo a conversion to   $  \delta  $  -phase, resulting in serious degradation in photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{[32]}  $   The degradation process is even accelerated under high relative humidity (RH).  $  ^{[33]}  $   To evaluate the effect of   $  NbF_{5}  $   incorporation on phase stability, the high RH stability of perovskite films with different contents of   $  NbF_{5}  $   is investigated (Figure S7, Supporting Information). Figure 6a shows the photographs of perovskite films stored under high RH of 75–80% in a humidity controller for different time. It is clear that the perovskite film with   $  NbF_{5}  $   can maintain the black color even after 20 h. In contrast, the black color of perovskite film without   $  NbF_{5}  $   gradually faded along with time and almost completely bleached after 20 h, which suggests that the film undergoes a phase change or decomposition. The SEM image for perovskite film with   $  NbF_{5}  $   has no obvious change after storage in high RH for 5 h (Figure S8, Supporting Information). In comparison, the perovskite film without   $  NbF_{5}  $   shows a clear change with the formation of more white dots on the film surface and the area of   $  \delta  $  -phase is enlarged after 5 h.

To further investigate the phase change of perovskite films without and with   $  NbF_{5}  $   under high RH, XRD spectra

of perovskite without and with   $  NbF_{5}  $   are measured after different period time of storage under high RH. The initially prepared perovskite films show the characteristic cubic perovskite peaks. For the pervskite without   $  NbF_{5}  $ , the cubic peaks begin to weaken and disappear, whereas new diffraction peaks at   $  2\theta = 11.58^{\circ}  $ ,   $  26.06^{\circ}  $ , and   $  41.46^{\circ}  $   rise during exposure time (Figure 6b). After 20 h the peaks of cubic phase completely disappear and the control perovskite is transferred from   $  \alpha  $ -phase to   $  \delta  $ -phase. In contrast, no change is observed in the XRD spectra for perovskite film with   $  NbF_{5}  $   even after 20 h (Figure 6c). These results indicate that the perovskite film with   $  NbF_{5}  $   additive has enhanced high humidity/phase stability and will likely lead to improved operational lifetime for the PSCs as well.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary,   $  NbF_{5}  $   is developed as a novel additive to improve the crystallinity, surface morphology, electronic property, and phase stability of FA-based perovskite materials. XPS, FTIR, and NMR results indicate that there is a strong interaction between the   $  NbF_{5}  $   and elements of perovskite, which is the key reason for the improved phase stability. Device Fabrication: FTO/glass substrates were cleaned by ultrasonication in isopropanol, acetone, and water for 10 min, respectively. Then, the substrates were dried by nitrogen flow and treated by an ultraviolet for 15 min before the deposition of   $  TiO_{2}  $  .   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers (40 nm) were grown by chemical bath deposition on washed FTO substrates, as reported by the groups before.  $  ^{[39,40]}  $   The perovskite precursor solution (1 M) was made by mixing   $  PbI_{2}  $  , FAI, and MAI in DMF/DMSO (4/1, v/v). The FA/MA mole ratio was maintained at 0.85/0.15. To prepare the perovskite film with   $  NbF_{5}  $   additive, the commercial available   $  NbF_{5}  $   was added into perovskite precursor solution with different contents. The mixed solution was spin-coated on top of   $  TiO_{2}  $   at 1000 rpm for 10 s and then at 4000 rpm for 45 s while dripping 150 mL chlorobenzene onto the substrate between 20 and 25 s into the second spinning step. Then, the substrates were heated at 150 °C for 30 min on a hotpot to obtain dark perovskite films. Spiro-OMeTAD solution, 90 mg of 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-dipmethoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene was dissolved in 1 mL of chlorobenzene and mixed with 36 μL t-BP, and 22 μL Li-TFSI solution (bis-trifluoromethane sulfonamide lithium salt dissolved in acetonitrile. The Spiro-OMeTAD solution with a concentration of 520 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   was spin coated onto dark perovskite film at 5000 rpm for 30 s to form an hole transport layer. Finally, a gold top electrode (=80 nm) was deposited on Spiro-OMeTAD by thermal evaporation under high vacuum.

Device Characterization: XRD spectra were obtained using a D/MAX 2400 diffractometer with Cu Ka radiation (Rigaku). The absorption was taken out by UV–vis NIR spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer, Lambda 950). Water contact angles were measured using a dataphysics OCA 20. Surface morphologies and cross-section images of inorganic perovskite films and the PSCs were characterized by SEM (HITACHI, SU-8020). PL (excitation at 532 nm) spectra were measured using a FLS980 spectrometer (Edinburgh Instruments Ltd). TRPL spectra were measured with a PicoQuant FluoQuant 300. XPS was carried out by using a photoelectron spectrometer (ESCALAB250Xi, Thermo Fisher Scientific). FTIR spectra were measured with a Bruker Vertex 70. NMR was performed by using JNM-ECZ400S/L1 with a frequency of 400 MHz and deuterated DMSO was used as solvent to dissolve   $  FA_{0.85}MA_{0.15}PbI_{3}  $  ,   $  FA_{0.85}MA_{0.15}PbI_{3}+NbF_{5}  $  , FAI, FAI+NbF  $  _{5}  $  , MAI, and MAI+NbF  $  _{5}  $  . J-V characteristics of the PSCs were collected by using a digital source meter (Keithley Model 2400) under an illumination of a AM 1.5 solar simulator (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , SAN-EIELECTRIC XES-40S2-CE), as calibrated by a NREL-traceable G  $  _{5}  $  -filtered silicon reference cell. The active area of solar cell was defined by a 9 mm  $  ^{2}  $   metal mask. All devices scanned with reverse and forward under standard test procedure at a scan rate of 30 mV s  $  ^{-1}  $  . EQE spectra of the PSCs were measured by a QTest Station 500TI system (Crowntech Inc., USA). The monochromatic light intensity for EQE was calibrated using a reference silicon detector.
