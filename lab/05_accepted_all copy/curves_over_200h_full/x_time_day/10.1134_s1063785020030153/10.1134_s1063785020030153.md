# Potassium Doping Effect on the Photovoltaic Performance of Perovskite Solar Cells

M. F. Vildanova  $  ^{a*}  $  , A. B. Nikolskaia  $  ^{a}  $  , S. S. Kozlov  $  ^{a}  $  , O. K. Karyagina  $  ^{a}  $  , and O. I. Shevaleevskiy  $  ^{a}  $

$  ^{a}  $   Emanuel Institute of Biochemical Physics, Russian Academy of Sciences, Moscow, 119334 Russia
*e-mail: mvildanova@sky.chph.ras.ru
Received November 27, 2019; revised November 27, 2019; accepted December 2, 2019

Abstract—We have fabricated a series of potassium-doped (K-doped) perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Structural investigations of the developed PSCs have shown that alkai doping affects the perovskite layer structure and morphology with the increase of the crystallite size and surface uniformity. K-doped samples exhibit higher photovoltaic (PV) performance and improved operational stability compared to the control series of undoped PSCs, due to retarded recombination processes.

Keywords: perovskite, solar cell, doping.
DOI: 10.1134/S1063785020030153

Hybrid organic–inorganic materials with perovskite structure   $  ABX_{3}  $  , where X (  $  Br^{-}  $  ,   $  I^{-}  $  ,   $  Cl^{-}  $  ) is an anion and A (  $  MA^{+}  $  ,   $  FA^{+}  $  ,   $  Cs^{+}  $  ) and B (  $  Pb^{2+}  $  ,   $  Sn^{2+}  $  ) are cations of different sizes, are used widely for fabrication of perovskite solar cells (PSCs), which are among the most advanced photovoltaic (PV) technologies currently available. Their efficiency is comparable to that of conventional silicon-based solar cells and it is attributed to the high conductivity and optical absorption of the perovskite layer in the visible range. The parameters of the photoactive perovskite layer depend to a considerable extent on the deposition method and the layer morphology and structure. In view of this, the methods for improving the layer crystallinity, uniformity, and quality have been studied extensively [1, 2]. Some of these methods involve the introduction of metal ions (doping). The issues of doping of each element (A, B, and X) of the material (specifically, the problem of partial substitution of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions [3, 4] and doping of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   with alkali metals [5, 6]) have been discussed. The introduction of alkali metals improves the perovskite layer crystallinity due to recrystallization of fine grains, thus reducing recombination losses and enhancing the PSC power conversion efficiency (PCE).

In the present study, the PV parameters of PSC samples fabricated with the use of photoactive potassium-doped perovskite layers have been investigated and compared to those of PSCs based on undoped systems.

The PSC fabrication process has been discussed in detail in our previous studies [8, 9].  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  was

doped with potassium ions by adding 0.02 mol KI to a 1.2 M perovskite precursor solution. Reference PSC samples based on undoped systems and PSCs with doped photoactive perovskite layers were constructed. The architecture of investigated PSCs was as follows: glass/FTO/TiO $ _{2} $ /CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ (Pb:K)I $ _{3} $ /spiro-MeOTAD/Au (FTO is fluorine-doped tin oxide and spiro-MeOTAD is C $ _{81} $ H $ _{68} $ N $ _{4} $ O $ _{8} $ , a hole conducting material). All these samples were fabricated under ambient air conditions (RH ~ 40%). The surface morphology of perovskite layers was examined with a Hitachi SU8000 (Japan) scanning electron microscope (SEM). The structural characteristics of the obtained materials were determined in XRD experiments with a DRON-3 powder diffractometer (Burevestnik, Russia) at room temperature in monochromatic CuK $ _{\alpha} $  radiation (35 kV, 28 mA). The PV parameters of PSC samples were measured under standard AM1.5G (1000 W/m $ ^{2} $ ) illumination using an Abet Technologies solar simulator (United States). The current density–voltage curves ( $ J-V $  characteristics) were measured with a Keithley 4200-SCS (United States) analyzer. The IPCE (incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency) spectra were recorded with an automated QEX10 system(PV Measurements, United States) in the 300–850 nm range. The PSCs operational stability was examined in air by measuring the  $ J-V $  characteristics periodically within a time interval of 45 days. The samples were stored with no influence of UV radiation and elevated temperatures in these experiments.

In PSCs, this deterioration is primarily due to the  $ J_{sc} $  reduction, which, structurally, is the result of partial decomposition of perovskite into  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and volatile  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{I} $  and  $ \mathrm{NH}_3 $  fractions under the influence of atmospheric moisture. This decomposition leads to a considerable reduction in conductivity of the perovskite layer. The  $ FF $  reduction is also associated with the formation of structural defects in the deteriorating perovskite layer: electron-conducting and hole-conducting layers are brought into direct contact, and the shunt resistance decreases. The degradation plots of potassium-doped PSCs in air demonstrate more stable  $ J_{sc} $  and  $ FF $  values (Fig. 3b). The obtained results confirm that doping with alkali metals makes the perovskite layer more resistant to oxidation [12]. Therefore, the introduction of potassium ions into the perovskite material in PSCs allowed us to improve both their PV characteristics and their stability by suppressing the degradation processes.

In summary, the photoactive layers of perovskite   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   doped with potassium ions were fabricated, and PSCs based on them were constructed. Comparative studies of the photovoltaic characteristics of PSCs based on undoped and doped photoactive layers revealed significant advantages of doped systems. It was found that doping improves the structural and morphological characteristics of the perovskite layer. This improvement can be explained by the suppression of recombination losses and the enhancement of transport properties of the photoactive layer. In addition, PSCs based on doped layers were demonstrated to be considerably more stable than undoped samples in operation in a high-humidity environment. The use of photoactive perovskite layers doped with potassium ions made it possible to increase the stability of PSCs and raise their power-conversion efficiency by 15%.
