# Passivation of Bulk and Interface Defects in Sputtered-  $  NiO_{x}  $  -Based Planar Perovskite Solar Cells: A Facile Interfacial Engineering Strategy with Alkali Metal Halide Salts

Namrata Pant,  $  ^{*}  $  ,   $  ^{\perp}  $   Ashish Kulkarni,  $  ^{\perp}  $   Masatoshi Yanagida, Yasuhiro Shirai,  $  ^{*}  $   Syuhei Yashiro, Masatomo Sumiya, Tsutomu Miyasaka, and Kenjiro Miyano

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Nickel oxide (NiO $ _x $ ) (deposited by sputtering (sp)) is a promising hole transport layer (HTL) for inverted planar perovskite solar cells. However, poor CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  crystallization, elimination of CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3^+ $ , and formation of residual PbI $ _2 $  grains, induced by defects present on the surface of sp-NiO $ _x $ , have limited the device efficiency. Herein, a facile approach is reported to passivate the surface defects in sp-NiO $ _x $  and simultaneously induce complete perovskite crystallization (without residual PbI $ _2 $  grains) via modifying the sp-NiO $ _x $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  interface with various alkali metal halide salts (AMHSs). Comprehensive film and device characterizations reveal the additional influence of AMHSs, especially cesium bromide (CsBr), on the structural, morphological, photophysical, and photovoltaic performance. It is found that incorporation of a CsBr interlayer significantly improves the perovskite crystallization, producing high-quality MAPbI $ _3 $  films with enlarged grain sizes (without any residual PbI $ _2 $  grains) contrasting without the CsBr-interlayer case. CsBr (and other AMHSs) additionally reduces the band tail states and passivates the surface defects in sp-NiO $ _x $  (as revealed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy and photodeflection spectroscopy), thereby suppressing interfacial disorder and recombination centers and improving the overall charge collection property across the sp-NiO $ _x $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  interface. This leads to improvement in the device efficiency (with active area = 1 cm $ ^2 $ ) and long-term operational stability at the maximum power point under continuous illumination for 8000 s and against ambient atmosphere for  $ \sim $ 2200 h. On the basis of the results, a possible crystallization process is discussed which provides insights into the engineering of the sp-NiO $ _x $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  interface rendering improved device performance.

KEYWORDS: nickel oxide, interface engineering, alkali halide salts, perovskite, solar cells

Varieties of HTLs have been explored to improve the performance and stability of PSCs.  $  ^{9,10}  $   Among various HTLs, nickel oxide (NiO  $  _{x}  $  ) has attracted significant attention due to its high optical transparency, wide band gap, deep-lying valence band, and inorganic nature making it more stable against oxygen and moisture.  $  ^{11}  $   While NiO  $  _{x}  $   HTL can be deposited by various processing routes,  $  ^{12-14}  $   deposition by sputtering (sp) has garnered recent research attention because of suitability for large-area roll-to-roll deposition, for monolithic tandem configuration especially on the textured or rough silicon surface and deposition at room temperature and low cost.  $  ^{15,16}  $   Moreover, a perovskite device employing sp-NiO  $  _{x}  $   HTL has shown high stability under continuous light illumination (up to 4000 h), signifying its importance for efficient and stable large-area PSCs.  $  ^{17}  $   Despite such advantages, PCE of an sp-NiO  $  _{x}  $   HTL based CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite device (in most of the reports) is still quite low mainly due to low open-circuit voltage (V  $  _{OC}  $  ) and short-circuit current density (J  $  _{SC}  $  ).  $  ^{18-21}  $   As shown in Supporting Information Table S1, the perovskite device efficiency based on undoped sp-NiO  $  _{x}  $   HTL lags far behind that of solution-processed NiO  $  _{x}  $   and/or even regular structure based devices. Despite various efforts, which include doping,  $  ^{22}  $   varying and controlling the composition, annealing the sp-NiO  $  _{x}  $   film in ambient atmosphere,  $  ^{23}  $   and tuning the film thickness,  $  ^{19}  $   the efficiency has remained low. Very recently, we performed a series of investigations  $  ^{24-27}  $   and reported the influence of sp-NiO  $  _{x}  $   on perovskite crystallization leading to loss of CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{2}^{+}  $   and I  $  ^{-}  $   from the perovskite structure and appearance of PbI  $  _{2}  $   grains at the perovskite grain boundaries (emerging from the bulk). This generated additional defect/trap sites not only in the bulk of the perovskite and the HTL/perovskite interface but even at the ETL/perovskite interface (due to unreacted PbI  $  _{2}  $   grains on the surface of perovskite) and thus low CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite device efficiency.  $  ^{26,27}  $   We further reported that the excess Ni  $  ^{3+}  $   species (due to the presence of NiOOH and Ni  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $  ) on the surface of sp-NiO  $  _{x}  $   cause interfacial disorder leading to poor perovskite crystallization,  $  ^{25-27}  $   and a very recent report by Boyd et al. described a similar phenomenon.  $  ^{28}  $   We hypothesize that suppressing the amount of Ni  $  ^{3+}  $   (on the surface of NiO  $  _{x}  $  ) can help in enhancing the overall device performance. And, recent reports on enhancing the perovskite device efficiency with surface modification of solution-processed NiO  $  _{x}  $   with ethanolamine derivative,  $  ^{29}  $   choline chloride,  $  ^{30}  $   KCl,  $  ^{31}  $   CsBr,  $  ^{32}  $   and so forth and doping with copper,  $  ^{33}  $   zinc,  $  ^{34}  $   and aluminum,  $  ^{35}  $   etc., support our hypothesis. However, it is important to mention here that the conductivity of stoichiometric NiO (originally an insulator) is attributed to the self-doping mechanism of NiO for which oxidation of Ni  $  ^{2+}  $   to Ni  $  ^{3+}  $   is essential.  $  ^{14}  $   Even though it is convenient to control the surface properties of sp-NiO  $  _{x}  $   by varying the deposition parameters (during sputtering deposition), the additional treatment seems necessary to suppress any surface defects that might be detrimental to perovskite crystallization and the overall device performance.  $  ^{26}  $   Therefore, investigation on the modification of the sp-NiO  $  _{x}  $ /perovskite interface is crucial to understand and mitigate the influence of sp-NiO  $  _{x}  $   on perovskite crystallization and simultaneously suppress defect/trap centers within the bulk and at the interface to improve the device efficiency and stability.

Interfacial modification of perovskite and charge transport layers with alkali metal halide salts (AMHS) has been previously reported for regular structure devices with the metal oxide ETL as the underlayer. For instance, incorporation of CsCl and CsBr at the   $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface, as reported by Li et al.,  $  ^{36,37}  $   improved the perovskite device efficiency and stability against UV light. Modification of the   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface with KCl and KI improved the perovskite coverage with enhanced grain size and suppressed defect centers to eventually enhance the performance.  $  ^{38}  $   Simultaneously, in the inverted structure devices, surface modification of PEDOT:PSS with NaI has been shown to eliminate the vacancies resulting in enhanced device stability.  $  ^{39}  $   Very recently, Zhang et al. reported that modifying solution-processed   $  NiO_{x}  $   with CsBr helps in tuning the lattice strain, resulting in improved device efficiency.  $  ^{32}  $   Although CsBr surface modification in solution-processed   $  NiO_{x}  $   has been reported, the surface modification with AMHSs (and even CsBr) in suppressing the negative effect of sp-  $  NiO_{x}  $   (excess   $  Ni^{3+}  $   species) and inducing complete   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite crystallization and its resultant device performance (in a large area) have rarely been investigated. According to previous reports, the thermal expansion coefficients and Young's moduli of AMHSs, especially CsBr, are close to that of perovskite.  $  ^{40,41}  $   From this, it can be inferred that interface modification of sp-  $  NiO_{x}  $   and perovskite with AMHSs might help in promoting better perovskite crystallization and simultaneously suppress the influence of sp-  $  NiO_{x}  $   (  $  Ni^{3+}  $   species) and enhance device efficiency, which motivated us to investigate the current approach.

Herein, we modify the   $  sp-NiO_{x}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite interface by incorporating various AMHSs such as potassium iodide (KI), sodium iodide (NaI), and cesium halide CsX (  $  X = I^{-}  $  ,   $  Br^{-}  $  , and   $  Cl^{-}  $  ). Among these, the devices with CsBr interlayer show the best performance (in   $  1 cm^{2}  $   device active area). As found in this study, incorporating AMHSs could effectively reduce band tail; suppress defects in   $  sp-NiO_{x}  $  , thereby inducing complete perovskite crystallization with high-quality   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films having enlarged and uniform grain sizes (without any residual   $  PbI_{2}  $   grains at perovskite grain boundaries); enhance charge carrier extraction; and suppress recombination. Additionally, a possible route illustrating the effect of   $  sp-NiO_{x}  $   on perovskite crystallization and how the AMHS effectively passivates the surface defects of   $  sp-NiO_{x}  $   and   $  NiO_{x}/perovskite  $   interfacial disorder is discussed. The resultant device demonstrated enhanced PCE of 15.7 and 22.5% under 1 Sun illumination and 1000 lux, respectively, with an active cell area of   $  1 cm^{2}  $   and showed stable efficiency against continuous 1 Sun illumination held at maximum power point tracking for 8000 s and against ambient atmosphere (relative humidity (RH) = ~40–60%) for 90 days (~2200 h).

Because the CsBr interlayer based device showed the best efficiency, we further investigated the effect of different concentrations of CsBr on device performance. The schematic illustration of the device architecture is depicted in Figure 1a. Device characteristic J-V curves and the photovoltaic parameters (collected from 20 devices in each case) of without and with different concentrations of CsBr are shown in Figure 1b and Figure 1c-f, respectively. From the device J-V curves (Figure 1b) and PCE box plot (Figure 1f), it is evident that 4 mg/mL concentration of CsBr modification is an optimized condition for obtaining the best-performing device. This indicates that by depositing a CsBr interlayer from 4 mg/mL solution concentration, a uniform layer is formed which helps in mitigating defect centers and improves perovskite crystallization and interfacial charge transport property. Details on the morphology and photophysical properties will be discussed in the next section. Further increasing the concentration of CsBr (to 5 mg/mL) leads to a reduction in the device performance, most probably due to increased resistance at the sp-NiO  $  _{x}  $  /perovskite interface. We have limited our investigation up to 5 mg/mL concentration because upon further increasing the concentration, CsBr was insoluble in the mixture of water and IPA. The best device efficiency showed   $  J_{SC} = 22 \, mA/cm^{2}  $  ,   $  V_{OC} = 1.09 \, V  $  , fill factor (FF) = 0.65, and PCE = 15.7% (active area = 1 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ). Since devices with CsBr interlayer showed enhanced efficiency (Figure 1b,f), we focus on further shedding light on the effect of CsBr on sp-NiO  $  _{x}  $   thin film and the structural, morphological, and photophysical properties of CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite film.

2. Effect of Alkali Metal Halide (CsBr) Salt on   $  sp-NiO_{x}  $   Thin Film. The top surface scanning electron micrograph (SEM) image of   $  CsBr/NiO_{x}  $   film, as shown in Figure S4a, obtained from 4 mg/mL solution showed an insignificant difference in comparison to pristine   $  NiO_{x}  $ . On increasing the concentration (5 mg/mL), aggregates of CsBr were observed on the surface of   $  sp-NiO_{x}  $   (Figure S4b) which could increase the resistance at the interface and thereby reduction in the device efficiency. The top surface SEM image of perovskite deposited on pristine   $  sp-NiO_{x}  $   showed small residual   $  PbI_{2}  $   grains (Figure 4c) near the grain boundaries, evidencing the effect of   $  Ni^{3+}  $   (on   $  sp-NiO_{x}  $  ) on the perovskite crystallization.  $  ^{26,27}  $   With the CsBr modification, these   $  PbI_{2}  $   grain formations were significantly suppressed, as shown in Figures 4d and S9, showing large and uniform perovskite grains with well-defined grain boundaries which explains enhancement in   $  J_{SC}  $   and thus the efficiency of the cell.

Although the perovskite morphology showed insignificant change with the change in the CsBr concentration (Figures 4d and S9), it was interesting to observe significant changes in the photophysical properties. In addition to playing a role in light absorption, the perovskite layer is also a medium for charge transport, which significantly depends on the film quality thereby governing the device performance. To ascertain the influence of the CsBr layer on the photophysical properties of the perovskite film, we characterized the perovskite films deposited on  $ \mathrm{sp - NiO_x} $  and  $ \mathrm{sp - NiO_x / CsBr} $  with photoluminescence (PL), and the results are shown in Figures 4e and S10a,b. The PL and TRPL were measured on the  $ \mathrm{ITO / NiO_x / } $  perovskite and  $ \mathrm{ITO / NiO_x / CsBr / perovskite} $  coated substrates, and the measurement was performed by shining the laser on the perovskite side. The significant difference in the PL peak intensities with the increase in the concentration (Figure S10a) depicts the clear effect of CsBr on the perovskite film quality. The enhanced PL peak for the perovskite film deposited on  $ \mathrm{NiO_x / CsBr} $  can be associated with enhanced radiative recombination or suppressed nonradiative recombination attributing to suppressed  $ \mathrm{Ni}^{3 + } $  species, reduction in the band tail, and complete and uniform perovskite crystallization (no residual  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ ) and is consistent with XRD, SEM, and PDS results. The larger grain size and continuous perovskite film ensures lesser bulk defect and trap sites, compared to the perovskite film with small grains and residual  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . Moreover, with the increase in the concentration of CsBr, the PL peak intensity increased up to  $ 4\mathrm{mg / mL} $  and thereafter it reduced, as shown in Figure S10a, most probably due to aggregation of CsBr. The increase in the PL peak intensity was also accompanied by a slight blue shift (Figure S10b), signifying the reduction in trap/defect sites in the bulk of the perovskite[26] and at the interface. Further, the charge carrier dynamics were quantified with time-resolved PL (TRPL), where the curves were fitted to biexponential decay function:  $ y = A_{1}\exp \left(-\frac{t}{\tau_{1}}\right) + A_{2}\exp \left(-\frac{t}{\tau_{2}}\right) $ . The decay curves, as shown in Figure 4f, demonstrate an enhanced lifetime of charge carriers for perovskite film deposited on  $ \mathrm{CsBr / NiO_x} $  substrate compared to pristine  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ , indicating reduced defect/trap sites. We attribute such reduced trap assisted recombination to be due to both superior thin film crystallinity of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  grown on  $ \mathrm{NiO_x / CsBr} $  and the quality of the resulting interface attributing to the passivation effect (suppression of  $ \mathrm{Ni}^{3 + } $  species) and reduced band tail. The complete perovskite crystallization, uniform and large grains

with suppressed nonradiative recombination, and enhanced carrier lifetime significantly attribute to enhancement in   $  J_{SC}  $  ,   $  V_{OC}  $  , and device performance. The perovskite crystallization under the direct influence of   $  sp-NiO_{x}  $   and CsBr modified   $  sp-NiO_{x}  $   is schematically illustrated in Figure 5. The perovskite crystallization under the direct influence of   $  sp-NiO_{x}  $   (with a higher amount of   $  Ni^{3+}  $   species) leads to the elimination of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}  $  and   $  I^{-}  $  from the perovskite crystallization, thereby creating vacancies and residual   $  PbI_{2}  $   grains at the grain boundaries (Figure 5a). On the other hand, CsBr modification, as shown in Figure 5b, suppresses the   $  Ni^{3+}  $   species and reduces the band tail, resulting in uniform perovskite crystallization with suppressed bulk and interfacial defects.

4. Device Performance and Stability. The hysteretic behavior in the device J-V characteristic curve underestimates the actual device performance.  $  ^{51}  $   Hysteresis is reported to be caused by several factors, such as ion migration,  $  ^{52}  $   ferroelectricity,  $  ^{53}  $   crystal and interfacial defects,  $  ^{7}  $   and nature of interface,  $  ^{54}  $   etc. Moreover, reports have shown that inverse device architecture shows negligible hysteresis in comparison to regular   $  TiO_{2}  $   based architecture, which is due to balance charge transport across the interface in the former case.  $  ^{55}  $   However, the device characteristic J-V curve of pristine   $  NiO_{x}  $   based PSCs, as shown in Figure 6a, showed hysteretic behavior indicating poor interface quality and imbalanced charge carrier extraction. On the other hand, the negligible hysteresis in the J-V curve for the CsBr modified sp-  $  NiO_{x}  $   based device indicates that the charge carriers are effectively extracted across the interface.  $  ^{56}  $   This further suggests that the defects at the sp-  $  NiO_{x}  $ /perovskite interface are significantly suppressed by the CsBr modification. The improvement in   $  J_{SC}  $   could also be explained not only by the improved transmittance for CsBr modified   $  NiO_{x}  $   (Figure S11) but also from the enhanced internal quantum efficiency (IQE) estimated from the reflectance as shown in Figure S12. In addition to enhancement in   $  J_{SC}  $   and   $  V_{OC}  $   for the CsBr modified case, a significant enhancement in the FF was also observed (Figure 6a), which corresponds to larger shunt resistance (  $  R_{sh}  $  ) and lower series resistance (  $  R_{s}  $  ). The larger   $  R_{sh}  $   and lower   $  R_{s}  $   for CsBr/sp-  $  NiO_{x}  $   based devices determine the ideal diode property in comparison to pristine sp-  $  NiO_{x}  $   based devices.  $  ^{27}  $   Light intensity dependence of   $  V_{OC}  $   for both pristine and CsBr modified sp-  $  NiO_{x}  $   based perovskite devices were studied to further understand the diode property and different kinds of recombination mechanisms that are prevalent in the perovskite device. The semilogarithmic plot of   $  V_{OC}  $   vs light intensity, as shown in Figure 6b, showed an ideality factor of 1.41 and 1.17 for without and with CsBr modified perovskite devices, respectively. The ideality factor closer to 1, for the CsBr modified device, indicates suppression in the trap state recombination centers and dominant bimolecular recombination and is in contrast to the pristine sp-  $  NiO_{x}  $   (without CsBr) based device.  $  ^{26}  $   Incident monochromatic photon to current conversion efficiency (IPCE) of the best-performing devices (without and with CsBr interlayer) were measured, and the results are shown in Figure 6c. The CsBr interlayer based device showed higher IPCE coverage in the entire visible range in comparison to the IPCE of a pristine sp-  $  NiO_{x}  $   based device. The integrated photocurrents obtained from IPCE spectra were 17.5 and 21.8 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   for without and with CsBr modified devices, respectively, and are in good agreement with the   $  J_{SC}  $   values observed in J-V characteristic curves. Perovskite device stability is one of the most vital issues that need to be addressed before commercialization.  $  ^{56-58}  $   Finally, we performed long-term stability tests to determine the influence of CsBr modification (with suppressed   $  Ni^{3+}  $   content) on perovskite device efficiency. Initially, we measured stability under continuous 1 Sun illumination held at maximum power point tracking (MPPT). As can be seen from Figure 6e, the CsBr modified device showed an amazingly stable efficiency of 15% up to 8000 s. The stabilized efficiency value is close to the efficiency value obtained from the J-V curve, indicating the feasibility and reliability of CsBr modification. In addition to this, the steady efficiency value implies a stable and robust interface mainly ascribing to the inorganic and robust nature of the   $  sp-NiO_{x}/CsBr  $   interface and its compatibility with perovskite. The long-term stability of the best-performing device against humidity was further measured. For this, the device was stored in an ambient atmosphere with a relative humidity of 40–50%, and J-V was measured with 5 days interval of time. The CsBr modified device showed stable efficiency without much degradation and maintaining more than 90% of its initial efficiency (Figure 6f), whereas the device performance of the pristine   $  sp-NiO_{x}  $   based device (despite encapsulation) declined to 75% of its initial efficiency after 1.5 weeks. The enhancement in the stability can be attributed to the suppression of defects in the bulk of the perovskite and also at the interface. The traps and defect sites are known to promote ion migration and eventually degrade the device performance. In the without CsBr interlayer case, the loss of   $  MA^{+}  $  and   $  I^{-}  $  from the surface (wherein we observed   $  PbI_{2}  $   grains on the surface) can cause ion migration  $  ^{59}  $   and can be the reason for the low stability of the device. The stable device efficiency against continuous light illumination and ambient atmosphere for the CsBr modified case is attributed to complete perovskite crystallization with uniform and large grain size, eliminating the influence of   $  sp-NiO_{x}  $   and substantially suppressed recombination.

# CONCLUSION

In summary, we perform modification of the  $ \mathrm{sp - NiO_x / } $  perovskite interface with various alkali metal halide salts (AMHSs). As found in this study, AMHSs (especially CsBr) modification suppresses the defect levels in the band gap of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  attributed to excess  $ \mathrm{Ni}^{3 + } $  species that are present on the surface of  $ \mathrm{sp - NiO_x} $ , resulting in a uniform and complete perovskite crystallization (without any unreacted  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ ). As a result of this, nonradiative recombination centers are significantly suppressed within the bulk and at the perovskite/ $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  interface leading to enhanced carrier lifetime and improved power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 15.6\% $  (device

active area = 1 cm $  ^{2}  $ ) under 1 Sun illumination and 22.5% under white light emitting diode with 1000 lux intensity. Moreover, the resultant device shows significant stability against continuous 1 Sun illumination for 8000 s and under ambient atmosphere for ~2200 h (~90 days). On the basis of these results, a possible perovskite crystallization process in the presence of excess Ni $  ^{3+}  $  species, and how AMHS interface modification induces uniform and complete perovskite crystallization, is discussed. Though the present study is conducted for CH $  _{3}  $ NH $  _{3}  $ PbI $  _{3}  $  perovskite, a similar approach on different organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite compositions with sp-NiO $  _{x}  $  as the hole transport layer can be realized to improve the efficiency and stability. The above results demonstrate a step forward in the development and commercialization of the perovskite photovoltaics employing sp-NiO $  _{x}  $ .
