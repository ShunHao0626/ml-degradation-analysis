# 1. Introduction

Due to the increasingly serious energy crisis and environmental pollution, the development and utilization of solar energy has attracted broad attention from both industry and academia in recent years. Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) deserve the most potential candidate for their outstanding photovoltaic (PV) performance and low-cost solution processed preparation technology   $  [1]  $  . Power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has dramatically boosted from 3.8% to 25.5% since it was first reported by Miyasaka et al.   $  [2,3]  $  . Many efforts have been devoted to enhance the performance of PSCs, such as the development of new functional materials, structure optimization of PSCs devices, as well as the interface engineering at different functional layers   $  [4–7]  $  .

To our knowledge, HTMs play an important role in extracting holes from perovskite active layer and suppressing charge recombination at the anode through blocking electron [8]. At present, Spiro-OMeTAD is always deemed as the preferred HTM for high-efficiency PSCs. However, its disadvantages including complex synthesis steps and harsh purification conditions make it ugly expensive. Moreover, the low intrinsic

The high cost of Spiro-OMeTAD and the poor stability of devices remain a critical challenge for commercialization of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Hence, the development of efficient hole-transporting materials (HTMs) seems imperative. Herein, three alkyls-modified HTMs named BTPA-C, BTPA-3C and BTPA-8C were designed and synthesized. Through tailoring their alkyl side-chain length, the structure-property relationships such as photophysical, electrochemical properties and the photovoltaic performance of corresponding PSCs were systematically investigated. Results demonstrate that the HTMs exhibit similar energy level, absorption and emission spectra. However, through tailoring their alkyl side-chain length, the film-forming and hole-transporting ability of HTMs can be greatly affected. Compared to BTPA-C or BTPA-8C, BTPA-3C with a moderate side chain length was endowed with the highest PCE of  $ 18.60\% $ , which was even comparable to that of PSCs based on Spiro-OMeTAD. Meanwhile, compared to the latter, PSCs based on BTPA-3C exhibited significantly improved stability at a relative humidity of  $ 45 \pm 5\% $ , maintaining more than  $ 80\% $  of the initial efficiency after aging for 30 days.

However, since the hygroscopicity of the salt as well as ion migration between hole-transporting layer (HTL) and active layer, the stability of devices will decrease [9,10]. Otherwise, more investigations have been conducted to investigate why PSCs lost their efficiency under prolonged thermal stress. Three main classes including inorganic HTMs, polymeric HTMs and organic-small-molecule HTMs are always utilized in PSCs. The organic-small-molecule HTMs surpasses inorganic and polymeric HTMs by merits of their flexible structure, low cost, solution-processibility and fine-tuned optoelectronic properties   $  [13–15]  $  . Many references demonstrated that the length and type of alkyl side chains on conjugated polymers also do crucial effect on molecular packing and thin-film morphology, which may ultimately affect PSCs performance [17-19].

Chen and co-workers achieved a compromise between steric interactions and morphological coverage by optimizing alkyl substituents of spiro-acridine-fluorene-based HTMs, yielding an impressive PCE of  $ 16.6\% $  [20]. Li and co-workers developed potential donor-acceptor small molecules (BTTI) as HTMs for normal planar PSCs [21]. Through tailoring its alkyl side-chain length, a PCE of  $ 19.69\% $  was realized. Kim et al. proved that the crystallinity and charge mobility of the anthracene derivatives may be improved by optimizing the side chain length [22]. However, the reports mentioned above are insufficient in systematic understanding of the alkyl side chains effects on HTMs.

Benzodithiophene core attracts a wide interest because of its symmetry and planarity structure as well as the rigid fused aromatic system, which can enhance electron delocalization, intermolecular interactions and thus improve charge transport. Liu and coworkers introduced benzodithiophene unit as the core to develop a new HTM (DERDTS-TBDT), the corresponding PSCs were endowed with high PCE for its matched energy levels and high hole mobility [23]. Li and coworkers synthesized two novel benzodithiophene-based HTMs H1 and H2, fluorinated strategy was used in H2 to improve the stability of the corresponding devices [24]. Herein, inspired by the outstanding thermal stability, large conjugated systems, and outstanding photoelectrical properties of benzodithiophene core [25], three novel benzodithiophene derivatives HTMs with different alkyl side chains 4,4'-(4,8-dimethoxybenzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']dithiophene-2,6-diyl)bis(N,N-bis(4-methoxyphenyl)aniline) (BTPA-C), 4,4'-(4,8-dipropoxybenzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']dithiophene-2,6-diyl)bis(N,N-bis(4-methoxyphenyl)aniline) (BTPA-3C) and 4,4'-(4,8-bis(octyloxy)benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']dithiophene-2,6-diyl)bis(N,N-bis(4-methoxyphenyl)aniline) (BTPA-8C) were reported. Triarylamine branches were introduced into the core to tune its energy levels, thermal stability and hole mobility. The effect of alkyl chains length on film morphology, optoelectronic properties as well as the photovoltaic performance of the PSCs was investigated systematically. Results indicated that although the target HTMs were endowed with desired energy level, HTM with the shortest alkyl side chain (BTPA-C) led to poor solubility and the one with the longest alkyl side chain (BTPA-8C) destroyed hole transportation ability greatly. Conversely, BTPA-3C with moderate side chain length was qualified with the highest hole mobility and the target PSCs obtained an optimal PCE of  $ 18.60\% $  , which is comparable to that of Spiro-OMeTAD-based PSCs  $ (18.74\%) $

Additionally, the stability of unencapsulated devices based on different HTMs was also measured. Long-term stability of the unencapsulated devices was tracked at a relative humidity of   $  45 \pm 5\%  $   under   $  25^{\circ}C  $  . Fig. 6a displays the changing process of photovoltaic performances during the long-term stability tests for a representative PSC device. It can be seen that after 30 days of aging, the devices based on BTPA-8C can still maintain 85% of the initial PCE, and BTPA-3C-based device can also reach more than 80% of the initial PCE. In contrast, the PCE of BTPA-C and Spiro-OMeTAD devices respectively decreased by 30% and 40% under the same conditions.

Water contact angles of the HTMs film coated on the surface of perovskite layer were measured to better understand their humidity stability (Fig. 6b). The water contact angle increases with the extension of alkyl side chain, from  $ 71.5^{\circ} $  (BTPA-C) and  $ 74.6^{\circ} $  (BTPA-3C) to  $ 76.8^{\circ} $  for BTPA-8C, while the smallest contact angle of  $ 63.6^{\circ} $  was obtained by Spiro-OMeTAD-based film. The trend of contact angle basically coincides with the results of stability results. Hence, it can be concluded that hydrophobicity of HTL is responsible for the different stability of devices. To further prove this, film morphology change of perovskite coated with different HTMs is recorded by SEM images under ambient condition with a relative humidity of  $ 45 \pm 5\% $  (Fig. 6c). It is observed that after 30 days, many voids appeared in the BTPA-C and Spiro-OMeTAD-based films, while BTPA-3C and BTPA-8C-based films deserved scarcely any change. Degradation of PSCs is mainly attributed to the decomposition of the perovskite which may result from corrosion by moisture and oxygen [40]. Consequently, the hydrophobicity caused by longer alkyl side chain in BTPA-3C and BTPA-8C film could be beneficial to resist the moisture incursion, which prevents erosion of the interfaces and passivates the voids or pinholes. Meanwhile, compared with Spiro-OMeTAD, less doping dose in novel HTMs can effectively reduce the adverse effect of ion migration on perovskite stability.

# 3. Conclusions

Three benzodithiophene-based small molecule HTMs with C1, C3 and C8 alkyl side chains were reported in this study. Molecules with shorter alkyl chain suffer from poor solubility and result in an inferior morphological coverage over the perovskite layer. While longer alkyl chain of HTMs destroy the ability of hole extraction and transfer, leading to a lower PCE for the PSCs. BTPA-3C with a moderate length of side chain achieved a compromise between factors mentioned above and

yielded an impressive PCE of 18.60%. It is obviously higher than that of BTPA-C (17.38%) and BTPA-8C (16.08%) based devices, and even comparable to the that of Spiro-OMeTAD based devices (18.74%). Furthermore, BTPA-3C based PSCs were endowed with impressive stability and reproducibility than that of BTPA-C and BTPA-8C, which retained over 80% of its initial PCE after 30 days under a relative humidity of   $  45 \pm 5\%  $  . This study provides a promising perspective to design novel and efficient HTMs for high-performance PSCs by optimizing their side chains.

Anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and chlorobenzene (CB) for device preparation were purchased from Aladdin. Spiro-OMeTAD, Li-TFSI, tert-butylpyridine (t-BP) and FTO were purchased from Advanced Election Technology Co., Ltd. Lead iodide (PbI), lead bromide (PbBr), methylamine bromide (MABr) and formamidine iodine (FAI) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Cesium iodide (CsI) was purchased from Taiwan Lumtec Co., Ltd.

# 4.2. Instrumentation and characterization

The   $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR and   $  {}^{13}  $  C NMR spectra were recorded on Bruker AVANCE IIITM HD 400 MHz Nano BAY. UV–vis absorption and fluorescence spectra were measured by Thermo evolution 300 UV/Visible spectrometer. Cyclic voltammetry curves of HTMs were measured in dichloromethane (0.1 mol L  $  ^{-1}  $   1-Bu  $  _{4}  $  NPF  $  _{6}  $   and 10  $  ^{-3}  $   mol L  $  ^{-1}  $   HTM) with scanning rate of 50 mV s  $  ^{-1}  $   (CHI760D, CH Instruments, Inc., USA). TGA and DSC were recorded on a TA Q20 instrument. TGA measurement was operated at a heating rate of 15 °C min  $  ^{-1}  $   from 20 to 800 °C under nitrogen atmosphere. DSC measurement was recorded at a heating rate of 10 °C min  $  ^{-1}  $   from 20 to 300 °C under N  $  _{2}  $   atmosphere. SCLC was conducted by a Keithley (Model 2400) digital source meter. SU8010 scanning electron microscope was used to explore the surface and cross-section morphologies at a voltage of 10 kV. AFM-based experiments were measured with an atomic force microscope (Dimension icon, Bruker, Germany). Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was carried out at a IVIUM electrochemical workstation with a bias voltage of 0.8 V. J-V curves of the devices were measured by a Keithley (Model 2400) digital source meter under 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $   illumination (AM1.5G).

# 4.3. Devices fabrication

The structure of PSCs devices applied in our research was FTO/c-TiO2/m-TiO2/perovskite/HTM/Au. The FTO glass substrate was cleaned with detergent, deionized water, ethanol and isopropanol in sequence and dried with argon. Compact TiO₂ layer was prepared by spray pyrolysis. TiO₂ precursor solution, which was attained by adding 860 μL of titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) and 570 μL of acetylacetone to 10 mL of 2-propanol, was sprayed on FTO substrate surface with an oxygen gas stream at 450 °C to form compact layer. After spraying, the substrate was naturally cooled to room temperature after holding for 5 min. Mesoporous TiO₂ layer was obtained by spin coating the dispersion (4500 rpm for 20 s) of mesoporous slurry in ethanol (mass ratio 1:6) which stirred for 6 h on the surface of dense layer, and then sintered at 450 °C for 30 min in muffle furnace. Cesium-containing triple cation perovskite solution (Cs₀.₀₅(FA₀.₈₃MA₀.₁₇)₀.₉₅Pb(Br₀.₁₇I₀.₈₃)₃) was achieved according to the reported literature [41]. Solution 1 were prepared with MABr (0.2 M), FAI(1 M), PbI₂(1.1 M) and PbBr₂(0.22 M) in a mixture of anhydrous DMF/DMSO (v/v: 4/1). Solution 2 was obtained by dissolving the CsI in DMSO (1.5 M). Perovskite solution was spin coated through a two-step program at 1000 and 6000 rpm for 10 and 30 s respectively. During the second step, 100 μL of chlorobenzene was poured on the spinning substrate for 15 s as antisolvent prior to the end of the program. The substrates were then annealed at 100 °C for 1 h in a nitrogen filled glove box. As for hole-transporting layer, the concentrations of three novel HTMs and Spiro-OMeTAD were 20.0, 72.3 mg/mL in chlorobenzene. The molar ratio of additives for HTMs was: 0.5 and 3.3 for Li-TFSI and TBP respectively. Then, 50 μL HTMs solution was spin coated at 4000 rpm for 20 s on the prepared perovskite film. To improve PCE, the hole-transporting layer need to be oxidized under the air environment for 12–24 h. At the end, PSCs devices was finished totally by vacuum evaporation of 80 nm gold top electrode on the HTL.
