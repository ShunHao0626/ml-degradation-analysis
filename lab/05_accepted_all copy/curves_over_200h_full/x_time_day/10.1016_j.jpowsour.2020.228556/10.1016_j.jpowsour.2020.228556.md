# GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT

# ABSTRACT

4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP), the only liquid phase constituent in hole transport layers (HTLs) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs), volatilizes easily to induce the aggregation, hydration, and ion penetration of lithium salts, thereby seriously impairing the thermal stability of devices. Herein, an efficient strategy is presented to address this issue by developing a novel thermally-stable metal-organic framework (MOF) In-Pyia with active pyridyl sites to replace the volatile t-BP. The In-Pyia-modified PSCs realize enhanced power conversion efficiency (PCE) with low performance fluctuation   $ 19.47 \pm 0.79\% $  , significantly outperforming the typical devices employing t-BP   $ 17.42 \pm 1.02\% $  . More importantly, In-Pyia fulfills a long-term effectiveness to restrain the negative morphological changes of HTL films, attributed to its robust framework and strong coordination effect between pyridine nitrogen atoms and   $ Li^{+} $  ions. Even after the overnight thermal treatment at 85 °C and subsequent exposure to the ambient environment (25 °C and relative humidity of ~40%) for 16 days without any encapsulation, the In-Pyia-modified PSCs still maintain 80.9% of the initial PCE, while below 20% is left to the t-BP-controlled devices. This is the first example involving a MOF material as the substitute of t-BP, providing a new feasible strategy to fabricate high-efficiency and thermally-stable PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have emerged as an extremely promising photovoltaic technology, due to their advantages of low cost, excellent compatibility with flexible substrates, and high power conversion efficiency (PCE)   $  [1-5]  $  . Typical PSCs possess a sandwich structure, which is composed of electron transport layers (ETLs)   $  [6-9]  $  , perovskite intrinsic layers   $  [10-15]  $  , and hole transport layers (HTLs)   $  [16-20]  $  . Investigating the functions of these components and the dynamic photovoltaic behaviors at corresponding interfaces are of great importance to effectively improve the efficiency and stability of PSCs.

Nowadays, 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis (N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) is the most commonly applied organic hole transport materials [21]. Nevertheless, pristine Spiro-OMeTAD suffers from relatively low conductivity, thereby generally requiring bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP) as additives to improve the hole transport performance [22]. The Li-TFSI acts as a p-dopant, triggering the oxidation of Spiro-OMeTAD [23,24], while t-BP is employed as a morphological controller to restrain the aggregation and hydration of lithium salts through the coordination effect with  $ \mathrm{Li^{+}} $  ions [25]. Unfortunately, as the only liquid constituent in PSCs, t-BP volatilizes easily during the fabrication and long-term storage/operation processes of devices, followed by the formation of pits and lithium salt aggregates on the surface of HTL films, obstructing the efficient hole-carrier conduction [26]. Meanwhile, the aggregated lithium salts could rapidly absorb water molecules, which seriously impair the stability of PSC devices [27]. In addition, partial  $ \mathrm{Li^{+}} $  ions, especially those have lost the bondage of volatilized t-BP, might permeate across the perovskite layers and then accumulate in ETLs, resulting in the carrier recombination [28,29]. Hence, it is extremely significant to develop other pyridine derivatives with the same functions as t-BP but higher thermal stability for enhancing the overall performance of PSCs.

Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs), broadly including metal-organic complexes (MOCs), inherit the physical/chemical stability and structure tunability from their inorganic metal ions/clusters and organic linkers, respectively. Meanwhile, MOF materials have obtained abundant pores and high specific surface area for multiple applications, such as catalysis, gas separation/storage, chemical sensing, drug delivery and ion trapping   $  [30–33]  $  . In 2013, FK209–Co(III) was first introduced into the HTLs of PSCs for improving the hole conduction properties   $  [34]  $  . Subsequently, several MOFs (MIL-125-Ti(IV)   $  [35–37]  $  , MOF-525-Zr(IV), UiO-66-Zr(IV), UiO-67-Zr(IV)   $  [38]  $  , MOF-808-Zr(IV)   $  [39]  $  , and ZIF-8-Zn(II)   $  [40]  $  ) and Cu(II)/In(III)-based MOCs   $  [41–43]  $   were employed to modify the ETLs, perovskite layers, and HTLs, exhibiting the excellent potential of MOF materials in PSC field. To our knowledge, these are the total examples of MOFs or MOCs used in PSCs, but investigations involving the replacement of t-BP by such materials have never been reported.

In this work, we designed and synthesized a thermally-stable In(III)-based MOF   $  [\mathrm{In}(\mathrm{HPyia})\mathrm{Cl}_{2}]\cdot\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{CN}  $   (In-Pyia), which was constructed by pyridine derived ligand 5-(pyridin-4-ylmethoxy) isophthalic acid   $  (\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{Pyia})  $   and   $  \mathrm{In}(\mathrm{CO}_{2})_{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}  $   block, to replace the volatile t-BP in HTLs of PSCs. Benefitting from its inherent porosity and open pyridine nitrogen sites, In-Pyia could not only effectively disperse the Li-TFSI for a uniform HTL film, but also coordinate with   $  Li^{+}  $  ions to restrain the hydration and permeation behaviors. In particular, In-Pyia possesses crystalline nature with excellent thermal stability, which endows itself with a long-term effectiveness to maintain the morphology of In-Pyia-modified HTL films. Consequently, an enhanced average PCE with low performance fluctuation of   $  19.47 \pm 0.79\%  $   was obtained for the In-Pyia-modified PSCs (  $  17.42 \pm 1.02\%  $   for the typical devices employing t-BP), and the initial PCE can be maintained over 80% (only 17.8% for t-BP-controlled device), even after the overnight thermal treatment at   $  85^{\circ}C  $   and subsequent exposure to the ambient environment (  $  25^{\circ}C  $   and relative humidity of   $  \sim40\%  $  ) for 16 days without any encapsulation.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

Solvents (AR grade) for the device fabrication were obtained commercially from Sigma-Aldrich. PC $ _{61} $ BM, FAI, MABr and CsI were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Spiro-OMeTAD was purchased from Toronto Research Chemicals, Inc. PbI $ _2 $  and PbBr $ _2 $  were obtained from Alfa and TCI. All general reagents and solvents were commercially available and used without further purification.

# 2.2. Preparation of   $  [In(HPyia)Cl_{2}]\cdot CH_{3}CN  $   (In-Pyia)

A well-stirred mixture of 5-(pyridin-4-ylmethoxy)isophthalic acid (  $  H_{2}Pyia  $  ) (27.00 mg, 0.10 mmol) and   $  InCl_{3}  $   (60.87 mg, 0.20 mmol) in acetonitrile (8.00 mL) was sealed in a 23 mL Teflon-lined stainless-steel autoclave and heated at 120 °C for 96 h. After slowly cooling down to room temperature and being washed with acetonitrile and deionized water, colorless block crystals of In-Pyia were collected in 70.5% yield (calculated by ligand).

# 2.3. Fabrication of PSCs

Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass (14 Ω m  $  ^{-2}  $  square, NSG, Japan) substrates were partially etched by zinc power and 4 M HCl solution, and ultrasonically washed with deionized water, acetone and ethanol for 30 min, respectively. Then, the substrates were blown to dry by nitrogen and treated with UV ozone for 15 min. The blocking titanium dioxide layers were prepared by mixing titanium (IV) isopropanol (175 μL) and hydrochloric acid (17.5 μL 3 M) in 1.25 mL isopropanol and spin-coating at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Then the substrates were sintered at 500 °C for 30 min and cooled to room temperature. Subsequently, PC  $  _{61}  $  BM (5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) was spin-coated onto the titanium dioxide layers at 3000 rpm for 30 s, and heated at 70 °C for 10 min. After cooling, the perovskite films were formed by further spin-coating a Cs  $  _{0.05}  $  FA  $  _{0.81}  $  MA  $  _{0.14}  $  PbI  $  _{2.55}  $  Br  $  _{0.45}  $  (1.2 M) precursor solution, which was prepared by mixing CsI, FAI, MABr, PbI  $  _{2}  $  and PbBr  $  _{2}  $  at 60 °C. Afterward, the substrates were heated at 100 °C for 15 min. Finally, the HTLs (72.3 mg of spiro-OMeTAD in 1 mL chlorobenzene with the addition of 17.5 μL of Li-TFSI solution (520 mg in 1 mL acetonitrile), and 28.8 μL of t-BP or 14.72 mg In-Pyia) were spin-coated above the perovskite films at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Lastly, Au was sublimated on top of HTL, serving as an electrode.

# 3. Results and discussion

Previous literature shows that  $ t $ -BP and Li-TFSI can form complexes based on the pyridine nitrogen atoms from  $ t $ -BP and  $ \mathrm{Li^{+}} $  ions from Li-TFSI [44]. As a result, the volatilization of  $ t $ -BP and its corrosion to perovskite layer could be weakened. Meanwhile, the aggregation, hydration, and ion permeation behaviors of lithium salts are also restrained to some extent. In this regard, active pyridyl sites is regarded as a key role for developing substitutes to replace the  $ t $ -BP constituent. With this in mind, 5-(pyridin-4-ylmethoxy)isophthalic acid ( $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{Pyia} $ ) ligand was selected as the organic linker for constructing MOF materials. As shown in Fig. 1a, there is also the electron-donating group in  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{Pyia} $  for its pyridine ring, just as the tertiary butyl in  $ t $ -BP, which is expected to enhance the functionality of pyridine nitrogen atoms.

# 3.2. Coordination effect between In-Pyia and Li-TFSI

To characterize the coordination effect between In-Pyia and Li-TFSI, fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) was applied for their mixtures with different molar ratios, as shown in Fig. 2a. The corresponding mixtures were prepared through dispersing the In-Pyia and Li-TFSI in acetonitrile by various molar ratios, followed by over 30 min of ultrasound treatment and drying at 85 °C for 12 h. Considering Li $  ^{+}  $  ion possesses 4 unoccupied orbitals (one 2s and three 2p orbitals), the investigation mainly focuses on a molar ratio range from 4:1 to 1:1 (In-Pyia: Li-TFSI). The peak at ~1548 cm $  ^{-1}  $ , which belongs to the stretching vibration of aromatic rings (benzene and pyridine rings), is selected for the further analysis. For 4:1 mixture, the selected characteristic peak shows a slight blue-shift (~1553 cm $  ^{-1}  $ ), attributed to the coordination effect between In-Pyia and Li-TFSI through the pyridyl sites and Li $  ^{+}  $  ions, which impair the electronegativity of pyridine nitrogen atoms, and enhance the aromatic property of pyridine rings (Fig. 2b) [45]. As the amount of Li-TFSI increases, more obvious blue-shifts of the selected peak are observed for 3:1 (~1556 cm $  ^{-1}  $ ), 2:1 (~1558 cm $  ^{-1}  $ ), and 1:1 (~1562 cm $  ^{-1}  $ ) mixtures, while the adjacent peaks show almost no changes. The continuous blue-shifts over the tested molar ratio range from 4:1 to 1:1 indicate that, In-Pyia could coordinate with Li-TFSI in a 1:1 mode. # 3.3. Morphological control effect of In-Pyia on HTL films

In order to investigate the morphological function of In-Pyia for HTL films, the scanning electron microscope (SEM) was performed for various hole-only samples (Spiro-OMeTAD/Li-TFSI/t-BP with 2:1:6 M ratio or Spiro-OMeTAD/Li-TFSI/In-Pyia with 2:1:1 M ratio, respectively). As shown in Fig. 3a, the top-view SEM image for the freshly prepared typical HTL film shows a few small pits on the surface, which can be ascribed to the partially volatilization of t-BP during the preparation process. For the fresh sample employing In-Pyia, a uniform and dense film without pits was obtained (Fig. 3b). It can be explained by that, the porous In-Pyia coordinates with Li $  ^{+}  $  ions through its open pyridine nitrogen atoms, taking over the function of t-BP to restrain the aggregation of lithium salts. More importantly, there is no loss of In-Pyia in the spin-coating process, benefitting from its excellent thermal stability. A more significant comparison can be observed in the top-view SEM images of 85 °C overnight thermally treated HTL films. For typical film employing t-BP, a large number of cracks appear on the surface, attributed to the rapid and complete volatilization of t-BP (Fig. 3c) [46,47], while the morphology of In-Pyia-modified sample is well maintained, as seen in Fig. 3d. As is known, moisture is one of the most threatening enemies to the PSC devices, which could accelerate the degradation of perovskite layers. Reducing the hygroscopicity of HTL films is regarded as one of the most effective approaches to improve the stability of PSCs. Considering the optimized morphology of films, it is believed that In-Pyia can effectively weaken the hygroscopicity of Li-TFSI in HTLs, especially after the thermal treatment at a relatively high temperature. To confirm this point, the corresponding hole-only samples were further characterized by FTIR. As shown in Fig. S5, both of the freshly prepared films of Spiro-OMeTAD/Li-TFSI/t-BP and Spiro-OMeTAD/Li-TFSI/In-Pyia display similar peaks of Spiro-OMeTAD, which is the major constituent of HTLs. Significantly, after the exposure to ambient environment (25 °C and relative humidity of ~40%) for 60 min, broad peak located at ~3440 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , which correspond to the O–H stretching vibration of water molecules, can be observed (Fig. 4). For fresh Spiro-OMeTAD/Li-TFSI/t-BP film before 85 °C overnight thermal treatment, the relatively uniform increase of water molecule characteristic peak should be attributed to the slow volatilization of t-BP, resulting in the continuous aggregation and hydration of partially exposed lithium salts (Fig. 4a). As seen in Fig. 4c, the fresh Spiro-OMeTAD/Li-TFSI/In-Pyia film also emerges a trend of rapid water absorption. This can be explained by that, the 1:1 coordinated lithium salts, especially those on the surfaces and/or near the channel windows of In-Pyia, could still contact with water molecules from the ambient environment. # 4. Conclusion

In summary, a novel thermally-stable In(III)-based MOF with active pyridyl sites was developed to replace the volatile  $ t $ -BP as a new morphological controller of HTL films for high-performance PSCs. It is demonstrated that, the porous In-Pyia acts like  $ t $ -BP to coordinate with  $ \mathrm{Li^{+}} $  ions through its open pyridine nitrogen atoms, effectively restraining the aggregation, hydration, and ion penetration behaviors of lithium salts. Meanwhile, benefitting from the excellent thermal stability of its framework structure, In-Pyia can fulfill a longer-term effectiveness than volatile  $ t $ -BP to prevent the negative morphological changes of HTL films during the fabrication, storage and operation processes of devices, even after the thermal treatment at a relatively high temperature. In addition, the matching energy level and positive effect on the oxidation of SpiroOMeTAD make In-Pyia a applicable additive of HTLs for more efficient hole extraction and conduction. Consequently, replacing  $ t $ -BP by In-Pyia endows the improved PSCs with a satisfactory average PCE possessing low performance fluctuation  $ (19.47 \pm 0.79\%) $ , which is superior to that of the typical devices  $ (17.42 \pm 1.02\%) $ . More importantly, a significantly enhanced long-term stability in ambient environment  $ (25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and relative humidity of  $ \sim 40\% $  ) is achieved for the In-Pyia-modified PSCs, further proving the feasibility of In-Pyia replacing  $ t $ -BP.
