# 1. Introduction

Organic cation based perovskite   $  \left(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{PbI}_{3}\right.  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) and CH   $  \left(\mathrm{NH}_{2}\right)_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{3}  $   (FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  )) thin-film solar cells have intensively investigated, which achieves high power conversion efficiency (PCE) over 20% [1–5]. The superior photovoltaic performance of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   solar cells can be explained as due to the high absorption coefficient [6], moderate reflective index [7], small exciton binding energy [8] and long exciton (carrier) diffusion length [9]. According to the theoretical prediction [10], the interaction between the organic cation and Pb-I framework is based on van der Waals force, which results in the chemical instability [11,12] and thereby corresponds to short device lifespan [13–15]. The device stability is also related to the uses of electron transport layer (ETL) and hole transport layer (HTL). For example, the PEDOT:PSS thin films [16–18] and Spiro-OMeTAD thin films [19–21] have widely used as the HTLs in inverted-type and regular-type highly efficient perovskite solar cells, respectively. In inverted-type perovskite solar cells, the PEDOT:PSS thin films can absorb water molecules which interact with the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   to form PbI  $  _{2}  $ , CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{2}  $   gas, HI vapor [22]. In regular-type perovskite solar cells, the I  $  ^{-}  $   and MA  $  ^{+}  $   in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   thin films can diffuse into the Li-doped Spiro-OMeTAD thin films, which makes the degradations of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   and Spiro-OMeTAD thin films simultaneously [23]. In addition, the partial substitution of iodide with bromide can reduce the halide vacancies and thereby decreases the hysteresis characteristics in current density-voltage (J-V) curve of the perovskite solar cells [42]. # 2. Experiments

MA $ _{x} $ Cs $ _{1-x} $ Pb(I $ _{x} $ Br $ _{1-x} $ ) $ _{5} $  precursors are prepared by mixing the MAPbI $ _{3} $  and CsPbBr $ _{3} $  precursor solutions. Transparent MAI crystals (117.6 mg) and PbI $ _{2} $  brown powders (346.8 mg) are dissolved in a 0.5-mL dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO)/γ-butyrolactonr (GBL) mixture (1:1, v/v), which results in a 1.5 M MAPbI $ _{3} $  precursor solution. CsBr white powders (53.2 mg) and PbBr $ _{2} $  white powders (91.8 mg) are dissolved in a 0.5-mL DMSO, which results in a 0.5 M CsPbBr $ _{3} $  precursor solution. PCBM powders (10 mg) are dissolved in a 0.5 mL chlorobenzene (CB). The perovskite precursor solution and PCBM/CB solution (2 wt %) are stirred at 400 rpm for 4 h under room temperatures.

In the materials characterizations, the   $  \mathrm{MA}_{x}\mathrm{Cs}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{I}_{x}\mathrm{Br}_{1-x})_{3}  $   thin films are fabricated on top of the glass substrates by using the one-step spin-coating method with a washing-enhanced nucleation (WEN) process [43–47]. The spin rate and time for the deposition of perovskite thin films are 5000 rpm and 20 s, respectively. A 0.3-mL toluene or iodobenzene (IB) is used as the washing solvent during the WEN process. After the spin coating of the perovskite precursors, the   $  \mathrm{MA}_{x}\mathrm{Cs}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{I}_{x}\mathrm{Br}_{1-x})_{3}  $   precursor/glass samples are heated at 100 °C for 10 min in order to form   $  \mathrm{MA}_{x}\mathrm{Cs}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{I}_{x}\mathrm{Br}_{1-x})_{3}  $   thin films on top of the glass samples. The x value is varied from 1, 0.9, 0.8, 0.7, 0.6 and 0. The thicknesses of the   $  \mathrm{MA}_{x}\mathrm{Cs}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{I}_{x}\mathrm{Br}_{1-x})_{3}  $   thin films are measured by using a surface profiler (Dektak 150, Veeco). The surface morphologies of the   $  \mathrm{MA}_{x}\mathrm{Cs}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{I}_{x}\mathrm{Br}_{1-x})_{3}  $   thin films are measured by using a scanning electron microscope (SEM) (JEOL JSM-6700 F, JEOL). The crystal structures of the   $  \mathrm{MA}_{x}\mathrm{Cs}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{I}_{x}\mathrm{Br}_{1-x})_{3}  $   thin films are characterized by using a X-ray diffractometer (XRD) (D8 ADVANCE Eco, Bruker). The absorbance spectra of the   $  \mathrm{MA}_{x}\mathrm{Cs}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{I}_{x}\mathrm{Br}_{1-x})_{3}  $   thin films are measured by using a broadband optical spectrometer (U-4100, Hitachi). The photoluminescence spectra of the   $  \mathrm{MA}_{x}\mathrm{Cs}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{I}_{x}\mathrm{Br}_{1-x})_{3}  $   thin films are measured by using a home-made optical spectrum system. A diode-pump solid-state green laser (  $  \lambda = 532  $   nm) is used as the excitation, and the incident power is controlled by using the combination of a half waveplate and a polarization beam splitter. A power detection system (818-SL/DB, Newport) is used to measure the laser power. A visible-to-near infrared objective lens (50 × , OLYMPUS) is used to focus the laser beam onto the sample surface and collect the back scattering light simultaneously. Then, the back scattering light is filtered by using a step-edge long-pass filter (LP03-532RE-25, Semrock) in order to block the optical signal from the excitation. An optical fiber with a core diameter of 200 μm (M84L01, Thorlabs) is used to guide the PL signal into the optical spectrometer (Mars TB3000+, GIE).

In the device fabrication, the ITO/glass samples are treated by using a UV-Ozone cleaner for 30 min. Before the deposition of the PEDOT:PSS thin films, the ITO/glass samples are heated at 120 °C for 10 min in order to remove the residual moisture on the substrates. Then, the 45 nm-thick PEDOT:PSS (1:6 wt%, AI4083) thin films are spin-coated on top of the ITO/glass samples with a post thermal annealing at 120 °C for 10 min, which are used as the HTL. The spin rate and time for the deposition of PEDOT:PSS thin films are 5000 rpm and 50 s, respectively. The MA $ _{x} $ Cs $ _{1-x} $ Pb(I $ _{x} $ Br $ _{1-x} $ ) $ _{3} $  thin films are fabricated on top of PEDOT:PSS/ITO/glass samples, where x = 1, 0.9, 0.8, 0.7 and 0.6. The spin rate and time for the deposition of perovskite thin films are 5000 rpm and 20 s, respectively. The 50 nm-thick PCBM thin films are spin-coated on top of the MA $ _{x} $ Cs $ _{1-x} $ Pb(I $ _{x} $ Br $ _{1-x} $ ) $ _{3} $ /PEDOT:PSS/ITO/glass samples to act as the ETL. The spin rate and time for the deposition of PCBM thin films are 1200 rpm and 30 s, respectively. Finally, the 100 nm-thick Ag films are thermally evaporated on top of the ETLs under low vacuum conditions

(<2x10 $  ^{-6}  $  torr), to act as a reflecting mirror and a cathode electrode. The active area of the MA $ _{x} $ Cs $ _{1-x} $ Pb(I $ _{x} $ Br $ _{1-x} $ ) $ _{3} $  solar cells is defined to be 0.2 × 0.5 cm $  ^{2}  $  with the use of a metallic shadow mask during the thermal evaporation process of Ag. The J-V curves of the MAPbI $ _{3} $  solar cells are measured by using a source-meter system (NI-USB 6356 DAQ) under one sun illumination (AM1.5G, 100 mW/cm $  ^{2}  $ ). The light intensity of the light-emitting diodes based solar simulator (VeraSol-2, Newport) can be self-calibrated by the built-in measurement system. To increase the PCE of the MA $ _{0.8} $ Cs $ _{0.2} $ Pb(I $ _{0.8} $ Br $ _{0.2} $ ) $ _{3} $  solar cells, a 5-nm thick bathocuproine (BCP) thin film is spin-coated on top of the ETL as the electron modification layer. The spin rate and time for the deposition of BCP thin films are 4000 rpm and 30 s, respectively.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we have fabricated a high-quality   $  MA_{0.8}Cs_{0.2}Pb(I_{0.8}Br_{0.2})_{3}  $   thin film, which results in an efficient and stable solar cell. The X-ray diffraction pattern and time-dependent photoluminescence spectra show that the preferred oriented crystalline and photo-stable   $  MA_{0.8}Cs_{0.2}Pb(I_{0.8}Br_{0.2})_{3}  $   thin film is formed. The open-circuit voltage, short-circuit current density and fill factor of the best performance   $  MA_{0.8}Cs_{0.2}Pb(I_{0.8}Br_{0.2})_{3}  $   solar cell are 1.07 V, 14.15 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and 66.2%, respectively, which results in a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 10.02%. The incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency spectra and thickness-dependent photovoltaic performance show that it is possible to increase the PCE by increasing the absorbance (thickness) of the   $  MA_{0.8}Cs_{0.2}Pb(I_{0.8}Br_{0.2})_{3}  $   thin film while keeping the recombination of charge carriers.
