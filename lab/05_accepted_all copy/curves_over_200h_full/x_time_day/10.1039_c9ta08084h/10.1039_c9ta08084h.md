# Highly efficient and stable perovskite solar cells via bilateral passivation layers

Tun Wang, Zhendong Cheng, Yulin Zhou, Hong Liu*, Wenzhong Shen*

Key Laboratory of Artificial Structures and Quantum Control (Ministry of Education), Institute of Solar Energy, School of Physics and Astronomy, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Shanghai 200240, P. R. China

# Abstract

$ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ -based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have drawn tremendous attention and achieved significant improvement in recent years. Interfacial engineering has been a promising route for enhancing performances of PSCs with larger open-circuit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{OC}}) $  and short-circuit current density  $ (J_{\mathrm{SC}}) $ . Herein, we report a simple processing method for improving the performance of PSCs by applying bilateral polystyrene layers between perovskite absorber and charge transport layers, respectively. The top and bottom surfaces of the perovskite layer are passivated and protected by ultrathin bilateral polystyrene layers, which results in highly efficient devices with larger perovskite grain size, fewer interfacial defects and suppressed the charge recombination. As a result, a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 19.99\% $  has been achieved without hysteresis and the  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  is as high as  $ 1.149\mathrm{V} $ , which is so far the best result for p-i-n PSCs based on pure  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ . Moreover, the devices have also shown improved long-term stability. This study provides a powerful strategy to design and prepare the highly efficient and stable perovskite solar cells.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted huge attention because of their desirable advantages such as simple fabrication process, adjustable band energy, efficient light absorption and high charge-collection efficiency, etc.  $  ^{1, 2}  $   The power conversion efficiency (PCE) record has been rapidly promoted over 24% in recent years, making them challenging candidates for new generation photovoltaic technology in the near future.  $  ^{3-5}  $   To achieve high-performance PSCs, all of the functional layers in a perovskite solar cell have to be successively prepared as perfect as possible by controlling over both morphological and electronic properties because each layer plays a vital role in the device performance.  $  ^{6}  $   Recently, as an outstanding representative of p-type metal oxide semiconductors, nickel oxide (NiO  $  _{x}  $  ) has been extensively studied as a hole transport layer (HTL) because of its suitable work function, high conduction band edge, large electrical conductivity, high thermal and chemical stability,  $  ^{7, 8}  $   which can be an excellent replacement for the popular organic HTL materials, such as Spiro-OMeTAD and PEDOT:PSS.  $  ^{9-11}  $   The hysteresis of PSCs based on NiO  $  _{x}  $   HTL can be effectively suppressed using inverted planar configuration (p-i-n) comparing with the normal n-i-p architecture.  $  ^{12, 13}  $   This benefits from the limited ion migration, balanced hole and electron transport efficiency, and appropriate

compensation for the slightly shorter diffusion length of holes than the electrons in the perovskite film for the inverted heterojunction devices. $ ^{10, 14} $  However, it is still challenging to prepare highly-efficient and stable PSCs. Firstly, the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite material  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  ( $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ ) is very sensitive to humidity and heat, which significantly degrades photovoltaic property and stability of the prepared PSCs when they are exposed into hot and humid environment. $ ^{15} $  Secondly, the pinholes or crystal defects of prepared perovskite layer induced by grain boundaries and roughness cannot be easily removed by solution-derived method even though the preparation process has been enormously progressed. This would enhance the non-radiative recombination and thus cause a loss in open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ ) and short-circuit current density ( $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ ). $ ^{16} $  What's more, the weak chemical interaction at perovskite/ $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  interface may also limit the efficiency of the device. $ ^{17, 18} $  It has been reported that the photoinduced carriers can penetrate through perovskite layer without significant charge recombination. $ ^{19} $  Therefore, enhancing the contact and transport of carriers at the interfaces of perovskite/charge transport layer and minimizing the charge recombination become increasingly significant.

Recently, several approaches have been proposed to reduce interfacial defects and achieve higher   $  V_{OC}  $   in optimized devices.  $  ^{20, 21}  $   Interfacial passivation has been developed from the point of perovskite grain boundary and interface modification, such as adding insulating polymers,  $  ^{19, 22-24}  $   ethanolamine molecules  $  ^{25}  $   or organic salt  $  ^{26, 27}  $   to cap perovskite layers. However, this approach only blocks the photogenerated charge recombination at one side of the perovskite layer, which may aggravate a

charge-carrier imbalance because of asymmetric charge extraction in HTL and electron transport layer (ETL).  $  ^{22}  $   Besides, the interfacial recombination centers cannot be effectively reduced in the other unprotected interface. For this sake, double-side passivation using poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA) has been proposed by Thomas et al. and a PCE of 20.8% based on multi-cation perovskite has been achieved.  $  ^{28}  $   However, their device structure and manufacturing processes were quite complicated, and the interfacial passivation mechanism has not been fully discussed and understood. They inserted mixed PCBM/PMMA film between perovskite and ETL working as the passivation layer and did not explain their respective functions. Besides, it has been not clear how the thickness of the passivation layer influences the photovoltaic performances of the PSCs.

In this work, we employ polystyrene (PS) as a passivation media to modify the surface of the electrochemically deposited  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  film to improve the perovskite quality. Furthermore, the PS mixed in chlorobenzene (CB) is used as antisolvent to slow down the perovskite crystal growth and fill the traps among perovskite grains on top. The utilization of bilateral passivation layers can lead to the efficient passivation of trap states at perovskite/HTL and perovskite/ETL interfaces. In the meantime, the photogenerated holes and electrons can pass through the bilateral passivation layers via tunneling effect from perovskite absorber to the HTL and ETL, respectively, while they are blocked in the inverse directions, thereby increasing the  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  and  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  without distinctly sacrificing the fill factor (FF). Consequently, the p-i-n PSCs with a structure of FTO/ $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ /PS/MAPbI $ _3 $ /PS/PCBM/Ag exhibits a PCE of  $ 19.99\% $  with a high  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of

Furthermore, the bilateral passivated devices show better long-term stability than that without passivation layers. The results in this study suggest that bilateral passivation for perovskite film is an effective procedure to improve the performances of PSCs and it also shows potential applications for developing the light-emitting diodes based on perovskite materials.

Moreover, considering the sandwich-like configuration of bilateral passivation layers and the perovskite layer, the stability of the PSCs should also be interesting to evaluate. # 3. Conclusion

We have developed a strategy to improve both the efficiency and stability of the inverted planar PSCs with a PCE of  $ 19.99\% $  and a  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $  of 1.149 V by inserting bilateral PS films on the double-surface of the perovskite layer. The ultrathin PS films work as passivation layers, which suppress the carrier recombination and shunt-current leakage at interfaces between perovskite and charge transport layers. Moreover, the bilaterally passivated devices have exhibited excellent long-term and thermal stability. Our studies indicate that the bilateral passivation may be an effective enhancement method for efficient and stable PSCs in general, and would have potential applications in other film-based electronic devices as well.
