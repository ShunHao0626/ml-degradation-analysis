# Diffraction-Grated Perovskite Induced Highly Efficient Solar Cells through Nanophotonic Light Trapping

Yang Wang, Peng Wang, Xue Zhou, Chang Li, Huizeng Li, Xiaotian Hu, Fengyu Li, Xiaoping Liu, Mingzhu Li,* and Yanlin Song*

Achieving light harvesting is crucial for the efficiency of the solar cell. Constructing optical structures often can benefit from micro-nanophotonic imprinting. Here, a simple and facile strategy is developed to introduce a large area grating structure into the perovskite-active layer of a solar cell by utilizing commercial optical discs (CD-R and DVD-R) and achieve high photovoltaic performance. The constructed diffraction grating on the perovskite active layer realizes nanophotonic light trapping by diffraction and effectively suppresses carrier recombination. Compared to the pristine perovskite solar cells (PSCs), the diffraction-grating perovskite devices with DVD obtain higher power conversion efficiency and photocurrent density, which are improved from  $ 16.71\% $  and  $ 21.67\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  to  $ 19.71\% $  and  $ 23.11\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ . Moreover, the stability of the PSCs with diffraction-grating-structured perovskite active layer is greatly enhanced. The method can boost photonics merge into the remarkable perovskite materials for various applications.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite material (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ ) is a hybrid inorganic-organic direct-bandgap semiconductor bandgap showing great promise in light absorbance especially as a light harvester for perovskite solar cells (PSCs). High-quality perovskite thin films are formed at low temperature, which show great advances for fabricating facile solar cells. $ ^{[1a,b]} $  According to the latest reports, $ ^{[2a,b]} $  PSCs have revolutionized thin film photovoltaics as efficiencies were reached 20.1% (2014) and 21.6% (2016), respectively. The photocurrent densities of their champion devices are 24.4 and 23.7 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , which are also far lower than that of theoretical device (26 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ). $ ^{[3]} $  Therefore,

tures have been developed such as nanobowl array,  $  ^{[8]}  $  inverse opal structure (  $  TiO_{2}^{[9a,b]}  $  and ZnO,  $  ^{[10]}  $  ) 3D micropyramid,  $  ^{[11]}  $  and moth-eye structures  $  ^{[12]}  $  to improve light harvesting efficiency (LHE) in photovoltaic devices. Most of these structures are made from templates prearranged on the electron transport layer,  $  ^{[13a,b]}  $  which will inevitably introduce the defects in perovskite active films. The optical structures for trapping light are seldom exploited in a perovskite active layer of PSCs to enhance light harvesting.  $  ^{[6b,14a,b]}  $  Because the perovskite active layer of PSCs is thin (  $  \approx350~nm  $  ), there is great challenge to construct large area of micro-nano-optical structures with well crystal quality of perovskite film. Nanoimprinting technique,  $  ^{[15a,b]}  $  with desirable controllability and reproducibility, has been regarded as a successful method to construct nanostructures for photon management (improving light absorption) in optoelectronic devices.  $  ^{[16]}  $  However, Si masters by focused ion beam milling over small areas exist the risk of damaging the expensive master (<300 nm in period, 1 cm  $  ^{2}  $ , almost  $  \approx30\ 000\ RMB  $ ) during imprinting.

Herein, we demonstrate a simple and reproducible imprinted method by optical discs to construct light trapping with periodic micro-nanodiffraction-grating structures on a perovskite active layer without extra support layer and achieve PSCs with high photovoltaic performance. Our approach is to utilize the commercial available CD-R or DVD-R as the original master, which overcomes the limitation of fragility of the conventional Si masters and time consuming of fabricated polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) masters in imprinting techniques. Meanwhile,

immense efforts are engaged in finding strategies to improve light harvesting and perovskite film quality (improving the crystallinity and decreasing the pinholes) to enhance the photovoltaic performance of PSCs. To obtain smooth and crystalline perovskite films with high electronic quality, many methods have been developed, such as vacuum-flash solution  $  ^{[4]}  $   and interface engineering.  $  ^{[5]}  $   Meanwhile, micro-nano-optical structures are also implemented to trap light and enhance optical absorption in PSCs.  $  ^{[6a-d]}  $   Gratzel and co-workers utilized the protonated salt to engineer the 2D/3D composite perovskite layer, which brought the enhanced stability of 2D perovskite with the panchromatic absorption and excellent charge transport of the 3D ones for PSCs.  $  ^{[7]}  $   Besides, many designed optical struc-

# 2. Results and Discussion

Patterned parts of two kinds of optical discs CD-R (Philips, 700 MB) and DVD-R (HP, 4.7 GB) are used as initial masters to make grating on the perovskite active layer by imprinting. The schematic fabrication process of imprinted PSCs by CD or DVD is depicted in Figure 1a (see the Experimental Section for the details of the fabrication process). The device structure is fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/TiO $ _2 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /Spiro-OMeTAD/Au. As the diffraction-grating structure is constructed on perovskite active layer, the solar cell does not need support layers for extra optical structures to enhance light harvesting. The thickness of the perovskite solar cell is reduced. The perovskite film with diffraction grating own two advantages for optoelectronic performance of PSCs: (1) utilize different order diffracting light to realize light harvesting and (2) improve charge extraction process and suppress electron-hole recombination, as shown in Figure 1b.

The masters of CD and DVD had  $ \approx 1.5 $  and  $ \approx 0.75\mu \mathrm{m} $  in period and 0.5 and  $ 0.25\mu \mathrm{m} $  in line width, respectively (Figure S1a,b, Supporting Information). Figure 2a,b shows the atomic force microscope (AFM) images of the prepared perovskite layer imprinted using CD and DVD masters. The imprinted perovskite films by optical discs obtain grating structure of  $ \approx 1.5 $  and  $ \approx 0.75\mu \mathrm{m} $  in period,  $ \approx 1.0 $  and  $ \approx 0.5\mu \mathrm{m} $  in line width, and  $ \approx 0.1\mu \mathrm{m} $  in height, which are corresponding with the CD and DVD masters. In stage II, a large number of small crystals generated slowly with volatilized solvent. Meanwhile, the perovskite achieves good crystalline within the spatial confinement.  $  ^{[14b]}  $

# 4. Experimental Section

Master Fabrication: The CD and DVD optical discs were soaked in ethanol for 10 min, and the metal films on optical discs surface were removed by 3M tape. Then, the discs were cleaned by ultrasonication in the ethanol for 15 min until the films removed and rinsed with ethanol. The masters were kept in an oven at 75 °C for 1 h.

Device Fabrication: Laser-patterned FTO-coated glass substrates (NSG, ≈15 Ω) were cleaned by ultrasonication in an alkaline, aqueous washing solution, rinsed with deionized water, ethanol and acetone, and subjected to an O₃/ultraviolet treatment for 15 min. To grow TiO₂ layer onto the FTO substrate, an aqueous stock solution of TiCl₄ (99.9%) was diluted to precursor solutions with 200 × 10⁻³ M TiCl₄ concentrations at 0 °C. The FTO substrates were then immersed into the precursor solution and kept in an oven at 75 °C for 1 h. Afterward the FTO substrates were washed with water and ethanol, and then dried at 100 °C in air for an hour. In order to deposit the CH₃NH₃PbI₃ active layer, CH₃NH₃I and PbI₂ (molar ratio = 1:1) were mixed into dimethyl sulfoxide and γ-butyrolactone. The CH₃NH₃PbI₃ precursor solution was deposited onto FTO/TiO₂ substrate by the spin-coating process at 4000 rpm for 30 s. Then toluene (130 μL) was dropped onto the substrate toward the end of spin-coating. The CH₃NH₃PbI₃ layer was dried at 50 °C (I) and 100 °C (II) for 5 and 30 min. The imprinted process was finished under 50 °C (I) and 100 °C (II) for 5 and 30 min. 30 μL of Spiro-OMeTAD solution was spin-coated on perovskite layer at 3000 rpm for 30 s. The solution was prepared by dissolving 72.3 mg of Spiro-OMeTAD in 1 mL chlorobenzene, 28.8 μL of 4-tert-butyl pyridine (Sigma Aldrich), and a solution of 520 mg of Li-TFSI (TCI) in 1 mL acetonitrile (Sigma Aldrich). Finally, Au electrode (≈70 nm) was deposited on Spiro-OMeTAD layer by thermal evaporation.

Characterization: The surface morphologies of the different films were evaluated by field-emission SEM (JEOL, JSM-7500F, Japan). Absorption spectra were obtained with a UV-vis spectrophotometer (Perkin Elmer Lambda 750). Current density-voltage characteristics of PSCs under 1 sun illumination were performed in ambient conditions using a programmable Keithley 2400 source meter under AM 1.5G solar irradiation at  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^2 $  (Enli Tech, Class AAA solar simulator), and the characteristics have been described elsewhere. EIS characteristics of the cells were measured by an electrochemical analyzer (CHI660D, Chenhua Instruments Co., Shanghai) under dark at room temperature. The EQE was measured by illumination with monochromatic (Enli Tech, Class AAA solar simulator, QE-mini).
