Abstract: Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) received high attention in the past few years due to their terrific photovoltaic performance and potentially low production cost. However, the use of hole transport materials (HTMs) with hygroscopic dopants causing the inevitable instability of device performance forward the commercialization. Herein, a dopant-free polymeric HTM with functional aromatic rings was used to optimize the HTM/perovskite interface and employed in a planar n-i-p configuration. Poly(1,4-(2,5-bis((2-butyloctyloxy)phenylene)-2,7-(5,5,10,tetrakis(4-hexylphenyl)-5,10-dihydro-s-indaceno[2,1-b:6,5-b']dithiophene)) (IDTB) copolymer constructed with indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b']dithiophene and bis(alkyloxy)benzene units adopts a S···O intramolecular bond linked ladder-like planar conjugated polymer backbone. Without any dopant, the hole mobility of IDTB is in the same order of magnitude as a doped spiro-OMeTAD. Also, the hydrophobic nature of IDTB facilitated the long-term stability of the perovskite underneath. The unencapsulated PSC devices made of IDTB-based HTM achieved a power conversion efficiency of 19.38% with a high moisture stability, retaining above 80% of initial PCE at 65% relative humidity for more than 10 days. The superior passivation effect to perovskite surface made a hysteresis of 0.44% was almost the least reported for regular planar undoped polymer HTM PSCs.

# Introduction

Perovskite solar cell (PSC) is one of the most rapidly growing solar technological fields as its current champion power

conversion efficiency (PCE) of >25% is as high efficiency as the well-developed silicon solar cells.  $  ^{[1]}  $   It is very attractive for global future energy needs owing to its low cost/efficiency ratio. Normally, highly efficient PSCs employ the n-i-p mesoscopic structure where the perovskite is sandwiched between electron (ETM) and hole transport materials (HTMs).  $  ^{[2]}  $   The performance of these n-i-p planar PSCs strongly depends on the electronic structure and interfacial properties of HTMs. HTMs are not only responsible for hole extraction and transport of the charges from the perovskite layer to the electrodes, but also minimize the charge recombination from a direct contact of perovskite and electrode together with reduce the energy barrier between them.  $  ^{[3]}  $   In parallel, HTMs offer the perovskite layer a good protection from a direct contact with the ambient atmosphere (moisture), which can cause a rapid degradation of perovskite films. Therefore, HTMs play an essential role in both boosting high performance and increasing PSC devices durability. PSCs with PCE of >20% are typically achieved with a standard HTM of 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenyl amine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) doped with bis (trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP).  $  ^{[4]}  $   Unfortunately, the migration and redistribution of the dopants added to enhance hole mobility of the HTM always unavoidably lead to significant hysteresis of the current density-voltage (J-V) curves and decrease of the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of PSCs.  $  ^{[5]}  $   Moreover, the hygroscopic aspect of such dopants also induce the instability of perovskite film toward the degradation by moisture absorption through the HTM layer.  $  ^{[6]}  $   To avoid such dilemmas, various dopant-free HTMs with common characters of high ambient stability, suitable energy

The two monomers were designed to adopt a S···O interaction-linked from the σ-hole type interaction  $  ^{[13]}  $   that induce the flat conformation,  $  ^{[14]}  $   forming a ladder-like conjugated polymer backbone (Figure 1a). This favours for an intramolecular π–π stacking, that can enhance charge carrier mobility and conductivity.  $  ^{[15]}  $   As of H. Sirringaus et al works, the indecanodithiophene-based copolymer achieved a stunning hole mobility of 1.5 – 2.5 cm² V⁻¹s⁻¹ due to the highly-planar of the backbone.  $  ^{[15a]}  $   With the same strategy, Z. Bo et al presented the flat conformation polymer from this S···O interaction as dopant-free HTM to boost PCE of PSC up to 18.30% nearly to 19.35% of spiro-OMeTAD device.  $  ^{[14c]}  $   Accordingly, an intensive coverage of its flat main chain to the perovskite layer allowed strong interactions of the S and O atoms in IDT and B units with unsaturated/uncoordinated Pb ions on the surface of the perovskite, which effected defect passivation and interfaces.  $  ^{[16]}  $   This would result in less charge accumulation at the interfaces, leading to a higher extraction efficiency of photogenerated holes and a lesser J-V hysteresis effect.  $  ^{[17]}  $   Moreover, the alkyl side chains substitution provided good solubility and a hydrophobic protection against ambient conditions to increase the device stability.  $  ^{[18]}  $   Subsequently, a comparable hole mobility to that of doped spiro-OMeTAD was achieved, and a champion PCE of 19.38% was attained with low hysteresis index (HI) of 0.44%. From our knowledge, this is almost the least HI with high PCE reported for undoped planar n-i-p structure (Figure 1b and Table S1, see the Supporting Information). The unencapsulated devices also hold a superior long-term stability where the PCE remains >80% of its initial value after being kept in high humid environments (~65% RH) for 10 days (Table S2).

# Results and Discussion

The Stille coupling polymerization of 1,4-dibromo-2,5-bis(2-butyloctyloxy)benzene and (5,5,10,10-tetrakis(4-hexyl phenyl)-5,10-dihydro-s-indaceno[2,1-b:6,5-b']dithiophene-2,7-diyl)bis(trimethylstannane) catalysed by Pd(PPh₃)₄, followed by termination of the polymer chain ends with 2-(tributylstannyl)thiophene and 2-bromothiophene afforded the target poly(1,4-(2,5-bis((2-butyloctyloxy)phenylene)-2,7-(5,5,10,10-tetrakis(4-hexylphenyl)-5,10-dihydro-s-indaceno[2,1-b:6,5-b']dithiophene)) (IDTB) copolymer as red solids in 85% yield. IDTB polymer exhibited a molecular weight (M₀) of ≈18,300 kDa with polydispersity index of 1.9 in the gel permeation chromatography (GPC) (Figure S1). The density functional theory (DFT) calculation was performed by Gaussian 09 using B3LYP/6-31G (d, p) level of theory. As shown in Figure 2, the calculated results indicate that co-polymer main

chain adopts a ladder-like planar conformation through the anticipated intramolecular S…O interactions. The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) were localized along three repeating units of the main backbone and displayed a sizeable overlap between the two orbitals, meaning a strong Coulomb interaction, which is beneficial for hole extraction and hopping. The presence of S…O intramolecular interaction in the polymer chain was further evidenced by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis, examining sulfur and oxygen species at the surface of IDTB co-polymer, mixed monomers and perovskite/IDTB copolymer films. As depicted in Figure 3a, the S2p XPS spectra of IDTB co-polymer and perovskite/IDTB co-polymer films show a clearer high energy peak S2p  $  _{3/2}  $  (165.3 eV) when compared to the spectrum of mixed monomers film. The O1s XPS spectrum of IDTB co-polymer film also exhibited a shoulder on the high energy comparing to the spectrum of mixed monomers film (Figure 3b). These observations could be attributable to the formation of the S…O interaction in the polymer chain.

The absorption spectra of IDTB (Figure 4a) showed that the toluene spectrum was red shifted about  $ \sim 15\mathrm{nm} $  from  $ 518~\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 533~\mathrm{nm} $  when casted into thin film, signifying a decent intermolecular  $ \pi -\pi $  stacking interaction between adjacent polymer chains present in the polymer film. As illustrated in Figure. 5a, X-ray diffraction pattern of the thin film showed a sharp peak at  $ 20^{\circ} $  of  $ 4.8^{\circ} $  according to the  $ d $  spacing of  $ 18.4\AA $ , relating to an intermolecular packing of the polymer chains. Thermal analyses

by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) measurements revealed a high temperature stability of amorphous polymer with high glass transition temperature (  $  T_{g}  $  ) of 234 °C and decomposition temperature (  $  T_{5d}  $  ) of 376 °C (Figure S2), ensuring a long-term stability of PSC devices. Electrochemical behaviour examined by cyclic voltammetry showed that IDTB experienced unseparated multi-irreversible oxidation couples and were electrochemically stable after repeating several scans (Figure S3a). IDTB undergo first oxidation at low potential of 0.58 V, implying that IDTB is susceptible to the hole formation or hole injection. This could give rise to an efficient hole extraction at the HTM/perovskite interface. The HOMO of IDTB thin film measured by photoelectron spectroscopy (AC-2) in air was -5.20 eV (Figure S3b), while the LUMO was deduced from the HOMO and optical bandgap (  $  E_{g,opt}=2.19  $   eV) to be -3.01 eV. As shown in Figure 4b, the HOMO level of IDTB is situated between the HOMO (-5.48 eV) of   $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.8}MA_{0.15}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $   (CFM) perovskite and work function (5.10 eV) of gold (Au) electrode, suggesting that polymer IDTB can act as an HTM to instantaneously extract holes from the perovskite layer, while its LUMO level is sufficiently high to block the photogenerated electrons from reaching the Au electrode. Moreover, the contact between IDTB layer and Au electrode is Ohmic,  $  ^{[19]}  $   which will enable the hole collection. It is noticeable that the HOMO of IDTB is deeper than that of spiro-OMeTAD (-5.12 eV), which would lead to an increase in   $  V_{OC}  $   value of the PSC,  $  ^{[3a,20]}  $  . The hole-transporting capability of IDTB was deduced using the theory of space charge limited current (SCLC) from current density–voltage (J-V) plots (Figure 5b) of the hole-only devices with a structure of FTO/PEDOT:PSS/HTM (100 nm)/Au.  $  ^{[21]}  $   Following the Mott-Gurney equation and Frenkel effect, the hole mobility of undoped polymer IDTB was estimated to be   $  2.89 \times 10^{-5}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $  ·s  $  ^{-1}  $  , which is slightly lower than that of doped spiro-OMeTAD (  $  5.97 \times 10^{-5}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $  ·s  $  ^{-1}  $  ). Such high hole mobility of IDTB in undoped conditions could be ascribed to its high planar ladder-like conjugated backbone, assuring a superior stability and high-performance PSC device. As illustrated in Figure 5c, the atomic force microscope image (AFM) of IDTB thin film coated on perovskite layer revealed a smooth uniform surface (RMS = 3.79 nm) and full surface coverage with a large perovskite grain being visibly observed and well-preserved (Figure 5d and S4d,), signifying high quality thin film with good interfaces. It is notably worthy that the perovskite film became smoother when coated with IDTB polymer.

To evaluate the efficacy of polymer IDTB as HTM, the photovoltaic performances of IDTB and Li-TFSI/tBP-doped spiro-OMeTAD as reference were methodically assessed in parallel. The optimized PSC devices were fabricated with structure of FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $  (120 nm)/SnO $ _2 $  (40 nm)/ Cs $ _{0.05} $ FA $ _{0.8} $ MA $ _{0.15} $ PbI $ _{2.55} $ Br $ _{0.45} $  (400 nm)/HTM/Au (40 nm), where compact TiO $ _2 $  (c-TiO $ _2 $ ) and tin(II) oxide (SnO $ _2 $ ) were used as the ETM and Au was used as the back contact electrode. Thickness of HTMs were 150 nm and 40 nm for doped spiro-OMeTAD and undoped IDTB polymer, respectively. The optimized perovskite layer was spin-coated from triple cation perovskite precursors solution followed by chlorobenzene treatment and annealing at 100 °C to yield a transparent black film, which was optically and structurally characterized as shown in Figure S4. To show a reproducibility of the fabrication method, 15 PSC devices of both reference and IDTB were fabricated and evaluated. As depicted in Figure 6a, the J–V curve of the unencapsulated champion devices measured in ambient condition employing IDTB-based PSC exhibit V $ _{OC} $  of 1.107 V, short-circuit current density (J $ _{SC} $ ) of 23.06 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , fill factor (FF) of 0.759, and a corresponding PCE of 19.38%, whereas the doped spiro-OMeTAD based reference device showed PCE of 18.22% with V $ _{OC} $  of 1.073 V, J $ _{SC} $  of 23.78 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  and FF of 0.714 (Table 1). As expected, the V $ _{OC} $  value of IDTB device is higher than that of the reference device because of its relatively deep HOMO level, while the sensible high FF of IDTB device could be attributed to its high hole mobility. $ ^{[22]} $  Such high V $ _{OC} $  and FF values of IDTB device render its PCE surpassing to that of the reference device. To elucidate the J $ _{SC} $ , the incident photon conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra were collected. As shown in Figure 6b, IPCE spectrum of IDTB device exhibits higher IPCE values in the range from 450 to 750 nm, and lower IPCE values and red-shift in the rage of 300-420 nm when compared with that of spiro-OMeTAD device. Accordingly, the integrated photocurrent densities for IDTB and spiro-OMeTAD devices were calculated to be 22.81 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  and 23.74 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , respectively, corresponding well with the J $ _{SC} $  values collected from the J–V curves. Such low IPCE values in the short-wavelength region of IDTB device could be affected from the self-absorption of polymer

IDTB, while the absorption of spiro-OMeTAD is in the UV region. This could be accounted for the slightly lower   $  J_{SC}  $   of IDTB device as compared to the spiro-OMeTAD device. The device stability of both devices were measured by soaking under AM1.5G light for 20 min. The results revealed that stabilized   $  J_{SC}  $   and PCE values at the maximum power point (MPP) for IDTB device were slightly declined from the initial values to 23.00 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and 19.26%, while   $  J_{SC}  $   and PCE values for the reference device were notably decreased to 22.44 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and 16.42% (Figure 6c), indicating more durable of IDTB device over the one using spiro-OMeTAD. As shown in Figure 6a, the difference in J-V curve of the reverse scan to forward scan is clearly observed in the reference device, while IDTB device showed very negligible. The magnitude was assessed as a J-V hysteresis index,  $  ^{[23]}  $   which was calculated to be 16.90% and 0.44% for reference and IDTB devices, respectively (Table 1).

The stability of the PSCs to moisture was further explored by keeping the unencapsulated devices in a controlled humidity of ~65% RH. The PCE at MPP of IDTB device remained >80% of its initial PCE after 240 h, in contrast to the reference device that suffered a PCE drop of ~80% after 48 h (Figure 6d). The result indicated that the moisture penetration into perovskite layer is well blocked by a hydrophobicity of polymer brought by long alkyl side chains of IDTB device.[18] As depicted in Figure 7c, a large degree of water contact angle of >100° verifies a high hydrophobic and moisture repellent surface of IDTB film. The reliability of IDTB as dopant-free HTM was confirmed by repeating the fabrication of 15 devices which showed that the average PCE was 16.89% comparable to 16.26% of spiro-OMeTAD devices (Figure 8a). The shelf-life of unencapsulated devices in ambient atmosphere with 30-40% RH and temperature of 30 °C was continuously monitored with regards to their device performance for 60 days. The average PCE of IDTB devices under J-V measurement was decreased to 11.75% (Figure 8b and Table S4). One of each aged device was sampled to measure with MPP, the results showed that the PCE of IDTB device remained higher (10.89%) while PCE of reference device drop to 9.59% (Figure 8d) indicating a durable stability of IDTB devices. As shown in Figure S5, the IDTB-based devices display lower J-V hysteresis with calculated HI values of 2.02% and 0.39% for M and FM perovskites, respectively. # Conclusion

In summary, a highly efficient dopant-free polymeric HTM IDTB was successfully developed by utilizing intramolecular S…O interactions between indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b']dithiophene and 2,5-dialkoxy-1,4-phenylene monomers, leading to a self-planarization ladder-like conjugated system. IDTB exhibited not only a high hole mobility, but also an intensive interaction of the backbone to perovskites via its S/O atoms, giving rise to a high hole-extracting ability and an effective passivation of the defects in perovskite material. The PSC devices fabricated using various perovskite structures with IDTB as dopant-free HTM attained not only PCEs comparable to the doped spiro-OMeTAD devices, but also high moisture stability and the lowest hysteresis reported for regular planar undoped polymer HTM PSCs. These results suggested that our design approach could be very promising for HTMs including polymers and small molecules as well as for other highly planar and π-conjugated materials in the field of organic electronics.

# Experimental Section

Device Fabrication: FTO was patterned by Zinc powder and 1.5 M HCl before cleaning with Liquinox® (0.1 vol%), deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol, respectively, for 15 min of each by sonication. The dried FTO glass surface were further cleaned with UV ozone cleaner (Ossila) for 15 min. A compact TiO₂ was spin-coated on top of FTO using 0.15 M of TiO₂ precursor in butanol (x 2) and 0.3 M in isopropanol at 2,000 rpm for 20 s. They were annealed at 450 °C for 1 h. After cooling down to room temperature, 0.1 M SnCl₂·2H₂O in ethanol was spin-coated over TiO₂ and annealed at 180 °C for 50 min. Then, the substrates were transferred into glove box filled with N₂ atmosphere, the triple cation perovskite precursor was prepared by mixing MAI 9.6 mg, FAI 54 mg, 315 μl PbI₂ (1.05 M), 90 μl PbBr₂ (1 M) and 13 μl CsI (1.5 M) in mixed DMF:DMSO (4:1 v/v) at 70 °C until all precursors were dissolved. Perovskite layer was obtained from two-step spin-coating at 1,000 rpm and 5,000 rpm. During the second step, 180 μl of chlorobenzene antisolvent was continuously dropped onto the perovskite at the 10th second. They were annealed at 100 °C, for 30 min to form a black-perovskite phase. After cooling down to room temperature, the Li-TFSI/tBP-doped spiro-OMeTAD or undoped IDTB polymer were spin-coated over the perovskite film at 4,000 rpm and 5,000 rpm, respectively. Finally, a 40 nm of gold was evaporated with the base pressure of ~10⁻⁶ Torr. PSC devices, with the controlled active area of 0.16 cm², were characterized by source meter (Keithley Model 2400) under ambient atmosphere. The AM1.5G standard light source (Solar Simulator, Scienetech SF-300-A) was calibrated with Si-reference cell (BS-520 Si photodiode, Bunnkoukeiki Co., Ltd, Japan) before the measurement. Incident photon to electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) measurements were performed by mean of an Oriel 150W Xe arc lamp fitted with a CornerstoneTM 130 1/8m monochromator as a monochromatic light

source, a Newport 818-UV silicon photodiode as power density calibration and a Keithley 6485 picoammeter.

Instruments and methods: NMR spectra were recorded on a Bruker AVANCE III HD (600MHz) spectrometer using CDCl₃ as solvent. Molecular weight of polymer was clarified by Gel Permeation Chromatography (GPC, Malvern, Viscotek TDAmax) in HPLC grade THF. UV–vis spectra were recorded on a Perkin-Elmer Lambda 1050 spectrometer in dilute toluene (HPLC grade). Thermal properties were examined by thermalgravimetric (Rigaku Thermo plus EVO2, TG-DTA8122) and differential scanning calorimetry (PerkinElmer, DSC-8500) at heating rate of 10 °C/min under N₂ flow. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was carried out under inert argon atmosphere with an Autolab potentiostat PGSTAT 101 using a three-electrode system (platinum rod as counter electrode, a glassy carbon as working electrode and Ag/Ag⁺ as reference electrode). HOMO energy level was measured by photoelectron spectrometer (AC-2, Riken Reiki). X-ray diffraction was recorded on X-ray diffractometer (Bruker, NEW D8 ADVANCE) using modified mode for thin film measurement. Morphology of perovskite was observed by Scanning Electron Microscope (JEOL, JSM-7610F) with a 1 kV accelerated electron beam. Morphology of thin films was observed with Atomic Force Microscope (Park Systems, NX-10) with non-contact mode with the scan area 5x5 μm². Thin film thickness was measured by Profilometer (Bruker, RektakXT). The PL emission and time-resolved PL experiments were recorded on Steady State, Fluorescence and Phosphorescence Lifetime Spectrometer (Edinburgh Instruments, FLS 980) under ambient condition. Contact angle was measured on thin film coating on glass slide. A water droplet image was taken by Ossila Contact Angle. X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS) was recorded on JEOL JPS-9010MC with a Mg Kα source (1253.6 eV) at 12 kV and 25 mA. All XPS spectra were measured under a high vacuum pressure of 10⁻⁸ Pa at a room temperature. Software for running experiment is SpecSurf ver.1.9.3. The samples were coated on glass and attached on carbon tape. The analysed area of each sample was a cycle spot with a diameter of 6 mm. The narrow scan spectra were measured with a pass energy of 10 eV and an electron-volt step of 0.1 eV. The obtained spectra were evaluated using JEOL software to obtain the chemical state of the probing elements and elemental composition. All the binding energy values were referenced to the carbon peak C 1s at 284.70 eV. The quantum chemical calculations were performed with Gaussian 09 program package using density functional theory (DFT).[28] The optimized geometry, HOMO and LUMO distributions, and calculated HOMO and LUMO levels were calculated by B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) basis set in CH₂Cl₂ solvent.
