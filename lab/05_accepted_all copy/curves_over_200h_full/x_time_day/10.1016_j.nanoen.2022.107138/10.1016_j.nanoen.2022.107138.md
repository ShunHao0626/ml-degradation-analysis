# 1. Introduction

Organometal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been undoubtedly regarded as one of the most promising photovoltaic technologies due to the rapid power conversion efficiency (PCE) improvement from initial 3.8% to certified 25.7% in the past decade   $  [1-5]  $  . However, they always suffer from serious phase instability under persistent attacks by moisture, heat and oxygen, which severely hinders their photovoltaic industrialization process and even applications in other fields   $  [6-8]  $  . Recently, all-inorganic perovskites   $  (\mathrm{CsPbX}_{3}, \mathrm{X} = \mathrm{I}, \mathrm{Br}, \mathrm{Cl}, \text{or mixed})  $  , mainly including   $  CsPbI_{3}  $  ,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  ,   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   and   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  , provide an opportunity to overcome this issue because of the removal of volatile organic components, enabling them excellent thermal stability   $  [9]  $  . Especially, tri-brominated   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   demonstrates the highest durability to harsh conditions along with an immense potential for the usage in tandem and high-voltage-required solar cells owing to its wide bandgap of   $  \sim2.3  $   eV   $  [10-12]  $  . Precise manipulation on buried interface of perovskite film highly determines the photovoltaic performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, we demonstrate a   $  SnO_{2}-SnS_{2}  $   heterojunction nanocrystal as an efficient electron transfer material to heal the defective interface for planar all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs. Arising from the matched lattice distance of   $  SnS_{2}  $   with   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $ , an all-in-one   $  SnO_{2}-SnS_{2}-CsPbBr_{3}  $   interface is fabricated, in which   $  SnS_{2}  $   plays a role of “bridge” to connect incompatible interface. Consequently, the charge recombination loss is minimized owing to the Type-II band alignment and epitaxial growth induced defect reduction, achieving a remarkable efficiency of 10.72% with a high open-circuit voltage of 1.635V. Considering the high tolerance to high humidity (80%) for 700 h and high temperature (80 °C) over 30 days, these preliminary results can provide a new perspective for the rational design of ideal charge transfer materials to improve the efficiency and stability of PSCs.

Because of the ionic nature and low formation energy of perovskite, the detrimental defects are inevitably formed during the rapid crystal growth process, which are prone to migrate from bulk to interface [14]. Mainstreams on defect passivation are mainly focused on the top surface and grain boundaries of perovskite film based on Lewis acid-base chemistry or lattice reconstruction [15–20]. However, the attentions on buried interface are insufficient. It is well known that the properties of underlying layer, generally referring to electron transfer layer (ETL) for regular n-i-p architecture, highly determine the interfacial energetics of supported perovskite film. According to previous reports, the unmatched crystal lattice and thermal expansion coefficient between ETL and perovskite will lead to the formation of sub-microscale extended imperfections and lead-halide inhomogeneities [21,22], which are regarded as the predominant sources for substantial charge recombination loss and unsatisfactory voltage output. Moreover, the unencapsulated device shows an enhanced long-term stability after exposure in air ambient with 80% relative humidity (RH) at 25 °C for 700 h and 80 °C over 30 days, respectively.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Materials and reagents

Stannous chloride anhydrous  $ (\mathrm{SnCl}_2, \geq 99.0\%) $ , thiourea  $ (\mathrm{CH}_4\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{S}, \geq 99.0\%) $ , thiacetamide (TAA,  $ \mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{H}_5\mathrm{NS} $ ), lead bromide  $ (\mathrm{PbBr}_2, \geq 99.0\%) $  and cesium bromide  $ (\mathrm{CsBr}, \geq 99.9\%) $  were brought from Aladdin Industrial Corporation. All materials and reagents were used directly without further purification.

# 2.2. Preparation of colloidal   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}-SnS_{2}  $   nanocrystals

$ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  nanocrystals were prepared by the following low-temperature solution technology:  $ 853~\mathrm{mg} $  of  $ \mathrm{SnCl}_2 $  and  $ 338~\mathrm{mg} $  of thiourea were added into  $ 30~\mathrm{mL} $  of deionized (DI) water and continuously stirred at room temperature for  $ 36\mathrm{h} $ . For  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 - SnS_2} $  nanocrystals,  $ 853~\mathrm{mg} $   $ \mathrm{SnCl}_2 $ ,  $ 338~\mathrm{mg} $  thiourea and different concentrations (30.0 mM, 45.0 mM, and 60.0 mM marked as  $ 10\% $ ,  $ 15\% $  and  $ 20\% $   $ \mathrm{SnO_2 - SnS_2} $ , respectively) TAA were added into  $ 30~\mathrm{mL} $  of deionized water and rapidly stirred at room temperature to form a milky white suspension. After continuous stirring for  $ 36\mathrm{h} $ , it became dark yellow solution. Both nanocrystal solutions were filtered with  $ 0.45\mu \mathrm{m} $  PTFE membrane before use.

# 2.3. Device fabrication

All experimental processes were conducted in air without any additional condition control. Firstly, the conductive FTO glass was etched with Zn powder and HCl (2 M), and then ultrasonically cleaned with deionized water and anhydrous ethanol in turn. The washed FTO substrate was treated in plasma equipment for 400 s to improve its wettability. The cleaned FTO glass and the SnO₂ or SnO₂-SnS₂ nanocrystal solution were preheated at 90 °C for 3 min. The preheated SnO₂ or SnO₂-SnS₂ nanocrystal solution was spin-coated on FTO substrate for 30 s at the speed of 2000 rpm, and annealed at 200 °C for 60 min to obtain a dense SnO₂ or SnO₂-SnS₂ ETL. Next, the N, N-dimethyl formamide (DMF) solution of 1 M PbBr₂ was spin-coated on the obtained ETL substrate (2000 rpm, 30 s) and heated at 90 °C for 30 min to obtain PbBr₂ film. After that, the methanol solution of 0.07 M CsBr was spin-coated (2000 rpm, 30 s) on PbBr₂ film surface and annealed at 250 °C for 5 min. The above step was repeated several times to prepare a high quality CsPbBr₃ film. At last, a conductive carbon paste was blade coated onto the CsPbBr₃ layer to control the active area of 0.09 cm², and then treated at 90 °C for 30 min to obtain a complete PSC.

This is further cross-checked by the element mapping images (Fig. S2). To deeply understand this hybrid structure, we have performed the HRTEM characterizations. Different from pure SnO₂ nanocrystal (Fig. 1c), there is a conjoined structure with distinct interplanar spacing of 0.316 nm for (100) plane of SnS₂ and 0.335 nm for (110) plane of SnO₂ in one nanocrystal (Fig. 1d) [27]. The intrinsic mechanism is mainly attributed to the low lattice mismatch, which provides a precondition for the growth of superior lattice heterostructure. In this fashion, when assembling into solar cells as ETL, the charge transfer can be maximally accelerated because of the seamless connection.

Aiming to better highlight this heterojunction and evaluate their geniuses in promoting photovoltaic performance, surface chemical state of nanocrystals was further investigated by XPS spectra (Fig. S3). Comparing to the control   $  SnO_{2}  $   sample, the presence of S element and its two deconvoluted peaks at 161.9 eV and 163.3 eV assigning to S   $  2p_{3/2}  $   and S   $  2p_{1/2}  $   agree well with the   $  S^{2-}  $   state in   $  SnS_{2}  $   [28]. Arising from the matched lattice,   $  S^{2-}  $   ions will chemically bond with the electron-deficient Sn atom at   $  SnO_{2}  $   surface during the growth process, such as oxygen vacancies, leading to the shift of Sn 3d peaks to higher binding energies (Fig. 1e) [25,29], which in turn up-shifts the Fermi level (  $  E_{F}  $  ) with n-type doping and therefore increases theoretical voltage value (see the following part). The epitaxial growth is further cross-checked by the reduction of surface oxygen-groups (Fig. 1f), in which the area ratio between   $  O^{2-}  $   state in   $  SnO_{2}  $   lattice (  $  O_{Sn}  $  , 530.95 eV) and adsorbed oxygen (  $  O_{H}  $  , 532.2 eV) is increased from 1.84 to 2.25 [30]. As a result, the defects are expected to be significantly reduced for   $  SnO_{2}-SnS_{2}  $  , benefiting the electron mobility and conductivity improvement by recording the dark J-V curves of the FTO/  $  SnO_{2}  $   or (  $  SnO_{2}-SnS_{2}  $  )/Ag devices (Fig. S4 and Table S2) [31,32].

As illustrated in Fig. 2a, we further fabricated all-inorganic  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  PSC to study the advantages of this heterojunction nanocrystal on photovoltaic performance. After optimizing  $ \mathrm{SnS}_2 $  dosage (Fig. S5), the champion PSC achieves an enhanced PCE up to  $ 10.72\% $  with  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of  $ 1.635\mathrm{V} $ , short-circuit current density ( $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ ) of  $ 7.80\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  and fill factor (FF) of  $ 84.04\% $  by recording  $ J - V $  curves (Fig. 2b) under one sun irradiation, which is much higher than control device with  $ 9.53\% $  efficiency. Together with the highly matchable results in EQE spectra (Fig. 2c) and steady power outputs (Fig. 2d), we can conclude the positive effect of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2\cdot \mathrm{SnS}_2 $  on charge scavenging. It should be noted that smaller  $ \mathrm{SnS}_2 $  dosage leads to the insufficient optimization to fabricate this novel all-in-one interface, while thicker  $ \mathrm{SnS}_2 $  layer located on  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  surface will hinder the electron transfer. Particularly, an ultrahigh  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  is obtained for the optimal device. As well known,  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  is controlled by the splitting of quasi-Fermi levels and recombination inside the perovskite [33]. Therefore, considering the nearly unchanged morphology and optical transmittance of underlying ETL on FTO substrate (Fig. S6), the performance enhancement is undoubtedly attributed to regulation of the interface contacted with upper perovskite film, which can be cross-checked by the reduced hysteresis (Fig. S7) because of its dependence on interfacial charge extraction-transfer behavior.

To determine the overall charge recombination loss and the origin of enhanced  $  V_{\mathrm{OC}}  $ , we first explored the evolution of interfacial energetics. From KPFM images of two nanocrystals shown in Fig. 2e and Fig. S8, obviously, the lattice-matched growth of  $  \mathrm{SnS}_2  $  enables a higher average potential of  $  \sim 483\mathrm{mV}  $  for  $  \mathrm{SnO}_2 - \mathrm{SnS}_2  $  film than  $  \sim 45\mathrm{mV}  $  for  $  \mathrm{SnO}_2  $  film, an indicator of decreased  $  E_{\mathrm{F}}  $  of the former [34,35]. According to UPS spectra shown in Fig. 2f, it can be seen that the  $  0.44\mathrm{eV}E_{\mathrm{F}}  $  reduction from  $  -4.74\mathrm{eV}  $  to  $  -4.30\mathrm{eV}  $  upon forming heterojunction highly agrees well with the potential increasement in KPFM images. Based on the previous report [36], this is mainly resulted from the lower work function of  $  \mathrm{SnS}_2  $  comparing to that of  $  \mathrm{SnO}_2  $ ; that is, when contacting with

each other, an n-n heterojunction with Type-II band alignment is formed, benefiting the charge transfer and extraction. The detailed energy level schematics are illustrated in Fig. 2h by determining the conduction band (CB) and valance band (VB) of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ ,  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ - $ \mathrm{SnS}_2 $ , and  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  based on the onset energies ( $ E_{\mathrm{onset}} $ ) in UPS spectra (Fig. 2g) and bandgaps derived from Tauc plots (Fig. S9, S10 and Table S3). From the optimized interfacial energy structure, such as the conversion from upward band-bending to downward band-bending at ETL/ $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  interface owing to the up-shifted  $ E_{\mathrm{F}} $  of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ - $ \mathrm{SnS}_2 $ , the reduced energy difference between CB of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  and  $ E_{\mathrm{F}} $  of ETL ( $ \Delta E_{\mathrm{e}} $ ) (1.03 eV vs. 1.47 eV) and the increased energy difference between VB of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  and  $ E_{\mathrm{F}} $  of ETL ( $ \Delta E_{\mathrm{h}} $ ) (1.30 eV vs. 0.86 eV), the electron accumulation in perovskite film can be effectively suppressed, thus reducing the recombination loss and improving voltage output [37].

Apart from the advancement of interfacial energetics, the surface properties of ETL also play non-negligible roles in regulating the perovskite crystal growth. There is a fact that the large deviation on lattice structure between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite will lead to the formation of out-of-order distortion [11,21], such as detrimental microstress and gaps at interface, as shown in Fig. 3a, which inevitably cause substantial charge-trapped states. Different from the pure   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanocrystals, the parasitic   $  SnS_{2}  $   on   $  SnO_{2}  $   lattice is expected to provide a desired cradle for   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   epitaxial growth because of the optimized surface roughness (Fig. S11) and their matched lattices at (001) plane direction (0.589 nm for   $  SnS_{2}  $   and 0.587 nm for   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  ) [38], playing a role of “bridge” to form an all-in-one   $  SnO_{2}-SnS_{2}-CsPbBr_{3}  $   interface. Upon spin-coating   $  PbBr_{2}  $   solution onto the   $  SnO_{2}-SnS_{2}  $   surface with better wettability (Fig. S12), a micro mosaic structure from interfacial Pb…S interaction benefits the formation of mesoporous   $  PbBr_{2}  $   film (Fig. S13) and therefore the high-quality   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film [39]. From the cross-sectional SEM images (Fig. 3b), there is a narrow gap at   $  SnO_{2}/CsPbBr_{3}  $   interface, while a seamless connection is obtained for   $  SnS_{2}  $ -containing case, agreeing well with the above concept of the chemically bonded interface, which undoubtedly attributes to the strong Pb-S bond confirmed by the shift of Pb 4 f XPS spectra to higher binding energies (Fig. S14). To confirm this conclusion, HRTEM images were further characterized to visually understand the   $  CsPbBr_{3}/ETL  $   interfaces. As shown in Fig. 3c, there is an obvious change from discontinuous contact to tightly connected interface after incorporating   $  SnS_{2}  $   into the system. In this fashion, comparing to the state-of-the-art separated interfaces, the proposed all-in-one interface can significantly reduce the buried defects. Afterwards, the Tetris-like lattice stacking in turn promotes the higher perovskite film quality with smoother surface (Fig. S15) and larger grains (Fig. 3d), which agrees well with the increased diffraction peak intensity and optical absorbance (Fig. S16). Combined with the interfacial energetics and improved film quality, this novel structure enables more electrons to be collected by the underlying ETL, leading to the higher average surface potential with narrower distribution for   $  SnS_{2}  $ -tailored perovskite film (Fig. 3d and Fig. S17). To quantitatively evaluate the defect density, the dark J-V curves of electron-only devices are recorded (Fig. 3e), and the smaller trap-filled limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ) for   $  SnO_{2}-SnS_{2}  $   tailored device directly indicates the reduced defect density from   $  1.80 \times 10^{16} cm^{-3}  $   to   $  1.41 \times 10^{16} cm^{-3}  $   for perovskite layer [40].

Fig. 4a and Fig. S20a-c shows the long-term stability of PSCs in air condition with  $ RH $  of  $ 80\% $  and temperature  $ (T) $  of  $ 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ . Obviously, the unencapsulated  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  PSC with  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 - SnS_2} $  remains nearly  $ 91\% $  of initial performance after  $ 700\mathrm{h} $  storage, slightly overwhelming the control device owing to the increased film quality and protection by carbon film [45]. In addition, arising from the lower energy barrier for ion migration at  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 / CsPbBr_3} $  interface, the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  tailored device suffers from serious performance degradation under high temperature because of the presence of expanded lattice owing to the unmatched thermal expansion coefficient [24]. By fabricating this all-in-one interface to effectively heal the buried interface, the detrimental ion migration or lattice-strain induced defects will be suppressed to trigger the phase degradation, and therefore improving the long-term stability. As shown in Fig. 4b and Fig. S20d-f, the incorporation of  $ \mathrm{SnS_2} $  into the interface also displays a positive effect on improving the device thermal stability at  $ 80^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  over 30 days.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we have successfully demonstrated a   $  SnO_{2}-SnS_{2}  $   heterojunction nanocrystal to improve the performance of all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSC. Owing to the double-plane matched lattices of   $  SnS_{2}  $   with   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $ , an all-in-one   $  SnO_{2}-SnS_{2}-CsPbBr_{3}  $   interface is fabricated. Arising from the Type-II band alignment and reduced buried defects, the energy loss during the charge transfer is minimized. Finally, a champion PCE of 10.72% with an ultrahigh   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.635 V has been achieved. Moreover, the unencapsulated device also shows excellent long-term stability under persistent attack by high humidity and temperature. Our findings may provide a new strategy for optimizing interface properties and perovskite film quality to achieve high-efficiency and stable PSCs.
