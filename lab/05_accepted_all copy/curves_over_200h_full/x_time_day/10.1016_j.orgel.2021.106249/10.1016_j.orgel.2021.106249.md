# 1. Introduction

In recent years, hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have captured extensive attention because of their unique optoelectrical properties, such as direct band gap, long carrier lifetime, large absorption coefficient, and low exciton binding energy [1-4]. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the PSCs has rapidly increased to  $ 25.5\% $  from  $ 3.8\% $  [5, 6]. The conventional PSCs ( $ n $ -i-p type) are usually composed of an electron transport layer (ETL), a perovskite (PVK) layer and a hole transport layer (HTL). The role of HTL is to transport holes and protect PVK from the influence of water and oxygen, which is vital to the performance and stability of PSCs. The reported highly efficient PSCs are usually based on organic hole transport materials, such as Spiro-OMeTAD (Spiro), PTAA or P3HT [7-9]. In order to improve the mobility and conductivity of the hole transport material (HTM), some dopants such as tBP and Li-TFSI must be added [10,11]. The Li-TFSI is hygroscopic and easy to absorb moisture, which leads to the degradation of perovskite. On the other hand, the organic HTM is very expensive, which seriously hinders the commercialization of PSCs.

In this paper, double-layered hole transport material (HTM) was designed and fabricated by adding a thin   $  CuInS_{2}  $   film between perovskite and Spiro-OMeTAD (Spiro) layers. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with double-layered HTM of   $  CuInS_{2}/Spiro  $   was improved to 19.63% from 17.97% for the devices with pure Spiro. Moreover, the operational stability of the PSCs with double-layered HTM of   $  CuInS_{2}/Spiro  $   was enhanced. The PCE of the PSCs with   $  CuInS_{2}/Spiro  $   retains 91% of the initial value after 30 days storage in ambient atmosphere. The experimental results indicate that the improved performance could be come from the energy band match between   $  CuInS_{2}  $   and Spiro, fast hole extraction and transport, and decreased charge recombination in the PSCs with double-layered HTM of   $  CuInS_{2}/Spiro  $  . This work provides a promising prospect to design a low-cost and high stability HTM for commercial PSCs.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Chemicals

$ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  colloid solution was provided by Alfa Aesar (15 wt% in  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  colloidal dispersion). Lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2, 99.99\%) $ , methylammonium bromide (MABr), methylamine hydrochloride (MACl), Spiro and formamidinium iodide (FAI) were purchased from the Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corporation. Powdered sulfur (S), diphenylphosphine (DPP), 1-octadecene (ODE) and oleylamine (OAm) were obtained from Shanghai Aladdin. CuI and In(OAc) $ _3 $  were provided by Sigma-Aldrich.

# 2.2. Synthesis of CuInS$_{2}$ nanocrystals

Inorganic hole transport material of  $ \mathrm{CuInS}_2 $  nanocrystals was prepared by the hot injection method in our previous report [20].

# 2.3. Device fabrication

The ITO substrate was rinsed by sonication in acetone, isopropyl alcohol (IPA) and ethanol for 20 min, respectively. A thin   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer was obtained by spin-coating the diluted solution of   $  SnO_{2}  $   (  $  \sim8\%  $  ) at 4000 rpm for 30 s, and annealing at 150 °C for 30 min on a hot plate. The substrate was transferred into a   $  N_{2}  $   filled glovebox (water and oxygen level < 1 ppm) for the preparation of perovskite and HTM layers. The perovksite layer was formed with two-step method. Firstly,   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution was spin-coated on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer at 1500 rpm for 30 s, and heated at 70 °C for 1 min on a hotplate. The   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution was obtained by dissolving   $  PbI_{2}  $   powder (599.3 mg) in mixed solvent of DMF (950   $  \mu  $  l) and DMSO (50   $  \mu  $  l) at 80 °C for 12 h. Secondly, the mixed solution of MABr, and MACl, and FAI was spin-coated on the   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer at 1300 rpm for 30 s, and annealed at 150 °C for 15 min. The mixture solution was prepared by mixing FAI (60 mg), MABr (6 mg) and MACl (6 mg) in 1 ml IPA. A thin   $  CuInS_{2}  $   film was prepared by spin-coating the   $  CuInS_{2}  $   dispersion solution on the perovskite layer at 4000 rpm for 30 s, and heated at 100 °C for 5 min. A Spiro layer was formed by spin-coating the Spiro solution at 4000 rpm for 30 s, which was obtained by mixing 72.3 mg Spiro, 17.5   $  \mu  $  l LiTFSI, 28.8   $  \mu  $  l tBP and 1 ml chlorobenzene. Finally, a 90 nm thick Au electrode was formed on the top of the HTL layer by thermal evaporation.

# 2.4. Characterization

Current density-voltage (J-V) measurements were performed with a Keithley 2400 source meter under standard AM  $ 1.5\mathrm{G} $  solar simulator. The effective area of the PSC was  $ 0.1~\mathrm{cm}^2 $  using a mask. UV-Vis absorption spectrum was recorded with a spectrophotometer (Varian Cary 5000). The ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) was recorded with a monochromatic He I light source  $ (21.2\mathrm{eV}) $  with a PHI5000 VersaProbe III analyzer. The steady state photoluminescence (PL) and the time-resolved photo-luminescence (TRPL) were recorded with FLS 980 E spectrometer (UK) using an excitation wavelength of  $ 515~\mathrm{nm} $  laser. The surface morphology and cross-section of cells were obtained using a scanning electron microscope (SEM, JSM-7001 F). The AFM graphics were characterized by using a Bruker Multimode 8 atomic force

microscope. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was recorded by a CHI660e electrochemical workstation. Incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) was conducted in the monochromatic light range from 300 to  $ 850~\mathrm{nm} $  with an EQE system (Crowntech Qtest Station 500ADX, America).

# 3. Results and discussion

Fig. 1a presents the XRD pattern of  $ \mathrm{CuInS}_2 $  nanocrystals. The three main diffraction peaks are according to the (116)/(312), (204)/(220), and (112) crystal planes, respectively [19,20], which indicates that  $ \mathrm{CuInS}_2 $  has a chalcopyrite structure. Fig. 1b shows the TEM image of  $ \mathrm{CuInS}_2 $  nanocrystals, which have uniform morphology and spherical shape. The average size of the  $ \mathrm{CuInS}_2 $  nanocrystals was estimated to be 6 nm. Fig. 1c displays the UV-vis absorption of the  $ \mathrm{CuInS}_2 $  nanocrystals. The energy band gap  $ (\mathrm{E_g}) $  could be determined to be  $ 1.62\mathrm{eV} $  from the Tauc plot derived from the UV-vis absorption as shown in Fig. S1 (Supporting Information).

To check the energy level of valence band (  $  E_{V}  $  ), ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) was performed. Fig. S2 (Supporting Information) provides the UPS of   $  CuInS_{2}  $  . Fig. 2a and b shows the magnified views of the high and low binding energy regions of the UPS for   $  CuInS_{2}  $  . From the UPS, the primary photoelectron cut-off binding energy (  $  E_{cut-off}  $  ) and the secondary photoelectron onset energy (  $  E_{onset}  $  ) can be obtained to be 16.6 eV and 0.79 eV, respectively. The   $  E_{V}  $   could be obtained with eq. (1) [21,22],

$$
\mathrm{E} _ {\mathrm{V}} = \mathrm{hv} - (\mathrm{E} _ {\text {cut - off}} - \mathrm{E} _ {\text {onset}})
$$

(1)

where  $  h\nu  $  is the incident photon energy ( $  h\nu = 21.2  $ ). Thus the  $  E_V  $  was determined to be  $ -5.39\mathrm{eV} $  for  $  \mathrm{CuInS}_2  $ . The energy of conduction band ( $  E_C  $ ) can be calculated to be  $ -3.77\mathrm{eV} $  with eq. (2),

$$
E _ {C} = E _ {V} + E _ {g}
$$

(2)

where the  $ \mathrm{E_g} $  is the energy band gap of the  $ \mathrm{CuInS_2} $  (1.62 eV).

Based on these results and the previous reports   $  [23,24]  $  , the energy level diagrams of the materials in the double-layered PSCs are schematically illustrated in Fig. 3a. The valence bands of perovskite,   $  CuInS_{2}  $   and Spiro are located at -5.64 eV, -5.39 eV and -5.20 eV respectively. This energy band alignment is beneficial for the hole separation and transport   $  [16,17]  $  . Thus we designed and assembled PSCs with a configuration of ITO/  $  SnO_{2}  $  /Perovskite/  $  CuInS_{2}  $  /Spiro/Au as shown in Fig. 3b, in which   $  CuInS_{2}  $  /Spiro was applied as a double-layered HTM. In this study, J-V measurements were performed by forward scan (FS) and reverse scan (RS). The photovoltaic parameters of the solar cells such as short-circuit current (Jsc), open-circuit voltage (Voc), fill factor (FF), and power conversion efficiency (PCE) were obtained by an average of the values extracted from the FS and RS scans. The statistical data of the photovoltaic parameters were obtained by an average of the values from 15 pieces of devices.

For the PSCs with double-layered HTM, the thickness of   $  CuInS_{2}  $   film is an important factor to affect the performance of the devices. We optimized the performance of the PSCs by changing the concentration of   $  CuInS_{2}  $   solution to regulate the thickness of   $  CuInS_{2}  $   layer. Fig. 4a provides the relationship between the PCE of the PSCs and the concentration of   $  CuInS_{2}  $   solution. As can be seen, the PCE of the device reaches a maximum value at the concentration of 3 mg/ml. Thereafter we fabricated PSCs with the optimized concentration of   $  CuInS_{2}  $   solution. Fig. 4b presents the cross-sectional SEM image of the PSC with   $  CuInS_{2}/Spiro  $   double-layered HTM.

# 4. Conclusion

In this work, double-layered HTM of   $  CuInS_{2}/Spiro  $   was prepared by deposition of a thin and smooth   $  CuInS_{2}  $   layer between the perovskite and Spiro layers. The performance of the PSCs with a double-layered HTM of   $  CuInS_{2}/Spiro  $   was improved compared with that of the devices with pure Spiro. Moreover, the operational stability of the PSCs with double-layered HTM of   $  CuInS_{2}/Spiro  $   was enhanced. The experimental results demonstrate that the addition of   $  CuInS_{2}  $   film makes the hole extraction and transport fast and the charge recombination decreased, and thus improves the performance of the PSCs. The reason of the enhanced stability for the PSCs with   $  CuInS_{2}/Spiro  $   could be that   $  CuInS_{2}  $   acts as a blocking layer for moisture and oxygen, as well as reduces surface roughness since the nanocrystals are small enough to penetrate the gaps between perovskite grains. Some inorganic semiconductors such as   $  WO_{3}  $ , NiOx and CuSCN have been combined with organic HTM of Spiro to construct double-layered HTM for the application of PSCs [16–18]. Compared with these materials, the advantage of   $  CuInS_{2}  $   is that the PCE of the solar cells with pure   $  CuInS_{2}  $   is higher than those of the devices with these materials, which demonstrates the excellent hole transport ability of   $  CuInS_{2}  $ . In this study, the surface of synthesized   $  CuInS_{2}  $   nanocrystal was coated by long-chain ligands, which will influence the charge transport due to their insulating property. This issue could be solved by an exchange of the long-chain ligands with short-chain ligands. Thus the performance of the solar cells based on   $  CuInS_{2}  $   nanocrystals is expected to be further increased in the future.
