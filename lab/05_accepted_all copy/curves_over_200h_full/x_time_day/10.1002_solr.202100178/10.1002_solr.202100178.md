# Abstract

Inorganic cesium lead halide perovskite solar cells are promising candidates for next generation photovoltaic applications. However, their phase instability and relatively low efficiency hinder their commercialization. In this work, hydrophobic organic ammonium halides (Cl, Br, I) were rationally employed for the modification of inorganic   $  CsPb(I_{0.75}Br_{0.25})_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells. Benefiting from their passivation effects and hydrophobic long alkyl chain, the modified devices exhibited enhanced efficiency and stability. Among them, hexadecyltrimethylammonium chloride (CTAC) modified device showed the best performance with a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 18.05%. Furthermore, a gradient triple anion inorganic perovskite   $  CsPb(I_{0.75}Br_{0.25})_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   layer was formed in-situ during the CTAC modification, which demonstrated better phase stability than   $  CsPb(I_{0.75}Br_{0.25})_{3}  $  . As a result, the modified device also showed excellent stability, maintaining 94% of the initial efficiency after 35 days in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere.

# Introduction

The photovoltaic field has witnessed a tremendous progress of three dimensional (3D) organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) in the past decade, with a certified PCE reaching 25.5%.  $  ^{[1-10]}  $   However, the limited stability of 3D organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs under moisture and heat resulting mainly from the volatility of organic molecules, such as methylammonium (MA) and formamidinium (FA), hinders their further long-term application.  $  ^{[11]}  $   Inorganic cesium lead halide perovskites   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   (X=Br, I), prepared by replacing volatile organic components with inorganic cation Cs, have been widely researched due to their potential to improve the stability of PSCs intrinsically.  $  ^{[12-16]}  $

$ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  possesses the most ideal band gap  $ (E_{\mathrm{g}}) $  of  $ 1.73\mathrm{eV} $  for solar cell application among the cesium lead halide perovskites. However, it suffers from the thermodynamic phase instability, as the photoactive black phase ( $ \alpha $ ,  $ \beta $ , and  $ \gamma $  phase) spontaneously transforms into a thermodynamically more stable yellow phase ( $ \sigma $ -phase) in ambient condition.[17, 18] Introducing Br into  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  can improve the phase stability, but at the same time it undesirably increases the band gap. In order to balance the band gap and phase stability,  $ \mathrm{CsPb(I_{0.75}Br_{0.25})_3} $  and  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  have been widely applied for highly efficient inorganic PSCs.[19, 20] Nevertheless, the phase instability of these I-rich inorganic perovskites still poses a significant challenge for the long-term application of inorganic PSCs.[13] In addition, the abundant defects in inorganic perovskite films limit the PCEs of inorganic PSCs.[21]

Interfacial modification has been verified as an effective method for improving the perovskite phase stability, passivating defects and enhancing PCEs of inorganic PSCs.  $  ^{[15, 22, 23]}  $   For example, organic ammonium salts, such as phenylethylammonium iodide (PEAI), octylammonium iodide (OAI) and choline chloride (CHI) et al.,  $  ^{[1, 15, 24-28]}  $   have been used as modifiers and successfully improved the performance of the devices. However, most of these reports focus on the organic ammonium cations without studying the halide effects in detail, and the exact roles and differences of various halide anions are still indistinct.  $  ^{[26, 29, 30]}  $   On the other hand, though different organic ammonium cations were studied, they are generally limited to aromatic ring and short alkyl chain molecules, which exhibit limited hydrophobicity effect.  $  ^{[31]}  $

Inspired by discussion above, to further enhance the hydrophobicity and elucidate the halide effects, here we introduce a series of hydrophobic organic ammonium halides, such as tetramethylammonium chloride (TMAC) and hexadecyltrimethylammonium halide (CTAX,  $ X = I $ , Br, Cl) to modify inorganic perovskite  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_{2.25}Br_{0.75}} $  devices. We find that the CTAX modified perovskite films all show enhanced hydrophobicity and the PCEs of the corresponding devices also increase. Furthermore, we compare the effects of halides using CTAI, CTAB and CTAC, and the CTAC modified device shows the best performance with a PCE of  $ 18.05\% $ . Interestingly, a gradient triple anion inorganic perovskite  $ \mathrm{CsPb(I_{0.75}Br_{0.25})_{3 - x}Cl_x} $  layer is formed in-situ during the CTAC modification, which demonstrates better phase stability than  $ \mathrm{CsPb(I_{0.75}Br_{0.25})_3} $ . As a result, the device also shows excellent stability, maintaining  $ 94\% $  of the initial PCE after 35 days in  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $  atmosphere.

Control   $  CsPbI_{2.25}Br_{0.75}  $   perovskite films were obtained by gradient annealing the fresh   $  CsPbI_{2.25}Br_{0.75}-0.5FAAc  $   films (detail in experimental section).  $  ^{[19, 32]}  $   Following that, the perovskite films were modified by different organic ammonium halides in iso-propanol (IPA) solution (  $  4*10^{-3}  $   mM), shown in Figure 1a. To compare the role of cations, the modified agents with the same chloride anion and different cations (such as TMAC and CTAC) are employed, whose chemical structures are shown in Figure 1b. The water contact angles of control film and the modified films with TMAC and CTAC are   $  52.01^{\circ}  $  ,   $  58.05^{\circ}  $   and   $  93.22^{\circ}  $  , respectively. The hexadecyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) and hexadecyltrimethylammonium iodide (CTAI) modified films also show large water contact angles as illustrated in Figure S1, which demonstrates that the introduction of the hydrophobic long alkyl chain of CTA  $  ^{+}  $  improves the hydrophobicity of perovskite films, in agreement with previous report.  $  ^{[33]}  $   Furthermore, the CTAC-modified film keeps black color for longer time in ambient condition and shows better phase stability than the control film and the TMAC-modified films, as shown in Figure S2. The CTAC-modified PSCs show better PCEs compared to control PSCs and the modified PSCs with TMAC. Thus, CTA  $  ^{+}  $  halides are chosen as the representative modulators for the following study.

In order to study the effects of halides in detail, (CTAX,  $ X = I $ , Br, Cl) are employed to modify the control perovskite films. The top view and cross-sectional scanning electron microscope (SEM) images of the control and CTAX modified films were presented in Figure S3, all of them display a pinhole-free morphology and compact interfacial contact between the tin oxide layer and perovskite layer. The ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) absorption spectra and the Tauc-plot spectra (Figure 2a-b) show that the band gap of control film and the CTAI modified film are similar with  $ 1.869\mathrm{eV} $ . The CTAB and CTAC modified films display an obvious increased band gap of  $ 1.874\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ 1.877\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively, and their values are further

Steady state photoluminescence (PL) spectra in Figure 2e show that PL intensities of the CTAX modulated films have obvious increase compared to that of control film. Among them, the CTAC-modified film shows the highest PL intensity. Furthermore, time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra in Figure 2f show the  $ \tau_{\mathrm{ave}} $  values of CTAX modified films increase from 15.80 ns of that of control film to 25.58 ns, 40.01 ns and 47.47 ns, respectively. These indicate that CTAX modifications passivate the defects and lead to the decrease of defect density, with CTAC showing the best role of passivation.

The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra of N 1s in Figure 2g show that there is negligible N in the control film, which indicates that high-temperature annealing leads to the removal of  $ \mathrm{FA^{+}} $  from the  $ \mathrm{CsPb(I_{0.75}Br_{0.25})_3} $  perovskite lattice and the formation of all-inorganic perovskite, which is consistent with the previous report.[19] The XPS spectra of CTAX modified films show the obvious peaks of N 1s, verifying that  $ \mathrm{CTA^{+}} $  exists in the modified films. The peak shifts (Cl 2p, Br 3d, I 3d) of the corresponding halides in CTAX compared to that of the CTAX modified films indicate the interaction between CTAX and the perovskite layers. ToF-SIMS depth profiles of CTAC modified  $ \mathrm{CsPb(I_{0.75}Br_{0.25})_3} $  film in Figure 2k display a gradient distribution of Cl content in the perovskite film, indicating the formation of a triple halide mixed perovskite layer with more Cl on the top and less Cl in the bottom section of the perovskite film. We further tested the UV-vis absorption spectra and XRD patterns of  $ \mathrm{CsPb(I_{0.75}Br_{0.25})_{2.95}Cl_{0.05}} $  and  $ \mathrm{CsPb(I_{0.75}Br_{0.25})_{2.90}Cl_{0.10}} $  films (Figure S8). The shift of the absorption onset and characteristic peaks indicate that Cl was incorporated into the perovskite lattice, which is consistent with the CTAC modified film.

This indicates that the formation of the gradient triple halide perovskite film by CTAC modification does not reduce the light absorbing wavelength range of device compared to the control binary perovskite film. The steady-state PCE of the best device is 17.48% with a stable current density of 15.89 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  under a constant bias voltage of 1.10 V for 300 s, as presented in Figure S10. The statistic of photovoltaic performances for control and CTAC modified devices in Figure 3c indicates a good reproducibility for CTAC modified devices. (i) The addition of CI improved stability and suppressed non-radiative recombination, in agreement with the previous reports $ ^{[34, 35]} $ . (ii) The gradient distribution of triple anion inorganic perovskite CsPb(I $ _{0.75} $ Br $ _{0.25} $ ) $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  layer promoted the carrier transport $ ^{[36, 37]} $ . The optimized concentration of CTAC solution is 4*10 $ ^{-4} $  mmol mL $ ^{-1} $  by comparing the CTAC-modified device efficiency with different concentrations (Figure S11).

To examine the long-term stability, the control film and CTAX modified films are exposed to ambient condition (RT=25 °C, RH=30%) (Figure S12). These modified films all show slower degradation rate than the control film, and the CTAC modified film is the best among them. To further study the roles of CTAC modification, the  $ J-V $  characteristics of control and CTAC-modified devices were measured under dark condition as shown in Figure 4a. The dark current density of the CTAC modified device is lower than that of the control device at low bias voltage between -0.2 V to 0.2 V, which indicates that the dark current originated from the carrier movements caused by defects is suppressed.[24, 38] Trap passivation by CTAC treatment was further proved by analyzing the dependence of  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  on the light intensity as shown in Figure 4b. By linear fitting  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  versus log-scaled light intensity, the trap-assisted recombination could be reflected by the slope value and  $ kT/q $  (k is the Boltzmann constant, T is temperature, and q is the electric charge). The smaller slope value of 1.81kT/q for CTAC modified PSCs compared to that of 1.97kT/q for control PSCs indicates a suppressed trap-assisted recombination in the solar cell. We further test the space charge limited current (SCLC) of the electron-only and hole-only devices as shown in Figure 4c-d. The trap density could be calculated according to equation:  $ N_{\mathrm{trap}} = V_{\mathrm{tfl}} \times (2 \times \varepsilon \times \varepsilon_0) / e \times L^2 $ , where  $ V_{\mathrm{tfl}} $  is the onset voltage of the trap-filled limit region,  $ \varepsilon $  is relative dielectric constant, and  $ \varepsilon_0 $  is vacuum permittivity, e is the elementary charge and L is the thickness of perovskite film in device.[39] The  $ V_{\mathrm{tfl}} $  value of CTAC modified film is lower than that of the control film, suggesting a reduced trap density, and thus leads to improved photovoltaic performance.

# Conclusion

In summary, we demonstrated that the organic ammonium halides with long hydrophobic alkyl chains could effectively improve the efficiency and stability of   $  CsPbI_{2.25}Br_{0.75}  $   solar cells. By comparing the modified agents with different halides, we found the CTAC modified perovskite solar cells displayed the best PCE of 18.05%, probably due to the stronger electronegativity of Cl compared to that of Br and I. Further investigation showed that the triple anion Cl/Br/I mixed perovskite layer was formed after CTAC treatment, which exhibited better stability than Br/I mixed phase perovskite.
