# ABSTRACT

Despite impressive power conversion efficiency (PCE) climbing up to 25.2%, complex processing and high cost of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) associated with hole transport material (HTM), Au counter electrode and transparent conducting oxide (TCO) electrode limit the commercial applications of PSCs. We herein fabricate an ultra-low-cost HTM-free C-PSC with the structure of AgNWs-graphene (AgNWs-G)/SnO  $  _{2}  $  /perovskite/carbon by using 1D-2D hybrid AgNWs-G transparent electrode instead of conventional ITO electrode. The optimized AgNWs-G film (AgNWs-G-2 film) with a relatively high transmittance (  $  \sim86\%  $  ) achieves a sheet resistance (Rs) of 11.0   $  \Omega  $   sq  $  ^{-1}  $   lower than that of ITO (13.5   $  \Omega  $   sq  $  ^{-1}  $  ). Compared with AgNWs film, the AgNWs-G-2 film exhibits fantastic stability of Rs within the entire duration of air exposure, even though at high temperatures or continuous illumination. Such the low-cost AgNWs-G based C-PSC achieves an outstanding PCE of 15.31% and a long-term stability of 87.5% for 60 days in air conditions. It is worth noting that all preparing processes from bottom electrode to counter electrode in the total PSC device are operated in actual ambient air condition, and the annealing temperature for each functional layer is no more than 150 °C, hence leading to the construction of ultra-low-cost PSCs.

As examples, chemical vapor deposition (CVD) growth graphene and graphene oxide (GO) have been used to wrap AgNWs, and the hybrid transparent conductive films provided a great sheet resistance (  $  R_{s}  $  ) of   $  22\ \Omega\ sq^{-1}  $   at the optical transmittance of 88% for AgNWs-graphene and an   $  R_{s}  $   of   $  14\ \Omega\ sq^{-1}  $   at a transmittance of 88% for AgNWs-GO, respectively   $  [34,40]  $  . In this context, we herein attempt to fabricate a 1D-2D hybrid AgNWs-graphene (AgNWs-G) transparent electrode instead of common ITO electrode by simple sequential spin-coating process. Note that the cost of AgNWs and graphene dispersions is roughly an order of magnitude lower than that of ITO electrode (Table S1). Graphene film was spin-coated onto glass/AgNWs substrates, which can contribute to polishing the surface of AgNWs film and preserving AgNWs from degradation. By optimization, the compact and smooth AgNWs-G-2 film achieves a lower  $ \mathrm{R_s} $  of  $ 11.0\Omega \mathrm{sq}^{-1} $  than that of ITO  $ (13.5\Omega \mathrm{sq}^{-1}) $ , and a relatively high transmittance of  $ 86\% $ . Compared with pristine AgNWs film, AgNWs-G-2 film exhibits fantastic stability of  $ \mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{s}} $  within the entire duration of air exposure, even though at high temperature and continuous illumination. Such a low-cost AgNWs-G-2 electrode film is implemented into a low-cost HTM-free C-PSC with the structure of AgNWs-G/  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ /perovskite/carbon (Fig. 1), achieving an ultralow-cost C-PSC with an outstanding PCE of  $ 15.31\% $  and a long-term stability of  $ 87.5\% $  for 60 days in air conditions. This study construct a kind of ultra-low-cost PSC suitable to scalable and practical application.

# 2. Results and discussion

AgNWs network was fabricated by spin-coating AgNWs dispersion at 1000 rpm for 30 s, 1000 rpm for 15s and 2000 rpm for 15 s, 1000 rpm for 15s and 3000 rpm for 15 s. Fig. 2a–c shows the top-view scanning

electron microscopy (SEM) images of the three transparent electrodes (AgNWs-1000, AgNWs-1000-2000 and AgNWs-1000-3000) by using different spin-coating conditions. The initial slow spin-coating process of 1000 rpm for 15s aims to spread AgNWs dispersion over the total substrate. However, the subsequent low spin-coating process of 1000 rpm for another 15s leads to the serious stacking of multilayered AgNWs (AgNWs-1000 film, Fig. 2a). Compared to commercial ITO electrode (transmittance of 93%), multilayer covered AgNWs decreases the transmittance of AgNWs-1000 to 79%. Boosting the spin-coating rate to 2000 or 3000 rpm for 15 s can effectively mitigate the stacking of AgNWs and consequently form thinner AgNWs electrode films (Fig. 2b&c). From Fig. 2c, AgNWs-1000-3000 film is uniformly covered by 1–3 layer AgNWs. Thinner AgNWs film greatly improves the transmittance of AgNWs electrodes, with the transmittance of 84% and 89% for AgNWs-1000-2000 and AgNWs-1000-3000 films, respectively. As a transparent conductive electrode used in solar cell,   $  R_{s}  $   is another important parameter, which is closely related with the thickness and evenness of conductive layer. The distribution statistics of   $  R_{s}  $   of ITO and the three home-made AgNWs electrodes were given in Fig. 2e. Compared to ITO (average 13.5 Ω sq  $  ^{-1}  $  ), AgNWs-1000 films show a lower   $  R_{s}  $   of 7.0 Ω sq  $  ^{-1}  $   in a narrower distribution, indicating good film reproducibility. The   $  R_{s}  $   is 8.5 Ω sq  $  ^{-1}  $   for AgNWs-1000-2000 film and 14.4 Ω sq  $  ^{-1}  $   for AgNWs-1000-3000 film, showing a wider statistics distribution. We implemented these AgNWs electrodes in C-PSCs to evaluate these opt-electric performances. Generally, transmittance and   $  R_{s}  $   are trade-off in a transparent conductive electrode for photoelectrochemical devices [41]. 2D material i.e. graphene can accommodate excellent electrical conductivity and optical transmittance together [42], which is ideal partner for AgNWs to solve the trade-off between transmittance and Rs in transparent conductive electrode. In this work, the high-transmittance AgNWs-1000-3000 film was chosen to further improve its conductivity without sacrificing transparency by graphene modification. Therefore, hybrid-dimensional (1D-2D) AgNWs-G electrode films were fabricated by spin-coating graphene dispersions onto AgNWs (AgNWs-1000-3000)/glass substrates at 1000 rpm for 30 s. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurement, steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra are used to study the internal charge transfer and carrier recombination behaviors in C-PSCs devices   $  [46–48]  $  . Nyquist plots for C-PSC devices based on AgNWs and AgNWs-G-2 electrode measured under dark condition at the applied bias voltage of 0.8 V are shown in Fig. 5a. The obtained data were fitted by using Z-View software according to a simplified equivalent circuit (Fig. 5b), and the well-defined semicircles of the EIS at negative bias voltages under dark condition stands for the recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ). Fig. 5a shows an increasing diameter of the semi-arc for AgNWs-G-2 based device compared with AgNWs based device, which indicates the larger   $  R_{rec}  $   of AgNWs-G-2 based device. This also indicates better interfacial contact and reduced carrier recombination in the AgNWs-G-2 based C-PSC device, resulting in an improved FF   $  [49]  $  .

PL spectra in Fig. 5c show that the intensity of emission peak is getting stronger from AgNWs/perovskite film to AgNWs-G-2/perovskite film, indicating that the PL quenching process is weaken [50]. This also indicates reduced defect states of the perovskite film based on AgNWs-G-2 substrate, which can lead to the enhanced FF and PCE of the device by decreasing the nonradiative recombination. Adhesion measurements on the as-prepared AgNWs-G-2 film show that the scotch tape cannot detach the film from the substrate, indicating its great physical stability (Fig. S6). Furthermore, we perform long-term stability measurements for C-PSC devices without capsulation under air conditions, and cell efficiency periodically as a function of time for the champion device was recorded in Fig. 6d. The AgNWs-G-2 based C-PSC device retains 90% of its initial efficiency after being stored in air up to 45 days, and still retains 87.5% for 60 days. On the contrast, the photovoltaic performance of AgNWs based device degrades sharply to only 23.6% of its peak efficiency after 60 days. This further indicates the superior stability of AgNWs-G-2 film. The photo-stability of C-PSC devices based on AgNWs and AgNWs-G-2 films under continuous 1 sun illumination at MPP tracking was also examined in Fig. S7. The photovoltaic performance of the AgNWs-G-2 based C-PSC varies slightly and still maintains 99.1% of its initial value during 200-h test period. By comparison, the PCE of C-PSC device with AgNWs drops to 84.8% of its initial value after 200 h. The device stability of C-PSCs under illumination and air exposure further indicates the superior stability of AgNWs-G-2 film (Fig. 6d and Fig. S7).

# 3. Conclusions

In conclusion, we have successfully prepared the 1D-2D hybrid AgNWs-G transparent electrode. By optimization, the compact and smooth AgNWs-G-2 film achieves a lower   $  R_{s}  $   of   $  11.0 \Omega sq^{-1}  $   than that of ITO (  $  13.5 \Omega sq^{-1}  $  ) with a narrower statistics distribution. At the same time, the AgNWs-G-2 film could attain a relatively high transmittance of 86%. Compared with pristine AgNWs film, AgNWs-G-2 film exhibits fantastic stability of   $  R_{s}  $   within the entire duration of air exposure, even though at high temperatures or continuous illumination, which can be contributed to graphene layer preserving AgNWs from degradation. Such a low-cost AgNWs-G-2 electrode film is implemented into a low-cost HTM-free C-PSC with the structure of AgNWs-G/  $  SnO_{2}  $  /
