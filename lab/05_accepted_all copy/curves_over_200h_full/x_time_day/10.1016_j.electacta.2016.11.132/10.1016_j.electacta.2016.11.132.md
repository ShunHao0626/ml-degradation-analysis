# Abstract:

The present work reports a comparative study between single and mixed organic cation based  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite devices fabricated in conjunction with low temperature processed ( $ < 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) ZnO electron transport layers.  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite devices demonstrate  $ 37\% $  higher power conversion efficiency compared to  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite devices developed on the ZnO ETL. In addition,  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  devices exhibit very low photocurrent hysteresis and they are three-fold more stable than

The study also investigates into the interfacial charge transfer characteristics associated with the ZnO/mixed organic cation perovskite interface and concomitant influence on the inherent electronic properties.

# 1. Introduction:

Organic inorganic metal halide perovskite solar cells have shown unprecedented potential to revolutionize the photovoltaic industry due to their high power conversion efficiency (PCE) combined with low-cost fabrication capability [1]. The PCE of the perovskite photovoltaic device has already gone up to over  $ 21\% $  [2] in just few years since their significant development by Miyaska et al. [3] in 2009. The generic formula for organic-inorganic methyl halide perovskite can be given as:  $ \mathrm{ABX}_3 $ , where A, B and X stand for organic cation (methyl ammonium  $ [\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3^+] $  or formamidinium  $ [\mathrm{HN} = \mathrm{CHNH}_3^+] $  cation), divalent metal ion (Pb or Sn) and an individual halogen element (Iodine, Chlorine, and Bromine) or a composition of them with a fixed molar ratio respectively. Of late, mixed organic cation based perovskite materials (combination of methyl ammonium and formamidinium cation in organic part of perovskite) [4] have emerged as excellent light harvesters due to their extended light absorption in the near infra-red (IR) light spectrum compared to that of pristine methyl ammonium cation based perovskite materials. In general perovskite tolerance factor,  $ t $  [5], is mathematically expressed as:

$$
\mathrm{t} = \frac {\mathrm{r} _ {\mathrm{A}} + \mathrm{r} _ {\mathrm{X}}}{\sqrt {2} \left(\mathrm{r} _ {\mathrm{B}} + \mathrm{r} _ {\mathrm{X}}\right)}
$$

(1)

where,   $  r_{A}  $  ,   $  r_{B}  $   and   $  r_{X}  $   denote as the ionic radius of organic cation, divalent metal ion and halide ion in perovskite structure respectively. Ideal or near-ideal tolerance factor (t~1) is necessary to ensure phase/entropic stability of perovskite solar cells. The higher perovskite tolerance factor in formamidinium perovskite can be attributed to the relatively large ionic radius (1.9-2.2 Å) [6] of formamidinium organic cation than that (1.8 Å) [6] of methyl ammonium cation. while maintaining the basic ABX $ _3 $  perovskite structure gives rise to enhanced symmetry in formamidinium based perovskite material which eventually results in the reduction of electronic bandgap to 1.4eV [4, 5]. Thus formamidinium based perovskite material offers higher light absorption and conversion of standard AM1.5 sunlight into electrical energy [4]. In addition, FAPbI $ _3 $  is much stable to heat stress than MAPbI $ _3 $  [7]. However, the black perovskite phase of pure FAPbI $ _3 $ , is thermodynamically stable only above 160 °C [6], forming a yellow δ phase below the phase transition temperature. To stabilize the perovskite phase of FAPbI $ _3 $  at room temperature, MAPbI $ _3 $  has been added to produce mixed cation PSCs [4]. Furthermore, it also improves the open circuit voltage (V $ _{OC} $ ) of the fabricated PSCs using mixed cation systems, which enhances the overall device performance significantly [8, 9]. In line with this, mixed organic cation based triiodide perovskite (MA $ _{0.6} $ FA $ _{0.4} $ PbI $ _3 $ , MA=methyl ammonium, FA=formamidinium) has demonstrated promising signs as an photoactive material because of its capability to benefit from red-shifted absorbance pattern compared to pristine MAPbI $ _3 $ , by virtue of its organic formamidinium (FA $ ^+ $ ) part [4]. Simultaneously, MA $ _{0.6} $ FA $ _{0.4} $ PbI $ _3 $  perovskite also demonstrates a more steep increase at the absorption onset compared to pristine FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite due to its methyl ammonium (MA $ ^+ $ ) component [4]. Thus, mixed organic cation based PSC can prevail the benefits from both of its organic constituent cations (MA $ ^+ $  and FA $ ^+ $ ). An average PCE of 13.5% has been reported [4] with this MA $ _{0.6} $ FA $ _{0.4} $ PbI $ _3 $  perovskite material as the light harvester in a mesostructured device employing TiO $ _2 $  as ETL which requires a high sintering temperature of ~500 °C. Apart from being a highly energy intensive process, this high temperature requirement is also a major bottleneck towards roll-to-roll processing for mass production of perovskite solar cells on flexible substrates like PET (Polyethylene terephthalate); as the flexible substrates cannot sustain any temperature over 150 °C [10, 11]. Therefore, finding a suitable low temperature processed alternative to TiO $ _2 $  ETL for perovskite device is highly indispensable. As such, research along this direction has resulted in low temperature processed ZnO nanostructured ETLs [12-15] for efficient perovskite devices. Despite being excellent endeavours towards low temperature processed ETL, the synthesis process of these ZnO nanostructured ETLs [12-15] involves a rigorous procedure of precipitation, dilution and washing process combined with a long reaction time and requires delicate handling and sophisticated machinery, which make the overall process complex and time-consuming. Recently, we have reported sol-gel ZnO ETL in conjunction with mono organic cation based MAPbI $ _3 $  PSC (perovskite solar cell) [16], which provides a simple solution-route fabrication process, having full temperature compatibility with flexible substrates. However, it is yet to investigate how the perovskite surface morphology, photovoltaic performance, hysteresis [17], charge transfer properties, degradation and electrode polarization phenomena [18] vary in single and mixed organic cation based PSCs in conjunction with our reported sol-gel ZnO ETL. The present work is aimed at closing this knowledge gap.

In this work, we have reported a comparative study between mono and mixed organic cation based  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite along with low temperature processed ( $ < 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  temp.) sol-gel ZnO (ETL) in a planar device configuration. The processing temperature for ZnO ETL is  $ 140^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  only, which is three times lower than that typically required with  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL [4]. The mixed organic cation perovskite ( $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ ) films deposited on top of the ZnO ETL exhibited more uniform and homogenous surface morphology. The PSCs thus fabricated with ZnO ETL exhibited the highest PCE of  $ 13.40\% $ . The ZnO/perovskite interface of the mixed cation devices was found to influence the electronic properties and concomitant process including the device stability significantly. A systematic degradation study (21 days) has been carried out to understand the device degradation characteristics and it has been found that  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  PSCs fabricated on ZnO ETL exhibit three times higher stability in contrast with conventional  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  devices. An in-depth analysis on the charge transport properties in both fresh and aged devices is carried out using impedance spectroscopy analysis. In addition to investigating into the improved device performance and lifetime, the present work also reveals substantial mitigation of the hysteresis phenomena with mixed organic cation based perovskite device.

# 2. Experimental Detail:

# 2.1 Device Fabrication:

ITO/glass substrates were cleaned sequentially with Hellmanex III, DI water, Acetone and Isopropanol with duration of 10 minutes for each. A 0.48 M sol-gel ZnO solution was prepared by dissolving Zinc Acetate Dihydrate (Zn(CH3COO) $ _2 $ .2H $ _2 $ O) in 2-Methoxyethanol (CH $ _3 $ OCH $ _2 $ CH $ _2 $ OH) with an additive Ethanolamine (NH $ _2 $ CH $ _2 $ CH $ _2 $ OH) by stirring for 24 hours. The sol-gel ZnO was spin coated on ITO/glass substrate with 4000 rpm for 60 s and then annealed on a hotplate at 140 °C for half an hour. 1 M PbI $ _2 $  solution in DMF (N, N-dimethylformamide) with 120 μL 4-TBP (4-tert-Butylpyridine, Sigma Aldrich) was prepared at a temperature of 70 °C. The PbI $ _2 $  solution was spin cast on ZnO coated substrate at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Before spin coating, both the substrates and the solution were kept at 70 °C. Then the substrates were annealed at 100 °C for one hour and then dipped in 10 mg/ml Methyl Ammonium Iodide solution in 2-Propanol in 0.0628M for 1 min for MAPbI $ _3 $  device. For MA $ _{0.6} $ FA $ _{0.4} $ PbI $ _3 $  device, the dipping solution was replaced with a 2-Propanol solution having a total iodide concentration of 0.0628M with the mole fraction of MAI and FAI to be 0.6 and 0.4 respectively. The device was annealed for 10 minutes at 75 °C for MA $ _{0.6} $ FA $ _{0.4} $ PbI $ _3 $  device, whereas MAPbI $ _3 $  device does not require any annealing. For HTL, 73.3 mg/ml Spiro-OMeTAD (2,2',7,7'-Tetrakis (N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamino) -9,9'-spirobifluorene) in Chlorobenzene was doped with 17.5 μL Li-TFSI (520 mg/ml in Acetonitrile) and 28.8 μL 4-TBP. The Spiro-OMeTAD layer was spin coated on the perovskite layer with 1200 rpm for 30s. Finally, 100 nm Ag

# 2.2 Device Characterization:

The current–voltage characteristics of the devices were measured with a NREL calibrated Keithley 2400 Source Meter under   $  100 \, mW/cm^{2}  $   (AM 1.5G) simulated sunlight. For optical characterization like transmittance, reflectance and absorbance measurement, a UV–VIS-NIR spectrometer (Perkin Elmer – Lambda 950) was used. X-ray diffraction (XRD) with CuKα radiation was performed by step-scanning with a step size of 0.02 degree. Surface topology and device cross sectional view were captured by Carl Zeiss AURIGA Cross Beam SEM (Scanning Electron Microscopy) and the surface roughness was measured with Bruker Dimension ICON SPM AFM (Atomic Force Microscopy) machine. The impedance analysis was conducted with an Autolab PGSTAT-30 equipped with a frequency analyzer module in the frequency range from 1 MHz to 10 Hz. AC oscillating amplitude was as low as 20 mV (RMS) to maintain the linearity of the response.

# 3. Results and Discussion:

In the present work, low temperature processed (140  $ ^{\circ} $ C) sol-gel ZnO thin film has been applied as electron selective contact with MAPbI $ _{3} $  or MA $ _{0.6} $ FA $ _{0.4} $ PbI $ _{3} $  perovskite light harvesting material in a planar, normal structured photovoltaic device. Although, the ZnO ETL in our work is low temperature processed, the formation of basic ZnO structure has been ensured with XRD measurement. The XRD patterns (Fig. S1) of the ZnO thin film show characteristic (002) and (101) diffraction peaks that ascertain the formation of hexagonal wurtzite ZnO phase. The optical properties of the low temperature processed ZnO films have also been investigated with a UV–VIS-NIR spectrometer. In a normal structured planar device, the basic optical role of an electron transport layer is to act as a highly transmitting, less reflective thin film layer to ensure maximum light absorption in the photo-active perovskite film. Fig. S2(A) and S2(B) illustrate high light transmittance and low light reflectance from the fabricated ZnO thin film respectively. Thus, the produced ZnO thin film ensures high light absorption for the overlying perovskite photo-absorber.

On top of ZnO thin film ETL,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  or  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite layer was grown by two step dipping method. Fig. 1 illustrates the XRD patterns of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite layer along with the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  XRD diffraction peaks for the comparison of perovskite conversion. As observed from Fig. 1, no  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  characteristic diffraction peak (001) is visible in  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  or  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite XRD pattern

which ensures full conversion of   $  PbI_{2}  $   to perovskite. This full conversion of perovskite in two step dipping technique was ensured by the incorporation of 4-TBP (4-tert-Butylpyridine) additive [19] into   $  PbI_{2}  $   precursor solution. In the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   XRD pattern, prominent (110), (220), (310), (224) and (314) diffraction peaks confirm the formation of tetragonal perovskite structure [20]. As the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite was prepared at ambient air condition under controlled humidity (35-40% relative humidity), a characteristic peak of dihydrate   $  [(CH_{3}NH_{3})_{4}PbI_{6}\cdot2H_{2}O]  $   at   $  11.4^{0}  $   [21] is also observed in   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   XRD pattern. At humidity controlled ambient condition, moisture facilitates the ion diffusion of unreacted MAI (methyl ammonium iodide) to form   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   and dihydrate and thus accelerates the crystallization of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   structure [21]. Typically, the hydrate peak disappears within 11 days of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   film formation [21]. Since the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   XRD pattern was observed within that time range, the dihydrate diffraction peak was visible. Although, the   $  MA_{0.6}FA_{0.4}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite layer was prepared in an identical ambient condition, there was no dihydrate peak in the XRD pattern. Since,   $  MA_{0.6}FA_{0.4}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite film necessitates an additional annealing (75 °C) step for 10 minutes, it can be inferred that the moisture assisted perovskite crystallization and drying of water-enriched dihydrate are accelerated by the short annealing session with   $  MA_{0.6}FA_{0.4}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite. From the amplified view (Fig. S3) of XRD pattern of   $  MA_{0.6}FA_{0.4}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite, it is prominent that there is a perovskite peak at   $  13.95^{0}  $   which is   $  0.15^{0}  $   right-shifted from the reference   $  \alpha-FAPbI_{3}  $   peak at   $  13.8^{0}  $   [4]. This right shift in   $  \alpha-FAPbI_{3}  $   peak, due to the mixing of MAI, is consistent with the previous literature [4]. No   $  \delta-FAPbI_{3}  $   peak [4, 6] was observed at   $  11.8^{0}  $   in the diffraction pattern of   $  MA_{0.6}FA_{0.4}PbI_{3}  $ , which ensures a mixed organic cation phase of MAI and FAI is formed in which both the organic cations are inserted in the same lattice frame.

After the formation of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite layer on top of ZnO ETL film, the optical characteristics of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  and  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  layers on ITO/glass substrate were examined by absorbance measurement. Fig. S4 shows the absorbance patterns of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  and  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  on ITO/glass substrate. As observed from Fig. S4, the onset of absorption for  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  film is at  $ \sim 800~\mathrm{nm} $ ; whereas, an extended red-shifted absorption onset ( $ \sim 820~\mathrm{nm} $ ) is found for  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  film. It is notable that, the thicknesses of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite films, reported in our work are almost identical ( $ \sim 320~\mathrm{nm} $  for  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and  $ \sim 315~\mathrm{nm} $  for  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite, measured with Dektak Stylus profilometer). As observed from Fig. S4,  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite films have higher absorbance compared to  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite film, despite their thicknesses being close-ranged. This confirms the superior optical characteristics of mixed organic cation perovskite compared to its mono organic counterpart.

Alongside investigating the surface morphology of single and mixed organic cation based perovskite on ZnO thin film, we have also conducted surface topography study of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  and  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  layers with atomic force microscopy (AFM) imaging. Keeping in line with the enhanced surface morphology observed from SEM image, the rms surface roughness for  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  (50.82 nm) was lower compared to that (57.50 nm) with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  layer. It is noteworthy that two step dipping process inherently results in higher RMS surface roughness of perovskite films [19] (even up to 66.56 nm [26]) compared to one step process as the dipping technique involves the drying of 2-Propanol over the perovskite film. Nonetheless, the lower RMS surface roughness of  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  layer in comparison with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  film connotes more favourable surface topography for  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  interface resulting in less leakage current [27] and more efficient charge extraction in a complete photovoltaic device.

Reduced interfacial trap states [28] owing to the smooth, defect free   $  MA_{0.6}FA_{0.4}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite/ZnO interface (Fig. 2(C)-(D)) can also contribute to suppressed charge recombination and thus higher   $  J_{SC}  $   values [29]. The notable enhancement (12%) in FF for   $  MA_{0.6}FA_{0.4}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite devices also connotes to more efficient charge extraction by the charge selective contacts [26] which is prominent from the 19% lower series resistance values compared to   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite devices. Additionally, the mitigated recombination phenomenon in mixed organic perovskite is also hinted by the 5% higher device shunt resistance relative to single organic cation perovskite devices. About 13% lower rms surface roughness of   $  MA_{0.6}FA_{0.4}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite/ZnO film also indicates lower leakage current [27] in   $  MA_{0.6}FA_{0.4}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite device which enhances the   $  J_{SC}  $   and FF in this device compared to single organic cation based   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite device. So, apart from the extended spectral response, the smooth, barrier-free enhanced surface morphology of mixed organic cation based perovskite also contributes to notable augmentation in device   $  J_{SC}  $   and FF and consequently to overall device performance in contrast with mono organic cation perovskite device.

To substantiate our inference regarding the enhanced performance with mixed organic cation perovskite, we have conducted electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) study that unravels conducive information about device electronic properties in terms of interfacial charge transfer phenomena and carrier recombination [30, 31]. A recent study by Belmont et al. [18] has proposed that two concurrent polarization processes take place in perovskite solar cells. One of them is electrode polarization which is originated due to substantial coulombic interactions in the vicinity of metal and ionic conductor interface [42]. In perovskite devices, electrode polarization has been attributed to the interfacial effect associated with migration of ions [18, 41] having slow kinetics and charge agglomeration in the interfacial trap states at the vicinity of perovskite and charge selective contacts. Trapped ionic carriers at the perovskite/ETL or perovskite/HTL interface alter the local electric field which gives rise to electrode polarization [18]. Taking ion continuity and Poisson electrostatic relation into consideration, electrode polarization results in the interfacial space charge at zero applied bias to be confined within the ionic Debye length,  $ \mathrm{L_D} $ , mathematically expressed as [18]:

$$
\mathrm{L} _ {\mathrm{D}} = \sqrt {\frac {\varepsilon_ {0} \varepsilon_ {\mathrm{r}} \mathrm{V} _ {\mathrm{T}}}{\mathrm{qn}}}
$$

(6)

where,  $ \varepsilon_0 $ ,  $ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}} $ ,  $ V_{\mathrm{T}} $ , q and n are free space permittivity, relative permittivity for perovskite, thermal voltage, elementary charge and density of states for the mobile ions forming the interfacial space charge respectively. The interfacial space charge capacitance,  $ C_{\mathrm{SC}} $  can be mathematically derived by equating the ionic Debye length,  $ L_{D} $  to the space charge width at zero applied bias, w, which results in the following expression [18]:

$$
\mathrm{C} _ {\mathrm{sc}} = \frac {\varepsilon_ {0} \varepsilon_ {\mathrm{r}}}{\mathrm{L} _ {\mathrm{D}}}
$$

(7)

As indicated by Eq. 7, interfacial trap assisted electrode polarization effect in perovskite device is exhibited by excess capacitance specially at low frequency regime in the frequency-dependent capacitance response [18]. Due to the excess low frequency capacitance, the required time period to reach the steady state condition is altered which results in photocurrent hysteresis [43]. # 4. Conclusion:

In summary, we have reported a comparative study between single and mixed organic cation based perovskite (MAPbI $ _3 $  and  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ ) devices in conjunction with a low temperature processed ( $ < 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) ZnO thin film ETL in a planar device structure. Along with  $ 37\% $  higher average device efficiency, low temperature processed mixed organic cation perovskite device in our study exhibits three times higher device stability and significantly suppressed hysteresis phenomena compared to mono organic cation based  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  device. Surface morphology and surface topography characterizations conducted in our work unravel that the pinhole free, smooth morphology at  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3/\mathrm{ZnO} $  ETL interface plays a vital role towards enhancement in PCE in mixed organic cation based perovskites. Significantly low contact resistance and larger recombination resistance attained from the impedance spectroscopic analysis also corroborate to the enhanced power conversion efficiency in  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  devices based on their superior interfacial electronic properties. In addition, reduced photocurrent hysteresis in  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite device has also been observed and explicated by the lower electrode polarization effect exhibited in mixed organic cation perovskite compared to its mono organic cation counterpart. A systematic 3 week-long degradation study including electrochemical impedance spectroscopy and frequency-dependent capacitance measurement of the aged devices, shows that the mixed organic cation perovskite device is endowed with higher stability against the decomposition of perovskite film due to reduced interfacial trap states at  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3/\mathrm{ZnO} $ . Thus, sol-gel ZnO ETL provides a simple, less energy intensive solution processed route towards low temperature processed perovskite device fabrication, having full temperature compatibility with flexible substrates in roll-to-roll process. As reported in our present study, mixed organic cation based  $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.6}\mathrm{FA}_{0.4}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  PSCs along with sol-gel ZnO ETL provide significantly enhanced device performance, substantially reduced photo-current hysteresis phenomena and notable device stability compared to their mono organic counterparts (MAPbI $ _3 $  PSCs).
