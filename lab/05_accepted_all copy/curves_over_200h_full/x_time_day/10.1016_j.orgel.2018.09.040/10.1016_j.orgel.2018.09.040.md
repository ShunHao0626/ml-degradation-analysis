# Abstract:

Conventional perovskites solar cells have been afflicted with serious hysteresis effect, resulting in difficult to accurate the real power conversion efficiency of devices. Here, an additive of KSCN was applied to enhance the crystallization of perovskite crystals, increased the perovskite grain size, reduced the density of defects, led to a high-quality thin film. Correspondingly, devices with a power conversion efficiency of  $ 19.23\% $  and fill factor of  $ 75.08\% $  under AM 1.5G  $ 100\mathrm{mW}\cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $  irradiation were achieved. In comparison with typical devices, these solar cells showed improved photovoltaic performance, stability, reproducibility, carrier mobility and lower hysteresis effect, which was attributed to the high-quality thin film. Our work demonstrated a facile and effective approach for fabricating solar cells with no-hysteresis and high-performance towards commercialization.

Key words: Perovskites solar cells; Hysteresis effect; KSCN; Power conversion efficiency

# 1. Introduction

In recent years, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials have set off a wave of research as promising optoelectronic materials in the field of solar cells. The organic–inorganic lead halide perovskites have shown enormous potential for producing low-cost and high efficiency thin-film solar cells. To date, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) haved raised from 3.8% to 22.1%  $  ^{[1-8]}  $  . Lead halide perovskite materials have exhibited superior photovoltaic properties, such as extremely high light extinction coefficient  $  ^{[9]}  $  , long carrier diffusion lengths  $  ^{[10, 11]}  $  , long carrier lifetime and high mobility  $  ^{[12]}  $  . Although PCE of perovskite solar cells has been rapidly improved, there are still problems such as hysteresis and stability hindering its commercialization process  $  ^{[13]}  $  .

The hysteresis of perovskite solar cells are mainly depended on the perovskite materials, electron and hole transport materials, test scan speed and external electric field [14]. The adverse effect of hysteresis on the stability of perovskite devices is mainly attributed to the following aspects [15, 16]: (1) The inconsistency of the positive and negative charge centers of gravity leads to the occurrence of electrical dipole moments and spontaneous polarization. Then, the direction of the electrical dipole moment will be changed due to the external electric field [17, 18]; (2) The barriers will be triggered by the migration and the aggregation of ions at the interface [19-23]; (3) The regulation charge transport is suppressed by the capturing and releasing deep defects [24, 25]. The defect leads to the presence of traps in the forbidden band of the perovskite, non-radiative recombination with electrons and holes, exacerbating the hysteresis effect of the battery[26]. Up to present, the origin of hysteresis is not yet clear. In order to reduce the hysteresis effect of the device, numerous improved methods and processes have been extensively exploited, including the promoting

charge transfer at the interface, inhibiting the diffusion of ions at different interfaces and reducing the concentration of defects in perovskite solar cells [27-30].

Recently, pseudohalide-based (SCN) perovskites have recently attracted much attention as alternatives to conventional lead halides, which have similar ionic radii with I  $  ^{[31, 32]}  $  . Incorporation of a pseudohalide instead of a conventional lead halide can improve the moisture tolerance, the optical absorption, radiative recombination and charge transport efficiency, leading to a higher photovoltaic performance. Furtheore, the incorporation of potassium into the perovskite absorber was proved an effective approach to significantly improve the photovoltaic performance of PSCs and diminish hysteresis, through modifying the crystal lattice, decreasing its band gap, and along with the vanishing of the grain boundary in it with a longer carrier lifetime  $  ^{[33-35]}  $  . Abdi-Jalebi et al. demonstrated that the non-radiative loss and photo-induced ion transport of the perovskite film interface could be substantially mitigated by passivating the surface and grain boundaries of the modified perovskite film with a potassium halide layer.  $  ^{[36]}  $

In this paper, we reported a facile method which could significantly improve quality of the perovskite film, thus effectively reduce the density of defect states in perovskite films, restrain the non-radiative recombination, prolong the electron lifetime, and improve the electron transport capability of the perovskite film. At the same time, the hysteresis effect could be effectively avoided, the forward and backward sweep efficiency of the device exceeded  $ 18\% $ , and the photovoltaic performance of perovskite devices was significantly improved.

# 2.2 PSC fabrication

Initially, FTO substrates (7Ω sq  $  ^{-1}  $  ) with an area of 1.5 x 1.5 cm  $  ^{2}  $   were cleaned sequentially in 2% Hellmanex detergent, 2-propanol and ethanol for 30 min each in an ultrasonic bath and dried with clean dry air. Then substrates were further treated with an ultraviolet ozone cleaner for 15 min. Hole-blocking layer of compact TiO  $  _{2}  $   film was deposited onto the treated FTO by spin-coating using a precursor solution of 0.15 M titanium diisopropoxide bis (acetylacetonate) in 1-butanol solution followed by heating at 125 °C for 10 min. After the spin-coating, the substrates were annealed at 450 °C for 30 min. Then, a mesoporous TiO  $  _{2}  $   layer was deposited by spin coating for 20 s at 4000 rpm with a ramp of 2000 rpm s  $  ^{-1}  $  , using a 30 nm particle paste (Dyesol 30 NR-D) diluted in 1-butanol to achieve 150-200 nm thick layer. After the spin coating, the substrates were dried at 100 °C for 10 min and then sintered at 550 °C for 30 min under dry air flow. Finally, the substrates coated with TiO  $  _{2}  $   film was further treated with 20 mM aqueous TiCl  $  _{4}  $   solution at 70 °C for 10 min and then annealed again at 500 °C for 30 min. The triple-cation-based perovskite precursor solution was prepared

according to the Ref [37], which was deposited from a precursor solution containing FAI (1 M),  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  (1.1 M), MABr (0.2 M) and  $ \mathrm{PbBr}_2 $  (0.2 M) in anhydrous DMF: DMSO 4:1 (v : v). Then CsI, pre-dissolved as a 1.5 M stock solution in DMSO, was added to the mixed perovskite precursor to achieve the  $ \mathrm{Cs_{0.05}(MA_{0.17}FA_{0.83})_{0.95}Pb(I_{0.83}Br_{0.17})_3} $  triple cation composition. For KSCN adding, 100 mM of KSCN stock solution in DMF was prepared by dissolving 0.097 g of KSCN in 10 mL of DMF. Different amount of the stock solution was mixed with the perovskite precursor solution for the case of 0 ~ 18 mM KSCN. The total DMF volume was set a constant. The solutions were filtered with 0.45 μm pore sized filter. The filtered solutions were spin-coated on the TiO₂ film at 1000 for 10s and 6000 rpm for 20 s respectively. During the second step, 100 μL of chlorobenzene was poured on the spinning substrate 5 s prior to the end of the program. The transparent brownish adduct film was formed right after deposition, which was heated at 100 °C for 60 min. After the perovskite annealing, the 20 uL of spiro-MeOTAD solution, containing of 86.2 mg spiro-MeOTAD (Merck), 35.2 μL of 4-tert-butyl pyridine and 16.7 μL of lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) solution (600 mg LiTSFI in 1 mL acetonitrile (99.8%, Sigma Aldrich) in 1 mL of chlorobenzen), was spin-coated on the perovskite layer at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, 70-80 nm of gold top electrode was thermally evaporated under high vacuum.

Thiocyanic (SCN) is a kind of pseudohalide, which has a similar chemical property as halide. Moreover, the evaporation of   $  SCN^{-}  $  also could increase the crystallinity during the annealing process  $  ^{[41]}  $  .

To investigate the effect of KSCN additive on the light absorption, and the mechanism of photogenerated carriers transfer in the perovskite films, we measured UV-vis absorption, photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) of perovskite films prepared under the same conditions except the different concentration of KSCN additives in perovskite precursor solution. The eliminated hysteresis could also be attribute to the higher crystalline quality and enlarged perovskite crystal size, resulting in the reduced the overall bulk defect density and the suppressed charge trapping during solar cell operation. # 4. Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated an innovative approach by introducing a certain amount of KSCN additive to enhance the performance of perovskite solar cells. The effects of KSCN additives with different concentration on perovskite film properties and photovoltaic performance were investigated. Perovskite films fabricated using the KSCN additive possess high crystallinity, large grain size, strong light absorption capacity, less defects and grain boundaries, leading to outstanding photovoltaic performance of device. With the addition of  $ 12\mathrm{mM} $  additive KSCN, the device achieved a photoelectric conversion efficiency of over  $ 19.23\% $  under the reverse scan conditions. At the same time, the forward scanning photoelectric conversion efficiency has reached  $ 18.82\% $ . The hysteresis effect has been effectively
