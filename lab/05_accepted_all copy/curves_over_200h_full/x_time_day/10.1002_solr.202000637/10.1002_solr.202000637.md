# Abstract

Realizing the rapid/controllable oxidation of Spiro-OMeTAD under an inert atmosphere, reducing the  $ J-V $  hysteresis and enhancing the air stability of devices are extremely significant to fabricate high-performance perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, the Fe(III) ion grafted metal-organic complexes  $ (\mathrm{Fe(III)}\subset \mathrm{MOCs}) $  are assembled and employed as the p-type dopants of hole transport layers (HTLs) to prepare efficient and stable PSCs. Consequently, the optimal  $ \mathrm{Fe(III)}\subset \mathrm{In}-2 $ -bpydc doped device presents a significantly enhanced power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 20.46\% $ , benefitting from the improved hole extraction and weakened carrier recombination at the interface between HTLs and perovskite films. More importantly, the modified device possesses a reduced  $ J-V $  hysteresis index (HI) of 0.094, and can maintain nearly  $ 90\% $  of its initial PCE value after being exposed to the air at  $ \sim 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and relative humidity (RH) of  $ \sim 35\% $  for 4 weeks, which is attributed to the restrained detrimental penetration behavior by the MOC carrier part of dopants. This work is of important guiding significance for the application of MOC materials in photovoltaic fields.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) emerged as a burgeoning photovoltaic technology, and achieved numerous significant breakthroughs in the device fabrication, composition modulation and interface optimization.  $  ^{[1]}  $   As a consequence, the optimal certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organo-inorganic lead halide PSCs has reached 25.2%, showing a great application potential.  $  ^{[2]}  $   Generally, the typical planar PSCs possess a stratified structure with the perovskite light-harvesting layers sandwiched by the electron transport layers (ETLs) and hole transport layers (HTLs). Besides a high-quality perovskite film, suitable charge transport layers with excellent carrier conductivity and matching energy level are also essential roles, which determine the photovoltaic parameters of resulting devices, such as the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ), short-circuit photocurrent density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ), fill factor (FF) as well as PCE value.  $  ^{[3]}  $   In most cases, the state-of-the-art organic hole transport material (HTM) 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) was employed in fabricating the high-efficiency PSCs for its excellent solution processability and high glassy transition temperature. Nevertheless, the Spiro-OMeTAD molecule suffers from a low conductivity in its pristine form, thereby generally requiring 4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP) and bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI) as the standard bi-dopants to induce the formation of oxidized Spiro-OMeTAD (Spiro-OMeTAD  $  ^{+}  $  ) and enhance the hole transport performance. In this regard, sufficient oxygen (  $  O_{2}  $  ) content and long oxidation process are both indispensable to realize adequate Spiro-OMeTAD  $  ^{+}  $ , which is bound to increase the time cost and simultaneously damage the controllability and reproducibility of photovoltaic performance. To address this problem, a series of low-cost, efficient and solution-processable ionic compounds, such as ferric chloride (  $  FeCl_{3}  $  ),  $  ^{[4]}  $   cuprous thiocyanate (CuSCN),  $  ^{[5]}  $   cuprous iodide (CuI),  $  ^{[6]}  $   stannic chloride (  $  SnCl_{4}  $  )  $  ^{[7]}  $   and silver bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (AgTFSI),  $  ^{[8]}  $   have been utilized as the p-type dopants of HTLs for realizing the rapid and controllable oxidation of Spiro-OMeTAD under an inert condition. However, these unrestrained ions might permeate along with the Au atoms

In recent years, metal-organic complexes (MOCs) with tunable binding sites and stable molecular structure attracted growing attentions as a kind of potential carrier material for assembling excellent composites, through a post-synthetic modification (PSM) treatment with various functional species. Specially, metal functionalized MOCs (M⊂MOCs) have exhibited extraordinary performance in the fields of adsorption, $ ^{[10]} $  catalysis, $ ^{[11]} $  fluorescence sensing $ ^{[12]} $  and proton conduction. $ ^{[13]} $  For instances, Zhang et al. developed two Pd- or Ag-nanoparticles (NPs) functionalized MOCs through cooperative ion-exchange and self-redox, and the Pd-NPs loaded composite presented an excellent heterogeneous catalytic activity for the hydrogenation of styrene. $ ^{[14]} $  Vaidhyanathan et al. assembled a Cs $ ^{+} $  ion functionalized MOC material by post-synthetic partial ion-exchange, and its proton conductivity was ten thousand times higher than that of the pristine MOC, showing great potential as a solid electrolyte for fuel-cell application. $ ^{[15]} $  However, the M⊂MOC materials have never been taken into consideration for the photovoltaic devices. Given that the robust interaction between the functional species and MOC carriers can effectively restrain the detrimental penetration behavior, we speculate metal ion functionalized MOCs would be an excellent candidate as the p-type dopants in Spiro-OMeTAD based HTLs. Moreover, the MOC carrier parts are believed to provide additional steric hindrance to weaken the erosion of perovskite films by Au atoms, Li $ ^{+} $  ions and H $ _{2} $ O molecules, significantly enhancing the long-term stability of devices. Of course, some Cu(II)-based MOCs have been directly used as the p-type dopants. $ ^{[16]} $  Nevertheless, the Cu(II) ions present relatively weak oxidizing ability and the stability of Cu(II)-based MOCs is generally not as good as that of MOCs constructed by the high-valence central metal ions. Distinctly, employing M⊂MOCs, prepared by metal ions with strong oxidizing ability and stable MOC carriers, as the p-type dopants

Herein, the 2,2'-bipyridine-6,6'-dicarboxylic acid (H₂bpydc) containing multiple binding sites was selected as the organic ligand to construct two new stable In(III)-based MOCs [In(bpydc)(H₂O)₂Cl (In-1-bpydc) and (Me₂NH₂)[In(bpydc)₂] (In-2-bpydc), respectively] by adjusting the reaction sources. The exposed carboxyl oxygen atoms made both of them promising carriers to prepare Fe(III)⊂MOC materials [Fe(III)⊂In-1-bpydc and Fe(III)⊂In-2-bpydc, respectively] through a facile PSM treatment (Scheme 1). The ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) absorption spectra confirmed the effectiveness of both Fe(III)⊂In-1-bpydc and Fe(III)⊂In-2-bpydc in the rapid and controllable oxidation of Spiro-OMeTAD under an inert atmosphere. In view of the more Spiro-OMeTAD⁺ contribution than that of Fe(III)⊂In-1-bpydc at the same dosage, the representative Fe(III)⊂In-2-bpydc was introduced into the HTL films as a p-type dopant for systematically investigating its positive roles in complete n-i-p PSCs. The results showed that the freshly fabricated PSCs containing Fe(III)⊂In-2-bpydc realized an optimal average PCE value with low performance fluctuation (19.24 ± 1.22%) at the doping concentration of 1.5 mg mL⁻¹, which was significantly superior to that of the O₂ treated reference devices without Fe(III)⊂In-2-bpydc (17.70 ± 1.81%). More importantly, the best Fe(III)⊂In-2-bpydc modified PSC possessed a reduced J-V hysteresis index (HI) of 0.094, and could maintain 87.6% of its initial PCE after 4 weeks of exposure to air without encapsulation at ~25 °C and relative humidity (RH) of ~35%, in contrast to the high HI (0.153) and rapid efficiency decay down to 60.2% of reference device. This work provides a new horizon to design and develop high-performance p-type dopants for commercial prospective PSCs, and points out the potential application of MOC materials in photovoltaic fields.

The UV-vis absorption spectra of pristine HTM solution (HTM-SLT for Spiro-OMeTAD/Li-TFSI/t-BP) and Fe(III)⊂MOC modified HTM solutions (HTM-SLTFel for Spiro-OMeTAD/Li-TFSI/t-BP/Fe(III)⊂In-1-bpydc and HTM-SLTFel2 for Spiro-OMeTAD/Li-TFSI/t-BP/Fe(III)⊂In-2-bpydc, respectively) were performed. # 2.4 J-V Hysteresis and Air Stability of the Fe(III)⊂In-2-bpydc Modified PSCs

In addition to a superior PCE value, the low  $ J-V $  hysteresis and high air stability are also important standards for the high-performance PSC devices. Hence, the  $ J-V $  curves in forward/reverse scanning directions and the time-dependent PCE decay curves of PSCs with or without the  $ \mathrm{Fe(III)}\subset \mathrm{In}-2 $ -bpydc doping were recorded. As shown in Figure 5a, the  $ \mathrm{Fe(III)}\subset \mathrm{In}-2 $ -bpydc (1.5 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ ) modified PSC presents a lower  $ J-V $  hysteresis index (HI) of 0.094 than that of the reference device (0.153), according to the formula  $ \mathrm{HI} = [J_{\mathrm{RS}}(0.8V_{\mathrm{oc}}) - J_{\mathrm{FS}}(0.8V_{\mathrm{oc}})] / J_{\mathrm{RS}}(0.8V_{\mathrm{oc}}) $ , where  $ J_{\mathrm{RS}}(0.8V_{\mathrm{oc}}) $  and  $ J_{\mathrm{FS}}(0.8V_{\mathrm{oc}}) $  represent the photocurrent density at 80% of  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  for the reverse scan and forward scan, respectively. This should be attributed to that the introduction of  $ \mathrm{Fe(III)}\subset \mathrm{In}-2 $ -bpydc not only effectively fixed the Fe(III) ions via the robust coordination interaction, but also provided additional steric hindrance to limit the penetration of Au atoms,  $ \mathrm{Li^{+}} $ ions through HTLs into the perovskite films. In this regard, the SEM-EDS line scan was carried out for the reference PSC and  $ \mathrm{Fe(III)}\subset \mathrm{In}-2 $ -bpydc (1.5 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ ) modified device, as shown in Figure S9. The accelerated attenuation of Au signal for the  $ \mathrm{Fe(III)}\subset \mathrm{In}-2 $ -bpydc (1.5 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ ) modified device has proved the barrier effect of  $ \mathrm{Fe(III)}\subset \mathrm{In}-2 $ -bpydc on the Au atom permeation. Although the Li signal cannot be collected, we speculate that the permeation of  $ \mathrm{Li^{+}} $ ions could also be suppressed, owing to the electro-positivity of  $ \mathrm{Li^{+}} $ ions and the abundant pyridine ring  $ \pi $  electron clouds of  $ \mathrm{Fe(III)}\subset \mathrm{In}-2 $ -bpydc. Moreover, the atomic force microscopy (AFM) and water contact

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, two Fe(III) ion grafted MOCs (Fe(III)⊂In-1-bpydc and Fe(III)⊂In-2-bpydc, respectively) were designed and developed as p-type dopants of HTLs to improve the efficiency and air stability of PSCs. The higher Fe(III) ion capacity allows Fe(III)⊂In-2-bpydc to rapidly and controllably contribute more Spiro-OMeTAD $  ^{+}  $  under an inert atmosphere than that of Fe(III)⊂In-1-bpydc at the same dosage. In consequence, the positive roles of Fe(III)⊂In-2-bpydc in realizing high-performance PSCs and corresponding mechanisms were systematically investigated. The

Therefore, the optimal PSCs modified by Fe(III)⊂In-2-bpydc with a doping concentration of 1.5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  presented significantly higher PCE value, lower J-V hysteresis and better air stability than that of the reference devices. Moreover, the overall performance of Fe(III)⊂In-2-bpydc doped devices is also outweigh that of the devices employing ionic dopants or Cu(II)-based MOC dopants. Undoubtedly, it is a facile and effective strategy to fabricate high-efficiency and long-term stable PSCs by employing M⊂MOCs as the p-type dopants of HTLs.
