# 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  Back Surface Field for Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Haobo Yuan, Jing Zhang,* Luting Yu, Tonghui Guo, Zequn Zhang, Yanyan Wang, Minghui Shang, Xiaohui Liu, Ziyang Hu, Yuejin Zhu,* and Liyuan Han*

In perovskite solar cells (PSCs), a defective perovskite (PVK) surface and cliff-like energy offset at the interface always slow down the charge extraction; meanwhile, interface ion diffusion causes oxidation of the metal electrode, inducing device instability. Here, the in situ grown 2D-(CH $ _3 $ NH $ _2 $ ) $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  (MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $ ) on the back surface of MAPbI $ _3 $  results in a more robust interface. MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  changes the MAPbI $ _3 $  surface to p-type and thus acts like a back surface field to drive charge extraction and suppress recombination, resulting in an obviously higher fill factor (FF) = 0.8 and power conversion efficiency (PCE) = 20.4% of SnO $ _2 $ /MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $ /Spiro-OMeTAD (2,2',7,7'-Tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spirobifluorene) PSC than the pure MAPbI $ _3 $  device. More importantly, strong chemical bonding of Sb-I prohibits ion diffusion, largely enhancing the thermal stability and longtime stability. Here, special 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  constructs' robust band alignment and chemical environment at the interface are highlighted for efficient and stable PSCs.

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are promising photovoltaic techniques for next-generation energy conversion devices.  $  ^{[1-4]}  $   Undesirably, surface dangling bonds as defect states and large interface energy cliff caused by energy offsets are always presented at the interfaces in planar structure, causing charge recombination and inefficient carrier extraction. Moreover, perovskite (PVK) surface with defects and soft chemical bonding induces inferior chemical environment, causing degradation of PVK by water/oxygen invasion and the oxidation of metal electrodes by I  $  ^{-}  $   released by PVK decomposition.  $  ^{[5,6]}  $   Outstanding improvements in PSC performance and stability can

Technical engineering of this interface is of great urgency. Energy level alignment strategies modulate the work function of the interface, promoting the charge flow from perovskite to charge transport layer.  $  ^{[5,10,13,20]}  $   The graded interface structures, either dimensionally graded or compositional graded, cause gradient interface energy levels favoring self-driven charge separation and reducing the recombination at the interface.  $  ^{[12,13,21-23]}  $   Prolonged device stability is also reported for PSCs with graded interface structures because of suppressed ionic movement or moisture-resistant nature of the interface.  $  ^{[5,6,22]}  $

In fact, the graded interface structure can be traced back to the back surface field (BSF) technique in silicon heterojunction solar cells, where the rear contact charge recombination is serious.  $  ^{[24-27]}  $   In n-p silicon solar cell, a   $  p^{+}  $  -doped silicon thin film is deposited at the back of p-Si as BSF, occurring the band bending to promote majority charge output and repel minority charge transport from the rear side of n-p-  $  p^{+}  $  wafer.

After thermal annealing MAPbI $ _3 $  at 85 °C on SnO $ _2 $  compact layer, SbI $ _3 $  is modified on MAPbI $ _3 $  surface and further heated at 95 °C for 10 min. By X-ray diffraction (XRD), photoluminescence (PL), and grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) characterizations, a new layer of 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  is formed on MAPbI $ _3 $  surface. In XRD plots (Figure 1b), a small diffraction peak at 9.3° emerges in the modified sample (indicated by a red star), which is assigned to (001) crystal plane as compared to the reported XRD of layered structure MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $ .[28,32,33] To clarify that this simple coating method can successfully obtain 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $ , we also check the XRD of two-step-deposited MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  by spin-coating SbI $ _3 $  on both thin and thick MAI films. As shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information), the main peaks at ≈17°, 25.2°, and 29.5° are (002), (003), and (022) planes of 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $ , respectively.[33,34] The two-step fabrication verifies that 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  can be grown on MAPbI $ _3 $  by SbI $ _3 $  reacting with the MA group on MAPbI $ _3 $  surface. In our density functional theory (DFT) calculation, it is noticed that a high-quality and intimate MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  interface can only be constructed by stacking (101) MAPbI $ _3 $  and (001) 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  slabs together, with a lattice mismatch of <5% (Figure S2, Supporting Information). Therefore, it is deduced that (001) 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  is selectively grown on MAPbI $ _3 $  surface to be a stable structure.

As previously reported, though the 0D phase is more preferred in the one-step growing process, the 2D structure with direct bandgap and narrower bandgap is more suitable for photoelectric devices.  $  ^{[35]}  $   To further confirm the 2D phase in our

work, we explore the   $  MA_{3}Sb_{2}I_{9}  $   with the one-step method  $  ^{[35]}  $   and our two-step method. The photos of real products were provided in Figure S3 (Supporting Information). The film of   $  MA_{3}Sb_{2}I_{9}  $   with 0D phase was obtained by the one-step method, showing a yellow color, which is confirmed with previous work.  $  ^{[35]}  $   However, interestingly, the red film was received by our two-step method, and the redshifted color represents narrower optical bandgap. Combining that with the XRD results, we deem that there is the 2D-layered structure   $  MA_{3}Sb_{2}I_{9}  $ , which has been received by our two-step process. Further, PL was employed to confirm the phase structure of   $  MA_{3}Sb_{2}I_{9}  $   in 2D/3D heterojunction (Figure 1c). The PL measured when exciting from the top side reveals a weak emission around 644 nm, corresponding with the 2D structure   $  MA_{3}Sb_{2}I_{9}  $   with the optical bandgap of 1.925 eV.  $  ^{[36]}  $

The GIWAXS analysis is further performed to examine the 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  phase on MAPbI $ _3 $ . The GIWAXS pattern in Figure S4a (Supporting Information) does not exhibit a clear information for 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  at low scattering vector  $ q < 8 \, \text{nm}^{-1} $  as the rectangle indicated. However, the azimuthally integrated scattering intensity plot in Figure S4b (Supporting Information) shows a small diffraction peak at around  $ 9^\circ $ , which is consistent with 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  diffraction peak in XRD (Figure 1a). GIWAXS results prove the 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  phase on MAPbI $ _3 $  and there is no special orientation of 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $ .

After the confirmation of 2D-MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  on MAPbI $ _3 $  surface, the n-i-p planar PSCs with SnO $ _2 $ /PVK/Spiro-OMeTAD structure are tested under 1 sun AM1.5 to explore the performance. As the  $ J-V $  curves shown in Figure 2a, MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  device achieves PCE = 20.4%, short-circuit current density ( $ J_{sc} $ ) = 23.6 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , open-circuit voltage (V $ _{oc} $ ) = 1.08 V, and fill factor (FF) = 0.80, much higher than the pure MAPbI $ _3 $  device with PCE = 17.7%,  $ J_{sc} $  = 22.8 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , V $ _{oc} $  = 1.06 V, and FF = 0.73. Statistics of device parameters for MAPbI $ _3 $  and MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  solar cells are presented in Figure S7 (Supporting Information), indicating good reproducibility of our devices. The obvious increment of FF to 0.8 and reduced hysteresis of MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  device (Figure S8, Supporting Information) indicate that its charge transport property is much better than the MAPbI $ _3 $  PSC. The thickness of the modified layer is also optimized. In Figure S9a (Supporting Information), the PSC with

higher amount of   $  SbI_{3}  $   solution modification shows reduced   $  J_{sc}  $  , ascribing to the poor PVK film quality exhibited in Figure S9b (Supporting Information).

The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra shown in Figure 2b indicate the higher EQE in MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  device. The integrated  $ J_{\text{sc}} $  of the solar cells is consistent with that in  $ J-V $  curves. Interestingly, it is observed that the EQE of MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  device is distinctly higher at the long-wavelength region, which is marked by a red oval. UV–visible spectra of PVK films in Figure S11 (Supporting Information) show that the absorbance of MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  is almost the same with MAPbI $ _3 $  film, indicating that MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  has negligible optical influence on MAPbI $ _3 $  film. Therefore, the enhanced EQE in MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  device corresponds to higher charge collection efficiency compared with the MAPbI $ _3 $  device. The stronger EQE at long-wavelength region suggests that the charge collection is especially good for low-energy carriers or near the back side of PVK, where the EQE is sensitive to long-wavelength light. $ ^{[25,38,39]} $  The EQE thus proves that MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  back layer greatly benefits for collecting the charges diffused to or generated at MAPbI $ _3 $  back surface and transferring to the HTM.

Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) is used to study the energy level of the PVKs. From the UPS measurement (Figure 3a), the work function (WF) of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  surface is enlarged to  $ 5.07\mathrm{eV} $ , comparing with  $ 4.92\mathrm{eV} $  of pure  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ ; besides, the energy difference between VBM and Fermi energy level  $ (E_{\mathrm{V}} - E_{\mathrm{F}}) $  in Figure 3a is reduced to  $ 0.7\mathrm{eV} $  compared to  $ 0.85\mathrm{eV} $  of the control sample. The downshift of Fermi energy level to VBM indicates that  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  surface become more p-type. The result of the UPS is in accordance with the Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) measurement in Figure 3b. The reduced surface potential value of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  compared with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  indicates the increased WF of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  surface, and thus the Fermi level is going deeper to VBM by  $ \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  modification.

Considering the bandgaps of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  (1.59 eV) and  $ \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  (1.92 eV), a corresponding energy level diagram under dark is plotted in Figure 3c. The  $ \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  interlayer caused a slight upward bending of the surface energy, which is beneficial for hole extraction under light. Meanwhile, the large energy offset of conduction band minimum (CBM) at  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  will repel electrons from the back surface, preventing bimolecular recombination at the interface (Figure S12, Supporting Information). To clarify the influence of changed interface energy structure on charge extraction ability of PVK/HTM, we further performed the steady-state PL and time-resolved PL (TRPL) based on the FTO/PVK/Spiro-OMeTAD structure. The PL intensity of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  -based sample is reduced significantly compared with the control sample in Figure 4a. It is ascribed to more efficient extraction of photogenerated holes in the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  sample, which reduce the radiative recombination. The TRPL in Figure 4b shows the same trend with steady-state PL. By fitting the decay by biexponential rate law, we

obtain a faster decay time of 5.6 ns for MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $ , compared with 16.2 ns for the pure MAPbI $ _3 $  sample. Comparing the fast and the slow decay components indicated in Figure 4b, it is clear that MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  shows a much shorter fast decay time  $ \tau_1 = 1.1 $  ns than 3.5 ns of the MAPbI $ _3 $  sample, proving more efficient charge transfer between MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  and Spiro-OMeTAD, than that in MAPbI $ _3 $ /Spiro-OMeTAD. The efficient charge transfer is ascribed to more p-type of MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  BSF, efficiently promoting the hole extraction. Besides, the slow decay time  $ \tau_2 $  for the MAPbI $ _3 $ /MA $ _3 $ Sb $ _2 $ I $ _9 $  sample is 42 ns, slightly longer than 37 ns in the MAPbI $ _3 $  sample. PSC, which means that the charge recombination is effectively suppressed in   $  MAPbI_{3}/MA_{3}Sb_{2}I_{9}  $   PSC. We further investigate the charge transfer in PSCs by electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) under dark and bias of 1 V. In fitted Nyquist plots (Figure S15, Supporting Information), the recombination resistance   $  R_{rec}  $   (fitted date in Table S2 of the Supporting Information) increases from   $  1.5 \times 10^{4} \Omega  $   in the control device to   $  2.4 \times 10^{4} \Omega  $   of   $  MAPbI_{3}/MA_{3}Sb_{2}I_{9}  $  -based PSCs, indicating that less carrier recombination occurs. From the above charge dynamics characterizations, the   $  MAPbI_{3}/MA_{3}Sb_{2}I_{9}  $   PSC exhibits enhanced charge transport and diminished carrier recombination, leading to a high FF of 0.8 and larger   $  V_{oc}  $   as Figure 2a presented.

The stability of the solar cell devices is examined. The 2D/3D heterojunction film has demonstrated superior stability than the control sample in the air of 60% relative humidity (RH) (Figure S16, Supporting Information). After 1 month storage in air at 25 °C and a relative humidity of 20%, it is obvious that a part of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  degrades to yellow PbI  $  _{2}  $  and Ag electrode suffers serious erosion in pure MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ -based PSC as indicated in Figure 5a. By contrast, the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ /MA  $  _{3}  $ Sb  $  _{2}  $ I  $  _{9}  $ -based PSC keeps the mirror-like Ag electrode, indicating reduced degradation in this device.

It is reported that oxidation of the Ag electrode is due to the degradation of PVK, which releases component   $  I^{-}  $  to permeate through HTM and react with Ag electrode.  $  ^{[42,43]}  $   A soft crystal lattice of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   with weak bonding nature is the root cause of instability.  $  ^{[6,16]}  $   Easy degradation of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   by external   $  H_{2}O/O_{2}  $   results in yellow   $  PbI_{2}  $   and release of MAI. Moreover, the movable nature of   $  I^{-}  $  makes the Ag oxidation even worse. According to the reduced ionic movement and less degradation of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  under moist and high-temperature environment, it is deduced that  $ \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  induces a more robust chemical interface, which prohibits the PVK degradation and releasing  $ \mathrm{I}^- $  to Ag electrode. In fact, it is observed in the XPS measurement that binding energies of Pb 4f and I 3d (Figure 5f) move to lower energy after  $ \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  modification. The changed bonding energies indicate that the new layer influences the chemical environment. First-principles simulation is performed to examine the interface bonding strength in the presence of  $ \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $ . A high-quality and intimate  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/ $   $ \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  heterostructure interface is constructed by stacking the  $ \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  slab on  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  with a lattice mismatch of  $ < 5\% $ . The relaxed configuration of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm{I}_9 $  heterostructure is shown in Figure 5g, where the Pb-I at the interface (noted by a rectangle frame) is stretched compared with the bulk counterparts. The averaged bond length of Pb-I increases by  $ 0.34\AA $  compared with the bulk phase. It implies that Pb-I is weakened by the Sb-I, consisting with XPS results. It is known that the Sb provides more valence electrons and its electronegativity is weaker than Pb. Thus, the chemical interaction of Sb-I is stronger than Pb-I, which weakens the Pb-I interaction at the interface. We also notice that the average bonding length of Sb-I is much shorter than Pb-I in Figure 5g, again proving more compact and stronger bonding of Sb-I. The stronger Sb-I binding induces a more robust interface to reduce I-movement and resist  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} / \mathrm{O}_2 $  attack on soft  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ , which largely improves the stability of the device.

In summary, we employ a novel   $  MAPbI_{3}/2D-MA_{3}Sb_{2}I_{9}  $   structure by in situ growing   $  MA_{3}Sb_{2}I_{9}  $   on   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   surface. It exhibits better energy alignment and strong chemical bonding at PSC interface, greatly enhancing the performance and stability of PSCs. The   $  MA_{3}Sb_{2}I_{9}  $   shifts down the surface Fermi energy and change   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   surface to be more p-type, thus constructing graded energy structure to drive charge transport at the interface. The interface energy structure is also proved by DFT calculation. The performance of   $  MAPbI_{3}/MA_{3}Sb_{2}I_{9}  $   PSC is enhanced to 20.4% with high FF = 0.8,   $  J_{sc} = 23.6  $   mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and   $  V_{oc} = 1.08  $   V, much better than 17.7% of the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   PSC. What is more, the ion diffusion from PVK to Ag is largely suppressed, which is also proved by KPFM measurements. The experiment and DFT simulation both suggest a stronger chemical bonding of Sb–I than Pb-I, which prohibiting I  $  ^{-}  $  diffusion and benefiting for the longtime stability under the moist and high-temperature environment. This work offers a promising interface method for efficient and stable PSCs.
