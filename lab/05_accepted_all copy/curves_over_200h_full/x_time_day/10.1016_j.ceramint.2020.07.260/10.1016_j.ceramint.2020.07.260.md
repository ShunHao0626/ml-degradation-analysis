# Abstract

Carbon nanotubes (CNT) are unique one-dimensional structures that have been widely used for enhancing optoelectronic devices because of their fascinating properties. In this work, the impact of multi-walled CNT (MWCNT) on photovoltaic (PV) performance of mesoporous-titanium dioxide  $ (\mathrm{m - TiO_2}) $  based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is investigated. First, the incorporation of 1-Butyl-3-methylimidazolium iodide (BMImI) within the lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2) $  precursor caused suppressing  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  residue from perovskite (PVK) film as proved via X-ray diffractometer (XRD). The loading of MWCNT in the  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  electron transfer layer (ETL) led to the formation of PVK with bigger grains and higher crystallinity with improved light absorbance. The power-conversion efficiency (PCE) of the PSC was remarkably boosted to  $ 21.4\% $  for the device containing  $ 0.2\% $  MWCNT versus  $ 12.5\% $  for the control device without any additives. MWCNT loaded in  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  provides a fast charge transfer within the ETL, resulting in an improving in the short circuit current (Jsc) value. This can be assigned to an enhanced band level alignment of the  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  after the incorporation of MWCNT. Moreover, it is found that the loading of MWCNT into  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  ETL decreases the hysteresis phenomenon and enhances the heat and humidity stability of the PSCs.

Keywords: Perovskite solar cells; Carbon nanotubes; Lead iodide; Electron transfer layer; BMImI

# 1. Introduction

Since 2009, organo-lead trihalide PVK-based light harvesters have received a considerable deal of awareness from the scientific society for its outstanding PV characteristics like high charge mobility (25 cm $  ^{2}  $ /V.s), high absorption coefficient (>10 $  ^{4}  $ cm $  ^{-1}  $ ), ambipolar transfer capability, long carrier diffusion length (>1 μm), and the ability of adjusting the bandgap energies (1.2 to 2.2 eV) [1-3]. Based on these unique PV features, PSCs have accomplished considerable progress in the sustainable and renewable energy field. To date, substantial and rapid advances in the PSC performance with efficiencies greater than 25% have been recorded in the last decade [4]. The major concentration of this study is to fulfill PCEs and stability closer to the commercially obtainable photovoltaics to compete and substitute the current silicon panels with cheaper PSCs [5,6].

A standard PSC that demonstrates superior cell performance comprises of a transparent conducting electrode such as fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO), compact layer, ETL, PVK photo harvester, hole transfer layer (HTL), and counter electrode (CE) such as noble metals. In such cell arrangement, the ETL semiconductor material plays a vital role in collecting and transferring photo-induced electrons from the PVK film to the CE and gives mechanical support and scaffold for the PVK material [7,8]. The highly efficient ETL is the fundamental for high-performance solar cells. Among all ETLs, titanium dioxide is widely employed as the ETL for PSCs due to its appropriate band-level alignment, high transparency (wide bandgap), eco-friendliness, good chemical stability, and ease of deposition by various coating methods [9]. This material is able of extracting the photo-induced electrons to the electrode and hinders the holes simultaneously [10]. However, the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -based ETL suffers from several disadvantages involving low charge mobility, high grain boundaries, and point or

Journal Pre-proof

lattice defects such as oxygen vacancies, which insert a random transport route for the electrons causing fast carrier recombination, thus reducing PSC performance [11]. This seems to be common issues in PSCs based on mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL. To solve these problems, the preparing of defects-freem- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  is significant in enhancing the electron injection, suppress charge recombination, and in turn to improve PSC performance [12].

There have been many reports of engineering the properties of m-  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  throughincorporating with appropriate additives such as  $ \mathrm{SiO_2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Li^{+}} $  ion, Al, Zn, Fe, F, N, and Mg for passivating the electronic trap states leading to enhanced charge transfer of PSCs [13-20]. Furthermore, the introducing of carbon-based nanostructures own high conductivity and charge mobility such as reducing graphene oxide (rGO), graphene quantum dots (GQDs), carbon nanodots (CnDs), and single-walled CNTs (SWCNTs) have also been proven to be an efficient approach to promote the electron collection and prolong the lifetime, thereby improving the PCE of PSCs [21-24]. However, the impact of CNT on the optoelectronic characteristics of PSCs by fabricating a  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /CNTs hybrid needs to be explained. There is a further problem with the morphology of PVK layer, which plays a vital role in PSC performance. The PVK morphological properties and crystal size can be modified using dopants or heat treatment [25]. Previously in the literature, several additives such as 4-tert-butylpyridine, 1,8-diiodooctane, tetra-n-butylammoniumtri iodide, and 1-chloronaphthalene have been used to improve the crystallization and quality of PVK film [26-29]. However, the long-period stability of the doped-PVK is still insufficient and needs further improvement. Hence, combining extremely conductive MWCNTs into  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  for employing in the modified-PVK solar cells is an

alternative method to provide fast charge transfer pathway to improve PSC efficiency and stability.

As far as we know, no study has investigated MWCNTs additive in the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL for modified-PSCs. This paper presents the incorporation of effective additive of BMIImI in the   $  PbI_{2}  $   precursor with higher stability in ambient conditions in addition to higher efficiency. An optimal amount of 10 mg/ml of BMIImI led to total conversion of methylammonium iodide (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  ) and   $  PbI_{2}  $   materials into   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   PVK, yielding PCE up to 17% with respect to the 12.5% for the unmodified PVK. Moreover, by using an optimum concentration of MWCNT (0.2%) in the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL, an important improvement in the PSC performance is realized compared to the reference device designed without MWCNT. Based on our experiments, we assign the PV boosting of PSCs achieved using MWCNT to the introduction of appropriate band alignment and suppressed electron and hole recombination because of the fast charge extraction by MWCNT/  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL. Interestingly, the device prepared with MWCNT/  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL revealed hysteresis-free and improved stability both under heat and during storage under moist conditions compared to the PVK device with pristine   $  TiO_{2}  $ . While prior research has demonstrated SWCNT/  $  TiO_{2}  $ , rGO/  $  TiO_{2}  $ , GQDs/  $  TiO_{2}  $ , and CnDs/  $  TiO_{2}  $ -based PSCs can fulfill efficiency values of 14.03%[21], 13.5%[22], 10.66%[23], and 19.45%[24], respectively, in this study, our maximum performing device yielded a PCE of 21.4%.

An aqueous TritonX-100 mixture with 2% amount was used to synthesize the stock solution of MWCNTs. MWCNTs (20 mg) were dissolved in the prepared Triton X-100 (20 ml) via an ultrasonic device for 40 minutes. A various amount (0.1%, 0.2%, 0.3%) of MWCNTs was employed for the preparing of   $  TiO_{2}/MWCNT  $   slurries by inserting a suitable volume of the MWCNT solution into 0.00035 mg of the diluted   $  TiO_{2}  $   mixture.

# 2.3. Device preparation

FTO-coated glass substrates were etched through 0.5 ml of hydrochloric acid (diluted in 20 ml distilled water) and Zn powder, followed by consecutive washing with a soap-water, Milli-Q water, and acetone under ultrasonication for 15 minutes each. Then, the cleaned substrates were dried with a flow of   $  N_{2}  $   gas and treated with oxygen plasma. A compact-  $  TiO_{2}(c-TiO_{2})  $   (0.16 M TAA in ethanol) was deposited at 5000 rpm for 30 seconds and baked at 250 °C for 15 minutes. Subsequently, the films of pristine m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   and/or   $  TiO_{2}/MWCNT  $   were spun at 5000 rpm for 35 seconds by utilizing a   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste dispersed in ethanol at 1:6 and sintered at 440 °C for 20 minutes. The photoelectrodes were immersed in 0.025 M titanium tetrachloride

aqueous solution at 60 °C for 30 minutes, then dried with   $  N_{2}  $   gas and again sintered at 440 °C for 30 minutes. For depositing PVK and modified-PVK films,   $  PbI_{2}  $   (0.457g) solutions without and with BMImI additive (5, 10, 15 mg/ml DMF) were obtained by stirring for 1 hour at 60 °C.   $  PbI_{2}  $   stocks were spun at 5000 rpm for 40 seconds and annealed at 80 °C for 25 minutes. After   $  PbI_{2}  $   coating, films were dipped into   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (10mg/ml) solution for 2 minutes and then spun PVK films at 5000 rpm for 15 seconds and then baked at 90 °C for 10 minutes. The HTL solution was deposited at 5000 rpm for 20 seconds. Finally, an 80 nm Au CE was thermally evaporated on the HTL.

# 3.3. Device measurements

To probe the optimal amount of the BMIImI additive in the   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer on the cell performance, four cells were fabricated based on the m-  $  TiO_{2}ETL  $   with various concentrations of BMIImI. The J-V plots of PSCs are demonstrated in Figure S3 and the corresponding PV parameters summarized in Table S1. The photovoltaic parameters of PSCs were improved after incorporating BMIImI additive in the   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer. As presented in Figure S3, 10 mg/ml was found as the optimal amount for BMIImI additive. The obtained Jsc, open circuit voltage (Voc), and fill factor (FF) values for the champion cell based on 10 mg/ml BMIImI content were 26.02 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , 0.84 V, and 70.8%, respectively, achieving a PCE of 17.47%. Hence, the concentration of 10 mg/ml was selected for further optimization of the PSCs to increase the device performance.

Hence, the reduced Jsc of PSC after loading  $ 0.3\% $  SWCNTs into the m- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL cannot be elucidated merely through slightly suppressed transmittance of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ . At concentrations higher than optimal, it is probably that the reduced PV parameters are due to the fact that the MWCNT give extra sites where charge recombination is possible. Munkhbayar and coworkers confirmed this hypothesis previously [21]. Therefore, in the m- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /MWCNT-based ETL, it is possible that the existence of excess concentrations of CNT will extend the lifetime of the charge carriers leading to promoted recombination processes in the ETL. This possibly demonstrates the suppressed Jsc and Voc values of the device after using high amounts of CNT into the m- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ . Siraj and coworkers proposed anther assumption [24]. They loaded CNT in the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and the performance of the device degraded beyond the optimal amount of CNT. They suggested this degradation to the decreased mobility of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ . Even though the incorporation of CnDs results in suppressing defect state density, electron mobility becomes lower than the PSC with low concentration of CnDs. This decreasing in mobility would reduce the charge transport from the light absorber PVK to the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , resulting in a degraded PCE.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, MWCNT was incorporated in the mesoporous-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ETL for high-performance BMImI-modified PSCs. The MWCNT additive improved the visible light absorbance of PVK film and contributed to substantial photoelectron generation. Moreover, the incorporation of MWCNT resulted in an enhancement in morphology of PVK layer by increasing the grain size and suppressing the grain boundaries. The loading of MWCNT and BMImI with optimized amounts resulted in significant improvements in the PV performance of PSCs, remarkable reductions in the J-V hysteresis of the cells, and increased stabilities when subjected to continuous

Journal Pre-proof

heat and humidity. The champion cell fabricated with 0.2% MWCNT/TiO  $  _{2}  $  ETL revealed a significant PCE of 21.4%, one of the highest PCEs ever recorded using conventional CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $  and m-TiO  $  _{2}  $  as the ETL. This significant performance of the device is especially assigned to enhanced electron conductivity and efficient charge injection because of the better energy level alignment at the ELT/PVK interface, confirmed by the PL, EIS and UPS measurements. While, the device without additive suffered from recombination issues, which restricted the PCE to 12.56%. We expect that this work will make a remarkable contribution in fabricating MWCNT-based ETL for highly effective solar cells.
