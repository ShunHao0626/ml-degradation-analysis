# Abstract

Non-obvious hysteresis and higher steady-state power conversion efficiency (PCE) were demonstrated by simply employing hematite  $ (\alpha -\mathrm{Fe}_2\mathrm{O}_3) $  as electron transporting layer (ETL) to replace the conventional titania  $ (\mathrm{TiO_2}) $  ETL in planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells. The achieved higher built-in potential across the perovskite layer for the devices using  $ \alpha -\mathrm{Fe}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  ETL led more efficient charge extraction/transport and less charge recombination than using  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL. As a consequence, a significant reduction in the charge accumulation at the perovskite/ $ \alpha -\mathrm{Fe}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  interface made the device much less sensitive to scanning rate and direction, i.e., lower hysteresis. Furthermore,  $ \alpha -\mathrm{Fe}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  based devices displayed good stability over 30 days of storage time with exposure to ambient air, owing to higher crystalline quality and uniform grain size for the perovskite films deposited on  $ \alpha -\mathrm{Fe}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  ETL than on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL.

Key words:   $  \alpha  $  -Fe  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $  ; electron transporting layer; planar structure; reduced hysteresis; solar cells.

# 1. Introduction

As the third-generation solar cells, the organometallic trihalide perovskite solar cells have advanced rapidly in only a few years after its invention, due to the low cost, high efficiency and straightforward architecture.  $  ^{1}  $   The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite cells has been increased from 9.7%  $  ^{2}  $   to 22.1%  $  ^{3}  $   with the usage of solid-state organic hole transport materials. To date, organometallic trihalide perovskites   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbX_{3}  $   (X is iodine or a mixture of iodine, chlorine and bromine) have been regarded as the most promising light absorption materials for solar energy conversion resulting from their wide absorption range,  $  ^{4}  $   high extinction coefficient,  $  ^{5}  $   ambipolar charge transport,  $  ^{6}  $   and long electron-hole diffusion length.  $  ^{7}  $   These advantages have led to the development of planar perovskite solar cells (a typical planar architecture of   $  SnO_{2}:F  $   (FTO)/compact   $  TiO_{2}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/spiro-OMeTAD/Au  $  ), in which the mesoscopic layer consisting of   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticles (or nanowires  $  ^{8}  $  ) has been replaced by a compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer only, working as an electron transporting layer (ETL) and hole blocking layer. However, the   $  TiO_{2}  $   based planar type device, though showing high PCE above 19%,  $  ^{9}  $   has shown two major demerits, i.e., mysterious hysteresis in current density-voltage (J-V) curves and poor durability of stable performance.  $  ^{10-11}  $   The hysteresis is observed to strongly depend on the device architecture, where the perovskite devices with planar structure suffer from more obvious hysteresis behavior than mesoporous structure devices.  $  ^{10}  $   A lower PCE can be observed when operating planar devices with a forward scan (from short circuit (SC) to forward bias (FB)) than the case of reverse scan (from FB to SC) in the J-V measurement.  $  ^{10,12}  $   The notorious hysteretic behavior has so far prevented a correct evaluation of their actual performance. hysteresis might be related to trapping/de-trapping of charge carriers, $ ^{13,14} $  changes in absorber or contact conductivity, $ ^{11} $  perovskite's ferroelectricity, $ ^{10,15-17} $  and ion migration. $ ^{18-20} $

ETL, a key component in high efficiency planar perovskite solar cells, needs have low charge transfer resistance and a matching energy level with the conduction band of perovskite absorbing layer to offer good electron extraction ability. However, planar perovskite solar cells with most widely used   $  TiO_{2}  $     $  ETL^{21-23}  $   suffer from serious hysteresis and inferior stability.  $  ^{10-12}  $   This hysteresis is mainly ascribed to both rich oxygen vacancies and electron traps on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface.  $  ^{24,25}  $   The hysteresis can be reduced through the passivation of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer with C60.  $  ^{14,26}  $   The passivation could minimize the trap states and improve electron transfer from   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   to   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL. On the other hand,   $  TiO_{2}  $   was replaced by phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) in the perovskite devices with an inverted architecture to eliminate the hysteresis performance. PCBM could extract electrons more efficiently, thereby leading to less charge accumulation at the   $  PCBM/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   interface than conventional   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL.  $  ^{27}  $   However, PCBM layer could be degraded through adsorption of oxygen or water in ambient air.  $  ^{28}  $   Although hematite (  $  \alpha-Fe_{2}O_{3}  $  ), the most stable iron oxide with n-type semiconducting properties under ambient conditions, has been used as anode materials for supercapacitors  $  ^{29}  $   as well as the photoanode of dye-sensitized solar cells to enhance the electron transfer rate,  $  ^{30}  $   its usage as an electron transporting layer to replace   $  TiO_{2}  $   has not been reported. Herein, we employed low cost   $  \alpha-Fe_{2}O_{3}  $   for the first time as the ETL material instead of   $  TiO_{2}  $   in the planar perovskite cells. The charge accumulation at the perovskite/  $  \alpha-Fe_{2}O_{3}  $   interface is significantly reduced and thus is prominently effective to reduce the hysteresis. # 2. Results and discussion

Figs. 1A and 1B present the top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of the   $  Fe_{2}O_{3}/FTO  $   and   $  TiO_{2}/FTO  $  , respectively. Both   $  TiO_{2}  $   and   $  Fe_{2}O_{3}  $   films exhibit continuous and compact surface. Four XRD diffraction peaks of   $  Fe_{2}O_{3}/FTO  $   centered at 24.33, 33.28, 35.68 and 54.45°, Fig. 1C, are assigned to (012), (104), (110) and (116) diffractions of   $  \alpha-Fe_{2}O_{3}  $  , respectively.  $  ^{31}  $   Fig. 1D displays the Raman spectra of   $  Fe_{2}O_{3}  $   films spun cast onto (a) FTO substrate and (b) glass slide, in which seven optical modes of even symmetry (  $  2A_{1g}+5E_{1g}  $  ) associated with   $  Fe_{2}O_{3}  $   as the hematite phase are observed for both samples. The top-view SEM images of the perovskite films on these two substrates exhibit crack-free coverage and flat surfaces, Figs. 1E&F. However, a closer inspection of the surface images suggests that more uniform and larger grain size can be obtained for perovskite film grown on   $  \alpha-Fe_{2}O_{3}  $   ETL than on   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL. It is known that ETL plays the role of seeds for the halide perovskite precursor crystallizing on substrate. To check the crystalline quality of the as synthesized perovskite, XRD characterization was conducted for the hybrid perovskite deposited on both   $  \alpha-Fe_{2}O_{3}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs, Fig. 1G. The perovskite shows the typical peaks of the tetragonal phase and no diffraction peaks arising from   $  PbI_{2}  $   are observed in both diffraction patterns. The diffraction peaks of the perovskite layer deposited on   $  \alpha-Fe_{2}O_{3}  $   ETL are much stronger than those of the perovskite layer grown on   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL, indicating that   $  \alpha-Fe_{2}O_{3}  $   favors crystallization and film formation of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  . This may be induced by the growth of the crystals along different planes, possibly allowing denser packing and better coverage.

The perovskite absorber layer is known to allow the photogenerated excitons to dissociate immediately into free electrons and holes when exposed to the sunlight,  $  ^{38,39}  $   and then drift and diffuse through the absorber and transport layers before being collected by the contact electrodes. Tao et al. have highlighted that the electron extraction strongly depends on the pre-polarization condition in the presence of a flat   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL, and further affects the device stability. Efficient charge extraction contributes to less sensitive performance to device polarization.  $  ^{12}  $   To obtain a deeper insight in the correlation between hysteresis behavior and charge extraction, the photoelectron spectra of   $  TiO_{2}  $   and   $  \alpha-Fe_{2}O_{3}  $   were characterized, Fig. S4. # 3. Conclusions

In summary, we have described a significantly enhanced steady-state PCE and less hysteresis performance of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  based planar-heterojunction perovskite solar cell with  $ \alpha\text{-Fe}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  thin film as ETL than that using conventional  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL. Benefiting from larger  $ V_{bi} $  across perovskite absorber layer,  $ \alpha\text{-Fe}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  based device provides a stronger driving force to facilitate the charge extraction/transport and suppress the carrier recombination compared to the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  based device, as witnessed by the analysis results of steady-state PL, TRPL and EIS. A significant reduction in the charge accumulation at the perovskite/ $ \alpha\text{-Fe}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  interface makes the device much less sensitive to scanning rate and direction. Finally, the fabricated solar cells with  $ \alpha\text{-Fe}_2\mathrm{O}_3 $  ETL present a good stability in exposure to ambient air without any encapsulation, which is expected to promote the development of novel perovskite photovoltaic technology.

# 4. Experimental Section

# 4.1 Preparation of   $  \alpha  $  -Fe  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $   and TiO  $  _{2}  $   ETLs

Prior to the deposition of ETL, fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)-coated glass sheets were etched with zinc powder and HCl (2 M) to obtain the required electrode pattern. The etched FTO substrates were then sonicated sequentially with detergent, distilled water, ethanol, acetone and 2-propanol for 15 min, respectively. The dried substrates were finally treated with UV-ozone for 15 min. 0.1 M Fe(NO $ _3 $ ) $ _3 $ ·9H $ _2 $ O dissolved in ethanol was spun on the as-cleaned FTO glass at 6000 rpm for 40 s, followed by sintering at 500 °C for 1 h to form a thin α-Fe $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  compact layer. For comparison, a TiO $ _2 $  compact layer was also spin-coated on FTO glass at 2000 rpm for 40 s using the precursor solution consisting of 350 μl titanium isopropoxide dissolved in 5 mL ethanol, and then sintered at 500 °C for 0.5 h.

# 4.2 Device fabrication

Before  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  fabrication, methyl ammonium iodide ( $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $ ) was first synthesized. In short,  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  crystals can be obtained through the reaction of  $ 24~\mathrm{mL} $  methylamine and 10 mL hydroiodic acid in  $ 100~\mathrm{mL} $  round bottomed flask at  $ 0^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  under stirring for  $ 2\mathrm{h} $ . Then the precipitate was recovered by evaporation at  $ 80^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 2\mathrm{h} $ , subsequently washed with diethyl ether under stirring for  $ 30\mathrm{min} $ . After this was repeated twice, snow-white  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  crystals were successfully synthesized by drying at  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  in vacuum oven for  $ 24\mathrm{h} $ . A mixture of as-prepared  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  (0.200 g) and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  (0.578 g) was dissolved in 1 mL anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), followed by stirring at room temperature for  $ 20\mathrm{min} $  to produce a clear  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  solution with concentration of  $ 45\mathrm{wt}\% $ .  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite film was

The 180   $  \mu  $  L of anhydrous chlorobenzene was quickly dropped onto the surface during this process, leading to the color change of the film into dark brown. X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern data were recorded by high resolution X-ray diffraction (HRXRD) using a Bede D1 X-ray diffractometer.
