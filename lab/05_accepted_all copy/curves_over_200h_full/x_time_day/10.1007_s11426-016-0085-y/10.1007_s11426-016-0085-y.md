# Enhancement of the efficiency and stability of planar p-i-n perovskite solar cells via incorporation of an amine-modified fullerene derivative as a cathode buffer layer

Xiaodong Liu  $  ^{1}  $  , Peng Huang  $  ^{1}  $  , Qingqing Dong  $  ^{1}  $  , Zhaowei Wang  $  ^{1}  $  , Kaicheng Zhang  $  ^{1}  $  , Hao Yu  $  ^{1}  $  , Ming Lei  $  ^{2}  $  , Yi Zhou  $  ^{1*}  $  , Bo Song  $  ^{1*}  $   & Yongfang Li  $  ^{1,3*}  $

$  ^{1}  $  Laboratory of Advanced Optoelectronic Materials; College of Chemistry, Chemical Engineering and Materials Science, Soochow University, Suzhou 215123, China

$ ^{2} $ Department of Chemistry, Zhejiang University, Hangzhou 310027, China

$  ^{3}  $  Beijing National Laboratory for Molecular Sciences; Institute of Chemistry, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, China

Received February 23, 2016; accepted March 30, 2016

A methanol-soluble diamine-modified fullerene derivative (denoted as PCBDANI) was applied as an efficient cathode buffer layer (CBL) in planar p-i-n perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) based on the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  absorber. The device with PCBDANI single CBL exhibited significantly improved performance with a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 15.45\% $ , which is approximately  $ 17\% $  higher than that of the control device without the CBL. The dramatic improvement in PCE can be attributed to the formation of an interfacial dipole at the PCBM/Al interface originating from the amine functional group and the suppression of interfacial recombination by the PCBDANI interlayer. To further improve the PCE of pero-SCs, PCBDANI/LiF double CBLs were introduced between PCBM and the top Al electrode. An impressive PCE of  $ 15.71\% $  was achieved, which is somewhat higher than that of the devices with LiF or PCBDANI single CBL. Besides the PCE, the long-term stability of the device with PCBDANI/LiF double CBLs is also superior to that of the device with LiF single CBL.

# 1 Introduction

Organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) have recently emerged at the forefront of solution-processable thin-film photovoltaic technologies due to their high absorption coefficient, excellent ambipolar

charge mobility, long excitons (electron-hole pairs) diffusion lengths, easy fabrication, and low-cost manufacturing   $  [1-8]  $  . In the past several years, great success has been achieved by elevating the power conversion efficiency (PCE) to over 19%   $  [9-11]  $  , thanks to the development of novel interfacial materials   $  [9,12-18]  $  , and careful optimization of perovskite film morphology and device architecture   $  [18-24]  $  . In generally, the pero-SCs are fabricated based on two different architectures, i.e., mesoscopic and planar heterojunction

(PHJ) structures. Although the impressive PCE of over 17% has been reported in mesoscopic pero-SCs, the high-temperature sintering (  $  \sim  $  500 °C) required for mesoporous zinc oxide electrode hinders their potential application in flexible devices [25]. In contrast, PHJ pero-SCs can be fabricated via a low-temperature solution process, which renders them compatible with flexible substrates, such as polyethylene terephthalate (PET).

Depending on whether the indium-tin-oxide (ITO) electrode is the anode or the cathode, the PHJ pero-SCs can be further classified as p-i-n or n-i-p structures, where n and p represent n- and p-type charge-transporting layers (CTLs), and i denotes the intrinsic perovskite layer [26]. The planar n-i-p pero-SCs commonly employ an n-type compact CTL (e.g.,   $  TiO_{2}  $   and ZnO) which often requires high-temperature annealing treatment. Moreover, this type of pero-SCs exhibits a pronounced hysteresis during measurement of the current density-voltage (J-V) curves, making the extracted device parameters ambiguous [27–29]. In contrast, the planar p-i-n pero-SCs with fullerene derivative as electron-transporting layer (ETL) show hysteresis-free J-V characteristics due to the trap passivation effect of fullerene derivative [30–32]. Despite these advantages, however, the PCE of the planar p-i-n pero-SCs still lags behind their n-i-p counterparts [9,10,33]. Additionally, the stability issue of planar p-i-n pero-SCs should also be solved before widespread commercialization is possible.

The common device structure used for the planar p-i-n pero-SCs is similar to the organic photovoltaic (OPV) architecture. The traditional organic charge-transporting layers, poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene): poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) and fullerene derivative, are employed as hole-transporting layer (HTL) and ETL, respectively, in the planar p-i-n pero-SCs [34]. Commonly, the metal electrodes such as Al and Ag are adopted as the top cathode to ensure efficient electron collection and transportation. However, the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels of fullerene derivatives (e.g., PCBM) do not match well with the Fermi levels of top metal electrodes, which leads to the formation of an energy barrier between the fullerene derivative and metal cathode. To circumvent this problem, an additional cathode buffer layer (CBL), such as LiF [12], Ca [35], bathocuproine (BCP) [30,31], ZnO [15,36], PDINO [15],  $ \mathrm{PN_4N} $  [37],  $ \mathrm{C_{60}} $  [38], or its derivatives [13,19] has been inserted between the ETL and top electrode. Besides, double CBLs, such as  $ \mathrm{C_{60} / LiF} $  [38],  $ \mathrm{C_{60} / BCP} $  [30,31], zwitterion/LiF [14], or PCBC/LiF [39] were also incorporated into planar p-i-n pero-SCs to facilitate the ohmic contact formation and suppress the interfacial charge recombination.

Among the various CBLs used in planar p-i-n pero-SCs, alcohol-soluble fullerene derivatives could be the ideal CBL materials due to their high electron mobility and good energy level matching with the fullerene ETLs (e.g., PCBM)

[40]. In this work, we used a methanol-soluble amine group-modified PCBM derivative,   $  [6,6]  $  -phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid 2-((2-(trimethylammonium)ethyl)(dimethyl)ammonium)-ethyl ester diiodides (PCBDANI), as the CBL between PCBM and the top Al electrode. An impressive PCE of 15.45% was achieved in the planar p-i-n pero-SCs with PCBDANI single CBL. This PCE is significantly higher than that (13.15%) of the control device without the CBL. The performance improvement is attributed to the formation of an interfacial dipole at the PCBM/Al interface arising from the amine functional group and the suppression of interfacial charge recombination by the PCBDANI CBL. To further enhance the device performance, PCBDANI/LiF double CBLs were employed. A promising PCE of 15.71% was obtained, which is slightly higher than that of the devices with LiF or PCBDANI single CBL. More importantly, the device with PCBDANI/LiF double CBLs showed superior stability when compared to the device with LiF single CBL. After storage in glovebox for 75 d, approximately 71% of the initial PCE still remained for the device with PCBDANI/LiF double CBLs while only 52% remained for the device with LiF single CBL.

# 2 Experimental

# 2.1 Materials

All the solvents involved were purchased from Sigma Aldrich (USA).   $  PbCl_{2}  $   (99.999%) was ordered from Alfa Aesar (USA) and PCBM was obtained from American Dye Source, Inc. (Canada). ITO-coated glass with resistance of 10   $  \Omega/sq  $   was acquired from CSG Holding Co., Ltd. (China). 1,8-Diiodooctane (DIO) were purchased from TCI Development Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). PCBDANI was synthesized according to the literature method [40]. Methylammonium iodide (MAI) was synthesized in our laboratory as reported earlier [38].

# 2.2 Preparation of the perovskite precursor

The   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   precursor solution was prepared by mixing MAI and   $  PbCl_{2}  $   in a 3:1 molar ratio in anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), and the final concentration of the perovskite was controlled at approximately 40 wt%. After about 5 min, an additive DIO (1% by volume relative to DMF) was added to the perovskite precursor solution. The mixture was then stirred overnight at 60 °C and filtered through a 0.45 μm filter before device fabrication. The perovskite precursor was stored in a glovebox.

PCBM with concentration of 15 mg/mL in chloroform was then spin-coated atop the perovskite layer at 1200 r/min for 60 s. Finally, LiF (1 nm) and Al electrode were sequentially deposited by thermal evaporation at a base pressure of 1×10⁻⁴ Pa. The deposition rate and film thickness were monitored with a quartz crystal sensor. A shadow mask was closely covered on the sample during the Al deposition. # 3 Results and discussion

For convenience of description, the voltage sweep from negative voltage (-0.5 V) to positive voltage (1.0 V) is denoted as forward scan (FS) and that from positive voltage to negative voltage is denoted as reverse scan (RS). # 3.2 Device stability of the p-i-n pero-SCs

Apart from PCE, stability is another important issue that must be addressed before the realization of practical applications of pero-SCs [3,53,54]. We found that the PCBDANI interlayer is helpful to improve the device stability. Figure 5 shows the normalized photovoltaic characteristics of the p-i-n pero-SCs with PCBDANI/LiF double and LiF single CBLs as a function of the storage time in the glovebox  $ (\mathrm{O}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} < 1 $  ppm). The device with PCBDANI/LiF double CBLs maintained  $ 71\% $  of its initial efficiency after  $ 75\mathrm{d} $  while the device with LiF single CBL retained only  $ 52\% $  of its initial efficiency. This indicates that the device stability can be greatly improved upon the incorporation of a PCBDANI interlayer.

From Figure 5, it can be seen that the PCE decay for both devices is dominated by the rapid decrease in FF, while the   $  J_{sc}  $   and   $  V_{oc}  $   remain almost constant. To gain more insight into the degradation process, dynamic evolution of the J-V curves as a function of the storage time in the glovebox for the devices with PCBDANI/LiF double and LiF single CBLs is presented in Figure S4. S-shaped kinks were observed near   $  V_{oc}  $   in the J-V curves for the device with LiF single CBL after 50 d, and the kinks became more pronounced with the further increase of storage time. In comparison, a less severe kink appeared in the device with PCBDANI/LiF double CBLs after 75 d. The appearance of the S-shaped kinks implies the existence of large   $  R_{s}  $   in the device, which impedes the charge transport and causes the rapid reduction of FF. Considering that there is no color change of the perovskite films during aging, the significant increase in   $  R_{s}  $   cannot be ascribed to the perovskite decomposition [55]. Therefore, the dramatically enhanced   $  R_{s}  $   probably results from the increased contact resistance at the PCBM/LiF/Al interfaces, which could be caused by some slow interaction (ionic diffusion or doping) between PCBM and LiF. The incorporation of the PCBDANI layer between the PCBM and LiF layers could retard the interaction, which leads to the slower increase of   $  R_{s}  $   with time and therefore the substantial improvement of the device stability. Further investigations into the detailed degradation mechanisms of the planar p-i-n pero-SCs are currently underway.

# 4 Conclusions

In this paper, we have demonstrated that the performance of the planar p-i-n pero-SCs based on the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  absorber can be greatly improved by introducing a methanol-soluble diamine-modified fullerene derivative, PCBDANI, as the CBL. A promising PCE of  $ 15.45\% $  was obtained for the device with PCBDANI single CBL, which is  $ 17\% $  higher than that of the control device without the CBL. The dramatic improvement in PCE is attributed to the simultaneous enhancement in the  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $ ,  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  and  $ FF $  arising from the generation of an interfacial dipole at the PCBM/Al interface as well as the suppression of interfacial recombination by the PCBDANI interlayer. The PCE of the pero-SCs was further improved to  $ 15.71\% $  by employing PCBDANI/LiF double CBLs, which is slightly higher than that of the devices with LiF or PCBDANI single CBL. More importantly, the device with PCBDANI/LiF double CBLs exhibited superior long-term stability when compared to the device with LiF single CBL. This can be ascribed to the excellent water/oxygen blocking ability of the PCBDANI interlayer. Our results suggest that amine-modified fullerene derivative, PCBDANI, is a promising candidate as CBL in high efficiency planar p-i-n pero-SCs.
