# Abstract

Semi-transparent and self-encapsulated perovskite solar cells could be fabricated by simply laminating the front sub-cell (ITO/poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS)/CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ ) and back sub-cell (FTO/compact-TiO $ _2 $ /mesoporous-TiO $ _2 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ ), without vacuum-evaporating metal electrode. The addition of chlorobenzene (CB) between two perovskite layers accelerated perovskite crystals interfusion and close interfacial contact from two separated sub-cells, which contributed to perovskite film with high crystallinity and light absorption in laminated cells. The self-encapsulated perovskite solar cell (device area of 0.39 cm $ ^2 $ ) with CB treatment not only showed power conversion efficiency of 6.9%, but also existed excellent stability even if soaking in water for 24 h. This novel approach to fabricate semi-transparent, solution-processible, cost-effective and high-stable perovskite solar cells may provide a reliable royal road

Recently, organic/inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) based on  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbX}_3 $  [X=I, Cl, or Br] have attracted tremendous attention because of perovskites possessing low-cost, solution spin-coating process and unique optoelectronic properties (such as, large absorption coefficient, long carrier diffusion length and high charge mobility) [1-5]. High power conversion efficiency (as high as  $ 22.1\% $ , certified) of PVSCs has been achieved by applying new preparation methods, composition and device structure optimization, interface engineering, and so on [6-11]. However, several challenges for large-scarce commercial application remain, of which the most critical is that PVSCs suffer from poor stability. Perovskite-related and interfacial degradations due to several independent factors (such as temperature, humidity, oxygen, light illumination, etc.) have been systematically studied [12-14]. So far, the usage of relative stable perovskite active layer and interfacial materials, as well as proper encapsulation, have been applied to alleviate these degradations [15-20].

Except that, metal electrode-related degradation was also found to greatly affect the stability of the devices. The Ag layer suffered from severe corrosion and formed silver halides (such as AgCl, AgI) in humid environments, which due mainly to the undesirable contact and reaction between Ag electrode and the mobile halide ions from the perovskite light absorber [21-23]. Exposing PVSCs to a temperature of  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  was enough to induce Au migration through the organic spiro-MeOTAD hole-transporting layer, and into the perovskite material, which in turn severely affected the device performance [24]. Therefore, it is crucial to protect the metal contacts from reactions with the perovskite material. Replacement (or protection) of the organic components with metal oxides, e.g., a chromium oxide-chromium

$ (\mathrm{Cr}_2\mathrm{O}_3 / \mathrm{Cr}) $  interlayer, as well as the fabrication of solar cells with all-solution-processed metal oxide charge transport layers, have recently been proposed [25,26]. Besides, Ag and Au electrodes also require a high-vacuum evaporation technique, thereby limiting their future large-scar application. Highly conductive polymer poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) as the top electrode replacing commonly used metal electrodes, has been prepared through a transfer-lamination technique rather than direct spin-coating [27].

Here, we propose a simple lamination technique to simultaneously fabricate and encapsulate PVSCs solar cells. Semi-transparent and self-encapsulated perovskite devices have been fabricated by laminating two separated perovskite-based substrate with the structure of FTO/compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  (c- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ )/mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  (mp- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ )/ $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and ITO/PEDOT:PSS/ $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ , which are defined as back sub-cell and front sub-cell, respectively. During the pressing process, chlorobenzene (CB) has been added between two perovskite layers to accelerate perovskite crystals interfusion and close interfacial contact from two separated sub-cells. The self-encapsulated ITO/PEDOT:PSS/ $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ /mp- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /c- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /FTO perovskite solar cell (device area of  $ 0.39~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ ) with CB treatment shows power conversion efficiency of  $ 6.9\% $  and excellent stability even if soaking in water for  $ 24\mathrm{h} $ . The novel approach to fabricate PVSCs has many advantages compared with the regular procedure. First of all, the device is self-encapsulated by UV curing adhesive, which has the functionality to protect from degradation induced by water and oxygen. Secondly, a vacuum-evaporation metal-free electrode is adopted, which is beneficial to avoid electrode-related degradation and reduce fabrication cost. Thirdly, the

In a word, the novel approach offers the possibility of fabricating semi-transparent, metal-free, cost-effective and water-stable perovskite solar cells with commercial application in exterior building window [28,29].

Schematic illustration of the fabrication process of the self-encapsulated perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) is shown in Figure 1, which is water-stable, cost-effective, solution-processible and absent of vacuum-evaporating metal electrode. Firstly, two separated perovskite-based substrate with the structure of FTO/compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  (c- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ )/mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  (mp- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ )/ $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and ITO/PEDOT:PSS/ $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  were fabricated, which were defined as back sub-cell and front sub-cell, respectively. Then, the self-encapsulated perovskite device (ITO/PEDOT:PSS/ $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ /mp- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /c- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /FTO) was formed by laminating two separated sub-cells following by drying. We fabricate perovskite films by one-step process with CB anti-solvent treatment in order to obtain smooth surface. Besides, we also dip the CB solvent between the perovskite layers to remove the air in between for increasing the adhesion. The complete fabrication procedure is illustrated in the experimental section. It is well known that the uniform morphology with full coverage and high crystallinity by controlling fabrication process and further optimization of perovskite films is in favor of enhancing the performance of PVSCs [30,31]. In particular, effective interdiffusion of both perovskite crystals from two separated sub-cells is of greatest importance for the laminated cells. So, the addition of CB between two perovskite layers has been applied to accelerate forming dense perovskite-perovskite layers with close interfacial contact. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns (in Figure 3a) of perovskite films in the front sub-cell, back sub-cell and laminated cell with and without CB treatment have been conducted to investigate the nature of perovskite crystal formation and interdiffusion following pressurization. There are characteristic diffraction peaks at  $ 14.34^{\circ} $ ,  $ 28.68^{\circ} $  and  $ 32.10^{\circ} $ , which can be respectively assigned to (110), (220) and (310) diffractions of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  tetragonal crystal phase. Moreover, the diffraction peak attributable to  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  is inexistent. The diffraction intensity of perovskite crystal in laminated cell, especially with CB treatment, is significantly higher than front sub-cell and back sub-cell, which suggesting that perovskite film with high crystallinity can be obtained in laminated cell. Figure 3b shows the ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) absorption spectra of perovskite films in front sub-cell, back sub-cell and laminated cell, where the absorption of laminated cell with CB treatment is obviously higher than the front and back sub-cells due to the formation of thicker perovskite film. The similar phenomenon has been found in  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbBr}_3 $ - $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite tandem solar cell, which has been fabricated by laminating FTO/bl- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $ / $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbBr}_3 $ /wet P3HT (or PTAA) substrate and PCBM/ $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ /PEDOT:PSS/ITO substrate [32]. Expect for ensuring high perovskite crystallinity and light absorption, the self-encapsulated perovskite solar cell possesses excellent water-soaked stability. From the photographs of the stability evaluation for traditional device (ITO/PEDOT:PSS/CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /PCBM/Al) and self-encapsulated device (ITO/PEDOT:PSS/CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /mp-TiO $ _2 $ /c-TiO $ _2 $ /FTO) (Figure 4), there is no obvious difference in color of perovskite films for self-encapsulated and conventional device in ambient environment (below  $ 40\% $  humidity, at room temperature). Upon soaking in water, the conventional MAPbI $ _3 $  device changed color from dark brownish to yellow gradually and completely degraded in no time. Remarkably, the origin dark brownish color was still remained in the self-encapsulated perovskite device even if soaking in water for  $ 24\mathrm{h} $ . We also tested stability of the self-encapsulated perovskite device in air conditions. Figure 5a compares the transmittance spectra of self-encapsulated device and conventional device. For the continuous perovskite film of conventional device, the transmittance is below  $ 20\% $  in the range of  $ 400 - 750\mathrm{nm} $ . A superior visible transmission of appropriate  $ 40\% $  is observed for the self-encapsulated device, of which both sides are transparent. Figure 5b shows the stability of corresponding perovskite solar cells in an ambient environment with  $ 40\% $  relative humidity at room temperature. Compared with conventional device, the self-encapsulated perovskite device presented high air-stability with PCE maintaining  $ 80\% $  of its initial value after exposure to air for 14 days. The air- and water-soaked stability fully proved that the self-encapsulated perovskite device could effectively prevent the oxygen and water from infiltrating the perovskite film, which playing a

The current density-voltage (J-V) curves of the self-encapsulated perovskite solar cells obtained with and without CB treatment under AM 1.5G illumination at 100 mW/cm² are shown in Figure 6. In conclusion, we successfully fabricated semi-transparent and self-encapsulated ITO/PEDOT:PSS/CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /mp-TiO $ _2 $ /c-TiO $ _2 $ /FTO perovskite solar cell by simple lamination technique of the front sub-cell (ITO/PEDOT:PSS/CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ ) and back sub-cell (FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $ /mp-TiO $ _2 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ ) with UV curing adhesive. The simple technique is solution-processible, cost-effective and of metal-free electrode. The addition of chlorobenzene between two perovskite layers accelerated perovskite crystals interfusion and close interfacial contact from two separated sub-cells. By comparison with the XRD patterns and UV-vis absorption spectra of perovskite films from the front sub-cell, back sub-cell and laminated cell, it can be found that perovskite film with high crystallinity and light absorption have been obtained in laminated cell with CB treatment. The self-encapsulated perovskite solar cells with device area of 0.39 cm $ ^2 $  treated by CB shows PCE of 6.9%,  $ J_{sc} $  of 13.90 mA/cm $ ^2 $ ,  $ V_{oc} $  of 0.99 V, and FF of 0.505. Preparation of  $  MAPbI_3  $  precursor solution: The perovskite precursor solution was prepared by dissolved lead (II) iodide ( $  PbI_2  $ , Sigma-Aldrich, 99%) and methylamine iodide (MAI, TCI, 99%) (molar ratio 1:1) in anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, Sigma-Aldrich) with final concentration of 40 wt%. The solution was stirred at 60 °C overnight in argon glovebox and filtered with a 0.45 μm polyvinylidene fluoride filter before use.

Fabrication of laminated perovskite solar cell: First, the fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) and indium-doped tin oxide (ITO) glass substrates were cleaned by sequential ultrasonic treatment in acetone, detergent, deionized water and isopropyl alcohol for 15 min each time and then dried with a nitrogen stream. Then the pre-cleaned FTO and ITO glass substrates were treated by ultraviolet (UV)-ozone for 20 min in UV chamber. A 30 nm thick compact TiO₂ (c-TiO₂) layer was spin-coated on FTO from a solution containing 0.3 mol/L in n-butyl alcohol at 3000 rpm for 30 s, and then dried at 125 °C for 10 min. The mesoporous TiO₂ (mp-TiO₂) layer was spin-coated from a solution containing 1 g 18NRT Dyesol paste in 7 mL ethanol at 5000 rpm for 30 s. And the TiO₂ coated substrate was annealed at 500 °C for 30 min. For the other ITO glass substrate, poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) was spin-coated onto ITO at 4000 rpm for 60 s and dried at 140 °C for 15 min in air. Then the two substrates were transferred to dry, oxygen-free glove-box. Perovskite

precursor solution was spin-coated on the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer and PEDOT:PSS layer at 5500 rpm for 15 s, respectively. During the perovskite precursor solution spin-coating process, 150   $  \mu  $  L chlorobenzene (CB, Sigma-Aldrich) was quickly added on the surface of the substrate after a specific delay time of 6 s, and then dried at 100 °C for 10 min. After cooling down to room temperature, a part of perovskite film on the two substrates was scraped off respectively by a knife and covered by a small amount of UV curing adhesive. Then the two perovskite films substrates with and without CB treatment were pressurized by double clip after exposed in UV analyzer for 1 min and subsequent dried at 90 °C for 10 min.

Characterization: The illuminated current density-voltage (J-V) characteristics were characterized using Keithley 2400. The currents were measured under  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $  simulated AM 1.5 G irradiation (Abet Solar Simulator Sun2000). Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images were conducted on SU8020 scanning electron microscope operated at an acceleration voltage of  $ 8\mathrm{kV} $ . X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements were performed with a Rigaku D/Max-B X-ray diffractometer with Bragg-Brentano parafocusing geometry, a diffracted beam monochromator and a conventional cobalt target X-ray tube set to  $ 40\mathrm{kV} $  and  $ 30\mathrm{mA} $ . The ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) spectra of the samples were recorded on a PerkinElmer Lambda 750 spectrophotometer.
