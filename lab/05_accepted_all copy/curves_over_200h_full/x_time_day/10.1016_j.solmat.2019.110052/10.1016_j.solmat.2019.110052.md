# 1. Introduction

Recently, cesium-based inorganic perovskites have developed into a new exciting materials for photovoltaic application owing to their superb thermal and light stability as well as their fast-growing efficiency   $  [1-3]  $  . Among the reported high efficiency inorganic PSCs reported so far (PCE > 14%), most of the PSCs are fabricated by using 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-pmethoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene(SpiroOMeTAD) as the HTL. Spiro-OMeTAD must be oxidized with humidity sensitive dopants, e. g., lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) and 4-tertbutyl pyridine (tBP), to improve its conductivity and mobility   $  [4-6]  $  . These dopants are notorious for causing instability of inorganic PSCs   $  [7-9]  $  . However, few studies have been carried out to synthesize new and sensitive-dopant-free HTL for inorganic PSCs. Therefore, the development of cheap and sensitive-dopant-free HTLs for

Hole transport layers (HTLs) plays critical role in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, few studies have been done to develop new and sensitive-dopant-free HTL for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. Here, a low-cost fluorinated N',N',N'',N''-tetrakis(4-methoxyphenyl)spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene]-2,7-diamine (2mF-X59) was synthesized and applied as sensitive-dopant-free HTL into   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. After modified the 2mF-X59 and   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   surface by 2,3,5,6-tetrafluoro-7,7,8,8-tetracyanoquino-dimethane (F4-TCNQ), a maximum power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 14.42% was obtained for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC with an impressive open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.23 V, which is higher than that of the most previously reported   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with the doped Spiro-OMeTAD. More importantly, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with the newly developed HTLs showed remarkable stability retaining more than 94% of their initial PCE value after aging in air for 30 days without encapsulation. Therefore, we firmly believe that our new 2mF-X59 HTL created via molecular engineering is a promising candidate for preparing high performance   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs.

Spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene] (SFX) is a well-known construct functional material for design new HTLs in high performance organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs. For example, in 2016, Xu et al. synthesized a low-cost SFX core unit based HTL, termed X60, via a two-step synthetic route [10]. The hybrid PSC with X60 as HTL showed a PCE of  $ 19.84\% $ , which is comparable with the performance of PSC based on SpiroOMeTAD. Bi et al. also developed a SFX based HTL, termed X59, a high efficiency of  $ 19.8\% $  was obtained in the hybrid PSCs [11]. In 2017, Xu et al. tailor-made two low-cost SFX-based 3D oligomers, termed X54 and X55, as HTL for hybrid PSCs, an impressive efficiency of  $ 20.8\% $  was obtained [12], which is much higher than that of the control devices with SpiroOMeTAD  $ (18.8\%) $ . using tBP dopant. Recently, Gao et al. demonstrated the feasibility of using di-SFX-based HTLs (termed G1, G2) for highly efficient hybrid PSCs, a PCE as high as 20.2% was obtained in hybrid PSCs with good stability [14]. However, all these new organic HTLs derived from SFX (X59, X60, X54, X55, G1 and G2) require sensitive dopants (Li-TFSI and/or tBP) [10–15], as summarized in a recent review paper [16].

In addition to the electronic properties of charge transport layer, the interface between the perovskite and the charge transport layer is also critical for the PSC performance. The interface property of perovskite would determine the charge transport/collection and influence the surface defect density   $  [17–19]  $  , which is one of the key factors to affect the performance of PSCs, especially their   $  V_{oc}  $     $  [20–22]  $  . So far, various self-assembly monolayers   $  [23,24]  $   small molecules   $  [25–28]  $   or ionic liquids   $  [29,30]  $   have been applied to modify the interface between perovskite layers and their upper HTLs in hybrid PSCs. However, few studies have been done to modify inorganic perovskite surface for improving the inorganic PSCs performance, which leaves us space to optimize the performance of inorganic PSCs via perovskite interface engineering.

In this work, we reported the design of fluorinated X59 and its application as a new sensitive-dopant-free HTL instead of Spiro-OMeTAD for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. Introduction electron withdrawing fluorine atoms into the X59 molecule is an effective way to downshift its highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level, which is facilitative to the charge transfer by controlling the energetic offset and can improve the   $  V_{oc}  $   value of 2mF-X59-based   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs [31,32]. As a result, a PCE of 13.42% was obtained in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with 2mF-X59 (doped with F4-TCNQ) as HTL, which is comparable to that of device with the conventional Spiro-OMeTAD (doped with Li-TFSI and tBP). The use of 2mF-X59 or 2mF-X59 + F4-TCNQ avoids the sensitive doping and oxidation process required by conventional Spiro-OMeTAD. It is found that the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs based on 2mF-X59 + F4-TCNQ achieve more efficient charge extraction at perovskite/HTL interfaces than the device based on conventional Spiro-OMeTAD. Meanwhile, 2mF-X59 + F4-TCNQ films show higher hydrophobic property, which is better for the stability of the PSCs. In order to further improve the performance of PCSs, F4-TCNQ was simultaneously serve as interfacial optimizer for PSCs, which displayed an excellent ability to change surface morphology, passivate defects and facilitate hole transfer at interface. This strategy further boosted the PCE of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with 2mF-X59 HTL to 14.42% with an impressive   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.23 V. These results demonstrated that 2mF-X59 is a new promising alternative to conventional Spiro-OMeTAD toward all high efficient and stable inorganic PSCs, and suggested the potential of surface passivation for the performance improvement of inorganic PSCs.

Device fabrication: The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs were prepared with a structure of   $  FTO/TiO_{2}/CsPbI_{2}Br/HTL/Au  $  . FTO/Glass substrates were cleaned with acetone, isopropanol and distilled water in an ultrasonic bath for 30 min successively. Then the substrates were treated with UV cleaner for 10 min, 40 nm   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers were fabricated by chemical bath deposition on cleaned FTO substrates [33,34]. The substrates were immersed in 200 ml aqueous solution with 4.5 ml Titanium tetrachloride for 60 min at 70 °C, followed by washing with distilled water and annealing at 200 °C for 30 min. The perovskite precursor solution was prepared by mixing CsI,   $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}(DMSO)  $   in DMF/DMSO (9:1, v/v) according to our reported procedure [1,35]. The concentration of perovskite precursor solution was 0.9 M. The perovskite solution was spin-coated on top of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   film in a two-step process, first at 1000 rpm for 10 s and then at 3000 rpm for 40 s while 200 μl of chlorobenzene antisolvent was dropped onto the substrate at 15 s before the end of spinning. For passivation of perovskite film, the F4-TCNQ with different concentration in chlorobenzene was used as antisolvent. After that, the samples were heated at 120 °C for 10 min and 200 °C for 2 min to obtain black   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films. Spiro-OMeTAD, 2mF-X59 and 2mF-X59 + F4-TCNQ were used as HTLs, respectively. To prepare the Spiro-OMeTAD solution, 90 mg of Spiro-OMeTAD was dissolved in 1 mL of chlorobenzene and mixed with 36 μL t-BP and 22 μL Li-TFSI (520 mg/ml) solution in acetonitrile. The Spiro-OMeTAD solution (90 mg/ml) was spin-coated onto   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   layer at 5000 rpm for 30 s as HTL. 30 mg of 2mF-X59 without or with F4-TCNQ (1.5 mg, 5 wt%) was dissolved in 1 ml of chlorobenzene. The solution of HTLs was spin-coated on top of the perovskite films at 5000 rpm for 30 s. Then the 2mF-X59 films were heated at 90 °C for 10 min. Finally, a gold layer (~80 nm) was evaporated on the top of device as anode and a shadow mask is applied to form the device active area of 0.09 cm².

Device characterization: Top-view and cross-sectional SEM images of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs were characterized by a HITACHI, SU-8020 microscope. The absorption spectra were obtained by using a UV/Vis NIR spectrophotometer (Per-kinElmer, Lambda 950). XRD patterns were conducted on a D/MAX 2400 diffractometer with Cu Ka radiation (Rigaku). PL (excitation at 532 nm) spectra were obtained by using a FLS980 spectrometer (Edinburgh Instruments Ltd) and TRPL spectra were measured with a PicoQuant FluoQuant 300. Water contact angles of films were observed using a Dataphysics OCA 20. The band structure of the HTL films were analyzed using ultra-violet photoelectron spectra (UPS, ESCALAB 250Xi, Thermo Fisher). XPS analysis of the perovskite films is carried out by using a photoelectron spectrometer (ESCALAB250Xi, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The J-V curves of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs were performed on Keithley 2400 source under ambient condition. The illumination intensity was calibrated to   $  100\ mW/cm^{2}  $   by a National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL)-traceable KG5-filtered silicon reference cell. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs was measured by using a QTest Station 2000ADI system (Crowntech Inc.), including a tungsten-halogen lamp, a Si detector and a monochromator.

# 3. Results and discussion

Fig. 1a exhibits the molecular structure of Spiro-OMeTAD, X59 and fluorinated X59 (2mF-X59). 2mF-X59 is readily synthesized with only 2 steps. Firstly, 2,7-dibromo-9-fluorenone reacted with 3-fluorophenol via one-pot cyclization to produce the key fluorine containing core moiety 2,7-dibromo-3',6'-difluoro-spiro (fluorene-9,9'-xanthene) (2Br2F-SFX) [11] Subsequently, 2Br2F-SFX is subjected to a palladium-catalytic Buchwald-Hartwig amination with 4,4'-dimethoxydiphenylamine to obtain the target compound 2mF-X59. 2mF-X59 exhibits a considerable solubility in commonly used organic solvents such as chlorobenzene, chloroform and dichloromethane. It should be noted that the synthesis and purification process of 2mF-X59 are very simple and easy to scale up, which are especially advantageous for its commercial production.

The morphology of HTLs is observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), the fibrillar crystalline domains can be clearly visualized in 2mF-X59 film [36,37]. When  $ 1.5\mathrm{mg} $  F4-TCNQ (Fig. S1, Supporting Information) is added into 2mF-X59 solution as an insensitive dopant, the size of the fibrillar crystalline domain and the roughness of 2mF-X59 film is then reduced, as shown in Fig. 1b. Water contact angles on the different HTLs are also measured (inset of Fig. 1b). Compared with Spiro-OMeTAD, the more hydrophobic nature of 2mF-X59 has a larger contact angle. Among the three HTLs,  $ 2\mathrm{mF - X59 + F4 - TCNQ} $  has more hydrophobic fluorine ions, resulting in its best hydrophobic nature, which can enhance the surface hydrophobicity of the underlying perovskite layer, and will be beneficial for the ambient stability of the

$ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs.

Steady photoluminescence (PL) measurement is conducted to investigate the charge transfer dynamics at the perovskite/HTL interfaces. Compared with the pure perovskite film, the emission intensity of perovskite coated with HTLs is significantly reduced (Fig. 1c). ~92% emission is quenched for the perovskite film coated with 2mF-X59 + F4-TCNQ, which is higher than that of perovskite films with 2mF-X59 (73%) and Spiro-OMeTAD (54%), indicating the more efficient hole-extraction capability at the perovskite/2mF-X59 + F4-TCNQ interface [38,39]. The time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurement is further applied to validate the carrier transfer dynamic at the perovskite/HTL interfaces (Fig. 1d). TRPL spectra is fitted by a double-exponential decay function, the fitted decay time of different films is summarized in Table S1 in the Supporting Information, with   $  \tau_{ave} = 22.65  $   ns for the bare perovskite,   $  \tau_{ave} = 9.59  $   ns for the perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD film,   $  \tau_{ave} = 6.63  $   ns for the perovskite/2mF-X59 film, and   $  \tau_{ave} = 4.71  $   ns for the perovskite/2mF-X59 + F4-TCNQ film, respectively. Obviously, the perovskite film with 2mF-X59 + F4-TCNQ shows the shortest lifetime compared to other films, indicating that the 2mF-X59 + F4-TCNQ can not only significantly inhibit trap-induced nonradiative recombination but also efficiently extract the hole, and thus reduces the photocurrent loss in perovskite solar cells. In order to gain more insight into the charge transport dynamics at the interface between the perovskite and HTL, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) analysis are carried out for  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs with different HTLs. Fig. 2f exhibits the Nyquist plots of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs with different HTLs. The series resistance is small all for all devices ( $ \leq 40\Omega $ ; Table S3, Supporting Information), indicating good charge transfer ability in the bulk device. The recombination resistance  $ (R_{rec}) $  and capacitance  $ (C_{rec}) $  are determined by fitting these curves using an equivalent circuit model (Fig. S6, Supporting Information) [41]. It is found that  $ R_{rec} $  of device with  $ 2\mathrm{mF - X59 + F4 - TCNQ} $  is double that of device with Spiro-OMeTAD, which suggests a better interface contact and reduced carrier recombination in  $ 2\mathrm{mF - X59 + F4 - TCNQ} $  based device. The bulk recombination lifetime  $ \tau_{rec} $  is obtained by  $ \tau_{rec} = R_{rec} \times C_{rec} $  [39,42]. # 4. Conclusion

In summary, a new sensitive-dopant-free HTL (2mF-X59) was synthesized and successfully applied in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. The synthesis process is simple and the cost is low.   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC based on 2mF-X59 exhibited a high efficiency and good long-term stability. Therefore, the newly developed HTL is an appealing alternative to conventional SpiroOMeTAD for the perovskite based photovoltaic technology. While, the surface quality of perovskite film was optimized through F4-TCNQ passivation, the F4-TCNQ passivated   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC with 2mF-X59 HTL exhibited an impressive PCE of 14.42% and displayed a remarkable stability, retaining above 94% of its initial PCE after storing in ambient atmosphere for 30 days. The surface passivation technology provides a feasible way for further optimization of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with respect to surface morphology, electronic properties, and stability toward reliable and efficient photovoltaic industry.
