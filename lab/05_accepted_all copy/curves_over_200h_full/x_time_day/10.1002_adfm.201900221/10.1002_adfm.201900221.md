# Tailoring the Functionality of Organic Spacer Cations for Efficient and Stable Quasi-2D Perovskite Solar Cells

Weifei Fu, Hongbin Liu, Xueliang Shi, Lijian Zuo, Xiaosong Li, and Alex K.-Y. Jen*

The recent development of quasi-2D perovskite solar cells have drawn significant attention due to the improved stability of these materials and devices against moisture compared to their 3D counterparts. However, the optoelectronic properties of 2D perovskites need to be optimized in order to achieve high efficiency. In this work, the effect of spacer cations, i.e., phenethylammonium (PEA), 4-fluorophenethylammonium (F-PEA), and 4-methoxyphenethylammonium (MeO-PEA) on the optoelectronic properties and device performance of quasi-2D perovskites is systematically studied. It is observed that both larger and more hydrophobic cations can improve perovskite stability against moisture, while larger size can adversely influence the device performance. Interestingly, with F-PEA or MeO-PEA, distribution of n value can be shifted toward high 3D content in quasi-2D perovskite layers, which enables lower bandgaps and possibly lower exciton binding energy. Due to the best charge transport and lowest bandgap, the F-PEAI-based quasi-2D perovskite (n = 5) solar cell shows a highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 14.5% with excellent stability in air with a humidity of 40–50%, keeping 90% of the original PCE after 40 d. It is believed that the approach may open a way for the design of new organic spacer cations for stable low-dimensional hybrid perovskites with high performance.

Nevertheless, fundamental understanding of how the properties and stabilities of quais-2D perovskites affected by organic spacer cations are less known, and it is still quite challenging to achieve quasi-2D PVSCs with both high performance and stability. In order to understand the underlying reason, we employed computation with a combined cluster and periodic model approach to study the water resistance of PEA, F-PEA, and MeO-PEA based perovskites. The perovskite unit cell was first optimized using the Viena ab initio simulation package (VASP) code  $  ^{[35,36]}  $  until the residue forces are less than 0.05 eV Å  $  ^{-1}  $  on every atom, the optimized structures are shown in Figure S4 in the Supporting Information. During the optimization, the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE)  $  ^{[37]}  $  exchange correlation functional and the projector-augmented wave (PAW) method  $  ^{[38-40]}  $  are used. The wavefunctions are expanded in plane waves with an energy cutoff of 500 eV. A Γ-point has been used to sample the Brillouin zone for the unit cell optimization. The convergence criterion in total energy is set to 10  $  ^{-6}  $  eV. Neutral clusters (H-/F-/MeO-(PEA)  $  _{24}  $  Pb  $  _{9}  $  I  $  _{42}  $  ) were then extracted from the supercell of the optimized crystal (Figure S5, Supporting Information).

For the computation simplicity, those slab models only contain one layer of Pb (n = 1), instead of the optimal n = 5 ones in the experiments. However, for the purpose of studying water resistance, a n = 1 model is sufficient since it includes most host–ligand interactions. A water molecule has then been placed on top of the PEA covered surface, centering at the “pocket” formed by the four adjacent PEA molecules. The water molecule was moved along the z-axis to scan the water perovskite binding energies. The series of cluster models were optimized using PBE0 density functional  $  ^{[41]}  $   along with LANL2DZ pseudopotential double-  $  \zeta  $   basis set for the Pb and I atoms and 6–31g(d) basis set for the organic part.  $  ^{[42,43]}  $   The Grimme’s density-functional theory (DFT)-D3 method  $  ^{[44]}  $   was used to correct the van der Waals interaction. Only hydrogens on the water molecule are relaxed during the calculation. All cluster related calculations are employed in the gas phase in Gaussian 16 program.  $  ^{[45]}  $

Stabilities of encapsulated quasi-2D perovskite solar cells are also investigated. After each J-V measurement under 1 sun illumination, the devices were kept in dark at room temperature with 40–50% humidity. Normalized PCE as a function of storage duration is shown in Figure 5d and other parameters are shown in Figure S6 in the Supporting Information. The MeO-PEAI and F-PEAI-based quasi-2D PVSCs show better stability which maintain 97% and 90% of their original PCEs after 40 d compared to PEAI-based quasi-2D PVSCs which decrease to 70% of their original PCEs. Photostability of the unencapsulated devices in the glovebox under AM 1.5G illumination was also test (Figure S7, Supporting Information). All devices show good photostability remaining over 97% of the original PCEs after 3 h and no apparent difference was observed with different organic spacer cations.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, the effects of organic spacer cations, PEA, F-PEA, and MeO-PEA, on the optoelectronic properties and stabilities of quais-2D perovskites were studied. DFT computation shows that the steric effect and electron repulsion to the water lone pair electrons can effectively prevent the water from penetrating the perovskite structures, thus MeO-PEAI and F-PEAI-based perovskites show much better stability than PEAI-based perovskite. Due to the best charge transport and lowest bandgap, F-PEAI-based quasi-2D perovskite   $  n = 5  $   solar cell shows the best PCE of 14.5%. It also shows excellent stability in air under a humidity of 40–50%, keeping 90% of the original PCE after 40 d. We believe our approach may open a way for the design of new organic spacer cations for stable low-dimensional hybrid perovskites with high performance.

Characterization: Absorption spectra were measured using a Varian Cary 5000 UV–vis–NIR spectrometer. PL spectra were collected by an Ocean Optics USB 4000 spectrometer, excited with a 532 nm CW laser. XRD measurements were conducted by a Bruker D8 Discover Microfocus XRD with a Cu Kα source (with 1D or 2D detector). Gamma versus intensity plots were obtained using 2θ-integration (across (111) diffraction ring) of 2D XRD scans, with identical integration parameters. SEM characterizations were performed by an FEI Sirion scanning electron microscope operated at 5 kV.

Device Fabrication and Measurements: ITO glass (sheet resistance   $  \leq 10\ \Omega\ cm^{-2}  $  ) was cleaned consecutively in detergent, deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol ultrasonic bath for 15 min, respectively, and was dried under nitrogen flow, treated with UV–ozone for 15 min before use. An   $  \approx 30  $   nm PEDOT:PSS was spin-coated onto the precleaned ITO at 4000 rpm for 40 s, and baked in air at 150 °C for 15 min. Then the substrate was transferred into glovebox for active layer deposition and further device fabrication. 40   $  \mu  $  L of the precursor solution was spin coated at 5000 rpm for 45 s onto the PEDOT:PSS substrate and annealed at 100 °C for 15 min. Thereafter, PC  $  _{61}  $  BM (20 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in chlorobenzene) and BCP (0.5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in absolute ethanol) were deposited subsequently at 2000 rpm for 30 s and 3000 rpm for 30 s, respectively. The sample was transferred into a vacuum chamber for further deposition of Ag (150 nm) to finish the device fabrication process. A device area of 0.12 cm  $  ^{2}  $   is defined by the overlap between ITO and metal electrode, a mask with an area of 0.06 cm  $  ^{2}  $   was used when measuring.

All the J-V curves and steady-state power output of the PVSCs were recorded by using a Keithley 2400 sourcemeter with a scan rate of   $  0.1 \, V s^{-1}  $   and a voltage step of 0.005 V under illumination by a 450-W Oriel xenon lamp with an AM 1.5G filter solar simulator (  $  100 \, mW cm^{-2}  $  ). The light intensity was calibrated with a standard Si photodiode detector (equipped with a KG-5 filter), which can be traced back to the standard cell of National Renewable Energy Laboratory. The EQE spectra were measured using a setup consisting of a xenon lamp (Oriel, 300 W) as a light source, a monochromator (Newport Cornerstone 130), a chopper with a frequency of 100 Hz, a lock-in amplifier (Stanford Research Corp SR830), an SMU (Keithley 2400), and a NIST-certified Si photodiode (Thorlabs FDS 100-CAL) for calibration. The EQE spectrum was integrated over AM 1.5G photon flux to attain photocurrent density. The fluctuation of xenon arc lamp source was within 5% during the entire period of measurement. The encapsulated devices were kept in air with a humidity of 40–50% for stability test.
