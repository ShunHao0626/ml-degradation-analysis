# ABSTRACT:

Hole-transport materials (HTMs) are the key material for preparing high-performing perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, we present a novel and efficient hole-transporting material, 9,9-dioctyl fluorenyl phenylamine carbazole linked methoxy triphenylamines (CzPAF-OMeTAD), which was synthesized via Buchwald-Hartwig amination reactions from very cheap raw materials such as phenylcarbazole with 9,9-dioctyl fluorene bridge and arylamine terminals. It is demonstrated the synthesized CzPAF-OMeTAD with 9,9-dioctyl fluorene as bridge shows a higher hydrophobic and further induce a higher water contact angle of  $ 91^{\circ} $  relative to that  $ (79^{\circ}) $  for commonly Spiro-OMeTAD with spirofluorene linked methoxy triphenylamines. Further, CzPAF-OMeTAD also exhibits a deeper highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy level of  $ -5.30\mathrm{eV} $  and a higher hole mobility of  $ 3.83\times 10^{-5}\mathrm{cm}^2\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{S}^{-1} $ , indicating a potential hole-transport material for high-performance perovskite solar cells. Thus, PSCs were fabricated by using the CzPAF-OMeTAD as HTMs, and the resulting cell demonstrates a high power

Journal Pre-proof

conversion efficiency (PCE) of 15.35% and very high stability. For example, after 20 days of aging with relative humidity of 60%, the PCE still exhibit retention more than 87% of their initial values for corresponding cell. These are ascribed to the improved film-forming ability by sufficient amount of alkyl chains in CzPAF-OMeTAD and a higher hole-mobility for CzPAF-OMeTAD. These results implied our current research provides huge potential direction in the development of highly efficient and stable PSCs with further optimizing HTMs in the future.

Keywords: hole-transporting material, phenylcarbazole, hole mobility, perovskite solar cells

# Introduction

Recently perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted enormous scientific and industrial interests due to their capabilities in achieving effective light harvesting and high power conversion efficiency (PCE) through simple solution fabrication processing with a very low cost  $  ^{[1-11]}  $  . The PCE for PSCs has increased exceptionally from   $  \sim3.0\%  $   to 25.2% in past ten years  $  ^{[12-16]}  $  . The significant enhancement of PCE has been noticed in most efficient PSCs with n-i-p structure by incorporating hole transporting materials (HTMs) as an essential role in promoting hole extraction and transportation and improving mitigation of undesired recombination at the interface between perovskite layer and metal electrode  $  ^{[17-20]}  $  . From this perspective, to the design and develop new and efficient HTMs is a prospective way to achieve high PCE in PSCs.

So far, 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N'-di-pmethoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) is still the current most conventionally used HTM for highly efficient PSCs  $  ^{[21-24]}  $  . However, the use of hydrophilic dopants degrades the device stability, the complex synthesis, the expensive synthetic cost and lower charge carrier mobility of Spiro-OMeTAD limits its application for commercialization  $  ^{[25-26]}  $  . Therefore, it is necessary to design suitable HTMs with low-cost, high mobility, good-hydrophobicity, and easy preparation process for stable and efficient PSCs in

Journal Pre-proof

commercialization. Besides, the perovskite has a strong harvest ability in sunlight wavelength  $  ^{[27]}  $  . Therefore, the introduction of wide band-gap HTMs does not affect the perovskite absorption and light harvesting properties of PSCs as the perovskite layer and increase the performance of the PSCs  $  ^{[28, 29]}  $  .

For a long time, it was demonstrated carbazole and fluorene possess wide band-gap, good hole transport capability, and are ideal groups for developing as efficient HTMs in photoelectronic devices  $  ^{[30-36]}  $  . These carbazole-based HTMs reveal many attractive advantages such as a low-cost and tuning electronic and optical properties. Several carbazole-based and fluorene-based HTMs have been studied and developed, however, their poor solubilities reduced the film-forming properties and the preparation of devices, and then restricting their commercial applications.

In this work, using phenylaminecarbazole as the core moiety, 9,9-dioctyl fluorene as the bridge, and arylamine as the terminals, we designed and synthesized one novel HTM, named as 7-(N,N'-di-p-methoxyphenylamine-4-yl)-N-(7-(N,N'-di-p-methoxyphenylamine-4-yl)-9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluoren-2-yl)-N-(4-(9H-carbazol-9-yl)phenyl)-9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluoren-2-amine (CzPAF-OMeTAD), which shows broad optical band-gaps ( $ E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\mathrm{opt}} $ ) and the efficient UV covered region and can be easily prepared via simple reactions with high yield of  $ 62\% $ . The obtained CzPAF-OMeTAD possesses excellent electrochemical properties, such as appropriate HOMO and Lowest Unoccupied Molecular Orbital (LUMO) levels of  $ -5.36 $  eV and  $ -2.47 $  eV and wide  $ E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\mathrm{opt}} $  of  $ 2.89 $  eV where the suitable HOMO levels mean the CzPAF-OMeTAD can be used as efficient HTMs. Further, it has also been demonstrated that the alkyl chains in CzPAF-OMeTAD are beneficial for obtaining big water contact angle (WCA) of  $ 91^{\circ} $  to enhance the whole surface hydrophobic characteristic. Interestingly, the optimized CzPAF-OMeTAD based dopant PSCs achieves an excellent PCE of  $ 15.35\% $  under AM 1.5 G illumination ( $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $ ), and the PCE still exhibits retention more than  $ 89\% $  of their initial values after 20 days of aging.

# Photophysical, Thermal, Electrochemical Performances and Density Functional Theory (DFT) Calculation

The normalized UV-vis absorption and PL spectra of CzPAF-OMeTAD in dilute toluene at room temperature and in film spin-coated on a quartz substrate are shown in Figure 1a. It can be seen that CzPAF-OMeTAD in the dilute solution shows a strong absorption peak at  $ 397~\mathrm{nm} $  in absorption spectrum and a strong emission peak at  $ 420~\mathrm{nm} $  in PL spectrum. In the film, the CzPAF-OMeTAD exhibits UV-vis absorption peak at  $ 400~\mathrm{nm} $  and the PL peak at  $ 430~\mathrm{nm} $ , which appears an obvious red shift of  $ 10~\mathrm{nm} $  compared to that in above dilute solution.

The thermal properties of CzPAF-OMeTAD were characterized by TGA and DSC and displayed in Figure 1b. It is observed that the decomposition temperature  $ (T_{d}) $  for CzPAF-OMeTAD is  $ 408^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , which is corresponding to a  $ 5\% $  weight loss under nitrogen atmosphere. Meanwhile, the glass transition temperatures  $ (T_{g}) $  is observed at  $ 277^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , indicating the compound CzPAF-OMeTAD possessing the good thermal

Journal Pre-proof

stability. This can be due to the introduction of carbazole with high thermal stability in CzPAF-OMeTAD.

The energy levels of CzPAF-OMeTAD were investigated by CV measurement, shown in Figure 1c. The data was also summarized in Table 1. The oxidation potential  $ (E_{\mathrm{ox}}) $  is around  $ 0.86\mathrm{V} $ . The HOMO level of CzPAF-OMeTAD is calculated by formulas  $ E_{\mathrm{HOMO}} = - (E_{\mathrm{ox}} + 4.5) $  (eV)[38]. The LUMO level is reckoned from the HOMO level and the  $ E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\mathrm{opt}} $  deduced from the intersection of the normalized UV-vis absorption spectrum and PL emission spectrum in dilute solution in the long-wavelength direction ( $ E_{\mathrm{g}} = 1240 / \lambda_{\mathrm{edge}} $ ). The HOMO level of CzPAF-OMeTAD is  $ -5.36\mathrm{eV} $ , which is  $ 0.21\mathrm{eV} $  deeper than that  $ (-5.15\mathrm{eV}) $  of Spiro-OMeTAD[39] and relatively close to the valence bond maximum  $ (-5.43\mathrm{eV}) $  of the perovskite layers[25]. In theory, the different quasi-Fermi levels between the holes in the HTMs and the electrons in the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  decide the open circuit voltage ( $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $ ) of traditional solar cells[40]. Thus, the  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  is expected to improve by reducing the HOMO level. Here, CzPAF-OMeTAD with its low lying HOMO level can thus possibly give higher  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  values relative to Spiro-OMeTAD. The LUMO level of CzPAF-OMeTAD was calculated to be  $ -2.36\mathrm{eV} $  by combining HOMO level and  $ E_{\mathrm{g}}^{\mathrm{opt}} $  value, indicating the CzPAF-OMeTAD could efficiently block electrons when is used in PSCs.

To better understand the molecular structure and electron distribution in HOMO and LUMO, the optimized molecular geometry and electron distribution for CzPAF-OMeTAD were simulated using DFT calculation at the level of CAM-B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) in Gaussian 09 program,  $  ^{[41, 42]}  $  shown in Figure 1d. The dihedral angles between the two benzenes in fluorene unit is around  $  90^{\circ}  $ , which can effectively inhibit the intermolecular  $  \pi-\pi  $  stacking and charge recombination, so as to enhance the extraction of holes from perovskite layer. The LUMO of CzPAF-OMeTAD is only distributed in the central core part fluorenes, while the HOMO is almost diffused on the entire molecular skeleton, except for the alkyl chain, which is favorable for enhancing holes transport  $  ^{[25]}  $ .

The current density–voltage (J-V) curves based on different concentrations CzPAF-OMeTAD and for are displayed in Figure 3 (inset for the corresponding devices structure), and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters are presented in Table 2. It is found that the optimized device with 45 mg/mL of HTMs exhibits a PCE of 15.35% with a   $  J_{SC}  $   value of 24.8 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , a   $  V_{OC}  $   value of 0.93 V and a fill factor (FF) equal to 67.08 % under AM 1.5 G illumination (100 mWcm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). To investigate the durability of devices based on both HTMs of Spiro-OMeTAD and CzPAF-OMeTAD, we performed long-term stability tests of PSCs without encapsulation and under air at room temperature with relative humidity of 60 % (storage time ≈20 days), which is critical parameter to evaluate the stability parameter of PSCs for practical application. As shown in Figure 6, the stability of PSCs with CzPAF-OMeTAD as HTM decreased along with the exposure time, and the PCEs retained only 87 % of its initial efficiency values after 20 days of aging. In addition, it was observed the black color of perovskite film was always consistent for whole film surface as shown in the inset of Figure 6. However, for Spiro-OMeTAD-based devices, the PCEs exhibit retention more than 54 % of their initial values, and the black color of perovskite film gradually fade away from the center. These results clearly indicated that PSCs based on CzPAF-OMeTAD as HTM have an excellent device stability, which lies with the hydrophobic nature of the HTL  $  ^{[44]}  $  and is consistent with the test result of water contact angle in Figure 2b with 91° for CzPAF-OMeTAD film compared to that (79°) for Spiro-OMeTAD film. This means that CzPAF-OMeTAD film effectively suppress moisture infiltration into the perovskite layer and reduce the defect state density, owing to the sufficient amount of alkyl chains improving hydrophobicity to be solution processable and then improve the stability of the resulting PSCs.

# Conclusions

In summary, we have successfully designed and synthesized a novel small-molecule material using very cheap carbazole derivatives and good solubility alkyl fluorene as a HTM applied in perovskite solar cells in high yields of  $ 62\% $ . The CzPAF-OMeTAD-based HTMs display low-costs, excellent film forming, good conductivity, high-hydrophobicity, appropriate energy levels and high hole transport ability, implying CzPAF-OMeTAD is suitable for applying as HTM for PSCs. The  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  PSC devices with CzPAF-OMeTAD as HTM layer achieve very impressive PCE of  $ 15.35\% $  and excellent device stability with PCE still reaching  $ 87\% $  of their initial values after 20 days of aging, which is outclass that  $ (54\%) $  of Spiro-OMeTAD-based contrast device placed in the same conditions. Studies implied our current research offers a new direction for further optimizing HTMs for highly efficient and stable PSCs in the future.
