# Abstract

The defects sited at the surface and internal grain boundaries in organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite always capture the carriers and become the recombination centers, causing the decrease of carrier lifetime. Meanwhile, these centers are inclined to become the adsorption sites for water and oxygen, thus leading to the decrease in power conversion efficiency (PCE) and stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, a facile strategy is demonstrated by utilizing self-assembled ammonium salt named phenyltrimethylammonium bromide (PTABr) to modify perovskite film. This way is efficient to passivate the   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  anti-defects and enhance the stability under air atmosphere condition. Experiments and theoretical calculations have demonstrated that self-assembled PTABr shows a hydrophobic aliphatic moiety-on orientation on the surface perovskite, effectively reduces the density of trap state in perovskite film and enhances the carrier lifetime. The strategy results in the enhancement in both photovoltaic properties and stability of PSCs, the champion PTABr treated device shows a PCE over 20%, and maintains 86% of its initial PCE after 60 days in ambient atmosphere without encapsulation. This method paves a novel way to improve the surface hydrophobicity and promote the overall properties of device.

Keywords: perovskite solar cells, surface modification, self-assembled molecule, hydrophobicity, long-term stability.

# Introduction

The past decade has seen the rapid development of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) in the field of photovoltaics as the promising candidates for efficient and low-cost thin film photovoltaics. Perovskite materials which used in PSCs have some unique photovoltaic properties, such as tunable direct band gaps, high optical absorption coefficients, high defect tolerance factors and long carrier diffusion length.  $  ^{1-7}  $   Since their first application in solar cells, the photovoltaic efficiencies have been improved rapidly during these years, the best power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has progressed from 3.8% to certified 25.2%.  $  ^{8-19}  $   Despite the numerous advancement has been achieved in the research of PSCs, there are still scores of unsolved problems in the field, such as abundant recombination centers and multifarious defects  $  ^{20-25}  $   which exist in the grain boundaries and surface of perovskite film result in the shorter carrier lifetime, relatively low open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ), inferior long-time stability and so on. These problems need serious consideration for future development of PSCs.

The performance of PSCs is strongly associated with the quality of perovskite film. For achieving high quality perovskite film, multitudinous additives have been used to control perovskite grain growth and gain uniform film through interfacial modification and antisolvent engineering strategy, aiming at reducing the recombination centers and gaining uniform film.  $  ^{[26,27]}  $   For molecule passivation method, polymers with repeating functional groups such as poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA), polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) and polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) are used as interface modifier at the interface

The functional groups in these polymers are efficient to interact with trap sites and suppress defect formation wherever introduced in bulk or the surface of perovskite.  $  ^{28-30}  $   However, charge conduction is poorer in polymers, the overall PCEs of PSCs always get compromise in this situation.

Apart from above-mentioned strategies, using small molecule containing functional groups at the surface of perovskite is an efficient method to promote the performance of PSCs.  $  ^{31-33}  $   The organic Lewis bases thiophene and pyridine were introduced to the HTL/perovskite interface to donate electron density to Pb atom and form a co-ordinate or dative covalent bond, thus neutralize the excess positive charge in the perovskite crystal and enhance the PCEs of PSCs.  $  ^{34}  $   2,3,5,6-Tetrafluoro-7,7,8,8-tetracyano-quinodimethane (F4TCNQ) is an efficient small molecule modifier for HTL/perovskite interface. The functional group in F4TCNQ can interact with the iodine defects and suppress the formation of non-radiation recombination, thus enhance the performance of PSCs.  $  ^{35}  $   Oleylammonium polysulfides (OPs) which was prepared by mixing oleylamine and sulfur powder can also be used as HTL/perovskite interface modifier. The introduction of OPs was beneficial for the defect passivation and prolonging carrier lifetime, thus the overall performance of PSCs got an enhancement.  $  ^{36}  $   The strategy of using small molecule containing functional groups can solve a part of problems which hinders the development of PSCs. However, the performance of PSCs remains dissatisfied, especially the moisture stability of devices. Therefore, multifunctional self-assembled molecules are necessary for obtaining PSCs with high photovoltaic properties and moisture stability.

In this work we report a facile strategy by using a self-assembled molecule ammonium salt named phenyltrimethylammonium bromide (PTABr) to modify the perovskite surface and form a layer of self-assembled film. Experiments and theoretical calculations demonstrate that due to the strong interaction between   $  \mathrm{N}^{+}(\mathrm{CH}_{3})_{3}  $   group and perovskite surface, PTABr molecule shows an aliphatic moiety-on orientation, further forming a hydrophobic layer on the perovskite film to prevent the process of water adsorption and penetration efficiently. This way is great for enhancing the stability of devices in moisture condition, and gains 86% of its initial PCE after 60 days in ambient atmosphere without encapsulation, contrasting with the 71% of PSCs without PTABr treated. By means of introducing PTABr to perovskite film,   $  PbI_{3}^{-}  $  anti-defects which exists in perovskite surface is efficiently reduced by the ammonium group, results in a significant reducing of the trap density and prolonging the carrier lifetime, thus boosts the PCE to 20.13% with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.093 V, a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 23.28 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and a fill factor (FF) of 79.13%, contrasting with the 18.48% PCE of PSCs without PTABr treated.
