# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid halide perovskite has emerged as a promising light-absorption material for solar cells due to excellent light harvesting properties, bipolar charge-transport characteristics, and low exciton binding energy (Huang et al., 2017; Qiu et al., 2017). In the past few years, the highest power conversion efficiency of perovskite solar cell (PSC) has raised from  $ 3.8\% $  to  $ 23.3\% $  (Kojima et al., 2009; You, 2018), which almost approaches the best efficiency of crystal silicon solar cell (Yoshikawa et al., 2017), exhibiting the potential of commercial applications (Green et al., 2018; Gu et al., 2018; Zuo et al., 2018; Li et al., 2016). Commonly, a PSC device is consist of conductive substrate, electron transport layer, perovskite light-absorption layer, hole transport layer and metal electrode. Among them, high-quality perovskite films are extremely crucial for fabricating efficient solar cells and achieving high efficiency (Qiu et al., 2016; Salim et al., 2014; Seo et al., 2014; Lu et al., 2016).

To date, several methods have been employed to fabricate high-quality perovskite films, including vapor-phase deposition, two-step sequential solution deposition and anti-solvent assisted spin-coating

# ABSTRACT

Organic–inorganic hybrid halide perovskite solar cells have attracted considerable attention owing to their high power conversion efficiency. Up to now, anti-solvent assisted one-step spin-coating crystallization method is still a major process to achieve high quality perovskite film. However, the toxicity of commonly used anti-solvents and the nonrepeatability of corresponding solar cell still obstruct the potential of commercial applications. Here, high-quality perovskite film, fabricated with anti-solvent engineering, is successfully developed by using a green mixed anti-solvent of ethylacetate with isopropanol as additive. Compared with pure ethylacetate anti-solvent, the mixed anti-solvent can significantly improve the surface morphology and passivate the grainboundary/surface defects of perovskite film, distinctly enhancing the photovoltaic property and the repeatability of P-i-N planar devices. The best-performing device exhibits a remarkable efficiency of 18.98% with negligible hysteresis and praiseworthy stability over 30 days in dry conditions. Our findings provide an avenue for high efficiency and stable planar perovskite devices with low cost and environmental-friendly manufacturing.

Compared with anti-solvent assisted spin-coating method, vapor deposition process may increase the manufacturing cost, and two-step sequential deposition method is relatively difficult to achieve precise constitution owing to disproportionate composition of precursor and specific film growth mechanism. At present, anti-solvent treatment method has proven to be a simple and efficient way to obtain high quality perovskite film in tuning crystal nucleation and film growth (Im et al., 2014; Li et al., 2018; You et al., 2015). In 2014, Xiao et al. firstly introduced chlorobenzene (CB) as anti-solvent in one-step coating method, which allows well control over the nucleation dynamics and grain growth of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_2\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  (Xiao et al., 2014). After that, more antisolvents were employed to achieve high performance perovskite film, including diethylether (DEE) and toluene (TL) (Ahn et al., 2015; Zhou et al., 2014; Jeon et al., 2014; Yang et al., 2017). However, DEE, TL and CB are not applicable for the commercialization of PSC owing to their toxicity (Bu et al., 2017). Based on this, Bu et al. employed a green solvent of ethyl acetate (EA) (Bu et al., 2017). While compared with typically used anti-solvents (e.g. DEE), the repeatability and performance of PSC device fabricated with EA is a little unsatisfactory

In this report, we employ an environment-friendly weak polar and low-boiling point alcohols (isopropanol) into EA for the fabrication of high efficiency PSCs. Ultimately, we obtained a high PCE of  $ 18.98\% $  with enhanced stability and negligible hysteresis. This result suggests a highly promising route to high efficiency and stable inverted planar PSCs production.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1 Materials and preparation

N,N-Dimethylformamide (DMF), chlorobenzene, quadrol, ethylene glycol, Isopropanol,   $  \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_{2}}  $   (99.998%), nickel (II) nitrate hexahydrate (99.9%), and zinc nitrate hexahydrate (99.9%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used as received. N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) was purchased from Sinopharm chemical reagent co. LTD,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (  $  \geq99.5\%  $  ) and   $  PC6_{1}BM  $   (99.5%) and bathocuproine (BCP, 99.99%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. China. ITO/glass substrate was etched by HCl and zinc powder, then washed by isopropanol with KOH, deionized water, and ethyl alcohol sequentially. After that, they were drought with nitrogen.

# 2.2 Device fabrication

Nickel nitrate and zinc nitrate were dissolved in ethanediol to form 1M Zn-Ni(NO)  $  _{3}  $  solution. Then the Zn-Ni(NO)  $  _{3}  $  solution was spin-coated on cleaned ITO glass at 5000 rpm for 30 s. The substrate was annealed at 400 °C for 60 min on a hot plate to form dense NiO  $  _{x}  $  hole transport layer. 30 wt% and 45 wt% equimolar ratio MAI and PbI  $  _{2}  $  precursors were dissolved in mixed solvent (NMP/DMF 9/8 vol ratio) to form 45wt % perovskite precursor solution and stirred at room temperature for 10 min. For preparing the mixed anti-solvent, isopropanol of different volume ratios e.g. 0%, 6%, 12%, was added into ethylacetate. The perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated on NiO  $  _{x}  $ -coated substrate at 4,500 rpm for 50 s and 20 s later, the anti-solvent (0.1 ml) was dripped on the fresh film, followed by heating at 120 °C for 10 min in a N  $  _{2}  $ -filled dry box. An electron transport layer (ETL) was spin-coated at 3000 r.p.m. for 40 s with solution consisting of 20 mg PCBM and 1 ml chlorobenzene. Then, BCP solution containing 0.5 mg BCP and 1 ml isopropanol was spin-coated on the perovskite film. Finally, 60 nm Ag layer was deposited on ETL layer by thermal evaporation. The active area of the solar cell is 0.085 cm  $  ^{2}  $ .

In order to verify the optical property of perovskite films washed with different anti-solvent, photoluminescence spectra were measured and displayed in Fig. 4a and b. Steady state photoluminescence (PL, Fig. 4a) spectra show red shift when EI6 was adopted as anti-solvent, which corresponds to the results of optical absorption spectra shown in Fig. 4c, and most possibility relate to the increasing grain size of perovskite film (Bastiani et al., 2014; Hao et al., 2015; Zeng et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2015). In addition, PL intensity distinctly increased with the intervention of EI6. However, visibly decline is observed when excess IPA was involved in. Generally, steady-state PL can be used to explain photo generated charge carries recombination. It has been reported that large density of charge traps, especially at grain boundary and interfaces, can significantly influence the growth of perovskite film (Cheng et al., 2016; Hao et al., 2015). For a certain perovskite film without quench layer, larger PL intensity and longer decay time correspond to less trap states and grain boundary which derived from suppressed nonradiative recombination. As a result, a solar cell with high device performances can be achieved (Shuai et al., 2016). Fig. 4b depicts the time-resolved PL (TRPL) spectra of different perovskite films. TRPL spectra can be well fitted with single-exponential decay model. A relatively long PL lifetime of  $ 128\mathrm{ns} $  is obtained for the film washed with mixed anti-solvent EI6, which is accountable for the suppressed nonradioactive recombination mechanism mentioned above.

Fig. 4c is optical absorption spectra of different  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films. The absorption intensity becomes slightly stronger with the volume ratio of IPA increasing from  $ 0\% $  to  $ 6\% $ . However, when further IPA was added into the mixed anti-solvent, the absorption reduces slightly, especially for wavelength over  $ 500~\mathrm{nm} $ . Moreover, as can be seen in Fig. 4c, it should be particularly noted that the band edge of the absorption shows obvious red shift with the increasing amount of IPA from  $ 0\% $  to  $ 6\% $ , which most possibility relate to the increasing grain size of the perovskite film.

To further evaluate the effects of different samples on recombination dynamics of PSCs, we compared the charge-recombination lifetime by Transient optical voltage (TPV). Compared with other three samples (0.37, 0.29 and  $ 0.2\mu \mathrm{s} $  for EA, EI12 and IPA, respectively), EI6 washed devices exhibit a longer charge-recombination lifetime  $ (1.1\mu \mathrm{s}) $ . It suggests that charge recombination was distinctly retarded when using EI6 as anti-solvent, which may in agreement with the results of film morphology and grain size discussed above.

When introducing a certain amount of IPA in EA, photovoltaic performance demonstrates considerable improvement, especially in Voc and Jsc (Fig. 6c). However, when excess IPA was added into anti-solvent, significant decline was obtained, which may relate to the degeneration of perovskite film discussed above. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra over AM 1.5G solar spectrum were also integrated and the results are shown in Fig. 6b. The resulting current density is in close agreement with the values obtained from I to V scans. In particularly, the solar cell fabricated with EI6 exhibits obvious extending in absorption active range in EQE spectra, which is related to the red shift of the absorption band edge discussed in Fig. 4c. However, when IPA was used as anti-solvent, distinct decrease can be observed at the range below 500 nm or over 650 nm. It may relate to the destruction of perovskite films via intersolubility between IPA and MAI. It is also worthy to note that the anti-solvents significantly affect the roughness of perovskite film (Fig. 2), and the PCE has a close relationship with the surface roughness of perovskite film. With the increase of surface roughness, the PCE decrease significantly as summarized in Table 1.

The best-performing cell with active area of  $ 0.085\mathrm{cm}^2 $  exhibits a high PCE of  $ 18.98\% $   $ (J_{\mathrm{SC}} = 22.0\mathrm{mA/cm}^2, V_{\mathrm{OC}} = 1.08\mathrm{V,FF} = 79.9\%) $  with negligible hysteresis behavior (Fig. 7a). It can be observed that the devices prepared by EI6 show best stability (90% PCE after 30-day storage) compared with other anti-solvent washed devices, suggesting more valuable application in the future.

# 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, we have successfully demonstrated effective and stable green mixed anti-solvent engineering for inverted planar PSCs. The green mixed anti-solvent process could improve the surface morphology and passivate the grainboundary/surface of perovskite film. Therefore, the photovoltaic performance of P-i-N planar PSC gets significantly enhanced. The best-performing device exhibited a remarkable efficiency of  $ 18.98\% $  with negligible hysteresis and a praiseworthy stability over 30 days in dry conditions. We believe that this development of mixed green anti-solvent process is a promising route for future development of low-cost, environmentally friendly perovskite solar cells.
