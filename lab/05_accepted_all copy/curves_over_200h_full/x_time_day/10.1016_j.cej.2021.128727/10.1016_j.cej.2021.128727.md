# 1. Introduction

Recent years, there has been a growing academic interest in organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) owing to their simple fabrication method, excellent optoelectronic properties and high power conversion efficiency (PCE) which has increased to a competitive efficiency of  $ 25.5\% $  [1-3]. It is no doubt that PSC has been one of the most promising solar energy conversion devices, however, researches have consistently demonstrated that the instability of hybrid perovskite under humid and high temperature conditions resulting from the precipitation of lead salts or the volatilization of organic counterparts  $ \mathrm{[CH_3NH_3^+ (MA^+)} $  or  $ \mathrm{HC(NH_2)^{2 + }(FA^+)}] $  seriously limits its commercial development [4]. Fortunately, the advent of all-inorganic perovskite halides which are prepared by replacing organic ions with inorganic cesium ions has availably overcome the decomposition of perovskite in thermal environment. # ABSTRACT

The perovskite film with large grain size and high charge mobility is critical for the improvement of the power conversion efficiency (PCE) and the operational stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, multifunctional brominated graphene oxide (Br-GO) is prepared and innovatively used as an effective additive to incorporate into   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   to form a large-grained high-quality perovskite film and to passivate defects at grain boundaries, which remarkably reduces the trap state density and carrier non-radiative recombination. Meanwhile, the hole mobility of the homogeneous   $  CsPbBr_{3} + Br-GO  $   photoactive layer is obviously increased due to the hole acceptor properties and high carrier mobility of Br-GO, resulting in an enhanced charge separation as well as transfer and suppressed carrier radiative recombination. Moreover, the Br-GO with a downshift valence band is employed as a hole-transporting material setting at perovskite/Carbon interface to further compensate energy levels and promote hole extraction. As a consequence, a champion PCE of 10.10% with an open-circuit voltage up to 1.602 V for the optimized PSCs with a configuration of FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}/CsPbBr_{3} + Br-GO/Br-GO/Carbon  $   is obtained, which is much higher than 6.28% efficiency for the reference device. The unencapsulated solar cells also exhibit a remarkable tolerance toward 85% RH and long-term AM 1.5G illumination in air.

In addition, the typical device structure based on FTO/TiO $ _2 $ /CsPbBr $ _3 $ /Carbon gets rid of hole-transporting materials (HTMs) and precious metal back electrodes, which greatly reduces the cost and simplifies the process, providing a promising opportunity for the commercial development of PSCs [6,7]. Among all the methods for optimizing the quality or charge mobility of perovskite films, additive engineering has been widely studied because of the ability to enlarge the grain size and/or passivate defects as well as to construct heterojunction structure, speeding up the extraction and transportation of photo-generated carriers [9,10]. Various types of additives, such as organic substances (HSCH $ _2 $ COOH, ammonium benzenesulfonate and MACl, etc.) [11–13], inorganic salts (Li $ _4 $ Ti $ _5 $ O $ _{12} $ , KSCN and SAS, etc.) [14–16], polymer (PEG, PVP and PAN, etc.) [17–19], low dimensional carbon materials (fullerenes, carbon nanodots, graphene oxide (GO), carbon quantum dots and graphdiyne, etc.) [3,20–22] have been introduced in perovskite films to retard charge recombination for improved PSCs performance. In particular, the incorporation of GO and its derivatives into perovskite structure has attracted widespread interest because of their excellent optical, activity and conductivity. For instance, Chung et al. prepared device with the hybrid perovskite:GO layer to achieve a balanced charge mobility and an increased performance with remarkable stability of PSCs by improving the contact at the interface of donor and acceptor of holes [22]. He et al. developed an in situ solution method to decorate CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  perovskites with reduced GO (rGO) to form p-n molecular junctions and to improve charge transfer, realizing a significantly enhanced photodetector performance [23]. Mahmoudi et al. suppressed the ion migration and elevated the thermal and light stability of PSCs by preparing perovskite/Ag-rGO composites active layer [24]. Moreover, it has been reported that the properties of GO can be regulated in a wide range by functionalization of GO through introducing functional groups such as amino, carboxyl, halogenated and chemical adding with B, P, N or S atoms into the carbon lattice [25–27]. Among the many functionalized approaches, the covalent bond modification of GO by halogen elements has been regarded as the most commonly used methods to modulate the band structure, conductivity and chemical properties of GO to apply in the field of photoelectrochemistry [25,27].

In this work, the brominated graphene oxide (Br-GO) with multiple functions is prepared and innovatively introduced into perovskite precursor solution to produce high-quality   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   crystal films with large grain size and low trap density as a result of the crystallization kinetics regulation and the defect states passivation of perovskite by Br-GO, which evidently retard the carrier non-radiative recombination. Besides, due to the p-type electron donor and the downshift valence band (VB) near that of perovskite of Br-GO, the hole mobility of the homogeneous   $  CsPbBr_{3} + Br-GO  $   photo-absorption layer is improved to substantially promote charge transfer and extraction as well as suppress carrier radiative recombination. Meanwhile, Br-GO is further employed as a HTM to enhance the hole extraction and to decrease the energy level difference between the perovskite layer and the carbon electrode, resulting in a remarkably suppressed charge recombination and energy loss of PSCs. Finally, a champion PCE of 10.10% is acquired for the device with a construction of FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}/CsPbBr_{3} + Br-GO/Br-GO/Carbon  $  , which is highly increased in comparison with the 6.28% efficiency for the reference   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSC. Furthermore, the optimal PSC without encapsulation presents a dramatically elevated stability after storing at 25 °C, 85% RH air ambient over 30 days and illuminating under AM 1.5G for 120 h. The introduction of Br-GO provides a promising method that not only obtains a high-quality perovskite film, but also effectively boosts charge migration, thereby significantly improving the performance of PSCs.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Materials and reagents

Lead bromide (PbBr $ _2 $ , 99.0%), cesium bromide (CsBr, 99.9%), Tetra-n-butyl titanate, AR,  $ \geq $ 98.0%, Isopropanol, AR, Dimethylformamide, AR,  $ \geq $ 99%, Hydrobromic acid, AR. Carbon paste was purchased from Shanghai Mater Win New Materials Co. Ltd, All the chemicals were used without further purification.

# 2.2. Synthesis of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  paste

The   $  TiO_{2}  $   colloid used in this work was prepared by a sol–gel method. The specific process was as follows: 10 mL of tetra-n-butyl titanate was slowly added dropwise to 100 mL of deionized water, and stirred at room temperature for 30 min to completely hydrolyze the tetra-n-butyl titanate to obtain a white powder. Subsequently, 10 mL of acetic acid and 0.8 mL of nitric acid were slowly added dropwise in turn to the above white powder, and stirred at 80 °C for 15 min to form a viscous emulsion. After adding 160 mL of deionized water, a light blue transparent liquid was obtained. The liquid was transferred to a high-pressure hydrothermal kettle, heated at 200 °C for 12 h, then cooled to room temperature. 0.4 g of P25 was next added, and after ultrasonic dispersion for 30 min, hydrothermal treatment was performed again at 200 °C for 12 h. After cooling to room temperature, 0.8 g of polyethylene glycol and 1 mL of OP solution (  $  V_{OP}:V_{isopropanol} = 1:10  $  ) was added, and concentrated for several hours to obtain 40 mL of   $  TiO_{2}  $   colloid.

# 2.3. Preparation of brominated graphene oxide:

Graphene oxide (GO) was prepared according to Hummers method   $  [28]  $  . Then, 250 mg of GO and 10 mL of hydrobromic acid were mixed and placed in a stainless steel autoclave lined with Teflon and heated at 240 °C for 5 h. After stirring for 20 min at room temperature to evaporate unreacted bromine, the product was washed with deionized water. Afterwards, the reaction mixture was separated on the nylon membrane by means of suction filtration, and washed with deionized water and methanol three times.

# 2.4. Assembly of all-inorganic perovskite solar cells

0.5 M ethanol solution of isopropyl titanate was spin-coated on a clean etched FTO substrate at a spin speed of 7000 rmp for 30 s, and then calcined in a muffle furnace at 500 °C for 2 h to obtain a dense TiO₂ layer (c-TiO₂). The mesoporous titanium dioxide (m-TiO₂) layer was prepared by spin-coating a titanium dioxide colloid at a spin rate of 2000 rmp for 30 s and calcining at 450 °C for 30 min. Next, the obtained c-TiO₂/m-TiO₂ was immersed in a 0.04 M TiCl₄ solution, heated at 75 °C for 30 min, washed with deionized water and ethanol, and then calcined at 450 °C for 30 min. For the preparation of perovskite film, 1 M DMF solution of PbBr₂ with different concentration of Br-GO (0.02, 0.04, 0.06 and 0.08 mg mL⁻¹ as 0.02, 0.04, 0.06 and 0.08 Br-GO, respectively) was ultrasonically dispersed at 40 kHz 400 W for 2 h and spin-coated onto m-TiO₂ layer at 2000 rpm for 30 s, and then dried at 90 °C for 30 min. After cooling the above PbBr₂ film to room temperature, 90 μL methanol solution of CsBr (0.07 M) was dropped and spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 30 s, immediately transferred to a heating table, and annealed at 250 °C for 5 min. This step of spin-coating CsBr was repeated eight times until a uniform and dense yellow CsPbBr₃ perovskite film was produced. The Br-GO HTM was prepared by spin-coating 10 mg mL⁻¹ isopropanol suspension of Br-GO on the perovskite film and then drying at 120 °C for 15 min in air. Finally, a certain amount of carbon slurry was coated on the perovskite film by a spatula and dried at 120 °C for 10 min to assemble an all-inorganic CsPbBr₃ PSC with an active area of 0.09 cm².

In order to explore the element chemical state of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film with Br-GO addition, high-resolution XPS of Cs, Pb, Br and O are carried out and illustrated in Fig. 3a-d. The reduction of the internal recombination reaction in PSCs, mainly including the trap-dominated non-radiative recombination and bimolecular radiative recombination, is indispensable for obtaining high-performance device. Hence,  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  and  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  as a function of light intensity are characterized to explore the photo-generated carrier recombination kinetics of the PSCs. The dependence of  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  on the incident light intensity (I) is fitted based on the power law equation:  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}} \propto I^{q} (\alpha \leq 1)  $ , where  $  \alpha  $  represents the ideal factor associated with the bimolecular recombination [46]. It can be found from Fig. 6a that the  $  \alpha  $  value of the  $  \mathrm{CsPbBr_3 + 0.04 Br - GO}  $  based device reaches 0.998, which is much closer to unity than 0.983 of the control PSC, indicating that the bimolecular radiative recombination inside the PSCs with 0.04 Br-GO addition is effectively decreased, that is, the reaction of photo-generated electrons-hole pairs are suppressed to more conducively separate and extract photogenerated charge. The well-matched energy level and high hole mobility of  $  \mathrm{CsPbBr_3 + 0.04 Br - GO}  $  film together with the superior p-type behavior of Br-GO are responsible for the remarkably eliminated bimolecular radiative recombination of electrons-hole pairs. At the same time, the relation between  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  as a function of I is shown in Fig. 6b and the curves are fitted in the light of the Shockley-Read-Hall recombination formula:  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}} = nkT\ln(I)/q + \text{constant}  $ , where  $  k  $  represents the Boltzmann constant,  $  T  $  is the absolute temperature,  $  q  $  is the elementary charge and  $  n  $  is the ideal factor related to the monomolecular recombination mechanism, the smaller the value of  $  n  $ , the less the degree of the trap-dominated non-radiative recombination [50]. # 4. Conclusion

In summary, an effective additive and interface engineering of incorporating Br-GO with high p-type behavior and suitable WF into perovskite film and perovskite/carbon interface is launched to fabricate high-performance   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs. The addition of Br-GO in perovskite not only delays the crystallization process to form a large-grained   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film but also passivates the positively charged trap states, causing a dramatically retarded carrier recombination. A promotion of charge extraction is also realized attributing to the improved hole mobility and energy-level alignment of   $  CsPbBr_{3} + Br-GO  $  . Upon further setting Br-GO as a HTM to effectively enhance hole extraction ability and reduce interfacial energy loss, the all-inorganic solar cells with a configuration of FTO/  $  c-TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}/CsPbBr_{3} + Br-GO /Br-GO/Carbon  $   achieves a champion PCE up to 10.10%, yielding an increase by 60.83% in comparison with the efficiency of 6.28% for the control cell. Additionally, the optimized device without encapsulation maintains 90% of the original efficiency after being continuously exposed to 85% RH in the air for 30 days and presents an outstanding photo-stability under AM 1.5G illumination for 120 h. This work provides a promising method for the PCE and stability improvement of all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs by introducing multifunctional Br-GO to increase the quality as well as the charge mobility of perovskite film and also to passivate the defects and boost charge separation and extraction.
