# Is Excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   Beneficial for Perovskite Solar Cell Performance?

Fangzhou Liu, Qi Dong, Man Kwong Wong, Aleksandra B. Djurišić,  $  ^{*}  $  Annie Ng, Zhiwei Ren, Qian Shen, Charles Surya, Wai Kin Chan, Jian Wang, Alan Man Ching Ng, Changzhong Liao, Hangkong Li, Kaimin Shih, Chengrong Wei, Huimin Su, and Junfeng Dai

# 1. Introduction

Unreacted lead iodide is commonly believed to be beneficial to the efficiency of methylammonium lead iodide perovskite based solar cells, since it has been proposed to passivate the defects in perovskite grain boundaries. However, it is shown here that the presence of unreacted   $  PbI_{2}  $   results in an intrinsic instability of the film under illumination, leading to the film degradation under inert atmosphere and faster degradation upon exposure to illumination and humidity. The perovskite films without lead iodide have improved stability, but lower efficiency due to inferior film morphology (smaller grain size, the presence of pinholes). Optimization of the deposition process resulted in   $  PbI_{2}  $  -free perovskite films giving comparable efficiency to those with excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   (  $  14.2 \pm 1.3\%  $   compared to   $  15.1 \pm 0.9\%  $  ) Thus, optimization of the deposition process for   $  PbI_{2}  $  -free films leads to dense, pinhole-free, large grain size perovskite films which result in cells with high efficiency without detrimental effects on the film photostability caused by excess   $  PbI_{2}  $  . However, it should be noted that for encapsulated devices illuminated through the substrate (fluorine-doped tin oxide glass,   $  TiO_{2}  $   film), film photostability is not a key factor in the device degradation.

For a comprehensive investigation of the effect of   $  PbI_{2}  $ , it is necessary to consider separately the effects of the decomposition of the perovskite layer, previously proposed to be the major contributing factor to device degradation,  $  ^{[17]}  $   and the overall device stability.

# 2. Results and Discussion

To investigate the effect of excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   on the performance and stability, we have prepared perovskite films under different preparation conditions to obtain films with (sample S1) and without   $  PbI_{2}  $   residue (samples S2, S3, and S4, prepared under different experimental conditions). Since the main cause of the device degradation was found to be the decomposition of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   layer,  $  ^{[17]}  $   we have performed a comprehensive study of the stability of the perovskite layer under different atmospheres (  $  N_{2}  $  ,   $  N_{2}  $   with 40% relative humidity (RH),   $  N_{2}/O_{2}  $   (25%   $  O_{2}  $  ),   $  N_{2}/O_{2}  $   with 40% RH, and ambient air with 40% RH) and subjected to different illumination conditions (no illumination, simulated solar illumination with and without UV-blocking filter), as shown in Supporting Information, Figures S1–S11. Since the   $  PbI_{2}  $   was prepared from an N,N-dimethyl formamide (DMF) solution which is known to result in crystalline   $  PbI_{2}  $   films,  $  ^{[38]}  $   (also confirmed by absorption measurements, Supporting Information, Figure S12), XRD was used as the main characterization tool to assess the film composition. Figure 1 shows the XRD pattern of samples S1 and S2 on glass and   $  TiO_{2}  $   substrates stored in nitrogen and exposed to simulated solar illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). The peaks corresponding to   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   and   $  PbI_{2}  $    $  ^{[33]}  $   can be identified. It can be observed that the sample containing   $  PbI_{2}  $   residue is unstable under illumination even under inert atmosphere. Thus, these samples are likely to exhibit stability problems regardless of the encapsulation due to intrinsic instability of the films containing a small amount of excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   despite their good initial device performance with average power conversion efficiency of 15.1%, as summarized in Table 1. However, it should be noted that there is a possible problem with perovskite sample stability in vacuum, resulting in the loss of methylammonium.  $  ^{[34]}  $   Furthermore, the proposed decomposition mechanisms  $  ^{[3,16,34]}  $   mainly differ in the proposed volatile reaction products. Neither of the proposed mechanism implies any role of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   in the decomposition. Different from previous work,  $  ^{[16]}  $   we observe degradation of the perovskite under illumination even under inert atmosphere. Thus, the observed degradation process under illumination and in inert atmosphere which occurs only in samples containing excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   is likely due to the photodecomposition of   $  PbI_{2}  $  ,  $  ^{[39]}  $   with the reaction products further reacting with the perovskite and resulting in its degradation. The degradation process is further accelerated on   $  TiO_{2}  $   substrates, likely due to photogenerated charge transfer at   $  TiO_{2}  $ /perovskite interface.

To further test this hypothesis, we have prepared samples without excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   and examined their degradation under illumination in the cases when droplets of   $  PbI_{2}  $   or   $  I_{2}  $   in isopropanol were placed on the samples (samples with pure isopropanol droplets were also prepared to ensure that solvent did not affect the perovskite film). Under exposure to simulated solar illumination in ambient air (60% RH), obvious degradation occurred in the area where   $  PbI_{2}  $   or   $  I_{2}  $   solution droplets were placed, as shown in Figure 3. The amount of   $  PbI_{2}  $   was low enough not to be detectable by XRD, i.e., lower than that in sample S1. Nevertheless, in these samples as well the illumination in inert atmosphere for 12 h resulted in the appearance of   $  PbI_{2}  $   peak (see Supporting Information, Figure S3). It is well known that methylammonium lead iodide perovskite is unstable in the presence of a liquid electrolyte containing iodide/triodide redox couple.  $  ^{[1,3,40,41]}  $   From the known corrosion of the perovskite in the presence of iodine  $  ^{[1,3,40,41]}  $   and the photoinstability of   $  PbI_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[39]}  $   combined with the evidence of enhanced degradation of the films under illumination in the area where excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   or   $  I_{2}  $   were applied, we can conclude that the likely degradation mechanism is described by previously proposed Equations (2) and (3),  $  ^{[3]}  $   with   $  I_{2}  $   originating from the photodecomposition of   $  PbI_{2}  $   rather than a reaction between photogenerated electrons in   $  TiO_{2}  $   and the perovskite.

This indicates that the   $  TiO_{2}/perovskite  $   interface is affected by illumination, as proposed previously.  $  ^{[15]}  $

Thus, photostability of the film is only one aspect which can possibly affect the device stability. Therefore, we have tested the stability of the encapsulated cells in ambient and under illumination. We have considered two testing conditions, one is alternating illumination (10 h) and dark (14 h) periods while recording but not controlling the temperature and humidity (60% RH) to simulate realistic outdoor condition. It can be observed that both S1 and S3 devices exhibit good stability, and for both devices with alternating light/dark periods we can observe some recovery of the efficiency upon illumination on the next day. It is expected that aged cells would have higher density of defects and traps and thus may exhibit improvement in performance upon illumination after storage in the dark. It should be noted however that there is no increase in efficiency on illumination for devices at 85 °C.

While the trends observed in S1 and S3 are the same, S1 exhibits slightly better performance, in particular at elevated temperature. To examine the reasons for this, we have first compared the effect of the illumination of the film directly and the illumination through the substrate. Obtained results are shown in Supporting Information, Figures S25 and S26. The illumination through fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/Glass substrate with   $  TiO_{2}  $   film attenuates the short wavelengths and thus considerably slows down the photodegradation, especially considering the fact that the devices are encapsulated and thus not exposed to significant levels of moisture. Since the difference between S1 and S3 devices is more pronounced at elevated temperature, we have also examined thermal stability of the films. Obtained results for thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) are shown in Supporting Information, Figure S27, and the obtained curves are in good agreement with literature reports.  $  ^{[42,43]}  $   The onset of the weight loss of the organic component occurs at lower temperature in S3 samples, and there is a more pronounced tail in the part of the curve corresponding to the thermal decomposition of   $  PbI_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[43]}  $   Thus, we can conclude that there are small differences in the thermal stability of the two samples, which can be expected with more pronounced device stability difference at elevated temperature. From the fact that S3 films have significantly better photostability compared to S1 films, but S1 devices exhibit small increase in stability compared to S3 devices in particular at elevated temperatures, we can conclude that other processes play a more significant role in device degradation compared to photostability (partly due to the attenuation of the short wavelength part of the spectrum by FTO glass/  $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate). Both devices exhibit good stability after encapsulation, and it takes more than a week of ambient exposure at 60% RH and 10 h of 1 sun illumination per day for efficiency to drop below 90% of the initial one. At 85 °C, there is a more rapid initial drop in the efficiency in the first several hours, after which the degradation proceeds at significantly slower rate, similar to a previous work involving elevated temperature and high humidity.  $  ^{[17]}  $   It was also previously reported that   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based perovskite solar cells degraded to 85% of their initial efficiency after 5h of illumination with a UV filter (435 nm cutoff).  $  ^{[15]}  $   Improved stability compared to previous work is possibly due to a different encapsulation technique. In a previous report, encapsulation method had a significant influence on the degradation of the perovskite cells in high

humidity under illumination and subjected to thermal stress.  $  ^{[17]}  $   After 20 h of illumination at 55 °C ambient temperature and 80% of RH, cells encapsulated by a simple method completely degraded, while for more advanced encapsulation the efficiency dropped below 40% of the initial value but the cells were still operational after 80 h.  $  ^{[17]}  $

# 3. Conclusion

We have demonstrated that a small amount of excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   has detrimental effects on the perovskite film stability, even though perovskite devices with excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   can exhibit high initial efficiency. The presence of a small amount of excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   results in the degradation of the perovskite layer under illumination even when stored in inert atmosphere, and it results in faster degradation upon exposure to ambient and illumination compared to the perovskite films without excess   $  PbI_{2}  $ . The perovskite film preparation procedure affects both the morphology of   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer and the resultant perovskite layer, with entire process having high sensitivity to minute details, such as covering the drying sample with a Petri dish. Optimization of the perovskite film preparation procedure can result in   $  PbI_{2}  $ -free perovskite films with comparable efficiency to that of films containing a small amount of excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   while having significantly improved film stability. However, perovskite film photostability appears to be a minor contributor to the overall device degradation, and encapsulated devices with excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   can still exhibit good stability.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: Titanium (IV) isopropoxide (TTIP, ≥97.0%), lead iodide (PbI₂, 99%), bis (trifluoromethane) sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI, 99.95%), 4-tert butylpyridine (96%), chlorobenzene (≥99.5%), and acetonitrile (anhydrous, 99.8%) were purchased from Sigma Aldrich. DMF (HPLC grade) and acetic acid glacial (GR grade) were purchased from Duksan Pure Chemical. MAI was purchased from Dyesol, and isopropanol (anhydrous, 99.5+) was obtained from Alfa Aesar, 2,2',7,7'-Tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) was purchased from Shenzhen Feiming Co., Ltd.

Experimental details: All samples were prepared on glass for degradation experiment, and patterned FTO/glass for device fabrication. The substrates were cleaned by sonication in acetone, toluene, acetone, ethanol, and deionized water in sequence, and dried in nitrogen flow.   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer was deposited onto the substrates by spin-coating a precursor solution (50   $  \mu  $  L of TTIP and 50   $  \mu  $  L of acetic acid dissolved in 2 mL of ethanol) at 3000 rpm followed by a ramped sintering at 450 °C for 3 h. Perovskite films were prepared by a two-step procedure  $  ^{[4]}  $   on the glass substrates as well as on   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layers. The preparation of precursor solutions, syntheses of perovskite films, and spin-coating of hole transporting material (HTM) solution were all performed in an Ar filled glove box (MBraun Labstar, <0.1 ppm   $  H_{2}O  $  , <0.1 ppm   $  O_{2}  $  ). For the precursor solutions,   $  PbI_{2}  $   was dissolved in DMF at a concentration of 1 M while stirring at 70 °C. MAI was dissolved in anhydrous isopropanol at a concentration of 30 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  .   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer was spin-coated onto the substrate at 2500 rpm for 30 s. To obtain perovskite films with and without excess   $  PbI_{2}  $ , four different procedures for handling the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film were employed before spin-coating MAI solution, and the corresponding samples were labeled as S1, S2, S3, and S4, respectively. In the common procedure which resulted in excess   $  PbI_{2}  $ ,   $  PbI_{2}  $   films were annealed at 70 °C for 30 min immediately after spin-coating, and these samples are labeled S1. Perovskite degradation experiments were conducted in the following conditions at room temperature: inert environment (pure   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere) with low humidity (3 ppm   $  H_{2}O  $  ),   $  N_{2}  $   (75%) /   $  O_{2}  $   (25%) mixed atmosphere with low humidity (3 ppm   $  H_{2}O  $  ),   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere with 40% RH,   $  N_{2}/O_{2}  $   mixed atmosphere with 40% RH, and ambient environment (with 40% and 65% RH, respectively). Controlled atmosphere experiments were performed in a vacuum chamber with the pressure pumped down to   $  \approx10^{-3}  $   torr followed by introducing the pure   $  N_{2}  $   or   $  N_{2}/O_{2}  $   mixed flow to a pressure of 600 torr. To obtain desired humidity of the environment, water vapor was vented into the chamber and the RH inside the chamber was monitored by a hygrometer. Experiments in ambient environment were conducted in a dry cabinet with adjustable humidity. The films were exposed to simulated solar illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) with or without UV blocking filter or kept in the dark. Further evaluation of the effect of illumination was performed by illuminating the as-prepared S1 and S3 films placed face up and face down under AM 1.5G illumination at ambient condition (26 °C/ 60% RH). In addition, to study whether excess PbI  $  _{2}  $   can accelerate the degradation of perovskite film, small amounts of isopropanol solutions of PbI  $  _{2}  $   (5 μL, 10 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) or I  $  _{2}  $   (5 μL, 0.2 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) were applied to as-prepared S3 perovskite films on TiO  $  _{2}  $  /FTO substrates. Pure isopropanol droplet (5 μL) was also applied as a control sample. The films were then dried at 70 °C for 5 min, followed by exposure to AM 1.5G illumination at ambient condition (26 °C/ 60% RH). XRD characterization was performed using a Bruker D8 advance diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda=0.154184  $   nm). TGA was performed by a Q50 Thermal Gravimetric Analyzer. TRPL signals of perovskite films were determined using a Chameleon Compact OPO pumped by Ti:Sapphire Oscillator as an excitation source, with the excitation wavelength, pulse frequency, pulse duration, and average power of 532 nm, 80 MHz, 140 fs, and 0.15 mW, respectively. The signal was detected by an Si detector with a resolution of 0.004 ns. Grazing incidence X-ray diffraction was performed using a Rigaku SmartLab X-ray diffractometer. Absorption spectra of PbI  $  _{2}  $   films were obtained by a Cary 50 Bio UV–vis spectrophotometer.

The solar cell performance measurements were performed in ambient on encapsulated devices. The devices were encapsulated according to the schematic diagram shown in the Supporting Information, Figure S18. The active area of the cells are protected by a 1 mm cover glass from Marienfeld and sealed with UV curable resin from ThreeBond. A piece of desiccant (180   $  \mu  $  m, Dynic Corporation) was placed below the cover glass before curing the epoxy. Current–voltage characteristics of the devices were obtained using a B1500A semiconductor device analyzer with Oriel Sol3A solar simulator equipped with an AM 1.5 filter. The illumination source was calibrated at 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  . The J-V curves were obtained

at a step of 0.01 V in the region from 1.2 to -0.2 V with a delay time of 0.01 s. External quantum efficiency (EQE) was measured by a Newport Oriel IQE-200 quantum efficiency measurement system at a scanning rate of 10 nm s  $  ^{-1}  $  . The stability of the S1 and S3 devices were tested both at room temperature and at 85 °C in ambient, with ABET Sun 2000 solar simulator equipped with an AM 1.5 filter as the light source. The RH and temperature were monitored during the testing. The irradiance level was adjusted at 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  . For testing without heating (ambient conditions 26 °C/60% RH), a light-dark cycle was applied to simulate the actual outdoor operation, and the devices at open circuit were exposed to the irradiance for 10 h and kept in the dark for 14 h, and the PCE of the devices was measured at specified intervals. For the stability testing at 85 °C, the devices at open circuit were continuously illuminated at 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $   for 36 h on a hot plate at 85 °C, and the PCE was measured at specified intervals.
