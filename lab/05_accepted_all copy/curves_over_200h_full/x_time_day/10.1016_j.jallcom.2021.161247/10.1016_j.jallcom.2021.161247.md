# 1. Introduction

With decade of the first report, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been dramatically skyrocketed from 3.8% to 25.5%, revealing very promising potential for commercial application   $  [1-4]  $  . The excellent photovoltaic performance is owing to their high optical absorption   $  [5]  $  , high carrier mobility   $  [6]  $   and long charge carrier diffusion lengths   $  [7]  $  . Nevertheless, the long-term instability of perovskite at moisture environments (85%) or high temperature (120 °C) has become a critical problem to be solved. The main source of the instability is the inevitable ionic nature of perovskite materials. On the other hand, their solution-based fabrication processes commonly lead to form plenty erodible defects at the grain boundaries and surfaces of perovskite film. # ABSTRACT

Organometallic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with high efficiency have attracted enormous attention for commercialization. However, the perovskite undergoes rapid degradation in humid condition, leading to serious decline in both device performance and stability. Herein, an extremely cheap, environmentally friendly and nature cellulose derivative with plentiful hydrogen bonds, cellulose acetate (CA), is introduced into perovskite precursor solution to serve as bifunctional green additive. The hydrogen bonding interactions between the perovskite and CA could passivate the defects at the grain boundaries to suppress the non-radiative recombination, improving the open-circuit voltage and power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSC. Besides, the long polymer chain of CA can eliminate the annealing-induced lattice tensile/compressive strain during the perovskite film fabrication under ambient environment to enhance its stability. As a result, the dense and defect-less morphology of CA-modified perovskite film with large sized grains, passivated grain boundaries and ideal surface coverage is obtained. The optimized PSC achieves an impressive 19.53% PCE and negligible hysteresis of the photocurrent. Moreover, the thermal stability and humidity resistance of CA-modified PSC are both significantly improved. This strategy provides a promising method for advancing the PSCs application and commercialization.

Recently, tremendous efforts from worldwide research groups have made to improve the efficiency and stability of PSCs, such as interfacial engineering [11], solvent engineering [12] and deposition process [13]. Commonly, because of the powerful chemical bonding between additives and perovskite, additive engineering is an effective method to assist the perovskite crystal growth and passivate defects at the grain boundaries, consequently improve the photovoltaic performance and stability of devices. Several kinds of additives have been reported, such as metal halide salts [14], small organic molecules [15], nanoparticles [16] and polymers. Among them, polymer additives have some unique merits like the low volatility, the ability to localize at grain boundaries enabled by their flexible long molecule chains and the capability to form reliable interactions with perovskite due to the rich functional groups. In this work, we introduce the earliest commercial cellulose derivative, Cellulose Acetate (CA), into perovskite precursor solution as bifunctional additive to form hydrogen bonding interaction with perovskite grains during crystallization and serve as a scaffold for the perovskite lattice during annealing. The normalized PCE of CA-modified device (non-encapsulated) could still maintain 82.60% after placed in 85% relative humidity (RH) for 15 days in the dark. This simple and green approach for fabricating high efficiency and well stability PSCs in ambient environment advances the PSCs application and commercialization [23,24].

# 2. Experimental methods

# 2.1.Materials

The glass substrates which etched fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) were purchased from Advanced Electronic Technology Co., Ltd. The Titanium diisopropoxide bis (acetylacetonate) (75 wt% in isopropanol) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. CH₃NH₃I (MAI) and PbI₂ were purchased from Kunshan Sunlaite New Energy Technology Co., Ltd. Cellulose acetate was purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. 2,2',7,7'-Tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxy-phenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD), Tris(2-(1 H-pyrazol-1-yl)-4-tert-butylpyridine)-cobalt(III)Tris(bis-(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide)) (FK 209 Co(III) TFSI salt), Lithium bis (trifluoromethanesulphonyl)imide (LiTFSI), 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corporation. Other materials were supplied by Aladdin Reagent (Shanghai) Co., Ltd.

# 2.2. Solution preparation

Firstly, the powder of FK 209 Co(III) TFSI salt and LiTFSI are dissolve separately in acetonitrile with concentration of 170 mg/ml and 150 mg/ml, as the additive of Spiro-OMeTAD solution later. Secondly, the solution of Titanium diisopropoxide bis (acetylacetonate) (75 wt % in isopropanol) was diluted to 150 mg/ml by absolute ethanol. Thirdly, the perovskite precursor solution (dissolving 1 M equimolar ratio of PbI  $  _{2}  $  and MAI in 0.6 ml N,N-Dimethylformamide (DMF) solvent) was compound with 0 mg/ml (pristine), 0.1 mg/ml CA, 0.5 mg/ml CA and 1.0 mg/ml CA. All kinds of the perovskite precursor solution were heated in 60 °C and stirred in 300 rpm for 2 h before using. Finally, the 65 mg Spiro-OMeTAD powder were dispersed in 1 ml chlorobenzene with 20 μl tBP, 50 μl FK 209 Co(III) TFSI salt solution and 70 μl LiTFSI solution as additives.

# 2.3. Device fabrication

The FTO-etched glass substrates were cleaned through ultrasonic treatment with acetone, ethanol and isopropyl alcohol for 20 min, respectively. After drying at 115 °C, the substrates were treated with ultraviolet (UV) ozone for 20 min to eliminate organic compounds on the surface of substrates. Then, the electron-transport layer (ETL),

TiO  $  _{2}  $  layer, was spin-coated on the clean FTO substrate [25,26]. After gradually raising the temperature to 450 °C (5 °C/min), the substrate was continuously annealed for 40 min. Afterwards, the perovskite precursor solution with or without CA was spin-coated on the TiO  $  _{2}  $  layer at 2000 rpm (revolutions per minute) for 5 s and 4000 rpm for 30 s in turn. Then the thermal treatment with 100 °C for 40 min was carried out to remove residual solvent from deposited layer, assist perovskite formation from its precursors, and enhance grain crystallization [27]. Especially, ethyl acetate was used as anti-solvent in the production process. After that, the spiro-OMeTAD solution as hole-transport layer (HTL) was spin-coated on the perovskite layer at 4000 rpm for 25 s. To oxidize Spiro-OMeTAD post deposition, the HTL-coated substrates were kept overnight in a desiccator before depositing the counter electrode [28,29]. Finally, approximately 70 nm thickness of Ag electrode were deposited via thermal evaporation on the top. The complete structure of perovskite solar cell (Glass/FTO/TiO  $  _{2}  $  /MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /spiro-OMeTAD/Ag) is exhibited in Fig. S1. Notably, the whole fabrication of well performance device was in ambient environment (RH ≈ 50%) without glove box. Hence, the extremely low-cost cellulose acetate additive and fabrication without glove box could greatly reduce the manufacturing costs and facilitate the process of commercialization.

# 2.4. Characterization

The morphology of MAPbI $ _3 $  films were investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Ultra55, ZEISS). The  $ ^{1} $ H nuclear magnetic resonance spectra were obtained in a solution of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) d $ _6 $  by using a high-resolution liquid NMR spectrometer ( $ ^{1} $ H NMR, Bruker AVANCE AV400MHz). The crystal structures of perovskite films were recorded by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Thermo ARL-X TRA) with Cu K $ \alpha $  radiation ( $ \lambda $  = 1.5418 Å). The absorption spectrum was measured by UV-Visible spectrophotometer (Lambda 900). The transient-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) were analyzed by PG2000-Pro-EX spectrophotometer (Shanghai Ideaoptics Corporation) and transient fluorescence spectrometer (FLS980, Edinburgh Instruments), respectively. The Fourier Transform Infrared Spectrometer (FTIR, Nicolet 5700) measurements were carried out in the transmittance mode. The elemental orbit was investigated by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, K-Alpha). Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was obtained by an electrochemical workstation (Zennium Pro). The current density–voltage (J–V) characteristics of the PSCs were recorded by a source meter (Keithley 2400-SCS) under AM 1.5 illuminations with intensity of 100 mW/cm $ ^2 $  Water-contact-angle images of perovskite films were obtained by a contact-angle instrument (Krüss Optronic).

# 3. Results and discussion

Significantly reducing the defects and traps of perovskite film is an indispensable issue for the well performance of PSCs. Herein we introduce an extremely low-cost, green and rich in hydrogen bonds cellulose derivatives, cellulose acetate (CA), into the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  precursor solution as morphology-modified agent. Fig. 1(a) demonstrates the chemical structure of CA, which has polymer long-chain formed via a six-membered heterocyclic unit containing hydroxyl and acetate functional groups. As indicated in Fig. 1(b), the  $ \mathrm{H^{+}} $  of  $ -\mathrm{NH}_3^+ $  in  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3^+ $  ( $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $ ) and  $ \mathrm{O^{-}} $  of  $ -\mathrm{OH} $  in CA could form strong hydrogen bonding interaction. Besides, it is also prospective that  $ \mathrm{I}^- $  in  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  forms a hydrogen bond with  $ \mathrm{H^{+}} $  of  $ -\mathrm{OH} $  in CA. The two kinds of hydrogen bonding can passivate the grain boundaries, decrease the trap density, and co-modify the morphology of perovskite film, resulting in forming cross-linked perovskite grains with less grain boundaries [30]. Therefore, the crystallinity of the perovskite layers is carefully investigated by XRD and demonstrated in Fig. 4(a) and (b). For pristine perovskite film, it reveals that the peaks correspond to the reflections from the (110), (220) and (310) planes of the orthorhombic space group (14/mcm), locating at  $ 14.20^{\circ} $ ,  $ 28.55^{\circ} $  and  $ 32.08^{\circ} $ , respectively. Hence, the lower angle and lager interplanar spacing indicate the larger lattice space of CA-modified perovskite. The XRD outcome is corresponding to the hypothesis of 'CA scaffold' as revealed in Fig. 1(c). The ultraviolet visible (UV-Vis) spectra in Fig. 4(c) check the light absorption ability of two perovskite films. Notably, the thickness of perovskite films is nearly the same and the light absorption is enhanced through CA modification. Thus, the light-harvesting capacity of the CA modified perovskite film is improved and mainly owing to the higher quality (enlarged grain size and dense morphology) of perovskite absorber. The stronger films' light absorption ability would bring about more effective sunlight utilization and seem to be directly related to the higher short circuit current  $ (J_{sc}) $  of ultimate PSCs [42-44]. Besides, the UV-Vis spectra of the various CA modified perovskite films are presented in Fig. S6. For evaluating the charge dynamics of the perovskite films with CA additive, the PL and TRPL spectra are measured. The detailed structure of tested samples is displayed in Fig. 4(d). Fig. 4(e) demonstrates the PL spectral of two samples. It's apparent that the PL intensity of CA-modified perovskite film is significantly enhanced compared to that of the pristine film. The obvious PL improvement provides a strong evidence for the suppressed non-radiative recombination [45,46]. To study more about the charge-carrier dynamics, the Time-resolved PL (TRPL) decay of perovskite films is employed. As displayed in Fig. 4(f), CA-modified  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film leads to a longer non-radiative charge decay lifetimes, implying the reduction in trap-mediated bulk or surface recombination. The fitted parameters of TRPL spectra are demonstrated in Table 1. All experimental data can be well-fitted to biexponential decay function  $ I(t) = I_0 + A_1e^{(-t - t_0 / \tau_1)} + A_2e^{(-t - t_0 / \tau_2)} $ . Where  $ A_{1} $  and  $ A_{2} $  are the decay amplitudes,  $ \tau_{1} $  is closely related to nonradiative recombination by traps/defects and  $ \tau_{2} $  is a component of radiative recombination from perovskite [47,48]. Moreover, the average decay lifetime could obtain from the equation  $ \tau_{avg} = (\sum_iA_i\tau_i^2) / (\sum_iA_i\tau_i) $ . The TRPL results demonstrate that the high quality of CA-modified perovskite film lead to reduction in the trap-assisted recombination. And this reduction increases average decay lifetimes from 68.72 ns to 336.71 ns. The suppressed non-radiative recombination could lead to fewer energy loss and higher open circuit voltage ( $ \mathbf{V}_{oc} $ ) of PSC.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, a novel cellulose derivative, Cellulose Acetate (CA), is introduced into perovskite precursor solution as bifunctional additive to fabricate excellent photovoltaic performance and well stability of PSCs under air environment without glove box. Firstly, the CA contains numerous hydrogen bonds, which could form hydrogen bonding interaction with perovskite grains to passivate defects during crystallization. On the other hand, the long polymer chain of CA serves as a scaffold for the perovskite lattice during annealing. As a result, the CA-modified perovskite films display dense and flat morphology with larger sized (the maximum achieve to 1.1   $  \mu  $  m), cross-linked grains. Furthermore, the passivated grain boundaries, defect-free film and ideal surface coverage, which caused via CA, could remarkably enhance the photovoltaic performance of PSCs. The optimized PSC achieves a dramatic 19.53% PCE and negligible hysteresis of the photocurrent. Moreover, both the thermal and moisture stability of CA-modified PSC are significantly improved. The CA-modified perovskite film displays no obvious degradation in absorbance after placed for 200 h in the dark at 85 °C. The normalized PCE of CA-modified device (non-encapsulated) can still maintain 82.60% after placed in 85% relative humidity for 15 days in the dark. Overall, this study provides a viable approach for advancing commercialization of PSCs arising from the green, cheap and nature additive, simplified fabrication under ambient environment and well stability.
