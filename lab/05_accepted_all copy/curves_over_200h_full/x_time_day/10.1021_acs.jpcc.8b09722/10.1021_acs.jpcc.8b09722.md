# INTRODUCTION

Organic/inorganic trihalide lead-based perovskite materials have been widely employed in solar cells as light absorption and charge transport layer, given their remarkable potential in optoelectronic applications  $  ^{1}  $  . MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite possesses superiorities over other photovoltaic materials, such as easy manufacturing by solution processing  $  ^{1,2}  $  , strong photon absorption and long carrier diffusion length (up to micrometers  $  ^{3-5}  $  ). Many efforts have been devoted to engineer perovskite layers to improve the photovoltaic properties of PSCs  $  ^{6-10}  $  . As a result, the efficiency of perovskite-based solar cells has quickly reached 23.3% in a few years  $  ^{11}  $  . Although perovskite solar cells have made a rapid advancement, some challenges still exist for large-scale industrial applications, including the toxicity of Pb and low tolerance against moisture/light/heat.

Among those disadvantages, instability in the humid environment is a critical issue for its practical large-scale application.  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  can be easily decomposed upon exposure to moisture in the atmosphere $ ^{12-15} $ . The methyl ammonium cation  $ (\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3^+) $  can diffuse out from the lattice and react with water when exposed to air $ ^{12,13} $ . Generally speaking, there are four routes to improve the anti-moisture stability of perovskite films, such as material engineering on the active layer $ ^{1,16,17} $ , optimizing the interface between the active layer and HTL $ ^{18-21} $ , utilizing inorganic HTL $ ^{22-25} $  materials, and ameliorating morphologies and properties of the surface of the overall device $ ^{26,27} $ . However, the methods mentioned above always lead to unfavorable drop in energy conversion efficiency. Enhancing the moisture tolerance of MAPbI $ _3 $  without sacrificing PCE at the same time is the focus of the present work.

Here we demonstrate an effective way to improve the moisture tolerance of perovskite films, via optimal molecular engineering of the interface between perovskite and HTL. To achieve this goal, only the molecules that meet the following requirements can be chosen: i) the molecules must have anchor groups that can bind to surface of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ ; ii) the molecules should hardly impact the performance of solar cells, as well as the interfacial charge transfer; iii) the molecules must be harmless in the formation of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer. After systematic screening, an optimal molecule, 1-Donecyl Mercaptan (NDM), can satisfy all the demands mentioned above. # METHODS

# Perovskite Film Deposition

The perovskite film was fabricated in ambient air by using the two-step method  $  ^{28}  $  . Firstly, the annealed   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticle film was coated with   $  PbI_{2}/PbCl_{2}  $   by spin-coating 0.5 M   $  PbI_{2}  $   (99.999%, Aldrich) and 0.15 M   $  PbCl_{2}  $   (98%, Aldrich) in N',N-dimethylformamide (99.7%) at 4500 r.p.m. for 60 s and dried at 100 °C for 15 min. After depositing   $  PbI_{2}/PbCl_{2}  $   twice, the perovskite film was made by spin-coating 10 mg/mL   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (Dyenamo) in 2-propanol at 1200 r.p.m. for 6 s and 4500 r.p.m. for 60 s and dried at 100 °C for 10 min.

To investigate the effect of interface engineering on the performance of PSCs, the photocurrent density-voltage   $  (J-V)  $   curves (Figure 2a) and the time evolution of the energy conversion efficiency (Figure 2b) of PSCs were measured under 1.5 AM illumination. Surprisingly, both efficiency and stability of PSCs were enhanced by introduction of NDM molecules. Firstly, the modified PSCs (blue line) showed a significant increase in device performance (Figure 2a). The key parameters of the best devices, including short-circuit current   $  (J_{sc})  $  , open-circuit voltage   $  (V_{oc})  $  , fill factor   $  (FF)  $   along with the power conversion efficiency (PCE,   $  \eta  $  ) are listed in Table 1. The   $  J_{sc}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF of the modified device were all higher than that of the device adopting pristine perovskite. We can infer that the addition of NDM molecules is beneficial to the charge transport at the interface or the morphology of perovskite films.

Secondly, NDM may suppress the electron-hole recombination at the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/\mathrm{HTL} $  interface, leading to an increased  $ J_{sc} $ . The simplified NDM molecules with a shorter carbon chain length ( $ \mathrm{NDM}^* $ ) were adopted.

When the -SH group interacts with  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $ , the spatial distribution of the electron density near the Fermi level is more delocalized due to the competition in binding  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  between -SH and  $ \mathrm{I}^{-} $ . Since the VBM of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  is dominated by  $ \mathrm{I}^{-} $ , the introduction of -SH could weaken the electronegativity of  $ \mathrm{I}^{-} $ , thus lowering the VBM of perovskite.

Considering that NDM molecules were inserted between the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  slab and the HTL in PSCs, photogenerated holes might inject from the valence band of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  to the HOMO of HTL. The lowered VBM will accelerate the hole injection at the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/\mathrm{HTL} $  interface following the Shockley equation:

$$
J = J _ {S} \left[ x p \left(\frac {q V}{k _ {0} T}\right) - 1 \right], \frac {E _ {F} ^ {n} - E _ {F} ^ {q}}{q} = V
$$

The higher energy difference will induce larger photocurrent at the interface by accelerating the injection of carriers. Consequently, modification with NDM at the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/\mathrm{HTL} $  interface could effectively improve the photocurrent of PSCs by accelerating the hole injection process, which offsets the negative impact of the enlarged spacing between  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and HTL by the adsorption of NDM molecules. In addition, the enlarged spacing may suppress the electron-hole recombination process at the interface.

NDM molecules binding to Pb  $  ^{2+}  $  of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  favor a better crystallization of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  layer, enhancing the efficiency of light harvesting and anti-moisture stability.
