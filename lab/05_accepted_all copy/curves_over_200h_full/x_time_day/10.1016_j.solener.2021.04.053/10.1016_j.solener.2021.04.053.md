# 1. Introduction

All-inorganic   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   (X = I, Br) perovskite materials have drawn tremendous attention because of their overwhelming thermal stability as compared to organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials (Heo et al., 2013; Conings et al., 2015; Liang et al., 2016; Ripolles et al., 2016). Similar to their hybrid counterparts, the band gap of   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   can be modulated successively from 1.73 eV (  $  CsPbI_{3}  $  ) to 2.30 eV (  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  ) by tuning the ratio of I/Br (Hu et al., 2017; Kulkarni et al., 2014; Lia-shenko et al., 2019). In spite of its suitable band gap,   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskites suffer from poor phase stability under environmental conditions (Luo et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2017; Li et al., 2018a). By contrast,   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskites exhibit good stability while its band gap is too broad to obtain wide light absorption range (Liang et al., 2016; Duan et al., 2018; Li et al., 2018c). As compared to the above two all-inorganic perovskites, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite exhibits a balance between the phase stability and band gap (  $  \sim  $  1.91 eV) (Han et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2019; Dong et al., 2018; Yan et al., 2018).

In recent years, researchers have adopted various strategies to maximize the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite solar cells (PSCs), and impressive improvements have been achieved (Tian et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2019b; Yin et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2019; Xiang et al., 2019). However, most efficient   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs are constructed with unstable and/or expensive organic hole-transporting materials (HTMs) in accompany with noble metal electrode. Carbon electrode based HTM free   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs (C-PSCs) thus stand out for its

Carbon electrode based hole transporting materials (HTM) free   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite solar cells have advantages of excellent thermal stability and low cost. However, it is still a challenge to provide an effective strategy to realize highly efficient and stable HTM-free devices. Herein, a small amount (0.2–0.5% vs Pb source) of magnesium acetate   $  (\mathrm{Mg}(\mathrm{Ac})_{2})  $   was introduced into the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution. It was demonstrated that the carbonyl groups of   $  \mathrm{Mg}(\mathrm{Ac})_{2}  $   can bind with all intrinsic elements of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , leading to improved crystallinity and passivation of defects. Therefore, the power conversion efficiency of HTM-free devices was increased from 10.84% to 13.08%, along with an enhancement in device stability.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  precursor solution was obtained by dissolving 2.0 mL of tetrabutyl titanate (  $  >99.8\%  $  , Aladdin) in 0.8 mL of diethanolamine (  $  >99\%  $  , Aladdin) and 14.0 mL of anhydrous ethanol. A mixed solution of 0.4 mL  $  H_{2}O  $  and 3.0 mL ethanol was then added dropwise.

To prepare perovskite precursor solution, 207.8 mg CsI (99.999%, Alfa), 184.4 mg PbI  $  _{2}  $  (99.9%, Aladdin) and 146.8 mg PbBr  $  _{2}  $  (99.999%, Alfa) were dissolved in 1 mL mixing solvent of DMF (0.8 mL, 99.8%, Aladdin) and DMSO (0.2 mL, 99.9%, Aladdin). For perovskites with Mg(Ac)  $  _{2}  $  (99.9%, Aladdin) additives, Mg(Ac)  $  _{2}  $  with 0.2–0.5% proportions were directly added into the above solution. Next, the mixture was stirred at 70 °C for 2 h to obtain a clear solution. Before spin coating, a 0.45 μm filter was used to filter the solution.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

The FTO substrate was cleaned in an ultrasonic cleaner with detergent, deionized water, acetone, and ethanol for 15 min in sequence. After being dried with nitrogen flow, substrates were immediately treated in a UV-ozone cleaner for 15 min.   $  TiO_{2}  $   precursor solution was then spin-coated (3000 rpm, 30 s) onto the cleaned FTO substrate. Subsequently, substrates were annealed at 500 °C for 1 h to obtain compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   (c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) layer. After being cooled to room temperature,   $  FTO/c-TiO_{2}  $   substrates were transferred into glove box. To fabricate   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films, 70 μL precursor solution was dripped onto the UV-ozone treated   $  FTO/c-TiO_{2}  $   substrate and was spin-coated at 500 rpm for 8 s and 2000 rpm for 45 s. During the spin-coating, 150 μL IPA (  $  \geq99.9\%  $  , Aladdin) was quickly dripped to wash the precursor film after 22 s spin-coating of the second procedure. The prepared   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor film was transferred onto a hotplate and annealed at 280 °C for 10 min. To complete the fabrication of the device, a carbon electrode was printed onto the surface of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film and heated at 120 °C for 15 min. After being cooled to room temperature, the device was sealed with a glass and UV-curable adhesives.

# 2.3. Device characterization

X-ray diffraction measurement was performed on a TD-3700 X-ray diffractometer (Dandong Tongda Technology Co., Ltd.). The voltage and current of the Cu target X-ray tube are set to 30 kV and 20 mA, respectively. Absorption spectra of perovskite films were measured using a Shimadzu UV-2600 spectrophotometer. The surface morphology of perovskite films were obtained via a scanning electron microscope (PhenomPro). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films were performed on a PHI-5702 spectrometer. All binding energies were calibrated by C 1s peak of 284.6 eV. Time-resolved photoluminescence measurements were performed on a fluorescence lifetime spectrometer (Hamamatsu, C12132-37), and samples were excited with a 532 nm pulsed laser. To obtain current-voltage curves, solar cells were illuminated with an AM 1.5G solar simulator (94023A, Newport), whose intensity was calibrated to 100 mW/cm² via a standard Si solar cell. During the illumination, electrical signals were recorded by Keithley 4200 digital source meter. The active area of devices is defined as 0.045 cm² with a metal mask. The external quantum efficiency spectrum was obtained using a QE-R666 system (Enlitech) without white light bias. EIS and C-V curves were measured on an electrochemical station (CHI660E, China) in the dark. The frequency range for EIS test is set to the range of 0.01 Hz to 1 MHz with a voltage offset of 0.8 V. The voltage range for C-V measurement is from -0.5 V to 1.3 V with a frequency of 1000 Hz.

# 3. Results and discussion

The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution was prepared by dissolving CsI,   $  PbI_{2}  $ , and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   with a molar ratio of 2:1:1 in the mixed solvent of DMF/DMSO. To obtain   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_{2}}  $ -containing precursor solution, the   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_{2}}  $   powder was added directly into the above solution. The targeted   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films were obtained by annealing the spin-coated precursor films at 280 °C. The thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) indicated that the thermal decomposition temperature of   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_{2}}  $   is higher than the annealing temperature of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films (Figure S1) (McAdie and Jervis, 1970). The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were first measured to explore the effects of   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_{2}}  $   addition on the crystallinity and phase structure of perovskite films. As shown in Fig. 1a, all films exhibited strong (100) plane and (200) plane signals at   $  \sim14.8^{\circ}  $   and   $  \sim29.8^{\circ}  $ , respectively (Parida et al., 2019). Besides, no non-perovskite phase was detected. Close examination of the (200) peak (Fig. 1a) discerns no obvious shift in the peak position. This result suggests that   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_{2}}  $   might be enriched at the surface of grains instead of being incorporated into the crystal lattice (Han et al., 2020; Gong et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2019a). In term of the peak intensity,   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_{2}}  $ -containing films exhibited slightly higher intensity than the reference one (Figure S2). Moreover, the full width half maximum (FWHM) of (100) and (200) peaks were calculated (Table S1). As compared to the reference film, films with   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_{2}}  $   rendered smaller FWHM, indicating the positive effect of   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_{2}}  $   addition on crystallinity. As reported in previous work, improved crystallinity may result in enhancement in absorption (Zai et al., 2018). Therefore, UV–vis absorption measurements were conducted and results were displayed in Fig. 1b. As observed, the reference and   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_{2}}  $ -containing films presented similar absorption edge, along with the nearly the same band gap (  $  \sim1.925  $   eV) as obtained from Tauc plots. However, the absorbance was slightly increased upon the   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_{2}}  $   addition which is in consistent with the improvement in crystallinity.

As shown in Fig. 2a-e, top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images were measured to reveal the effects of  $ \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2} $  addition on the film morphology. All films were pin-hole free, which is in favor of blocking the direct contact between anode and cathode. However, the average grain size decreased gradually from  $ 1.20\mu \mathrm{m} $  for the reference film to  $ 0.65\mu \mathrm{m} $  for the film with  $ 0.5\% $   $ \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2} $  (Figure S3). The nuclei density is critical to grain size. For example, our group found that the grain size increased with the content of perovskite phase in the precursor film (Dong et al., 2018). To check whether the  $ \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2} $  addition have effect on the content of precursor film, we measured the UV-vis spectra of precursor films. As shown in Figure S4, the spectrum of precursor film without  $ \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2} $ , which exhibited an apparent absorption edge at around  $ 620~\mathrm{nm} $ , had similar characteristic as compared to the annealed film. By fitting the Tauc plot of the precursor film without  $ \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2} $ , a band gap of  $ 1.982\mathrm{eV} $  was obtained, which is larger than that of the annealed film ( $ \sim 1.925\mathrm{eV} $ ). This result implies that composition of the perovskite phase in the precursor film is different from the annealed film. It should be due to the fact that the incorporation of bromine ion into the perovskite crystal is prior to iodide ion because of stronger complexation ability of bromine ion to lead ion. Upon annealing, iodide ion will then be incorporated into the perovskite crystal. Therefore, blue shifted band edge of precursor film is observed (Xie et al., 2019). As compared to precursor film without  $ \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2} $ , the precursor film with  $ \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2} $  exhibited much lower absorbance and no apparent perovskite absorption characteristic was observed, indicating much less perovskite phase in the film. Above results implies that the addition of  $ \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2} $  suppresses the nucleation during the spin-coating process, and the rapid "burst" of nucleation upon annealing results in reduced grain size. In contrast, the pre-existed nucleus in the precursor film without  $ \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2} $  have more chance to grow bigger during annealing. It has been previously reported that molecular additives exit their influence on crystallization kinetic via the interaction with the component of perovskite (Yu et al., 2020; Yuan et al., 2021). EQE is the ratio between the collected carriers and the incident photons. Accordingly, larger EQE value may be due to the increased absorbance of the perovskite film as reported previously (Zhang et al., 2018; Zeng et al., 2018b). However, the contribution of improved charge carrier transporting can't be exclude. Therefore, the charge transporting in the device was studied in the following section.

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were performed to further investigate the electrical properties of the devices. To extract the recombination resistance   $  \left(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{rec}}\right)  $  , the equivalent circuit illustrated in Fig. 5a was used to fit the obtained Nyquist plots (Guo et al., 2019). As summarized in Table S2, a much larger   $  R_{rec}  $   (83 kΩ vs 51 kΩ) was obtained for the device with 0.4%   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2}  $   than the one for the reference device, suggesting the suppressed charge carrier recombination in the former (Zeng et al., 2018b; Ye et al., 2019). This improvement in charge carrier transporting in the device may originate from the prolonged carrier lifetime of the   $  CsPbI_2Br  $   film and/or the enhanced carrier transporting at the interface (Chen et al., 2018). Therefore, the time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra were first measured to compare the carrier lifetime of the reference and 0.4%   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2}  $   films. As shown in Fig. 5b, both spectra were fitted by a biexponential function, from which a fast decay lifetime   $  \tau_{1}  $   and slow decay lifetime   $  \tau_{2}  $   can be extracted. According to previous works,   $  \tau_{1}  $   is due to the trap-assisted recombination, and   $  \tau_{2}  $   is related to the radiative recombination in the film (Yang et al., 2018b; Wang et al., 2019b). Thus, reduction in recombination centers is expected to prolong the carrier lifetime. As listed in Table S3, relatively short   $  \tau_{1}  $   (0.72 ns) and   $  \tau_{2}  $   (3.26 ns) were recorded for the reference film. Upon the addition of 0.4%   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2}  $  , the value of   $  \tau_{1}  $   and   $  \tau_{2}  $   increased to 2.20 ns and 5.12 ns, respectively. It thus indicated that the addition of   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2}  $   can effectively reduce the defect density in the   $  CsPbI_2Br  $   film, which in turn leads to weak charge carrier recombination (Duan et al., 2018; Ye et al., 2019; Zhao et al., 2018; Chang et al. (2016); Zhao et al., 2017). Fig. 5c illustrates the   $  C^{-2}-V  $   plots obtained from C-V measurements on those two devices. According to the Mott-Schottky equation:   $  1/C^{2}=2(V_{\mathrm{bi}}-V)/q\varepsilon v_{0}A^{2}N  $  , where V is the applied voltage, q is the elementary charge,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative permittivity of   $  CsPbI_2Br  $  ,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity, A is the active area of the device, and N is the doping concentration, respectively, the built-in electric field (  $  V_{bi}  $  ) can be obtained by prolonging the linear part of   $  C^{-2}-V  $   plots to cross the horizontal axis. The determined   $  V_{bi}  $   is 1.06 V for the device with 0.4%   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2}  $   and 0.89 V for the reference device. The relatively higher   $  V_{bi}  $   means a stronger driving force for charge separation at the interface, which favors the sufficient carrier collection (Zhao et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2015; Wang et al., 2018). In brief, above results reveal that the   $  \mathrm{Mg(Ac)_2}  $   addition can simultaneously suppress the recombination in the   $  CsPbI_2Br  $   film and at the interface, and thereby
