# Fully Inorganic   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   Mesoporous Perovskite Solar Cells with High Efficiency and Stability via Coadditive Engineering

Huaxia Ban, Tao Zhang, Xiu Gong, Qiang Sun, Xiao-Li Zhang, Nuttapol Pootrakulchote, Yan Shen, and Mingkui Wang*

The small optical bandgap of a   $  CsSnI_{3}  $  -based inorganic perovskite film makes it a hopeful candidate as an absorber layer in solar cell applications. Herein, a coadditive 2-aminopyrazine (APZ) in the precursor solution to form   $  SnF_{2}-APZ  $   complex with the aim to restrain   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation and thus improve the   $  CsSnI_{3}  $  -based device performance is proposed. It is found that the amino group of APZ significantly suppresses oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   through a Lewis acid–base addition reaction. Consequently, the   $  CsSnI_{3}  $  -based mesoporous perovskite solar cells that use a printable c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/NiO  $  /carbon framework with high reproducibility achieve a power conversion efficiency of 5.12%. This is a champion efficiency for fully inorganic   $  CsSnI_{3}  $  -based mesoporous devices reported up to now. Furthermore, after 60 days of storage in a   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glovebox, the device still maintains an initial efficiency of 92%.

Lately, the perovskite-structured inorganic   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   compound with ideal bandgap (  $  \approx1.31~eV  $  ) was initiated for

efficient photovoltaic applications.  $  ^{[7,8]}  $   The environmentally friendly   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   has become a particularly promising option for optoelectronic device applications, not only due to its crystal structure similar to   $  CsPbI_{3}  $ , but also its strong light-absorption ability.  $  ^{[12-15]}  $   However, the inferior stability and low device performance of   $  CsSnI_{3}  $ -based PSCs have been widely observed, which originate mainly from the chemical instability of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   component and nonuniform perovskite crystalline layers.  $  ^{[16,17]}  $   As an essential inhibitor of   $  Sn^{4+}  $ ,   $  SnF_{2}  $   has been reported to decrease the conductivity of tin-based perovskites and alleviate the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $ .  $  ^{[18]}  $     $  SnF_{2}  $   also acts as a tin compensator to supply   $  Sn^{2+}  $ .  $  ^{[19]}  $   So, this additive is commonly used for fabrication of tin-based PSCs nowadays. However, it was found   $  SnF_{2}  $   can induce severe phase separation of   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   crystallization due to an inhomogeneous dispersion during the evolution process of thin films. Consequently, the presence of micrometer-sized   $  SnF_{2}  $   aggregation is easily observed, which is believed to significantly reduce the smooth surface and interrupt the corresponding device performance, for instance. This is one of the main causes for lower PCE values of Sn-based PSCs than Pb-based PSCs.  $  ^{[20-22]}  $   Therefore, it is a challenge for fabricating a dense and uniform   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   thin layer without phase separation in the presence of   $  SnF_{2}  $   as additive.

# 1. Introduction

Recently, thermally stable Pb-based halide inorganic perovskites have received considerable interest within the field of photovoltaic conversion because of their potential of replacing the easily degraded organic–inorganic hybrid perovskites.  $  ^{[1-5]}  $   Theoretically, the maximum power conversion efficiency (PCE) of a perovskite solar cell (PSC) using a single absorber with a bandgap of about 1.34 eV can reach 33.4% under 1 sun light illumination.  $  ^{[6]}  $

# 2. Results and Discussion

Currently,   $  CsSnI_{3}  $  -based solar cells still suffer from chemical instability and limited PCE mainly due to the easy oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   to   $  Sn^{4+}  $  . Adding   $  SnF_{2}  $   into a precursor solution is a common method to restrain   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation and thus improve the device performance. We first fabricated   $  CsSnI_{3}  $  -based solar cells with   $  TiO_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/NiO/carbon  $   architecture in the presence of   $  SnF_{2}  $   to rapidly evaluate the impact of   $  SnF_{2}  $   on mesoporous-structured device performance. We found that the devices' PCE increased from a low value of 0.95% for the pristine device to 1.82% when 20 mol%   $  SnF_{2}  $   was used (Figure S1, Supporting Information). This demonstrates the importance of   $  SnF_{2}  $   additive in enhancing device performance of   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   devices. However, it is prone to impede solvents' penetration in the mesoporous framework, after dropping this solution onto the prepared substrate, due to an inhomogeneous dispersion of   $  SnF_{2}  $   in precursor solution. During the thermal annealing procedure,   $  SnF_{2}  $   tends to aggregate on the surfaces or boundaries of   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   grains and thus therefore damages film morphology. This might be the reason why the PCE of   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   mesoporous PSCs is relatively low.

To date, only a few open data on the application of   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   in solar cells are available, mainly due to the challenge of manufacturing uniform films. In this study, we demonstrated an effective approach to enable homogeneous spreading of   $  SnF_{2}  $   in perovskite films, so as to achieve uniform films via the secondary addition of APZ to form   $  SnF_{2}-APZ  $   complex in the precursor solution (Figure 1a). One of the main reasons for the poor stability of tin-based PSCs is the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   to   $  Sn^{4+}  $  . This may easily produce Sn vacancies and lead to p-type self-doping effect, which can cause severe nonradiative recombination.  $  ^{[30]}  $   McGehee and coworkers suggested the detailed pathways of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation for tin-based perovskites as follows.  $  ^{[31]}  $

$$
2 \mathrm{ASnI} _ {3} + \mathrm{O} _ {2} \rightarrow 2 \mathrm{AI} + \mathrm{SnO} _ {2} + \mathrm{SnI} _ {4}
$$

(1)

where A stands for the cation including   $  Cs^{+}  $  . The APZ may donate a pair of electrons via its amino group to the Lewis acids (i.e.,   $  SnO_{2}  $  ,   $  SnI_{4}  $  ,   $  SnI_{2}  $  , and/or   $  SnF_{2}  $   in this study). Considering that   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnI_{4}  $   (with tetravalent tin) have higher acidity than tin halide (with divalent tin), the former offers a higher possibility for Lewis acid–base addition reaction.   $  {}^{1}H  $   NMR characterization in Figure S4b (Supporting Information) shows a clear chemical shift for the proton at position A of the amino group in both APZ/  $  SnI_{4}  $   and APZ/  $  SnF_{2}/CsSnI_{3}  $   samples compared with APZ only. This indicates a strong chemical interaction between the amino group of APZ and   $  Sn^{4+}  $  . Furthermore, FTIR characterization in Figure 2c shows that the C–N stretching vibration peak at   $  1305\ cm^{-1}  $   of APZ has an obvious red-shift to   $  1278\ cm^{-1}  $   in both APZ-  $  Sn^{4+}  $   and APZ-  $  CsSnI_{3}-SnF_{2}  $  . As the vibrational frequency is proportional to the square root of the force constant in the harmonic motion of the diatomic model, the reduction in the vibration of the Lewis base (amino group) signifies the bond strength between the amino group (nitrogen), and carbon decreases upon the formation of APZ and   $  Sn^{4+}  $  , suggesting a strong Lewis acid–base addition reaction between   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   perovskite compound and the amino group of APZ (Figure 2a).  $  ^{[32]}  $   We first stored unencapsulated   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   films without and with APZ addition in ambient environment at disparate times to certify the impact of APZ on inhibiting   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation. As shown in Figure S7, Supporting Information, the   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   film with APZ nearly kept its initial color (black) after 3 h of exposure to ambient air and turned into yellow slightly after 6 h, whereas the film without APZ became yellow completely after 6 h of exposure to ambient air. This indicates that APZ can inhibit the   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation effectively in the   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   film. We further conducted X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) characterization of the   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   films (containing 20 mol%   $  SnF_{2}  $  ) without and with

APZ addition. We noted that   $  Sn^{2+}  $   could transform to   $  Sn^{4+}  $   rapidly (within a few minutes) when the as-prepared   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   films are exposed to air during samples' preparation and measurements.  $  ^{[20]}  $   Figure S8a and S8b, Supporting Information, exhibit the XPS spectra, where the deconvolution of four peaks at 487.4 and 495.8 eV and 486.6 and 495.0 eV are related to   $  Sn^{4+}  $   and   $  Sn^{2+}  $ , respectively. We assessed the effect on APZ addition on the suppression of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation by estimation of the area ratio of   $  Sn^{4+}/Sn^{2+}  $ , which decreased from 68.2% in the absence of APZ to 4.7% in the presence of APZ on the surface, indicating that due to the alkaline environment provided by APZ, the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   was effectively inhibited.

Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) was conducted on   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   films (including 20 mol%   $  SnF_{2}  $  ) with and without APZ on top of quartz substrates to understand the effect of APZ on the trap states. The laser was irradiated with a wavelength of 532 nm in PL measurements. The PL intensity of the   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   film containing APZ was increased by   $  \approx690\%  $   compared with that of the film without APZ, which indicates that the density of defect states in the perovskite film is reduced and the suppression of trap-induced nonradiative recombination is enhanced (Figure 3a). Figure 3b shows the time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra of the   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   and   $  CsSnI_{3}  $ -APZ samples. The decay curves are fitted with a double-exponential decay equation.

$$
Y = A _ {1} \exp (- t / \tau_ {1}) + A _ {2} \exp (- t / \tau_ {2})
$$

(2)

where the shorter   $  \tau_{1}  $   represents the rapid lifetime component attributed to the quenching of charge carriers on the surface, whereas the longer   $  \tau_{2}  $   corresponds to trap-assisted nonradiative recombination.  $  ^{[33-35]}  $   The corresponding parameters are shown in Table S1, Supporting Information. The average PL decay lifetime of the   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   sample was calculated to be 3.41 ns. After incorporating APZ, the average PL lifetime was increased dramatically to 8.58 ns. This means that the charge recombination in the perovskite film caused by trap states is suppressed effectively.

As shown in Figure S9, Supporting Information, we observed that the   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   films (containing   $  SnF_{2}  $  ) fabricated without and with APZ exhibited a nuanced absorption edge at about 947 nm in the UV–vis absorption spectra, corresponding to the bandgap with a subtle difference (Figure S10, Supporting Information). Figure 3c shows an interesting phenomenon of an obvious absorption at an absorption edge beyond 950 nm without adding APZ, which indicates the transformation involving Sn vacancy-related intermediate deep trap states. As reported, tiny electronic states' vacancy band just below the conduction band can be induced by the high vacancy content, which is in accordance with the narrower bandgap of the film without APZ addition.  $  ^{[36-38]}  $   However, the   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   film with APZ addition has almost no absorption in the same region, verifying that coadditive engineering strongly decreases the concentration of Sn vacancies compared with   $  SnF_{2}  $   only. A decrease in density of Sn vacancies leads to a reduction in nonradiative recombination caused by them.

The stability of tin-based PSCs is the key to replace Pb-based PSCs. To verify the application effect of this coadditive strategy, we carried out some stability measurements with and without APZ addition. Figure 5a shows the steady-state output of the  $ \mathrm{CsSnI}_3 $ -APZ device at the maximum power point in air. A stabilized output of  $ \approx 4.75\% $  was exhibited toward the  $ 300~\mathrm{s} $  scan, which is on the verge of PCE value from its typical  $ J - V $  curve, indicating that the device performance is reliable. We then monitored the PCE evolution of unencapsulated devices without and with APZ addition stored in a  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $ -filled glovebox. The APZ-incorporated solar cell exhibited good long-term stability during the storage within 60 days and maintained  $ 92\% $  of its initial efficiency, as shown in Figure 5b. In contrast, the device without APZ showed an obviously reduced PCE with the increase in storage time. For the encapsulated devices stored in air environment (humidity:  $ 60\% $  and temperature:  $ 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ), the device containing APZ additive maintained  $ \approx 90\% $  PCE of its original value after  $ 100\mathrm{h} $  of storage, whereas the device without APZ had almost no PCE after  $ 70\mathrm{h} $  of storage (Figure 5c). We also noticed that both  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  and  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  degraded more slowly in the APZ-containing device than in the device without APZ, indicating that the nonradiative recombination rate is slower in the former.[52] Furthermore, the encapsulated APZ-incorporated device maintained  $ 85\% $  of its PCE under continuous illumination in air after  $ 120\mathrm{min} $  of storage, whereas the device without APZ only maintained  $ 19\% $  of its original value in the same time (Figure S20, Supporting Information). We also fabricated  $ \mathrm{CsSnI}_3 $ -based PSCs using the mesoscopic  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / Al_2O_3 / NiO / carbon} $  framework with pyrazine or APZ addition, respectively, to compare the device performance between them. Supporting Information, the devices with APZ addition presented higher PCE, EQE, longer storage stability, and better reproducibility than the devices with pyrazine, demonstrating that adding APZ is more useful to inhibit   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation and achieve high-performance   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   solar cells.

# 3. Conclusion

To sum up, we proposed coadditive engineering strategy (APZ together with   $  SnF_{2}  $   in this study) to obtain high-performance   $  CsSnI_{3}  $  -based solar cells. This coadditive engineering strategy enables a homogeneous dispersion of   $  SnF_{2}  $   by forming a complex with APZ and significantly suppresses the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   through the Lewis acid–base addition reaction with the aid of the amino group in APZ, providing salutary effects on inhibiting the unwanted   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation and ameliorating the surface morphology. The   $  CsSnI_{3}  $  -based PSCs with the mesoscopic metal–oxide framework presented the champion PCE of 5.12% with long-term stability and good reproducibility. Benefitting from the retarded   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation and the uniform perovskite film after adding APZ, the defect density and charge recombination rate were reduced dramatically. Our research provides an effective method for the realization of high-performance and environmentally sustainable   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   PSCs.
