# Solution processed nanostructured p-type NiO electrode for efficient inverted perovskite solar cell

Sawanta S. Mali  $  ^{a}  $   Hyungjin Kim  $  ^{a}  $  , Sang Eun Shim,  $  ^{b}  $   and Chang Kook Hong  $  ^{a*}  $

Here we report, the solution processed nanostructured NiO as a hole transport layer (HTL) for the efficient inverted  $ \mathrm{MAPb(I_{1 - x}Br_x)_3} $   $ (x = 0.3) $  perovskite solar cells (PSCs). The best performing p-i-n PSC exhibits  $ 15.32\% $  power conversion efficiency with current density  $ (J_{\mathrm{SC}}) $   $ 19.85\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , open circuit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{OC}}) $  1.056V and fill factor (FF) 0.73. The developed method is simple and cost effective.

Development of inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on various organometallic halide perovskite materials, hole transport layers (HTL) and electron transport layers (ETL) have been applied.  $  ^{1-5}  $   The poly (3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrene sulphonate (PEDOT:PSS) is mostly promising HTL for inverted type perovskite solar cells. It is known that p-type metal oxides demonstrate much higher carrier mobility and superior stability than the organic materials.  $  ^{6,7}  $   NiO has higher hole mobility (  $  10^{-5}  $   to   $  10^{-3}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $  V  $  ^{-1}  $  s  $  ^{-1}  $  ) and shows higher open circuit voltage than PEDOT:PSS. Recently few reports were demonstrated that, pulsed laser deposited (PLD)  $  ^{8}  $   and sputtered  $  ^{9}  $   techniques have been used for the synthesis of p-type NiO HTL for inverted PSCs with 17.3% and 11.6% respectively. However, nanostructured p-type NiO metal oxides can also be processed easily via solution at low temperatures. The solution method is simple and cost effective and can be easily control the HTL thickness.

As per as PSCs concern, methylammonium mixed halide (MAPblx) and formamidinium lead iodide (FAPblx) have achieved 16.2% $  ^{3}  $  and 21.1% $  ^{2}  $  power conversion efficiencies (PCE), respectively. Moreover, negligible hysteresis via the optimization of each layer, an extremely uniform and dense structure via toluene dripping, tuning of the band gap via composition variation, a long diffusion length (>100 nm) and a high absorption coefficient $  ^{3-7}  $  are key features of perovskite materials. The variety of n-type metal oxides ETL such as

blocking titanium oxide (BI-TiO $ _2 $ ), $ ^{10} $  mesoporous titanium oxide (mp-TiO $ _2 $ ), $ ^{11} $  Y:TiO $ _2 $ , $ ^{12} $  ZnO, $ ^{13} $  SnO $ _2 $ , $ ^{14} $  Zn $ _2 $ SnO $ _4 $   $ ^{15} $  and BaSnO $ _3 $   $ ^{16} $  and poly(3-hexylthiophene-2,5-diyl) (P3HT), $ ^{17} $  2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-pdimethoxyphenylamino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD), $ ^{18} $  poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA), $ ^{19} $  CuI, $ ^{20} $  CuSCN $ ^{21} $  as a HTL have been used for regular mesoporous and planar structured PSCs. While PEDOT:PSS, p-type NiO as a HTL (or electron blocking later) and C $ _{60} $ , its derivatives like [6,6]-phenyl C $ _{61} $  butyric acid methyl ester (PC $ _{61} $ MB), $ ^{22} $  PC $ _{71} $ BM have been used as a ETL for inverted p-i-n type PSCs. However, polymer based HTL are suffered formation low energy and relatively low charge mobility compared to p-type metal oxides. On the other hand, optimization of each layer with maintaining their charge mobility and electron balance becomes typically necessary steps in the fabrication of efficient inverted p-i-n perovskite solar cells. $ ^{5,8,9} $  There were two device configurations were used for metal oxide ETL p-i-n type based perovskite solar cells. The p-i-n type inverted perovskite has been divided into two categories. Firstly the metal oxide ETL and C $ _{60} $  or PCBM as HTL. $ ^{23-29} $  While second type containing p-type metal oxide as HTL and n-type metal oxide as ETL for inverted type perovskite solar cells. $ ^{30} $  Detailed literature survey has been discussed in Table S1. Wang et al. used p-type mesoscopic NiO and demonstrated 9.51% PCE for first type inverted PSCs. $ ^{26} $  Kim et al. developed a solution-processed Cu:NiO as a HTL and demonstrated 15.40% efficiency. $ ^{27} $  developed On the other hand, Chen et al. synthesized heavily p-doped Ni $ _x $ Mg $ _{1-x} $ O and n-doped TiO $ _x $  by chemical process and used respectively HTL and ETL for PScs with 16.2 % PCE. $ ^{30} $  Yang et al. demonstrated the influence of a monolayer surface modification of NiO nanocrystal layer using ethanolamine and demonstrated 15.90% efficiency. $ ^{31} $  Recently You et al. demonstrated solution processed p-type NiO and n-type ZnO for p-i-n inverted PSCs with 16.1% PCE. $ ^{32} $  However, these two reports are very different from the current investigation.

To the best of our knowledge, there is no single report based on solution processed nanocrystalline NiO (as HTL) and PCBM (as ETL) for inverted p-i-n type perovskite solar cells

Scheme I Synthesis of NiO nanoparticles for inverted p-i-n type PSCs via solution process.

Scheme 1 shows the simple methodology for synthesis of NiO thin film via reflux method. The desired amount of ethanolic precursor solution containing nickel diacetate tetrahydrate (Ni(CH₃COO)₂·4H₂O) and HCL was refluxed at 60°C for 2h. The green coloured solution was allowed to cool at room temperature for another 30min. The resulting clear green colored solution was further filtered by using syringe filter (Whatman GD/X PVDF pore size 0.45μm). The clear filtered solution was spin-cast onto ITO substrate at a spin speed 3,000 r.p.m. for 30s followed by annealing at 300°C for 90min in ambient air. In the present investigation we have used only (Ni(CH₃COO)₂·4H₂O as a NiO precursor and dissolved in the ethanol. However, (Ni(CH₃COO)₂·4H₂O cannot be dissolved completely in ethanol. The solution becomes turbid. Most of the previous reports suggest that, they have used mixed solvents such as nickel formate dehydrate in ethylene glycol solution containing ethylenediamine,  $  ^{23}  $   2-Methoxethanol (C₃H₈O₂) and monoethanolamine (C₂H₇NO, MEA),  $  ^{33}  $   nickel acetate tetrahydrate in ethanol with monoethanolamine,  $  ^{34}  $   nickel(II) nitrate hexahydrate (Ni(NO₃)₂·6H₂O) in ethylene glycol/ethylenediamine  $  ^{32}  $   solution as a solvent and stabilizer. However, in our case we have used only single precursor therefore in order to stabilize the precursor solution with addition of 0.5 ml conc. HCl. After the addition of HCl, our (Ni(CH₃COO)₂·4H₂O/ethanol solvent becomes very transparent which is most important for the complete dissolution of nickel precursor. Furthermore, the clear and transparent solution will be helpful for the deposition of uniform and highly dense NiO thin film. The possible general chemical reaction takes place as follows

In conclusion, we have successfully synthesized p-i-n type inverted perovskite solar cells based on nanostructured highly dense and smooth NiO thin films. Our results revealed that, the thickness of p-type NiO HTL player play a key role in enhancing the performance of inverted perovskite solar cells. The champion cell having 75nm thin NiO HTL exhibited 15.32% PCE with   $  J_{SC}=19.85\ mAcm^{-2}\ V_{OC}=1.06V  $   and FF=0.73. The NiOx HTL based devices show much higher stability than PEDOT:PSS based PSCS. We believe that the present method is free from sophisticated equipment like PLD or sputtering and HTL thickness can be control easily.
