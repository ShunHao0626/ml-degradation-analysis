# A Dopant-Free Zwitterionic Conjugated Polyelectrolyte as a Hole-Transporting and Interfacial Material for Perovskite Solar Cells

Yihong Huan, Chao Tan, Bo Wu  $  ^{*}  $  Xingcui Feng, Wenting Xu, Deqing Gao  $  ^{*}  $

Keywords: perovskite solar cells, zwitterionic conjugated polyelectrolyte, hole-transporting material, double-layer capacitance, interface

Abstract: Achieving rapid extraction and equivalent transport of charge carriers is an effective way to improve the performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). In this report, a thiophene-based zwitterionic conjugated polyelectrolyte (POWT) is introduced into PSCs as a hole-transporting and interfacial material. The polyelectrolyte has high hole mobility of  $ 5.74 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~cm}^2 \mathrm{~V}^{-1} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} $  (similar to that of PTAA) and compatible covalence level relative to the perovskite. Terminated with a zwitterionic pair of  $ a $ -amino acid, POWT layer builds up a weak double-layer capacitance at the interface which is not strong enough to induce the migration of  $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $  ions in the perovskite layer. Deep electrical study on the PSC with the structure of ITO/POWT/FA $ _{0.2} $ MA $ _{0.8} $ PbI $ _{2.9} $ Br $ _{0.1} $ /C $ _{60} $ /BCP/Ag discloses that the device has low carrier transfer resistance, low leakage current density and minor interfacial charge accumulation. The open-circuit voltage and short-circuit current density are much improved, and the power conversion efficiency (PCE) is up to  $ 17.5\% $ . With  $ a $ -amino acid zwitterions, POWT passivates the surface charge defects and grain boundaries of the perovskite film. The PSC presents negligible hysteresis and high stability. After 56 days, the unencapsulated PSC still remains  $ 85\% $  of the original efficiency.

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved rapid development since their introduction in 2009  $  ^{[1]}  $  and power conversion efficiency (PCE) has exceeded 25%.  $  ^{[2-4]}  $  Due to the excellent properties of the perovskite light-absorbing layer,  $  ^{[5,6]}  $  such as broad absorption spectrum, good charge-transporting characteristics and low-temperature solution processability, PSCs are considered as the most promising new thin film photovoltaic technologies. The hole-transporting layer (HTL) in the perovskite solar cells extracts the photogenerated holes from the perovskite layer, transfers them to the anode, and blocks the electron injection. Furthermore, it can prevent the quenching effect induced by direct contact between the perovskite active layer and the anode. Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) is the most widely used hole-transporting material in inverted PSCs, because of the advantages such as high conductivity, high light transmittance and good film formation by solution process.  $  ^{[7]}  $  However, the its shallow valence band compared to the perovskite leads to potential energy loss and reduces the built-in potential of the cells. In addition, due to the large number of sulfonic acid groups in the PSS, the PEDOT:PSS film has relatively strong hygroscopicity and acidity, which may accelerate the degradation of the device performance and adversely affect the long-term stability.  $  ^{[8-12]}  $  Besides the commonly-used hole-transporting materials, such as PEDOT:PSS, 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifuorene (spiro-OMeTAD) and poly(triarylamine) (PTAA),  $  ^{[13]}  $  various other materials have been reported.  $  ^{[14-16]}  $  However, high-performance hole-transporting materials with suitable valence band alignment and good stability are still much desirable.

Zwitterions have been widely studied in photovoltaic research for interfacial optimization in recent years.  $  ^{[17-19]}  $   They have excellent solubility in polar solvents and are easy to process in solution. They form dipoles at the interface and improve carrier transfer. By generating a built-in electric field the open-circuit voltage and PCE of the solar cells are improved. Up to now, the only hole-transporting zwitterionic conjugated polyelectrolyte which was reported in PSCs effectively extracted carriers; however, the cells exhibited a serious hysteresis. The reason was attributed to the

In the report, we present a conjugated polyelectrolyte poly(5-amino-5-carboxy-3-oxapentyl)-2,5-thiophene (POWT), a thiophene-based polymer with a side chain terminated with a zwitterionic pair of  $ a $ -amino acid, as a hole-transporting and interfacial material. It forms a weak double-layer capacitance at the interface and exhibits unique electrical, optical and morphological properties. It facilitates hole-extraction, transporting and collection from the perovskite active layer. It also regulates the growth of perovskite crystals and improves the morphology and the crystallinity of the perovskite absorber layer. The based solar cell delivered negligible hysteresis, and significantly improved PCE and the long-term stability. The results show that POWT has great potential to be a high-performance hole-transporting and interfacial material.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Synthesis and Characterization

Polymer POWT was chemically synthesized by following the literature (the details are shown in the Supporting Information).  $  ^{[21]}  $   As shown in Scheme 1, 2-(3-thiophene) acetic acid was subjected to the reduction reaction by lithium aluminum hydride to form 2-(3-thienyl) ethanol (1). A sulfonyl reagent, p-methylbenzenesulfonyl chloride, reacted with 2-(3-thienyl)ethanol (1) to deliver 2-(3-thienyl)-p-toluenesulfonyl ethanol (2). Then, compound (2) reacted with N-t-BOC-L-serine to produce a monomer 3-[2-(3-thienyl)-ethoxy]-2-tert-butoxycarbonylamino-propionic acid (3). Finally, anhydrous ferric chloride was selected as the catalyst to carry out a free radical polymerization to deliver the polymer POWT. It is soluble in polar solvents, like methanol and water.

# 2.6. Photoluminescence of HTL/perovskite films

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) is an effective characterization method to investigate the charge transfer and charge recombination in solar cells. Figure 6d shows the Nyquist plots of the planar heterojunction PSCs with different HTLs, which were recorded at  $  V_{OC}  $  under light illumination in the frequency range from 1 MHz to 0.1 Hz. The inset in Figure 6d is the equivalent circuit model corresponding to the Nyquist plots. Generally, the diameter of the semicircle in the high-frequency region represents the charge transfer resistance ( $  R_{ct}  $ ) which is negatively correlated with the charge transfer capability, and the diameter in the low-frequency region represents the recombination resistance ( $  R_{rec}  $ ) which is related to the recombination rate of electrons and holes. $ ^{[33]} $  As shown in Figure 6d, the Nyquist plot has only one semicircle, and most of the plots in the semicircles correspond to those in the high frequency regions, indicating that the arc is mainly attributed to the charge transfer process in the device. As  $  R_{ct}  $  is proportional to the half circle radius in the Nyquist plots, it is clear that the POWT device has lower  $  R_{ct}  $  than the PEDOT:PSS device. So the replacement of PEDOT:PSS by POWT can significantly reduce  $  R_{ct}  $ , improve interfacial contact and charge transfer. In the present PSCs, only HTLs are different and the difference in  $  R_{ct}  $  mainly depends on the change in the interfacial resistance between HTL and the perovskite. $ ^{[34]} $  The lower  $  R_{ct}  $  value resulted in higher  $  J_{SC}  $  values.

Finally, the long-term stability of the inverted perovskite solar cells without encapsulation and stored in the nitrogen-filled glove boxes at room temperature was tested. Figure 7a shows the gradual changes in PCE of PSCs with the increasing of aging time. After 56 days, the PCE of the PEDOT:PSS device dropped to  $ 73\% $  of the original value. However, the PCE of the POWT device still remained  $ 85\% $  of the original efficiency, indicating the improvement of the stability by using POWT as HTL instead of PEDOT:PSS. The hydrophobicity property was studied by water contact angles. As shown in Figure 7b, the water contact angles of POWT and PEDOT:PSS surfaces are  $ 53.7^{\circ} $  and  $ 24.5^{\circ} $ , respectively; indicating the hydrophobic POWT film could protect the perovskite layer from atmospheric moisture and helps improving the stability of PSC. In addition, the interfacial feature of POWT facilitates the formation of the less-defect perovskite film with large grain size and uniform film surface.

# 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, a thiophene-based zwitterionic conjugated polyelectrolyte (POWT) was introduced into PSCs as a hole-transporting and interfacial material. Different from PEDOT:PSS film, POWT film formed by annealing the spin-coating layer is neutral in terms of acidity and does not erode the adjacent layer. By comparison, hydrophobic POWT film had higher hole mobility and an more appropriately HOMO energy level. Terminated with a zwitterionic pair of  $ a $ -amino acid, POWT layer built up a weak double-layer capacitance at the interface. Deep electrical study disclosed that the POWT-based PSC had low carrier transfer resistance, low leakage current density and minor interfacial charge accumulation, contributing to high  $ V_{OC} $  and  $ J_{SC} $ . The PCE was up to  $ 17.5\% $ , being  $ 23\% $  higher than that of the PEDOT:PSS-based devices. The unencapsulated device has a long-term stability, and the PCE still remained  $ 85\% $  of the original efficiency after 56 days. This work indicates that the type of zwitterionic conjugated polyelectrolytes that do not form a double-layer capacitance or form a weak double-layer capacitance have significant advantage in PSCs.

Fabrication of PSCs: The inverted (p-i-n) perovskite solar cell had the structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/FA $ _{0.2} $ MA $ _{0.8} $ PbI $ _{2.9} $ Br $ _{0.1} $ /C $ _{60} $ /BCP/Ag. The devices were fabricated on Indium tin oxide-coated glass substrates. The fabrication procedure was as follows. The ITO substrate was ultrasonically washed with deionized water, acetone, and isopropyl alcohol in sequence for 15 minutes each. Then the substrates were dried with high purity N $ _2 $  and irradiated for 20 minutes in an UV-ozone cleaner. Next, PEDOT:PSS was spin-coated onto the ultraviolet ozone-treated ITO substrate at 6000 rpm for 30 s, followed by annealing at 120 °C for 15 min. The POWT layer (thickness: 8 nm) was prepared by spin-coating POWT solution with concentration of 2 mg/ml in solvent mixture of methanol and deionized water (1/1, v/v) at 3500 rpm for 40 s, followed by drying at 100 °C for 15 min. Then the substrates were transferred to glovebox (filled with N $ _2 $ ).
