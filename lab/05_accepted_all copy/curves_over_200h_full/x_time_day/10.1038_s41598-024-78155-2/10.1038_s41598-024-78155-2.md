# PEN Study on enhancing water stability and efficiency of inverted perovskite solar cells with guanidine iodide

Xinqi Ai, Feiping Lu  $  ^{✉}  $  , Yongjun Wei, Ju Lei, Yong Bai, Ziang Wei, Ziyin Chen, Weijun Ling & Yuxiang Zhao

Non-radiative recombination of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) will increase as a result of the numerous crystallographic defects that the solution-grown perovskite films will cause, particularly at the grain boundary and film surface. As a result, it negatively influences the performance of the device. Consequently, lowering perovskite film defects is a useful strategy for raising the efficiency of PSCs. This study reports a grain regeneration and passivation approach that can decrease the recombination loss of the perovskite layer/charge transfer layer interface and the grain border. Guanidine iodide (GAI) treatment of perovskite films is the means by which this objective is accomplished. Unlike most methods that use GAI to post-treatment the perovskite layer or add GAI into the perovskite precursor solution, this work uses GAI for pre-treatment before spin coating the perovskite layer. It can effectively passivate surface defects and increase the grain size of perovskite films by controlling the crystallization process. The water stability of devices was enhanced, the short-circuit current ( $ J_{sc} $ ), filling factor (FF), and power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs were markedly improved, and non-radiative recombination was successfully reduced. The best efficiency of PSCs was  $ 20.56\% $  after the additional GAI treatment was applied to the perovskite layer, an  $ 11.9\% $  increase over the efficiency of the control device without GAI treatment. This method has the advantage of being simple and straightforward, providing a feasible pathway for the low-cost preparation and commercialization of PSCs.

Keywords Perovskite solar cells, Defects passivation, Power conversion efficiency, Water stability, Guanidine iodide

The overuse of fossil fuels in modern society has led to a severe threat to the sustainable development of human society by exacerbating environmental issues like the greenhouse effect  $  ^{1,2}  $  . The utilization of renewable clean energy is considered one of the many effective solutions to addressing this crisis  $  ^{3-5}  $  . Solar energy is a clean and renewable energy source that holds a significant position in the field of renewable clean energy  $  ^{6}  $  . A number of solar cell designs have been developed in an effort to completely utilize solar energy  $  ^{7-9}  $  , and improving the PCE of solar cells is a key factor to fully utilize solar energy and reduce the cost of photovoltaic power generation  $  ^{10,11}  $  . In recent years, as the dominant technology for photovoltaic power generation, the efficiency of silicon solar cells has reached a record high of 26.81%  $  ^{12}  $  . However, the technology required to improve the PCE of silicon cells is becoming more and more complex, and the cost has increased sharply, and its efficiency is basically close to their Shockley Quayser (S-Q) efficiency limit of   $  \approx27\%  $  , the potential for improving the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of silicon-based solar cells is extremely limited  $  ^{13}  $  . Therefore, developing high-performance, low-cost, and stable photovoltaic materials and device technologies is a core issue in solar cell research. PSCs have been regarded as a potential photovoltaic technology since the first report because of their advantages, which include a high PCE, low material cost, and an easy preparation method  $  ^{14}  $  . PSCs currently have an efficiency of 26.9%, which can rival that of silicon-based solar cells  $  ^{15}  $  . Importantly, PSCs are far from reaching their theoretical efficiency limit. However, its susceptibility to moisture and oxygen-related instability  $  ^{16,17}  $  , as well as issues such as non-radiative recombination losses at grain boundaries and surfaces within the perovskite film, contribute to lower open-circuit voltages (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) in PSCs, severely impacting their performance. In order to enhance the performance of PSCs through surface passivation, the massive organic ligand guanidine (Gua $  ^{+}  $ ) is frequently utilized to generate two-dimensional (2D) perovskite on the top film surface $  ^{23-30}  $ . It is challenging to precisely build a 2D layer at the buried bottom interface by pre-introducing large organic cations on the substrate because they dissolve rapidly in the precursor solution. Therefore, large organic ligands are often introduced into PSCs structures as additives or surface passivators in precursor solutions. Yadav et al. demonstrated the integration of Gua $  ^{+}  $  ions in the electron transport layer and perovskite absorption layer by studying the impact of Gua $  ^{+}  $  on the  $  V_{\text{oc}}  $  of PSCs based on (Gua) $  _{x}  $ (MA) $  _{1-x}  $ PbI $  _{3}  $ . At the contact, an area of aggregation for both electrons and ions forms, which greatly increases the  $  V_{\text{oc}}^{23}  $ . Gua $  ^{+}  $  prevents undesirable lattice distortion and suppresses halogen vacancy defects more effectively than benzene ammonium (PA $  ^{+}  $ ) and phenylethyl ammonium (PEA $  ^{+}  $ ), according to research conducted by Tan et al. using DFT investigations. It is anticipated that guanidinium tetrafluoroborate (GuaBF $  _{4}  $ ), when added, will inhibit the development of halogen vacancy defects with negligible effect on the perovskite lattice. Comparing the additive-using devices to the control devices, the average  $  V_{\text{oc}}  $  and  $  FF  $  were significantly improved $  ^{25}  $ . The viability of a perovskite layer based on triple cations was demonstrated by Zhang et al. combined MA $  ^{+}  $ , FA $  ^{+}  $ , and Gua $  ^{+}  $  triple cations with perovskite and shaped it into a 3D structure using a two-step deposition process. Gua $  ^{+}  $  was added, and this helped the device performance even better by passivating defects and suppressing non-radiative recombination. The PCE of the device was 21.3%, and stability was also improved $  ^{27}  $ . GuaBr and n-OABr were used by Mozaffari et al. to introduce guanidine and n-octyl ammonium cations. Better than the single-cation passivation layer, the double-cation passivation layer increased the  $  V_{\text{oc}}  $  of PSC to 1.21 V and its PCE to 23.13% $  ^{30}  $ .

In this work, we developed passivation and controlling the crystallization process technique for perovskite film grains, which can effectively improve device performance by reducing recombination losses at the interfaces between the charge transport layer and the perovskite grain. When GAI is used as an additive to precursor solutions, it may alter the crystal structure of perovskite. Whereas in this work GAI is mainly used to modify and optimize the surface of perovskite thin films and reduce the defect density. The results demonstrate that the GAI addition can control the crystallization process of perovskite grains and lower the density of defect states, smoothing out the grain borders and improving the smoothness of perovskite film. The passivation approach ultimately led to an improvement in the efficiency and moisture stability of PSCs. We achieved the champion device for wide bandgap PSCs with a PCE of 20.56% (  $  V_{OC} = 1.15  $   V,   $  J_{sc} = 22.61  $   mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and FF = 79.1%).

# Experiment

Every material utilized in this experiment was bought and used exactly as it was received. The structure of the PSCs conceived and manufactured in this study is depicted in Fig. 1. A mask featuring a  $ 0.05\mathrm{cm}^2 $  aperture area was employed. After cleaning the ITO substrate for 15 min with detergent, deionized water, ethanol, and isopropanol, it was dried for  $ 8\mathrm{h} $  at  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  in a drying oven. Prior to applying each functional layer to the ITO substrate, the substrate was cleaned for ten minutes using UV ozone treatment. Solution  $ A $  is the mixture of  $ \mathrm{PbBr}_2 $  (551.4 mg) and MABr (153.2 mg) dissolved in 1 ml of mixed solvent (N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF): dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO)=4:1). Solution  $ B $  is the mixture of FAI (247.4 mg) and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  (695 mg) dissolved in 1 ml of DMF:DMSO=4:1). Solution  $ C $  is the result of dissolving CsI in DMSO at a concentration of  $ 400\mathrm{mg}/ $  mL. After being fully dissolved in nitrogen for eight hours, three different types of solutions  $ A $ ,  $ B $ , and  $ C $  were combined in various ratios to create the precursor solution.  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.83}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.05}\mathrm{MA}_{0.12}\mathrm{PbI}_{2.64}\mathrm{Br}_{0.36} $  ( $ \mathrm{E_g}=1.65\mathrm{eV} $ ) is the chemical formula. After spin-coating  $ 200~\mu\mathrm{L} $  of PTAA solution onto the substrate for  $ 30\mathrm{s} $  at  $ 5000~\mathrm{rpm} $ , the material was annealed for  $ 20\mathrm{min} $  at  $ 120^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ . Following the substrate cooling to room temperature,  $ 200~\mu\mathrm{L} $  of GAI solution was spin-coated onto it for  $ 20\mathrm{s} $  at  $ 5000~\mathrm{rpm} $ , and it was subsequently annealed for  $ 10\mathrm{min} $  at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ .  $ 130~\mu\mathrm{L} $  of the perovskite precursor solution was put onto the GAI membrane once it had cooled to room temperature. Apply spin coating at a low speed of  $ 2000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 5\mathrm{s} $ , followed by a high speed of  $ 4000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 25\mathrm{s} $ .  $ 240~\mu\mathrm{L} $  of EA is swiftly dropped on the film as an antisolvent in the final  $ 10\mathrm{s} $ , and it is annealed at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 20\mathrm{min} $ .  $ 250~\mu\mathrm{L} $  of PCBM was put onto the perovskite film and spin-coated on the substrate for  $ 25\mathrm{s} $  at  $ 4000~\mathrm{rpm} $  after allowing it to cool to room temperature. Ultimately, a  $ 110~\mathrm{nm} $ -thick silver film was created as the cathode by vacuum thermal evaporation at a rate of  $ 5\mathring{\mathrm{A}}.\mathsf{s}^{-1} $ . Figure 1 illustrates the device production flow chart. Using a Keithley 2400, the current density-voltage  $ (J-V) $  properties of PSC were measured. With the use of a silicon solar cell (Newport 91, 150 V), the light intensity was measured. There is no preprocessing involved in this task, and the scanning speed is fixed at 0.15 volts per second. Using the silicon solar cell as a reference, the spectral response was calculated using the external quantum efficiency (EQE) system of the appropriate spectral measurement instrument (Enlitech QE-R).

By improving contact with the transport layer, the smooth surface of perovskite layer inhibits nonradiative recombination and facilitates charge extraction at the interface  $  ^{26}  $  . According to this data, there is better charge transfer and less recombination at the interface between the GAI-based perovskite layer and the HTL than there was in the reference sample  $  ^{23,26,30}  $  .

# Conclusions

In conclusion, we present a passivation technique for grains that lowers recombination losses in perovskites at the interface between the HTL and grain boundaries at the same time. The perovskite film is processed using GAI in this procedure. Perovskite grain crystallization processes are induced by GAI, which also facilitates  $ \mathrm{Gua}^+ $  penetration into the HTL/perovskite bottom contact. This technique can greatly improve the performance of device by lowering the non-radiative recombination losses at the interface and body.  $ J_{sc} $  and PCE improve by  $ 9.2\% $  and  $ 11.9\% $ , respectively, to achieve  $ 22.61\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  and  $ 20.56\% $ , respectively. The FF and  $ V_{OC} $  of PSCs both rose. With the help of our study, nonradiative recombination within the perovskite absorber body and interfacial nonradiative recombination in the perovskite/HTL can be reduced simultaneously.
