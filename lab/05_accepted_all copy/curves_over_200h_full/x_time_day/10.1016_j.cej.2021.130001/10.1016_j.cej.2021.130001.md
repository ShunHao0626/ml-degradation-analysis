# 1. Introduction

Nowadays, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs), bearing the advantages of facile preparation and preeminent performance, have enormous potentialities to push the emerging photovoltaic technology to a new generation. With the perpetual evolution and advancement of this field, the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the single-junction PSC has skyrocketed to an impressive efficiency of over  $ 25\% $  by virtue of the omnifarious efforts [1,2], gradually surpassing the state-of-art performance of conventional crystalline silicon solar cells. So far, note that the most commonly utilized and effective architecture for perovskite photovoltaic devices are based on the layer-by-layer stacking structure, where the organic lead trihalide perovskite photoactive layer is generally sandwiched between an ETL and a hole transport layer (HTL), and completed with a metal electrode

# ABSTRACT

Organic-inorganic halide perovskites have become promising materials for the next generation of photovoltaic devices on account of the preeminent optoelectronic characteristics and boundless potentialities. Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) normally require a complex structure design with an electron transport layer (ETL) to provide a built-in electric field and depress the probability of carrier recombination. Nonetheless, the construction of a proper ETL is not cost-effective, which precludes the practical commercialization of the PSCs. In this respect, a simplified ETL-free PSC is successfully fabricated by inserting a facile and efficient 1-[N-(2-Hydroxyethyl)-4'-piperidyl]-3-(4'-piperidyl) propane (PPPDE) small-molecule thin interlayer between the FTO substrate and perovskite film. Compared with the bare FTO-based PSCs, this surface engineering can prominently ameliorate the photovoltaic performance parameters, yielding an extraordinary PCE of up to  $ 19.71\% $  with a ca.  $ 31\% $  efficiency enhancement, which can be attributed to the optimized interface energy-level alignment with a barrier-free contact, the elevated charge transfer and collection as well as the suppressed electron-holes recombination at the FTO/perovskite interface. Synchronously, the long-term air stability is also improved with mitigated  $ J - V $  hysteretic behavior due to the larger grain size of perovskite and the suppressed defect-induced degradation. Furthermore, an augmented performance of  $ 15.87\% $  was achieved for the flexible PPPDE embedded ETL-free PSC fabricated on ITO/PEN substrates via a low-temperature deposition process. The demonstrated PPPDE interlayer presents a brand-new strategy that can simplify the cell configuration and improve the photovoltaic performance of ETL-free PSCs.

However, compared with the relatively cheap perovskite materials, the cost of charge transport materials is generally expensive and the deposition of multilayer films is usually time-consuming [3,4]. At present, the extensively utilized and efficient ETL materials reported in literature is the inorganic n-type  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  compact layer [5,6]. Unfortunately, in this kind of structure, the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -based PSCs generally require an energy-consuming annealing process (greater than  $ 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) to completely crystallize into the desired polymorph and ensure a good electron transport property, which undoubtedly increases the production costs and fabrication complexity while inhibiting the possibility of manufacturing flexible devices [7-9]. $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , researchers have adopted many approaches, such as the employment of UV filters, the incorporation of another n-type oxide between the photoactive layer and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL and interface passivation [6,10,11]. Undoubtedly, the PSCs with layer-by-layer stacking architectures would inevitably enhance the cost and complexity of cell manufacture [12]. Accordingly, the researchers have attempted to challenge this conventional view and a multitude of efforts have been made to remove the carrier transport layers to construct ETL-free PSCs.

From a practical point of view, simplifying the manufacture procedure of cells may be a significant exploration, which should be settled to fleetly mass-produce the devices and to economize energy consumption. The ETL-free PSCs possess huge commercial potential because they simplify the device configuration while guaranteeing high photovoltaic performance. Further efficiency upswing of ETL-free PSCs has been achieved by virtue of the grain size control [13], energy level alignment [14,15], and UV/O $ _{3} $  plasma treatment [16,17]. Nonetheless, these reported performances of ETL-free devices still lag far behind those of traditional ETL-embedded devices. Further amelioration of the cell efficiency is mainly restricted by some factors, containing poor-quality perovskite layers with inferior charge mobility, high defect densities, short diffusion length, inadequate surface coverage and uniformity, low built-in potential and mismatched energy level alignment between perovskite and conductive substrates [18]. Especially, a pivotal challenge for the ETL-free PSCs is the ineffective electron collection from the perovskite layer to the bottom electrode because of the mismatch of the energy-level discrepancy ( $ \Delta E $ ) between the conduction band minimum (CBM) of the perovskite film and the Fermi level ( $ E_{F} $ ) of FTO substrate, engendering tremendous carrier recombination,  $ V_{oc} $  loss, severe hysteresis behavior and eventually instability in the devices [19]. For the preparation of high-performing ETL-free devices, it is imminently desired to alleviate or even completely eliminate the value of  $ \Delta E $ . In general, two popular strategies have been devoted to minimize the electron barrier, one of them being the optimization for doping type or composition engineering of perovskite material. Cui and coauthors shifted the Fermi level upwards by enhancing the heat treatment temperature from 60 °C to 150 °C, which altered the doping levels from p-type to n-type, thereby achieving the composition management of perovskite layer. As a result, the spontaneously formed n-p heterojunction can effectively promote the separation and extraction of charges, and retard the carrier recombination, thus enabling an intensive efficiency of 15.69% [20,21]. Nevertheless, the electronic structure of perovskite is sensitively dependent on the preparation conditions and precursor compositions, which shows that the reduction of  $ \Delta E $  value by tailoring the  $ WF $  and CBM of perovskites is relatively complicated and limited.

The other common and tractable strategy to diminish  $ \Delta E $  is to incorporate an inorganic or organic thin layer on top of the conductive substrates, regulating the  $ E_{F} $  of the FTO substrate toward the CBM of active layer. On one hand, it has been shown that the non-conductive polymer PEIE interlayer is employed to tune the FTO surface, which can induce a strong induced dipole moment at the interface of perovskite layer and FTO substrate, leading to the equivalent of an n-i homojunction forming within the perovskite layer [22]. Nonetheless, this ETL-free cell displays a relatively low efficiency of only  $ 15.0\% $ , which is substantially lower than that of the corresponding ETL-inserted device. In addition, considering the unbalanced charge transfer rate as well as the lack of permanent built-in field, this type of ETL-free cell is generally subjected to serious hysteresis issues and unstable steady-state power output [23,24]. On the other hand, the functionalized fullerene derivatives self-assembled monolayer is utilized to reshape the surface of FTO substrates, thereby boosting the performance and reducing the hysteresis behavior of PSC devices [25,26]. However, expensive cost and complicated preparation process are required for fullerene derivatives in this approach. Thereby, it is extremely significant to exploit a simple but effective route to prepare ETL-free PSCs those are comparable to the efficiency of devices employing discrete charge selective extraction

layers.

In this contribution, to our knowledge, we for the first time developed a novel PPPDE interface material to modify the FTO substrate, thereby achieving efficient rigid and flexible ETL-free PSCs with an alleviated hysteresis behavior and extended long-term stability. The embedded PPPDE organic thin layer allows the formation of an interfacial dipole layer, which decreases the WF of bottom electrode and favorably influences the band bending direction at the interface of FTO and perovskite, thus resulting in the facilitated charge extraction and suppressed photogenerated electron-hole recombination. Additionally, the target PPPDE interlayer can facilitate the crystal growth to obtain high-quality perovskite films with low trap densities, great grain sizes and ameliorated crystallinity. As a result, a superb champion performance of 19.71% has been achieved for the constructed PPPDE-based PSCs as compared to the pristine control FTO counterpart (15.01%). This research demonstrates the great potential of our ETL-free PSC with PPPDE inorganic interlayer modification in terms of commercialization, and provides additional promise for the preparation of high-performance ETL-free PSCs.

# 2.2. Device manufacturing

Fig. S1 displays the fabrication process of the complete PSCs. In brief, the FTO coated glasses or the ITO/PEN flexible substrates were ultrasonically rinsed with Hellmanex 2% aqueous solution, ultrapure water, acetone, ethanol, and IPA for 15 min. Next, the PPPDE interlayer was deposited by spin-coating the PPPDE isopropanol solution with various concentrations (0.3, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 mg/mL) onto the substrates at 1500 rpm for 30 s. For the flexible devices, the PPPDE solution concentrations we used are 0.5, 1.0, and 2.0 mg/mL, respectively. Then the samples were annealed at 90 °C for 10 min under ambient conditions (humidity ~ 25%, temperature ~ 23 °C). Thereafter, the perovskite layer was deposited from a mixed hybrid precursor solution including PbBr₂ (0.2 M), PbI₂ (1.1 M), MABr (0.2 M) and FAI (1.0 M) in anhydrous mixture of DMF/DMSO (4:1, v:v). Afterwards, the 1.0 M CsI stock solution (in DMSO) was added to the above precursor solution to obtain the required perovskite component in a N₂ glovebox. The as-prepared perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated on the substrates in two steps at 1000 rpm for 10 s and 6000 rpm for 20 s, respectively. During the second step, 100 μL of CB anti-solvent was drop-cast on the rotating samples in a dry air glovebox, 5 s prior to the end of spin-coating. Then the as-spun perovskite layers were annealed at 100 °C for 40 min in a dry air glovebox (H₂O < 0.01 ppm). The typical thickness of the perovskite films is 650–700 nm. For the preparation of hole-selective material solution, spiro-OMeTAD (72.3 mg) is dissolved in 1 mL of chlorobenzene solvent and doped with TBP (29 μL), and Li-TFSI acetonitrile stock solution (520 mg mL⁻¹) additives. The thickness of the spin-coated spiro-OMeTAD hole transport layer is approximately in the range of 200–250 nm. Subsequently, the HTL was deposited on the top of perovskite films by

# 2.3. Device characterization

To access the defect density of the perovskite deposited on different substrates, the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) examinations with a single-carrier device architecture of FTO/PPPDE (0.5 mg/mL)/perovskite/PCBM/Au are conducted. Fig. 4g,h display the dark J-V plots of the corresponding electron-only devices. It can be observed that two typical regions are obtained by the SCLC measurement, including the ohmic region and the trap-filled limit region. The linear correlation at low bias stands for the ohmic-type response. The kink point voltage of the curve is commonly determined as the trap-filling limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ). When the bias voltage exceeds above the kink point, the traps will be totally filled at the   $  V_{TFL}  $  , contributing to a noticeable elevation of current. The   $  V_{TFL}  $   value is closely correlated with the trap density (  $  N_{t}  $  ), which can be obtained from the following formula [37–39]

$$
V _ {T F L} = \frac {q N _ {t} L ^ {2}}{2 \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0}}

here,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity,   $  \varepsilon  $   represents the relative dielectric constant of perovskite, L denotes the thickness of perovskite film, and   $  V_{TFL}  $   refers to the trap-filled limit voltage, q is the electron charge [40]. Obviously, the measured   $  V_{TFL}  $   for the reference sample is   $  \sim1.11  $   V while the value is relieved to 0.77 V upon depositing the PPPDE layer. The lower   $  N_{t}  $   values implies that the PPPDE layer can decrease the electron trap substantially, thus contributing to the suppressed recombination and the improved   $  V_{oc}  $   in the derived devices.

One of the issues with PSCs is the hysteresis phenomenon [44], where the  $ J - V $  characteristics alters with the scanning directions. In the reflection of the  $ J - V $  data (Fig. 5d,e), the forward and reverse scan efficiencies of the solar cell based on the untreated FTO are  $ 12.95\% $  and  $ 15.01\% $ , respectively. Commonly, to quantify the hysteresis degree, a hysteresis index "H-index" was introduced:

$$

$$

The value of  $ H $ -index was decreased from  $ 13.72\% $  to  $ 7.10\% $  upon depositing the PPPDE layer. Consequently, the PPPDE embedded device presents an alleviated hysteresis phenomenon in comparison with the reference device. As is known, the hysteresis of PSCs is normally assigned to the interfacial capacitance originated from the charge accumulation at the interface, which arises from the high trap density, ion migration as wells as the unbalanced charge transport in the PSCs [45]. The above data from the SCLC observation attests to the markedly relieved trap density of the perovskite layer upon addition of the PPPDE interlayer, one of the prime causes for the attenuated hysteresis.

Amazingly, our fabricated PPPDE-based device exhibits a good reliability and reproducibility with a small standard deviation compared to the reference devices. It is notable that the leakage current of the PPPDE-embedded PSC is lower than that of the control PSC. Synchronously, the PPPDE-containing PSC presents a higher rectification ratio and lower reverse saturation current as compared with the untreated device, signifying that interface charge-carrier recombination losses are considerably suppressed, benefitting to the augmented   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF [46]. We also periodically monitored the PV performances of unencapsulated solar cells without and with PPPDE (0.5 mg/mL) interlayer after several days against humid environments (25 ± 5%, in dark). After aging in an ambient atmosphere for 40 days, the performance of the reference device dropped by 30% of the incipient PCE, while those of the PPPDE embedded PSC showed a slow degradation with the loss of 17% initial value under the same storage condition, indicative of the ameliorated environment stability of the latter compared to the former. As discussed above (Fig. 4), the grain size of the perovskite can be increased by depositing the PPPDE interlayer on the FTO. As is known, the larger grain size can effectively retard the moisture penetration at grain boundaries [47]. Taking into account the above analysis, it is thus safe to conclude that the ameliorated environmental stability for the PPPDE-coated devices can be credited to the larger grain size of perovskite and the suppressed defect-induced degradation.

Apart from the long-term stability, the humidity stability is a significant for the application of PSCs. As shown in Fig. S10a, the humidity long-term PCE stability measurement of the un-encapsulated devices with and without PPPDE passivation was performed in an ambient environment with a constant RH level of 50–70% and a temperature of 25 °C in dark conditions. Obviously, the efficiencies of the devices without and with PPPDE interlayer reduced with the enhancement of the exposure time and the normalized efficiency of both devices are over 40% after 60 h. In addition, thermal stability of PSCs is also a key factor influencing the applicability. Thus we compared the thermal stability of PPPDE-embedded un-encapsulated devices to that of bare FTO substrates. Fig. S10b displays the normalized efficiency versus the time plot for both PSCs (stored in air) that aged at an elevated temperature of 80 °C over time. Here, six investigated solar cells without any encapsulation were measured for each condition. It is evident that the two types of devices show a degradation of the initial PCE after thermal ageing testing for 150 h in air and the device performance remains about 75% of the initial efficiency, showing the comparable stability for the cells with and without PPPDE layer.

The values of  $ G_{max} $  can be calculated according to the formula  $ J_{sat} = qG_{max}L $ , here,  $ L $  is the thickness of perovskite film and  $ q $  is the electronic charge [61]. exhibits an extended   $  G_{max}  $   value. # 4. Conclusions

To summarize, we have put forward a novel interface modification strategy to construct efficient and stable ETL-free PSCs through introduction of a facile low-temperature solution-processed PPPDE interlayer on the FTO substrates and demonstrated the potential working mechanism in this contribution. The inserted PPPDE interlayer can effectively induce the interface dipole moment and tune the energy band structure at the interface, which can give rise to an elevation in built-in potential and charge transfer, this enabling the high   $  J_{sc}  $   and   $  V_{oc}  $   of the devices. Furthermore, this novel PPPDE interlayer has been proved to promote the crystallization of perovskite and reduce the surface defects in the perovskite film. With optimized solution concentration, the new PPPDE-modified ETL-free PSC exhibits a considerably enhanced champion PCE of up to 19.71% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.13 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.78 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and FF of 77%, which far surpasses that of the untreated pristine FTO (15.01%). Synchronously, the long-term environmental stability of devices has been improved due to the inhibited degradation of perovskite at the grain boundaries caused by the larger grain size. In addition, we have also fabricated the flexible ETL-free devices with an efficiency of 15.87% by means of the low temperature processing for PPPDE interlayer. The PPPDE modified FTO substrate we proposed is a facile and practical processing strategy that can ameliorate the efficiency and stability of the ETL-free solar cells. This provides insights for the design of device structure and processing methods in PSCs, which is beneficial to the mass production and commercialization of the ETL-free PSCs as well as other perovskite-based optoelectronic devices in the future.
