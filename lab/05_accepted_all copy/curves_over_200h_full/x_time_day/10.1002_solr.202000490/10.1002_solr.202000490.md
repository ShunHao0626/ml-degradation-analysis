The energy loss was suppressed from 0.49 eV to 0.35 eV by passivating surface defects in hybrid perovskites via Lewis acid-base interactions with the combination of electron-donating aromatic nucleus in PS and tertiary amine in TPA, leading to perovskite solar cells with a high open-circuit voltage of 1.18 V and a fill factor of about 80%, and a power conversion efficiency of 22.1%. # Introduction

Perovskite solar cells, as one of the most promising next-generation photovoltaic technology, have exhibited lab-scale power conversion efficiency (PCE) of   $  \sim25\%  $  , which is close to that of single crystalline silicon solar cells.  $  ^{[1]}  $   However, it is a significant challenge to obtain a PCE approaching the

This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved

WILEY-VCH

theoretical Shockley-Queisser limit and reliable long-term device stability under ambient environmental conditions.  $  ^{[2]}  $   The central solution to this challenge is the use of hydrophobic materials that can not only prevent water molecules breaking apart humid-sensitive perovskites  $  ^{[3]}  $   but also suppress energy loss during the charge generation and extraction processes in the perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{[4]}  $   The energy loss (  $  E_{Loss}  $  ) can be estimated by an empirical equation (  $  E_{Loss}=E_{g}-qV_{OC}  $  ),  $  ^{[5,6]}  $   where   $  E_{g}  $   and   $  V_{OC}  $   represent the optical bandgap and open circuit voltage, respectively. It was generally reported that the   $  E_{Loss}  $   was about 0.4-0.9 eV in solar cells based on halide perovskites.  $  ^{[7]}  $   The current situation leaves large room for reduction of the   $  E_{Loss}  $   in perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{[8]}  $   Non-radiative recombination loss should be suppressed by passivating deep-level defects at grain boundaries and interfaces of the perovskite layer.  $  ^{[9,10]}  $   In addition, the suppression of   $  E_{Loss}  $   is critically important for high open-circuit-voltage solar cells that are particularly demanded to drive portable consumer electronics.  $  ^{[11]}  $

Recently, a thin layer of an insulating polymer, such as poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA) $ ^{[12]} $  and polystyrene (PS), $ ^{[13]} $  has been proved to be a feasible interfacial layer between the perovskite layer and charge-transporting layer to improve device stability and reduce the charge recombination loss when the photogenerated charges were allowed to tunnel through the interfaces. Due to their electrically-insulating features of PMMA and PS polymers, it is essentially important to reduce the  $ E_{\text{Loss}} $  further by modifying the insulating polymeric layer with functional groups through chemical or physical approaches to allow a more efficient passivation in the deep-level defects. $ ^{[14]} $  Lewis acid-base interactions have been shown to effectively passivate the defects in perovskite films by using molecular materials such as thiophene $ ^{[15]} $  and caffeine (1,3,7-trimethylxanthine) with carboxyl (C=O) groups. $ ^{[16]} $  It has been demonstrated that carbonyl or cyano based organic molecules with lone

This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved

WILEY-VCH

pair electrons can be employed as Lewis base to passivate the vacancy defects at the grain boundary to improve both photovoltaic performance and environmental stability of the devices.  $  ^{[17, 18]}  $   Therefore, compared to the relatively complicated modification of the insulating polymeric layer by chemical synthesis, a physical method based on the Lewis acid-base interaction is expected to be simpler by blending organic molecules with particular functional groups and lone pair electrons into the insulating polymeric layer.

In this work, we report that the triphenylamine (TPA) as a Lewis base was blended into PS polymeric layer to form an interfacial layer. TPA molecules have tertiary amine which has lone pair electrons to form the Lewis acid-base interactions with the positively charged defects such as under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   or Pb clusters in the perovskite. Meanwhile, since TPA is also soluble in DCB, we are able to blend the Lewis-base molecule TPA into PS polymeric interlayer to tailor the interfacial properties and passivate surface defects. The tertiary amine with lone pair electrons in TPA was introduced to establish a stonger Lewis acid-base interaction with positive-charged defects in the perovskite to further suppress the non-radiative recombination and thus reduce   $  E_{Loss}  $  . The combination of both TPA and PS to form an interfacial hydrophobic layer resulted in a high PCE of 22.1% and a high   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.18 V along with a low   $  E_{Loss}  $   of 0.35 V while maintaining a good resistance to moisture attack as suggested by the observation that the PCE still retained ~91% of the initial value after 10-days storage under ambient condition with relative humidity of 40-60%.

To understand the origin of improvement on photovoltaic performance with an interfacial layer of the TPA:PS blend, both UV-Vis spectra and steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra were obtained to reveal the variation of optical properties of pristine perovskite film and the films after coating a thin layer of PS or TPA:PS polymer. As shown in Figure 3a, the UV-vis absorption spectra exhibit similar absorption onset for three samples, suggesting that the interfacial layer treatment has little impact to the optical bandgap and absorption properties. However, we found that the PL peak exhibited an evident blue-shift by about  $ 4\mathrm{nm} $  for the perovskite film with a TPA:PS layer compared to the pristine perovskite film and the perovskite film with a PS layer. In addition, the PL intensity increased significantly after spin-coating a layer of TPA:PS blend on the perovskite film whereas the PL intensity only increased slightly with the PS-only treatment (Figure 3a). The blue-shift of the PL peak and the significant enhancement of the PL intensity should be attributable to the reduction of surface defects in perovskite film after employing the Lewis base TPA,[10,23] resulting in a reduction of trap-assisted recombination and thereby a stronger PL emission.

To further reveal influence of PS and TPA on carrier recombination dynamics in perovskite solar cells, the fluorescence lifetime measurements were performed to compare the PL decay behavior between the pristine perovskite film and the perovskite film after coating a layer of PS or TPA:PS blend. To unveil how the trap density   $  (N_{\mathrm{d}})  $   in the perovskite changes with the introduction of TPA into the PS polymeric layer, the space-charge-limited-current (SCLC) method was employed to assess the electron-only devices with an architecture of ITO/SnO  $  _{2}  $  /perovskite/TPA:PS/C  $  _{60}  $  /BCP/Ag. As shown in Figure 5a, the J-V curves (measured in the dark) exhibited three regions with increasing the applied voltage, including the linear ohmic region, the trap-filling region and the SCLC region. The trap density   $  (N_{\mathrm{d}})  $   in devices can be determined from the following equation:  $  ^{[25]}  $

$$
N _ {d} = 2 \varepsilon_ {0} \varepsilon V _ {T F L} / q L ^ {2}
$$

Where,   $  V_{TFL}  $   is the trap-filled limit voltage;   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   and   $  \varepsilon  $   represent the vacuum permittivity and the relative dielectric constant of perovskite, respectively; L is the thickness of the perovskite layer; and q is the elementary charge. The   $  N_{d}  $   in the perovskite film after coating a PS-only interfacial layer was estimated to be   $  3.08 \times 10^{16} \, cm^{-3}  $   which was slightly lower than that of   $  3.31 \times 10^{16} \, cm^{-3}  $   in the pristine perovskite; whereas the   $  N_{d}  $   in the perovskite film after coating a layer of TPA:PS was significantly reduced to   $  2.09 \times 10^{16} \, cm^{-3}  $  . This observation confirms that the Lewis base TPA plays a dominant role in passivating the trap states in the perovskite film to reduce   $  N_{d}  $   significantly despite the presence of passivation effect from electron-donating aromatic nucleus in PS to interact with the surface defects in hybrid perovskites.  $  ^{[21]}  $   This result is consistent with the significantly increased PL intensity and longer carrier lifetime in the perovskite films, and also agrees with the suppressed J-V hysteresis in the perovskite solar cells.

# Conclusion

We have demonstrated an efficient interfacial engineering approach with a thin layer of TPA:PS blend to concurrently suppress  $  E_{\mathrm{Loss}}  $  and improve device stability in the ambient environment. The

WILEY-VCH

Lewis base TPA with a lone pair of electrons plays a dominant role in passivating the defects via Lewis acid-base interactions and thus reduce the non-radiative recombinations to decrease  $  E_{\text{Loss}}  $ . The treatment of TPA:PS interfacial layer leads to a high PCE of 22.1%, a large  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $  of 1.18 V, a low  $  E_{\text{Loss}}  $  of 0.35 V in perovskite solar cells. Owing to the strong hydrophobicity of TPA:PS interfacial layer, the device stability was significantly improved as suggested by maintaining 91% of initial PCE after 10-days storage in the ambient environment with relative humidity of ~40-60%. As for the selection rules of Lewis base molecules that are blended into PS polymeric interlayer to tailor the interfacial properties and passivate surface defects, on one hand, this molecule should include groups that have lone pair electrons to form the Lewis acid-base interactions with the positively charged defects such as under-coordinated  $  \text{Pb}^{2+}  $  or Pb clusters in the perovskite; On the other hand, it is soluble in the DCB solvent to form a mixed solution with PS polymer, which is a prerequisite condition to spin coat a passivation layer onto the perovskite film. The interfacial engineering approach with the TPA:PS blend not only reduces the  $  E_{\text{Loss}}  $  for superior photovoltaic performance but also offers a way to improve the device stability in the ambient environment, both of which are critically important toward real-world applications. More importantly, our findings are applicable in a variety of halide perovskites based optoelectronic devices, such as photodetectors, light emitting diodes, and lasers, that need to suppress  $  E_{\text{Loss}}  $  and improve air-stability.
