# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite is a rising material for the photovoltaic technology, it has evolved rapidly owing to outstanding properties, for instance, suitable bandgap, high light absorption ability, low binding energies. its efficiency has increased from  $ 3.8\% $  to more than  $ 25\% $  in a few years [1-5], the rapidly increased efficiency can attribute to the advancement in material designing, interface engineering, facile fabrication processes and so on.

High quality perovskite absorber films are necessary for higher PCE and better stability, such as compact and smooth surface, less defects in the surface or boundary. Additive engineering was always reported to have the ability to assist the formation of perovskite to obtain good quality films with less defects [6-9]. Among them, several efficient surface passivation of additive engineering have been adopted to reduce the grain boundaries defects and control the perovskite growth process [10-12], such as nanoparticles, salts, molecules, polymers and so on [13-15]. For instance, Polyethylene glycol(PEG)was used as additive to improve the morphology of perovskite film, solar cells with PEG show a PCE of  $ 16\% $  and the solar cells retained  $ 65\% $  of its initial efficiency under

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted much attention as a novel photoelectric converter. The quality of perovskite films plays a key role in the efficiency and stability. Among them, defects in the films surface restrict the performance of solar cells. Surface passivation is an effective route to eliminate defect of perovskite films. In this paper, we introduce PVB as a novel polymer additive, it can assist perovskite films with better crystallinity and morphology, as well as less defects. Perovskite solar cells with 1.5 mg/mL optimized concentration PVB exhibit power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 19.04% than 16.34% of control cells. Meanwhile, the cells with PVB demonstrate less hysteresis than that without additive, as well as excellent reproducibility. Additionally, perovskite solar cells based on PVB can retain around 90% of its original efficiency under fully ambient air of   $  65 \pm 5\%  $   relative humidity or under   $  65^{\circ}C  $   after aging for 30 days. The finding provides a potential additive candidate for fabrication of higher performance devices.

Polyvinyl butyral (PVB) is widely used in organic light-emitting diode due to its excellent performance PVB is a kind of non-toxic and harmless material with low cost, it also has good ability on resistance to water and heat. Herein, we present PVB as a unique and facile surface passivation additive to assist the perovskite growth on crystal quality and the morphology. The film of perovskite with PVB demonstrate a good quality, including smooth surface with large grain. Meanwhile, the valid elimination of defects can also be confirmed by extended exciton lifetimes and shifted PL spectra. Compared with the control solar cell, the efficiency of cells with PVB additive at 1.5 mg/mL is obviously improved from 16.34% to 19.04%, while it shows short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) value of 23.13 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) value of 1.06 V, and the fill factor (FF) value of 77%. More importantly, the solar cell with PVB show better stability, around 90% of the original PCE can be maintained when it aged under 65 ± 5% relative humidity or 65 °C after 30 days, respectively. It will be a new additive candidate for the better performance, especially on long-term stability.

# 2.2. Synthesis of methylammonium iodide (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  ) and perovskite solution

32 mL of methylamine was reacted with 30 mL of HI solution in a 250 mL flask at   $  0\ °C  $   stirring constantly for 6 h. Removing the solution by rotary evaporator then precipitate collected, it followed by washing four times with diethyl ether then drying in vacuum oven for 12 h.

The mixture of DMF/DMSO in 9:1 vol ratio, then dissolving   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   and   $  PbI_{2}  $   in the mixture, the 1.5 M solution was stirred constantly for 12 h at 50 °C. The 1.5 M solution filtered with 0.45 μm pore size PTFE filter and the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite solution was prepared.

# 2.3. Solar cells fabrication

FTO substrates washed by isopropanol, acetone, ethanol, and deionized water, separately. Deposited Compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer on clean FTO though spin-coating at 2000 rpm for 30 s, then baking compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   at 500 °C in muffle furnace. Mixing D30-NRT with absolute ethanol in 1:5.5 wt ratio, the mesoporous layer paste was obtained, the paste deposited by spin-coating at 4000 rpm for 30 s, baking mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   at 500 °C. The perovskite precursor solution were prepared according to the above mixture ratio or reported literatures [19,29]. The precursor solution containing   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  ,   $  PbI_{2}  $   dissolved into the mixture solvent system, then Film formation on the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   at 5000 rpm for 35 s by spin-coating, during the precursor solution spin-coating process, PVB (0.5 mg/mL, 1.0 mg/mL, 1.5 mg/mL, and 3.0 mg/mL) containing chlorobenzene as the anti-solvent dripped onto the surface of film. Sequentially, the deposited perovskite films annealed at 100 °C for 60 min in ambient air. # 3. Results and discussions

As anti-solvent solution, different amounts of PVB containing chlorobenzene (0 mg/mL, 0.5 mg/mL, 1.0 mg/mL, 1.5 mg/mL, 3.0 mg/mL) were added. UV–Vis absorption spectrum of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film based on different amounts of PVB could be seen in Fig. 2(a). From the UV–Vis spectrum, with the increased concentration of PVB, the slightly enhanced intensity can be observed. The optimization of concentration of PVB was confirmed at 1.5 mg/mL [30,31]. In addition, a uniform and large perovskite film is necessary in order to obtain better performance. In order to confirm the influence of PVB on the morphology of perovskite films, the surface morphology films which treat with PVB is characterized by scanning electron microscopy, the image is shown in Fig. 2(b–f). For the untreated perovskite film, the control perovskite thin film is mainly consisted of small size grain crystal (  $  \sim  $  300–400 nm). After treatment with PVB, the perovskite thin films demonstrate the larger crystal grain size, At the concentration of 1.5 mg/mL, significantly, grain size of perovskite increased to 600–700 nm, the amount of grain boundary was obviously reduced, which is benefit for the reduction of defect density. The films with larger grain size can help to achieve solar cells with better performance and stability. As previously reported literatures, many additions have similar effect on the perovskite growth [32,33] to obtain the better quality thin films, facilitating the improvement of performance. However, when further increased concentration of PVB to 3.0 mg/mL, the morphology and crystallization became worse, resulting in the decreased intensity of absorption

Long-term stability of perovskite solar cells is a necessary for the further industrial application. Thermal and moisture will promote the degradation process of perovskite thin film. To investigate PVB influence on long-term stability, XRD date of perovskite film under humidity or thermal shows on Fig. 8. Peak at  $ 13.2^{\circ} $  could assigne to the face of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , peak intensity of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  gradually increased under  $ 65 \pm 5\% $  and  $ 65^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 30 days, which can be regard as the degradation of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ . For the fims without PVB, there were obvious increase-ment of intensity peak of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , suggesting distinct degradation of perovskite. At the same condition, perovskite film with PVB maintained more stable, there was no obvious  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  peak after aging 30 days whether under humidity or thermal atmosphere. With the introduction of PVB, the degradation of perovskite can be efficiently inhibited, resulting from the better crystallinity and morphology, as well as the reduction of film defect.

The corresponding device stability was carried out under the same condition. As shown in Fig. 9, the value of PCE without PVB decreased to  $ 5\% $  after aging 30 days with a relative humidity of  $ 65 \pm 5\% $ , however, under same condition, the efficiency of solar cell with PVB can remain  $ 89\% $  of its initial efficiency.

Meanwhile, the devices with PVB also show a better stability than control cells at  $ 65^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  (humidity of  $ 30\% $ ) after aging 30 days, the PCE of devices with PVB can remain  $ 90\% $  of its original efficiency, but the PCE without PVB decreased to  $ 20\% $  of the original efficiency. The perovskite films with PVB have an obvious improvement on the quality, such as more compact and smoother surface, the larger grain size with fewer defects. As a result, the perovskite solar cells after modification show better long-term stability than control solar cells, which is benefit for the later industrial application.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, a novel and facile was successfully introduced to fabricate perovskite films. Based on PVB additive engineering, the obtained perovskite films exhibit better performance, such as excellent uniformity, large crystal rain size, passivation of defects and so on. The PCE of 19.04% could achieve via adding PVB 1.5 mg/mL, which is 16.5% more than that of the control devices. More importantly, the devices with PVB exhibit better long-term stability compared with control solar cells. When aged under   $  65 \pm 5\%  $   relative humidity or at   $  65^{\circ}C  $   after 30 days, it can remain around 90% of its initial efficiency. It is a novel method for preparation higher efficient and stable solar cells.
