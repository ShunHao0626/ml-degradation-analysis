# Introducing an Organic Hole Transporting Material as a Bilayer to Improve the Efficiency and Stability of Perovskite Solar Cells

Veera Murugan Arivunithi  $  ^{†,1}  $  
Ho-Yeol Park  $  ^{†,1}  $

Abstract: Although perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved high power conversion efficiency (PCE) by utilizing 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N'-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-Spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) as hole transporting material (HTM), the reproducibility and stability of PSCs are still a pressing concern. Herein, we introduced a solvent processed organic-organic bilayer based on 2-(4-(7-(9,9-dimethylacridin-10(9H)-yl)-9,9-diethyl-9H-fluoren-2-yl)phenyl)-1-phenyl-1H-phenanthro[9,10-d]imidazole (AFpPPI) and Spiro-OMeTAD in layer to layer as HTM in PSCs. The devices configured with AFpPPI/Spiro-OMeTAD bilayer achieved a maximum PCE of 19.9% in mesoporous-TiO  $  _{2}  $  (mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $  ) structure with perovskite absorber of Cs  $  _{0.05}  $  Rb  $  _{0.05}  $  (FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{0.76}  $  (MAPbBr  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{0.14}  $ . The properties of the bilayer structure were analyzed with steady-state photoluminescence, ultra-violet photoelectron and impedance spectroscopy. The AFpPPI/Spiro-OMeTAD improved open-circuit voltage (V  $  _{OC}  $  ) by lowering the

quasi-Fermi energy level for holes and reducing the charge recombination, resulting in high   $  V_{oc}  $   (1.14 V in the champion cell) and high fill factor (FF) that lead to high PCE. The addition of AFpPPI layer improves the quality of Spiro-OMeTAD and provides pinhole-free film. Moreover, the stability is improved in controlled temperature and humid conditions. This work affords a new approach for commercial applications of PSCs with better stability.

Keywords: bilayer, perovskite solar cell, hole transporting material, morphology, stability.

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic halide perovskites have structured with ABX  $  _{3}  $  (A: organic cation, B: metal cation and X: halide) makes black phase perovskites for photovoltaic (PV) applications and show excellent charge transporting properties.  $  ^{1-3}  $  The compositional engineering with halide substitutions could help in tuning the bandgap so as to attain an ideal bandgap for highly efficient perovskite solar cells (PSC's).  $  ^{4,5}  $  The attraction of this class materials is because of their outstanding properties, admirable light harvesting capacity,  $  ^{6}  $  long carrier diffusion lengths,  $  ^{7,8}  $  low exciton binding energy,  $  ^{9}  $  a tunable bandgap,  $  ^{10}  $  easy preparation methods,  $  ^{11-14}  $  etc. In the last decades, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs sky rocketed from initial 3.8% (2009) to 25.2% (2020).  $  ^{15}  $  PSCs are usually made of a cathode, an electron transport layer (ETL), a perovskite layer, a hole transport layer (HTL), and an anode. The work shows the importance of impeding ion migration into organic HTMs and the significance of using bilayer HTM for PSC's and effective way to enhance the long-term stability of PSC's.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Synthesis of AFpPPI  $  ^{42}  $

To a solution of toluene/ethanol (15/5 mL), 10-(9,9-diethyl-7-(4,4,5,5-tetramethyl-1,3,2-dioxaborolan-2-yl)-9H-fluoren-2-yl)-9,9-dimethyl-9,10-dihydroacridine (0.50 g, 0.90 mmol),   $  Na_{2}CO_{3}  $   (2 M, 5.0 mL), and 2-(4-bromophenyl)-1-phenyl-1H-phenanthro[9,10-d]imidazole (0.49 g, 1.08 mmol) was added one by one. The reaction mixture was purged with   $  N_{2}  $   for 30 min and then   $  \mathrm{Pd(PPh_{3})_{4}}  $   (42 mg, 0.04 mmol) was added, and then the reaction mixture was stirred under   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere at 100 °C for 12 h. After completion of the reaction, the extraction and purification methods were followed as per the previous literature. The yield of AFpPPI was 86%.

2.1.1. Materials

Lead(II) iodide (PbI $ _2 $ ) and lead(II) bromide (PbBr $ _2 $ ) (99.99%) were purchased from TCI. Formamidinium iodide (FAI) and methylamine bromide (MABr) were purchased from Dyesol. Titanium diisopropoxide dis(acetylacetonate) was purchased from Sigma Aldrich (Korea). Spiro-OMeTAD (99.5%) was obtained from Lumtec (Taiwan). Acetonitrile (99.8%), chlorobenzene (CB, 99.8%), DMF (99.8%), and DMSO (99.9%) were purchased from Sigma Aldrich (Korea).

# 2.2. Fabrication of PSCs

Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glasses (Pilkington, TEC-8, 15 Ω/ sq) were etched using zinc powder and HCl solution (2 M). After cleaning by detergent, diluted water and acetone, they were sonicated with isopropyl alcohol (IPA) in an ultrasonic bath for 60 min, followed by UV- $  O_{3}  $  treatment for 20 min. A 30 nm blocking  $  TiO_{2}  $  (Bl- $  TiO_{2}  $ ) layer (0.15 M titanium diisopropoxide dis(acetylacetonate) with 1-butanol) was deposited on top of the FTO by spin coating  $  TiO_{2}  $  precursor solution at 2800 rpm for 30 s. Next, it was heated at 125 °C for 5 min in air. The mesoporous titanium oxide (m- $  TiO_{2}  $ ) layer was coated on Bl- $  TiO_{2}  $  substrates at 2500 rpm for 20 s to achieve 150-200 nm thickness using a diluted  $  TiO_{2}  $  paste with ethanol and annealed at 550 °C for 1 h. The perovskite solution was prepared by dissolving 0.507 g of  $  PbI_{2}  $ , 0.080 g of  $  PbBr_{2}  $ , 0.022 g of MABr, and 0.171 g of FAI in 0.8 mL of anhydrous DMF and 0.2 mL of anhydrous DMSO. CsI (1.5 M in DMSO) and RbI (1.5 M in 4:1 V/V DMF/DMSO) was added to the solution. The spin coating procedure was performed in a glove box with nitrogen flow. Perovskite precursor solution was spin coated on the substrate at 1000 rpm for 10 s, and 5000 rpm for 20 s. 200 μL of CB was injected on the substrate 5 s prior to the finish and then crystallized at 100 °C for 60 min in the glove box. AFpPPI synthesized in our lab (8 mg/1 mL CB) was dynamically spin coated at 2000 rpm for 30 s followed up with annealing of 80 °C for 2 min. Then, Spiro-OMeTAD solution ( $  35.0 \times 10^{-3}  $  M LiTFSI,  $  231 \times 10^{-3}  $  M tBP,  $  2.42 \times 10^{-3}  $  FK209) was spin coated at 5000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, a 100 nm gold electrode was vacuum deposited on the Spiro-OMeTAD over layer.

# 2.3. Device characterizations

Surface morphology of the films were characterized using field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) (HITACHI S-4800 field emission scanning electron microscope) and atomic force microscopy (AFM). The AFM images were acquired using (Park System Corp.) in tapping mode. The PL spectra were recorded with Hitachi F-4500 fluorescence spectrophotometers at room temperature. The samples were excited by a pulsed laser with a wavelength of 470 nm. The J-V curves were measured (Oriel® Sol3A™ Class AAA solar simulator, models 94043A) at 25 °C under AM1.5G (100 mW cm $  ^{-2}  $ ) illumination using both forward (from  $  I_{\text{SC}}  $  to  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $ ) and reverse (from  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $  to  $  I_{\text{SC}}  $ ) scan modes adjusted using a standard PV reference cell (2 cm × 2 cm monocrystalline silicon solar cell, calibrated at NREL, Colorado, USA) and a computer controlled Keithley 2400 source measure unit. The incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency spectrum was measured using Oriel® IQE-200™ equipped with a 250 W quartz tungsten halogen lamp as the light source and a monochromator, an optical chopper, a lock-in amplifier, and a calibrated silicon photodetector. Prior to the illumination, the spectral response and light intensity were calibrated using a monosilicon detector. The impedance response was measured over the range of 1 Hz to 1 MHz with an oscillation amplitude of 15 mV under dark condition (Bio-Logic VMP-3). The experimental data were simulated using commercial Z-view software to estimate the values for each component of the corresponding equivalent circuits. WCAs were

# 3.2. Morphology of the bilayer HTM

The surface morphology of the AFpPPI and AFpPPI/Spiro-OMeTAD was analyzed using AFM and FE-SEM to better understand the surface morphology of the new HTM.  $  ^{43}  $   Figure 2 represents the roughness of the films, and the RMS value of the AFpPPI was higher compared to Spiro-OMeTAD film. The roughness values from AFM images (perovskite/AFpPPI and perovskite film) are nearly identical with a uniform porous AFpPPI spin coated as a thin layer on the rough perovskite layer (Figure 2(d)). Two different devices with a configuration of fluorine doped tin oxide (FTO)/TiO  $  _{2}  $  /Cs-perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD and FTO/TiO  $  _{2}  $  /Cs-perovskite/AFpPPI/Spiro-OMeTAD were demonstrated to understand the impact of AFpPPI layer formation on the Spiro-OMeTAD layer. Figure 3 depicts that the AFpPPI layer works as a platform to achieve an extra uniform and flat overlayer and this leads to a highly efficient and homogeneous charge collection towards counter electrode. To understand the surface morphology of the films, FE-SEM was performed for as prepared films. As depicted in Figure 3, the AFpPPI/Spiro-OMeTAD exhibits a smoother surface morphology. A uniform AFpPPI layer was observed when spin coated. The Spiro-OMeTAD layer in Figure 3(a) comprises of several holes due to lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) dopant. Generally, holes of a material offer a pathway between the air and active layer that results low PCE and poor stability. The Spiro-OMeTAD layer with AFpPPI layer shows a flat uniform morphology with LiTFSI,  $  ^{32}  $   signifying that the AFpPPI layer delivers a smooth and pinhole-free Spiro-OMeTAD layer as shown in Figure 3(c). This superior nature of this layer improves PCE, stability and reproducibility. It is noted that Spiro-OMeTAD with AFpPPI delivered smooth surface coverage with higher surface area.

# 3.5. Stability study

Since stability is one of the major factors for the commercialization of PSCs, we examined the stability test for the Spiro-OMeTAD and AFpPPI/Spiro-OMeTAD based devices as shown in Figure 5(d). The aging test was conducted without any encapsulation on the bilayer HTM (temperature:  $ 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , humidity:  $ 40\% $ ) device. The bilayer HTM configured with AFpPPI/Spiro-OMeTAD provides an outstanding long-term durability over  $ 720\mathrm{h} $  with minimal reduction of its original PCE of the PSCs. This longer stability shows the higher hydrophobic nature of the AFpPPI material compared to Spiro-OMeTAD. More importantly, with AFpPPI above Spiro-OMeTAD layer, the moisture penetration into the perovskite layer could be better shielded, thus improv

ing the longevity of PSCs. Overall, the AFpPPI/Spiro-OMeTAD based devices is highly stable compared to the commercial Spiro-OMeTAD based devices. The enhanced long-term durability of the AFpPPI/Spiro-OMeTAD based PSCs were analysed with water contact angle (WCA) to study the effect of water in bilayer and are shown in Figure 6. The measured WCA for Spiro-OMeTAD and AFpPPI/Spiro-OMeTAD were found initial 67.1° and 69.3° and after 10 s the WCA of Spiro-OMeTAD reduced to 25.1°. However, WCA of AFpPPI/Spiro-OMeTAD was stable after 10 s with 69.1° as shown in Figure 6. Since these WCA is better to the bilayer HTM surface,  $  ^{43}  $  which is related to the moisture permeability of the AFpPPI/Spiro-OMeTAD bilayer. Hence, this analysis evidenced the hydrophobic nature of the bilayer films, which therefore enhanced the moisture stability of the AFpPPI/Spiro-OMeTAD based PSCs.
