# Highly stable and Efficient Perovskite Solar Cells Based on FAMA-Perovskite-Cu:NiO Composites with 20.7% Efficiency and 80.5% Fill Factor

Yousheng Wang, Tahmineh Mahmoudi, and Yoon-Bong Hahn*

To solve critical issues related to device stability and performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs),   $  FA_{0.026}MA_{0.974}PbI_{3-\gamma}Cl_{\gamma}-Cu:NiO  $   (formamidinium methylammonium (FAMA)-perovskite-Cu:NiO) and   $  Al_{2}O_{3}/Cu:NiO  $   composites are developed and utilized for fabrication of highly stable and efficient PSCs through fully-ambient-air processes. The FAMA-perovskite-Cu:NiO composite crystals prepared without using any antisolvents not only improve the perovskite film quality with large-size crystals and less grain boundaries but also tailor optical and electronic properties and suppress charge recombination with reduction of trap density. A champion device based on the composites as light absorber and   $  Al_{2}O_{3}/Cu:NiO  $   interfacial layer between electron transport layer and active layer yields power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 20.67% with   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.047 V,   $  J_{SC}  $   of 24.51 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and fill factor of 80.54%. More importantly, such composite-based PSCs without encapsulation show significant enhancement in long-term air-stability, thermal- and photostability with retaining 97% of PCE over 240 d under ambient conditions (25–30 °C, 45–55% humidity).

# 1. Introduction

The advent of metal-halide based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has stunned the photovoltaic community due to a remarkable power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to 25.2% in a decade.  $  ^{[1]}  $   However, for the practical use of PSCs, several issues related to device instability and fabrication processes should be addressed. It is known that organic–inorganic halide perovskite solar cells have successfully achieved a high PCE by using antisolvent engineering inside glovebox or vapor-assisted strategy because the pristine perovskite (for example, MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) is not stable under ambient conditions. The main purpose of antisolvent-engineering strategy is to assist the

recombination, even accelerating device degradation.  $  ^{[2]}  $   Furthermore, the antisolvent-assisted perovskite crystal growth strategy is incompatible with the scalable fabrication methods such as roll-to-roll and doctor-blade printing techniques.  $  ^{[3]}  $

As well known, the methylammonium (MA) lead triiodide   $  \left(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{PbI}_{3}\right.  $   or   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   based cells easily degrade under direct exposure to sunlight due to the intrinsic thermal and moisture-laden air instability of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   and the phase transition from tetragonal to cubic structures at about 57 °C.  $  ^{[4]}  $   Besides, it was found that in addition to ions migration,   $  I^{-}  $  and   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions in the perovskite are easily oxidized and reduced to   $  I^{0}  $   and metallic   $  Pb^{0}  $   under thermal stress and light illumination, which causes charges recombination, lowers device performance, and accelerates the degradation of perovskite.  $  ^{[5]}  $   Furthermore, the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   is extremely vulnerable to moisture air to decompose into   $  PbI_{2}  $ ,   $  NH_{3}  $ , and   $  CH_{3}  $ .  $  ^{[6]}  $   As compared to the widely used   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ , formamidinium (FA) lead triiodide   $  \left(\mathrm{HC}\left(\mathrm{NH}_{2}\right)_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{3}\right.  $   or   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   has a lower bandgap of   $  \approx1.48~eV  $ , broader absorption spectrum, long charge diffusion length, and good photo- and thermal-stability. However, the   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   generally suffers from a structural phase transition from black metastable   $  \alpha  $ -phase   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   (cubic) to yellow wide-bandgap (  $  E_{g}=2.88~eV  $  ) 1D   $  \delta  $ -phase (hexagonal) polymorphs within several hours under ambient conditions, attributed to the presence of an anisotropic strained lattice (111) in intrinsic instability of 3D   $  \alpha  $ -phase and the larger radius of   $  FA^{+}  $  cation (  $  \approx2.79~\AA  $  ) than that of   $  MA^{+}  $  cation (  $  \approx1.8~\AA  $  ).  $  ^{[7]}  $   To address

formation of intermediate phase from perovskite precursor colloids prior to annealing treatment. Although the utilization of antisolvent strategy is beneficial for the growth of perovskite crystals having large grains and pinhole free coverage and uniformity, the halogenated antisolvents such as toluene and chlorobenzene are toxic and detrimental to the environment. Meanwhile, it is difficult to control the dosing parameters such as antisolvent volume and deposition time, which often trigger spatially inhomogeneous nucleation and unfavorable surface crystal morphology, so it becomes a burden for device reproducibility and large-scale solar cell fabrication. In this work, to solve both the stability and performance issues of PSCs, we prepared the FAMA-perovskite-Cu:NiO (i.e.,   $  FA_{x}MA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Cl_{y}-Cu:NiO  $  ) composite without using any antisolvents and fabricated the PSCs through fully-ambient-air processes by incorporating the composite in AL and the mesoporous   $  Al_{2}O_{3}/Cu:NiO  $   in interfacial layer (IL) with fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/c-  $  TiO_{2}/mp-(Al_{2}O_{3}/Cu:NiO)/FAMA-perovskite-Cu:NiO/spiro-OMeTAD/Au  $   configuration. The PSCs based on the FAMA-perovskite-Cu:NiO composite AL with mp-  $  (Al_{2}O_{3}/Cu:NiO)  $   IL enhanced not only the growth of crystalline perovskite with few GBs and few surface defects but also improved the air-, thermal- and photostability, retaining 97% of initial value of PCE over 240 d under ambient conditions.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Characterization of FAMA-Perovskite-Cu:NiO Composites

The FAMA-perovskite-Cu:NiO composites were prepared by nonstoichiometric alloying of   $  MAPbI_{3-y}Cl_{y}-Cu:NiO  $   composite with FA through fully-ambient-air processes. The presence of small quantity of Cu:NiO nanoparticles (size 10–15 nm) in perovskite mixed-solution assists the formation of homogeneous perovskite nucleation and more orientated crystalline domains during crystallization, resulting in highly stable perovskite crystals attributed to the strong chemical interactions and bonding or crosslinking between Cu:NiO and perovskite molecules (for details, see Section 2.5). It is worth mentioning that in the   $  MAPbI_{3-y}Cl_{y}-Cu:NiO  $   composite the atomic ratio of Cl was 0 < y < 0.15 and the Cu-dopant

concentration was 4 at%. To explore the structural and optical properties of the FAMA-mixed perovskite composite films, X-ray diffraction (XRD), UV-visible (UV-vis) absorption, and photoluminescence (PL) spectra were examined as a function of FA content (x) in the composite. Figure 1a shows two intense diffraction peaks of (110) and (220) at 14.12° and 28.44°, which confirm the successful formation of perovskite crystals. Both peaks slightly shifted toward lower angles with FA content (Figure 1b,c), indicating a reduction in lattice parameter and successful incorporation of FA into MAPbI $  _{3-y}  $ Cl $  _{y}  $ . Furthermore, the FA $  _{x}  $ MA $  _{1-x}  $ PbI $  _{3-y}  $ Cl $  _{y}  $ -Cu:NiO composite films (x = 0.026 or 0.5 wt% FA) exhibit the strongest peak and lowest full-width-at-half maximum (FWHM) value at 2θ = 14.12° and 28.44° (Figure 1d). All these provide a strong evidence that an inclusion of slight amount of FA in MAPbI $  _{3-y}  $ Cl $  _{y}  $ -Cu:NiO can effectively improve the growth of crystalline perovskite and the formation of stable cubic perovskite phase. More interestingly, when 2θ is expanded from 11° to 13.3° as a function of x (i.e., orange shadow area in Figure 1e), two distinct peaks at 11.76° and 12.79° were observed, attributed to 1D δ-FAPbI $  _{3}  $  and PbI $  _{2}  $ /PbCl $  _{2}  $ , respectively. It is reported that a slight amount of PbI $  _{2}  $ /PbCl $  _{2}  $  not only can further improve solar cell performance with lower J-V hysteresis but also enhance its stability due to passivation effects, lower defects and more oriented crystall lattice growth direction.[20] Plus, the presence of little amount of δ-FAPbI $  _{3}  $  only at grain boundaries (GBs) (see high-resolution transmission electron microscopic (HR-TEM) images in Figure 4d–f) does not significantly affect solar cell performance, but it can effectively protect perovskite grains from degradation in ambient air conditions. The UV-vis absorption and PL spectra measured from FTO/c-TiO $  _{2}  $ /Al $  _{2}  $ O $  _{3}  $ /Cu:NiO/FA $  _{x}  $ MA $  _{1-x}  $ PbI $  _{3-y}  $ Cl $  _{y}  $ -Cu:NiO/spiro-OMeTAD films show a redshift toward longer wavelength from 793 to 796 nm with FA content (Figure 1g,i). The redshift is attributed to the decrease of bandgap from 1.548 eV (x = 0.0) to 1.499 eV (x = 0.034 or 0.7 wt% FA) (Figure 1h). The FA $  _{x}  $ MA $  _{1-x}  $ PbI $  _{3-y}  $ Cl $  _{y}  $ -Cu:NiO composite films (x = 0.026 or 0.5 wt% FA) show the strongest light absorption and fastest PL quenching, indicating efficient light harvesting and fast charge carriers separation and conduction, which also agrees well with the XRD analysis. Thus, x = 0.026 is an optimal content of FA (Figure 1f). The optimal content of FA was further confirmed in terms of film morphology with the aid of field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) and atomic force microscope (AFM) images shown as a function of FA content (Figure 2a,b and Figure S1, Supporting Information). The MAPbI $  _{3-y}  $ Cl $  _{y}  $ -Cu:NiO film without FA shows small grains, many GBs, and rough surface (root-mean-square (rms) = 113 nm) with small pinholes (Figure 2a and Figure S1a, Supporting Information). By contrast, the FA $  _{x}  $ MA $  _{1-x}  $ PbI $  _{3-y}  $ Cl $  _{y}  $ -Cu:NiO composite film with x = 0.026 on mp-(Al $  _{2}  $ O $  _{3}  $ /Cu:NiO) IL exhibit highly crystallized dense morphology with large grains over 3 μm, less GBs and smoother surface (rms = 80 nm) without observable pinholes (Figure 2b and Figure S1c, Supporting Information). This result verifies that the antisolvent strategy beneficial for the growth of perovskite crystals having large grains and pinhole free coverage and uniformity is not needed in this work. # 2.4. Device Stability

To examine the long-term air-stability, all the as-fabricated FAMA-perovskite-Cu:NiO based champion cells without encapsulation have been exposed to ambient conditions (25–30 °C, 45–55% humidity) under lamplight illumination. Compared to the pristine MAPbI $  _{3-\gamma}  $ Cl $  _{\gamma}  $  and MAPbI $  _{3-\gamma}  $ Cl $  _{\gamma}  $ -Cu:NiO based cells, the FAMA-perovskite-Cu:NiO composite based champion cells with mp-(Al $  _{2}  $ O $  _{3}  $ /Cu:NiO) IL showed an excellent long-term stability with retaining 97% of initial PCE and 96–99% of V $  _{OC}  $ , J $  _{SC}  $ , and FF over 240 d (Figure 4a and Figure S5 and Table S3, Supporting Information). In addition, the thermal- and photostability of the devices were examined by thermally heating at 85 °C in air for 522 h (≈22 d) (Figure 4b) and exposing to 1 sun illumination for 10 min (Figure 4c) and 12 h rest in dark under ambient conditions, respectively. The FAMA-perovskite-Cu:NiO based champion cells exhibited remarkable thermal-stability with retaining ≈90% of the original PCE and 93–99% of V $  _{OC}  $ , J $  _{SC}  $ , and FF over 522 h (Figure 4b and Figure S6 and Table S4, Supporting Information). By contrast, the pristine MAPbI $  _{3-\gamma}  $ Cl $  _{\gamma}  $  showed severely deteriorated thermal stability after merely 120 h (Figure S6 and Table S5, Supporting Information). It is also fascinating to find that the FAMA-perovskite-Cu:NiO based champion cell with mp-(Al $  _{2}  $ O $  _{3}  $ /Cu:NiO) IL presented excellent photostability with retaining 96% of PCE and 97–99% of V $  _{OC}  $ , J $  _{SC}  $ , and FF (Figure S7 and Table S6, Supporting Information) than those of the pristine MAPbI $  _{3-\gamma}  $ Cl $  _{\gamma}  $  (Figure S7 and Table S7, Supporting Information) and MAPbI $  _{3-\gamma}  $ Cl $  _{\gamma}  $ -Cu:NiO based (Figure S7 and Table S8, Supporting Information) cells by J-V sweeps under 1 sun continuous illumination for first 10 min illumination. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, phase-pure nonstoichiometric FAMA-perovskite-Cu:NiO composites not only enhance the charge carriers generation with harvesting more sunlight but also effectively improve charge mobility with the reduction of recombination and trap density, resulting in dramatic enhancement of device performance. The composite-based PSCs with   $  Al_{2}O_{3}/Cu:NiO  $   interface engineering showed PCE of 20.7% and FF of 80.54%. More importantly, the composite-based PSCs without any encapsulation showed not only remarkable enhancement in thermal- and photostability but also long-term air-stability with retaining 97% of the initial PCE over 240 d under ambient conditions. We believe that the FAMA-perovskite-Cu:NiO composites with   $  Al_{2}O_{3}/Cu:NiO  $   interface engineering is an effective strategy to achieve highly stable and efficient PSCs.

# 4. Experimental Section

Synthesis of Methylammonium lodide (MAI) and FAI: The synthesis process of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (MAI) and   $  NH_{2}CH=NH_{2}I  $   (FAI) can be clearly seen in previous works.  $  ^{[12,13,27]}  $   For MAI, 15 mL hydroiodic acid (57 wt% in water, YAKURI) was reacted with 13.96 mL methylamine (40 wt% in water, Sigma-Aldrich) under continuous stirring in a 200 mL round-bottom flask in an ice bath (0 °C) for 2 h. For FAI, similarly, 15 mL hydroiodic acid (57 wt% in water, YAKURI) was reacted with 7.5 g formamidine acetate (Sigma-Aldrich) under continuous stirring in an ice bath (0 °C) for 4 h. And then the well-mixed solution (MAI and FAI) was evaporated in a rotary evaporation system (SB-1200) at 60 °C to remove all solvents and obtain crystal MAI and FAI. As-synthesized MAI and FAI was redissolved in ethyl alcohol (99.9%, SAMCHUM CHEMICALS) and recrystallized with diethyl ether (99.5%, SAMCHUM PURE CHEMICALS) for several times, and finally dried at 60 °C in a vacuum oven for 24 h.

Preparation of Cu Doped NiO Nanoparticles (Cu:NiO NPs): The preparation of Cu-doped NiO nanoparticles (Cu:NiO NPs) is available in ref. [16]. Briefly,  $ 98 \times 10^{-3} $  M nickel (II) nitrate hexahydrate  $ (\mathrm{Ni(NO_3)_2\cdot 6H_2O} $ , Sigma-Aldrich) and  $ 1.96 \times 10^{-3} $  M copper (II) nitrate trihydrate  $ (\mathrm{Cu(NO_3)_2\cdot 3H_2O} $ , Sigma-Aldrich) were added into  $ 100~\mathrm{mL} $  deionized (DI) water under continuous stirring to obtain a dark green transparent solution. Next, as-prepared NaOH solution  $ (10\mathrm{mol}L^{-1}) $  was added dropwise to the above solution until  $ \mathsf{pH} = 10 $  under continuous stirring for  $ 5\mathrm{min} $ . Then, as-formed colloidal precipitates were thoroughly washed with DI water several times and dried at  $ 80^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  under oven for  $ 6\mathrm{h} $ . Finally, the obtained green powders were calcined at  $ 270^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 2\mathrm{h} $  to obtain dark-black  $ 2\mathrm{at}\% $  Cu doped NiO NPs  $ (10 - 15\mathrm{nm}) $ .

Preparation of   $  FA_{x}MA_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Cl_{y}-Cu:NiO  $   Composites: For preparation of FAMA-perovskite-Cu:NiO composite solution, multisources including   $  2.99 \times 10^{-3}  $   M MAI,   $  0.80 \times 10^{-3}  $   M Lead (II) chloride (PbCl  $  _{2}  $  , 99.999%, Sigma-Aldrich), and   $  0.2 \times 10^{-3}  $   M lead (II) acetate trihydrate (Pb(CH  $  _{3}  $  CO  $  _{2}  $  )  $  _{2}  $  ·3H  $  _{2}  $  O, 99.999%, Sigma-Aldrich) in 1 mL were used to formulate 45 wt% MAPbI  $  _{3-y}  $  Cl  $  _{y}  $   (nonstoichiometric ratio MAI: (PbCl  $  _{2}  $   + Pb(CH  $  _{3}  $  CO  $  _{2}  $  )  $  _{2}  $  ·3H  $  _{2}  $  O) = 3:1) and 1.5 wt% Cu:NiO loading with nonstoichiometry of different contents of FAI and then ultrasonicated for 30 min. Finally, as-prepared composite solution was homogenized for 20 min in a homogenizer (THINKY Mixer System, K-CORPORATION), followed by stirring overnight.

Devices Fabrication: All the fabrication processes of PSCs were carried out under ambient conditions (45–50% relative humidity), not in a glovebox. FTO substrates were thoroughly cleaned by using detergent, diluted water, acetone, and ethanol. As-prepared   $  TiO_{2}  $   precursor (16 wt% titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) in butanol) was spin coated on FTO substrates at 4000 rpm for 40 s; then,   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   solution (1 mL 20 wt% colloidal   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   was diluted in 3 mL isopropanol) and Cu:NiO NPs solutions (0.5 wt% Cu:NiO NPs in water) were spin coated successively on the FTO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   substrates at 4000 rpm for 40 s; after each step was followed by annealing at 250 °C for 10 min. The preheated   $  FA_{x}MAO_{1-x}PbI_{3-y}Cl_{y}-Cu:NiO  $   solution at 130 °C was then spin coated on the FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}/Al_{2}O_{3}/Cu:NiO  $  /at 1500 rpm for 25 s, followed by annealing at 130 °C for 1.5 h. After cooling down to room temperature (RT), the hole-transport material (i.e., spiro-OMeTAD) was deposited on the top of composite film by spin coating at 4000 rpm for 40 s under ambient air environment. Finally, Au electrodes were deposited through a shadow mask in a thermal evaporator (JV15EVA-15K2P). The dimension of the tested solar cells were 3 cm × 3 cm. The device performances were examined with 0.17 cm² mask per aperture.
