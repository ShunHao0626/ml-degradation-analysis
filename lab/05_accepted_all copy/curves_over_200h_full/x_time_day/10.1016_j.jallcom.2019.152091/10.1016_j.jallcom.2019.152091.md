# Abstract

An efficient planar perovskite solar cell (PSC) was fabricated with a bi-layer consisting of the poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophne):poly(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) and sulfonic acid functionalized graphene oxide (SrGO) as a hole interfacial layer. The low temperature and solution processed PEDOT:PSS/SrGO interlayer produced better power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 16.01\% $  than other single layers. Comprehensive investigations reveal that the increased PCE is mostly due to the decreased recombination and the increased built in potential for better charge transport and extraction. In addition, the device with PEDOT:PSS/SrGO showed excellent long-term stability in ambient air conditions. Consequently, these results support that the introduction of graphene materials into interlayers can be a promising approach for high performance and cost-efficient solar devices.

Keywords: Perovskite solar cell; Hole transporting layer; Reduced graphene oxide; PEDOT:PSS; Stability.

# 1. Introduction

Hybrid lead halide-based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been focused as a promising next-generation solar cell technology due to the superior optical and electrical properties, simple manufacturing processes, and low-cost production [1-3]. In the last few years, the PSC efficiency has been achieved up to 23.7% due to the rapid development and improvement in the key materials, process, and device research fields [4-7]. In perovskite solar cells, mostly two types of cell structures were used: mesoporous and inverted planar structures [8]. Mesoporous structure PSC generally consisted of a front electrode/compact TiO₂/mesoporous TiO₂/perovskite/hole transport layer (HTL)/a rear electrode. However, this device could consume high energy due to the mesoporous TiO₂ as it required a high temperature sintering procedure of 500°C. Hence, this material cannot be augmented in flexible device applications [9]. Nowadays, p-i-n type inverted structure solar cells have gained considerable attention because of their simple fabrication methods with low temperature and negligible photocurrent density-voltage (J-V) hysteresis [10-12]. The inverted planar structure usually consists of poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophne):poly(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) as HTL and [6,6]phentyl-C₆₁-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) as electron transport layer (ETL). Specially, the PEDOT:PSS could be the most popular HTL in solar devices, because it involves low temperature processability and simple solution-process ability [13-15]. However, the device stability and the relatively low efficiency of the PEDOT:PSS as a HTL material still remain a problem [16-18].

Recently, for the better HTL fabrication, many trials have been conducted on the use of two different materials to provide synergetic effect on the hole transporting ability   $  [19-25]  $  . Specially, the effectiveness of graphene derivatives (graphene oxide (GO) or reduced graphene oxide (rGO)) for composite or bilayer interlayers has been recently reported   $  [23-26]  $  . In fact, with

their excellent features including low temperature solution-processability, simple conductivity controllability, and high material stability, [18, 27] graphene material based composite or bilayer HTLs improved the device-efficiency and stability compared to the single layer HTL [23-25]. However, GO has an insulating property with low conductivity, and the conventional and commercial rGO could have poor dispersion-ability and aggregation problems due to highly limited hydrophilic functional groups [26].

In this work, we report the effects of the PEDOT:PSS/SrGO double HTL on the device-performance and stability, and this double layer was investigated in planar p-i-n structure perovskite solar cells as a novel HTL. We found that the PEDOT:PSS performance was enhanced with the aid of recently synthesized sulfonic acid functionalized rGO (SrGO) to provide a higher work function and conductivity than conventional graphene derivatives and PEDOT:PSS and also provide a higher solution dispersion-ability than conventional rGO [27]. As a result, the PSCs based on PEDOT:PSS/SrGO HTL produced a highly enhanced PCE of  $ 16.01\% $ , compared to PCEs of  $ 13.5\% $  and  $ 11.66\% $  for control cells with the single PEDOT:PSS and SrGO HTL layer. In addition, with the use of PEDOT:PSS/SrGO, the PSC-stability was improved, compared to the PEDOT:PSS HTL probably due to the use of more hydrophobic and chemically stable SrGO, thus supporting that the PEDOT:PSS/SrGO device can provide both high efficiency and excellent stability simultaneously, which could be difficult to be achieved in single HTL-based devices.

Solution of PEDOT:PSS was prepared by mixing 1 ml of pristine PEDOT:PSS (Clevios™ P VP AI 4083, Heraeus), 60 mg of sodium polystyrene sulfonate (Sigma Aldrich), and 5 ml of deionized water. This solution was stirred using magnetic stirrer for 10 min. Synthesis of graphene oxide was carried out using the well known modified Hummer's method [28]. Synthesis of SrGO was done using 4-hydrazino benzene sulfonic acid as the reducing agent [27]. For the reduction, 15 ml of GO solution with of 0.5 mg/ml concentration was ultrasonicated for 5 min followed by adding 600 mg of 4-hydrazino benzenesulfonic acid with stirring for 5 hours. The solution was then washed with water and DMF for 3 times to remove impurities. Finally, SrGO was dispersed in DMF (0.5 ml/mg). Perovskite solar cells were fabricated on glass substrates with pre-patterned indium tin oxide (ITO). The glass substrates were sequentially clean with deionized water, ethanol, acetone, and iso-propanol under ultrasonication, and then dried and treated with UV-Ozone cleaning for 10 mins. The PEDOT:PSS layer was spin-coated onto the cleaned ITO substrates at 3000 rpm for 40s and annealed at 150°C in ambient atmosphere for 10 min by using hot plate. SrGO was identically spin-coated at 3000rpm for 40s and then annealed at 120°C in ambient atmosphere for 10 min by using hot plate. The perovskite solution was prepared by dissolving 1.2 M lead iodide (PbI₂) and 1.2 M methyl ammonium iodide (MAI) with the equivalent molar ratio in a mixed solution of dimethyl formamide (DMF) and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (volume ratio 2:1). The perovskite film was fabricated by spin-coating the precursor solution onto the PEDOT:PSS, SrGO, and PEDOT:PSS/SrGO with a successive spinning condition of 500 rpm for 10s and 4000 rpm for 45s. During this process, a 200 μL of chlorobenzene was dripped onto the perovskite film as an anti-solvent treatment. After this, all samples were annealed at 100°C for 10 min. The electron transport layer, PCBM, was spin-coated onto perovskite films at 1000 rpm for 60s. Finally, we

The device configuration and chemical structure of SrGO used in this study are illustrated in Fig. 1(a). The p-i-n planar perovskite device was manufactured with an arrangement of ITO/HTL/CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /PCBM/BCP/Ag. The current density and voltage curves (J-V) of all PSCs with PEDOT:PSS, SrGO, and PEDOT:PSS/SrGO were shown in Fig. 1(b). The device having PEDOT:PSS as the HTL showed the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 13.5\% $  accompanied by an open-circuit voltage (Voc) of  $ 0.99\mathrm{V} $ , a short-circuit current density (Jsc) of  $ 18.32\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , and fill factor (FF) of  $ 76.4\% $ . These observations indicate the insertion of PEDOT:PSS/SrGO bilayer can be an effective approach to increase the performance of the p-i-n planar-structure PSCs.

To study why the double layer showed a more improved cell-efficiency, the quantum efficiency was investigated as shown in Fig. 2 (a) and (b). As shown in Fig. 2(a), the PEDOT:PSS/SrGO based device resulted in higher EQE than that of other single HTLs. In addition, Jsc values calculated from the EQE data were all within  $ 3\% $  error with the Jsc values of the J-V curve. More importantly, it was confirmed that the higher EQE of the PEDOT:PSS/SrGO device mainly resulted from the IQE enhancement as shown in Fig. 2(b). These results imply that enhanced EQE obtained with the PEDOT:PSS/SrGO was due to the enhancement of charge transport capability, thereby providing a higher Jsc, FF, and PCE in PSCs [29, 30].

To further study the reason why PEDOT:PSS/SrGO showed enhanced cell performances, various investigations on the absorption, work function (WF), and AFM were analyzed as shown in Fig. 3. As shown in the Fig 3 (a), there is no significant difference in the absorption properties, but WF values to have a significant influence on the PSC-efficiency showed somewhat large difference as shown in Fig.3 (b) and (c) [31]. WF of PEDOT:PSS was estimated to be  $ 5.02\mathrm{eV} $ , while the WF of PEDOT:PSS/SrGO was  $ 5.34\mathrm{eV} $ , which is quite similar to the WF of SrGO  $ (5.38\mathrm{eV}) $ . This indicates that the larger WF of the PEDOT:PSS/SrGO double layer is due to incorporation of SrGO, which has sulfonic acid groups on the graphene nanosheets to produce a negative dipole moment from the graphene surface [32-34]. In particular, considering that the higher WF to increase the internal built-in field and minimizing the energy level

For detailed studies on carrier transport properties of HTL, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra, shunt or series resistance, and Voc dependence on different light intensities were analyzed as presented in Fig. 4. As shown in Fig. 4(a), PEDOT:PSS/SrGO showed better PL quenching effect than other single layers. This could indicate that the holes generated in perovskite layer are more efficiently transported with the use of the PEDOT:PSS/SrGO double layer, and the carrier recombination in bi-layer based PSCs is relatively decreased than those in single layer based PSCs [41-43]. For better comparative investigation, the series resistance (Rs) and shunt resistance (Rsh) were also calculated as shown in Fig. 4(b). Fig. 4(b) showed that the average Rs and Rsh of PEDOT:PSS, SrGO, and PEDOT:PSS/SrGO were 1.4 and  $ 2608\Omega \mathrm{cm}^2 $ , 1.6 and  $ 1676\Omega \mathrm{cm}^2 $ , and 1.1 and  $ 3623\Omega \mathrm{cm}^2 $ , respectively; the PEDOT:PSS/SrGO provided the lower Rs and higher Rsh, indicating superior charge transport ability of the bi-layer probably due to its higher WF and lower roughness [18, 41-44]. This statement could be consistent with the dependence of Voc on the light intensity. As

Finally, to further confirm that the PEDOT:PSS/SrGO can be a better alternative to PEDOT:PSS even in terms of cell-stability as well as cell-efficiency, we examined the device stability as a function of time to an ambient environment condition according to the ISOS-D-1 protocol [27]. In Fig. 5, the photovoltaic characteristics of PEDOT:PSS based devices showed a continuous and somewhat rapid degradation under the ambient air. Meanwhile PEDOT:PSS/SrGO based devices showed quite better stability than the PEDOT:PSS single-layer devices. It could be explained that the perovskite layers are easily degraded in oxygen and moisture condition, thus the interlayers near the perovskite layer should function as a protection film against oxygen and moisture [25]. In addition, as is well known, PEDOT:PSS has the hydrophilic and high acidic nature to induce decomposition of the ITO electrode and perovskite, which limits the stability and performance of perovskite optoelectronic devices [24, 50]. In contrast, graphene sheet is a single atomic carbon layer with honeycomb lattice to provide a noticeable stability for chemical agents and to efficiently block the penetration of small molecules like oxygen, hydrogen, and water [51, 52]. Therefore, the improved device-stability could be due to hydrophobicity and chemical inertness properties of the graphene sheet in SrGO,

# 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, we demonstrated that the combination of PEDOT:PSS and SrGO material can noticeably raise the cell-efficiency and stability of p-i-n structure PSCs. The PSC having the PEDOT:PSS/SrGO HTL showed a quite higher PCE of  $ 16.01\% $  than those of the single PEDOT:PSS  $ (13.5\%) $  and SrGO  $ (11.66\%) $  HTL. Inclusive examinations revealed that the PCE improvement in the PEDOT:PSS/SrGO based PSCs mostly originated from better energy-levels matching, lower recombination, and better charge transport than those of single HTLs. In addition, with the aid of PEDOT:PSS/SrGO HTL, the device-stability was more enhanced than the PEDOT:PSS probably due to the use of SrGO having hydrophobic and chemical inertness properties, thus suggesting that the PEDOT:PSS/SrGO combination can provide both high efficiency and excellent device-stability simultaneously, which could be quite difficult to be obtained in conventional single HTLs. These overall observations confirm that the hybrid interlayers including graphene materials can be a versatile approach for the realization of higher performance and stable PSCs.

▶ Bi-layer consisting of the PEDOT:PSS and sulfonic acid functionalized graphene oxide (SrGO) as a hole interfacial layer is introduced in perovskite solar cells (PSCs).

The PEDPT:PSS/SrGO enables better energy-level matching, lower recombination, and better charge transport.

The PEDPT:PSS/SrGO based PSCs provides a higher cell-efficiency and better stability than PEDOT:PSS based PSCs.
