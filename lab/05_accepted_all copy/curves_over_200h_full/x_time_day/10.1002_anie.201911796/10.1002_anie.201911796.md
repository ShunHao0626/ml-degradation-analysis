# Enhanced Lifetime and Photostability with Low-temperature Mesoporous  $ \mathrm{ZnTiO_3} $ /Compact  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  Electrodes in Perovskite Solar Cells

Fengwan Guo[a], Xiangyu Sun[b], Bing Liu[c], Zijiang Yang[a], Jing Wei[d]*, Dongsheng Xu[a]*

Abstract: Perovskite solar cells (PSC) which have exceeded power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of 25% are mainly demonstrated by using   $  SnO_{2}  $   or   $  TiO_{2}  $   as electron-transporting layers (ETLs). However, high-performance planar PSCs need precise process, which is difficult for large-scale production. Mesoporous structure shows better operability but with high-temperature process. Besides, as the most used mesoporous materials, the strong photocatalytic effect of   $  TiO_{2}  $   significantly limits the practical stability of PSCs under illumination (including ultraviolet light). Here we propose Zinc Titanate (  $  ZnTiO_{3}  $  , ZTO) as mesoporous ETLs due to its weak photo-effect, excellent carrier extraction and transfer properties. Uniform mesoporous films were obtained by spinning coating the ZTO ink and annealed below 150°C. Photovoltaic devices based on   $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.81}MA_{0.14}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $   perovskite sandwiched between   $  SnO_{2}  $  -mesorporous ZTO electrode and Spiro-OMeTAD layer achieved the PCE of 20.5%. The PSCs retained more than 95% of their original efficiency after 100 days lifetime test without being encapsulated. Additionally, the PSCs retained over 95% of the initial performance when subjected at the maximum power point voltage for 120 h under AM 1.5G illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ), demonstrating superior working stability. The application of ZTO provides a better choice for ETLs of PSCs. Moreover, the low temperature deposition method of inorganic ETL furnishes a way of low power consumption, large-scale and flexible preparation of PSCs.

This high-temperature process not only limits the selection of many compact layers and electrode substrates which cannot withstand heat, such as indium doped tin oxide (ITO) and many organic ETLs; but also restricts the application of flexible substrates and complicates the manufacturing process  $  ^{[33]}  $  .

Today, the efficiency of PSCs has grown from 3.8% in single-junction solar cells in 2008 to more than 25%  $  ^{[1]}  $  , and the tandem solar cells has even reached 28%  $  ^{[2]}  $  . Up to now, the key issues of PSC have become its stability, performance and large-scale production.  $  ^{[3-8]}  $   This requires optimization of the film morphology, interface, device structure and the fabrication process.  $  ^{[9]}  $   Among various structures,   $  SnO_{2}  $  -ETLs shows better application potential  $  ^{[10]}  $   than the traditional   $  TiO_{2}  $  -ETL (generally requires high annealing temperature to rutile phase)  $  ^{[11]}  $   for their good performance and facile process, especially the ultraviolet light (UV)-inert characters. As the strong photocatalytic activity of   $  TiO_{2}  $   often leads to unstable operating state and hysteresis current-voltage curves of PSCs, the UV-inert   $  SnO_{2}  $  -ETLs now are widely used in PSCs.

A suitable ETL should meet some basic requirements for high device efficiency  $  ^{[12]}  $  . SnO  $  _{2}  $  -based devices typically require an ETL of less than 30 nm to achieve high electron mobility and efficiently extract carriers from the active layer to avoid charge recombination  $  ^{[13, 14]}  $  . Such thin films need precise process, which is not suitable for large-scale production. In addition, the wettability of the SnO  $  _{2}  $   film to the perovskite precursor solution is poor, affecting the process of film formation and crystallization of the perovskite  $  ^{[15, 16]}  $  . With this in mind, mesoporous PSCs demonstrate great development prospect due to their tremendous compatibility with the process.  $  ^{[17, 18]}  $   What's more, mesoporous structure provides protection for perovskite from water and oxygen in air, greatly improving the stability and lifetime of devices.  $  ^{[19-21]}  $   In fact, it has recently been demonstrated that the interface between the perovskite and the transport layer can significantly affect the charge recombination process and material stability within the working device.  $  ^{[22, 23]}  $   The infiltration of perovskite into mesoporous layer enlarges charge separation region at the perovskite/ETL interface, allowing rapid electron-injection into ETL from the active layer, then resulting in slower degradation of perovskite and non-radiative recombination reduction.  $  ^{[23-30]}  $   Nowadays, PSCs with PCE of more than 22% and lifetime of more than 1 year are mostly equipped with thin mesoporous TiO  $  _{2}  $   (mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $  ) layers  $  ^{[9, 31, 32]}  $  . Therefore, considering device performance, lifetime, stability and industrialization, compact SnO  $  _{2}  $  -ETL combined with mesoporous layer will be a good choice for PSCs.

Besides, as the most commonly used mesoporous material,   $  TiO_{2}  $   usually leads to light-induced instability for its intense photocatalysis, which needs complex device optimization. For instance, Leijtens, T. et al.  $  ^{[34]}  $   and Seok et al.  $  ^{[35]}  $   proposed and further proved the UV-induced degradation of the   $  TiO_{2}  $  -perovskite interface.

To overcome these problems, we introduced   $  ZnTiO_{3}  $   (ZTO) as a new mesoporous layer material for perovskite solar cells. Compared with   $  TiO_{2}^{[36]}  $  , ZTO possesses little photocatalysis, similar wide bandgap and excellent chemical stability. However, the crystallization temperature of ZTO under conventional methods is about 1100 °C, which is not suitable to deposit on glass. Here we employed a sol-gel method to reduce the processing temperature to 600 °C and got uniform ZTO nanoparticles (NPs) of about ~15 nm. Then the particles were dispersed into 2-methoxyethanol with PEG as surfactant to form ZTO ink. Mesoporous ZTO film can be deposited by spin coating the ZTO ink and annealing at 150 °C. With proper energy level, the meso-ZTO film can be used for PSCs with different compact layers, for example,   $  TiO_{2}  $  , ZTO,   $  SnO_{2}  $   and ZnO ETL et al. In addition to exacted electrons, the mesoporous layer can modify the interface between compact layer and perovskite. What's more, this low-temperature ZTO mesoporous layer avoids many interface problems in other structures, for example, the photo-induced defect from   $  TiO_{2}^{[37]}  $  , film pinholes of   $  SnO_{2}^{[16]}  $  , surface recombination and poor stability of ZnO-based PSCs  $  ^{[12]}  $  , etc. For the non-infiltration of   $  SnO_{2}  $  -ETL and the interface of perovskite/  $  SnO_{2}  $  , firstly, the application of ZTO mesoporous layer aids in the growth of perovskite crystals by increasing the number of nucleation sites available for layer formation  $  ^{[38]}  $  ; secondly, ZTO layer reduces the trap state density on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer and improves the surface wettability, thereby ameliorating the morphology of the perovskite film  $  ^{[39-41]}  $  ; thirdly, protects perovskite at the perovskite-ETL interface, thus improving the stability of devices.

Based on   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}/mesoporous-ZTO  $   (mp-ZTO), we fabricated a low-temperature prepared photovoltaic device with a 1.63-eV-band gap perovskite (ie   $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.81}MA_{0.14}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $  ). Compared to   $  SnO_{2}  $  -based control devices, the best mp-ZTO-based devices achieve a higher power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 20.5%, with an open circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.13 V, and a fill factor (FF) of 80%. Compared to 5% of   $  SnO_{2}  $  -PSCs, the inter-batch PCE variation of mesoporous ZTO-based equipment was reduced to 2.5%. Additionally, with UV-inert ETL, the PSCs retained over 95% of the initial performance when subjected at the maximum power point voltage (  $  V_{MPP}  $  ) for 120 h under AM 1.5G illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ), demonstrating superior working stability. This change broadens the choice of ETL for the mesoporous structure and opens up novel thinking for the selection and matching of PSC materials with different structures.

To investigate the characteristics of spin-coated ZTO films, a well-dispersed ZTO solution containing high purity ZTO powder is required. A common sol-gel method was used for the synthesis of ZTO (more details see Experimental Section in Supporting Information). The structural characterization of the as-prepared samples was performed by power x-ray diffraction (XRD) technique. The XRD patterns of ZTO is shown in Figure 1a. For the ZTO sample, the peaks at  $ (30.03^{\circ}, 35.38^{\circ}, 42.99^{\circ}, 53.34^{\circ}, 56.86^{\circ} $  and  $ 62.44^{\circ}) $  are well matched with the cubic-structured

ZnTiO  $  _{3}  $  (JCPDS 39-0190). No obvious impurity crystalline peak is detected, revealing the high purity of ZTO.

Furthermore, the hierarchical energy arrangement of the CBM between SnO $ _2 $ , ZTO and perovskites facilitates carrier transport and collection, which is a prerequisite for achieving superior device performance. To reveal the role of the present mesoporous ZTO-ETL, the planar PSCs with compact  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  layers were also prepared for comparison. Figure 3a shows the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra of perovskite films on glass,  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ , and SPM films, respectively. The PL peaks of the perovskite deposited on  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  and ZTO were significantly quenched, indicating that  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  is aligned with the ideal band edge of the perovskite. Time-resolved PL (TRPL) spectroscopy was used to further confirm the effective charge transfer kinetics between perovskite and ETL as shown in Figure 3b. The  $ \tau_{\mathrm{ave}} $  for the pristine perovskite-glass only sample is 238 ns, indicative of the high-quality film. The average PL decay time of the perovskite is reduced to 88 ns and 77 ns upon introducing the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  and mp-ZTO ETL respectively, indicating effective electron transfer of  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  and mp-ZTO/ $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  ETLs. It is well known that reduced PL decay time facilitates suppression of charge recombination at the interface and effectively improves device performance. The new

structure can not only increase the contact area of ETL/perovskite, but also shorten the electron diffusion path in the perovskite layer, which is beneficial to charge separation. Moreover, the bilayered ETL structure results in the formation of graded energy alignment at the interface, improving carrier transport between each layer.

Figure 4a and Figure 4b reflect the cells' efficiency and working state over time after 10 days aging test in ambient air. In Figure 4a, the  $ J - V $  characteristics of the device based on mp-ZTO is basically unchanged, while the efficiency of the control one reduces to  $ 13.2\% $ . The steady-state output efficiency measured at the maximum power point voltage was then investigated to evaluate the working state of the mesoscopic and planar devices, as shown in Figure 4b. The PCE of the mesoscopic device is stable at  $ \approx 19.9\% $  and remained stable for  $ 1000~s $ , compared to a steady output efficiency of  $ 13.5\% $  for planar devices and continued attenuation. These results demonstrate the superiority of the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{ZTO} $  mesoporous structure in achieving efficient and stable PSC devices. The improvement in stability attributes to the better interface properties, followed by the mesoporous layer's protection of the interface. As mentioned in many device stability reports for n-i-p structure, the long-term stability of PSC is significantly affected by the ETL/perovskite interface.[16, 24, 25, 42, 43] The fatal short plate in the n-i-p device structure originates from the electrons accumulated at the interface and the water/oxygen permeation from the periphery of the device. Therefore, improving the bonding ability of the ETL/perovskite interface can reduce the accumulation of electrons and water/oxygen permeation at the interface and thus enhance the stability of the n-i-p device.[44, 45]

Using mp-ZTO, the device performances are expected to be improved for three reasons: First, for   $  SnO_{2}  $   film thickness below 30 nm, pinholes can be reduced by the mesoporous layer; second, the hierarchical energy arrangement of the CBM between ETL and perovskites facilitates carrier transport and collection; last but not the least, the protection of mesoporous layer to perovskite at the interface helps to improve the stability, as well as the performance of devices. The stability of the device determines the potential for future industrial production of PSC. As shown in Figure 4c, PCE of the unpackaged device maintains its initial efficiency of more than 95% after storage for 100 days in an environment with a humidity range of 30% to 80%, which proves its stability and high resistance to humidity. After such a long period of aging, the J-V characteristics of ZTO-PSCs shows excellent performance, even with a increased open-circuit voltage (Figure S2, Table S2), indicating that both crystal quality of perovskite and the interfaces are not greatly destroyed. The increased   $  V_{OC}  $   may be attributed to full oxidation of spiro-OMeTAD, which we will study in our next work. Figure 4d shows a histogram of the PCE distribution for 100 devices fabricated on   $  SnO_{2}-ZTO  $   or   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL. The mesoporous PSC has excellent reproducibility and a narrow PCE distribution of 18.5-21%, while the PCE distribution of planar PSC is more extensive, ranging from 15% to 20%. The higher reproducibility could be mainly attributed to better interface and feasibility of   $  SnO_{2}-ZTO  $   PSCs. It is clear that the corresponding distribution was narrower than the control device.

To further study the characteristics of mesoporous ZTO on PSCs, the devices were compared with the conventional titanium dioxide (TiO $ _2 $ ) mesoporous-structured PSCs, as FTO/ c-TiO $ _2 $ / mp-TiO $ _2 $ / perovskite/ spiro-OMeTAD/ Au. The result is analyzed in the supporting information, and PSC based on c-SnO $ _2 $ /mp-ZTO shows obviously better performance, suppressed hysteresis and improved operating stability than its TiO $ _2 $ -based competitor (Figure S3), due to the optimized ETL/perovskite interface and UV-inert character of ZTO and SnO $ _2 $ . To investigate the effects of UV light on the degradation of PSCs, the devices were exposed to UV illumination (365 nm, 15 mW cm $ ^{-2} $ ) in air (20%~50% humidity, 25-35°C) or in a glove box (Nitrogen atmosphere, 25-30°C). Here pure UV light with the intensity about 3 times higher than that in the AM 15.G 100mW/cm $ ^2 $  were used to eliminate the effects of heat, moisture, oxygen, and longer wavelength light et al. Figure S4 shows the crystal quality of perovskite on different substrates before and after UV illumination (Supporting Information). Both in air and in glovebox, perovskite on mp-TiO $ _2 $  film showed obvious peaks of PbI $ _2 $ , while perovskite on mp-ZTO film showed negligible change. Further analysis shows that the UV instability of TiO $ _2 $ -PSCs is mainly due to the photocatalysis of TiO $ _2 $  under UV illumination. This result confirms our speculation that the UV-inert property of mp-ZTO layer can effectively improve the stability of perovskite in devices, either in air or under UV light.

Figure 4e shows the long time continuous operating state of a 10-days-aged mp-ZTO PSCs at the maximum power point under standard AM 1.5G illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). In summary, we have developed a low-temperature solution-processed treatment for UV-inert mesoporous layers  $ (\mathrm{ZnTiO_3}) $  to improve the performance and stability of PSCs. In addition to higher PCE, the formation of mesoporous structures and well-matched energy levels result in better transfer and collection of charge carriers. The mesoporous structure device achieved stabilized PCE of  $ \approx 20\% $  under continuous illumination (AM 1.5G) as long as 5 days, demonstrating excellent long-term operational stability. With simple, low-temperature process and interface modification, ZTO mesoporous layer effectively improves the process feasibility and reproducibility. It as well breaks through the estrangement of the choice for transmission layer and electrode materials between planar structure and mesoporous structure due to high or low temperature requirements. This work not only expands the scope of material selection, but also offers a new way to large-scale production of flexible PSC's.

Enhanced Lifetime and Photostability with Low-temperature Mesoporous  $ \mathrm{ZnTiO_3} $  /Compact  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  Electrodes in Perovskite Solar Cells

Low-temperature-processed, UV-inert   $  ZnTiO_{3}  $   as mesoporous layer was introduced in   $  SnO_{2}  $  -based planar perovskite solar cells. The application of   $  ZnTiO_{3}  $   modified the interface of   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite, and significantly improve the UV stability and lifetime of devices. Low-temperature mesoporous structure not only expands the choice for transmission layer and electrode materials, but also offers a new way to large-scale production of flexible devices.
