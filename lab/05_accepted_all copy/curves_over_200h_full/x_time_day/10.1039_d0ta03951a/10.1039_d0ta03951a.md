# Spiro-OMeTAD or CuSCN as a preferable hole transport material for carbon-based planar perovskite solar cells?

Yang Yang, Minh Tam Hoang, Disheng Yao, Ngoc Duy Pham, Vincent Tiing Tiong, Xiaoxiang Wang, Hongxia Wang *

School of Chemistry, Physics and Mechanical Engineering, Science and Engineering Faculty, Queensland University of Technology, Brisbane 4001, Australia

*Corresponding Author.

E-mail address: hx.wang@qut.edu.au (H.X. Wang).

# ABSTRACT

A hole transport layer (HTL) plays the role of extracting hole carriers while improving interfacial contacts of perovskite/carbon in a planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells using carbon based electrode (C-PSCs). For future application of C-PSCs, HTL also needs to have good stability and easy to process while maintaining high device efficiency. Herein, we compare the behaviour of the most widely used HTL based on spiro-OMeTAD to the much less studied HTL based on CuSCN in planar C-PSCs. The results show that 14.7% of power conversion efficiency (PCE) is obtained for CuSCN based C-PSCs with good reproducibility and negligible hysteresis behaviour. In contrast, the C-PSCs using spiro-OMeTAD shows a much lower PCE (12.4%) and significant hysteresis phenomenon. We conduct a systematic characterisation of the electronic and energetic properties of the C-PSCs to understand this phenomenon. We find that a more favourable energy level alignment of CuSCN with the perovskite than spiro-OMeTAD leads to reduced charge injection barrier, which favours a more efficient hole extraction capability, inhibited carrier recombination and reduced ionic capacitance. This in turn leads to better PCE and lower hysteresis with the C-PSCs. Moreover, the CuSCN based C-PSCs also demonstrates better stability against moisture with high PCE retention ratio of 93% under a humid environment (55-70%) for 80 days without encapsulation.

# 1. Introduction

The unprecedented advancement of power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) makes this new photovoltaics (PV) technology highly promising in the future PV market to deliver cost-effective solar electricity.  $  ^{1-3}  $   However, most of the state-of-the-art PSCs employ noble metals such as gold as back contact and expensive organic semiconductor materials as hole transport layer (HTL), which yield high performance but sacrificing cost and life-span of the devices.  $  ^{4-5}  $   To address these issues, carbon materials, which are cheap, conductive and chemically inert and resistance to moisture, have been exploited as either hole conductor or current collector for PSCs.  $  ^{6-8}  $   Although some HTL-free carbon based PSCs have shown good stability, insufficient hole extractive ability of carbon material and poor interfacial contact of perovskite/carbon cause a strong hysteretic behaviour in J-V plot and lower conversion efficiency.  $  ^{9-10}  $   In the meantime, in order to simplify fabrication process and reduce energy consumption, PSCs with planar structure with ultrathin electron transport layer (ETL) that can be fabricated at low temperature has been considered as the most appropriate choice for future large scale production of PSCs.  $  ^{11}  $   Many attempts have been made to enhance the device performance by modifying ETL in PSCs such as by using amorphous   $  TiO_{2}  $  -coated ETL to improve short circuit current density and efficiency of the devices due to the presence of oxygen vacancies  $  ^{12}  $   or by incorporating edge-enriched graphene nanoribbons and   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanocrystals to prepare ETLs.  $  ^{13}  $   Nevertheless, the impact of HTL itself on performance and stability of carbon based planar perovskite solar cells (C-PSCs) is not well studied yet.

As the representative of hole transport material in PSC, organic spiro-OMeTAD (2, 2', 7, 7'-tetrakis-(N, N-di-4-methoxyphenylamino)-9, 9'spirobifluorene) has been widely employed in PSCs to acquire high PCE when in combination with precious metal based electrode due to its proper ionization potential, decent hole mobility and good film-formation capacity.  $  ^{14-16}  $   Because of its success in producing high PCE of PSCs, it has been widely used as a reference material to evaluate other hole extraction materials in PSCs. However, there is lack of detailed study of using spiro-OMeTAD in C-PSCs. Practically the efficiency of C-PSCs using spiro-OMeTAD is much lower compared to the device using metal based back contact. The main reason is that certain organic solvents contained in carbon paste that is used to make carbon back contact electrode are incompatible with the spiro-OMeTAD, and thus they can potentially destroy underneath HTL, even the perovskite layer.  $  ^{17}  $   To address this issue, Zhang et al.  $  ^{18}  $   adopted pressing transfer approach, in which they prepared a free-standing carbon film first which was then pressed onto spiro-OMeTAD layer to assemble C-PSCs. demonstrated a method involving hot-pressing a pre-made carbon film on a spiro-OMeTAD layer to effectively address the interfacial contact problems between carbon and HTL.  $  ^{19}  $   In addition to spiro-OMeTAD based organic HTL, inorganic p-type semiconductor based on copper thiocyanate (CuSCN) with wide band gap and high mobility has shown the potential of enhancing PCE of C-PSCs.  $  ^{8,20}  $   In regard with PSCs using noble metal electrode, Arora et al.  $  ^{21}  $   compared spiro-OMeTAD and CuSCN in Au based devices, which showed similar PCE for the device with the two different hole transport materials. In this work, we conducted comprehensive comparison of the effect of spiro-OMeTAD and CuSCN on photovoltaic, electronic, energetic and durable properties of planar C-PSCs. All the characterizations were performed with the C-PSCs that were made with optimal thickness of both spiro-OMeTAD and CuSCN layers. We believe this comparison is important both practically and fundamentally to reveal the underlying reasons that cause the different device performance and hysteresis behaviour. It was found that the power conversion efficiency of the C-PSCs using CuSCN as HTL (close to 15%) was much higher with a much smaller hysteresis behaviour than the spiro-OMeTAD based devices. This is ascribed to the favourable energy level alignment between CuSCN and the perovskite material for hole carrier extraction and ionic capacitance suppression. Benefiting from the hydrophobicity of CuSCN, the corresponding C-PSCs also demonstrated excellent moisture resistance with more than 93% retention of the initial efficiency under a humid condition (relative humidity: 55-70%) after aging for over 80 days without encapsulation.

# 2.2 Device assembly

Hydrochloric acid (HCl, 35.5 wt%) and zinc powder were used to partially etch the fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass substrates (Nippon Electric Glass, 15 Ω square  $  ^{-1}  $  ). The etched substrates were then cleaned under ultrasonic bath in sequence: an aqueous solution of Decon-90 detergent (5 wt%), deionized water and a mixed solution of ethanol, isopropanol and acetone with the volume ratio of 1:1:1. To completely remove organic residuals, the glass substrates were treated with UV-Ozone for 30 min. A tin (II) chloride (SnCl  $  _{2}  $  ) solution (0.1 M in ethanol) was spin-coated onto the FTO-coated glass at 3000 rpm for 30 s. After being annealed at 185 °C for 1 h in a muffle furnace, a compact tin dioxide (SnO  $  _{2}  $  ) electron transport layer was obtained. A perovskite film was deposited onto the SnO  $  _{2}  $  -coated substrate in a N  $  _{2}  $  -filled glove box by spin-coating a triple cation perovskite precursor (CsFA  $  _{0.83}  $  MA  $  _{0.17}  $  PbI  $  _{2.53}  $  Br  $  _{0.47}  $  ) in a two-step process.  $  ^{8}  $

Two different hole transport layers were applied in this work. With regards to spiro-OMeTAD based PSCs, a precursor was prepared firstly by dissolving spiro-OMeTAD (72.3 mg), 4-tert-butylpyridine (28.8   $  \mu  $  l) and bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium (Li-TFSI) solution (17.5   $  \mu  $  l) consisting of Li-TFSI (520 mg) and acetonitrile (1 ml) into 1 ml of chlorobenzene. Then a spiro-OMeTAD layer was achieved via spin-coating the prepared precursor on top of perovskite film at 4000 rpm for 30s. A thin copper thiocyanate (CuSCN) layer was deposited onto perovskite-coated substrate by a dynamic dripping method   $  {}^{21}  $   to make CuSCN based devices. Specifically, 35   $  \mu  $  l of CuSCN precursor made by dissolving 35 mg of CuSCN into 1 ml of diethyl sulfide was drop-casted on a spinning perovskite-coated substrate at 5000 rpm within 2 s to yield a flat thin layer.

A carbon electrode of the device was made by hot-pressing a free-standing carbon film onto hole transport layer at 80 °C under a pressure of 30 MPa for 3 min.  $  ^{19}  $   The standalone carbon was obtained by peeling off the as-prepared carbon film, which was firstly coated onto a cleaned microscope slide by doctor-blading the commercial carbon slurry and then was immersed into ethanol to remove the residual solvent.  $  ^{18}  $   For simplicity, the PSC with spiro-OMeTAD is named as PSK/spiro/C while the one with CuSCN is labelled as PSK/CuSCN/C.

Hysteresis is caused by the imbalanced charge extraction rate at the two most critical interfaces in PSCs, namely perovskite/ETL (  $  SnO_{2}  $   in this work) and perovskite/HTL   $  [^{30}]  $  . Quite often, the transport rate of hole from perovskite to adjacent HTL is lower than the transport of electron from perovskite to the ETL, leaving accumulation of hole in the device. The charge accumulation is reflected by the different capacitance of the device. To confirm whether there is any difference in density of accumulated charge between the CuSCN based C-PSCs and the Spiro based C-PSCs, the capacitance of the C-PSCs devices with CuSCN or spiro-OMeTAD was investigated by analysis of the impedance spectroscopy of the device (Figure 4b and 4c). There are two main plateaus in the capacitance vs frequency plot which correspond to electrode polarization domain at low-frequency region and dielectric polarization domain at high-frequency region (Figure 4b). Generally, the PSC presents a relatively stable dielectric polarization domain as a result of dielectric relaxation of bulk perovskite, while it behaves differently at the electrode polarization domain, which is directly associated with ion accumulation at interfaces.  $  ^{35-36}  $   The PSK/spiro/C shows a constant ionic capacitance around 2   $  \mu F/cm^{-2}  $   at 0.5 Hz regardless of the applied voltage (Figure 4c). When an external voltage bias (  $  V_{app}  $  ) of 1 V is applied to the C-PSCs, the Fermi level of electron increases toward the valence band and generates an accumulation regime of ions and electrons at the bending edge, simultaneously, leading to an accumulation of holes in the HTL.  $  ^{38,45}  $   The   $  V_{app}  $   exceeds the   $  V_{bi}  $   by 0.08 V for the PSK/spiro/C whereas the   $  V_{app}  $   is almost equal to the   $  V_{bi}  $   for the PSK/CuSCN/C. Therefore, a more severe inversion on the bending is induced by the applied electrical field for the PSK/spiro/C. The formed inverse bending is more beneficial for the negative ions to transfer back to the interface of perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD, leading to charge accumulation. Thanks to the different energy levels between spiro-OMeTAD and CuSCN, this accumulation occurs on a higher voltage in the PSK/CuSCN/C compared to the PSK/spiro/C. The charge gathering at perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD interface is always higher than perovskite/CuSCN interface when an external voltage bias is applied. Furthermore, due to the energy barrier at the interface of perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD is closer to the perovskite side, hole injection from spiro-OMeTAD into perovskite is banned, thereby forming a thin highly charged space charge accumulation layer on the spiro-OMeTAD side. Such accumulation leads to a capacitance “across” the interface that is larger than the one formed at perovskite/CuSCN interface causing anomalous hysteretic response. Overall, the different hysteresis behaviour of the C-PSCs with CuSCN and spiro-OMeTAD can be well interpreted by this physical model. Based on the simulation analysis, tuning the energy level alignment of perovskite and HTL is of great importance to eliminate hysteresis behaviour for carbon based planar PSCs.

# 3 Conclusions

In summary, we investigated the influence of hole transport materials on the performance of the C-PSCs by using spiro-OMeTAD and CuSCN as HTL. The PSK/spiro/C demonstrated a PCE of  $ 12.4\% $  with obvious hysteresis whereas a higher PCE of  $ 14.7\% $  with negligible hysteresis was obtained in the PSK/CuSCN/C. Further characterization of the devices regarding recombination and energy level alignment has revealed the origin for the better device performance and smaller hysteretic behaviour for the CuSCN based C-PSCs which is due to reduced charge injection barrier at the interface of perovskite/CuSCN compared to perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD. Furthermore, the PSK/CuSCN/C also presented more robust resistance against moisture and thus over  $ 93\% $  performance retention was achieved for the device after being stored in a humid environment  $ (55 - 70\%) $  over 80 days. As a result, compared with spiro-OMeTAD, the low-cost and stable CuSCN is a preferable hole extraction maternal for planar carbon based perovskite solar cells to fulfil industrial and commercial production.

# Graphical abstract

Carbon electrode-based planar perovskite solar cells (C-PSCs) demonstrated higher device performance and reduced hysteresis by using CuSCN based hole transport material (HTM) owing to its favourable energy level alignment with the perovskite compared to conventional spiro-OMeTAD based HTL.

# Research highlights

- Carbon based planar perovskite solar cells with spiro-OMeTAD or CuSCN as HTL were fabricated.

- A comprehensively comparative study of spiro-OMeTAD and CuSCN as HTL for C-PSCs was carried out.

- Origin of the hysteresis for the C-PSCs was unravelled by studying the electronic and energetic properties of the different HTLs.

- The CuSCN based C-PSCs showed better stability against humidity than spiro-OMeTAD based devices.
