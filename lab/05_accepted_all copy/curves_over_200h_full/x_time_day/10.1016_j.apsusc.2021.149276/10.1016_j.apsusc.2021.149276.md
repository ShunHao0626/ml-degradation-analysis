# 1. Introduction

During the past few years, the photovoltaic applications of hybrid organic-inorganic perovskites (HOIPs) have developed rapidly owing to their outstanding photo-physical properties and low processing cost   $  [1-3]  $  . To date, the highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has reached 25.5%   $  [4]  $  . The main device architectures of PSCs can be classified into mesoporous and planar structures. In planar devices, the inverted p-i-n PSCs have attracted extensive attention, which provide several advantages: simpler fabrication, reduced hysteresis and lower cost   $  [5]  $  .

Hole transport layers (HTLs) play essential roles in inverted planar p-i-n PSCs, where organic and inorganic p-type semiconductors are commonly employed [6]. Compared to the organic HTLs, inorganic HTLs own higher carrier mobility and better stability, which have been widely applied to improve the efficiency and stability of PSCs [7–10]. Of these, nickel oxide (NiO $ _{x} $ ) has been demonstrated to be the efficient HTL

Recently,   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based inverted planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved great progress. However, the lattice mismatch between   $  NiO_{x}  $   hole-transport layers (HTLs) and perovskite active layers will result in interfacial defect formation, which severely influences device efficiency and stability. In this work, this is alleviated by coating of polyethylenimine cations (PEI•HI) on the surface of   $  NiO_{x}  $   HTLs. The introduction of PEI•HI can not only effectively passivate the surface defects of   $  NiO_{x}  $   films, but also induce the in-situ-generation of polyethylenimine (PEI)-based two-dimensional (2D) perovskite interlayers between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ). Such PEI-based 2D perovskite interlayer can dramatically mitigate the lattice mismatch between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and perovskites to suppress the interfacial defects formations and promote the high-quality crystal nucleation and growth of above perovskite films. Meanwhile, it is demonstrated that the molecular weights of PEI act the pivotal part in controlling the nucleation quality of perovskites. When employing PEI with molecular weight of 10,000 (PEI-1000), the high-crystalline perovskite film with large-sized grains and reduced interfacial defects is obtained and the champion PSC delivers a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 19.23% with weak hysteresis effect and improved reproducibility and stability. Our work provides a promising approach to improve the lattice match between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and perovskites for high-performance   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based inverted planar PSCs.

Various approaches have been applied to prepare NiO $ _{x} $  films for inverted planar PSCs, such as pulsed laser deposition (PLD) [13], sol–gel method [14], atomic layer deposition (ALD) [15], combustion route [16], as well as spin-coating from pre-synthesized nanocrystals [17], etc. Up to now, the PCEs of NiO $ _{x} $ -based inverted planar PSCs have reached 22.13% with an organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite composition [18].

Despite the encouraging progress in   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based inverted planar PSCs, the device performance is still inferior to the normal mesoporous device, which is mainly due to the low conductivity of intrinsic   $  NiO_{x}  $   films, the inferior interface interaction, poor perovskite crystalline quality, as well as unoptimized energy level alignment between   $  NiO_{x}  $   films and perovskite films [19]. Many kinds of interface modifiers have been applied between   $  NiO_{x}  $   films and perovskites, e.g. self-assembled monolayers (SAM) (diethanolamine (DEA) [22–23] and 4-bromobenzoic acid [24]), ferrocenedicarboxylic acid [25], poly(trial amine) (PTAA) [19,26], poly[(9,9-bis(30-((N,N-dimethyl)-N-ethylammonium)-propyl)-2,7-fluorene)-alt-2,7-(9,9-dioctylfluorene)] dib-romide (PFN-P2) [27], and so on. It has been demonstrated that insertion of a thin interlayer between   $  NiO_{x}  $   HTLs and perovskite active layers can effectively passivate the surface defects of   $  NiO_{x}  $ , tune the surface wettability, minimize the energy offset between the   $  NiO_{x}  $   and perovskites, improve the crystal growth of perovskites, as well as strengthen and stabilize interfaces. However, the lattice mismatch between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and perovskites is still a great challenge for   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based inverted planar PSCs, which will cause the interface structural disorder, thereby, resulting in the poor perovskite nucleation quality and the formation of interface defects, eventually deteriorating device performance [28]. Improving the lattice match between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and perovskites to boost the nucleation quality of perovskites and suppress interfacial defect formation is a promising route to realize high-performance   $  NiO_{x}  $  -based inverted planar PSCs.

Generally speaking, the lattice mismatch between the materials with similar crystal structures is small. In our previous work, we have demonstrated that the polyethylenimine (PEI)-based two-dimensional (2D) perovskite interlayer can be readily in-situ generated between poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)-poly (styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) and perovskites by coating the PEI cations on top of PEDOT:PSS, followed by the deposition of perovskite precursors [29]. As we know, the structure of 2D perovskites is derived from the 3D perovskites [30]. Therefore, incorporation of such PEI-based 2D perovskite interlayers between  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  HTLs and perovskite active layers will effectively suppress the setback of lattice mismatch. In addition, PEI is a typical polymer and the molecular weight is an important feature for polymers. Polymers with different molecular weights signify different molecular lengths, which will also influence device performance. However, the effects of PEI molecular weights on the device performance have not been elucidated in our previous research.

With this in mind, in this contribution, PEI cations (PEI  $  ^{+}  $  , i.e. PEI-HI) are introduced between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) to in-situ generate the PEI-based 2D perovskite interlayers in p-i-n inverted planar PSCs. The impacts of molecular weights of PEI cations (weight-average molecular weights (  $  M_{w}  $  ) of 1800, 10,000 and 70,000 for PEI) on the properties of   $  NiO_{x}  $   films, nucleation and crystal growth of perovskite films, as well as the device efficiency and stability, are investigated in details. It is found that the in-situ-generated PEI-based 2D perovskite interlayer can effectively reduce the lattice mismatch between   $  NiO_{x}  $   HTLs and perovskite active layers to boost the high-quality nucleation of perovskites, thus suppressing defect formation at interface and acquiring perovskites with high crystallinity. Meanwhile, it is proven that the molecular weight of PEI plays important roles in controlling the nucleation and crystal quality of perovskite films. As a consequence, the champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 19.23% is achieved with the optimal molecular weight of PEI (  $  M_{w} = 10000  $  ), along with negligible hysteresis effect and improved device reproducibility and stability.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

Super dehydrated solvents of N,N-Dimethylformamide (DMF) (99.8%), dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) (99.9%) were purchased from Alfar Aesar. Chlorobenzene (CB) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Anhydrous solvents of methanol, ethanol, acetone and isopropanol were purchased from Damao Chemical Reagent Factory. Hydroiodic acid (HI) (57 wt% in water with 1.5% hypophosphorous acid), NaOH, Polyethyleneimine (PEI, M $  _{w}  $  = 1800, 10,000 and 70000) (99%), Ag

(99.999%) and Nickel(II) nitrate hexahydrate   $  (\mathrm{Ni}(\mathrm{NO}_{3})_{2}\cdot6\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O})  $   (99.99%) were purchased from Aladdin.   $  PbI_{2}  $   (99.999%) and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (MAI) (99.999%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. 2,9-dimethyl-4,7-diphenyl-1,10-phenanthroline (BCP) and [6,6]-phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (  $  PC_{61}BM  $  ) were purchased from J&K Chemicals. All of the chemicals and materials were purchased and used as received without further purification.

# 2.2. Preparation of PEI·HI

0.5 g PEI with different molecular weighs (  $  M_{w} = 1800, 10{,}000  $   and 70000) was dissolved in 150 mL ethanol in a 250 mL round bottom flask. Then, 2.5 mL HI was added dropwise at 0 °C under continuous stirring. After complete reaction, the white sediment of PEI-HI was extracted by rotary evaporation at room temperature and then was washed with diethyl ether. In the end, the PEI-HI was dried in the vacuum oven for 24 h at room temperature.

# 2.3. Preparation of the  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  HTL and PEI-HI-coated  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  HTL

$ \mathrm{Ni(NO_3)_2\cdot 6H_2O} $  (3.63 g) was dispersed in  $ 2.5~\mathrm{mL} $  of deionized  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  completely. Then, the pH of the solution was adjusted to 10 by adding  $ 10\mathrm{MNaOH} $  aqueous solution drop by drop under continuous stirring. After being stirred for another  $ 30\mathrm{min} $ , the green colloidal precipitation of  $ \mathrm{Ni(OH)_2} $  was extracted at room temperature and washed with deionized  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  twice. Subsequently, the green sediment was dried at  $ 80^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 8\mathrm{h} $  and then calcined at  $ 270^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 2\mathrm{h} $  to obtain  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  nanoparticles as the black powder. Afterwards, the  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  nanoparticles were dispersed in deionized water to acquire  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  nanoparticles aqueous solution  $ (20\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1}) $ , which was then spin-coated on the ITO substrate at  $ 3000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 30~s $  followed by annealing at  $ 150^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 20\mathrm{min} $  in ambient to prepare the  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  HTL. Then the prepared  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  film was moved into the glove box filled with nitrogen.

For the fabrication of the PEI·HI-coated   $  NiO_{x}  $   HTL, the as-prepared PEI·HI was dissolved in DMF with the concentration of 10 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   and the solution was spin-coated on the   $  NiO_{x}  $   HTL at 3000 rpm for 30 s.

# 2.4. Fabrication of perovskite solar cells

Indium tin oxide (ITO,   $  \sim15\ O\ sq^{-1}  $  ) on glass was cleaned with deionized water, acetone and isopropanol under sonication for 15 min sequentially. PbI  $  _{2}  $  :MAI (1:1, mol/mol) mixture was dissolved in DMF: DMSO (4:1, v/v) at a concentration of 1.25 M, the solution was stirred overnight in the nitrogen glove box. The precursor solution of perovskites was spin-coated at 3000 rpm and 200   $  \mu  $  L of chlorobenzene was dripped on the spinning substrate in 10 s. The substrate was annealed at 100 °C for 15 min to form the perovskite films. PC  $  _{61}  $  BM solution (20 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in chlorobenzene) was spin-coated at 1500 rpm for 30 s, then annealed at 90 °C for 30 min. BCP (1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in methanol) was spin-coated at 5000 rpm for 20 s. In the end, the Ag electrode (150 nm) was deposited on the top of films through thermal evaporation.

# 2.5. Characterization

The crystallinity of the samples were characterized by the X-ray diffraction (XRD) using a Bruker D8 Focus X-ray diffractometer operating at 30 kV and 20 mA with a copper target (  $  \lambda = 1.54 \AA  $  ) under a scanning rate of   $  1^{\circ} min^{-1}  $  . The morphological observations of films by scanning electron microscope (SEM) were made with a JSM-6701F scanning electron microscope with gold vapor deposition. The surface roughness of   $  NiO_{x}  $   films were measured by an atomic force microscopy (AFM) (Digital Instrument Nanoscope 31). Elemental analysis was characterized by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, ESCALAB 250Xi, Thermo Fisher Scientific) and Monochrome Al Kα source. The effects of PEI-HI on the morphologies, crystalline structure and optical properties of   $  NiO_{x}  $   films are explored by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), atomic force microscopy (AFM), X-ray diffraction (XRD) and ultraviolet–visible (UV–vis) spectrometer. As shown in Fig. 1b, there is no obvious difference for the SEM images of   $  NiO_{x}  $   films without and with PEI-HI. Moreover, AFM images (insert of Fig. 1b) exhibit a slight decrease in the roughness of   $  NiO_{x}  $   films after PEI-HI is coated. The root-mean-square roughness (RMS) is 9.58 nm, 9.52 nm, 8.12 nm and 7.71 nm for   $  NiO_{x}  $ ,   $  NiO_{x}/PEI-1800  $ ,   $  NiO_{x}/PEI-10000  $   and   $  NiO_{x}/PEI-70000  $   films, respectively. The results show that the introduction of PEI-HI can make   $  NiO_{x}  $   films become smooth, which will be beneficial to the growth of above perovskite films. Fig. S1 shows the XRD patterns of the corresponding   $  NiO_{x}  $   films without and with PEI-HI. All films exhibit the similar XRD curves, indicating that the PEI-HI coating cannot change the crystal structures of   $  NiO_{x}  $   films. Meanwhile, similar optical transmission spectra are also be observed for   $  NiO_{x}  $   films without and with PEI-HI, shown in Fig. S2. All samples reveal a high transmission of over 80%, illustrating that the coating of PEI-HI will not affect the optical transmittance of   $  NiO_{x}  $   films. Further, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis is performed to examine the interaction between   $  NiO_{x}  $   and PEI-HI. Fig. 1c showcases the XPS spectra of Ni   $  2p_{3/2}  $   core level for   $  NiO_{x}  $ ,   $  NiO_{x}/PEI-1800  $ ,   $  NiO_{x}/PEI-10000  $   and   $  NiO_{x}/PEI-70000  $   films, respectively. It can be seen that similar XPS spectra are presented in all films. Typically, Ni   $  2p_{3/2}  $   core level peaks can be well fitted by three components: NiO,   $  Ni_{2}O_{3}  $   and NiOOH, in agreement with previous literatures [31–32]. In general, when the atom obtains a higher electron density, the binding energy of the core electron level will shift to a lower energy value. In our case, the shift of Ni   $  2p_{3/2}  $   binding energy might originate from the existence of chemical interaction between the Ni

Observably, all of the thick  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films grown on  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  with PEI-HI present the continuous and dense morphologies with larger grain size, and more ordered grains and less grain boundaries exist at the interface between  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  and perovskites, especially for the PEI-10000 case. XRD diagram gives further insight into the crystallinity of the thick perovskite films (Fig. 3b). Typical characteristic peaks of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  at  $ 14.1^{\circ} $ ,  $ 28.4^{\circ} $  and  $ 31.8^{\circ} $  are shown in all samples, corresponding to (110), (220) and (310) planes of tetragonal perovskite phase, respectively. Nevertheless, higher XRD diffraction intensity is measured for the thick perovskite film deposited on  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  with PEI-HI, inferring that the in-situ-generated PEI-based 2D perovskite interlayer enables the growth of perovskite films with better crystallinity. Accordingly, the perovskite films grown on  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  with PEI-HI exhibit stronger absorption and obvious red-shift in the absorbance spectra compared to that on  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  without PEI-HI (Fig. 3c), mainly attributing to the higher crystallinity and larger grains originated from the improved nucleation quality and suppressed interfacial defects induced by the in-situ-generated PEI-based 2D perovskite interlayer.

To clarify the underlying mechanism for the enhanced photovoltaic efficiency, we perform the optical and electrical measurements to investigate interfacial carrier transport properties. Fig. S5 presents the steady-state PL spectra of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films grown on  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  films without and with PEI-1800, PEI-10000 and PEI-70000, respectively. Notably, by introducing PEI-HI, PL intensity of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  is greatly quenched compared to the control. Generally, the PL intensity of perovskite films is correlated with carrier recombination and charge transport capability at the perovskite/charge transport layer (CTL) interface. In our cases, the significant PL quenching observed in the samples with PEI-HI suggests that holes can be efficiently transported from  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite active layers to  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  HTLs. The time-resolved PL (TRPL) measurement is further carried out to gain quantitative insight into the charge transport dynamics (Fig. 4c). The bi-exponential decay function in Eq. (1) [40] is applied to analyze the carrier lifetime:

$$
\mathrm{I} (t) = A _ {1} \exp \left(- \frac {t}{\tau_ {1}}\right) + A _ {2} \exp \left(- \frac {t}{\tau_ {2}}\right)
$$

(1)

where   $  A_{1}  $   and   $  A_{2}  $   are the relative amplitudes,   $  \tau_{1}  $   is the lifetime for the fast recombination, which is mainly attributed to the defect-related nonradiative recombination from the surface or interface.   $  \tau_{2}  $   is the lifetime for the slow recombination, which is related to the radiative recombination in bulk perovskites. The fitted results are summarized in Table S1. As compared with the perovskite film deposited on   $  NiO_{x}  $   without PEI-HI, the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films grown on   $  NiO_{x}  $   with PEI-HI display smaller   $  \tau_{1}  $   and larger   $  \tau_{2}  $ , demonstrating that the in-situ-generated PEI-based 2D perovskites can facilitate hole extraction from MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   to HTL and reduce trap-assisted nonradiative recombination. In addition, transient photo-voltage decay is measured (Fig. 4d). It reflects the stronger charge extraction and less recombination at the interface for   $  NiO_{x}/PEI-10000/perovskite  $   sample among all devices, which is consistent with the result of PL and TRPL.

So as to further study the process of carrier transfer and recombination at the interface, the Mott-Schottky analysis and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) are conducted. Ordinarily, the energetic offset between   $  E_{Fn}  $   (the quasi Fermi level of CTL/electrode contact) and   $  E_{p}  $   (the quasi Fermi level of perovskite films) can be lowered by the trap-related charge recombination at perovskite active layers and device interfaces, which will cause the decline of the flat-band potential [41]. Additionally,   $  C^{-2}-V  $   characteristics in the light of the Mott-Schottky model can unveil the relationship between the interfacial charge accumulation and the potential barrier following the Eq. (2) [42–43].

$$
C ^ {- 2} = \frac {2 (V _ {b i} - V)}{A ^ {2} q \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0} N _ {A}}
$$

(2)

Where C is the measured capacitance,   $  V_{bi}  $   represents the build-in potential, V represents the applied bias, A represents the active area, and q represents the elementary charge.   $  \varepsilon  $   represents the static permittivity,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   represents the permittivity of free space.   $  N_{A}  $   refers to the density of excited states. These Mott-Schottky plots in Fig. 4e exhibit the increased   $  V_{bi}  $   of the devices with PEI-HI compared with the control device. The calculated   $  N_{A}  $   values are   $  1.98 \times 10^{15} \, cm^{-3}  $  ,   $  2.89 \times 10^{15} \, cm^{-3}  $  ,   $  3.16 \times 10^{15} \, cm^{-3}  $  , and   $  2.66 \times 10^{15} \, cm^{-3}  $   for PSCs without and with PEI-1800, PEI-10000 and PEI-70000, respectively. Correspondingly, the Nyquist plots in Fig. 4f portrays the higher recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) for the devices with PEI-HI. The increased   $  V_{bi}  $  ,   $  N_{A}  $   and   $  R_{rec}  $   values infer the reduced charge recombination and accumulation in the devices

applying PEI-HI, which may explain the improved   $  V_{oc}  $   in the devices with PEI-HI. Microscopically, the PEI-based 2D perovskites in-situ-generated at   $  NiO_{x}/MAPbI_{3}  $   interface by introducing PEI-HI can promote high-quality perovskite nucleation, suppress the interfacial defect formation and improve crystallinity of perovskite films, ultimately shut off the charge recombination paths and improve the charge transport efficiency.

Finally, the hysteresis behavior, reproducibility and stability of PSCs without and with PEI-10000 are evaluated. Fig. 5a and b display  $ J-V $  curves of the best-performing PSCs without and with PEI-10000 measured in reverse and forward scans. The PCE of the control device is  $ 15.54\% $  under the forward scan (FS) direction, reducing  $ 7.8\% $  compared with  $ 16.86\% $  under the reverse scan (RS) direction, showing the severe hysteresis effect. In comparison, the device with PEI-10000 obtains a PCE of  $ 18.91\% $  from FS and  $ 19.23\% $  from RS, which implies little hysteresis effect. It is well-known that the hysteresis behavior of PSCs is mainly assigned to the interfacial capacitance due to charge accumulation at interfaces, originating from ion migration, high defect density, as well as unbalanced charge transport in PSCs [44-46]. In our case, derived from the in-situ-generated PEI-based 2D perovskites, the interface defects are effectively suppressed and the crystallinity of above perovskite films is greatly improved. Consequently, the hole charge trapping is eliminated and charge transport is enhanced at the perovskite/HTL interface, accounting for the reduced hysteresis effect. Moreover, Fig. 5c presents the histogram showing the statistical performance for PSCs without and with PEI-10000 obtained from 60 devices, validating the improved reproducibility for the device with PEI-10000. Apart from the device efficiency, device stability is always considered as another vital performance of PSCs for real application. We evaluate the device stability by recording the photovoltaic performance change of the unencapsulated PSCs under the ambient environment (relative humidity (RH) of  $ 30 \pm 2\% $ ,  $ \mathrm{T} = 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) for 30 days. Fig. 5d exemplifies the fluctuations of PCEs for PSCs without and with PEI-10000. After 30 days, the PSCs with PEI-10000 can maintain  $ 81\% $  of their original efficiency compared with the control devices, which only retain  $ 45\% $  of their initial PCE. The superior stability of unencapsulated PSC with PEI-10000 can be ascribed to the stable HTL/MAPbI₃ interface with less interfacial defects and larger grain size of the above MAPbI₃ film with high-crystallinity grown on the in-situ-generated PEI-based 2D perovskite.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, we have demonstrated that the efficiency and stability of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ -based inverted planar PSCs can be markedly boosted by coating PEI-HI on top of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ . The introduction of PEI-HI can passivate the surface defects of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  films through Ni-N interaction. More importantly, PEI-based 2D perovskite interlayers can be in-situ generated between  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  HTLs and perovskite active layers upon introducing PEI-HI, which can effectively improve the lattice match between  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  and perovskites to realize high-quality perovskite nucleation with suppressed interfacial defects, thus promoting crystallinity and morphologies of perovskite films, and ultimately facilitating interface charge transport and reducing charge accumulation at  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ /perovskites interface. Moreover, it is proven that the molecular weights of PEI can significantly affect the nucleation and crystal growth of above perovskite films. Benefiting from these merits, the champion PCE of  $ 19.23\% $  is achieved when applying PEI-10000. In addition, the hysteresis behavior, reproducibility and stability of devices are also improved. Our work provides a promising approach to resolve the issue of lattice mismatch between  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  and perovskites, thereby boosting the efficiency and stability of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ -based inverted planar PSCs.
