# Abstract

The defects at the surfaces and grain boundaries of organic–inorganic halide perovskite films are detrimental to both the efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells. Here, we introduce an in situ method with a new nonfullerene small molecule (IT-4F) that can effectively passivate ionic defects of hybrid perovskite with their positive-charged components under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $  , during the anti-solvent process of perovskite film formation. This efficient defect passivation reduces the charge trap density and elongates the carrier recombination lifetime. Furthermore, it reduces the open-circuit-voltage deficit of the p–i–n-structured device, and boosts the efficiency to a value of 18.3%. Moreover, the defect healing also significantly enhances the stability of films in ambient conditions. Our findings provide an avenue for defect passivation to further improve both the efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells.

# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) show great potential as a renewable energy source due to their exceptional optoelectronic properties, such as strong light absorption, $ ^{1} $  high charge carrier mobility, $ ^{2,3} $  and long intrinsic carrier recombination lifetime. $ ^{4,5} $  Photoconversion efficiency has reached  $ 22.1\%^{6} $  a few years after the first reports on liquid electrolyte solar cells in 2009. $ ^{7} $  In addition, the success of these materials mainly lies in that high-efficiency devices are prepared with perovskite films that can be grown using solution methods. $ ^{8-10} $  But inevitably, solution-processed polycrystalline films also tend to have a high density of defect states and exhibit a large degree of electronic disorder. $ ^{11-14} $

According to Yin et al.  $  ^{15}  $  , defects can be the main source of the increase in carrier recombination and interface resistance. The defects with transition levels in the band gap will attract electron/hole and acts as nonradiative recombination centers. Defect state levels cause the Fermi level to be pinned near the defect levels. Charge recombination via those defect states induced nonradiative recombination, constitutes a major energy loss mechanism, and they would significantly impact the fill factor (FF) and open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of the devices. The theoretical modeling and experimental studies have already shown that the   $  V_{oc}  $   in a photovoltaic device is determined by the quasi-Fermi level splitting of electrons and holes in the whole device under illumination, which is affected by the occupation of available electronic states by photogenerated charge carriers of perovskite layer. The band tail caused by the energy disorder of the defects or trap states that extends into the forbidden gap introduces additional electronic states.  $  ^{16, 17}  $   The non-equilibrium photogenerated electrons tend to relax down to these trap states, which results in reduced quasi-Fermi level splitting and smaller   $  V_{oc}  $  .  $  ^{18, 19}  $   Moreover, these defects can cause other device instability issues, including ion migration, associated current hysteresis and device degradation in ambient environment. Lewis acids, such as phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM), could interact with the negative-charged under-coordinated halide ions or Pb-I antisite defects PbI  $  ^{3-20-22}  $  . During the annealing process of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskites, methylammonium ions and halide could be lost from the crystal,

resulting in under-coordinated Pb atoms both on the crystal surface and at the grain boundaries. Recent modeling and experimental studies have shown that these undercoordinated Pb atoms, and clusters in some cases, can act as electronic trap states within the perovskite material. $ ^{23, 24} $  This under-coordination of the Pb atom could result in the formation of a net positive charge on the atom, which would then create favorable conditions for coordination, or bond formation with electron-rich molecules such as sufficiently weak lewis bases, such as thiophene or pyridine. $ ^{23, 25} $  Nonfullerene provide Lewis bases carbonyl (C=O) and cyano (C≡N), act as electron-withdrawing end groups, which can efficiently passivate the trap state on the surface and at the grain boundaries of the perovskite thin film through the Lewis acid–base reaction with the under-coordinated Pb $ ^{2+} $ . $ ^{26, 27} $  Meanwhile, this organic n-type π-conjugated molecules can extract the electrons generated from MAPbI $ _3 $  and then transport them to the device cathode. So far, there have been only limited research reports about on nonfullerenes application in PSCs. $ ^{28-31} $  For example, Huang et al. introduced a π-conjugated Lewis base (IDIC) into perovskite solar cells as an interlayer between the perovskite and the cathode, resulting in much improved efficiency from 13.1 to 19.4%. $ ^{29} $  Recently, Zhou et al. have demonstrated efficient perovskite solar cells employing IDIC as electron transport material. $ ^{30} $  Hou et al. have demonstrated that IT-4F (Fig. 1a) exhibited excellent performance in organic solar cells (OSCs). $ ^{32} $  It contain carbonyl (C=O), cyano (C≡N) and thiophene that have been reported to be excellent passivation agents. Meanwhile, IT-4F combine a series of optimal electrical properties, including lower LUMO levels of 4.14 eV, and increased electron mobility of  $ 5.05 \times 10^{-4} \text{ cm}^2\text{V}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1} $ , which are beneficial for electrons extraction. it is a novel small molecule with different functionalities that have not been previously checked for passivation in perovskite optoelectronic devices.

In this study, we have successfully developed a new method to introduce additives in situ in a halide perovskite thin film, during the anti-solvent deposition step. The lewis base IT-4F can effectively passivate the lewis acid traps (under-coordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $ ) of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ , and this n-type  $ \pi $ -conjugated material possesses chemical properties that promote electron extraction, which can remarkably increase both photovoltage and fill

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The principle of passivating under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   by Lewis base IT-4F (molecular structure in Fig. S1) adducts is illustrated in Fig. 1a, where the loss of iodine in perovskite leads to vacancy sites (yellow spots) and a resulting net positive charge settled in the Pb atom (green spots). Our lewis base small molecules IT-4F can donate electron density to the   $  Pb^{2+}  $   and form a coordinate or dative covalent bond, effectively neutralizing the excess positive charge in the crystal. We use minute amounts of IT-4F in chlorobenzene as anti-solvet to fabricate p-i-n perovskite solar cells with a device structure of fluorine doped tin oxide (FTO)/Li-  $  NiO_{x}  $  /MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (IT-4F)/PCBM/bathocuproine (BCP)/Ag (Figure 1c). Although in the field of PSC, there are many related work focus on the inorganic hole transport materials.  $  ^{33-35}  $   Here, the lithium-doped   $  NiO_{x}  $   was identified as the hole transporting layer, because Li-  $  NiO_{x}  $   has crystalline surface with multidomain that can assist the perovskite nucleation process during film formation. Furthermore, the Li-  $  NiO_{x}  $   has high surface energy when processing with the carrier solvent, leading to a “nonwetting” surface that can assist the crystal formation.  $  ^{36}  $   Recently, Nie et al. also reported sable tetragonal phase MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   can be formed on lithium-doped   $  NiO_{x}  $   surface using first-principles computational modeling.  $  ^{37}  $   In particular, Li-  $  NiO_{x}  $   devices are found to form better interface energy level alignment, suppressed trap-assisted recombination in the bulk and at the interfacial contacts. Our previous works also have demonstrated that   $  NiO_{x}  $   fabricated by spray pyrolysis method could yield superior and highly reproducible PCEs. The space-charge-limited current (SCLC) measurements under different bias voltages were also undertaken to investigate the function of IT-4F, particularly its effects on the trap density   $  (n_{\mathrm{trap}})  $   and charge-carrier mobility   $  (\mu)  $   of the perovskite films. Fig. 2(a) show the SCLC curves of the neat perovskite and perovskite films prepared by IT-4F, respectively. The curves are divided into three different regions, in the range between linear ohmic region and SCLC region, the current exhibited a rapid non-linear increase and the voltage is known as the trap-filled limit voltage   $  V_{TFL}  $  , determined by the trap states filled limit. Hence, the trap density   $  (n_{\mathrm{trap}})  $   can be calculated using equation (1).

$$
V _ {T F L} = \frac {e n _ {t r a p} L ^ {2}}{2 \varepsilon_ {0} \varepsilon}
$$

Where e is the elementary charge, L is the thickness of the perovskite film,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity, and   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative dielectric constant of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  . The   $  V_{TFL}  $   values of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films prepared with and without IT-4F are 0.64 and 0.94 V, respectively. The corresponding electron trap density is   $  5.68 \times 10^{15}  $   and   $  1.52 \times 10^{16}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  , respectively. The defect density of the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (IT-4F) film is much lower than that of the neat MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film, which further confirms the nature of the decreased defects by IT-4F additives. It was reported that the passivation of the defect can effectively can de-pin the grain boundary and drive larger grain formation. The X-ray Diffraction spectra (XRD) and atomic force microscope (AFM) were measured (Figure 3A) to investigate the crystal growth and morphology. The XRD patterns in Figure S4 show no additional peaks or peak shifts upon addition of trace amounts of IT-4F additive in comparison with the bare  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film, implying that the added IT-4F additive exists in an amorphous form. The (110) and (220) orientation peaks of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  were enhanced with addition of IT-4F additive, whereas the intensity of the other peaks was essentially unchanged. Furthermore, the full width half-maximum (FWHM) values of the peaks originating from perovskite were reduced (from 0.133 to 0.109 for the (110) peak), confirming that IT-4F additive interacted with perovskite precursors during crystal growth to enhance crystallinity and induce the preferred orientation of the film. The effect of IT-4F additive on the surface morphology of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer is investigated and the results are shown in Figure S5. The constitutive grains were significantly enlarged from about  $ 200\mathrm{nm} $  to larger than  $ 500\mathrm{nm} $ , which correlates with enhanced FWHM in the XRD measurements.

The current-voltage relation (I-V) characteristic curves of the PSC devices with and without polymer passivation are shown in Fig. 3a, and the corresponding device parameters are summarized in Table 1. The best PSC with negligible hysteresis shows a high reverse scanned PCE of  $ 15.92\% $  (averaged efficiency of  $ 15.2 \pm 0.7\% $  from more than 36 devices), with a  $ V_{OC} $  of  $ 1.05\mathrm{V} $ , a  $ J_{SC} $  of  $ 20.63\mathrm{mA/cm}^2 $ , and a fill factor (FF) of 0.73. With IT-4F additive treatment, the PCE of the PSC shows a significant increase in reverse-scanned to  $ 18.35\% $  (averaged efficiency of  $ 17.6 \pm 0.7\% $  from more than 36 devices), where the highest  $ V_{oc} $  is  $ 1.12\mathrm{V} $ ,  $ J_{SC} $  is  $ 21.35\mathrm{mA/cm}^2 $ , and FF is 0.76. The influence of thickness of  $ \mathrm{Li - NiO_x} $  and different amounts of IT-4F content on the device performance are also studied, and the I-V characteristic curves are shown in Fig. S6 and Fig. S7, respectively. The best device performance is obtained when the concentration of IT-4F is  $ 0.05\mathrm{mgml}^{-1} $ . Less IT-4F content will cause insufficient passivation, and excessive IT-4F might potentially cause charge transport problems.

Next, the carrier dynamics of the perovskite films were investigated to gain more insight into the mechanism of PSC device performance changes with the polymer passivation. In Fig. 4a, the time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) study of the perovskite films revealed efficient charge extraction from at  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/\mathrm{PCBM} $  interface. The bare  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film shows biexponential decay with fast ( $ \tau_1 = 50.1 $  ns) and slow ( $ \tau_2 = 134.7 $  ns) components, whereas different biexponential decay was observed ( $ \tau_1 = 88.9 $  ns,  $ \tau_2 = 504.5 $  ns) with IT-4F component. The average decay time  $ \tau $  might be related to trap-mediated monomolecular non-radiative recombination derived by traps. In contrast, the PL decay profile of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film with IT-4F shows a biexponential decay with  $ \tau = 305.4 $  ns, which suggests a significantly decreased defect density after the addition of IT-4F. The decrease in defect density with the addition of IT-4F can be attributed to decreased grain boundaries and their associated defects, which serves to passivate the grain boundary defect sites and remove trap states and will facilitate charge transport and enhance the quasi-Fermi level and thereby  $ V_{OC} $ .

# Conclusion

In conclusion, we have successfully demonstrated a bifunctional Lewis base additive to boost perovskite grain growth and passivate grain boundaries. As a result of the enhanced crystallinity and passivated grain boundaries, the trap densities in both the grain interior and at the boundaries were significantly reduced, resulting in an enhanced carrier lifetime. Finally, the champion PCE of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based solar cell was enhanced from  $ 16.08\% $  to  $ 18.35\% $  upon an optimized addition of IT-4F. The encapsulation-free devices based on the IT-4F demonstrated long-term stability, remained over  $ 80\% $  of its initial efficiency after 30 days storage in the ambient air at  $ 10\% $  RH humidity.
