# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted much more attention owing to the advantages of simple manufacturing process, low cost and its rapidly increasing efficiency.  $  ^{1-6}  $   So far, the highest published value of the device power conversion efficiency (PCE) has already achieved up to 24.2 %.  $  ^{7}  $   Nowadays, mesoporous structure and planar heterojunction are acceptable by researchers. Among them, the inverted structure of planar heterojunction, whose basic structure is ITO/HTL/Perovskite/ETL/Electrode, has the characteristics of low processing temperature, simple manufacturing process and low hysteresis.  $  ^{8-10}  $   Hole transport layer (HTL) which sites in the cathode and optical absorption layer of perovskite solar cells, is used to gather a great amount of photogenerated carrier in inverted planar PSCs. Hence, it has dual functions of transporting holes and blocking electrons. For HTL materials, it mainly includes organic small molecules semiconductors, such as 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) and its derivatives,  $  ^{11-14}  $   organic polymers, such as P3HT and PTAA,  $  ^{15-17}  $   poly(3,4-

Page 3 of 23

Journal of Materials Chemistry C

ethylenedioxythiophene):polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) and its derivatives,  $  ^{[3](1),198,29]}  $   and inorganic semiconductors such as CuSCN,  $  ^{[22-24]}  $     $  NiO^{25-28}  $   etc. However, compared to inorganic p-type semiconductors, the organic materials have lower hole carrier mobilities and worse chemical stability. Therefore, more and more researchers use inorganic p-type semiconductors as the HTL. In contrast to other inorganic semiconductors, NiO has wider band gap, higher transmittance and better chemical stability. Besides, in the case of energy level, NiO is more suitable to the perovskite layer, which is unhindered to hole collection and electron blocking.  $  ^{[25,29-32]}  $   Meanwhile, the perovskite layer with NiO also shows better stability compared with PEDOT:PSS, PTAA and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD HTLs.

The further improvement of NiO based devices is limited due to the low intrinsic electrical conductivity of pristine NiO. To solve this problem, several approaches such as metal ion doping, surface treatment etc. have been developed to improve the film quality of NiO. $ ^{28,33-37} $  Among them, metal ion doing has been proved to be a powerful strategy to tune the electrical properties of semiconductor films. For example, To improve the film conductivity of NiO, several kings of metal doings, such as Cu, Co, Cs, and Li, have been used. $ ^{33,36-38} $  Among them, Li has been regards as a promising dopant for metal oxide, has also been used as in NiO HTL, and achieved a good performance and stability. Meanwhile, co-doping with lithium and metal ions such as Cu and Mg has been proved to have a synergistic effect on the NiO properties and corresponding PSCs performance. $ ^{39} $

In this paper, we firstly investigated  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  co-doped with Li and Co as an effective

Journal of Materials Chemistry C

Page 4 of 23

hole transport layer. It was found that the hole mobility could be effectively improved by Li and Co co-doping, resulting in enhanced charge transfer and extraction. Meanwhile, the Li and Co can enhance the perovskite film crystallinity and film quality. Finally, significantly improvements of the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ), short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ), fill factor (FF), and PCE of PSCs are observed. Moreover, the device stability has also been improved. Most importantly, the doped films are prepared by a simple solution-based method, and they are highly reproducible and cheap, hence, it is compatible with large area production.

The patterned ITO glass substrates were sequentially ultrasonic cleaned with detergent, de-ionized water, acetone, and isopropyl alcohol at 50 °C for 20 min, respectively. Then, the ITO substrates were dried with nitrogen gun and treated in a UV ozone oven for 15 min before a thin layer of NiOₓ or doped NiOₓ was spin-coated on the substrates at 3000 rpm for 42 s and annealed at 250 °C for 40 min according to our previously reported results.  $  ^{25}  $   After that the substrates were transferred into a nitrogen-filled glovebox. The perovskite layer was prepared by a two-step method as our reported previously.  $  ^{40}  $   1.36 M PbI₂ and 0.24 M PbCl₂ were dissolved in 1 mL DMF, and stirred for 2 h at 75 °C. 70 mg MAI and 30 mg FAI were dissolved in 1 mL IPA with additional 10 μL DMF. The samples were annealed at 100 °C for 10 min on a hotplate. Next, a layer of PCBM (20 mg mL⁻¹ in chlorobenzene) was spin coated on the top of the perovskite layer at 2000 rpm for 40 s. After that, a thin layer of BCP (0.5 mg mL⁻¹ in IPA) was spin coated on the top of the PCBM layer. The devices were finished by thermally evaporated 100 nm Ag in high vacuum (≤5×10⁻⁴ Pa) through a shadow mask. All the devices had an area of 7.5 mm². To gain deeper insight into the performance enhancement, TPC and TPV measurements were taken to investigate the charge transfer and charge recombination behaviors (Figure 6). The shorter carrier lifetime based doped  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  based devices indicates the efficient hole transfer/collection from the perovskite layer to  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ . Transient photovoltage can provide the information for the charge recombination lifetime under open-circuit conditions. As shown in Figure 6b, the fitted recombination lifetime were  $ 3.63\mathrm{ms} $ ,  $ 4.31\mathrm{ms} $  and  $ 5.24\mathrm{ms} $  for pristine  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ , Li- $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  and Li and Co co-doped  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ , respectively. Hence, enhanced charge recombination lifetime for perovskite devices based on doped  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  suggests the reduced charge carrier recombination in the devices.

# Conclusion

In summary, we have studied the influence of Li and Co co-doped  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  HTL on the photovoltaic performance of PSCs. It was found that at proper doping ratio of Li and Co, the  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  films exhibited improved film conductivity, hole mobilities, and better energy level alignment with perovskite films. Finally, enhanced charge transfer/extraction and suppressed recombanition was achieved based on doped  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  HTLs.
