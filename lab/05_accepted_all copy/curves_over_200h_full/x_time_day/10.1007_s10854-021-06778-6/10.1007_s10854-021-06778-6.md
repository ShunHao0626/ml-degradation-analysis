# ABSTRACT

In the conventional perovskite solar cells (PSCs) structure,   $  TiO_{2}  $   is the most commonly used electron transport layer (ETL) as it has good energy-level matching with perovskite layer. However, oxygen vacancy defects will appear when   $  TiO_{2}  $   is exposed to ultraviolet light for a long time, which would reduce its carrier extraction ability. Here, we report a simple and effective interface engineering method for   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL to achieve a highly efficient PSCs. An ultra-thin [6,6]-phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) layer is used to modify the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite layer interface. The PCBM effectively passivates defects on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface, promotes the extraction of electrons, and improves the quality of the perovskite film. Finally, a high efficiency of 16.4% was achieved for the modified device, much higher than 13.5% of the reference devices. After storing for 12 days in an atmosphere with an air humidity of   $  30 \pm 5\%  $  , the efficiency of the PSCs maintains more than 60% of its initial level. This strategy is beneficial to enhance the efficiency and working stability of PSCs.

# 1 Introduction

Inorganic–organic hybrid perovskite materials have drawn tremendous attention due to their high absorption coefficient, long carrier diffusion length, high carrier mobility, and bandgap tunability   $  [1-6]  $  . Within few years of development, the energy conversion efficiency records of perovskite solar cells

(PSCs) have improved rapidly from 3.8 to 25.5% [7–17], which is extremely close to the Shockley–Queisser (S–Q) limit of single-junction solar cells [18]. However, the instability of the device is a primary factor restricting its commercial use. Well-designed charge transport layers are decisive for an efficient performance and long-term stability [19–21]. In the structure of PSC, perovskite layer as a light absorber is sandwiched between electron transport layer (ETL) and hole transport layer (HTL) [24]. ETL plays an important role in extracting and transporting photo-induced electron, passivating traps in perovskite, and even improving long-term stability, which has been proved to be a decisive factor for effective PSCs [25–28]. Currently,   $  TiO_{2}  $   has been extensively explored as ETL due to its wide bandgap, long charge lifetime, and high chemical stability. Unfortunately,   $  TiO_{2}  $   has inherent drawbacks. For example, the electrical conductivity of   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer (  $  \sim 10^{-11} S cm^{-1}  $  ) is rather low [29]. In addition, mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   (m-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) surface has a lot of oxygen vacancy [30, 31], which will induce photogenerated electron aggregation in the transfer process from perovskite to   $  TiO_{2}  $   and decrease the current output of the device. To overcome these shortcomings, various surface modification strategies have been developed [32–35]. As fullerene molecules could act as a good electron transporter (collector), they had been reported to modify the   $  TiO_{2}  $ /perovskite interface [36]. With similar interfacial layer, the cell performance was enhanced and the photocurrent hysteresis behavior was significantly reduced [37]. The PCBM interlayer had been also investigated and widely applied between perovskite absorber and electron-transporting layers. Park et al. reported dependence of hysteresis on perovskite layer thickness in a normal planar structure based on c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   and PCBM as ETL [38]. Chang and Zhang et al. used the combination of   $  TiCl_{4}  $   and PCBM to modify the c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   layer, which could largely improve the PSC device performance with power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 16.4% and reduce hysteresis due to the synergistic effect [36].

PSCs based on  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  film exhibit outstanding photovoltaic performance, but defects on surface of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  have seriously bad influence on interface charge transfer and device stability as the large surface area of the mesoporous film. Therefore, PCBM treatment was carried out to optimize the  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $ /perovskite interface to achieve significant enhancement of both device efficiency and stability. We found that PCBM

decoration could enlarge the grain size of perovskite layer, which lead to an increase in photovoltaic performance of the device. The prepared optimal PSC reached an efficiency of 16.4%, with an open-circuit photovoltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.034 V, a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 21.5 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and a fill factor (FF) of 0.74, compared to PCE of 13.5% for the control one. Moreover, the PCBM interface modification could also apparently enhance the long-term working stability of PSCs.

# 2 Experimental section

# 2.1 Device fabrication

Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO, Pilkington, TEC 15) glass substrate was cleaned with deionized water, ethanol, isopropanol, and absolute ethyl alcohol for 30 min. A dense blocking   $  TiO_{2}  $   was deposited via spin coating method, which was followed by a heating process at 450 °C for 30 min. For the m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   film,   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste (Dyesol, 18 NRT) was mixed with absolute ethanol in the ratio 1:5.5 (weight ratio), which was spin coated on the substrate at 5000 rpm for 30 s, then dried at 130 °C in air for 8 min, and annealed at 500 °C in air for 20 min. The PCBM precursor solutions with solvent of chlorobenzene were prepared in concentrations of 2.5, 5, and 10 mg/mL. 50 μL of PCBM solution was evenly dropped on the m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   layer and then was spin coated at 1500 rpm for 30 s. The   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   and   $  PbI_{2}  $   were mixed in anhydrous DMSO in a molar ratio of 1:1 and stirred at 70 °C for 3 h to obtain a perovskite precursor solution. Perovskite precursor solutions were spin coated on mesoporous films at 1000 rpm for 10 s and then at 5000 rpm for 30 s. 1 mL toluene was dropped quickly on the substrate at 5000 rpm for 15 s. The perovskite film was then annealed at 110 °C for 10 min. The HTL solution was prepared by SpiroOMeTAD powder (72.3 mg) with additives of 17.5 μL Li-bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI), 28.8 μL 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP), and 1 mL chlorobenzene. The HTL was deposited by spin coating at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, a silver electrode with a thickness of about 60 nm was evaporated on the hole transport layer.

# 2.2 Characterization

The surface roughness of PCBM-modified   $  TiO_{2}  $   was characterized by a step profiler (838-179B, Bruker). The morphology, phase, optical, and photovoltaic properties of the PCBM-decorated   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers and related films were characterized by field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM, SU8220, Hitachi), X-ray diffraction (XRD, D8 Advance, Bruker), and UV–visible spectrophotometer (UV–Vis, Cary 300, Varian). Interface charge transfer process was demonstrated by steady-state photoluminescence spectra (PL, Cary 300, Varian). Photocurrent–voltage (J–V) curves of PSCs were characterized by an electrochemical workstation (Keithley 2420 Source Meter) under solar illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , Oriel Sol 3A, Newport) which was calibrated by a standard silicon solar cell (Oriel Instrument). The incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) was measured using a power source (Newport 300 W Xenon lamp, 66902) with a monochromator (Newport Cornerstone 260) and a power meter (Newport 2936-C).

# 3 Results and discussion

# 3.1 Microstructure analysis

The surface morphology of perovskite film has a great effect on the performance of PSCs. As shown in Fig. 1, the concentrations of PCBM that deposited on  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  film change obviously the morphology of perovskite films. The average grain sizes are 143, 158, 126, and  $ 112\mathrm{nm} $ , respectively, for perovskite films deposited on  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  film without and with treatment by 2.5, 5, and  $ 10\mathrm{mg / mL} $  PCBM (Fig. S1). It indicates that surface modification of  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  by appropriate PCBM can provide more favorable sites for the crystallization of perovskite crystals. However, excess PCBM on  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  is not beneficial for perovskite growth as the low grain sizes of perovskite film. To investigate the influence mechanism of the surface modification process, surface roughness of  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  film is measured. As shown in Fig. S2, the surface roughness values gradually decrease from 21.79 to  $ 5.79\mathrm{nm} $  as PCBM concentrations increase. A smooth  $ \mathrm{m - TiO_2} $  film is beneficial to obtain uniform perovskite film with large grain size, but the poor wettability between PCBM and DMF/DMSO

solvents is adverse to perovskite growth. This competition between film roughness and wettability reaches a balance as PCBM concentration is 2.5 mg/mL, and the corresponding perovskite film presents a uniform morphology with the highest grain size. Additionally, the modification could also affect perovskite film thickness, the values decrease from 375 to 280 nm (Fig. S3), which is induced by the low roughness and poor wettability of m-TiO  $  _{2}  $  after PCBM treatment. A thick perovskite film has advantages in enhancing light absorption, but might induce serious charge carrier recombination. While, a thin perovskite film presents an opposite effect [39–42]. Therefore, considering the optical and electrical properties of perovskite film, a proper perovskite film thickness for PSCs is needed.

# 3.2 Optical performance analysis

Perovskite film with high light absorption is a precondition for high-efficient PSCs. As shown in Fig. 2a, the perovskite film deposited on m-  $  TiO_{2}  $  treated by 2.5 mg/mL PCBM exhibits the highest light absorption. Although the film is relatively thinner than the pristine one, its larger grain size could compensate this disadvantage. As the concentration of PCBM increases, the absorption of corresponding perovskite films decrease obviously, which should be attributed to the gradual decrease of perovskite film thickness. Interestingly, as demonstrated by SEM images, the perovskite film deposited on m-  $  TiO_{2}  $  modified by 5 mg/mL PCBM has lower grain size and thinner film thickness than the pristine one, but it presents relatively higher light absorption. We think that this phenomenon might be induced by the additional light absorption of PCBM, this will reduce the effective absorption of the perovskite layer, and may be harmful for the final device performance. Apart from high light absorption of perovskite film, an efficient charge transfer process at interfaces is also essential for an excellent device. The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) were carried out to explore the charge transport process at ETL/perovskite interface (Fig. 2b). PL quench could be observed clearly in all PCBM-modified m-  $  TiO_{2}  $ -related perovskite films, and the one used 2.5 mg/mL PCBM exhibits the weakest PL intensity. The increased  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  mainly originate from the larger perovskite grain size, in which it is greatly possible that carrier could pass the perovskite film without encountering much grain boundaries. Moreover, the increased light absorption and improved interface charge transfer are also beneficial to improve  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  of the devices. Additionally, PCBM can improve compactness of the ETL and avoid the direct contact between perovskite carrier interface recombination in the device; compactness enhancement of ETL by PCBM and the positive result reflected by PL measurement both indicate that introduction of PCBM with concentration of  $ 2.5\mathrm{mg / mL} $  could obtain a device with high  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $ . As the concentration of PCBM increases to  $ 5\mathrm{mg / mL} $ , the corresponding device presents close performance to the pristine one, although its perovskite film has relatively lower grain size and thinner film thickness. This could be caused by the better charge transfer process at ETL/perovskite interface as demonstrated in Fig. 2b.

However, as the concentration of PCBM further increases to 10 mg/mL, the photovoltaic performance of corresponding device, especially the   $  J_{sc}  $  , decreases apparently, mainly because of its little perovskite grain size and thin perovskite film thickness. Hysteresis effect commonly exists in the testing of PSCs, and charge accumulation at interfaces in the device is one of the main reason. As shown in Table 1, after introduction of PCBM, all hysteresis index values (n) of corresponding devices decrease obviously, and the one with PCBM concentration of 2.5 mg/mL exhibits the minimum value, indicating the least charge accumulation in the device. The device using 2.5 mg/mL shows the highest photo-to-electron conversion efficiency (Fig. 3a), especially at longer wavelength region (600–800 nm), which indicates a less sun light loss. The integrated current density determined according to the EQE curves (Fig. 3b) is 20.7 and 17.7 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   for the champion cell and pristine one, respectively. The difference between integrated   $  J_{sc}  $   and testing results (Table 1) is less than 10%, demonstrating the accuracy of our

Long-term photostability is important for PSCs. Although   $  TiO_{2}  $   is a widely used material for selective electronic contact, it usually causes rapid degradation of perovskite devices under light. In order to investigate the air stability of our devices, we measured the PCE of unencapsulated PSCs that were stored in ambient air under different times (the relative humidity is kept at 25–35% and the temperature is in the range of 20–25 °C). We know that water molecules in the air could quickly decompose the perovskite film, which will seriously affect the stability of the device. As shown in Fig. 4a, the performance of the control device shows serious deterioration, only 30% of the initial PCE being preserved after 12 days. By contrast, the PCBM-modified (2.5 mg/mL) PSCs can still maintain 60% of the initial efficiency. This enhancement may be attributed to the partial inhibition of contact between m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   by PCBM. We further explored the influence of PCBM layer between the m-  $  TiO_{2}  $   and the perovskite layer on the stability of the perovskite. As shown in the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns (Fig. 4b), both the modified and control perovskite films present a sharp diffraction peak at 14.3°, assigning to (110) lattice plane of perovskite, which indicates an excellent perovskite crystallinity. The characteristic peaks at 28.6° and 32.0° should be assigned to (220) and (310) lattice planes of tetragonal perovskite. In the XRD pattern, the diffraction peak at 12.60° belongs to   $  PbI_{2}  $  , a kind of decomposition product of perovskite.

# 4 Conclusion

In conclusion, we show a simple strategy to achieve high-efficient PSCs using ultra-thin PCBM-modified m-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite layer interface. We prepared a device with the structure of FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /m-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /PCBM/MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /Spiro-OMeTAD/Ag. Through the modification of the PCBM layer, the average grain size of the perovskite is increased and the grain boundary is reduced, resulting in stronger light absorption. Furthermore, the introduction of the modified layer of PCBM effectively promoted the interfacial charge transport, which is manifested in the rapid quenching of photogenerated carriers at the m-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface and reduced interfacial charge accumulation. As a result, the device with PCBM exhibits a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 21.5 mA·cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.034 V, a fill factor of 0.74, and a PCE of 16.4%, which is much higher than the control device. In addition, the modified device exhibits lower J-V hysteresis. Under the conditions of a relative humidity of 30 ± 5% and a temperature of 20 ± 5 °C, PCE dropped by 40% after 12-day exposure, showing a good working stability. This strategy is beneficial to enhance the efficiency and working stability of PSCs.
