# Enhanced Moisture Stability of Perovskite Solar Cells With Mixed-Dimensional and Mixed-Compositional Light-Absorbing Materials

Jiajiu Ye, Haiying Zheng, Liangzheng Zhu, Guozheng Liu, Xuhui Zhang, Tasawar Hayat, Xu Pan,* and Songyuan Dai*

In spite of perovskite solar cells have achieved great progress in recent years, the long term stability of perovskite material is always a big obstacle before commercialization, especially the moisture instability. In this paper, we reported a layered perovskite solar cell based on   $  (\mathrm{CF}_{3}\mathrm{CH}_{2}\mathrm{NH}_{2})_{2}(\mathrm{FA}_{0.825}\mathrm{MA}_{0.15}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.025})_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}(\mathrm{l}_{0.85}\mathrm{Br}_{0.15})_{3n+1}  $   series   $  (n=1-\infty)  $  , which possess excellent optoelectronic property and stability. The perovskite film displayed an ultrahigh surface coverage and provided high-quality thin films which lead to an advancement in solar cells performance. We had fabricated solar cells based on   $  (\mathrm{CF}_{3}\mathrm{CH}_{2}\mathrm{NH}_{2})_{2}(\mathrm{FA}_{0.825}\mathrm{MA}_{0.15}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.025})_{29}\mathrm{Pb}_{30}(\mathrm{l}_{0.85}\mathrm{Br}_{0.15})_{91}  $   light absorber and got the best PCE of 18.17%. The average PCE of 17.84% was achieved with   $  J_{sc}  $   of 21.05 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.095V, and FF of 77.4%. Moreover, the multi-layered perovskite showed excellent property of moisture-resistant compared to three-dimensional type PSCs. Under relative humidity level of 65%, the devices without any encapsulation did not deteriorate for up to approximately 28 days with the PCE maintaining over 90%.

Among light absorber candidates, the most prominent choices were three-dimensional (3D) methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI $ _3 $ ), formamidine lead iodide (FAPbI $ _3 $ ), and its diverse combinations. It can be ascribed to the outstanding properties includ-

ing high extinction coefficient, long exciton, charge diffusion lengths, medium band gap, and small exciton binding energy, etc.  $  ^{[9]}  $   In spite of this tremendous progress, the moisture instability has been poorly addressed. So devices were always stored under low humidity (RH) for keeping devices performance.  $  ^{[10]}  $   The long term stability of the perovskite material in the presence of water should be overcame before commercialization. In the case of high humidity, the perovskite (taking   $  \mathrm{HC}(\mathrm{NH}_{2})_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{3}  $   for example) degrades into the original   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  \mathrm{HC}(\mathrm{NH}_{2})_{2}\mathrm{I}  $   and thus results in a yellow solid, which corresponds to the experimentally observed yellow film. At the beginning, a water molecule interacting with the proton in   $  \mathrm{HC}(\mathrm{NH}_{2})_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{3}  $   crystal induces the degradation process and ultimately degrades into aqueous HI, solid   $  PbI_{2}  $   and volatile   $  \mathrm{HC}(\mathrm{NH}_{2})_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[11]}  $

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic perovskite and its derivatives as a new branch of solar cells have achieved rapid development since miyasaka

A lot of works had been addressed the issue of degradation due to moisture, including hydrophobic HTMs,  $  ^{[12]}  $  graphene/ conductive polymer composite as a multifunctional coating,  $  ^{[13]}  $  alumina coatings on the surface of the perovskite,  $  ^{[14]}  $  interfacial layer modification,  $  ^{[15]}  $  device geometry design,  $  ^{[16]}  $  mixed compositional perovskites etc.  $  ^{[17]}  $  However, few of these approaches can effectively prevent the films from moisture percolation and deterioration. An effective way to protect perovskite film from moisture is to increase the tortuosity in the path of water molecules penetrating into the inside of perovskite. Takeo and

and co-workers reported perovskite solar cells (PSCs) in 2009.  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   However, they did not attract a lot of attention because of fast dissolution of perovskites in redox electrolyte solution. In the past few years, PSCs have shown unexpected and rapid progress, Park et al. reported Spiro-OMeTAD(2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N, N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine) -9, 9'-spirobifluorene) as solid hole transport materials (HTM) used in PSCs and got photoelectric conversion efficiency of 9.7%.  $  ^{[4]}  $   Within a few years, this state-of-the-art lab scale solar cells prepared in a dried glove box have refreshed up to 22.1% until today, reported by KRICT.  $  ^{[5-8]}  $

Liu P used   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   as precursor solution additives to improve the performance of devices,  $  ^{[18]}  $   Li X modified the surface of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite in the presence of butylphosphonic acid 4-ammonium chloride in order to enhance devices resistance to moisture. It acts as a crosslink between neighboring grains in the perovskite structure, through strong hydrogen bonding of the   $  -\mathrm{PO(OH)}_{2}  $   and   $  -NH^{3+}  $   terminal groups to the perovskite surface.  $  ^{[19]}  $

Besides additives, two-dimensional (2D) or layered MD perovskite light absorber can enhanced moisture stability impressively.  $  ^{[20-21]}  $   In the early period of 1980s, the most well studied compounds of 2D homologous halide perovskites had been known as   $  (\mathrm{RNH}_{3})_{2}\mathrm{MI}_{4}  $   (n=1-18) family, where M is a group Pb, Mn, Fe, Cu, Cd, or lanthanide metal, R is a primary alkyl ammonium cations.  $  ^{[22-23]}  $   But few works were concerned with optical spectra of these materials since Ishihara reported   $  (\mathrm{C}_{10}\mathrm{H}_{21}\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{4}  $   in 1989.  $  ^{[24]}  $   2D perovskite can be regulated flexibly due to the stacking of the metal halide lattices (n) and amino source. As a result, the dimensionality and exciton binding energy could be modulated that will make these perovskite materials suitable for light harvesting applications. Nevertheless, most of the low-dimensional perovskite solar cell suffered low photovoltaic performance. Not long ago Smith et al. reported the structure of layered perovskite   $  (\mathrm{RNH}_{3})_{2}(\mathrm{MA})_{n-1}\mathrm{PbX}_{3n+1}  $   (  $  R=\mathrm{PhC}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{5}^{+}  $  ) as a photovoltaic absorber in a solar cell and got a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 4.73% with an open-circuit voltage of 1.18 V,  $  ^{[25]}  $   and Lioz Etgar's group got maximum voltage of 1.46 V.  $  ^{[26]}  $   Recently, Kanatzidis et al. fabricated a series 2D perovskite   $  (\mathrm{CH}_{3}(\mathrm{CH}_{2})_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3})_{2}  $   (  $  MA)_{n-1}PbnI_{3n+1}  $   (n=1, 2, 3, and 4) films and achieved PCE of 4.02%.  $  ^{[27]}  $   Yao reported   $  (\mathrm{PEI})_{2}(\mathrm{MA})_{n-1}\mathrm{PbnI}_{3n+1}  $   (n=3, 5, 7) by incorporating polyethylenimine (PEI) cations within the layered structure. The moisture-stable nature of this multilayered perovskite materials allow for the simple one-step fabrication of cells with an aperture area of   $  2.32\,cm^{2}  $   under ambient humidity that have a PCE up to 8.77%.  $  ^{[28]}  $   On the basis of the Smith's work, Sargent reported the reduced-dimensionality (quasi-2D) perovskite with certified PCE of 15.3% (n=60) by using phenylethyl ammonium, observing greatly improved performance longevity. Although the devices have lower efficiencies compared with conventional 3D perovskite absorbers, but the layer structure brings distinct advantages in the aspect of against moisture.  $  ^{[29]}  $   This type multilayered perovskites with the generic structural formula of   $  (\mathrm{A})_{2}(\mathrm{MA})_{n-1}\mathrm{PbI}_{3n+1}  $   (n is an integer, from 1 to   $  \infty  $  ), where A is a primary or secondary ammonium cation (aliphatic or aromatic alkyl ammonium) acting as a spacer between the perovskite lattices to form the unit layers. The corner-sharing   $  [PbI_{6}]^{4-}  $   octahedra structural units was confined between interdigitating bilayers of ammonium cations branched chain. The units are stacked together by coulombic and hydrogen bonding forces to maintain the structure integrity. Finally, the octahedral crystal structure of 3D perovskite was changed to layered structure, which was formed by slicing the 3D skeleton into slabs.

In this article, we reported mixed-dimensionality and mixed-compositional (MDMC) lead iodide perovskite fabrication and photoelectric properties based on   $  [CF_{3}CH_{2}NH_{2}]_{2}(FA_{0.825}MA_{0.15}Cs_{0.025})_{n-1}Pb_{n}(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_{3n+1}  $   series   $  (n=1-\infty,\quad CF_{3}CH_{2}NH_{2}  $

was abbreviated as TFEA).  $  ^{[30]}  $   As a result the MDMC perovskite solar cells possess the properties of enhanced stability of 2D perovskite and the excellent optoelectronic parameters of 3D perovskite. Different from 3D perovskite film, MDMC lead iodide perovskite film was easy to yield high-quality films with smooth and shining surface by using simple one-step spin-coating approach.

Meanwhile, we've got a suitable n values to make a better balance between stability and optoelectronic properties. We have successfully fabricated solar cells based on   $  [TFEA]_{2}  $     $  (FA_{0.825}MA_{0.15}Cs_{0.025})_{29}Pb_{30}(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_{91}  $   thin films by one step deposition method, and overall power conversion efficiency over 18% was achieved. The TPRL results showed that the carrier lifetime increased from 1.97 to 4.61 ns with increase of dimensionality. Moreover, the perovskite solar cells based on this multilayered structures show the capacity of excellent resistance to moisture. The devices were exposed to 65% humidity condition without any encapsulation for stability testing. After 28 days, performance of MDMC PSCs was still maintained over 90%, rendering them to be candidate for future commercial application. In contrast, 3D conventional devices began to deteriorate after preparation and decomposed to   $  PbI_{2}  $   gradually.

# 2. Result and Discussion

In case of the   $  [CF_{3}CH_{2}NH_{2}]_{2}(FA_{0.825}MA_{0.15}Cs_{0.025})_{n-1}Pb_{n}(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_{3n+1}(n=1-\infty)  $   compound (as shown Figure 1), the perovskite crystal slabs were isolated between ammonium ions. The ammonium cation of   $  -NH_{2}^{+}  $  looks like “head” which was directly pointed to the center of the rhombic corner crowded by four adjacent   $  [PbI_{6}]^{4-}  $   octahedra, and the “tail” of hydrophobic alkyl chain pointed out to the interlayer space. The layered perovskite slabs were formed by this spatial orientation of the head and tail architecture. The structure was stabilized by coulomb attraction and hydrogen bonding force of   $  NH_{2}-H\ldots I-Pb  $  . Both of hydrophobic alkyl chain and layer upon layer structure act as an impermeable barrier.

This MDMC lead iodide perovskite films were fabricated by one-step spin coating deposition method with DMF/DMSO precursor solutions. Generally, simple one-step processing of FAPbI $ _3 $  precursor cannot afford continuous surface and the films always with crack. The film quality will be improved by introducing [TFEA] $ _2 $ (FA $ _{0.825} $ MA $ _{0.15} $ Cs $ _{0.025} $ ) $ _{n-1} $ Pb $ _n $ (I $ _{0.85} $ Br $ _{0.15} $ ) $ _{3n+1} $  series precursor as shown in polarizing microscope images of Figure S1. This type of multilayered perovskite films would grow into stacked layer, showing a sheet structure and small grain size. There is a suitable content of ammonium cation increased (the integer of  $ n $ ) to form well-defined films. Herein, the ammonium is trifluoroethylamine cation which has a hydrophobic trifluoroethyl group linked with nitrogen atom. The layered structure as protective shield protected the perovskite layer from water infiltration. Even though the uppermost layers of perovskite will be damaged, but the hydrophobic alkyl branches will prevent the water from further damage. In other words, it is hard for the water entering into the perovskite layer.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we have demonstrated a way to fabricate MDMC perovskite of (TFEA)  $  _{2}  $  (FA  $  _{0.825}  $  MA  $  _{0.15}  $  Cs  $  _{0.025}  $  )  $  _{n-1}  $  Pb  $  _{n}  $  (I  $  _{0.85}  $  Br  $  _{0.15}  $  )  $  _{3n+1}  $  series (n=1-∞) and their application in photovoltaics by one-step deposition method. This MDMC layered perovskite combines features of high performance and stability, which originated from 3D and 2D type perovskite. It provided a facile access to ultrasmooth and high surface coverage thin films which lead to an advancement in high performance solar cells. We have successfully fabricated solar cells based on [TFEA]  $  _{2}  $  (FA  $  _{0.825}  $  MA  $  _{0.15}  $  Cs  $  _{0.025}  $  )  $  _{29}  $  Pb  $  _{30}  $  (I  $  _{0.85}  $  Br  $  _{0.15}  $  )  $  _{91}  $  thin films, offering broader absorption and the possibility to achieve higher photocurrent. The best device with a PCE of 18.17% has been obtained in this mixed dimensionality perovskite system and the average PCE of 17.84% with J  $  _{sc}  $  of 21.05 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , V  $  _{oc}  $  of 1.095V, and FF of 77.4%. The TRPL results showed that the carrier lifetime increased from 1.97 to 4.61 ns. Moreover, due to hydrophobic trifluoroethylamine chain and layer upon layer structure act as an impermeable barrier, the multilayered perovskite showed excellent property of moisture-resistant compared to 3D perovskite. The MDMC PSCs (n=30) without any encapsulation did not deteriorate with the PCE maintaining over 90% of the initial PCE under relative humidity of 65% at room temperature for up to approximately 28 days. In contrast, the photoelectric properties of 3D conventional device decreased continuously. All of these particular strengths enable them to be more attractive for large-scale industrial implementation.

# 4. Experimental

Materials: Lead (II) lodide (99.99%, trace metals basis) [for Perovskite precursor] and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.,   $  TiO_{2}  $   (particle size: about 30 nm, crystalline phase: anatase)   $  TiO_{2}  $   from dysol. DMF, chlorobenzene, lithium bis (trifluoromethylsulphonyl) imide (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) from Aldrich, and spiro-MeOTAD from Borun New Material Technology Co., Ltd. trifluoroethylamine hydriodide was prepared in lab. All chemicals were directly used without further purification. Patterned FTO-coated glass substrates with a sheet resistance of 15 ohm sq  $  ^{-1}  $   were cleaned sequentially by ultrasonication in mild detergent, deionized water, acetone and ethanolamine. The substrates were exposed to UV-ozone for 20 min prior to the spin coating step.

Solar Cell Device Fabrication: A thick dense blocking layer of   $  TiO_{2}  $   (bl-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) was deposited onto F-doped   $  SnO_{2}  $   (FTO) substrate by spin coating to prevent direct contact between the FTO and the hole-conducting layer. A   $  TiO_{2}  $   blocking layer was deposited on the cleaned FTO by spray pyrolysis, using dry air as carrier gas, at 460°C from a precursor solution of titanium diisopropoxide (0.6 ml) and bis

(acetylacetonate) (0.4 ml) in anhydrous isopropanol (7 ml). A mesoporous TiO  $  _{2}  $  layer (dysol, particle size: about 30 nm, crystalline phase: anatase, diluted to w/w = 1/6 (in ethanol) about 160-180 nm thickness film was deposited by spin coating at 4000 rpm for 20 s onto the bl-TiO  $  _{2}  $ /FTO substrate. After spin coating, the substrate was immediately dried on a hotplate at 80°C, and the substrates were then calcined at 500°C to remove organic components. The perovskite film was deposited by spin coating onto the TiO  $  _{2}  $  substrate. The prepared 1.4M Pb  $  ^{2+}  $  precursor solution of FA  $  _{0.81}  $  MA  $  _{0.15}  $  Cs  $  _{0.025}  $  PbI  $  _{2.5}  $  Br  $  _{0.45}  $  and [CF  $  _{3}  $  CH  $  _{2}  $  NH  $  _{2}  $ ]  $  _{2}  $  (FA  $  _{0.825}  $  -MA  $  _{0.15}  $  Cs  $  _{0.025}  $ )  $  _{n-1}  $  Pb  $  _{n}  $  (I  $  _{0.85}  $  Br  $  _{0.15}  $ )  $  _{3n+1}  $  precursor solution were stirred in a mixture of DMSO and DMF (v/v = 8:2) at 60°C for completely dissolved. The resulting solution was coated onto the mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $ /bl-TiO  $  _{2}  $ /FTO substrate by a consecutive two-step spin-coating process at 1100 and 5000 r.p.m for 20 and 30 s respectively. During the second spin-coating step at last 10s, the substrate was treated with anti-solvent (chlorobenzene1) to form the perovskite crystal. FAI was synthesized from hydroiodic acid reacting with formamidine acetate according to reference. The HTM layer was deposited by spin-coating at 4000 rpm for 20 s, using a solution of spiro-MeOTAD, 4-tert-butylpyridine, and lithium bis (trifluoromethylsulphonyl) imide and the Co (III)-complex. Finally, Gold (80 nm) counter electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation on top of the device to form the back contact.

Characterization: The PCE and J-V curves were measured by using a solar simulator (Newport, Oriel Class A, 91195A) with a source meter (Keithley 2420) at 100 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  illumination AM 1.5G and a calibrated Si-reference cell certified by NREL. The J-V curves for all devices were measured by masking the active area with a black mask 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $ . UV-vis absorption spectra were recorded on a UV-vis spectrometer (Hitachi U-3300). Incident photon to current efficiency (IPCE) were confirmed as a function of wavelength from 300 to 800 nm (PV Measurements, Inc.), with dual Xenon/quartz halogen light source, measured in DC mode with no bias light used. The setup was calibrated with a certified silicon solar cell (Fraunhofer ISE) prior to measurements. Film morphology was investigated by using a high-resolution scanning electron microscope equipped with a Schottky Field Emission gun. PL spectra were recorded by exciting the perovskite films deposited onto mesoporous TiO  $  _{2}  $  at 490 nm with a standard 450 W Xenon CW lamp. The signal was recorded by a spectrofluorometer (photon technology international) and analyzed by the software Fluorescence. The time-resolved PL experiments were performed with the same Fluorescence Lifetime Spectrofluorometer using a pulsed source at 450 nm (the illumination source is a pulsed nitrogen dye laser) and the signal was recorded at 765 nm by the Time Correlated Single Photon Counting (TCSPC) technique. The samples were excited from the perovskite side. X-ray diffraction spectra of the perovskite films were recorded on an X'Pert MPD PRO (PANalytical). The data were collected at room temperature in the 2θ range 10–50°. The automatic divergence slit (10 mm) and beam mask (10 mm) were adjusted to the dimension of the films. A baseline correction was applied to all X-Ray thin film diffractograms to compensate for the broad feature arising from the FTO glass and anatase substrate.
