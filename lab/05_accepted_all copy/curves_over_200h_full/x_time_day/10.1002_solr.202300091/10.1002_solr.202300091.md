# Zwitterionic Ionic Liquid as Additive for High-Performance FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  Perovskite Solar Cells with Negligible Hysteresis

Xiangdong Li, Yu Zou, Shangbo Yu, Xin Zhao, Wenjin Yu, Shuang Yang, Haoqing Guo, Lixin Xiao, Zhijian Chen, and Bo Qu*

Imidazolium-based ionic liquids have been established as promising candidates for additives in perovskite solar cells. Herein, 1-(1-ethyl-3-imidazolium)propane-3-sulfonate (EIMS) zwitterionic ionic liquid is studied as additive to enhance the performance of the  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  perovskite solar cells. The addition of EIMS leads to the generation of porous  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film, which promotes the reaction of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  with FAI solution and thus improves the quality of  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  perovskite film during the two-step sequential spin coating. It is revealed that EIMS accelerates the extraction of electron, inhibits the formation of pinhole, and reduces the defect density, which leads to an increased efficiency of  $ 22.1\% $ . The hysteresis index is also reduced from 0.142 to 0.009, which indicates the addition of EIMS can realize a negligible hysteresis behavior in the solar cells. Moreover, devices prepared with EIMS retain above  $ 96\% $  of the original power conversion efficiency (PCE) value after being stored in dry air for 45 days. All these results demonstrate that the imidazolium-based zwitterionic ionic liquid additive strategy is a promising way for high-performance perovskite solar cells.

# 1. Introduction

Single-junction lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved an unprecedented power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.7% in just a few years,  $  ^{[1]}  $  owing to their high absorption coefficient,  $  ^{[2]}  $  long carrier transport lengths,  $  ^{[3,4]}  $  and high defect tolerance.  $  ^{[5]}  $  Compared to silicon solar cells, the long-term stability of PSCs is not satisfying enough, especially when they suffer from the erosion of moisture, oxygen, and light,  $  ^{[6]}  $  which hinder the commercialization of PSCs. To achieve higher PCE and better long-term stability, various approaches have been studied,

Zhang et al. designed imidazolium iodide salts modified with various unsaturated aliphatic chains as additives and studied their physical properties and effect on the performance of the PSCs.  $  ^{[20]}  $   Bai et al. also revealed the influence of different counterions on PSC devices and achieved an impressive long-term stability.  $  ^{[21]}  $

Zwitterions are ideal candidates for interfacial modification materials due to their unique solubility and dipolarity.  $  ^{[22,23]}  $   In addition, the ionic nature of zwitterions and perovskite precursors makes the zwitterions promising additives to passivate the charged defects in PSCs.  $  ^{[24]}  $   In this context, Zheng et al. illustrated that the zwitterionic 3-(decyldimethylammonio)-propanesulfonate could control the crystallization of perovskite films to passivate their defects, leading to a high PCE of 18.3% for blade-coating large-area devices.  $  ^{[25]}  $   Herein, we introduce an imidazolium sulfonate zwitterion 1-(1-ethyl-3-imidazolium)propane-3-sulfonate (EIMS) as additive for FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite solar cells. We demonstrate that the addition of this additive could achieve an improved PCE of 22.1% with, more importantly, an ultralow, negligible hysteresis index of 0.009. After stored in ambient atmosphere with a relative humidity about 15% for 45 days, the devices prepared with EIMS could still retain above 96% of the original PCE. These enhancements have been rationalized to the generation of EIMS-induced porous PbI  $  _{2}  $   film, which improved the crystallization quality of perovskite. # 2. Results and Discussion

The molecular structure of EIMS is shown in Figure 1a. We doped EIMS into the solution of   $  PbI_{2}  $   to deposit the   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films through the two-step sequential spin coating method.  $  ^{[26,27]}  $   Figure 1b–g shows the top surface scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   films with and without the EIMS additive. The addition of EIMS led to the generation of porous   $  PbI_{2}  $   films. Perovskite films prepared from these porous   $  PbI_{2}  $   films were dense. In contrast, without the additive,   $  PbI_{2}  $   films were found to be dense, but pinholes existed in the perovskite films prepared from these   $  PbI_{2}  $   films. These results indicated that the addition of EIMS significantly improved the quality of crystallization of the perovskite films. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns are shown in Figure 1h. The identical peaks at 12.63, 13.92, and 28.07° corresponded to excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   and the (110), (220) planes of the   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite structure, respectively. The peak intensity of   $  PbI_{2}  $   reduced after the addition of EIMS, which suggested that the content of unreacted   $  PbI_{2}  $   was reduced. These results could be attributed to that the FAI solution could more easily diffuse through the film to react with   $  PbI_{2}  $   more completely due to the porosity of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   films prepared with EIMS, which led to the improvement of the quality of the perovskite films.  $  ^{[28,29]}  $

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated a new and efficient additive engineering strategy on FAPbI $ _3 $  PSCs by introducing the imidazolium-based zwitterionic ionic liquids EIMS additive. The additive can substantially promote the quality of the perovskite film by forming porous PbI $ _2 $  films. The additive can also accumulate at the buried interface and accelerate electron extraction, which lead to significant improvement of the current density of the devices and the suppression of the hysteresis nearly completely. Under the optimized EIMS doping concentration of  $ 0.2\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  in PbI $ _2 $  precursor, we can achieve a champion PCE of  $ 22.1\% $  with a higher  $ J_{sc} $  parameter and the hysteresis index can be reduced to as low as 0.009. In the future, we shall design more zwitterionic additives with different ionic groups and chains to achieve increased perovskite performance.

# 4. Experimental Section

Preparation of EIMS: 1,3-Propane sultone (25 g, 0.122 mol) was added dropwise to a solution of 1-ethylimidazole (10 g, 0.104 mol) in acetone (40 mL), stirred, and then cooled on an ice bath overnight. A white precipitate was recovered from the reaction solution through filtration and washing with acetone.  $  ^{[39,40]}  $

Device Fabrication: The film of   $  SnO_{2}  $   electron transport layer, the Ag electrode, and the spiro-OMeTAD hole transport layer was prepared according to the previous literature.  $  ^{[41]}  $   For the interface modification, EIMS was dissolved in the mixture of DMF and DMSO (9:1, v/v). The solution was spin-coated on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   at 4000 rpm. For the perovskite layer, 691.5 mg   $  PbI_{2}  $   was dissolved in 1 mL mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO (9:1, v/v) and then spin-coated on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   at 1500 rpm for 30 s. The   $  PbI_{2}  $   films were formed by annealing at 70 °C in   $  N_{2}  $   glove box. 90 mg FAI, 6.4 mg MAI, and 9 mg MACI were dissolved in 1 mL IPA to prepare the FAI solution. For a dynamic spin coating process, 0.175 mL FAI solution was spin-coated onto   $  PbI_{2}  $   films by dropping at 2000 rpm in 2 s and kept spinning for 30 s. Then, the substrate was transferred from the   $  N_{2}  $   glove box to a humidity-controlled air glove box and annealed at 150 °C for 10 min with 30–40% relative humidity.
