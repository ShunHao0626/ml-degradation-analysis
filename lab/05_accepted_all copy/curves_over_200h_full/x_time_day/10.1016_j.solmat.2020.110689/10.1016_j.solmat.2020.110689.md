# 1. Introduction

Photovoltaic devices based on metal halide perovskite have attracted vast interest due to their excellent photoelectronic properties and the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) increased rapidly from 3.81% to 25.2% since the firstly reported in 2009 [1–6]. Despite the great progress in perovskite solar cells (PSCs), the complicated preparation process [7], instability [8–12], and low materials utilization [13] of materials are the drawbacks which limited PSCs for further application. Currently, the multi-step anti-solvent spin-coating methodology [14] is widely used for fabrication of perovskite films and n-i-p/p-i-n PSCs. This technique is proved to fabricate uniform and pin-hole free perovskite films, however, the areas and materials utilization of PSCs are greatly restricted. To overcome the imperfections, various low cost and large area strategies were developed for the deposition of perovskite film including doctor blading [15], slot-die coating [16], printing [17], and soft-cover deposition [18]. These original stationery deposition techniques ensured the growth of large area perovskite film with high

The major obstacles delaying the commercialization of thin film solar cells based on hybrid organic halide perovskite are the expensive materials, instability of the devices and the complicated multi-step fabrication process. To solve these challenges, we present a simple and high material utilization strategy which employing the polyethyleneimine (PEI) interface abduction to in-situ fabricate perovskite layer and hole transporting layer (HTL) from  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and Spiro-OMeTAD (SPI) mixed precursors in single step. Analyzation find that the in-situ phase separation is induced by the PEI abduction. The existence of PEI blocks SPI form Ti-O bond with bottom  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  surface and converting the interaction between SPI and bottom interface to Van der Waals interaction. Employing this fabrication technique for PSCs, remarkably improves materials utilization of perovskite and SPI, as well as exhibiting superior stability of about  $ 95\% $  initial efficiency even after 68 days of storage in ambient condition without encapsulation. We believe that our strategy will pave a new way for simple, economical and stable PSCs preparation, and provide an inspiration to simplify fabrication process for organic-inorganic electronic devices.

# 2. Experiment section

# 2.1. Materials and preparation

$  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  (99.5%),  $  PbI_{2}  $  (99.5%) and Spiro-OMeTAD (99.5%) purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. The PEI (99%) and Tris(pentafluorophenyl)boron (BCF) were purchased from Aladdin. Titanium isopropoxide (99.999%) was supplied by Sigma-Aldrich. All other solvents and chemicals were obtained from commercial sources and used as received without further purification. The compact  $  TiO_{2}  $  layer precursor solution was same to our previous work [8]. The PEI solution was diluted to 0.4 wt% in 2-methoxyethanol (Sigma-Aldrich, anhydrous, 99.8%). The precursor solution for PSF was prepared by mixing the perovskite solution and Spiro-OMeTAD solution at 1:1 vol ratio, this perovskite solution was prepared by mixing 0.6 mmol  $  PbI_{2}  $  and 0.6 mmol  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  in 500 μL of N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) and stirred at 60 °C for 12 h, the Spiro-OMeTAD solution was prepared by dissolving 15 mg Spiro-OMeTAD in 500 μL of DMF. For the precursor solution of spin-coated perovskite films, it was prepared by mixing 1.2 mmol  $  PbI_{2}  $  and 1.2 mmol  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  in 1 mL of DMF and stirred at 60 °C for 12 h, and the Spiro-OMeTAD solution was prepared by dissolving 72.3 mg Spiro-OMeTAD in chlorobenzene (CB).

# 2.2. Devices fabrication

All component layers were prepared in an   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glove box. The pre-cleaned, FTO-coated glass substrates were treated with UV-ozone 30 min before device fabrication, then the compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   solution was spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 50 s, followed by heating at 150 °C for 15 min, and then the compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   films were gradually heated to 500 °C and baked at this temperature for 30 min. For the PSF and corresponding devices, the PEI solution was firstly spin-coated at 5000 rpm for 30 s on the compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface after the PI film pasted around the   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer margin, then the PE film was covered the substrates (  $  2 \times 2 \, cm^{2}  $  ) after adding 15 μL mixed solution (the   $  4 \times 4 \, cm^{2}  $   substrate using 60 μL mixed solution) on the PEI substrates, and then waiting for 3 min. The next, the substrates were moved to the warm table and heated 25–30 s at 100 °C. After this process, the PE film was removed and put the substrates to the warm table kept 20 min at 100 °C, finally, the PSF was obtained. The surface doping of BCF was prepared by spin-coating the BCF precursors (5 mg/mL in the isopropanol) on the PSF at 3000 rpm. Finally, the Au film was deposited on the Spiro-OMeTAD-coated film performed by the thermal evaporation method with a film thickness gauge (the vacuum degree was below than   $  10^{-4}  $   Kpa and the film thickness was set as 100 nm).

For the spin-coating films and devices, the perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated onto the compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   or the PEI modified   $  TiO_{2}  $   substrates at 3000 rpm for 55 s. During the spin-coating, 100   $  \mu  $  L of CB was dropped at the center of the substrate (  $  2 \times 2 \, cm^{2}  $  ) after 6 s. The spin-coated films were heated at 100 °C for 20 min, resulting in the formation of dark brown perovskite films. Subsequently, Spiro-OMeTAD was deposited on the perovskite layer by spin-coated at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, to obtain the corresponding devices, the Au film was deposited on the PSF film which the condition is same to the standard devices and the active area of each device was   $  0.09 \, cm^{2}  $  .

# 2.3. Characterization

The morphologies of the perovskite and PSF were observed by scanning electron microscope (SEM, JEOL JSM-7600F). The  $ J-V $  curves of the PSCs were measured by using an electrochemical workstation (CHI 660E, Shanghai Chenhua) under AM 1.5G simulated solar light  $ (100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2}) $  (CHF-XM-500W, Trusttech Co. Ltd., Beijing, China). The incident light intensity was calibrated with a standard Si solar cell. The water contact angles were measured by using commercial setup (HHUAVE A23-605L). The elemental distribution of PSF was measured by time of flight-second ion mass spectrometry (TOF.SIMS 5, ION-TOF GmbH, Germany). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) characterizations of the samples were carried out on a spectrometer (Kratos XSAM800). The high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) was measured by using commercial setup (FEI Tecnai G2 F20), and the HRTEM sample of PSF is prepared by spin-coating the mixed precursor on the copper network and then drying on the hot plate, which is consistent with the literature [22].

# 2.4. DFT calculations

All the geometrical optimization of DFT calculations were performed by  $ \mathrm{Dmol}^3 $  module [23,24]. To simplify the calculations, the monomer of PEI polymer and the five monomers of PE polymer were employed to investigate the interactions between the film surface and the ions/molecule in the mixed precursor solution. The Generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) was selected as the exchange correlation function [25,26] to describe exchange and correlation effects and the build-in double numerical plus polarization (DNP) basis set is used to expand the electronic wavefunction. The DFT-D was used to describe the van der Waals interaction. During the geometrical optimization, the tolerances of energy and force were set as  $ 1\times 10^{-5}\mathrm{Ha} $  and  $ 0.002\mathrm{Ha / A} $ , as well as the maximum displacement was  $ 5\times 10^{-3}\mathring{\mathrm{A}} $ , respectively. The convergence threshold in self-consistent field (SCF) electronic calculations is set as  $ 10^{-6} $  a.u on the total energy. For the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  related slabs, the certified stability (110) surface [27] with  $ 25\AA $  vacuum layer was used to establish the ions/molecule adsorbed configurations. The Monkhorst-Pack k-point mesh was  $ 1\times 2\times 1 $  for the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  related slab and  $ 2\times 2\times 2 $  for other models. The binding energy  $ (E_{\mathrm{b}}) $  was calculated as  $ E_{\mathrm{b}} = E_{\mathrm{combine}} - E_{\mathrm{surface}} - E_{\mathrm{molecule/ions}} $ , where the  $ E_{\mathrm{combine}} $ ,  $ E_{\mathrm{surface}} $  and  $ E_{\mathrm{molecule/ions}} $  denote the total energy of surface (PEI, PE,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ ) adsorbed with the ions/molecule  $ (\mathrm{MA^{+}},\mathrm{Pb^{2 + }},\mathrm{I^{-}} $   $ [\mathrm{PbI}_3]^- $  and SPI), total energy of the surface and the relaxed ions/molecule, respectively. The charge density difference was defined as  $ \Delta \rho = \rho_{\mathrm{combine}} - \rho_{\mathrm{surface}} - \rho_{\mathrm{molecule/ions}} $ , where  $ \rho_{\mathrm{combine}} $ ,  $ \rho_{\mathrm{surface}} $  and  $ \rho_{\mathrm{molecule/ions}} $  denote the charge density of the optimized surface (PEI, PE,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ ) adsorbed with the ions/molecule  $ (\mathrm{MA^{+}},\mathrm{Pb^{2 + }},\mathrm{I^{-}} $   $ [\mathrm{PbI}_3]^- $  and SPI), the corresponding surface and the relaxed ions/molecule in respectively.

# 3.2. Mechanism of phase separation

To investigate the mechanism of phase separation phenomenon in perovskite and SPI by the interface induced effect. Firstly, the interface interactions clearly by the acknowledged Good's equation which expressed enthalpy of interface formation  $ \Delta H_{1,2} $  is induced [29,30]:

$$
\Delta H _ {1, 2} = - 2 \left(\sqrt {\gamma_ {1} ^ {d} \times \gamma_ {2} ^ {d}} + \sqrt {\gamma_ {1} ^ {p} \times \gamma_ {2} ^ {p}}\right)
$$

Where the subscripts of 1 and 2 represent different film surfaces and the  $ \gamma $  denote the component of surface energies, the superscript  $ d $  and p respectively represent dispersive part and polar part of the surface

energy (the different surface energies were calculated in Table S3). It clear that the PEI and PER (the PER represents the perovskite) films exhibited similar and high polarities of  $ 38.86\% $  and  $ 33.38\% $  inverse the low polarities of PE  $ (2.95\%) $  and SPI  $ (0.61\%) $ . These surface polarities are the fundamental to explain surface wettability and interfacial formation, and substances with similar polarities always tend to form a robust interface, however, the substances with different polarities tend to segregate [31]. To further comprehend the interface interactions, enthalpies of interface formation between different materials were calculated in Table S4. The  $ \Delta H $  of PER/PEI, PER/PE, SPI/PEI and SPI/PE interface are  $ -88.42\mathrm{mJ}\mathrm{m}^{-2} $ ,  $ -71.43\mathrm{mJ}\mathrm{m}^{-2} $ ,  $ -78.38\mathrm{mJ}\mathrm{m}^{-2} $  and  $ -84.12\mathrm{mJ}\mathrm{m}^{-2} $ . The more negative value of  $ \Delta H $  denotes stronger interface formation and vice versa. Thus, the perovskite should form a sturdy interface with PEI film, rather than the PE film, analogously, the SPI tends to form sturdy interface with PE film.

To explore the mechanism of in-situ phase separation at the molecule level, the density functional theory (DFT) was performed to explore the interactions of precursor components and interface. As shown in Fig. 3a, the   $  MA^{+}  $  ,   $  Pb^{2+}  $  ,   $  I^{-}  $  ,   $  [PbI_{3}]^{-}  $   and SPI are the main components of the mixed precursor; the   $  TiO_{2}  $  , PE and PEI are the relevant contact interfaces. For the bare   $  TiO_{2}  $   interface, the proved stable (110) surface [27] was selected to realize the interface interactions between   $  TiO_{2}  $   and components of mixed precursor (Fig. S6 and S7), as a result, the binding energy (  $  E_{b}  $  ) of   $  MA^{+}  $  ,   $  I^{-}  $  ,   $  Pb^{2+}  $  ,   $  [PbI_{3}]^{-}  $   and SPI on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   (110) surface are -56.97, -76.93, -163.96, -143.26 and -214.23 kcal/mol, respectively (Fig. 3k). It obvious that the SPI on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface shows higher   $  E_{b}  $   than the perovskite components of precursor (the more negative value indicated the higher   $  E_{b}  $  ), which is attributed to the formation of two Ti–O bonds by the O atoms of SPI and the surface Ti atoms (Fig. S6). Contrast to the bottom interface, the top interface of PE also has been investigated (Fig. S8) and the   $  E_{b}  $   of   $  MA^{+}  $  ,   $  I^{-}  $  ,   $  Pb^{2+}  $  ,   $  [PbI_{3}]^{-}  $   and SPI on the PE respectively are -39.62, -4.21, -21.58, -12.35 and -46.35 kcal/mol (Fig. 3l), which are much lower than the interactions of perovskite precursor components on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface due to the intermolecular Van der Waals interaction. It implied the SPI should prefer to aggregate at the surface of PE film than the perovskite components, which consist with the tendency predicted by the theory of enthalpy of interface formation. However, when the mixed precursor within the bottom   $  TiO_{2}  $   and top PE interface, the phase separation with forward direction will be not accomplished due to the SPI shows much higher   $  E_{b}  $  .

# 3.3. Photovoltaic properties

To confirm the photovoltaic properties of the PSF prepared by one step, the PSCs (SPI doped-free) were fabricated. The device based on PSF shows performance parameters with  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  of 0.893 V,  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  of 18.3 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and FF of 0.54, resulting in PCE of 8.83% which is better than the PCE (8.04%) of PSCs fabricated by spin-coating at the same conditions

(Fig. 4a). We also tested the stability of devices under working conditions (Fig. S10), both devices based on different methods exhibit high stability under the working condition and show stabilized PCE of 8.48% for the PSF and 7.85% for the spin-coating method after 650 s light soaking. It is extremely comparable to the PCEs obtained from the J-V characteristics. To realize the reproducibility of PSCs, '20' independent devices based on different methods were statistically analyzed (Fig. S11). The results show that the reproducibility of devices based on spin-coating is higher than it compared to PSF method. However, the PSF exhibited higher mean value of PCE than the spin-coating method. The higher PCE of PSCs based on PSF also in accordance with the faster interface carriers transfer which is verified by the much more quenching of steady-state photoluminescence (PL) at the 770 nm region of PSF based sample (Fig. 4b). # 3.4. Stability of PSCs

The long-term stability of devices without encapsulation in the ambient condition (the temperature is about 25–30 °C and the humidity is about 50–70%) were also studied (Fig. 5a–d). The devices based on PSF (without BCF) showed an excellent stability and retained 95% of the initial PCE even after 68 days. However, devices based on spin-coating retained only 10% after 49 days. Initially, 1D-grazing incidence X-ray diffraction (1D-GIXRD) was employed to measure the degradation effect of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  in both types of devices after 15 days aging (Fig. S12). The samples based on PSF exhibited no additional PbI  $  _{2}  $  peak, however, the spin-coating sample showed obvious additional reflections MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  hydrate (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ·H  $  _{2}  $  O) and PbI  $  _{2}  $  peaks. It indicated the dramatically decomposition of the perovskite and the absorption of water in the devices based on spin-coating, while the PSF can protect the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  and resist moisture effectively. This may be attributed to the hydrophobic SPI (contact angle shown in Fig. S4) mixed with the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  at the contact interface (Fig. 2d). To further realize this excellent stability accurately, 2D grazing incidence X-ray diffraction (2D-GIXRD) was used to in-situ investigate the degeneration effect of the devices based on PSF. Fig. 5e and f reveal the 2D-GIXRD profiles of devices which are fresh and aged 60 days, respectively. There are complete MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  diffraction spots at q ≈ 10, 20, 22.6 nm  $  ^{-1}  $  in the fresh and aged 60 days samples. Astoundingly, the PbI  $  _{2}  $  phase does not appear in the aged samples from Fig. 5f. It is further confirmed that the PSF fabricated by one step can suppress the decomposition of perovskite and protect itself effectively.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, the in-situ co-deposit vertical phase segregated MAPbI $ _3 $ -SPI heterojunction via single step from the mixed precursor was firstly achieved by PEI abduction. The central principle of the phase separation is the interface induced effect by the different surface polarities and interactions of molecules. Analysis reveal that the surface PEI will prevent the SPI forming Ti–O coordination on the TiO $ _2 $  and interact with the Pb $ ^{2+} $  and [PbI $ _3 $ ] $ ^{-} $  at the bottom surface by Pb–N bond, which is the main reason for the in-situ phase separation. Ultimately, we are pleasantly surprised by the fact that the materials utilization of perovskite and SPI are dozens of times than the layer by layer spin-coating process, as well as the specific phase separated interface in perovskite and SPI provide better interface hole transportation ability and outstanding air stability. It paved a promising one step strategy for low cost, stable and efficient PSCs fabrication.
