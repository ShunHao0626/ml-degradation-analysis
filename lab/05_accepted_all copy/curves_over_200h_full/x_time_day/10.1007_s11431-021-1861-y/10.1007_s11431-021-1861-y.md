# Room-temperature sputtered-  $  SnO_{2}  $  modified anode toward efficient  $  TiO_{2}  $ -based planar perovskite solar cells

LI Dan  $  ^{\dagger}  $  , LI Yong  $  ^{\dagger}  $  , LIU ZhiKe, WANG DaPeng  $  ^{*}  $   & LIU ShengZhong (Frank)  $  ^{*}  $

Key Laboratory of Applied Surface and Colloid Chemistry, Ministry of Education, Shaanxi Key Laboratory for Advanced Energy Devices, Shaanxi Engineering Lab for Advanced Energy Technology, School of Materials Science and Engineering, Shaanxi Normal University, Xi'an 710119, China

Received January 12, 2021; accepted May 18, 2021; published online July 22, 2021

It is of the utmost importance to promote the charge transport and retard the heterojunction barrier in the functional layers and at their adjacent interfaces to pursuit excellent photovoltaic characteristics of the perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, room-temperature sputtered- $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  films are introduced to modify FTO anode for improving carrier extraction and aligning energy band in the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -based planar PSCs. For the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ -modified substrate with suitable sputtering duration, the following  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film exhibits smoother surface roughness, less surface defects, and lower the trap-assisted interfacial recombination than that directly on the FTO, further facilitating the growth of perovskite grain size and promoting the extraction efficiency of charge carriers. On the basis of the optimized  $ \mathrm{SnO_2(6nm) / TiO_2} $  stack layer as electron transport layer (ETL), the PSC exhibits an outstanding power conversion efficiency of  $ 21.45\% $ , which is much better than that of the single  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -based device ( $ 19.68\% $ ). More importantly, the cell with the bilayer ETL shows a long-term stability against air, maintaining over  $ 76\% $  of its initial efficiency after  $ 40\mathrm{d} $ . These achievements suggest that this study provides a feasible path to design energy level alignment and low temperature ETL for highly efficient and stable PSCs.

# 1 Introduction

In the past decade, we have witnessed the rapid development of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials, especially in the photovoltaic field, due to their outstanding optoelectronic properties, including great absorption coefficient, long charge transport lifetime, high charge mobility, and low intrinsic trap density [1-6]. A crucial photovoltaic parameter of power conversion efficiency (PCE) in the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has sharply soared from  $ 3.8\% $  to a certified  $ 25.5\% $  during that period [7,8]. In the planar-heterojunction

device, the electron transport layer (ETL) plays a pivotal role in charge extraction and transport, retarding the charge carrier recombination at the interface of transparent electrode and perovskite layer. Thus, the ideal ETLs should possess low intrinsic defect density of state, high conductivity, suitable energy-level, and remarkable electron transport capacity. The magnetron sputtering technology has been approved as one of the most prospective methods to prepare high quality ETLs for low-temperature and large scale PSCs   $  [9–11]  $  . For instance, Qi et al.   $  [12]  $   adopted the magnetron sputtering technology to deposit the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer as ETL for the planar PSC, contributing to a PCE of   $  \sim20.0\%  $   with long-term T80 stability.

ETLs in both of mesoporous and planar PSCs owing to its proper transparency to visible light, simple and diversified preparation process, and structural stability   $  [13–15]  $  . Nevertheless, the low carrier mobility and imbalance energy level alignment with electrode lead to the charge accumulation and recombination at its adjacent interfaces, directly causing PSC efficiency loss and hysteresis deterioration. In recent years,   $  SnO_{2}  $   has gradually become research concern as a promising ETL candidate because of lower fabrication temperature, higher intrinsic mobility, wider band gap, deeper conduction band, and weaker catalytic activity in comparison with those of   $  TiO_{2}  $     $  [16–18]  $  . On the other hand, the single   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL with any quality defects, such as void and pinhole, may result in shunt paths and ETL deficiency between light absorption layer and substrate, inevitably leading to leakage current rise and carrier recombination aggravation. To alleviate the inevitable shortage of   $  TiO_{2}  $   region, an effective approach is to introduce another ETL. You et al.   $  [19]  $   have designed a   $  TiO_{2}/WO_{3}  $   bilayer as ETL to efficiently extract the carrier from perovskite to substrate, giving rise to a PCE of 20.14%. Lin et al.   $  [20]  $   have reported that the ZnO-modified ITO substrate indirectly contributes to the generation of large perovskite grain size, yielding to the high efficiency of 20.45%. Although some positive results have been achieved in bilayer ETLs, there are still some issues and room for improvement. It mainly includes the following two aspects. One is to adjust the appropriate thickness of the introduction layer because the carrier transportation is determined by the mobility and transmission length of the insertion layer   $  [21,22]  $  . Another is that the introduced intercalation matches the energy level of the existing ETL and perovskite layers, which is conducive to the electron transportation and collection.

Herein, the FTO anode modified by a room-temperature sputtered   $  SnO_{2}  $   film is utilized in the   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based planar PSCs. In addition to the appropriate energy level matching for the insertion layer, the carrier transportation distance can be adjusted by optimizing the deposition duration. The bilayer structure is beneficial for the formation of large perovskite grain size and the extraction of charge carriers, thus contributing to the improvement of photovoltaic parameters. The champion PSC exhibits a standout PCE of 21.45% and outstanding air stability over 40 d.

# 2 Experimental section

# 2.1 Fabrication of dual ETLs

The FTO glass substrate is ultrasonically treated according to the procedures of ethanol, acetone, isopropanol, ethanol, and distilled water, and cleaned by air flow gun. For the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2/ $   $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  stack layer, the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  films are fabricated by radio frequency magnetron sputtering system at room temperature.

A 2-inch   $  SnO_{2}  $   (4N) ceramic target is located on a cathode 18 cm away from the substrate. The substrate on an anode is rotated at the speed of 12 r min  $  ^{-1}  $  . During the sputtering process, the deposition conditions including sputtering power of 40 W, operation pressure of 2 Pa, the total gas flow rate of 20 sccm with the   $  Ar/O_{2}  $   ratio of 2:3, and deposition temperature of 30°C, are fixed only to change the deposition duration. Finally, the   $  SnO_{2}  $   films with thicknesses of 2, 6, 10, and 14 nm are obtained, respectively. After 10 min UV-ozone treatment, all samples are immersed into the   $  TiCl_{4}  $   aqueous solution at 70°C for 1 h to form ~50 nm thick   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer based on our previous publication [23]. Consequently, the   $  SnO_{2}/TiO_{2}  $   dual ETLs with various   $  SnO_{2}  $   thicknesses are successfully constructed. Certainly, the single-layer   $  TiO_{2}  $   is also prepared as a control sample.

# 2.2 PSC device fabrication

For the perovskite precursor solution, FAI, MAI, and   $  PbI_{2}  $   (molar ratio, 0.85:0.15:1) are dissolved into 4:1 DMF/DMSO of 1 mL in the glove box at room temperature. The obtained ETL substrates are annealed in air at 200°C about 1 h in air. In a glove box, the perovskite precursor solution is spin-coated onto the ETL substrate with 1000 r min  $  ^{-1}  $   for 10 s and 4000 r min  $  ^{-1}  $   for 45 s and then chlorobenzene is dropped into the precursor solution as an anti-solvent. Through 30 min heat-annealing at 150°C, the dark films are obtained. As for the hole transport layer, spiro-OMeTAD of 90 mg, tert-butylpyridine of 36 μL, and lithium bis(tri-fluoromethylsulphonyl)imide of 22 μL in acetonitrile are dissolved in 1 mL chlorobenzene and the precursor is coated on the perovskite film with 5000 r min  $  ^{-1}  $   for 30 s. Finally, 80 nm thick Au film with the effective area of 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $   is deposited with a thermal evaporation device. As a result, the device with the structure of FTO/without or with SnO  $  _{2}  $  /TiO  $  _{2}  $  /FA  $  _{0.85}  $  MA  $  _{0.15}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $  /spiro-MeOTAD/Au is obtained.

# 2.3 Characterization

The top views of each layer and cross-sectional view of the PSCs are monitored utilizing a SU-8020 SEM (HITACHI). The corresponding surface topographies are surveyed adopting AFM (MULTIMODE 8). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis is carried out by ESCALAB 250Xi system of Thermo Fisher Scientific. The energy level assessment of double ETL is measured by UPS (CALAB 250Xi, Thermo Fisher). The transmittance and absorption spectra are characterized on UV-vis-NIR spectrophotometer (Ocean Optics). Photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL spectra are measured using an Edinburgh FLS980 spectrometer with the excitation wavelength of  $ 510\mathrm{nm} $ . The density-voltage  $ (J - V) $  properties are characterized on a Keithley 400 digital source meter with a standard AM 1.5

# 3 Results and discussion

Figure 1(a)–(e) exhibit the typical AFM images of   $  TiO_{2}  $   film spin-coated on the bare and thickness-varied   $  SnO_{2}  $  -modified FTO substrate. For the   $  TiO_{2}  $   film fabricated on the pristine FTO, the root-mean-square (RMS) roughness is 20.4 nm, suggesting that the rough surface morphology is mainly originated from the substrate. When the sputtered   $  SnO_{2}  $   film irrespective of the thickness is introduced on the FTO, the RMS is significantly improved, indicating that the introduced   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer effectively smooths the substrate roughness. The roughness in all   $  SnO_{2}  $  -inserted samples becomes similar, in a small range of 17.9–18.7 nm. The smoothest RMS of 17.9 nm is achieved for 6 nm thick   $  SnO_{2}  $   film. The enhanced RMS demonstrates that the sputtered-  $  SnO_{2}  $   effectively enhances the surface flatness of FTO substrate. In addition, as exhibited in the insets in Figure 1(a)–(e), the contact angle results further indicate that the smooth bilayer shows the superior hydrophilic character in comparison with that of the single   $  TiO_{2}  $   film, which is propitious to the formation of the compact perovskite layer.

Considering that the insertion  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  film may affect the bulk quality of the stack layer, the devices with the architecture of FTO/without or with  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{TiO}_2 / \mathrm{Au} $  are designed. Figure 2(a) describes the corresponding  $ I - V $  relationship. It is well-known that the  $ I - V $  curves are divided into three regions. The first inflection point voltage between the ohmic and nonlinear response curves is called trap-filled limit voltage ( $ V_{\mathrm{TFL}} $ ). The trap densities of states designed structure are decided by the extracted  $ V_{\mathrm{TFL}} $  [24]. Because  $ V_{\mathrm{TFL}} $  is proportional to the density of defect states, the bilayer ETL has a low defect density when the thickness of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  film is  $ 6\mathrm{nm} $  (Table S1). These results imply that the bulk defects are significantly reduced by the excellent sputtering quality of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  insertion layer accompanied with the enlarged  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  grains. Furthermore, the conductivity  $ \sigma $  can also be obtained from the same structure on the basis of functional relationship of  $ I = \sigma A d^{-1} V $  [25], in which  $ A $  is the sample area. For the double layer structure with  $ 6\mathrm{nm} $  thick  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ ,  $ \sigma $  is about 2 times higher than that of the single  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  case (Table S1). The results suggest that the appropriate bilayer structure is more conducive to the electron transportation for the PSCs. Besides, although  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  has wider optical transmittance than that of  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $ , it has the similar transmittance of over  $ 80\% $  and the same absorbance behavior for the  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  film without or with  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  buffer layer (Figure S2).

To further explore how different substrates influence the morphology of the perovskite film, the SEM measurements are performed (Figure 3). The perovskite films formed on all substrates exhibit good coverage without void and rupture. To study the impact of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  modification layer on the photovoltaic properties of the devices, the typical PSCs without or with  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  insertion layer are designed (Figure S4). It is found that the energy level position of metal oxide semiconductors has certain fluctuation with different preparation technologies. To determine the energy level position of sputtered  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and chemical bath deposition formed  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $ , the ultraviolet photoemission spectroscopy (UPS) measurements for evaluating the corresponding valence band ( $ E_{\mathrm{V}} $ ) and conduction band ( $ E_{\mathrm{C}} $ ) energies are carried out. After the calculation (Figure S5) [27], the  $ E_{\mathrm{C}} $  values of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  are  $ -4.11 $  and  $ -3.93\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. The corresponding energy band distributions of each layer in the PSCs are depicted in Figure 4(a). Obviously, the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  energy level inserted between the anode and  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  ETL smooths the energy level distribution, which is benefit to the transportation and collection for photogenerated carriers.

The curves of the current density versus the measured voltage are depicted (Figure 4(b)) and the extracted photovoltaic parameters are tabulated (Table 1). The PSC with single  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL exhibits  $ 1.06\mathrm{V} $  open-circuit voltage  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ ,  $ 24.95\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  short-circuit density  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ , and  $ 74.1\% $  fill factor FF, yielding a maximum PCE of  $ 19.68\% $ . When the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  modification layer is introduced, the photovoltaic performance gradually improves with the increase in the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  thickness. For the  $ 6\mathrm{nm} $   $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ -based device, the champion PCE is hoisted to  $ 21.45\% $ , which is attributed to the ameliorative performance indexes of  $ 1.07\mathrm{V} $   $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ ,  $ 25.00\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2}J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ , and  $ 79.1\% $  FF, respectively. The measured EQE are expressed in Figure 4(c). The corresponding photocurrent densities are integrated to 23.63 and  $ 24.91\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  for the single ETL and bilayer ETL with  $ 6\mathrm{nm} $   $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  layer, respectively, which are comparable with the calculated  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ . Moreover, the significant change in the FF is closely related to the series  $ (R_{\mathrm{s}}) $  and shunt  $ (R_{\mathrm{sh}}) $  resistances.

To confirm the preparation uniformity of the PSCs, 30 cells with single and double layer ETLs are fabricated. The statistical distributions of each parameter are shown in Figure S6 and Table S3. The single  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL-based devices show the average efficiency of  $ 19.21\% \pm 0.28\% $ , calculating from  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of  $ 1.06\pm 0.01\mathrm{V} $ ,  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  of  $ 24.57\pm 0.35\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , and FF of  $ 74.3\% \pm 0.7\% $ . In contrast, all indicators in the cells with  $ 6\mathrm{nm} $  thick  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 / TiO_2} $  ETLs are enhanced, including  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of  $ 1.07\pm 0.01\mathrm{V} $ ,  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  of  $ 24.98\pm 0.12\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , and FF of  $ 78.1\% \pm 1.0\% $ , contributing to the PCE of  $ 20.94\% \pm 0.37\% $ . Meanwhile, the variations in the hysteresis effect of the devices with monolayer and double layer ETLs are investigated in Figure S7. The respective hysteresis index is evaluated by the equation of  $ |\mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{reverse}} - \mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{forward}}| / \mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{reverse}} $  [6], as listed in Table S4. The pristine PSC is extremely severe of hysteresis with a relatively large index of  $ 12.14\% $ , whereas the  $ 6\mathrm{nm} $  thick  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  modified device possesses a small hysteresis with an index of  $ 5.39\% $ . The hysteresis results indicate that the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  insertion film improves the qualities of FTO/TiO₂ interface and following bulk layers, which is conducive to the extraction of photogenerated electrons and achieves the transportation balance of electrons and holes [28,29]. When the maximum power point in accompany with standard light illumination condition is set up, the stable output behaviors of  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  and efficiency of the devices with the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 / TiO_2} $  layers are shown in Figure 4(d). The control PSC presents a continuous non-oscillatory  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  of  $ 23.95\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  with a stable efficiency of  $ 18.93\% $ , while the  $ 6\mathrm{nm} $   $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  modified device exhibits quick response and finally reaches the steady-state with  $ 24.66\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2}J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  and  $ 20.08\% $  PCE. The light-soaking results suggest that the proper  $ \mathrm{SnO_2 / TiO_2} $  structure promotes efficient and stable operation of the device even if the thickness of the ETL is a little thicker.

To reveal photo-induced electron transfer process in the devices with monolayer and bilayer ETLs, the steady-state PL and TRPL are performed (Figure 5(a) and (b)). The perovskite layer prepared on all substrates shows the iden-

tical PL position at around  $ 800\mathrm{nm} $ . The strong PL emission for the single ETL-based perovskite is gradually quenched as the thickness of inserted  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  layer is thickened. For the  $ 6\mathrm{nm} $   $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  case, the PL intensity is remarkably attenuated. Such low PL emission demonstrates that the suitable bilayer ETL acts as a key factor for improving electron extraction efficiency. However, the bilayer substrate with too thick  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  is to the disadvantage of the transport of photogenerated charge carrier. Moreover, the TRPL measurements are operated to quantitatively analyze charge carrier transfer dynamics. Through biexponential fitting evaluation with two decay times [30], the control perovskite film on the  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  substrate possesses an average decay time ( $ \tau_{\mathrm{ave}} $ ) of 12.1 ns (Table 2), whereas the FTO/6 nm  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{TiO}_2/ $  perovskite sample presents a shorter  $ \tau_{\mathrm{ave}} $  of 9.18 ns, suggesting that the suitable energy level distribution as well as proper transfer length is helpful to separate and extract photogenerated electrons efficiently.

To further probe into the effects of bilayer ETLs on the charge transport and recombination properties, the routine electrical impedance spectra are measured (Figure 5(c)). # 4 Conclusions

By adopting a room-temperature sputtered- $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  film to modify FTO anode, the improved carrier extraction and aligning energy band for the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -based cells are investigated. In the case of the  $ 6\mathrm{nm} $  thick  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ -treated substrate, the insertion layer not only improves the interface quality between the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and FTO layers, but also makes the following  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film smoother surface roughness and reduces the trap-assisted interfacial recombination. The bilayer

structure has the multifunctional effects of improving the   $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface quality, further contributing to the growth of perovskite grain size and promoting the extraction efficiency of charge carriers. Consequently, the PSC with proper   $  SnO_{2}/TiO_{2}  $   ETL gains a high FF of 79.1% and yields a PCE of 21.45%, which is remarkably improved than that of the monolayer   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based cell. Simultaneously, the device with the stack layer ETL exhibits the ambient stability after 40 d. This study provides promising prospects for efficient and stable PSCs with energy level matching and low temperature ETL strategy.
