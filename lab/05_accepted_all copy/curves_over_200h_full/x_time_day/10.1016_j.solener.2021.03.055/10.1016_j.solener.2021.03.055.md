# 1. Introduction

It is urgent to develop clean and renewable energy sources in order to solve the energy crisis and environmental pollution problems. Among various new energy sources, solar energy has drawn intensive attention due to its cleanness, inexhaustibility, and easy conversion to electricity with the aid of solar cells. Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites are ideal light absorbing materials due to their tunable band gaps, large light absorbing coefficient, long and balanced charge carrier diffusion length, easy fabricating process and low fabricating cost. Therefore, perovskite solar cells have become a new start in the thin film solar cells and experienced rapid development in the last decade with power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of monolithic perovskite solar cells skyrocketing from 3.8% in 2009 to 25.5% in 2020 (Kojima et al., 2009; Best Research Cell Efficiency Chart). The perovskite films are most commonly fabricated via one-step anti-solvent method, in which anti-solvent is dropped onto the wet perovskite films during spin-coating to induce fast crystallization of perovskite phase (Xiao et al., 2014; Yang et al., 2019; Rong et al., 2020; Jin et al., 2020). Therefore, the recipe of the perovskite precursors and the choice of anti-solvent play a key role in determining the final morphologies of the perovskite films and the PCEs of the corresponding perovskite solar cells.

Power conversion efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells are greatly influenced by the defects of the perovskite films. To enhance the performance of perovskite solar cells, the defects of perovskite films should be effectively passivated. In this work, we provide an easy and feasible method to passivate the defects of perovskite films using chlorobenzene/perylene mixed anti-solvent. The validity of the defects passivation is confirmed experimentally. Under the optimized concentration, chlorobenzene/perylene mixed anti-solvent can boost the power conversion efficiency of the perovskite solar cells to  $ 19.42\% $ , which is  $ 13.63\% $  higher than the reference solar cells  $ (17.09\%) $ . A champion power conversion efficiency of  $ 20.05\% $  is also demonstrated.

Further improving the qualities of the perovskite films can be achieved by mixing additional additives into the commonly used anti-solvent. For example, phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM), C60 o-quinodimethane bisadducts  $ (\mathrm{C60(QM)_2}) $ , NiPc, P3HT, PFPPY et al. have been selected as additives in chlorobenzene (Zhang et al., 2017; Bi et al., 2016; Wu et al., 2016; Yoon et al., 2017; Li et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2019), methanol has been used as additives in diethyl ether (Yang et al., 2018), 4-tertbutyl-pyridine has been used as additive in ethyl acetate (Arias-Ramos et al., 2020). All these additives have been successfully demonstrated to promote the PCEs of the corresponding perovskite solar cells via one or more of the following effects: (1) modifying the crystallization process of perovskite films and leading to enlarged perovskite crystals (Bi et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2017; Kong et al., 2020), (2) passivate the defect states of the perovskite films (Li et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2019; Jiang et al., 2020). Small organic molecules, which can serve as electron transporting materials or hole transporting materials in the perovskite solar cells, are promising additives among the above mentioned additives. These charge transporting materials can infiltrate into the perovskite grains or grain boundaries during the crystallization of the perovskite film. Thus, these organic molecules can substantially enhance the performances of the perovskite solar cells. Perylene is a widely used small molecule in optoelectronic devices with high hole mobility in the crystalline state. In 2014, T. Miyasaka et al. have demonstrated that perovskite solar cells with vapor deposited perylene hole transporting layer can deliver a high  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  of  $ 1.22\mathrm{V} $  with a minimum voltage loss of  $ 0.33\mathrm{V} $  for the first time (Ishii et al., 2014). Considering its small molecular size, high hole mobility, and suitable HOMO level, perylene might be a potential additive for the commonly used chlorobenzene anti-solvent.

The perylene concentration is optimized, which results in perovskite films with suppressed defect densities. Therefore, perovskite solar cells fabricated using optimized chlorobenzene/perylene mixed anti-solvent demonstrate an average PCE of 19.42%, which is enhanced by 13.63% compared with that of perovskite solar cells fabricated using pure chlorobenzene as anti-solvent. PCE of 20.05% is also demonstrated in our work using the optimized chlorobenzene/perylene anti-solvent.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Materials

Commercial   $  SnO_{2}  $   solution (15% in   $  H_{2}O  $  ) is purchased from Alfa Aesar and diluted with deionized water (1:5 v/v) before use. 461 mg   $  PbI_{2}  $   and 159 mg MAI are weighted and added into a mixture of 900   $  \mu L  $   DMF and 100   $  \mu L  $   DMSO, then stirred at room temperature for 12 h. Perylene is purchased from Energy Chemical. To prepare the chlorobenzene/perylene mixed anti-solvent, perylene is dissolved into chlorobenzene with a concentration of 1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , then this perylene solution is diluted with proper amount of chlorobenzene to prepare perylene solution with concentrations of 2, 4, 6, and 8   $  \mu g  $   mL  $  ^{-1}  $   respectively. For clarity, perovskite films and perovskite solar cells fabricated using pure chlorobenzene and 4   $  \mu g  $   mL  $  ^{-1}  $   chlorobenzene/perylene solution as anti-solvent are referred as P0 and P4 respectively in the following context. Spiro-OMeTAD solution is prepared by dissolving 72.3 mg Spiro-OMeTAD, 17.5   $  \mu L  $   Li-TFSI solution (520 mg Li-TFSI dissolved in 1 mL acetonitrile) and 28.8   $  \mu L  $   tBP in 1 mL chlorobenzene.

# 2.2. Fabrication of the perovskite solar cells

Perovskite solar cells are fabricated according to our previous reports (Cao et al., 2020; Cao et al., 2020). In brief, patterned FTO glass is ultrasonically cleaned using deionized water and ethanol for  $ 15\mathrm{min} $  and

Fig. 1. Schematic fabricating process of the perovskite films using chlorobenzene/perylene mixed anti-solvent.

dried in an oven at 70 °C before use. Ultraviolet-ozone treatment for 15 min is used to enhance the wettability of the FTO glass. SnO₂ electron transporting layer is prepared by spin-coating diluted commercial SnO₂ paste onto the FTO glass at 4000 rpm for 30 s, annealed at 180 °C for 30 min, and then ultraviolet-ozone treated for 15 min. Schematic process to prepare the perovskite films is depicted in Fig. 1. Perovskite precursor solution is spin-coated onto the SnO₂ film at 1000 rpm for 10 s, and then 4000 rpm for 30 s in an Ar filled glovebox. When spinning at 4000 rpm for 6 s, 150 μL chlorobenzene or chlorobenzene/perylene mixed solution is dropped onto the wet perovskite film to induce fast crystallization of the perovskite phase. The obtained perovskite film is further dried at 100 °C for 10 min. Hole transporting layer is prepared by spin-coating Spiro-OMeTAD solution onto the perovskite film at 3000 rpm for 30 s, and then kept at a desiccator overnight. Metal electrode is prepared by thermally evaporating Ag granules at 5 × 10⁻⁴ Pa.

# 2.3. Characterizations

XRD of the perovskite films are measured using SmartLab 9 kW, Rigaku with Cu kα radiation. UV–vis absorbance spectra of the chlorobenzene/perylene solution and perovskite films are measured using Lambda 950 spectrometer. Planar SEM images of the perovskite films are observed using Hitachi S4800. Current density–voltage curves of the perovskite solar cells are tested using Keithley 2450 sourcemeter in the ambient atmosphere. The standard AM 1.5 simulated solar light is provided by XEF-300 solar simulator, which is calibrated using a standard silicon cell. The effective area of our solar cells is 0.04 cm². IPCE spectra of our perovskite solar cells are measured using QE-R system from Enli Technology Co., Ltd. Long-term stability of the perovskite solar cell are tested every two days, during the testing interval, perovskite solar cells are stored in a desiccator. Hole only devices are fabricated with the device structure FTO/PEDOT:PSS/MAPbI₃/SpiroOMeTAD/Ag, current–voltage curves of the hole only devices are tested in the dark using a Keithley 2450 sourcemeter. Time-resolved photoluminescence spectrum is measured using an Edinburgh Instrument (FLS980) with exciting at 480 nm and probing at 760 nm.

Therefore, the mixed chlorobenzene/perylene anti-solvent can increase the hydrophobicity of the perovskite films, which contributes to the enhanced long-term stability of the corresponding perovskite solar cells.

The performances of perovskite solar cells are closely related to the defects of the perovskite films. To characterize the defects related charge recombination of the perovskite film using chlorobenzene/perylene mixed anti-solvent, J-V curves of perovskite solar cell at different light

intensities from 20 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  to 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  are measured. According to references, relationship between  $  V_{oc}  $  and natural logarithm of light intensity can be expressed with a linearly fitting line, while the slope of this line is  $  nk_{B}T/q  $ , where n,  $  k_{B}  $ , T, and q are ideality factor, Boltzmann constant, absolute temperature, and elementary charge, respectively. The lower ideality factor value for the P4 perovskite solar cell indicates that less trap assisted recombination in the corresponding solar cells (Yang et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2019). Therefore, we can safely conclude that the chlorobenzene/perylene mixed anti-solvent can effectively passivate the defect of the perovskite films. To quantitatively characterize the defect density of the perovskite films, hole-only devices with the structure FTO/PEDOT:PSS/MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ /Spiro-OMeTAD/Ag are fabricated. # 4. Conclusion

In summary, perylene has been studied as a potential additive for the commonly used chlorobenzene anti-solvent. Variations of photovoltaic parameters of perovskite solar cells with perylene concentrations are studies. The optimized concentration of perylene is found to be  $ 4\mu \mathrm{g} $   $ \mathrm{mL}^{-1} $ . Under this optimized concentration, perovskite solar cells have demonstrated an average power conversion efficiency of  $ 19.42\% $ , which is enhanced by  $ 13.63\% $  compared with perovskite solar cells using pure chlorobenzene as anti-solvent. Champion power conversion efficiency of  $ 20.05\% $  has also been demonstrated. Variation of open-circuit voltage with light intensity and quantitative trap density calculation from the hole-only devices further confirm that the defect states of the perovskite films can be effectively passivated using the chlorobenzene/perylene mixed anti-solvent. Therefore, our study provides an easy and feasible method to passivate the defects of the perovskite films and enhance the performances of the corresponding perovskite solar cells.
