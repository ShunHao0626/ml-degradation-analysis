# 1. Introduction

With the surge in the demand of energy rising day by day, the need of clean energy resources like wind, sunlight, waves, geothermal and rain has become manifold (Singer et al., 2017; Jacobson et al., 2017). Solar cells are considered to be an important candidate for the renewable energy devices, which involved a great amount of scientific effort and money leading to a rapid progress day by day (Mussard, 2017; Manju and Sagar, 2017; Sampaio and González, 2017). Organo-lead halide based perovskite solar cells (PSC) have been a point of attraction among the scientific community due to its high power conversion efficiencies (PCE) of  $ 23.3\% $  achieved in a span of less than a decade (Yang et al., 2017; Correa-Baena et al., 2017; http://www.nrel.gov, 2016, 2018). Even though the efficiencies have surged closer to the commercially available silicon solar cells, the stability still remains an unsolved problem (Kim et al., 2017; Hwang et al., 2015; You et al., 2016; Dong et al., 2015).

# ABSTRACT

In this work, it is reported for the first time the synthesis of high-purity Lead Sulfide (PbS) quantum dot (QD) using the nano-emulsion method assisted by an ultrasonic bath, which was tested as a hole transporting material (HTM) in perovskite devices. Further, these PbS QDs were introduced in  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  based perovskite device, between the perovskite layer and the HTM to enhance its stability against humidity. The hydrophobic nature of the PbS protects the perovskite by preventing the diffusion of water into the device. To compare the effect, the perovskite devices with and without the layer of PbS QDs were tested under high humidity conditions ( $ \sim 70\% $ ) without encapsulation. The pristine perovskite devices completely degraded after a span of 10 days, whereas the devices with PbS QDs showed huge resistance to humidity by retaining  $ 76\% $  of its initial efficiency after a span of 60 days. Such enhanced stability under adverse environmental conditions has never been reported. Also, these results would open up a new insight of PbS QDs being utilized as a barrier against high humid conditions for making air-stable devices.

In addition, several inorganic compounds like   $  MoO_{x}  $  , Cr, CuS,   $  MoS_{x}  $   and functionalized graphene nanoplatelets have been utilized for improving the stability in ambient conditions (Kim et al., 2017; Kaltenbrunner et al., 2015; Capasso et al., 2016; Sanehira et al., 2016; Kim et al., 2017). However, most of stability studies were carried out under normal environmental conditions or after encapsulation. Recently, the stability study under high humidity condition using fluorine functionalized graphene showed more than 80% degradation in a span of 30 days (Kim et al., 2017).

Lead sulfide (PbS) quantum dots are one of the extensively studied direct bandgap semiconductor material, owing to a large exciton Bohr radius of 18 nm (Basit et al., 2017). Hence, a wide range of bandgap can be obtained just by varying the particle size of the PbS nanocrystals (Moreels et al., 2009; Brown et al., 2014). Yi et al., demonstrated the use of PbS QDs as a hole transporting material in perovskite solar cells (PSCs) achieving a maximum efficiency of 7.88% (Li et al., 2015). It also has been utilized as a light harvester in PbS solar cells achieving a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 8.55% (Chuang et al., 2014). In addition, there have been several reports of the hydrophobic nature of PbS quantum dots (Lin et al., 2008; Yang et al., 2017; Sadovnikov et al., 2018). However, this hydrophobic nature has never been tested in perovskite based solar cells.

In this work, for the first time, we report the synthesis of PbS QDs using nano-emulsion method assisted by an ultrasonic bath. Nanoemulsions are formed by the application of high shear force like an ultrasound or high pressure homogenizer in the presence of a surfactant (Mason et al., 2006; Landfester, 2001). Some of inorganic nanoparticles that have been synthesized by this method includes ZnO, porous nanoparticles of TiO₂, SiO₂ CeO₂, metallic Ag nanoparticles, and Cd(1-x)ZnₓS QDs (Dolcet et al., 2013; Muñoz-Espí et al., 2012; Rosiles-Perez et al., 2017). Considering the point of large scale synthesis, nanoemulsions are an economical method for the production of QDs, as there is no high temperature involved as in the case of hot injection or thermal decomposition techniques (Mason et al., 2006; Landfester, 2001; McClements, 2012). High purity PbS QDs with an average size of ~5.4 nm having a cubic crystalline structure was obtained. The synthesized QDs were tested as a hole transporting layer (HTL) in

perovskite devices and further incorporated between the perovskite and HTL (spiro-MeOTAD) considering the hydrophobic nature of PbS, to prevent the ingress of water attaining high stability perovskite devices. A long term shelf-life stability of the perovskite device was studied under adverse environmental conditions without encapsulation. The devices were observed to retain 76% of initial efficiency after a span of 60 days with a humidity of ~70%, a dramatic improvement when compared to the reference device which has never been reported.

# 2. Results and discussion

# 2.1. Synthesis and characterization of PbS quantum dots

PbS QDs were synthesized by a nanoemulsion method assisted by an ultrasonic bath. The detailed schematic diagram involved in the synthesis process is depicted in Fig. S1. Two nanoemulsions were prepared, one with the lead precursor and the other with the sulfur precursor having the same aqueous volume/organic volume ratio (v/v = 0.2). The organic solution was prepared with 6 wt% of sodium bis(2-ethylhexyl)sulfosuccinate (AOT) in toluene. For the lead and sulfur precursor, 0.1 M of   $  \mathrm{Pb(NO_{3})_{2}}  $   and 0.1 M of   $  Na_{2}S  $   were dissolved in deionized water and then mixed with the organic solution of AOT. Both nanoemulsions were put in the ultrasonic bath for 15 min to provide the shearing force. Then, the sulfide nanoemulsion was added to the lead nanoemulsion followed by 15 min of sonication resulting in the formation of PbS quantum dots.

Nanoemulsion with the PbS quantum dots was removed from the ultrasonic bath and the sample were stored at room temperature for 24 h, during which, a phase separation occurs. The upper phase was a clear black organic solution (light, organic phase) and the lower phase was an opaque black aqueous suspension (heavy, aqueous phase). Each phase was separated manually with a micropipette, and finally the organic phase was placed in an oven at 50 °C for 24 h in order to evaporate the solvent and collect the solid material, which is the PbS QDs. Synthesized QDs were characterized for the morphology and optical characteristics.

The absorption spectrum of the synthesized PbS QDs recorded from 400 to  $ 1350\mathrm{nm} $  is shown in Fig. 1a. The bandgap was calculated using the tauc plot and was found to be  $ 1.14\mathrm{eV} $  (see the inset, Fig. 1a). The

# 2.3. Device performance and characterization

Initially, a perovskite device with PbS as the HTL was fabricated (as presented in the experimental section of supplementary information) to evaluate its photovoltaic performance. Fig. S7(a) depicts the current-voltage (J-V) curve of the perovskite device (glass/FTO/bl-TiO $ _2 $ /meso-TiO $ _2 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /PbS/Au) in forward and reverse scan. A power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 5.83% (in forward scan) corresponding to a current density (J $ _{sc} $ ) of 14.74 mA/cm $ ^2 $ , open-circuit voltage (V $ _{oc} $ ) of 0.88 V and fill factor (FF) of 45% was observed. While a PCE of 4.36% corresponding to a J $ _{sc} $  of 14.50 mA/cm $ ^2 $ , V $ _{oc} $  of 0.86 V and FF of 35% was obtained in the reverse scan. It indicates the hole transporting nature of the PbS QDs which is related to an appropriate valence band alignment of the perovskite with the QDs. The IPCE spectra of the perovskite device with PbS as the HTL was recorded and shown in Fig. S7(b). As observed, there was no extension in the IPCE curve of CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /PbS based solar cell in the NIR region, due to its negligibly

small absorbance as verified by the absorption spectra (see Fig. S6(b)). However, the absorption spectra shown in Fig. S6(b) indicates an increased absorption in the visible region with the addition of PbS QDs, but the IPCE measurements shows that the external quantum efficiency is having a reduced magnitude when compared to the bare perovskite (see Figs. S7(b) and 4) along the visible region. It is an indication that more electrons are photogenerated but lesser electrons are extracted from the cells. This could suggest that the entire electrons photo generated in the PbS QDS are not injected to the perovskite due to an inappropriate conduction band alignment. The integrated  $ \mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{sc}} $  was calculated from the obtained IPCE curve and was found to be  $ \sim 14.50\mathrm{mA}/ $   $ \mathrm{cm}^2 $ , closely matching with the  $ \mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{sc}} $  value extracted from the J-V curve.

In order to investigate the effect of PbS QD layer thickness on the device performance, the perovskite device with various concentration of colloidal PbS solution, introduced between the perovskite and the spiro-MeOTAD were fabricated and the photovoltaic parameters were characterized. Fig. 4a and b depicts the cross-sectional SEM image of the fabricated devices. The perovskite device under study was composed of Glass/FTO/bl/meso-  $  TiO_{2}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/PbS  $  QDs/spiro-MeOTAD/Au as shown in Fig. 4b. The reference cell with Glass/FTO/bl/meso-  $  TiO_{2}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/spiro-MeOTAD/Au  $  was also prepared under the same conditions and is shown in Fig. 4a. The optimized perovskite device comprises of 50 nm thick compact  $  TiO_{2}  $  layer, 150 nm thick mesoporous  $  TiO_{2}  $  layer, 250 nm thick perovskite capping layer, 200 nm thick spiro-MeOTAD layer, 100 nm PbS layer and 60 nm Au electrode. Fig. 4c depicts the J-V curves of the best performing cells with and without the layer of PbS QDs measured under 100 mW/cm $  ^{2}  $  at AM1.5G irradiation. The detailed photovoltaic parameters are listed in Table S1. A box-plot of the PCE distributions is shown in Fig. S8. # 3. Conclusions

In summary, we for the first time successfully demonstrated the development of highly stable mesoscopic perovskite device using PbS QDs as a protective layer. These high purity PbS QDs with an average

crystallite size of 5.4 nm were synthesized for the first time by the nanoemulsion method assisted by an ultrasonic bath. It was further tested as a HTL for the perovskite device achieving a maximum efficiency of 5.83%. Also when introduced as a protective layer, the chemically inert and hydrophobic nature of the PbS layer prevented the degradation of the perovskite film beneath. With the presence of PbS QDs, after 60 days of exposure to ambient humidity of ~70% at room temperature, the perovskite devices were able to maintain 76% of the initial PCE. However, the pristine perovskite devices were observed to degrade completely after 10 days. The stability test results indicate the water repellant nature of the PbS coated perovskite films compared to the pristine perovskite films. This work paves the path toward resolving the main problem in mesoscopic perovskite devices in ambient conditions.
