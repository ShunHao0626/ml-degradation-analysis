Carbon-based hole transport material (HTM)-free   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite solar cells (C-PSCs) have garnered considerable attention due to their super thermal stability. The energy-level mismatch between the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite and carbon electrodes, however, results in major energy loss and reduced power conversion efficiency (PCE) of C-PSCs. Herein, 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium tetrafluoroborate (  $  BMIMBF_{4}  $  ) is used to manage the energy level, reduce defect densities, and improve the interface quality between   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and carbon electrodes. Preliminary results demonstrate that the   $  BMIMBF_{4}  $   modifier can passivate the surface defects of the perovskite film and reduce the energy-level mismatch between the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   layer and the carbon electrode. A PCE of 14.03% is achieved by introducing   $  BMIMBF_{4}  $   which is improved by 23% compared with the control device (11.37%). Moreover, stability—whether in the case of C-PSCs or   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films—is also improved.

Organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have gained popularity in recent years because of their excellent photoelectric properties, including a 25.5% improvement in power conversion efficiency (PCE).  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   Volatile organic components such as methylammonium (MA  $  ^{+}  $  ) and formamidinium (FA  $  ^{+}  $  ), however, render PSCs unstable in humidity and heat, which impedes widespread commercial deployment.  $  ^{[4-6]}  $   Studies indicate that

structural stability, and increase the Goldschmidt tolerance factor, as well as the octahedral factor.  $  ^{[14]}  $   For example, these two factors are higher for   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   than   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   (0.814 and 0.62, respectively).  $  ^{[15]}  $   Although   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   has relatively robust stability, the large bandgap (2.23 eV) limits its performance; to date, the highest recorded PCE is around 10%.  $  ^{[16-18]}  $   Considering the balance of phase stability and light absorption,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   may be considered to be a promising alternative. Many strategies have been used for increasing the performance of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cells such as surface passivation,  $  ^{[19-21]}  $   composition engineering,  $  ^{[22,23]}  $   and interface modification.  $  ^{[24-26]}  $   To date, the highest efficiency of a   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cell with a metal electrode is in excess 18%.  $  ^{[27]}  $

Although the efficiency of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs has improved rapidly, the stability of a   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   device may be reduced by the use of organic hole transport material (HTM) and evaporated noble metal silver (Ag) electrode.  $  ^{[28-31]}  $   To solve these problems, PSCs based on carbon electrodes and HTM-free structures have been developed, which demonstrate particular advantages, such as low cost and simple means of fabrication. The PCE of carbon-based solar cells, however, is lower than that of its counterparts due to the energy-level mismatch between the valence band maximum (VBM) of perovskite and work function (WF) of the carbon electrode.  $  ^{[32,33]}  $   In this regard, managing energy levels between the perovskite and carbon layers is a promising strategy for enhanced PCE.

We found that the sulfur atom of the target molecule can interact with Pb, dramatically decreasing the trap density of the perovskite film and suppressing the charge carrier recombination.  $  ^{[35]}  $   Ionic liquid (ILs) is another potential passivation material for perovskite films because of its low toxicity, good ionic conductivity, and high thermal stability.  $  ^{[36]}  $   Liang's group applied 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium tetrafluoroborate (BMIMBF  $  _{4}  $  ) for passivating the perovskite surface, which dramatically enlarged the grain size of the perovskite film, resulting in improved stability and an enhancement of the PCE from 19.1% in control device to 20.4% in the IL device.  $  ^{[37]}  $   Recently, various ILs were applied in PSCs such as 1,3-dimethyl-3-imidazolium hexafluorophosphate (DMIMPF  $  _{6}  $  ),  $  ^{[38]}  $   1-viny-3-propionate ethyl imidazolium chloride ([PEVIM]Cl),  $  ^{[39]}  $   1-butyl-2,3-dimethylimidazolium chloride ([BMMIm]Cl),  $  ^{[40]}  $   and imidazolium iodide (ImI).  $  ^{[41]}  $   These show significant scope for reducing recombination and improving the performance of PSCs.

In this study, a simple strategy of applying 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium tetrafluoroborate (BMIMBF $ _4 $ )-modified perovskite surface in CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs (C-PSCs) was used. The results demonstrate that high-quality perovskite film and efficient charge collection and transport ability were achieved. Furthermore, the energy-level mismatch was reduced at the interface between CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film and carbon electrode, resulting in lower energy loss. The prepared C-PSCs with ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /CsPbI $ _2 $ Br/BMIMBF $ _4 $ /carbon architecture exhibited the highest PCE of 14.03% with an open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{OC} $ ) of 1.27 V, a photocurrent density ( $ J_{SC} $ ) of 14.68 MA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and a fill factor (FF) of 0.75. The best performance of the control device exhibited a PCE of 11.39%, with a  $ V_{OC} $  of 1.14 V, a  $ J_{SC} $  of 14.33 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and an FF of 0.69. Moreover, both CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film and device stability were improved by BMIMBF $ _4 $  introduction. In addition, the target device maintained 58% of its initial performance during the 68-day storage period in air at 80 °C, whereas the control device dropped to nearly zero.

# 4. Experiment Section

Materials: Lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ , 98%), cesium bromide (CsBr, 99%), and 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium tetrafluoroborate (BMIMBF $ _4 $ , 98%) were purchased from TCI. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and  $ N,N $ -dimethylformamide (DMF) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. SnO $ _2 $  colloid (tin (IV) oxide) precursor was purchased from Alfa Aesar. SnCl $ _2 $  2H $ _2 $ O (99.9%), isopropanol, and ethanol (99.5%) were purchased from Wako. The ITO-coated glass was purchased from Yingkou OPV Tech Co., Ltd. The conductive carbon paste was purchased from Ningbo Borun New Material Technology Co., Ltd.

Device Fabrication: The 1.4 M CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite precursor was prepared by dissolving CsBr and PbI $ _2 $  at the molar ratio of 1:1 in DMSO and DMF with v/v of 7:3. The ITO-coated glass substrates were cleaned with ethanol, acetone, isopropanol, and ethanol by ultrasonication for 15 min and then dried in an oven, followed by treating with UV ozone for 20 min. The SnO $ _2 $  colloid precursor (Alfa Aesar, 15% in H $ _2 $ O colloidal dispersion) was diluted by H $ _2 $ O to 2.67% and was spin coated onto ITO/glass substrates at 3000 rpm for 30 s and then baked on a hot plate in ambient air at 150 °C for 30 min. After being treated with UV ozone for 20 min, 0.42 M SnCl $ _2 $  2H $ _2 $ O ethanol solution was deposited on the

$  SnO_{2}  $  nanoparticle (NP) film and baked at 100°C for 10 min and 180°C for 1 h.

Subsequently, in the nitrogen-filled glove box, the perovskite precursor solution was deposited on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer via a spin-coating program at 1000 rpm for 10 s and then 3000 rpm for 30 s. After the spin-coating process, the obtained films were placed on a hot plate at 40 °C for 5 min and then annealed at 160 °C for 10 min. As for the devices treated with ILs, BMIMBF  $  _{4}  $   in IPA with different concentrations was spin coated onto CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br films at 4000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at 70 °C for 10 min.

The carbon paste was deposited on top of the device by doctor-blading technology. Then, the devices were heated at   $  80^{\circ}  $  C for 30 min on a hot plate in ambient conditions to evaporate the residual solvents.

Characterization: XRD patterns were recorded using a Rigaku Co. Ltd. (Tokyo, Japan) X-ray diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda = 1.54056  $   Å). The morphology of the absorber layers was captured by field-emission SEM (FESEM) (JSM-6701, JEOL). XPS analysis was conducted by Shimadzu Kratos AXIS-NOVA spectrometer using monochromatic Al Kα (1486.6 eV) as the radiation source. The XPS spectra were calibrated by the binding energy of 284.8 eV for C 1s. The UV–vis absorption spectra were recorded using a UV/vis–NIR spectrophotometer with a wavelength ranging from 300 to 900 nm at room temperature (V-670, JASCO Co. Ltd., USA). Steady-state PL measurements (HORIBA scientific, USA) were obtained at room temperature using a Fluorlog-3-2 UltraFast fluorescence spectrometer (HORIBA scientific, USA) with an excitation wavelength of 420 nm. The TRPL spectra were obtained on a DeltaFlex fluorescence spectrometer (HORIBA scientific, USA) using a time-dependent single-photon counting method. Photovoltaic performances were measured
