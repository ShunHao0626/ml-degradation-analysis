# GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT

# ABSTRACT

In inverted planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs), fullerene   $  \left(\mathrm{C}_{60}\right)  $   and its derivatives are widely utilized as electron-transport layer (ETL) materials, however, possess some drawbacks such as low electron mobility, interfacial degradation, and relatively inferior stability. In this work, we develop a facile thermal annealing-free hydrothermal route to synthesize high-crystalline   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanocrystals through controlling the crystallization process with   $  HClO_{4}  $  . An interlayer of   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanocrystals is inserted between   $  C_{60}  $   ETL and Ag electrode. The   $  SnO_{2}  $   interlayer can not only serve as an ETL to help improve electron extraction and transport but also act as a passivation/barrier layer to prevent oxygen and moisture ingress and ion migration. Based on the p-i-n structure of fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/  $  NiO_{x}/MAPbI_{3}/C_{60}/SnO_{2}/Ag  $  , the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the champion device is up to 16.36% with almost negligible hysteresis. The unencapsulated inverted PSCs retain more than 80% of its initial PCE value after storage in atmosphere for 90 days (>2100 h), and degrade only about 20% its initial efficiency after 41 h under heating at 85 °C. More important, all the preparation processes are performed under ambient conditions. Our work provides a viable strategy for assembling highly efficient and stable inverted PSCs in the air.

# 1. Introduction

Today, great progress has been made in perovskite solar cells (PSCs) as promising photovoltaic devices   $  [1,2]  $  . It was previously established that high-performance PSCs could only be achieved in well-controlled glove box because of the sensitiveness of perovskite materials to water and oxygen. However, our and other groups have recently successfully prepared efficient air-processed PSCs   $  [3–12]  $  . This ambient condition has potential attractiveness for large-scale PSCs production and industrialization. Despite the success in fabricating fully air-processed PSCs, poor reproducibility, severe hysteresis, and relatively low performance still are the main issues.

Compared to the n-i-p structure PSCs, the inverted p-i-n structure PSCs have gained special attention because they exhibit better stability and less hysteresis   $  [13]  $  . Up to now, the highest efficiency of inverted planar PSCs has achieved more than 20%   $  [14]  $  . In the inverted planar PSCs, poly (3, 4-ethylenedioxythiophene): poly (styrenesulfonic acid) (PEDOT: PSS) is the most widely used as hole-transport layer (HTL). However, PEDOT: PSS as an organic semiconductor has a significant adverse effect on the long-term stability of PSCs due to its acidic and hygroscopic nature   $  [15]  $  . Thus, various inorganic semiconductor, such as CuSCN   $  [16]  $  ,   $  Cu_{2}O  $     $  [17]  $  ,   $  CoO_{x}  $     $  [18]  $  ,   $  V_{2}O_{x}  $     $  [19]  $  , and   $  WO_{3}  $     $  [20]  $   have been introduced to replace PEDOT: PSS. Especially,   $  NiO_{x}  $   has been chosen as a promising HTL because of its high air stability and hole mobility   $  [21]  $  . On the other hand, the top electron-transport layer (ETL) on the perovskite film has an even more influence on device performance   $  [22,23]  $  . The self-packaging property of ETL can prevent oxygen and moisture ingress in ambient conditions, and block the escape of species from perovskite under operation conditions   $  [24–27]  $  . In most efficient inverted PSCs   $  [6,6]  $  , -phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) is used as ETL   $  [16,28–30]  $  .

Recent reported works have demonstrated that inserting suitable interlayer between ETL and the top metal electrode interface will obtain high device performance. This interface modification can reduce interfacial energy barriers, and thus eliminate the trap density and nonradiative recombination [31,32]. Recently,  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  nanocrystals have been successfully used as electron-transporting and/or interface-modification materials in PSCs due to their high conductivity, wide optical bandgap, and excellent chemical stability [33-36]. For example, Zhu et al. exploited the high crystallinity  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  nanocrystals and applied it to the inverted PSCs. The champion device can yield a high power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 18.8\% $  [37]. Wang et al. demonstrated that the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  nanocrystals can form a smooth and compact double-layer ETL with PCBM, which further to improve the performance of the device, reaching PCE of  $ 19.7\% $  [38]. Indeed, most of these  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  materials need high-temperature or low-temperature annealing treatment to obtain the ideal crystallization for high quality of ETLs, which increase production cost and is limited in flexible devices and inverted PSCs [34,37,39].

Herein, we developed a facile thermal annealing-free hydrothermal method to fabricate   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanocrystals and constructed   $  SnO_{2}/C_{60}  $   bilayer electron-transport materials for efficient and stable inverted PSCs. The   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanocrystals were first prepared by a low-temperature (90 °C) hydrothermal process. Then, the bilayer-structured   $  SnO_{2}/C_{60}  $   ETL was constructed through spin coating of the as-synthesized   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanocrystals onto   $  C_{60}  $ . The   $  SnO_{2}  $   interlayer can act as a passivation/barrier layer as well as an ETL. Compared with its derivatives,   $  C_{60}  $   has the advantages of low-cost and high-throughput [40]. As a result, the inverted PSC based on such   $  C_{60}/SnO_{2}  $   ETL with the structure of FTO/  $  NiO_{x}/MAPbI_{3}/C_{60}/SnO_{2}/Ag  $   yielded a champion PCE of 16.36%, a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 21.61 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.08 V, a fill factor (FF) of 70.10%, and showed low hysteresis effect. Furthermore, the long-term ambient and thermal stability were also significantly improved. It is worth noting that all of the assembly processes of PSCs are done in the atmosphere.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Reagents and materials

Perchloric acid (HClO $ _4 $ , 70%) and hydrochloric acid (HCl, 38%) were purchased from HuShi. Carbamide (CO(NH $ _2 $ ) $ _2 $ ) was purchased from Tianjin Tianli Chemical Reagent Corp. Tin(IV) chloride pentahydrate (SnCl $ _4 $  $ \cdot $ 5H $ _2 $ O) was obtained from Macklin. Nickelous acetate (Ni (Ac) $ _2 $  $ \cdot $ 4H $ _2 $ O) and 2,2,2-Trifluoroethanol (TFEA) were purchased from Aladdin. Lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ ) and methyl ammonium iodide (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Other anhydrous solvent was purchased from J&K Chemical. Where all reagents were used as received without any further purification.

# 2.2. Preparation of  $ \mathsf{SnO}_2 $  nanocrystals

$ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  nanocrystals which treated by HCl were prepared according to a previously reported procedure [41].  $ \mathrm{SnCl_4\cdot 5H_2O} $  (0.1 g),  $ \mathrm{CO(NH_2)_2} $  (1 g) and fuming HCl (2 mL) were added into deionized water (40 mL) with mild stirring, then the pH value of the formed transparent aqueous solution was determined. For  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  nanocrystals which treated by  $ \mathrm{HClO_4} $ , other steps were same, using  $ \mathrm{HClO_4} $  instead of HCl to adjust the pH of the solution to achieve the same pH value. Two kinds of solution were transferred into Telflon-lined stainless autoclave (50 mL) and maintained at  $ 90^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 24 h, respectively. The obtained  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  nanocrystals were collected by centrifugation at  $ 6000~\mathrm{rpm} $ . These products were washed with ethyl alcohol and deionized water for three times to remove residual precursors, and then freeze-dried overnight. Finally, the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  nanocrystals were dispersed into a solution of TFEA to form the 20 mg/mL  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  nanocrystals solution. The  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  nanocrystals solution was filtered with  $ 0.45\mathrm{mm} $  polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) filter.

# 2.3. Preparation of  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  precursor solution

$ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  precursor solution was prepared according to a previously reported procedure [42]. First,  $ 0.1\mathrm{mmol} $   $ \mathrm{Ni(Ac)_2\cdot 4H_2O} $  and ethanolamine  $ (\mathrm{NH}_2(\mathrm{CH}_2)_2\mathrm{OH}) $  were dissolved in  $ 1\mathrm{mL} $  of isopropanol. The mole ratio of  $ \mathrm{Ni}^{2 + } / \mathrm{EA} $  was maintained at 1:1. The solution was stirred under air at  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  overnight, and deep green solution was obtained.

# 2.4. Perovskite solar cells fabrication

The glass/FTO was cleaned with soap water, deionized water, isopropyl alcohol, acetone, ethanol and UV-ozone treatment to obtain a clean surface. The   $  NiO_{x}  $   film was deposited by spin casting the precursor solution at 1500 rpm for 30 s. Then, the   $  NiO_{x}  $   film was annealed at 300 °C for 1 h in the air. To prepared perovskite precursor solution, 484 mg   $  PbI_{2}  $   and 159 mg   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   were added into 1 mL mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO (4:1v/v) and stirring for 30 min. The perovskite precursor solution was coated onto the FTO/  $  NiO_{x}  $   substrates with speed of 1000 rpm for 5 s and 4000 rpm for 60 s. Ethyl acetate (200 μL) was dropped on the spinning substrate at 15 s from the start of the procedure. The substrates were annealed on a hot plate at 85 °C for 10 min and 100 °C for 15 min. The   $  C_{60}  $   solution was coated onto the perovskite film at 1500 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 100 °C for 20 min. Then, The   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanocrystals solution was coated 4 times at 3000 rpm for 30 s without any further annealing or treatment. Finally, about 60 nm Ag counter electrode was deposited on top of   $  SnO_{2}  $   film via vacuum thermal evaporation. The active area of PSCs was confirmed to be 0.12 cm². All processes including fabrication, measurement and storage of devices were carried out under ambient conditions.

PL measurements are used to reveal the charge extraction and transport mechanisms in the inverted PSCs with   $  SnO_{2}-HClO_{4}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}-HCl  $   ETLs. In principle, obvious PL quenching can be observed when perovskite layer is contact with ETL. This phenomenon can be attributed to the fact that the ETL enables efficient photo induced electrons extract and transfer before the carrier recombination occurs [38]. As shown in Fig. 5a, the   $  C_{60}/SnO_{2}-HClO_{4}  $   ETL affords a higher PL quenching efficiency than that of   $  C_{60}/SnO_{2}-HCl  $   ETL, suggesting more efficient electron transfer from perovskite layer to   $  C_{60}/SnO_{2}-HClO_{4}  $   ETL. Furthermore, it is interesting to note the peak of the sample with   $  C_{60}/SnO_{2}-HClO_{4}  $   ETL shifted from 783 nm to 776 nm compared with the one with   $  C_{60}/SnO_{2}-HCl  $   ETL. The blue-shifted emission peak demonstrates that the surface traps in the sample with   $  C_{60}/SnO_{2}-HClO_{4}  $   ETL can be effectively passivated [54,55].

To further investigate the charge transport kinetics, EIS measurements are carried out under dark conditions. Fig. 5b and Table S2 show the measured Nyquist plots of the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ - $ \mathrm{HClO}_4 $ -based and  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ - $ \mathrm{HCl} $ -based devices, and equivalent circuit shown in the inset. In our case, the high-frequency feature is suggested to be related to carrier transfer resistance at the perovskite/ETL interface, because of the identical  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $ /perovskite interface for the two samples [56,57]. Apparently, the transfer resistance ( $ R_{CT} $ ) value of the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ - $ \mathrm{HClO}_4 $ -based device (101.5  $ \Omega $ ) is much lower than that of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ - $ \mathrm{HCl} $ -based device (147.6  $ \Omega $ ). The long-term stability and thermal stability of PSCs are critical issues for future commercial applications. Fig. 6a–d, Tables S3 and S4 show the detail long-term stability parameters of the unpacked   $  SnO_{2}-HClO_{4}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}-HCl  $   based PSCs in atmospheric conditions. As expected, the   $  SnO_{2}-HClO_{4}  $   based devices can maintain its performance above 80% after storage for 90 days in the air with relative humidity of 55% ± 10%, by contrast, the   $  SnO_{2}-HCl  $   based devices only maintain 15%. The enhanced long-term stability can be attributed to the better encapsulation effect and more stable interfacial layer of better crystallinity and more compact   $  SnO_{2}-HClO_{4}  $   ETL. On the other hand, the thermal-induced instability cannot be avoided through encapsulating or protecting devices. To explore the devices thermal stability, the inverted PSCs were heated at 85 °C in air. As shown in Fig. 6e,   $  SnO_{2}-HClO_{4}  $   based devices can maintain more than 80% of the original efficiency for 41 h, while the PCE of   $  SnO_{2}-HCl  $   based devices only keep 52% of initial value. A recent study shown that trapped charges can cause the irreversible decomposition of perovskite [58]. In the present work, as mentioned earlier,   $  C_{60}/SnO_{2}-HClO_{4}  $   ETL shows higher electrical conductivity, which originates from the increased electron mobility [59]. This indicates low traps and could improve charge transport and suppress charge accumulation [60,61], which will result in highly stable devices, thus enhance the thermal stability of inverted PSCs.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, the high-efficiency and stable PSCs with a device structure of   $  FTO/NiO_{x}/MAPbI_{3}/C_{60}/SnO_{2}-HClO_{4}/Ag  $   have been fabricated under ambient conditions, which can achieve an excellent efficiency of 16.36%. The efficiency of the inverted PSCs can still maintain more than 80% of its original value after storing in an atmosphere for 90 days. In addition,   $  SnO_{2}-HClO_{4}  $   based device also exhibits excellent thermal (85 °C) stability. The improvement in PCE and stability is attributed to   $  SnO_{2}-HClO_{4}  $   with high crystallinity, matched energy level and   $  C_{60}  $   passivation effect. Meanwhile, the preparation process is completed in the atmosphere. Our work provides a new path to large-scale production of efficient and stable PSCs.
