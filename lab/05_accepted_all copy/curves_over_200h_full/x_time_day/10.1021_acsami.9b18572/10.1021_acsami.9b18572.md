ABSTRACT. Despite the rocketing rise in power conversion efficiencies (PCEs), the performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is still limited by the carrier transfer loss at the interface between perovskite (PVSK) absorbers and charge transporting layers (CTLs). Here, we propose a novel in situ passivation strategy by using [6,6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) to improve the charge dynamics at rear PVSK/CTL interface in the n-i-p structure device. A pre-deposited PCBM doped  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  layer is re-dissolved during PVSK deposition in our routine, establishing a bottom-up PCBM gradient that is facile for charge extraction. Meanwhile, the surface defects are in situ passivated via PCBM-PVSK interaction, which substantially suppresses the trap-assisted recombination at the rear interface. Due to the synergistic effect of charge-extraction promotion and trap passivation, the fabricated PSCs deliver a champion PCE of  $ 20.10\% $  with attenuated hysteresis and improved long-term stability, much higher than  $ 18.39\% $  of the reference devices. Our work demonstrates a promising interfacial engineering strategy for further improving the performance of PSCs.

# Materials and Methods

Materials. All solvent reagents were anhydrous from Acros.  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  colloid precursor (tin oxide,  $ 15\% $  in  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  colloidal dispersion) was purchased from Alfa Aesar. [6,6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) was purchased from Yingkou You Xuan Trade Co., Ltd. The organic cation halide salts and inorganic compounds for PVSK, phenethylammonium iodide (PEAI), and spiro-MeOTAD were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Co. Bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide (Li-TFSI), 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) and tris(2-(1H-pyrazol-1-yl)-4-tert-butylpyridine)cobalt(III) tri[bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide] (FK209) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, TCI and Dyesol, respectively.

In situ passivation on rear PVSK layer. The PCBM@PbI $ _2 $  precursor solution was prepared by adding 80 μL PCBM solution (10 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  in chlorobenzene) into 1 mL PbI $ _2 $  solution (200 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  in DMF:DMSO 9:1 (v:v)). The PCBM@PbI $ _2 $  thin layer was prepared by dynamically spin coating the precursor onto SnO $ _2 $  layer at 4000 rpm for 35 s and annealing at 70 °C for 1 min. After cooling down, a PVSK precursor solution composed of PbI $ _2 $ :PbBr $ _2 $ :FAI:MABr (507:74.4:173:22.4 mg) in DMF:DMSO (800:200 μL), with addition of 45 μL CsI stock solution (1.5 M in DMSO), was dripped on the PCBM@PbI $ _2 $  layer. The semitransparent intermediate film was annealed at 100 °C for 30 min on hot plate to form a dark-brown PVSK layer. For control device, the PVSK was spin-coated on the SnO $ _2 $  using the same procedure.

Schematic illustration of the in situ passivation procedure is depicted in Figure 1a. In this routine, a PCBM@PbI $ _2 $  layer is initially deposited on SnO $ _2 $  layer by dynamically spin-coating PCBM-doped PbI $ _2 $  solution, followed by annealing to evaporate the solvent. Then, the PVSK precursor solution with a typical triple cation recipe (Cs $ _{0.05} $ (FA $ _{0.85} $ MA $ _{0.15} $ ) $ _3 $ Pb(I $ _{0.83} $ Br $ _{0.17} $ ) $ _3 $ ) is spin-coated on the PCBM@PbI $ _2 $  layer in a conventional one-step antisolvent manner. Upon dropping the none-orthogonal solvent, the PCBM@PbI $ _2 $  layer dissolves immediately, releasing the solute of PbI $ _2 $  and PCBM into PVSK solution. The passivating agent PCBM disperses from bottom and bonds to the trap sites near the rear PVSK/CTL interface during PVSK precursor self-assembly, fulfilling an in situ rear interface passivation. Here, two hindrances encountered in previously PCBM-passivating strategies are overcome in our method. First, the premature crystallization of precursor in pre-mixed PCBM–PVSK solution, which is typically prepared by pre-adding PCBM in PVSK solution, could give rise to poor dendrite morphology in resultant films. $ ^{34} $  In our method, doping PCBM occurs exactly at the PVSK deposition stage, which could avoid precursor nucleation prior to drop-casting. Second, the PCBM with low surface energy (< 40 mJ m $ ^{-2} $ ) usually leads to a severe de-wetting issue in the subsequent deposition of PVSK on PCBM layer. $ ^{39} $  As shown in Figure 1b–c, the contact angle of PVSK solution on PCBM film is 36.7°, much higher than that on SnO $ _2 $  surface (4.5°). The inferior wettability of PVSK solution on PCBM substrate results in the poor coverage of the resultant PVSK films even on the 1.5 cm × 1.5 cm substrate (Figure 1e). # Conclusion

In summary, a novel interfacial engineering protocol featured with in situ rear PVSK/CTL interface passivation in n-i-p structure device is developed to fabricate highly reproducible, efficient and stable PVSK solar cells. In this protocol, a bottom-up PCBM gradient is established through re-dissolving a pre-deposited PCBM@PbI $ _2 $  layer during PVSK preparation. The interfacial charge transfer is efficiently promoted by this structure, revealed as rapidly quenched photogenerated carrier at SnO $ _2 $ /PVSK interface as well as reduced interfacial charge accumulation. Meanwhile, significant reduction in surface trap density is demonstrated due to in-situ formed fullerene–halide radicals, which could passivate Pb–I antisite defects effectively. Thus, the trap-assisted SRH recombination is substantially repressed as indicated by the reduced diode ideality factor ( $ n $ ) and increased recombination resistances. Benefiting from the synergistic effect of promotion on charge transport and suppression of nonradiative recombination, an overall enhancement in  $ V_{oc} $ ,  $ J_{sc} $  and FF is yielded to boost the champion PCE of passivated devices to an impressive 20.10%, much higher than that of 18.39% of control devices. Moreover, our protocol avoids the wettability issue encountered in interlayer manner, as well as overcomes the premature nucleation in solution-mixing strategy. The minimum limitations on the physical properties of the passivating agents, such as solubility and wettability, rich the choices of semiconducting organics. It is believed
