# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved excellent power conversion efficiency (PCE) exceeding 25% due to their unique optoelectronic properties like suitable bandgap, high carrier mobility, low exciton binding, and long charge transport length [1-5]. However, the thermal and moisture stability of the organic cations such as methylammonium and formamidine of hybrid perovskite films remains a vital concern for the long-term operation of corresponding photovoltaic devices [6-8]. To overcome the instability issue of PSCs, all-inorganic perovskites have attracted much interest due to their remarkable thermal stability [9-11]. In particular, Cs-based perovskites (CsPbX $ _3 $ , X = I, Br, Cl) exhibit similar optoelectronic properties to organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites with a suitable bandgap and high charge carrier mobility, which recently delivered a record PCE up to 19% in CsPbI $ _3 $  [12]. # ABSTRACT

Although all-inorganic perovskites possess suitable bandgap and thermal stability for photovoltaic applications, the unstable film morphologies, and poor moisture stabilities lag behind the organic-inorganic hybrid counterparts. Herein, we demonstrate that high quality  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br films with improved phase stability, high crystallinity, and pinhole-free film morphology was achieved by employing tetramethylammonium chloride (TMACI) as an additive. As a result, the TMACI-added CsPbI $ _2 $ Br films exhibited high power conversion efficiency of 14.12% with an open-circuit voltage of 1.19 V, a short-circuit current density of 15.08 mA/cm $ ^2 $ , and a fill factor of 0.78. More importantly, the TMACI addition imparts hydrophobicity to the film surface of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br, which delivered excellent stability up to 30 days in the ambient condition and thermal stability at 85 °C for 156 h, respectively. Moreover, the TMACI-added device also exhibited excellent PCE of 27.16% under indoor light source (1000 lux), which presents a promising approach for designing stable but high performance all-inorganic perovskite solar cells.

In this regard, the mixed-halide all-inorganic perovskite such as CsPbI $ _2 $ Br has been assumed to be a promising candidate serving as an absorber layer in PSCs. CsPbI $ _2 $ Br possesses a reasonable bandgap of 1.92 eV, relatively low transition temperature ( $ <200^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ), and good phase stability [16-19]. However, CsPbI $ _2 $ Br is still structurally unstable and easily undergoes the transformation into a non-perovskite orthorhombic phase in moisture, and exhibits much lower device PCE in comparison with their organic-inorganic hybrid counterparts [20]. Therefore, the enhancement of ambient stability retaining high photovoltaic performance remains a big challenge for further advance of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br-based PSCs.

Recently, some approaches for improving the photovoltaic performance of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films, such as elemental substitution, doping, or additive engineering, have been proposed. Partial substitution of Cs, Pb, or halogen-site has been demonstrated to optimize the crystal structure and film morphology of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  , and the Goldschmidt tolerance factor, lattice strain, bandgap structure, and electronic properties were effectively tuned to achieve high photovoltaic performance [21–30]. gineering is also utilized to obtain phase stability, reduce defect density, and increase the Fermi level of  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br to improve the performance of the PSCs. Wang et al. [31] reported that CuBr $ _2 $ -incorporated CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs improved open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{OC} $ ) due to enlarged band gap ( $ E_g $ ) by reducing trap states. Zeng et al. [32] doped Pb(AC) $ _2 $  in the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br and demonstrated that the acetate anion helps to obtain stable  $ \alpha $ -phase by fast nucleation and effectively reduce energy loss ( $ E_{loss} $ ) of PSC. Han et al. [33] showed that CaCl $ _2 $ -additive enhances crystallinity and suppresses the trap density by passivating the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite material. Although those results demonstrated effective ways to improve not only the perovskite film morphology but also its photovoltaic property, the hygroscopic nature of the ionic additive/dopants materials still limits further improvement in ambient stability against moisture of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br-based PSCs.

Herein, we introduce tetramethylammonium chloride (TMACl) as an additive to simultaneously enhance the electronic properties and the ambient stability of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films. Tetraalkylammonium cations have occasionally been used as a moisture tolerant small molecule in organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites due to their amphiphilic property [34]. Yang et al. reported that the hydrophobic alkylammonium cations can act as a water-resistant layer on perovskite films [35]. Tetrabutylammonium iodide was also used as an additive for improved coverage and crystallinity of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite films, which further demonstrated enhanced moisture stability [36]. Thus, alkylammonium cations can be a candidate for additive in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   with enhanced moisture stability as well as improved film properties of   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br. However, the alkylammonium cation with long alkyl chains may have low solubility in polar solvents, which adversely affects the processing of perovskite film deposition. Taking this into account, TMACl was selected as an additive in this work, because it possesses balanced solubility in precursor solvent and hydrophobicity. By incorporating TMACl, the film morphology of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   was improved considerably including pinhole-free surface, smooth and largely-grained film surface without adversely affecting the lattice parameter or electronic structure. Consequently, the optoelectronic properties of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   such as PL lifetime and tail states were improved with TMACl, enabling a PCE up to 14.12% with an   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.19 V, a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 15.08 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , and a fill factor (FF) of 0.78. The addition of TMACl also provides a hydrophobicity of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   surface, which enhances the stability of the inorganic PSCs in ambient condition up to   $  \sim  $  28 days. The optimized device prepared with TMACl also exhibited an outstanding PCE of 27.16% under white light-emitting diode (LED) light (6500 K) at 1000 lux, which is the highest efficiency for the inorganic PSCs under indoor lighting condition. Therefore, this study clarifies the effect of TMACl additive for achieving high photovoltaic performance via controlled film morphology and provides crucial directions for designing optimal additives to enhance the ambient stability of PSCs.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Materials

Cesium iodide (Csl, 99.999%), lead iodide (PbI  $  _{2}  $  , 99.999%), lead bromide (PbBr  $  _{2}  $  , 99.998%), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, anhydrous), and Poly[(9,9-dioctylfluorenyl-2,7-diyl)-co-(4,4'-(N-(4-sec-butylphenyl)diphenylamine) (TFB) were purchased from Sigma Aldrich. Tin oxide (SnO  $  _{2}  $  ) nanoparticles were purchased from Alfa Aesar. Tetramethylammonium chloride (TMACl) was purchased from Samchun Chemicals. Poly(3-hexylthiophene-2,5-diyl) (P3HT) was purchased from Rieke Metals.

# 2.2. Fabrication of thin-film perovskite solar cells

ITO-coated glass (sheet resistance   $  \sim20\ ohm/sq  $  ) was cleaned with deionized water (DI-water), acetone, and isopropyl alcohol for 15 min and then dried by nitrogen flow. Those were treated then with UV-  $  O_{3}  $   for 30 min. The   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer was cast by spin-coating at 3000 rpm for 30 s, and then the films were annealed at 100 °C for 1 h. The 1.0 M   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution was prepared by mixing   $  CsI, PbBr_{2},  $   and   $  PbI_{2}  $   (2:1:1 in molar ratio) in DMF and DMSO (2:1 in volume ratio). For the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution with TMACl additive, TMACl was added at 1, 3 and 5% molar ratio to the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution. The perovskite solution was spin-coated onto   $  SnO_{2}  $   film at 2500 rpm for 45 s. The substrates were then sequentially annealed at 50 °C for 2 min, 100 °C for 2 min, 300 °C for 10 min. The TFB layer was formed by spin-coating the TFB solution at 5000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. The P3HT solution was dynamically spin-coated on TFB film at 3000 rpm for 30 s, followed by annealing at 100 °C for 10 min. Finally, 80 nm thick Au electrode was evaporated using a thermal evaporator under   $  <5\times10^{-6}  $   Torr at an evaporation rate of 0.4 Å/s onto substrates. The device area is 10 mm².

# 2.3. Characterizations

X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern was acquired on Bruker XRD (D8 Advance, Bruker Corporation). The absorption spectra were measured using a UV-vis spectrophotometer (Cary 100, Agilent). The steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) spectra were characterized by fluorescence spectrometers (FS5 spectrofluorometer, Edinburgh Instrument) with an excitation wavelength of  $ 405\mathrm{nm} $ . X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurement was carried out using an AXIS ultra-delay line detector (DLD) (Kratos, UK) equipped with monochromatic Al  $ K\alpha $  (1486.6 eV) as an X-ray source (or He I gas (21.22 eV) as a UV source). The surface morphologies of perovskite films were confirmed by field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM, S-4800, Hitachi) and atomic force microscopy (AFM, CoreAFM, Nanosurf) at the Core Facility Center for Analysis of Optoelectronic Materials and Devices of the Korea Basic Science Institute (KBSI). The photovoltaic performance of PSCs was measured by a solar simulator with a parameter analyzer (4200-SCS, Keithley) under AM 1.5G  $ (100\mathrm{mW/cm}^2) $  illumination, calibrated using an NREL-certified photodiode. A white light-emitting diode was applied as a light source under indoor conditions. The photon flux density of the light was measured using a spectrometer (Spectrolux, GL Optics) to calculate the light intensity. The intensity-modulated spectroscopies and the electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) were measured using a potentiostat (Compactstat, IVIUM) under the illumination of a white light-emitting diode (IM1225, IVIUM). The wetting property of the film was studied by the contact angle analyzer (Phoenix 300, SEO).

The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films with different TMACl concentrations were characterized in Fig. 2(a). Although discernible (110) peak appeared at   $  20.8^{\circ}  $   from the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film with 5 wt% of TMACl, the other films turned out to possess very low (100) diffraction, as shown in Fig. S2. Besides, CsPbI $ _2 $ Br films with 3% TMACl additive have the narrowest full at half maximum (FWHM) of (200) peak (Fig. 2(c)). The FWHM of XRD peaks is conversely associated with the crystal coherence length, indicating that the crystallinity of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite film with 3 wt% of TMACl is much improved as compared to the pristine one [38] (Table S1).

The absorption spectra of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film with TMACl additive were investigated by optical analysis, as shown in Fig. 2(d). The absorption edge of all films was   $  \sim626  $   nm, but the perovskite film with 3 wt% TMACl showed intense absorptivity in 400 to 600 nm, indicating an improvement in the crystallinity of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film with TMACl addition. Therefore, the improved crystallinity of the perovskite film with 3 wt% TMACl additive may enhance the electronic properties of   $  \alpha  $  -  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film. Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements were also studied to understand the charge extraction and recombination of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films with and without TMACl additive. As shown in Fig. 2(e), the PL intensity of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film with 3% TMACl additive is higher than 0, 1, and 5 wt%, indicating that the crystal defects related to the grain boundaries are significantly reduced. Furthermore,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films prepared with TMACl additive showed blue-shifted PL emission as compared to the reference film. The blue shift of PL emission indicates the reduction of the defect densities of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films as TMACl was employed. To study the extent of TMACl to the electronic structure, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis of the perovskite absorber layer was studied. As shown in Fig. S3, there was no shift in peak positions of Cs 3d, Pb 4F, I 3d, and Br 3d. Besides, the peaks of Cl 2p and N 1s were not detected from  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  films with varied concentrations of TMACl. These results indicate that TMACl is not incorporated into the crystal lattice of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  perovskite. The energy levels of the perovskite films were further investigated by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) (Fig. S4). The Fermi level  $ (E_{\mathrm{F}}) $  and valence band minimum of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  films were almost unchanged ( $ \Delta E_{\mathrm{F}} \sim 0.05 $  eV) regardless of TMACl addition, so the interaction of TMACl on the electronic structure of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  was negligible as TMACl was employed.

The planar PSCs were fabricated with the structure of Au/poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT)/ Poly[(9,9-dioctylfluorenyl-2,7-diyl)-co-(4,4'-(N-(4-sec-butylphenyl)diphenylamine)

(TFB)/CsPbI $ _2 $ Br/SnO $ _2 $ /ITO/glass to analyze the photovoltaic performance of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br-based PSCs, where P3HT and TFB have been utilized as an additive-free hole transporting layer and a buffer layer due to its high thermal property and cascade energy level matching between P3HT and CsPbI $ _2 $ Br interface, respectively [42-44]. Fig. 3(a) compares the cross-section of the devices prepared with and without TMACl, and their current density-voltage density (J-V) curves under 1-sun illumination with various concentrations of TMACl additive are shown in Fig. 3(b) (Table 1 for corresponding device parameters). The reference device prepared without TMACl showed a PCE of  $ 11.03\% $ , with  $ V_{OC} $  of  $ 1.16\mathrm{V} $ ,  $ J_{SC} $  of  $ 13.78\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $ , and FF of 0.69. Interestingly, the addition of TMACl improved the

photovoltaic properties of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based devices. The optimal condition for TMACl additive was 3 wt%, which achieved a PCE of 14.12% with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.19 V,   $  J_{SC}  $   of 15.08 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , and FF of 0.78. The notable increase in the performance of the device with 3 wt% TMACl additive is attributed to improved   $  J_{SC}  $   and FF, which are mainly attributed to the improved film morphology without pinholes and defects, as discussed above by SEM and AFM results. It is noted that the film morphology has a great influence not only on the champion device performance but also on the average values of the device performance and reliability. We also verified the cross-sectional SEM images of the devices with and without TMACl in which many pinholes were present at the ETL/perovskite interface. Those will cause unintended charge recombination, thus causing serious   $  E_{loss}  $   as well as charge carrier leakage at the interfaces of the device. Fig. 3(c) shows the external quantum efficiency (EQE) of the devices without and with TMACl additive. The EQE spectrum of the device with 3% TMACl is slightly higher than that of the reference in the wavelength ranging from 450 to 640 nm, which is consistent with the absorption spectra. The integrated   $  J_{SC}  $   values of all the devices studied in this work were reasonably in good agreement with the   $  J_{SC}  $   measured from the J-V measurements. The histogram of PEC distribution from 25 individual devices fabricated with 3 wt% TMACl shows excellent reproducibility and overall high efficiency than devices fabricated without TMACl, as shown in Fig. 3(d). Also, we compared hysteresis of J-V scan of devices with and without TMACl additive. The hysteresis index (HI) value of the devices decreased as fabricated with MACl additive as compared to the reference device at the scan rate of 5 mV/s (Fig. 3(e)), and the tendency was retained at varied scan rates (Fig. S5 and Table S3).

To estimate the influence of MACl additive on recombination behavior of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based device, light power intensity-dependent   $  V_{OC}  $   variation was investigated in the devices with and without TMACl additive. As displayed in Fig. 3(f), the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   device with 3 wt% TMACl additive has a quite lower loss of   $  V_{OC}  $   compared to the reference device. The linearly fitting of   $  V_{OC}  $   versus the natural logarithm of light intensity can be expressed with the following equation:   $  V_{\mathrm{OC}} = nkT\ln(I)/q  $  , where n denotes the ideality factor of the device [45]. The deviation of the slope from   $  2k_{B}T/q  $   indicates the dominance of monomolecular recombination close to the   $  V_{OC}  $   of the devices, so a higher slope of the device reveals suppressed carrier recombination of PSCs with TMACl, which contributed to reduced   $  E_{loss}  $   and enhanced   $  V_{OC}  $  . The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was investigated to further study the internal resistance of charge carriers of the device. Fig. 4(a) shows Nyquist plots of the devices with and without TMACl, which were measured under dark conditions in the frequency ranging from  $ 10\mathrm{Hz} $  to  $ 100\mathrm{MHz} $ . The results were fitted based on the equivalent circuit diagram, as shown in the inset image. The small semicircle in the Nyquist plot indicates charge transfer resistance  $ (R_{\mathrm{trans}}) $  in the high-frequency range [46]. The device with TMACl additive effectively reduced  $ R_{\mathrm{trans}} $  from 188 to  $ 38\Omega $ . Notably, the significant reduction in the  $ R_{\mathrm{trans}} $  value of the device with  $ 3\% $  TMACl additive indicates that the interfacial recombination was reduced and charge transfer was efficiently accelerated, which enhanced the photovoltaic parameters such as  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ , FF, and PCE. We further investigated charge transport and recombination of the devices using intensity-modulated photocurrent/photovoltage spectroscopy (IMPS/IMVS) measurements [47].

# 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, we have demonstrated high quality  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite film by employing TMACI as an additive. The controlled CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film influenced by TMACI additive possessed improved film quality with pinhole-free compact grains, smooth surface, reduced trap density, and better phase stability. All these favorable film properties induced improved optoelectronic properties of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br such as suppressed nonradiative recombination and elongated carrier lifetime. As a result, the optimized PSCs with TMACI achieved impressive PCEs of 14.12% and 27.16% under 1-sun illumination and white LED irradiation, respectively. The TMACI additive also contributed to the hydrophobic properties of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br films, which thus presented excellent ambient and thermal stabilities of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br-based PSCs. All these results verify that TMACI is an effective additive for CsPbI $ _2 $ Br based all-inorganic PSCs. This study opens up a new avenue to realize highly efficient but stable all-inorganic PSCs operating in outdoor/indoor light harvesting environments.
