# Efficient Perovskite Solar Cells Based on a Solution Processable Nickel(II) Phthalocyanine and Vanadium Oxide Integrated Hole Transport Layer

Ming Cheng, Yuanyuan Li, Majid Safdari, Cheng Chen, Peng Liu, Lars Kloo, and Licheng Sun*

An organic-inorganic integrated hole transport layer (HTL) composed of the solution-processable nickel phthalocyanine (NiPc) abbreviated NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $  and vanadium(V) oxide (V  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $  ) is successfully incorporated into structured mesoporous perovskite solar cells (PSCs). The optimized PSCs show the highest stabilized power conversion efficiency of up to 16.8% and good stability under dark ambient conditions. These results highlight the potential application of organic-inorganic integrated HTLs in PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Recently, organic–inorganic hybrid perovskites with the composition   $  ABX_{3}  $   [A =   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}  $   (MA), NH =   $  CHNH_{3}^{+}  $   (FA) or   $  Cs^{+}  $  ; B =   $  Pb^{2+}  $   or   $  Sn^{2+}  $  ; X = I  $  ^{-}  $  , Br  $  ^{-}  $  , or Cl  $  ^{-}  $  ] have been demonstrated as efficient light harvesting materials in solid state solar cells offering a power conversion efficiency (PCE) exceeding 20%.  $  ^{[1,2]}  $   Considering the high efficiency and facile fabrication process, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have become a promising low-cost photovoltaic (PV) technology that can rival conventional inorganic technologies. The state-of-the-art PSCs routinely employ hole transport materials (HTMs), which play an important role

(Spiro-OMeTAD)], a large number of novel organic hole conductors based on facile synthetic routes have been developed for PSCs.  $  ^{[3,5,6,8-11]}  $   However, the overall performance of these new HTMs has rarely been able to compete with Spiro-OMeTAD.  $  ^{[9-11]}  $   Moreover, just as for Spiro-OMeTAD, most reported and highly efficient HTMs suffer from low charge carrier densities in their pristine form. Therefore, p-type dopants and additives (lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP)) are usually needed to render devices with high conversion efficiencies.  $  ^{[9-11]}  $   Many reports have shown that such p-type dopants and additives also induce device instability.  $  ^{[7,12]}  $   Therefore, it is of great importance to explore alternative and dopant free HTMs with comparable or even superior properties relative to the widely used HTM, Spiro-OMeTAD.

Phthalocyanine has been extensively used as a p-type semiconductor in thin film field effect transistors and PV devices due to properties such as high hole mobility, as well as excellent thermal and chemical stability.  $  ^{[13-15]}  $   More intriguingly, vacuum evaporated pristine copper phthalocyanine (CuPc) thin films (20 nm) have been successfully incorporated into carbon counter electrode based PSCs, showing conversion efficiencies up to 16% and excellent long-term durability.  $  ^{[16]}  $   Nazeeruddin and co-workers reported the use of a solution processable nonaggregated zinc phthalocyanine (TT80) as HTM in PSCs rendering a PCE of 6.7% together with LiTFSI and TBP as additives.  $  ^{[17]}  $   However, to date, there are no reports on PSC devices employing nickel(II) phthalocyanine (NiPc) as HTM.

# 2. Results and Discussions

UV–vis absorption spectra of NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   in dichloromethane (DCM) solution and coated onto a compact TiO  $  _{2}  $   film are depicted in Figure 1b. In DCM solution, NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   shows an intense Q-band maximum absorption peak located at 742 nm. In the thin film state, NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   exhibits a 16 nm redshift of the maximum absorption and to some extent the peak broadens due to intermolecular   $  \pi-\pi  $   stacking in the film; this is expected to facilitate hole mobility and conductivity.  $  ^{[18-20]}  $   Analysis of the cutoff absorption edge of NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   in the film state allows an estimate of the band gap of NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   amounting to 1.45 eV.

Solution cyclic voltammetry was performed to roughly evaluate the energy levels of NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   in comparison to SpiroOMeTAD. As displayed in Figure 1c, the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy level is located at -5.06 eV. Based on the estimated HOMO energy level and band gap of NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $  , the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital energy level of NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   can be deduced to -3.61 eV. The HOMO energy level of NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   matches the low lying valence band (VB) of the mixed ion perovskite material quite well (-5.48 eV, see Figure 3c),  $  ^{[21]}  $   indicating that NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   should be able to effectively extract holes generated in the perovskite layer.

For HTMs, hole mobility and conductivity are two central parameters to evaluate. Hole mobility measurements were conducted by investigating the space-charge-limited current. By fitting the  $ J - V $  curves (Figure 2a), the extracted hole mobility for pristine NiPc-(OBu) $ _8 $  is obtained as  $ 1.90 \times 10^{-4} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  S $ ^{-1} $ , which is slightly lower than that of Spiro-OMeTAD ( $ 3.41 \times 10^{-4} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  S $ ^{-1} $ ) but of the same magnitude. The conductivities of pristine NiPc-(OBu) $ _8 $ , LiTFSI, and TBP doped NiPc-(OBu) $ _8 $ , as well as LiTFSI, TBP, and FK209 doped Spiro-OMeTAD, were subsequently examined. The corresponding results are displayed in Figure 2b. In its pristine form, the conductivity of NiPc-(OBu) $ _8 $  is evaluated to be  $ 1.18 \times 10^{-4} $  S cm $ ^{-1} $ . The conductivity increases to  $ 1.97 \times 10^{-4} $  S cm $ ^{-1} $  once doped, which is even slightly higher than that of doped Spiro-OMeTAD ( $ 1.91 \times 10^{-4} $  S cm $ ^{-1} $ ). Considering the high hole mobility and conductivity of NiPc-(OBu) $ _8 $  both in its pristine and doped form, it represents a material worth to explore as dopant-free HTM in PSCs.

$ \mathrm{NiPc - (OBu)_8} $  was initially tested as dopant-free HTM in mesoscopic PSCs with a device structure of fluorine doped tin oxide (FTO) glass/compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $   $ (\mathrm{c - TiO_2}) $  /mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $   $ (\mathrm{m - TiO_2}) $  /perovskite/NiPc-(OBu)8/Au (see Figure 3a). More details regarding solar cell fabrication procedures and component parameters are described in the Supporting Information. A significant difference in  $  V_{oc}  $  was observed when comparing PSCs based on pristine NiPc-(OBu) $ _{8} $  and doped Spiro-OMeTAD. Recent research work manifested that photovoltage of PSCs is nearly independent of the HOMO energy level of HTM but mainly depends on the morphology of perovskite and selective contact material. $ ^{[22]} $  Considering the same fabrication procedure was used for all of the other layers, a possible reason may relate to the morphology of the hole transport layer.

In order to confirm this hypothesis, scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was employed. For Spiro-OMeTAD, the conductivity dramatically increases when LiTFSI, TBP, and FK209 are used as additives and dopants, thus a thicker HTL can be used and the concentration of Spiro-OMeTAD is optimized to 80 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  . As shown in Figure S4a,c (Supporting Information), the perovskite crystals are nearly fully covered by doped Spiro-OMeTAD at high concentration, while the coverage by NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   film fabricated from 30 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   solution is much less complete with some perovskite crystals still being visible. Thus, recombination spots may form when gold as contact material is evaporated on the top resulting in lower   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF for devices based on pristine NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $  .  $  ^{[23-25]}  $   Two methods can be considered to resolve this issue. One is to increase the thickness of the HTL, which can be easily achieved by either increasing the concentration of the HTM solution or by decreasing the spin coating speed; the other is to introduce

a buffer layer. The buffer layer may of course also consists of a material with hole conducting properties.

First, we tried to increase the thickness of the HTL by adjusting the concentration of the HTM solution. As shown in Figure S4b (Supporting Information), when the concentration of HTM was increased to 50 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , the resulting HTL nearly fully covers the perovskite layer. Disappointingly, although the corresponding devices showed an improved   $  V_{oc}  $   and   $  J_{sc}  $  , the PCE obtained is still low due to a significant decrease in FF (see Figure S1 and Table S1 in the Supporting Information) as a consequence of the higher HTL resistance. Thus, the use of a thicker NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   film will require doping with LiTFSI to increase the overall conductivity leading to an improved FF. The PSCs containing doped NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   with   $  30 \times 10^{-3}  $   M LiTFSI and   $  300 \times 10^{-3}  $   M TBP, as expected, exhibit a higher efficiency, 17.0% (see Figure 4b).

The introduction of a buffer layer was also considered to improve the device PCE. Typically, when deploying multiple layers of different materials, solvents of very different character, in order to dissolve as little as possible of the already deposited layers, need to be carefully optimized.   $  V_{2}O_{5}  $   is a well-known "p-type" metal oxide that has been widely used as hole-conductor in organic solar cells. Its low-lying VB (-5.0 eV), facile fabrication process, and solvent compatibility with the perovskite and HTM layer during the fabrication process make it an ideal choice as buffer layer. In our case, the   $  V_{2}O_{5}  $   can be easily fabricated by drop-casting vanadium(V) oxytrisopropoxide isopropanol solution and then heating at 120 °C for 10 min. The volume ratio of vanadium(V) oxytrisopropoxide to isopropanol is fixed to 1:200. No perovskite crystals and only the compact   $  V_{2}O_{5}  $   sheet can be detected using SEM. Under the same conditions, when the   $  V_{2}O_{5}  $   film was drop-casted directly onto the perovskite film, as shown in Figure S4e (Supporting Information), the   $  V_{2}O_{5}  $   film does not become as compact as when deposited on top of the NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $ , and both the HTL and the perovskite can be detected. As shown in Figure S2b (Supporting Information), the NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   layer is much less crystalline and thus imposes less such constraints, and therefore it is easier to generate a uniform   $  V_{2}O_{5}  $   layer on top of the NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   layer. In order to confirm that the combination of a metal–organic HTL and an inorganic HTL/buffer layer indeed plays an important role for the high efficiencies obtained, also PSCs with only   $  V_{2}O_{5}  $   as HTL were investigated. As shown in Figure 4c, the highest average efficiency recorded for such devices only reached 10.8%. As mentioned above, the low efficiency may mainly be due to the incomplete   $  V_{2}O_{5}  $   film formed on the perovskite layer. This result indicates that it is the combination of the metal–organic HTL and the inorganic buffer/HTL layer that is the key factor for achieving the high efficiencies. Compared with the devices containing pristine NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   or V  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $   alone, the   $  V_{oc}, J_{sc}  $ , and FF of the PSCs based on the combined HTL and buffer layers (NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $ /V  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $ ) all are greatly improved. The better   $  V_{oc}  $   obtained most likely originates from less recombination losses

between the perovskite and Au layers when perovskite crystals are fully covered by the integrated HTL.

The devices containing Spiro-OMeTAD and   $  V_{2}O_{5}  $   integrated HTL were also examined. Very different from NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $  /V  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $   based PSCs, due to the low conductivity of pristine Spiro-OMeTAD (about   $  10^{-7}  $   S cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ), even with decreased concentration of 15 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , the corresponding PSCs containing pristine Spiro-OMeTAD/V  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $   integrated HTL still exhibit poor photovoltaic performance; in particular, low FF (see Figure S3a in the Supporting Information). The integrated HTL composed of doped Spiro-OMeTAD and V  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $   was further investigated. Once Spiro-OMeTAD was doped with LiTFSI, TBP, and FK209, all photovoltaic parameters were significantly improved. As shown in Figure S3 and Table S2 (Supporting Information), the concentration of Spiro-OMeTAD also has big effects on device performance. When the concentration of Spiro-OMeTAD in the spin coating solution was fixed to 40 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , the integrated devices showed the highest PCE of 16.7%. Further increase of Spiro-OMeTAD solution concentration leads to lower FF due to the increase in HTL resistance. The combination of Spiro-OMeTAD and V  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $   into an integrated HTL does not show the same high efficiency as for the metal–organic HTM. Still, the optimized PSCs based on doped Spiro-OMeTAD alone show a PCE of 18.2%.

The incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra of PSCs containing the NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $  /V  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $   integrated HTL are shown in Figure 4f. The devices display a wide spectral response to 800 nm, yielding a broad IPCE plateau of over 80% in the range of 450–750 nm. The integrated current density from the IPCE spectra of the devices based on the NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $  /V  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $   integrated HTL is calculated to be 22.7 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , which is in good agreement with the extracted current from the J-V experiment.

In our cases, as shown in Figure 4 and Table 1, all devices exhibit different extent hysteresis behaviors. To rule out the effect of hysteresis on PCE and better evaluate this organic-inorganic integrated HTL, stabilized photocurrent density and PCE as a function of time at maximum point were tested (see Figure 5 and Figure S5 (Supporting Information)).  $  ^{[26-29]}  $   As shown in Figure 5, for NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $  /V  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $   and doped SpiroOMeTAD based PSCs, stable photocurrent density of 19.5 and 20.3 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and stable PCEs of 17.5% and 18.3% can be obtained, respectively.

The reproducibility of the PSCs was studied by investigation of ten devices containing each of the different HTLs. As can be seen from the statistics shown in Figure 6, all devices showed reasonable reproducibility. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, organic-inorganic integrated HTLs consisting of the solution-processable NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   and V  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $   have been successfully incorporated into mesoscopic PSCs. The appearance of NiPc-(OBu)  $  _{8}  $   layer on perovskite surface facilitates the formation of a compact V  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{5}  $   layer on top.
