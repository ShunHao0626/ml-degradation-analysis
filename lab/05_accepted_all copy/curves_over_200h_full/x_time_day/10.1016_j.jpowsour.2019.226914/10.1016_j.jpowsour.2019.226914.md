# 1. Introduction

Hybrid organic-inorganic perovskite solar cell has become a superstar in the PV community as its power conversion efficiency (PCE) is soared from the initial 3% to recently certificated 23.7% [1-14].   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , with a band gap about 1.55 eV, is the most studied perovskite material in photovoltaics. In fact, high efficiency solar cells have been reported using various electron extraction layers such as mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer [15-18], one-dimensional nanostructures such as   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanorods, nanofibers, ZnO nanorods and   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   etc [19-22]. High quality   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{(3-x)}Br_{x}  $   perovskite film with full surface coverage and fewer trap states was prepared using a single-step deposition process and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   doped perovskite precursor solution. Specifically, the thin film surface becomes rougher and the grain size gets larger as the   $  PbBr_{2}  $   concentration was increased from 1% to 10 mol%. Ultraviolet–visible spectroscopy (UV–vis) and incident photon-to-current efficiency (IPCE) spectra show that abnormal absorption onset shifts to longer wavelength with increasing the content of   $  PbBr_{2}  $ , because the larger grain size can reduce the stress accumulated in the lattice over the Pb–I bond angle. It is surprising to see that when 5 mol% of   $  PbBr_{2}  $   was used, the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) shows the best thermal stability, with the decomposition onset increased by about 20 °C. The photoluminescence (PL) peak of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{(3-x)}Br_{x}  $   film shifts to smaller wavelength as more   $  PbBr_{2}  $   was used owing to decreased trap states in perovskite films. It is found that at the optimal   $  PbBr_{2}  $   content of 5 mol%, the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{(3-x)}Br_{x}  $   based solar cell gives open-circuit voltage of 1.08 V with power conversion efficiency (PCE) as high as 18.97%, about 16.6% higher than the device based on the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   without Br.

To the best of our knowledge, the incorporation of Br in perovskite always makes its band gap bigger. However, in this work, we find the band gap of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   becomes smaller by adding   $  PbBr_{2}  $   into the precursor as the amount of   $  PbBr_{2}  $   less than 7.5% due to lower stress accumulated in the lattice, which is caused by larger grain size with increasing the contents of   $  PbBr_{2}  $ . Meanwhile, the crystalline quality of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{(3-x)}Br_{x}  $   film is improved by   $  PbBr_{2}  $   additive. Furthermore, photovoltaic performance and thermal stability of perovskites have a close relationship with   $  PbBr_{2}  $   content. The PCE is increased by 14.28% compared to the cells without adding   $  PbBr_{2}  $ , and the champion PCE reached 18.97%. The decomposition temperature could be elevated almost 20 °C. It is believed Br incorporation will be an effective way to improve both the stability and photovoltaic performance. Both crystalline quality and the grain size of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{(3-x)}Br_{x}  $   film have been improved by   $  PbBr_{2}  $   additive. We speculate that the grain surface enhancement effectinduded red shift of the absorption onset, and faster electron transfer between perovskite and   $  TiO_{2}  $   are beneficial to the improvement of solar cells performance.

# 2. Results and discussion

XRD measurement was conducted to characterize the crystal structure of each perovskite sample deposited on the   $  TiO_{2}  $  -coated FTO substrates. As shown in Fig. 1, all samples show major diffraction peaks

similar to the perovskite phase of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , which is indicated by the dominant peaks located at   $  14.08^{\circ}  $  ,   $  28.40^{\circ}  $  , and   $  31.86^{\circ}  $  , corresponding to diffractions from the (110), (220), and (310) lattice planes of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , respectively [41,42]. It could also been observed that the diffraction peak shifts toward higher angle (2θ) as the amount of   $  PbBr_{2}  $   exceeding 7.5%. However, the peak intensity increases with the   $  PbBr_{2}  $   concentration. The shifts of diffraction peaks toward higher degree are due to the replacement of the larger I  $  ^{-}  $  anionic with the smaller Br-anionic, which will make the crystal cell shrink. The higher peak intensity may be attributed to better crystallinity of the perovskite film. As shown in Fig. 1, the   $  PbI_{2}  $   impurity peak intensity increases with the concentration of   $  PbBr_{2}  $   in the precursor, which may form a passivating layer at the grain boundaries and should be benefit for photovoltaic performance [43–45].

Fig. 2a shows the UV–vis absorption spectra of a series of perovskite   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{(3-x)}Br_{x}  $   films. The absorption region extends over the full visible spectrum up to 800 nm with two distinct peaks located at around 480 nm and 760 nm. The   $  E_{g}  $   values for   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{(3-x)}Br_{x}  $   perovskite films are in the range of 1.58 and 1.62eV as estimated using Tauc plot. It is clear that the absorption edges display red-shifts linearly with the Br amount. The absorption onsets shift differently with previous reported results, in which light absorption onsets would blue-shift by adding Br element into the precursor [46], that is caused by reduced stress accumulated in the lattice over the Pb–I bond due to larger grain size with increasing   $  PbBr_{2}  $   contents [47,48]. It was also proved by IPCE spectra, as below discussed. Furthermore, the samples with   $  PbBr_{2}  $   show significantly higher absorbance in the entire visible spectrum from 400 to 760 nm, indicating high quality   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{(3-x)}Br_{x}  $   films, in agreement with the XRD results and the following SEM results. According to literature report [44,47–50], the surface effects cause the absorption edge to blue shift, and the red shift is often related to the stress in the film. According to the surface enhancement effect, the radius is r, the surface tension is   $  \sigma  $  , and the generated surface additional pressure   $  \Delta P  $  :

$$
\triangle \mathrm{P} = 2 \sigma / \mathrm{r} = - 3 \triangle \mathrm{a} / \mathrm{a} \mathrm{X} _ {\mathrm{T}}
$$

$$
\triangle \mathrm{a} = - 2 \mathrm{a} \sigma \mathrm{X} _ {\mathrm{T}} / 3 \mathrm{r}
$$

$  X_{T}  $   is an isothermal compression ratio, a is a lattice constant, and   $  \Delta a  $   is a change in lattice constant caused by the presence of surface additional pressure.   $  \sigma  $   and T are physical parameters that are independent of r. The negative sign indicates that the grain compression caused by the surface additional pressure will make the lattice constant smaller,   $  \Delta a < 0  $  . The surface additional pressure   $  \Delta P  $   generated in the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{(3-x)}Br_{x}  $   film with different doping concentration of   $  PbBr_{2}  $   changes, and the absorption edge of the film also moves. The analysis suggests that the increase of stress may be the cause of the red shift of the absorption edge.

The morphology of the perovskite thin films is further evaluated by SEM (Fig. 3). It is well known that the surface coverage of the absorber layer has a great influence on the device performance because of the low surface coverage leading to direct electrical contact between the HTL and the ETL and carrier recombination. Fig. 3 (A-E) shows top-view SEM images of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{(3 - x)}\mathrm{Br}_x $  films prepared with adding different amount of  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  in the precursor solutions. The average grain size does not show remarkable changes with the amount of  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  content in the range from  $ 1\% $  to  $ 5\% $ . However, as the amount of  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  increased to  $ 7.5\% $ , the grain sizes of perovskite films have enlarged obviously. The large grain size may serve to increase the carrier mobility within perovskite film and reduce recombination [56,57]. Fig. 3 (F) shows the cross-sectional SEM image of perovskite solar cells, which consists of a  $ 400~\mathrm{nm} $ -thick perovskite absorber layer with well-defined grains.

X-ray photoluminescence spectroscopy (XPS) was used to further examine the presence of Br in the film. The results of XPS measurements were summarized in Fig. 4. Shown in Fig. 4 are the C 1s, N1s, I 3d, and Pb 4f core level peaks of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{(3 - x)}\mathrm{Br}_x $  films. The C 1s peaks of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{(3 - x)}\mathrm{Br}_x $  film located at  $ 286.02\mathrm{eV} $ , similar to the reported value.  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{(3 - x)}\mathrm{Br}_x $  have peaks assigned to  $ \mathrm{Pb_{4f}} $  and  $ \mathrm{I_{3d}} $ . The resulting spectra revealed no detectable Br signal in  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and the presence of a weak signal in  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{(3 - x)}\mathrm{Br}_x $ , which are in agreement with recent characterizations of Br in  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{(3 - x)}\mathrm{Br}_x $  films. However, although a significant amount of Br atoms was present in the initial precursors, Br could be hardly detected in the resulted film (XPS measurements), pointing toward the loss of Br element during the preparation process, which is similar with the  $ \mathrm{PbCl_2} $  adding into the precursor. The amount of  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  in the film is very small, and it is difficult to detect the bromine peak by using XPS measurement. The weak Br 3d peak indicates the presence of a small amount of bromine in the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{(3 - x)}\mathrm{Br}_x $  film, this conclusion corresponds well with previous reports [58].

Fig. 5 shows thermal analysis of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{(3-x)}\mathrm{Br}_x $  perovskite power. TGA is used to evaluate the thermal stability of Br doped  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  by measuring the mass loss behavior of the individual organic and inorganic components, which make up the building blocks of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{(3-x)}\mathrm{Br}_x $  as a function of temperature. The sublimation behavior of the isolated organic component is examined in depth and used to interpret the TGA data of various perovskite precursor solutions and of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{(3-x)}\mathrm{Br}_x $  powder. TGA spectra of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{(3-x)}\mathrm{Br}_x $  powder confirmed that both of them undergo three-step sublimation, with initial sublimation

temperatures of 216 and 270 °C for   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{(3-x)}Br_{x}  $  , respectively. The material first undergoes 20% mass loss of HI around 270 °C, followed by 6% loss of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{2}  $   component around 337 °C, indicating that the amine group is bound into the perovskite matrix more tightly than HI. Intriguingly, Br doped sample shows high sublimation temperature at the first step, indicating a better thermal stability of Br doped perovskite film.

Their photovoltaic performances have been evaluated by  $ J-V $  test under the simulated sunlight illumination (AM 1.5G, 100 mW/cm $ ^{2} $ ), which has been calibrated with a NREL traceable Si reference solar cell with KG5 filter. The best photovoltaic parameters of a series of CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _{(3-x)} $ Br $ _x $  perovskite films based solar cells have been summarized in Fig. 6a and Table 1. The control device fabricated using the pristine MAPbI $ _3 $  solution without PbBr $ _2 $  exhibits are relatively lower PCE of 14.12%, with  $ J_{sc} $  = 20.64 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , V $ _{oc} $  = 1.06 V and FF = 0.65. Interestingly, when 5% PbBr $ _2 $  added into the CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  solution, the J $ _{sc} $  increases to 23.37 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , V $ _{oc} $  to 1.08V, FF to 0.75, and PCE to 18.97%. It is seen that the performance improvement is mainly due to the increased values of J $ _{sc} $  and FF. Both of them will benefit the charge collection efficiency. The incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) measurement was carried out to investigate the spectrum response of the cell with best performance (as shown in Fig. 6 b). The integrated J $ _{sc} $  value is 21.30 mA/cm $ ^{2} $  for the CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _{(3-x)} $ Br $ _x $  perovskite solar cell, which shows the similar current value as measured by  $ J-V $  scan. When the PbBr $ _2 $  concentration is further increased to 7.5–10%, PCE of the solar cells are dropped to 15.64% and 13.60%, respectively. It is apparent that the optimized PbBr $ _2 $  additive ratio is 5%. Comparing to the reference solar cell prepared without additive, the cell efficiency is increased by 16.99%. We have also studied the  $ J-V $  characteristics of the champion device under both reverse and forward scan modes, as shown in Fig. 6c. This cell shows a PCE of 17.01 (16.68)% with an V $ _{oc} $  of 1.06 (1.04) V, a J $ _{sc} $  of 22.83 (22.75) mA/cm $ ^{2} $ , and a FF of 70 (70)% measured at a reverse (forward) scan. It is noted that our solar cells still exhibit a negligible hysteresis. Increasing the grain size and improving the crystalline quality of perovskite films should be effective approaches to reduce the hysteresis of perovskite solar cells [59–61]. Fig. 6d displays the histogram data of the PCEs of 35 separate solar cells, which were prepared using 5% PbBr $ _2 $  in the precursor and measured under reverse scan mode. The average V $ _{oc} $ , J $ _{sc} $ , FF, and PCE for these 35 solar cells are 1.05 ± 0.02 V, 22.81 ± 0.76 mA/cm $ ^{2} $ , 68.70 ± 2.21, and 16.42 ± 0.42%, respectively. Fig. 6d shows PCEs of 35 individual devices, most of which were in the range of 15–19% with a champion value of 18.97%. We have listed the performance parameters of 20 solar cells with and without adding lead bromide in the precursor solution, as seen in Table S1 and S2. These results demonstrates adding 5% PbBr $ _2 $  into perovskite precursor solution is a highly reproducible method for fabricating CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _{(3-x)} $ Br $ _x $  solar cells with commercially competitive photovoltaic performance. The stability of the CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _{(3-x)} $ Br $ _x $  solar cells were investigated and shown in Fig. 6e. Fig. 6e shows the photovoltaic performance during the long term aging test of the two devices. After 60 days of aging, the initial efficiency of 14.35% of the MAPbI $ _3 $  based cell decreased to 12.36%, giving a 13.87% reduction. The initial efficiency of 17.65% of the CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _{(3-x)} $ Br $ _x $  based cell with 5% PbBr $ _2 $  also decreased to 16.24%, giving a 8% reduction. The improved stability is associated with the better crystallinity and the compact and stable structure.

It can be seen that the TRPL decay times of FTO/TiO $ _2 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  are  $ \tau_1 $ =0.11 ns and  $ \tau_2 $ =0.34 ns, corresponding to amplitudes of 36.54 and 63.46%, respectively. The cells using 5% PbBr $ _2 $  perovskite films, which have the lowest decay times, should have the highest  $ J_{sc} $  and PCE, in perfect agreement with the J-V results.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we have demonstrated a highly reproducible method to fabricate of the high-quality   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{(3-x)}Br_{x}  $   perovskite film for solar cells. The key is addition of   $  PbBr_{2}  $   into the precursor solution, which will affect the crystallization process and results in smooth   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{(3-x)}Br_{x}  $   films with full surface coverage as well as less trap density. In addition, the band gaps of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{(3-x)}Br_{x}  $   films become smaller owing to decreased stress accumulated in the lattice with increasing   $  PbBr_{2}  $   contents. As a result, perovskite solar cells with Br incorporated absorber layers exhibited good reproducibility, improved thermal stability and higher PCE of 18.97% with good thermal stability. These results demonstrate that   $  PbBr_{2}  $   addition should be an effective way to improve the device performance.
