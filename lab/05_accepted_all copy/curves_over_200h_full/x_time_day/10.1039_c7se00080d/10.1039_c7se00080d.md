# High efficient and stable perovskite solar cell prepared from an in-situ pre-wetted  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  nano-sheet array film

Xuhui Zhang  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Zhaoqian Li  $  ^{a}  $  , Yong Ding  $  ^{c}  $  , Linhua Hu  $  ^{a}  $  , Jiajiu Ye  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Xu Pan  $  ^{*a}  $   and Songyuan Dai  $  ^{*c}  $

$  ^{a}  $   Key Laboratory of Novel Thin-Film Solar Cells, Institute of Applied Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Hefei 230088, China. *E-mail: xpan@rntek.cas.cn.

b. University of Science and Technology of China, Hefei 230026, China.

c. State Key Laboratory of Alternate Electrical Power System with Renewable Energy Sources, North China Electric Power University, Beijing 102206, China. *E-mail: sydai@ncepu.edu.cn.

# Abstract:

As its good pore-filling and controllable morphology, two-step deposition was widely used in the perovskite film preparation. Whereas, the initial   $  PbI_{2}  $   film for two-step deposition was usually dense, crystalline and layered, leading to a poor quality perovskite film with an amount of   $  PbI_{2}  $   residue, thus resulting in an inferior stability and low power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the device. In this work, we developed an in-situ pre-wetted   $  PbI_{2}  $   nano-sheet array film to achieve high-quality   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) perovskite films via two-step deposition. By introducing a small amount of hydrogen chloride isopropanol solution (IPA HCl) into the   $  PbI_{2}  $   precursor solution, an in-situ pre-wetted   $  PbI_{2}  $   nano-sheet array film was obtained, then a smooth, continuous, uniform, dense, and   $  PbI_{2}  $  -free perovskite film was formed by reacting with   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $ . After optimization, device prepared by using the in-situ pre-wetted   $  PbI_{2}  $   nano-sheet array film achieved a promising PCE of 19.19%. Moreover, it also had a good reproducibility and well long term stability, the devices based on the in-situ pre-wetted   $  PbI_{2}  $   nano-sheet array film remained over 80% of the initial PCE after espousing in the ambient air for two months.

Since the first breaking reported by Miyasaka in 2009, $ ^{1} $  perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted great scientific and technological interest due to its excellent performance and simple process. $ ^{2-5} $  Over the past 7 years, the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has increased from  $ 3.8\% $  to  $ 22.1\% $ . $ ^{1,6-12} $  The impressive photovoltaic performance can be attributed to the material's intriguing optoelectronic properties, such as a relative suitable bandgap, high charge carrier mobility, long carrier diffusion length, and high absorption coefficient and lifetime. $ ^{13-17} $

As one of the most classic organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (  $  MAPbI_{3}  $  ) has been frequently used as light absorber in the PSCs. The film quality of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   absorber is very crucial to the photovoltaic performance of PSCs.  $  ^{18}  $   Currently, various processes have been developed to improve the crystallinity, uniformity, coverage, and morphology of the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   layer, such as one-step approach,  $  ^{19-21}  $   two-step approach,  $  ^{22-24}  $   vapor approach  $  ^{8,25}  $   and vapor-assisted solution approach.  $  ^{26,27}  $   Two-step approach for   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskites preparation was initially adapted by Michael Grätzel and his colleagues to fabricate mesoscopic PSCs,  $  ^{22}  $   in which   $  PbI_{2}  $   film was first coated on the substrate and then reacted with   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (MAI). However,   $  PbI_{2}  $   from the initial dimethyl formamide (DMF) solution would form a dense crystalline and layered film on the substrate,  $  ^{28,29}  $   which is hard for the conversion of   $  PbI_{2}  $   and formed a poor morphology of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   film, leading to two main problems.  $  ^{30,31}  $   First, an uncontrollable surface morphology and crystal size of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   are unconducive to the improvement of PCE and reproducibility for the PSCs. Second, the residual   $  PbI_{2}  $   in   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   films also has a negative impact on the reproducibility and stability of devices. Various methods have been developed to improve the quality of   $  PbI_{2}  $   precursor films, such as adding additives (4-tert-butylpyridine,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $ ,   $  H_{2}O  $   or hydrochloric acid) into   $  PbI_{2}  $   precursor solution,  $  ^{30,32-34}  $   careful thermal or gas-quenching treatment,  $  ^{35,36}  $   synthesising nanoporous   $  PbI_{2}  $ ,  $  ^{37}  $   using mixed solvent or mixed   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  PbCl_{2}  $   precursor solution.  $  ^{29,38}  $   By means of all these methods, the crystallinity of   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer was decreased and adjustable morphology was obtained, which could make a better conversion from   $  PbI_{2}  $   to   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ , and precisely control of the final morphology

Herein, we developed an in-situ pre-wetted   $  PbI_{2}  $   nano-sheet array film (HP-  $  PbI_{2}  $  ) to achieve high-quality   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) perovskite films via two-step deposition. By introducing a small amount of hydrogen chloride isopropanol solution (IPA HCl) into the   $  PbI_{2}  $   precursor solution, an in-situ pre-wetted   $  PbI_{2}  $   nano-sheet array film was obtained. This novel porous   $  PbI_{2}  $   nano-sheet array film could accelerat the reaction between   $  PbI_{2}  $   and MAI, then a smooth, continuous, uniform, dense and   $  PbI_{2}  $ -free MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film was prepared. Compared to the devices prepared from the conventional   $  PbI_{2}  $   film, the PCE was enhanced from 12.89% to 19.19% under optimum addition amount of IPA HCl. Furthermore, the stability and consistency of the devices also had been improved significantly.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1 Materials and Reagents

Fluorine-doped   $  SnO_{2}  $  -coated transparent conducting glass (FTO, 15 Ω per square) was obtained from Pilkington TEC.   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (MAI, 99%) and 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Titanium isopropoxide (98%), lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulphonyl) imide (Li-TFSI, 99.99%) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP, 96%) were bought from Aldrich. Bis (acetylacetonate) (99%), isopropanol (IPA, 99.7%), N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.9%) and hydrogen chloride isopropanol solution (IPA HCl, for GC, ~1.25?M (T)) were purchased from J&K Chemicals.   $  TiO_{2}  $   (particle size: about 30 nm, crystalline phase: anatase) was got from Dysol. Lead(II) iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  , 99.999%) was purchased from TCI. All chemicals were used as received without further purification.

A mesoporous TiO₂ layer (100-150 nm in thickness) was then deposited on the c-TiO₂/FTO substrate by spin coating at 5000 rpm for 30 s. For depositing the MAPbI₃ perovskite layer, first a layer of PbI₂ was spin-coated on top of mesoporous TiO₂ layer from 1.0 M of PbI₂ DMF precursor solution (with or without IPA HCl additive) at 2500 rpm for 30 s, then a 45 mg mL⁻¹ of MAI IPA solution was spin coated on the PbI₂ film at 2500 rpm for 30 s immediately. The deposited film was then annealed at 105 °C for 60 min to form the perovskite layer. The HTM layer was deposited onto the perovskite layer by spin-coating at 2500 rpm for 20 s, using a solution of spiro-MeOTAD, 4-tert-butylpyridine, and lithium bis (trifluoromethylsulphonyl) imide and the Co (III)-complex. Finally, a 60-80-nm-thick Au counterelectrode was deposited by thermally evaporating on the top of the device.

# 2.3 Measurement and Characterization

Photocurrent and voltage curves (J-V) were measured with a solar simulator equipped with 450 W xenon lamp (AAA simulator, Oriel USA) and a Keithley 2400 source meter. Light intensity was adjusted with an NREL-calibrated Si solar cell with a KG-2 filter for approximating 1 sun light intensity (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). While measuring current and voltage, the cell was covered with a black mask to define the active area of the devices, and this case was 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  . The incident monochromatic photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) was measured using an IPCE measuring system (Newport Corporation, CA) equipped with a Xe lamp as the light source. Time-resolved photoluminescence (TR-PL) spectra were collected using a transient state spectrophotometer (F900, Edinburgh Instruments). Samples were excited with a 660 nm pulsed diode laser with a repetition rate of 2.5 MHz and an excitation intensity of about 14 nJ cm  $  ^{-2}  $  . Steady-state fluorescence (PL) spectra were recorded by exciting the perovskite films without or with TiO  $  _{2}  $  layer at 473 nm with a standard 450 W Xenon CW lamp. The signal were recorded by a spectrofluorometer (photon technology

Since the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite film prepared by the HP- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , a uniform, density and homogeneous film was obtained (Figure 4c and 4d). This change can be ascribed to the following reason. The MAI droplet will react with  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  grains quickly when it contacts to the surface of the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film. For the C- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , it was dense and fast crystallized, the perovskite ripening process only occurs on the surface of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film and periphery of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  grains. Moreover, during the perovskite ripening process, there was about a great volume expansion appeared for the material because the density of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film is only  $ 4.29~\mathrm{g / cm^3} $  while the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film is  $ 6.16~\mathrm{g / cm^3} $ . As the C- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  was too dense, there was no space for horizontal volume expansion of perovskite ripening process. Then the volume expansion was most occurred at vertical direction of the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film. Hence, there were huge amounts of uneven grains on the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film prepared by the C- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . In contrary, for the HP- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , it was homogeneous, porous and in-situ pre-wetted by the IPA, which could be infiltrated by the MAI sufficiently. After annealing, a high  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  quality perovskite film was formed. For the mesoscopic PSCs, a poor quality of perovskite film results in poor light absorption and increase the charge recombination, so the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film prepared by HP- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  is more suitable for fabrication of high performance PSCs.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, we have developed an in-situ pre-wetted  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  nano-sheet array film to achieve high-quality  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  (MAPbI₃) perovskite films via two-step deposition. By introducing a small amount of IPA HCl into the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  precursor solutions, an in-situ pre-wetted  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  nano-sheet array film was obtained. HCl solution have a positive impact on the formation of the perovskite film. Through the IPA indiffusion channels in the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film, MAI could diffuse to the interior and bottom of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film rapidly. Meanwhile, the existence of HCl broke the continuous   $  PbI_{2}  $   crystals in   $  PbI_{2}  $   film into   $  PbI_{2}  $   nano-sheet arrays. The ordered   $  PbI_{2}  $   crystal structure and good permeation of MAI in the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film can make the reaction between the   $  PbI_{2}  $   and MAI completely and homogeneously. After reacting with the MAI, a smooth, continuous, uniform, dense, and   $  PbI_{2}  $  -free perovskite film was formed. The influence of the addition of IPA HCl on the formation rate, morphology, chemical composition, crystallization, absorbance, and carrier transportation properties of the perovskite films were systemically investigated. With the optimized amount of IPA HCl which 100   $  \mu  $  L of IPA HCl added in 1 mL of   $  PbI_{2}  $   DMF precursor solution, a promising PCE of 19.19% was achieved with the photovoltaic performance such as   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.12 V,   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.52 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , FF of 0.77, which was much higher than the devices prepared from the conventional   $  PbI_{2}  $   film. Additionally, the devices fabricated by using in-situ pre-wetted   $  PbI_{2}  $   nano-sheet array films showed a very good long-term stability in ambient air due to its   $  PbI_{2}  $  -free and high quality perovskite film. It is expected that our work gives a simple and rapid way to prepare the stable and high efficiency PSCs.
