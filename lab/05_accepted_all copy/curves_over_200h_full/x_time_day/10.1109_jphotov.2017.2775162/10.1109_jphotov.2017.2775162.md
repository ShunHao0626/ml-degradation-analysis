# Controllable Crystal Film Growth via Appropriate Substrate-Preheating Treatment for Perovskite Solar Cells Using Mixed Lead Sources

Yaling Wang  $  ^{ID}  $  , Shaowei Liu, Qi Zeng, Rui Wang, Wenjing Qin, Huanqi Cao, Liying Yang  $  ^{ID}  $  , Lan Li, Weiwei Ji, and Shougen Yin

Abstract—We employed mixed   $  PbCl_{2}:PbAc_{2}  $   as lead sources and followed the two-step sequential deposition method to prepare the perovskite films. We focused on the influence of substrate-preheating treatment on crystal growth, morphology, optical and electrical properties of the final perovskite film, as well as the device performance. A combination of characterizations including scanning electron microscope (SEM), conducting atomic force microscopy (C-AFM), energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX), X-ray diffraction (XRD), UV–vis absorption, time-resolved photoluminescence spectra (TRPL), and surface photovoltage spectroscopy (SPS) was utilized to investigate the effect of substrate-preheating treatment. It was demonstrated that appropriate substrate-preheating treatment could realize controllable perovskite crystallization, leading to high-quality perovskite film with improved optical and electrical properties. The devices with the optimized perovskite films exhibited good reproducibility and stability, delivering an ideal PCE of 15.37%, with decent FF of 73.84%, high Voc of 0.98 V, and Jsc of 21.24 mA/cm².

Index Terms—Crystal film growth, morphology, perovskite solar cells, substrate-preheating.

# I. INTRODUCTION

ORGANIC–INORGANIC hybrid perovskite materials are considered to be the most promising candidates for the next generation solar cells owning to many advantages, such as good light harvesting, long charge carrier diffusion and lifetime, solution processability, and so on [1]–[5]. Within the past six

years, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells has been improved from original 3.8% to 22.1% [6]–[9], which is expected to achieve commercial production in the near future.

Up to now, the most successfully and generally used perovskite materials for photovoltaic applications are methylammonium lead halide perovskite (MAPbI $ _3 $ ), which is obtained by methyl-ammonium iodide (MAI), and lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ ), as well as its mixed-halide analogue MAPbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $ , synthesized by either replacing PbI $ _2 $  with lead chloride (PbCl $ _2 $ ) or adopting methyl-ammonium chloride (MACl) instead of MAI. How to control the perovskite crystallization such as operating the nucleation dynamic and crystal growth to obtain high quality perovskite films is a very crucial issue, which is essential for efficient perovskite solar cells, especially for planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells [10], [11]. A large number of research work have been reported to improve the quality of perovskite films and device performance by various processing techniques, regulating precursor composition, solvent engineering, incorporating additives, and so on [12]–[18].

According to the previous reports, incorporating Cl can affect the perovskite crystal growth and improve the film morphology [12]. The resulting  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  perovskite had higher electrical conductivity [19] and much longer carriers diffusion length in the  $ \mu \mathrm{m} $  range compared with the pure  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite with only about  $ 100\mathrm{nm} $  [1], [20]. Snaith et al. declared that an ultrasmooth and pinhole-free perovskite film could be synthesized using lead acetate  $ (\mathrm{PbAc}_2) $  [21]. They demonstrated that the byproduct MAAc was thermally unstable and was much easier to be removed than byproduct MAI and MACl, thus leading to much higher nucleation density and accelerating the perovskite crystallization [21]-[23]. However, the resulting perovskite film was comprised of small grain size because of the too fast crystal growth rate, which might increase the bulk defects and the traps at grain boundaries [12], [24]. Because the byproducts dependent on the anion type may influence the crystallization kinetics and the final film morphology. It is a reasonable design principle to employ  $ \mathrm{PbCl}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{PbAc}_2 $  as mixed lead sources, which combines the advantages of different anions while avoids their drawbacks to achieve controllable perovskite crystallization for improvement in film morphology and device performance. Controllable perovskite crystallization can be realized through moderate substrate-preheating treatment. Consequently, the well reproducible, smooth, and compact perovskite thin films with large grain size and almost pinhole free were easily obtained. # II. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

As the previous literature reported, both  $ \mathrm{PbAc_2} $  and Cl incorporation have influence on the perovskite crystal growth [25], [26]. In this study, we first investigate the influence of different  $ \mathrm{PbCl_2} $ :  $ \mathrm{PbAc_2} $  ratio in the first-step precursor solution consisting of  $ x\mathrm{MPbCl}_2 $ ,  $ y\mathrm{MPbAc}_2 $ , and 1 M MAI (here,  $ x + y = 1 $ ) on the perovskite films morphology. By

comparing the scanning electron microscope (SEM), X-ray diffraction (XRD), and UV–vis test results (see Figs. S1 and Fig. S2 in Supplementary Material), the optimized   $  PbCl_{2}  $  :   $  PbAc_{2}  $  : MAI ratio in the first-step precursor solution is determined to be 0.5: 0.5: 1, presenting ideal morphology of the final perovskite film, with relatively large crystal grain size and fewer pinhole. Based on the optimized   $  PbCl_{2}  $  :   $  PbAc_{2}  $   ratio in the first-step precursor solution, the influence of substrate-preheating treatment on perovskite crystal film growth, optical and electrical properties of perovskite film, and device performance was investigated systematically.

# A. Perovskite Crystal Film Growth

We fabricated a series of perovskite films through a two-step sequential deposition method. In the first-step precursor solution with   $  PbCl_{2}  $  ,   $  PbAc_{2}  $  , and MAI in molar ratio of 0.5:0.5:1, the three solutes   $  PbCl_{2}  $  ,   $  PbAc_{2}  $  , and MAI will combine into   $  PbCl_{2}\bullet MAI  $   and   $  PbAc_{2}\bullet MAI  $   through formation of coordinative bonds. When the first-step precursor solution is spin-coated on the substrate, byproducts MAAc and MACl are liable to release out. Therefore, the substrate-preheating temperature directly affects the properties of the intermediate phase films obtained after the first-step spin-coating process, which further influences the performance of the final perovskite films obtained after the second-step spin-coating and annealing processes. The photos of the intermediate phase films and correspondingly final perovskite films with different substrate-preheating treatment are shown in Fig. S3 (available in Supplementary Material).

A combination of SEM, energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX), and XRD characterizations was utilized to evaluate the effect of substrate-preheating treatment on the intermediate phase films. For reference, the sample without substrate preheating (room temperature) was also fabricated. From the SEM images (see Fig. 1) and XRD patterns (see Fig. S4 in Supplementary Material), the reference sample without preheating (room temperature) has dense and smooth surface morphology. Gradually, very tiny holes appear in the samples stemmed from substrate-preheating at low temperature (60 °C and 90 °C), while the film surface still remains dense and smooth. Combined with the XRD patterns, almost no diffraction signal are detected except for the diffraction peaks of ITO, which indicates that no crystal is formed and the PbCl₂, PbAc₂, and MAI precursors exist in the form of PbCl₂•MAI and PbAc₂•MAI coordination compounds so that the intermediate phase film has the amorphous feature. As the substrate-preheating temperature rises to 110 °C and 130 °C, the intermediate phase still retains the amorphous characteristic, while the tiny holes appearing in film surface become increasingly large and obvious. The morphology change can be attributed to the different extent of the generation and sublimation of the byproducts MAX (X = Ac or Cl). When substrate-preheating temperature is higher, because of the thermal driving force, the byproducts of MAAc and MACl are more liable to be generated and sublimated from the intermediate phase films during the spin-coating process. According to the above-mentioned analysis, adopting the mixed leads sources as the first-step precursors, the effect of substrate preheating should not be ignored. Both either too low or too high preheating temperature is detrimental to the full conversion of perovskite. Too low temperature leads to a uniform and dense intermediate phase film, not favorable to the infiltration of MAI and the crystal growth in the following procedure. While too high temperature results in the formation of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  crystals, which also causes the incomplete perovskite transformation with  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  residual in the final film. Appropriate substrate-preheating temperature facilitates the full conversion of perovskite and enhances the crystallinity, contributing to the high quality perovskite film with large grain size and no pinhole. Therefore, the perovskite crystal film growth can be effectively regulated via the substrate-preheating treatment.

Fig. S6 shows the optical absorption spectra and thickness of the perovskite films with substrate-preheating treatment at different temperatures. The reference film with 102 nm thickness shows relatively weak absorption, especially in the long

Correspondingly, the absorbance is also enhanced, covering the entire visible region with an onset at about 775 nm, in accordance with the previous reported absorption spectrum of the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  [34], [35]. The improved absorption in long wavelength benefits from the increased film thickness. The reason for the increased thickness is probably because of the promoted perovskite conversion and enhanced crystallinity. However, the thickness and the absorbance drop apparently for the sample with substrate preheating at either 150 °C or higher, which mainly attribute to the incomplete perovskite conversion resulted from the PbI  $  _{2}  $  residual in the final perovskite films.

Fig. S7 displays the time-resolved photoluminescence spectra (TRPL) at 765 nm for the perovskite films from substrate-preheating treatment at different temperatures. The PL lifetime was fitted with a double exponential decay function, containing two decay processes. Generally, the fast decay (  $  \tau_{1}  $  ) is related to either the charge transfer or extraction from the perovskite active layer to the charge transport layers. While the slow decay (  $  \tau_{2}  $  ) is dominated by the radiative recombination of free charges, which reflects the nonradiative recombination in the perovskite material. All perovskite samples were deposited on glass substrates. The   $  \tau_{2}  $   values for the samples stemmed from substrate preheating at room temperature 60 °C, 90 °C, 110 °C, 130 °C, and 150 °C are 15.9 ns, 20.3 ns, 30.9 ns, 43.1 ns, 68.5 ns and 33.5 ns, respectively. It is clearly found that as the preheating temperature rises, the PL lifetime of the resultant perovskite film increases. Especially, the perovskite film with appropriate substrate-preheating treatment at 130 °C has the longest carrier lifetime, which mainly benefits from the enhanced crystallinity and enlarged grain size. The increased carrier lifetime indicates longer charge length and fewer trap states, which facilitates the charge transport and contributes to the improvement in Jsc and FF. However, the PL lifetime of the perovskite film from preheating at higher temperature (150 °C) decreases sharply, suggesting intensified nonradiative recombination. It may be caused by the residual PbI  $  _{2}  $   generated in the intermediate phase, which leads to the increase of film defects intensity.

Conducting atomic force microscopy (C-AFM) and surface photovoltage spectroscopy (SPS) measurements were carried out to further investigate the effects of substrate-preheating treatment on electrical properties of perovskite films and device performance. Fig. S8 (available in Supplementary Material) shows the C-AFM images of the perovskite films on the substrates with preheating treatment at different temperature, wherein the bright features are attributed to the conductive perovskite crystalline regions with high crystallinity, while the dark features correspond to the regions with poor conductivity such as the grain boundaries. The potential value is a comprehensive reflection of the film conductivity. The reference sample has a low potential value of 0.98 mV with bright spots distributed unevenly. As the substrate-preheating temperature rises from 60 °C to 130 °C, the potential value of the resultant perovskite film increases from 1.92 to 11.3 mV, and the bright spots also distribute more uniformly, indicating better local conductivity. Moreover, the photovoltaic properties of these samples were also investigated by SPS measurement. SPS is an excellent technique used to explore the separation, transport, and recombination of photo-generated electron-hole pairs in the system   $  [36]  $  . Fig. 4 shows the surface photovoltage response in the spectral region of 400–1000 nm for the samples with different substrate-preheating treatment. The sample from substrate-preheating at 130 °C shows the strongest photovoltaic response with the largest threshold values and the highest intensity. Theoretically, the surface photovoltage is generated by the separation of photo-generated electrons and holes in the system. Both C-AFM and SPS results prove that the electrical properties of pervoskite film depend on the substrate-preheating temperature to a large extent. Appropriate substrate-preheating treatment facilitates the crystal growth and perovskite conversion, endowing the final perovskite film with fewer bulk defects and optimal morphology. Therefore, the perovskite film from appropriate substrate-preheating exhibits better electrical properties.

When the sweep rate is faster than the response time of device, the observed result no longer reflect the real steady-state performance, and the scan direction can also further influence the hysteresis phenomenon. Consequently, in order to obtain the accurate test results close to the steady-state power output, in this study, the  $ J-V $  curves measurements were conducted with a slower sweep rate of 10 mV/s for both forward and reverse scan. Under illumination, the devices based on substrate-preheating at 130 °C exhibit the best performance with average Voc of  $ (0.97 \pm 0.01) $  V, Jsc of  $ (20.95 \pm 0.80) $  mA/cm $ ^{2} $ , FF of  $ (71.01 \pm 2.43)\% $ , and PCE of  $ (14.39 \pm 0.57)\% $ , which benefit from the controllable crystallization of perovskite and the resultant high-quality perovskite films. The  $ J-V $  curves comparison of the best device for each condition is presented in Fig. 5(b). Fig. 5(d) shows the histograms of device efficiency measured for 32 separate cells fabricated under the optimized condition, presenting good reproducibility in efficient perovskite solar cells. The champion device shows the PCE of 15.37% with the decent FF of 73.84%, high Voc of 0.98 V, and Jsc of 21.24 mA/cm $ ^{2} $ . No distinct hysteresis can be found between the forward scan and reverse scan  $ J-V $  curves, which are shown in Fig. 5(c), reflecting the fewer trap states, better interfacial contact, and charge transport. The corresponding incident photocurrent conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra of the champion device is depicted in Fig. S9 (available in Supplementary Material). The IPCE spectra show efficiencies above 80% in the wavelength range between 400 to 600 nm, giving the integrated Jsc of 19.88 mA/cm $ ^{2} $ , which is closed to the Jsc value calculated from the  $ J-V $  curve. The enhanced Jsc and FF are also attributed to the improved conductivity and reduced defects of the active layer, which benefits from the optimized perovskite film via appropriate substrate-preheating treatment.

# III. CONCLUSION

In summary, a series of perovskite films were prepared through a two-step sequential deposition method using mixed   $  PbCl_{2}:PbAc_{2}  $   as lead source. The influence of substrate-preheating treatment on crystal growth, morphology, optical and electrical properties of the final perovskite film, and device performance were systematically investigated. A combination of SEM, EDX, and XRD measurements proved that the substrate-preheating temperature had significant effect on the intermediate phase film, further affecting the final perovskite film. Appropriate substrate-preheating temperature facilitates the full conversion of perovskite and enhances the crystallinity, realizing the controllable crystallization. The final perovskite film from the substrate-preheating treatment at 130 °C showed the ideal morphology with large grain size and no pinhole, as well as the optimal thickness and absorbance. TRPL demonstrated that the perovskite film with appropriate substrate-preheating has longer carrier lifetime. C-AFM and SPS results also suggested the optimized perovskite film had better local conductivity and strong surface photovoltage response, indicating fewer defects and reduced charge recombination. Compared with the reference devices, the optimal devices based on the substrate-preheating treatment exhibited good reproducibility, enhanced performance, and stability, delivering a champion PCE of 15.37% with decent FF of 73.84%, Jsc of 21.24 mA/cm², and Voc of 0.98 V. The improvement were mainly attributed to the controllable crystal film growth and the consequent high-quality perovskite film.
