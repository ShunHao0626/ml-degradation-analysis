# Abstract:

Fullerene derivatives have been proved to be useful as additives into active layer to improve perovskite solar cells (PSCs) performance in the power conversion efficiency, stability and reproducibility. In this work, to unravel the influences of chemical compositions of fullerene derivatives on the PSCs performance, we systematically compare a series of fullerene-derivatives additives, including  $  C_{60}  $ , PCBM and  $  C_{60}  $ -Taurine. Two important conclusions are achieved: (i) the bare  $  C_{60}  $  cage primarily plays a role to accelerate the electron extraction and transport; (ii) the side chains adhered to the  $  C_{60}  $  cage could further help to improve the stability of PSCs by reducing the defect states and improving the perovskite crystalline quality. It is

Journal Pre-proof

also noteworthy that these two goals may be hard to be simultaneously achieved because the insulation property of side chains leads to the decline of electron extraction capacity. Therefore, an intelligent design of fullerene derivatives as the additives is strongly suggested to balance the stability and PCE.

# 1 Introduction

In recent years, organic-inorganic halide perovskites have attracted more and more attention for solar cell application owe to their excellent inherent nature, including direct bandgap, high absorption coefficient, high charge carrier mobility, low exciton binding energy and soluble processing[1-3]. However, it is easy to form defect states on the surface or at grain boundaries of the solution-prepared perovskite film. These defect states inevitably incur non-radiative recombination loss, decrease carrier lifetime, and hence limit power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). What's more, the perovskite film with defects states is also more sensitive to moisture and oxygen, which accelerates the degradation of PSCs[4, 5]. Therefore, high quality fabrication of and defect suppression inside perovskite films are essential for achieving highly efficient and stable PSCs.

Many efforts have been done to increase the crystal quality of and reduce the undesirable defect states of perovskite film by adding external additives. It is well known that fullerene or fullerene derivatives have been implemented as electron transporting layer (ETL) covering perovskite films in inverted PSCs because of their high charge carrier mobility[6-9], low chemical capacitance[10-12] and strong passivation effect on defect states[13, 14]. What's more, some investigations have indicated that the perovskite-fullerene bulk heterojunction (BHJ)[15-28] is also an effective way to tune crystallization quality, passivate defect states, and increase electron transport of perovskite films. For example, the  $ C_{60} $  as additive has been

Journal Pre-proof

introduced into perovskite active layer to tune its crystallization process and passivate its grain boundaries, and hence reduce hysteresis and improve stability of PSCs [23]. Also, the PCBM incorporating into perovskite can reduce hysteresis and charge carrier recombination loss by filling pinholes and vacancies of the grain boundary[24], passivating iodide-rich trap sites and reducing anion migration[25]. The  $ \alpha $ -bis-PCBM is an additive better than PCBM in enlarging perovskite grain size, passivating interface trap states at grain boundaries, accelerating carrier separation and transportation, and thus give a substantially larger enhancement in efficiency and stability[27]. The fluoroalkyl-substituted fullerene (DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $ ) incorporated perovskite active layer also achieve a high efficiency and ambient stability PSCs because DF- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  effectively passivate the defects of perovskite film and facilitating the charge transport/collection[28]. Based on these investigations, it is found that the resulting influence on optoelectronic properties of PSCs is differential for different fullerene species. This mean that the side chain grafted onto the fullerene cage also plays an important role in boosting the photovoltaic performance of PSCs. Therefore, it is necessary to clarify the respective role of the  $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  cage and the grafted side chain, and then unravel the effect mechanism of  $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  derivatives as additives in active layer for developing more effective fullerene additives.

It is well known that the organic molecules containing S, N or O donor can significantly passivate defect states and improve crystallization quality of perovskite films[29-32]. Additionally, the alkali cations  $ (\mathrm{Rb}^{+}, \mathrm{Na}^{+}, \mathrm{K}^{+}) $  with a smaller ionic radius can raise the migration energy barrier for native halide defects by occupying

Journal Pre-proof

the interstitial sites of the perovskite lattice and thus suppress finally the ion migration [33]. Therefore, in this work, we synthesize a fullerene derivative containing N, O, S atoms and  $ \mathrm{Na^{+}} $  simultaneously by the reaction of  $ \mathrm{C_{60}} $  dissolved in toluene with the excess of  $ \mathrm{NH_2(CH_2)_2SO_3Na^+} $  (named as  $ \mathrm{C_{60} - Ta} $ ), and then add it into perovskite precursor to afford the perovskite-fullerene BHJ structure. As a comparison, PCBM and pristine  $ \mathrm{C_{60}} $  are also investigated as acceptors in perovskite-fullerene BHJ structure to understand the role played by the  $ \mathrm{C_{60}} $  cage and the grafted side chain. The results indicate that the  $ \mathrm{C_{60}} $  cage mainly facilitate the carrier separation and increase electron extraction and transport inside perovskite films, while the grafted side chain mainly improve the crystal quality of perovskite film, enlarge grain size and reduce grain boundaries. Because of the insulation property, on the other hand, the grafted side weakens the electron transfer and transport performance. Accordingly, PSCs with  $ \mathrm{C_{60} - Ta} $  as additive achieved a PCE of  $ 16.46\% $ , which is slightly less than the  $ 16.59\% $  of the  $ \mathrm{C_{60}} $  PSC, but higher than the  $ 15.94\% $  of PCBM PSC. Therefore, choosing the side chain with high electrical conductivity, good hydrophobic property and excellent passivation effect is crucial. This work provide an important guidance for developing more effective fullerene additives.

# 2.2 Synthesis of  $ \mathrm{C_{60} - Ta} $

$  C_{60}  $  -Ta was synthesized by the following method as presented in Fig. 1. Taurine (191 mg, 1.527 mmol) and NaOH (61.6 mg, 1.540 mmol) were dissolved in water (5 ml), and ethanol (45 ml), and the mixture solution was added dropwise to a solution of   $  C_{60}  $   (110 mg, 0.153 mmol) in toluene (80 ml). Then, the mixture was heated at 80°C under a nitrogen atmosphere for 24 h. A brown-black precipitate was precipitated from the reactant mixture. We collected the precipitate and dried it with vacuum drying oven under 80°C. Finally, the unreacted   $  C_{60}  $   was removed by purification with toluene (2×30 ml). The final product was characterized by Fourier infrared absorption spectra (FTIR) (Fig. 1 and Fig. S1).

# 2.3 Device fabrication

$ \mathrm{C_{60} - Ta} $ ,  $ \mathrm{C_{60}} $  and PCBM were separately dissolved in DMSO solution (1.5 mg/ml, 0.1mg/ml, 3.5 mg/ml) and filtered with  $ 0.25\mu \mathrm{m} $  filters. The precursor solution of perovskite  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  was prepared by dissolving  $ 0.3482\mathrm{g} $  of MAI and  $ 0.922\mathrm{gPbI}_2 $  (MAI:  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 = 1.095:1 $ ) into the mixture solution of DMF and DMSO (including  $ \mathrm{C_{60}} $

derivatives). ETL was prepared by dissolving PCBM into chlorobenzene (20 mg/ml) and the buffer layer was prepared by dissolving Bphen into ethyl alcohol (0.7 mg/ml).

The patterned indium tin oxide (ITO) glass substrates were sequentially ultrasonic treated with deionized water, ethanol, acetone and isopropanol. Drying the ITO glass substrate and then treated them in an ultraviolet-ozone chamber for 5 min. Next, PEDOT:PSS (filtered by a 0.45 μm filters) was spin on the treated glass substrates at 5500 rounds per minute (rpm) for 30 s and then annealed the spin-coated films for 15 minutes at 130°C. The mixed perovskite precursor solution was spin-coated on the PEDOT:PSS layer at 6000 (rpm) for 30 s in the glovebox, during the spin-coating process 8-10 s, 2-butyl alcohol (250 μl) served as an anti-solvent to drop on the perovskite active layer. The obtained perovskite films were annealed at 90°C for 30s in the glovebox and then annealed at 100°C in ambient air for 20 min and continuously annealed in DMSO atmosphere at 100°C for 15 min. PCBM solution (25 μl) and Bphen solution (25 μl) were sequentially spin-coated at 3000 rpm and 6000 rpm for 30 s, respectively. Finally, Ag electrode (100 nm) was deposited by vacuum thermal evaporation.

# 3.2 The influence of   $  C_{60}  $   derivatives on photovoltaic performance of PSCs

The p-i-n planar heterojunction PSC with the architecture of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/Perovskite/PCBM/Bphen/Ag were adopted, as shown in Fig. 2(a). Herein, PEDOT:PSS is used as hole transport layer (HTL). Perovskite photoactive layers with and without fullerene additive were produced by a one-step method. PCBM serves as ETL, Bphen works as cathode interfacial buffer layer. The energy level structure of the prepared PSC is displayed in Fig. 2(b). One can see that the energy levels of  $  C_{60}  $  and  $  C_{60}  $ -Ta are more matching with that of perovskite compared with PCBM, which could facilitate the electron transfer from perovskite to fullerene more effectively, and thus reduce the electron-hole recombination possibility and lengthen carrier lifetimes. In addition,  $  C_{60}  $ -Ta has better hole blocking ability than  $  C_{60}  $  and PCBM.

# 3.3 The role of   $  C_{60}  $   derivatives as additive in PSCs

In order to understand the effect of   $  C_{60}  $   derivatives on the photovoltaic performance of PSCs and clarify the exact role of   $  C_{60}  $   cage and the grafted side chain, the interaction between   $  C_{60}-Ta  $   and   $  PbI_{2}  $   was investigated using FTIR, as displayed in Fig. 4a and b. It can be seen from Fig. 4a that the S=O vibration peak shifts from 1115.3, 1210.4 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   to 1111.3, 1199.4 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   and the   $  C_{60}-N  $   vibration peak from 1223 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   to 1212.5 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   upon interaction between   $  C_{60}-Ta  $   as Lewis base and   $  PbI_{2}  $   as Lewis acid, which is an obvious indicative of the formation of an intermediate adduct   $  C_{60}-Ta-PbI_{2}  $  . This adduct could control perovskite crystal speed and then improve crystallization quality of perovskite films. However, it can be observed from Fig. 4b

Journal Pre-proof

that the character peaks of   $  C_{60}  $   cage located at 525 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   is unchanged, indicating that   $  C_{60}  $   cage may be not sensitive to   $  PbI_{2}  $  . To verify the above conclusions, X-ray diffraction (XRD) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) were further measured. From Fig. 4(c), the 2θ values have no obvious shift and no new diffraction peaks appear in the XRD patterns of perovskite-fullerene BHJ films, indicating that the addition of fullerene has no influence on the lattice structure of perovskite film, that is,   $  C_{60}  $   derivatives could distribute in the crystal boundary rather than into the crystal lattice of perovskite. But it is found that the main XRD peaks (110) of perovskite film located at 14.20° are obviously strengthened and its full widths at half maximum (FWHM) (see Fig. S5) become smaller from 0.0914° of perovskite, to 0.0869° of perovskite-  $  C_{60}  $  -Ta, 0.0909° of perovskite-  $  C_{60}  $   and 0.0901° of perovskite-PCBM. Based on the SEM results, as shown in Fig. 4(d)-(g), it is found the average grain size of perovskite gradually grows up from 562 nm of perovskite, to 765 nm of perovskite-  $  C_{60}  $  -Ta, 620 nm of perovskite-  $  C_{60}  $  , and 691 nm of perovskite-PCBM. The results show that the   $  C_{60}  $   cage slightly increase the grain size, while   $  C_{60}  $   derivatives can more effectively control the nucleation and crystalline of the perovskite due to the existence of the side chain. Further, the SEM image of perovskite film with Taurine additive was measured (see Fig. 4(h)). One can see that the average grain size of perovskite made with Taurine (743nm) is much larger than those of perovskite films with   $  C_{60}  $   additive or control samples. Accordingly, it can be deduced that the side chain plays a more major role in inducing grain growth of perovskite than the   $  C_{60}  $   cage.

# 4.Conclusion

In summary, we systematically compared the performance of a series of PSCs with   $  C_{60}  $  , PCBM,   $  C_{60}  $  -Ta, and Taurine as additives into active layer. All of these additives play the positive role to improve the PCE and stability. The devices with   $  C_{60}  $   has the best PCE of 16.59%, which is slightly higher than those with   $  C_{60}  $  -Ta (16.46%) or PCBM (15.94%), and significantly higher than that with pristine perovskite (14.87%). Our investigations demonstrate that the roles of   $  C_{60}  $   cage and grafted side chains were different as additives into perovskite layer. The   $  C_{60}  $   cage principally facilitate electron extraction and transfer. While the grafted side chains mainly improving the perovskite crystalline quality and passivate the defective state, and hence suppress the nonradiative recombination and improve the device stability. However, the grafted side chains tend to reduce the carrier transfer due to its insulation property. To simultaneously achieve the best performance in both PCE and stability, much more effort should be delivered to design the   $  C_{60}  $   derivatives with high electrical conductivity, good hydrophobic property and excellent passivation effect. This work could intrigue a wide interest in developing excellent fullerene material for PSC application.
