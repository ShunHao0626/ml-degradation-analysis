# 1. Introduction

Methylammonium lead halide (MAPbX $ _3 $ ) compounds were first studied in 1978 after which it was established that it had three structural phases (cubic, tetragonal and orthorhombic) [1]. After a study conducted by Miyasaka et al., in 2009, it was revealed that MAPbX $ _3 $  can be used as a light absorber material due to its tunable band gap property [2]. The efficiency was very low at this time at about  $ 3.8\% $  when the research for perovskite solar cell was still in its infancy. After a landmark study conducted by Kim et al., in 2012, in which the liquid electrolyte of perovskite was replaced with a solid HTL (Hole-transport layer), there was a growth spurt in the efficiency of the PSC (perovskite solar cell) [3]. As of 2021, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells have reached up to  $ 25.6\% $  [4]. This improvement in efficiency was possible by variety of changes in the synthesis methods, the structural configuration of PSCs, the electrodes used for the PSCs and the chemical constituents that make up the PSCs [5-8]. However, the PSCs are still plagued with certain problems that limit its commercial scalability [9,

The use of organolead trihalide perovskite material has been an important milestone in the field of photovoltaics with power conversion efficiencies reaching up to  $ 25.6\% $  in 2021. The light absorbing layer of the perovskite solar cell (PSC) plays a pivotal role in establishing long-term efficiency and stability. Stabilization of these perovskite phases is crucial for the efficient charge transfer dynamics, leading to a better optoelectronic performance. However, the perovskite layer may undergo undesirable phase transitions when exposed to environmental stressors. This impediment can be allayed by having a better understanding of the stress testing regimes and proper passivation strategies leading to their commercial scalability. In the present study, three types of perovskite materials are used for stress testing so as to observe the influence of atmospheric stressors on the type of perovskite materials used. An accelerated ageing test was performed on PSCs with simultaneous combination of heat and humidity. Our results show that FA-MA-Cs based mixed cationic lead halide perovskite are better suited for endurance against environmental stressors retaining almost  $ 50\% $  of PCE post stress test. The champion PSC samples were then subjected to two different modes of low-cost passivation techniques for perovskite absorber layer using acetic acid and  $ \mathrm{CaCl}_2 $  as passivating agents. Acetic acid treated samples managed to have lower hysteresis values, lower yellow phase-based degradation and a PCE of up to  $ 15\% $  as compared to control samples.  $ \mathrm{CaCl}_2 $  treated samples on the other hand had improved open-circuit voltage values and had more homogenous distribution with better resistance against recombined electrons.

To investigate the effect of changing temperature on PSC, thermal stress test was performed according to IEC convections   $  [23,25,26]  $  . A high humidity condition (RH>85%) was also conducted simultaneously in the present study to bring about an accelerated degradation state in the PSCs.

# 4. Passivation strategies on best performing PSC

# 4.1. The use of acetic acid as an additive in anti-solvent for solvent engineering in PSC

Chlorobenzene (CB) has been widely used in perovskite cell film preparation as an anti-solvent. Researchers have used different additives with chlorobenzene for crystallizing and passivating perovskite film   $  [29,30]  $  . Li. et al. utilized four conjugated polymers in antisolvent chlorobenzene solution for passivating methylammonium lead iodide   $  (MAPbI_{3})  $   perovskite   $  [29]  $  . They observed superior film, improved surface morphology and reduced defects due to passivation. This led to a power conversion efficiency of up to 18.7%. Another study by Wang et al. focused on the use of amine functional groups like benzylamine, aniline as passivating agents in chlorobenzene anti-solvent for the avoidance of moisture ingress   $  [28]  $  . The controlled study showed that the additive treated perovskite films were stable for more than 2900 h after exposure to medium levels of humidity (  $  \sim50\%  $  ) and the untreated ones degraded within 90 h. Previous studies have shown that the use of acetic acid in perovskite solar cell fabrication have a marked improvement in optoelectronic performance   $  [30,31]  $  . Due to its low cost and availability, it has been used in different ways, in context of solar cell preparation. A study by Li.et al. have shown a unique facile method of preparation for improving the crystallinity of the perovskite by using 8 vol% of acetic acid in context of mixed cationic perovskite   $  [30]  $  . It has been shown in this study that an increase in the volumetric concentration of acetic acid beyond 8% would trigger the corrosion of perovskite crystal and hamper the film morphology. There have not been any studies on the impact of acetic acid on carbon electrode-based perovskite solar cells. # 4.2. The use of   $  CaCl_{2}  $   as an additive to improve the   $  V_{oc}  $   losses

Amongst the rapid ascent of perovskite solar cell research, Cs-based perovskites have achieved considerable efficiencies to garner its attention for its practical applicability in solar cells. As discussed previously, Cs-based perovskite layer have enabled controlling the yellow-phase of perovskite solar cell. This has enabled its use as a mixed cationic perovskite in many different device architectures. However, Cs-based perovskite layer suffers from problems related to lower   $  V_{oc}  $  . Researchers have focused on improving the open circuit voltage, which contributes to a lower power conversion efficiency [36,37]. Guo et al. have used   $  SnCl_{2}  $   solution for passivating the SnO2 based ETL layer. This method effectively aids in surface passivation by reducing recombination of electrons [36]. This caused a big spike in the value of   $  V_{oc}  $   (1.31 V), which is one of the highest reported   $  V_{oc}  $   values in the literature for Cs-based perovskite solar cell. Chen et al. have used a gradient thermal annealing (GTA) method to control the growth of perovskite crystals to have a   $  V_{oc}  $   of up to 1.23 V [37]. Lyu. et al. demonstrated the use of alkaline earth metal halides like (  $  MgCl_{2}  $  ,   $  CaCl_{2}  $  ) for improving the power conversion efficiency [38].

There has not been any study so far utilizing passivating agent like  $ \mathrm{CaCl}_2 $  in a carbon Cs-based perovskite solar cell. We have utilized  $ \mathrm{CaCl}_2 $  as a passivating to improve the charge carrier density, crystal quality

and defect density of a low-cost PSC. Previous studies on different configurations have shown that a small amount of  $ (\sim 0.6\mathrm{M} $  ratios of  $ \mathrm{CaCl}_2 $  ) in perovskite precursor solution is enough to improve the optoelectronic properties of solar cell [39]. In this study, PSC samples were treated with  $ 0.6\% $  of molar ratio of  $ \mathrm{CaCl}_2 $  in perovskite precursor solution during perovskite layer fabrication and compared with untreated samples.

The   $  CaCl_{2}  $   treated samples were annealed for 15 min at 90 °C. This post-annealing treatment would pave the way for slow crystallization of the perovskite structure making it more homogenous. JV-Characteristics and FE-SEM images were obtained for different molar ratios of   $  CaCl_{2}  $   to see any discernible traits, if any for PSC samples. The JV- characteristics show that increasing the ratio of   $  CaCl_{2}  $   beyond a certain amount (in this case 0.6%) would lead to lowering the JV-characteristics (Fig. 8a). The highest   $  V_{oc}  $   obtained was in the case of 0.6% ratio of   $  CaCl_{2}  $   with 1.18 V. In contrast, the   $  V_{oc}  $   values obtained in the previous section for champion cell treated with acetic acid was around   $  \sim0.99  $   V). Thus, there was an increase of 19% in the open circuit voltage of PSCs treated with   $  CaCl_{2}  $ . The highest PCE achieved was at 13.4% for 0.6% ratio of   $  CaCl_{2}  $   (Table 2). This was also complemented by the fact that the FE-SEM images also showed a more uniform homogenous layer for 0.6% ratio of   $  CaCl_{2}  $   than the other ratios (Fig. 9c). The charge defects and pin-hole formations were more conspicuous for untreated sample (Fig. 9a).

The XRD plots for   $  CaCl_{2}  $   treated and untreated perovskite samples are shown in Fig. 10a. The plots show characteristic peaks of   $  PbI_{2}  $   and perovskite at   $  \sim12.5^{\circ}  $   and   $  \sim14^{\circ}  $  , respectively. Rs, the series resistance takes into account the series resistance offered by electrical contacts, metallic wires etc.  $ \mathrm{R_1} $ ,  $ \mathrm{C_1} $  and  $ \mathrm{R_2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{C_2} $  denotes the resistance and capacitance offered by mobility of electrons and recombination charges.

For treated samples its much higher. Also, the series resistance is also found to be lower for treated samples as can be seen from Table 3.

The series resistance value is higher for untreated sample 122.35  $ \Omega $  indicating more resistances offered at the carbon contact, metallic wire interface and a lower recombination value, R2.

From the CV and EIS analysis, it can be stated that the additive   $  CaCl_{2}  $   improves the trap defect density and open circuit voltage considerably by passivating it, thereby promoting charge carrier transport and increasing the opto-electronic performance of the solar cell.

# 5. Conclusions and future outlook

FA-MA-Cs (FA $ _{0.55} $ MA $ _{0.25} $ Cs $ _{0.2} $ PbI $ _3 $ ) based mixed cationic perovskite solar cells showed better endurance against heat and moisture which was observed through the accelerated ageing study. The FA-MA-Cs mixture-based PSC managed to retain almost 50% of PCE values ranging from 6 to 7%. The steepest reduction in PCE values was observed FA-MA (FA $ _{0.5} $ MA $ _{0.5} $ PbI $ _3 $ ) based perovskite which also showed poor absorption post thermal stress test. This could be because of the absence of Cs-cation which aids in crystallization of black-stable perovskite phase leading to delayed yellow phase formation.

The use of acetic acid in chlorobenzene solution proved beneficial for the improvement of overall efficiency and resilience against heat and moisture-based agents. The acetic acid treated samples had an overall efficiency which was much better as compared to the untreated samples. The hysteresis curve obtained for treated samples showed less amount of deviation in forward and backward scan values. Untreated samples showed a slight deviation in the values (especially current density values which has a big impact on the overall efficiency). When exposed to natural weather cycles of temperature and humidity, the treated samples showed a better overall optoelectronic performance as compared to untreated samples.

A small amount of   $  CaCl_{2}  $   doped onto the perovskite layer improved the open-circuit voltage of perovskite considerably with an increase of almost   $  \sim25\%  $  . The   $  CaCl_{2}  $   treated samples showed higher absorbance, better built-in potential and a higher recombination resistance offered against recombined electrons. This eventually led to an improved power conversion efficiency of 13.4%. Additionally, stress testing on passivated samples showed a better overall PCE for acetic acid treated samples and lower   $  V_{oc}  $   losses for   $  CaCl_{2}  $   treated samples.

Thus, our work has demonstrated that in context of mixed cationic perovskite, passivation can be achieved through low-cost means with the use of common laboratory reagents like acetic acid and  $ \mathrm{CaCl}_2 $ . This type of economical mode of compositional engineering for PSC might lay the groundwork of commercial scaling of PSC technology into mainstream photovoltaic industry.
