# Abstract

A facile halide exchange route based on   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Br  $   solution post-treatment has been successfully employed to transform the two-step spin-coated   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films into the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   films, whose morphologies and compositions could be modified simultaneously. With the increase of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Br  $   solution concentration, the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   films exhibited increased grain size, prolonged charge carrier lifetime, and enlarged bandgap with slightly reduced light absorption compared with the parent   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film. The mesostructured perovskite solar cells based on the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   films yielded the optimal power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 14.25%, which is much higher than that of device with the parent   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film. In particular, the device based on the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   film retained up to 93% of its original PCE after exposed to air for 14 days without any encapsulation, presenting a favorable stability. Our work suggests a novel and attractive way to fabricate high-performance perovskite solar cells with excellent stability.

# Introduction

Recently, perovskite solar cell as a new kind of photovoltaic (PV) devices has become a rapidly rising star due to the swiftly boosted power conversion efficiency (PCE), easy fabrication, and sufficient supply of raw materials   $  [1-4]  $  . Metal organic perovskite materials such as   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   are the most important components in the cells. The investigations focused on these perovskite films have revealed their unique features, including high absorption coefficient   $  [4,5]  $  , ambipolar charge transport properties   $  [6,7]  $  , long carrier diffusion lengths   $  [8,9]  $  , and low-temperature solution processability   $  [10-12]  $  . All such features are crucially important for their applications in high-performance and low-cost PV devices.

Critical to fabrication of perovskite solar cells, in particular mesostructured devices, is the realization of high-quality metal organic perovskite films [13-15], because the morphology and crystallinity of the films play an important role for the ultima device performances [16,17]. For example, Wang et al. [17] investigated the coverage of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  capping layer on mesoporous  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer and found the PCE of the devices increased with the increase of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  coverage. By controlling the cuboid size of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  capping layer, Park et al. [18] realized a device with the best PCE of  $ 17.01\% $ . Meng et al. [19] introduced a hot-pressing method to improve the morphology and crystallinity of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  capping layer, which resulted in significantly enhanced PV performances for both hole-transporting-material-free type and spiro-OMeTAD based solar cells. Besides improving the morphology and crystallinity of metal organic perovskites, chemical management of their compositional elements has been verified as an effective way to further refine their properties [3,20-23]. Specifically, mixture of multiple halogen elements in metal organic perovskites can bring at least three positive effects [24]. The first is boosting their stability. In the seminal work of Lee et al. [6], they found that  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  was remarkably stable compared with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  when processing in air. Noh et al. [20,25] also revealed that incorporating Br into  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  could effectively improve the devices' stability. The second is improving the carrier transport properties of metal organic perovskite films, thus the device performances. It has been revealed that  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  possessed much longer carrier diffusion length than  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  [8,26]. Besides, the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Br}_x $  and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbBr}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  were confirmed having improved carrier mobility and reduced carrier recombination rates [27-29]. The third is increasing the open circuit voltage ( $ V_{oc} $ ) of the devices. Previous works reported that the  $ V_{oc} $  for solar cell with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbBr}_3 $  ( $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbBr}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $ ) can reach as high as  $ 1.3\mathrm{V} $  (1.5 V) [28,30,31]. Apparently, by modifying the morphology and/or composition of metal organic perovskite films, the performance of perovskite solar cells can be effectively improved.

Since the discovery of the ion exchange reaction of inorganic nanocrystals by the Alivisatos [32], much attention has been paid to the simultaneous modulation of composition and micro-structure of inorganic nanocrystals as well as nanostructured films via this type reaction [33-37]. More recently, Pellet et al. found that when the metal organic perovskite with the formula  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbX}_3 $  ( $ X = Cl $ , Br, or I) was infiltrated in a mesoscopic scaffold, halide exchange on the

perovskite lattice was strikingly fast, being completed within seconds or minutes after contact with the halide solution [38]. Similarly, Solis-Ibarra et al. [39] revealed that post-synthetic halide conversion can occur in both three- and two-dimensional metal organic perovskites. Inspired by these interesting results, we expect that it is possible to simultaneously modify the morphology and compositional elements by post-synthetic halide exchange, thus improving the PV performance of perovskite solar cell.

In this study, a facile halide exchange route has been successfully employed to prepare   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   films from the two-step spin-coated   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films reacted with   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Br  $   solution, which allows modifying the films' morphologies and compositions simultaneously. Compared with the parent   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film, the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   films exhibited increased grain size, prolonged charge carrier lifetime, and enlarged bandgap with slightly reduced light absorption with the increase of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Br  $   solution concentration. The PCE of the mesostructured perovskite solar cells based on the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   films could be optimized to be 14.25%, which is much higher than that of device with the parent   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film. In particular, the PCE of device based on the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   film could retain up to 93% after exposed to air for 14 days without any encapsulation.

# Experimental section

# Materials

Lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ , 99.999%), Li-bis-(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI, 98%) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP, 98%), dimethylformamide (DMF, anhydrous 99.5%) were purchased from Aladdin Reagents. Isopropanol (IPA), diethanolamine, chlorobenzene, acetonitrile, tetrabutyl titanate and were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (China). 2, 2', 7, 7'-tetrakis (N,N-di-p-methoxy-phenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-MeOTAD, ≥ 99%) was purchased from Shenzhen Feiming Science and Technology Co., Ltd. (China). Hydroiodic acid (47 wt% in water), hydrobromic acid (48 wt% in water) and methylamine (33 wt% in absolute ethanol) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ Br and CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I were synthesized by the recipe reported previously.

# Solar cell fabrication

Preparation of the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   films: Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass substrates with sheet resistance of about   $  14\Omega sq^{-1}  $   were cleaned by ultrasonic washing in detergent solution, acetone, absolute alcohol, and deionized water for 30 min in succession, and then cleaned by UV-ozone treatment for 30 min.   $  30\mu L  $     $  TiO_{2}  $   sol was spin-coated on the pre-cleaned substrates to form compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers with the thickness of about 60 nm by annealing at   $  480^{\circ}C  $   for 40 min in air.   $  30\mu L  $   diluted   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste was then dropped onto the   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact film to form mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer with the thickness of about 200 nm by spin-coating at 5000 rpm for 30 s, followed by annealing at   $  500^{\circ}C  $   for 30 min in air.

A halide exchange engineering for  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Br}_x $  perovskite solar cells

Figure 1 Schematic procedures for preparation of the parent   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film by the two-step spin-coating method and transformation to   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   film via halide exchange.

Preparation of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films and halide exchange with Br by   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Br  $   post-treatment: Firstly,   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution prepared by dissolving 461 mg   $  PbI_{2}  $   in 1 mL DMF was spin-coated on the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   films at 3000 rpm for 30 s. After spinning, the films were dried at 70 °C for 5 min and then 95 °C for 15 min. To convert the   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer to   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $ , 0.2 mL   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   solution in IPA with the concentration of 8 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   was loaded on the top of   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer for 25 s (loading time), followed by spun at 4000 rpm for 20 s and dried at 95 °C for 15 min. In the halide exchange process, 0.2 mL   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Br  $   solution in IPA with the concentration of 1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $ , 3 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $ , or 5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   was loaded on the top of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film at 50 °C for 30 s (loading time), followed by spun at 4000 rpm for 20 s and dried at 95 °C for 15 min. Thus, the sample   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   films were obtained and the corresponding samples with the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Br  $   concentration to be 1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $ , 3 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $ , and 5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   were labeled as S1, S3, and S5, respectively. The sample without the halide exchange process was also prepared and labeled as S0 for comparison. The above experiment processes for the preparation of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film and halide exchange with Br are also schematically shown in Figure 1.

Fabrication of Devices: 72.3 mg spiro-MeOTAD, 28.8 mL TBP and 17.5 μL lithium Li-TFSI in acetonitrile (520 mg/ml) were mixed in 1 mL chlorobenzene to form the hole transport material (HTM) solution. Then, the solution was coated on the CH₃NH₃PbI₃₋ₓBrₓ (or CH₃NH₃PbI₃) sample to form HTM layer by spin-coating at 3000 rpm for 30 s. After being preserved in dark in dry box for 24 h, the Ag counter-electrode with the thickness of about 100 nm and the area of 0.28 cm² was deposited on the top HTM layer by thermal evaporation technique.

# Characterizations

A FEI NOVA NanoSEM230 scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was employed to characterize the morphological properties of the samples. X-Ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were collected on a Rigaku Ultima III X-ray diffractometer (Cu Kα) in the range of 10-60°. The chemical composition of the samples was characterized by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, PHI-5702) using Al Kα monochromatic radiation. A Shimadzu UV-2550 UV-vis spectrometer fitted with an integrating sphere was used to investigate the absorption properties of the samples. Time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) decay was recorded on PL spectrometer, FLS 900, Edinburgh

Instruments, excited using a 445 nm laser beam pulsed at frequency of 5 MHz. The incident photon conversion efficiency (IPCE) was measured using a 150 W xenon lamp (Oriel) fitted with a monochromator (Cornerstone 74004) as a monochromatic light source. The J-V curves of the fabricated solar cells were carried on a Keithley 2400 source measurement unit under AM1.5 illumination (standard 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) cast by an Oriel 92251A-1000 sunlight simulator calibrated by the standard reference of a Newport silicon solar cell. A black mask with a circular aperture (0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) was applied on top of the solar cell.

# Results and discussion

The surface morphologies of the samples were analyzed by SEM. As exhibited in Figure 2(a)-(d), each of the samples had a uniform morphology in a large scale. Moreover, the perovskite films fully covered the underlying  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  porous layer, which is not only beneficial to reduce the shutting paths but also to guarantee the effective light absorption in the ultimate devices [17,40]. It should be noted that after spin-coating of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  porous layer was infiltrated with  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , and a capping layer of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  was also formed, as shown in Figure S1. After  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  coating, the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  was converted to  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ . Therefore, the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  not only existed inside the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  pores, but also on the top of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  layer and formed the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  capping layer [41]. The insets give the corresponding high-resolution SEM images. As can be seen, all of capping layers were composed of densely connected grains. And, as revealed in Figure 2 (e) and (f), the parent  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film had an average grain size of  $ \sim 206~\mathrm{nm} $ , and showed a narrow size distribution. By comparison, the average grain sizes were increased for the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{Br} $  post-treated samples. For samples S1, S3, and S5, the grain sizes were determined to be  $ \sim 338~\mathrm{nm} $ ,  $ \sim 393~\mathrm{nm} $ , and  $ \sim 433~\mathrm{nm} $ , respectively, which was also accompanied by the broadening of the grain size distribution. Obviously, the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{Br} $  treatment can modify the morphology of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite film, especially in increasing the grain size of capping layer.

The issue of stability is another critical concern for perovskite PV devices. The major factor affecting the stability of perovskite solar cells may be largely attributed to the decomposition of the perovskite film when exposed to moisture [4,46]. It has been reported that  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Br}_x $  film has a low sensitivity to the humidity benefited from its compact and stable structure [20,29]. In the present case, we also found that the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Br}_x $  films, such as sample S3, were much stable than the parent  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film stored at room temperature in air with the relative humidity about  $ 40\% $ , as can be easily identified from the transmission change of the films along with the storage time, Figure 8(a) and Figure S4. # Conclusion

In summary, we reported the facile halide exchange route to transform the two-step spin-coated   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films into   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   films with simultaneously tunable morphologies and compositions. The PCE of mesostructured perovskite solar cells with the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   films can be optimized to 14.25%, which is much higher than that of device with the parent   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film. Especially, the device based on the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   film possessed improved environment stability with retained PCE of up to 93% after exposed to air for 14 days without any encapsulation. Such excellent PV properties can be attributed to the combination of increased grain size, prolonged charge carrier lifetime, and enlarged bandgap with slightly reduce light absorption of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   films. Our work suggests a novel and attractive way to fabricate high-performance perovskite solar cells with excellent stability.
