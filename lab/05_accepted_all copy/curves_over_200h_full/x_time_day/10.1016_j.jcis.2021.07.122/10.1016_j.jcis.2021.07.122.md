# GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT

# ARTICLE INFO

All-inorganic perovskite materials (Typically:  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $ ) have attracted enormous attention due to their illustrious thermal stability and appropriate bandgap, and their use in perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been extensively investigated. However, the inevitable defects of the perovskite layer, energy level mismatch between perovskite and carbon electrodes, and the phase instability of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  limit the power conversion efficiency (PCE) and stability of carbon-based  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  PSCs. Herein, we demonstrate a simple and effective strategy for regulating energy level, inhibiting carrier recombination, and delaying the degradation of perovskite by modifying the surface of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  with a new type of 2D perovskite  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $ . The carbon-based  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  PSCs achieve a higher PCE (13.69%) than the control device (11.10%). The excellent matching of the energy level and suppression of charge carrier recombination should be responsible for the improvement in efficiency. Furthermore, the excellent hydrophobic performance of  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $  enhances the moisture resistance of the device. This study provides a potential strategy for improving the performance and stability of all-inorganic  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  PSCs.

ing volatile-free all-inorganic perovskite materials, such as   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   (X = I, Br, and Cl or their mixtures), is an efficient strategy. Among inorganic perovskites,   $  CsPbX_{3}  $  -based PSCs demonstrate superior photoelectric performance and thermal stability.  $  ^{[13,14]}  $   Among   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   materials,   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   has the most proper bandgap (approximately 1.73 eV) but is easily converted to   $  \delta  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   with a bandgap of 2.82 eV at room temperature, this feature does not take full advantage of visible light.  $  ^{[15]}  $   By contrast,   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   is stable at room temperature, but its wide bandgap (2.34 eV) limits light absorption and utilization. As a member of the   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   family,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   with a bandgap of 1.91 eV has become the most promising absorber layer, making a trade-off between efficiency and stability. In recent years, various strategies have been applied to highly efficient   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based PSCs, such as of ion doping,  $  ^{[16-18]}  $   interface modification,  $  ^{[19-22]}  $   and developing hole transfer layer.  $  ^{[23-25]}  $   To date, the efficiency of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based PSCs has exceeded 17%, with a traditional device architecture of conductive substrate/electron transfer layer (ETL)/perovskite/HTL/Au, exhibiting a brilliant development prospect.  $  ^{[26,27]}  $

However, the usage of HTL (such as spiroOMeTAD and poly (3-hexylthiophene-2, 5-diyl) can greatly reduce the long-term stability of the devices due to the hydrophilicity of these organic materials and the hydrophilic character of its additives.[28-30] For these problems, carbon materials which is widely used in the optical fields is selected as HTL and back electrode, further simplifying the device structure (ITO/ETL/perovskite/carbon).[31] This approach not only prevents the hydrophilicity problem of HTL materials and their additives but also reduces the cost of production (organic HTL materials and noble metal).[32,33] However, carbon-based  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs (C-PSCs) have evident drawbacks. The weak hole extraction ability of carbon electrodes results in severe carrier recombination and poor device performance due to the energy level mismatch between carbon electrodes and perovskite layers.[34-36] Energy level mismatch is one of the important causes of energy loss ( $ E_{loss} $ , defined as  $ E_{loss} = E_g - eV_{oc} $ ;  $ E_g $  is the bandgap of perovskite, and  $ V_{oc} $  is device open-circuit voltage), which is directly correlated with the  $ V_{oc} $  of the devices. Apart from energy level mismatch, perovskite crystal quality, charge carrier extraction rate, and defect density also affect the  $ E_{loss} $ .[37-39] Perovskite doping and interface engineering between perovskite and carbon electrodes are the two efficient strategies for reducing  $ E_{loss} $ . Zhao et al. indicate that acetate anion doping in perovskite precursors for the replacement of some halogen atoms can change the morphology of perovskites film and improve  $ V_{oc} $  from 1.19 V to 1.30 V.[40] Yan et al. propose the modification of the interfaces of perovskite and HTL with ZnO. The  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{ZnO} $  ETL layer provides a favorable matching of energy band, which improves the  $ V_{oc} $  from 1.06 V to 1.23 V.[41]

In this study, we propose a double perovskite material our previous reported,  $  ^{[42]}  $     $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $  , which is a material with a narrow bandgap of 1.37 eV, high water stability, high thermal stability, and high charge carrier mobility. It is used in modifying   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite for the optimization of the interface and reduction of energy level mismatch, as well as improvement of HTM-free C-PSC performance. The results show that   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $   can adjust the energy levels between the interfaces of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  /carbon electrode and fill in the defects of perovskite surfaces and grain boundaries. As a result, the champion PCE of 13.69% was achieved, with corresponding   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.28 V,   $  J_{SC}  $   of 14.85 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and FF of 0.72. Additionally, after   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $   introduction, the stability of the device is significantly improved under various conditions.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1.Materials

The main materials were purchased from the following companies: Yingkou OPV Tech Co. Ltd (transparent conductive substrate ITO), Alfa Aesar (  $  SnO_{2}  $   colloid, tin(IV) oxide), Wako. Co. (tin(ii) chloride dihydrate, 99.9%), TCI (Lead iodide, PbI2, 99.999 %), Sigma-Aldric (Cesium Bromide, CsBr, 99.0 %), and Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. (PtI  $  _{2}  $  , CsI). The remaining related reagents and materials were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich. All the materials used as received.

# 2.2. Synthesis of  $ Cs_{2}PtI_{6} $

The methods for synthesizing   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $   were provided in our previous work. For the preparation of   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $   crystals,   $  PtI_{2}  $   (1 M) and CsI (2 M) were dissolved in DMSO solution, and then hydroiodic acid with 1% volume ratio was added to the hot solution. Next, we cultivated the crystal using the solvent replacement method. Finally, we used acetone and deionized water to wash the crystal four or five times. The crystal was dissolved in DMSO for the preparation of a 0.1 M solution for subsequent use.

# 2.3. Device fabrication

ITO/glass was washed in an ultrasonic cleaner with the following solutions: deionized water, ethanol, acetone, isopropanol, and deionized water. After drying, the ITO/glass was treated in UV-ozone for 20 min. The   $  SnO_{2}  $   aqueous solution with a mass fraction of 2.75% was spin-coated on the ITO/glass at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Then, the film was annealed at 150 °C for 40 min. After 20 min of UV-ozone treatment, the   $  SnCl_{2}  $   solution (0.01 M in absolute ethanol) was spin-coated on the top of the film with 6000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 100 °C for 10 min and 180 °C for 1 h. For   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , a solution of CsBr and   $  PbI_{2}  $   in a mole ratio of 1:1 in DMSO/DMF with a volume ratio of 9:1 was spin-coated onto ETL at 1000 rpm for 10 s and 3000 rpm for 30 s in an argon-filled glove box. And then the obtained films were annealed by sequential 40 °C for 5 min and then 160 °C for 10 min. The solutions with different volume ratios of   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $   solutions and IPA were spin-coated on the PVK films at 3000 rpm for 30 s, and annealing was performed at 160 °C for 10 min. Finally, we obtained the carbon electrode with a doctor-blading technology. Residual solvents in the films were evaporated through annealing at 80 °C for 30 min on a hot plate under ambient conditions.

The second one is the passivation of the perovskite surface and grain boundary for the reduction of defect density, as well as inhibition of charger carrier recombination. UV-vis absorption spectra and ultraviolet photoemission spectroscopy (UPS) measurements were used in exploring the influence of  $ \mathrm{Cs_2} $ - $ \mathrm{PtI}_6 $  on energy level. As shown in Fig. 2g, the bandgaps  $ (E_g) $  in the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  films with and without  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $  show no significant changes, demonstrating that the introduction of  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $  has no effect on the bandgap of the perovskite. Overall absorption intensity is improved compared with that of the control sample owing to increase in perovskite thickness. It is consistent with the results of the cross-sectional SEM measurements. The UPS cut-off edge  $ (E_{cut-off}) $  and onset regions are shown in Fig. 2h, and the data are exhibited in Table S2. The  $ E_{cut-off} $  value of the perovskite increases from  $ 16.84\mathrm{eV} $  to  $ 17.05\mathrm{eV} $  in the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  films without and with  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $  treatment, showing that the fermi level  $ (E_F) $  shifts from the original  $ -4.38\mathrm{eV} $  to  $ -4.17\mathrm{eV} $  after the addition of  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $  showing more  $ n $ -type semiconductor character. For the comprehensive analysis of Fig. 2g and Fig. 2h, the values of conduction band  $ (E_{CB}) $  and valence band  $ (E_{VB}) $  were obtained. The energy level structure diagram of the C-PSCs is drawn in Fig. 2i. The energy offset  $ (A_{H}) $  between the perovskite layer and carbon layer decreases from  $ 0.73\mathrm{eV} $  to  $ 0.51\mathrm{eV} $ , corresponding to  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  films without and with  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $  treatment. This result suggests that hole transport and the hole extraction ability of the devices are enhanced after the introduction of  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $ . [34] Meanwhile, increase in match energy level and more  $ n $ -type semiconductor characteristic can reduce  $ E_{loss} $  and facilitate carrier separation and transport.

To achieve the best C-PSC performance, we optimized the concentration of   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $  , as shown in Fig. S4 and Table S3. The best performance is exhibited by 0.1 v%   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $   in IPA. The introduction of   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $   can dramatically improve the   $  V_{OC}  $   of the device. The photocurrent density voltage (J-V) curves of the champion cells with the best   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $   content are shown in Fig. 3a, and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table 1. The control device displays the highest PCE (11.10 %), corresponding to 14.69 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   for   $  J_{SC}  $  , 1.16 V for   $  V_{OC}  $  , and 0.65 for FF. In the devices with   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $   modification, the PCE reaches a maximum of 13.69%, and the   $  J_{SC}  $   is 14.85 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{OC}  $   is 1.28 V, and FF is 0.72. The notable improvements in   $  V_{OC}  $   and FF are attributed to the energy level arrangement of   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $   to   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and the enhancement of charger carrier transport property.

The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectrum measurements of C-PSCs were used in verifying the accuracy of the  $ J - V $  scan results. As shown in Fig. 3b, the integrated current densities are obtained in the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  films with and without  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $  (14.64 and  $ 14.27\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , respectively). We measured the stable power output (SPO) curves. The output current densities shown in Fig. 3c are 13.28 and  $ 10.20\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  for devices with and without  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $ , respectively, and the PCE values are  $ 13.19\% $  and  $ 10.30\% $ , respectively. These values are in agreement with those from the  $ J - V $  measurement. The reproducibility of device performance was studied by testing 50 independent devices with and without  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $ , as shown in Fig. 3d. The average PCE values are  $ 12.80\% $  and  $ 9.95\% $  for devices with and without  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $ , respectively. The devices with  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $  exhibit higher performance and higher reproducibility than control. Fig. 3e shows the statistical photovoltaic parameters ( $ J_{SC} $ ,  $ V_{OC} $ , FF, and PCE), which exhibit high reproducibility, and enhanced of  $ J_{SC} $ ,  $ V_{OC} $ , and FF contribute to the increased efficiency of devices with  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $ .

# 4. Conclusions

We propose a double perovskite material,   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $  , as an interface modifier for managing energy level mismatch and enhancing the performance and stability of C-PSCs because of the advantages of   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $  , such as high mobility, high water stability, and high thermal stability. The PCE of the best device with   $  Cs_{2}PtI_{6}  $   is 13.69%, which is higher than that of the control device (11.10%). Enhanced energy level matching, fast charge carrier transport,

and the suppression of recombination are the main reasons for PCE improvement. The excellent stability of  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PtI_6} $  significantly enhances the perovskite film and device stability at various conditions. This study provides a simple and effective approach for obtaining C-PSCs with excellent performance and long-term stability.
