# 1. Introduction

Lead-based halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are the frontrunner of photovoltaic studies, and within a decade the power conversion efficiency (PCE) has increased sharply from 3.8% to 25.5% [1,2], making it the fastest growing technology in the solar cell community. An interest in perovskites originates from their highly desired optoelectronic properties, low-cost processing, high charge carrier diffusion length, and broad absorption spectra [3–5]. However, the toxicity of lead (Pb) is a critical bottleneck for the practical use of PSCs. The potential for Pb exposure (or leakage) during the extensive production and operation of PSC technology requires extreme caution due to toxicity concerns to the environment and humans. Therefore, it is important to eliminate or replace Pb with other nontoxic or less-toxic elements which can overcome environmental issues. To address this issue, a number of nontoxic replacements such as tin (Sn), germanium (Ge), bismuth (Bi) and antimony (Sb) has been studied [6]. Among them, the Sn-based perovskites have shown great potential with reduced bio-toxicity and similar properties to Pb-based perovskites in terms of optical and electrical

Tin (Sn) halide perovskite has been a promising candidate in lead-free perovskite solar cells (PSCs), but its chemical instability attributed to   $  Sn^{2+}/Sn^{4+}  $   oxidation reduces device performance and stability. To address this problem, we propose a new approach, i.e. fabrication of mesoporous n-i-p Sn-based PSCs with the photoactive composite made of mixed-organic-cation Sn halide perovskite and reduced graphene oxide (rGO) sheets anchored with Sn quantum dots (i.e.,   $  FA_{0.8}MA_{0.2}SnI_{3}/rGO-SnQDs  $  ). The rGO-Sn QDs in active layer not only suppress the destructive   $  Sn^{2+}/Sn^{4+}  $   oxidation and recombination but also accelerate charge transport, improve charge-carrier lifetime and reduce trap state density. Compared to the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of Sn-PSC without Sn QDs, the composite-basted champion device showed 55% increase in efficiency, attributed to strong suppression of   $  Sn^{2+}/Sn^{4+}  $   oxidation and recombination. Furthermore, the champion device showed remarkable reproducibility and stability improvement, representing an essential step for the practical use of low-cost and lead-free PSCs.

Hence, the Sn-based halide perovskites (i.e.,  $ \mathrm{ASnX_3} $ , where A is a univalent organic or inorganic cation and X is a monoanionic halide ion) are promising materials for the development of environment-friendly Pb-free PSCs. In 2014, a Sn-based PSC based on methylammonium (MA,  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3 $ ) tin iodide perovskite  $ (\mathrm{MASnI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Br}_x) $  was first reported, achieving PCE of  $ 5.73\% $  [10]. Recently, Sn-based PSCs as a promising lead-free alternative reached a highest PCE of  $ \sim 5 - 7\% $  for normal n-i-p configuration and  $ \sim 13\% $  for inverted p-i-n structures, respectively [11-13]. Such lower efficiency than that of Pb-based PSCs is attributed to several factors such as rapid oxidation of  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2 + } $  to  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{4 + } $ , fast crystallization of Sn-based perovskite leading to the formation of large numbers of small grains, and instability of  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2 + } $ -containing precursors that cause unbalanced cation/anion ratio in  $ \mathrm{ABX}_3 $  crystal network, resulting in formation of defects acting as charge-trap centers. Especially, compared to the Pb-based PSCs, a critical problem of the Sn-based PSCs is the rapid oxidation of  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2 + } $  to  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{4 + } $  in the presence of oxygen and humidity, resulting in the structural defects and Sn vacancies which cause high level of self p-doping and

Thus, the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   results in poor device performance and stability with lowering photovoltaic parameters and shortening long-term stability of cells [14–16]. Many approaches have been examined to inhibit the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}/Sn^{4+}  $ , such as the use of   $  SnF_{2}  $   and   $  SnCl_{2}  $   additives as source of extra   $  Sn^{2+}  $   [17–22] and the elimination of   $  Sn^{4+}  $   defect states using reducing agents such as hypophosphorous [23], hydrazine [24], ethylenediammonium [25]. 1,4-bis(trimethylsilyl)-2,3,5,6-tetramethyl-1,4-dihydropyrazine (TM-DHP) reducing agent have been used for in situ reduction of   $  SnF_{2}  $   in perovskite precursor to Sn(0) to scavenge   $  Sn^{4+}  $   impurities [26]. However, with the use of tin halide (  $  SnX_{2}  $  ) additives, it has been reported that excessive   $  SnX_{2}  $   leads to the formation of   $  SnX_{2}  $   aggregation and leads to undesirable consequences that can deteriorate not only morphology but also optoelectronic quality and device performance [21]. Moreover, the use of reducing agents increases the risk of full reduction of   $  Sn^{4+}  $   and   $  Sn^{2+}  $   salts to Sn metal. Other studies reported different strategies by introducing hydrogen bonding agents and ligands to passivate the defect states in Sn-perovskite film and to reduce the self-doping oxidation [27]. Theoretical studies show that   $  Sn^{4+}  $   impurity in precursor solution and perovskite film, can react in ambient air and the choice of the interlayer material can have a significant role on device stability [21]. Despite many studies to reduce   $  Sn^{4+}  $   oxidation states and optimize film composition, Sn-perovskites are still facing chemical side effects of additives impurities and their toxicity. Therefore, an alternative strategy for simultaneously enhancing the performance and suppression of the   $  Sn^{2+}/Sn^{4+}  $   oxidation with non-toxic components are urgently required. To the best of our knowledge, no work has been reported on the suppression of the p-doping oxidation with the aid of graphene-tin quantum dots composites incorporated in Sn-perovskite photoactive layer.

In this work, in order to suppress the   $  Sn^{2+}/Sn^{4+}  $   oxidation and to improve perovskite film morphology, we propose a new approach, i.e. fabrication of the mesoporous Sn-based perovskite solar cells (Sn-PSCs) based on a photoactive composite of mixed-organic-cation tin halide perovskite/graphene-tin quantum dots (i.e.,   $  FA_{x}MA_{1-x}SnI_{3}/rGO-SnQDs  $  ) in active layer (AL), where rGO reduced-graphene oxide. The incorporation of graphene-tin quantum dots (rGO-Sn QDs) composite in the photoactive layer not only inhibits the oxidation of   $  Sn^{+2}  $   with reducing structural defects but also enhances the light harvesting and charge carrier transport. A series of experiment were conducted to elucidate the effect of rGO-Sn QDs composite on structural properties of Sn-based perovskite and device performance. By optimizing the content of mixed-cation (i.e., FAMA) in Sn-based perovskite and the content of rGO-Sn QDs in the photoactive composite layer, we were able to obtain significant improvement in device performance and stability with remarkable reduction of trap states density.

# 2. Materials and experimental

# 2.1. Synthesis of GO and Sn-rGO composite

The synthesis of graphene oxide (GO) is reported in the previously reported [28]. In Brief, commercially available graphite  $ (5\mathrm{g}) $  and  $ \mathrm{NaNO}_3 $   $ (7.5\mathrm{g}) $  were mixed in  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{SO}_4 $   $ (120~\mathrm{mL}) $  and stirred in an ice bath for  $ 1\textrm{h} $ . Potassium permanganate was added to the prepared solution and stirred overnight in an ice bath. Deionized water  $ (230~\mathrm{mL}) $  was added to the solution while stirring. Lastly, hydrogen peroxide  $ (30\% \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2) $  was added, which made a dark brown GO solution. The produced GO solution was filtered, washed and centrifuged in water-ethanol mixed solution.

For the synthesis of rGO-Sn QDs composite, GO (8 mg), Sn(IV) chloride pentahydrate (0.7 g) and glucose (2 g) were dissolved in 60 mL of DI-water and the prepared solution was sonicated for 2 h at room temperature. The solution then was transferred to a Teflon-lined stainless steel autoclave and kept in an oven at 140 °C for 5 h. As-formed precipitates were collected for further reduction process. A 200 μL of

hydrazine   $  \left(\mathrm{N}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{4}\right)  $   (0.25 vol%) was added to the solution and sonicated for 24 h at RT, followed by annealing at 200 °C under inert atmosphere for 1 h.

# 2.2. Synthesis of methylammonium iodide  $ (\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I}) $

Synthesis of methylammonium iodide (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I, MAI) is available elsewhere [29,30]. In brief, 27.9 mL of methylamine (40 wt% in water, Sigma-Aldrich) and 30 mL of hydroiodic acid (57 wt% in water, YAKURI) were mixed and stirred in an ice bath for 1 h. Afterward, the excess solvent was evaporated using rotary evaporator and obtained brownish crystalline MAI was dissolved in excess ethanol and precipitated with ethyl ether. The final products of white MAI powders were washed with ethyl ether for several times and then transferred to a vacuum oven and dried at 60 °C for 24 h.

# 2.3. Perovskite Film Preparation

The preparation of perovskite films was completed in an argon-filled glovebox. To prepare  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 / \mathrm{MASnI}_3 $  precursor solution,  $ 172\mathrm{mg} $  FAI (Greatcell Solar, Cas No. 879643),  $ 159\mathrm{mg} $  MAI and  $ 372\mathrm{mg} $   $ \mathrm{SnI}_2 $  were dissolved in  $ 1.25~\mathrm{mL} $  of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). The  $ (\mathrm{FA})_x(-\mathrm{MA})_{1 - x}\mathrm{SnI}_3 $  ( $ x = 0.2 $ , 0.5, 0.8 and 1.0) solutions were prepared by mixing  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{MASnI}_3 $  solutions with different volume ratios. As-prepared rGO-Sn composites was added to the perovskite solutions and homogenized by stirring for  $ 1\mathrm{h} $ , followed by film formation by spin-coating.

# 2.4. Device fabrication

The Sn-PSCs were fabricated with the configuration of FTO/SnO $ _2 $ /Al $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ - graphene/(FA $ _x $ MA $ _{1-x} $ SnI $ _3 $ /rGO-Sn)/spiro-OMeTAD/Au. The fabrication process of perovskite films was performed in an Argon-filled glovebox. For compact layer of SnO $ _2 $ , 0.086 M solution of tin chloride pentahydrate (SnCl $ _4 $ , 5 H $ _2 $ O, Sigma Aldrich) in isopropanol was prepared under stirring at 50 °C until a transparent solution was obtained. For mesoporous Al $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ -graphene layer, a graphene solution in IPA (1 mg/mL) was mixed with solution of Al $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  NPs in IPA with weight ratio of 0.1 wt%. SnO $ _2 $  and Al $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ -graphene films as compact and mesoporous ETL, respectively, were formed by spin coating the prepared solutions on FTO substrates, followed by annealing at 250 °C [30]. As-prepared (FA) $ _x $ (MA) $ _{1-x} $ SnI $ _3 $  precursor solutions were spin-coated on FTO/S-nO $ _2 $ /mp-AG at different spinning rates for 60 s, followed by annealing at 110 °C for 20 min. For hole transport layer (HTL), 73.5 mg of spiro-OMeTAD was dissolved in chlorobenzene (1 mL), tetrabutylpyridine (28.9 μL) and 17.25 μL of lithium bis (trifluoromethylsulphonyl) imide solution in acetonitrile (529 mg/mL) and spin-coated at 4000 rpm for 10 s, followed by drying at RT for 10 h. Finally, a gold electrode was deposited on the HTL through a shadow mask by thermal evaporation (JV15EVA-15K2P). The device dimensions were 3 cm x 3 cm and photovoltaic parameters measurements were performed with 0.16 cm $ ^2 $  mask aperture.

# 2.5. Characterization

TEM and SEM analysis were performed by Bio-TEM (Hitachi) and FESEM (Hitachi S4700, and SUPRA 40VP). XRD analysis of films was examined by high performance X-ray diffraction (Rigaku) with Cu-Kα radiation. The chemical bond state was examined by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). The photovoltaic parameters were measured with a solar simulator (Oriel Corp., Model 18 91160A) having 150 W Xenon lamp with a source meter (Keithley series 2400) at 100 mW/cm² under AM 1.5 illumination (calibrated with silicon reference at 25 °C). The electronic impedance spectroscopy (EIS) analysis were performed at 250 mV applied bias in the range of 1 Hz–300 kHz. Details of materials synthesis and devices fabrication are available in Materials and Experimental. The  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}SnI_3} $  films were subject to XRD analysis (Fig. 1) as a function of FA content (x). Fig. S1a exhibits the XRD patterns of  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}SnI_3} $  films as a function of x. The main peaks of (101) and (202) planes for  $ \mathrm{MASnI}_3 $  are observed at  $ 2\theta = 14^{\circ} $  and  $ 28^{\circ} $ , respectively. For the  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  film, these peaks become much weaker but an extra peak of (201) appears at  $ 2\theta = 24^{\circ} $ , which is a highest peak. It is clearly exhibited that in the  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}SnI_3} $  films the peak intensities of (101) and (202) planes decrease with FA content but the peak of (201) appears because of partial substitution of MA with FA cations, resulting in even distribution of MA and FA cations in the lattices [31]. Furthermore, the peak intensities of (101) and (202) planes decrease with FA content and the peaks shift towards lower diffraction angles (Fig. S1b), indicating an expansion in the lattice parameters of  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}SnI_3} $  because the larger FA cations tend to replace the smaller MA cations [31]. The effect of FA content on the optical properties of the Sn-based perovskites was also examined via absorption spectra of the  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}SnI_3} $  films formed on glass substrates (Fig. S1c), but its influence is not distinct because of the formation of sub-bandgap states ascribed to the oxidation of  $ \mathrm{Sn^{2 + }} $  in the course of measurements in ambient atmosphere [31,32]. To adequately identify the optical property, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) of the perovskite films was measured as a function of x (Fig. S1d). From the PL peaks the bandgap energy of  $ \mathrm{FA_xMA_{1 - x}SnI_3} $  films was estimated to be  $ 1.27\mathrm{eV} $  at  $ x = 0.0 $  and  $ 1.32\mathrm{eV} $  at  $ x = 1.0 $  (Table S1). In order to optimize the FA content in terms of device performance, the Sn-PSCs having different cation ratios were fabricated and their photovoltaic performances were measured under the simulation of AM 1.5G. Photocurrent density vs. voltage  $ (J - V) $  curves and photovoltaic parameters of the devices were examined as a function of x, resulting in a best performance at  $ x = 0.8 $  (see Fig. S1e and Table S2). Therefore, unless mentioned, the optimal content of FA is  $ x = 0.8 $  (i.e.  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.8}\mathrm{MA}_{0.2}\mathrm{SnI}_3 $ ).

Figs. 1a and S2 show the TEM and FESEM images of as-prepared rGO-Sn QDs composites, respectively. It is observed that numerous Sn QDs (average size  $ \sim $ 6 nm) are uniformly distributed on graphene sheets (Figs. 1a and S2). The crystallinity of the rGO-Sn QDs composite film was also examined by comparing the XRD patterns of rGO, rGO-Sn and Sn (Fig. 1b). The rGO showed a wide peak centered around  $ 2\theta = 23.5^{\circ} $  related to diffraction of (002) plane [33]. However, the rGO-Sn QDs film clearly exhibits the peaks at  $ 2\theta = 30^{\circ} $ ,  $ 32^{\circ} $ ,  $ 43^{\circ} $ ,  $ 45^{\circ} $  and  $ 55^{\circ} $  equivalent to (200), (101), (220), (211) and (301) planes of Sn metal, [35] respectively, suggesting that crystalline Sn QDs are well formed on the graphene sheets. Based on the quality of rGO-Sn QDs, the  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.8}\mathrm{MA}_{0.2}\mathrm{SnI}_3/\mathrm{rGO}-\mathrm{Sn} $  photoactive composite was prepared and its film morphology was compared with those of  $ \mathrm{MASnI}_3 $ ,  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.8}\mathrm{MA}_{0.2}\mathrm{SnI}_3 $ . The FESEM image of  $ \mathrm{MASnI}_3 $  film showed rather rough surface having pinholes along grain boundaries, but  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  film showed pinhole-free smoother morphology (Fig. 1c). The mixed-organic-cation perovsklite (i.e.,  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.8}\mathrm{MA}_{0.2}\mathrm{SnI}_3 $ ) films exhibit uniformly distributed pinhole-free dense crystal grains (Fig. 1d). More importantly, the  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.8}\mathrm{MA}_{0.2}\mathrm{SnI}_3/\mathrm{rGO}-\mathrm{Sn} $  QDs composite film shows uniform, dense morphology without pinholes (Fig. 1e), having larger grains than those of  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.8}\mathrm{MA}_{0.2}\mathrm{SnI}_3 $ , (see the size distribution histogram in Fig. 1f). Such improvement of film morphology not only retards the formation of defects and deep charge-trapping states but also diminishes pathways for oxygen penetration into its inner domain, leading to enhancement of device performance. It is also worth to note that graphene sheets block moisture penetration and ions migration, resulting in stability improvement with less  $ J-V $  hysteresis [28,29]. Hence, the structurally optimized  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.8}\mathrm{MA}_{0.2}\mathrm{SnI}_3/\mathrm{rGO}-\mathrm{Sn} $  QDs composite films have been used for the fabrication of Sn-PSCs. In order to ensure the reliability of device performance, thicknesses of ETL and AL were optimized as a function of device performance. The individual thicknesses of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ , mp-AG and  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.8}\mathrm{MA}_{0.2}\mathrm{SnI}_3 $  layers were examined with varying the spin-coating rate of each solution, and the effect of layer thickness on photovoltaic

parameters is presented in Fig. S3 and Tables S3–S5. The optimal thicknesses, measured from the FESEM images, were   $  \sim40  $   nm for   $  SnO_{2}  $  ,   $  \sim410  $   nm for mp-AG, and   $  \sim395  $   nm for   $  FA_{0.8}MA_{0.2}SnI_{3}  $   layers. Based on the optimal thicknesses of ETL and AL, the Sn-PSCs were fabricated with using the   $  FA_{0.8}MA_{0.2}SnI_{3}/rGO-Sn  $   QDs composites in AL. The cross-sectional FESEM image of the   $  FA_{0.8}MA_{0.2}SnI_{3}/rGO-Sn  $   photoactive composite film on FTO/  $  SnO_{2}/mp-AG  $   substrate is illustrated in Fig. 1g with the energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) elemental mapping that shows distributions of elements (i.e., Sn, Al, I, O and C) in each layer.

# 3.2. Performance of  $  FA_{0.8}MA_{0.2}SnI_3 / rGO - Sn  $  QDs composite based cells

To determine the optimal content of Sn QDs in the rGO-Sn composite, device performance was examined with varying the content of Sn QDs from 0 to 60 wt%. Fig. 2a illustrates the schematic configuration of the composite based Sn-PSCs (i.e., FTO/SnO  $  _{2}  $  /mp-AG/(FA  $  _{0.8}  $  MA  $  _{0.2}  $  SnI  $  _{3}  $  /rGO-Sn)/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au). It is worthwhile to note that the rGO-Sn content in the FA  $  _{0.8}  $  MA  $  _{0.2}  $  SnI  $  _{3}  $  /rGO-Sn composite was optimized to 0.1 wt% (see Fig. S4). The photovoltaic parameters as a function of Sn QDs content are summarized in Table S6. Compared to the performance of the cell without Sn QDs (i.e.,   $  V_{OC} = 0.51  $   V,   $  J_{SC} = 15.24  $   mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , FF = 63.45%, and PCE = 4.96%), the device with 40 wt% Sn QDs in the rGO-Sn QDs composite yielded a best performance (i.e.,   $  V_{OC} = 0.68  $   V,   $  J_{SC} = 16.63  $   mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , FF = 68.1%, and PCE = 7.7%), showing 55% increase in PCE. It is worthwhile to note that compared to the previously reported about cell performance with n-i-p structures [12], the PCE of the rGO-Sn QDs composites based cell (i.e., 7.7%) is quite noticeable and significantly high. Thus, unless mentioned, the champion device fabricated with the FA  $  _{0.8}  $  MA  $  _{0.2}  $  SnI  $  _{3}  $  /rGO-Sn photoactive composite contains 40 wt% Sn QDs in rGO-Sn. Such remarkable enhancement of photovoltaic parameters of the composite-based champion cells highlights the impact of rGO-Sn QDs on the performance of lead-free Sn-PSCs, which is attributed to both the suppression of the Sn  $  ^{2+}  $  /Sn  $  ^{4+}  $   oxidation thanks to Sn QDs and fast charge separation/conduction due to the presence of high mobility graphene. This is further confirmed by incident photon-to-charge conversion efficiency (IPCE) measurements (Fig. 2b), which exhibited more absorption of sunlight over 350–870 nm than the reference device based on FA  $  _{0.8}  $  MA  $  _{0.2}  $  SnI  $  _{3}  $  . The integrated values of the   $  J_{SC}  $   from IPCE were calculated 15.8 and 16.6 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   for the reference and champion devices, respectively, which were close to the measured values.

# 3.3. Trap states density and recombination behavior

To examine the trap states density, the electron only devices (EODs) were fabricated with FTO/ETL/AL/PCBM/Au configuration based on reference (i.e.,   $  FA_{0.8}MA_{0.2}SnI_{3}  $  ) and composite (i.e.,   $  FA_{0.8}MA_{0.2}SnI_{3}/rGO-Sn  $  ) in AL. Current-voltage plots of the EOD (Fig. 2c) exhibits that the current increases linearly up to trap-filled limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ), indicating an ohmic-contact, but above   $  V_{TFL}  $   it increases nonlinearly because of injected charge carriers into trap states. It is interesting to observe that for the composite-based champion cell the   $  V_{TFL}  $   shifts to a lower voltage value, suggesting a decrease in trap state density. # 3.5. Suppression mechanism of  $  Sn^{2+} / Sn^{4+}  $  oxidation by rGO-Sn QDs

It is known that the performance of Sn-PSCs suffers from self-doping reaction (Sn(II)/Sn(IV) oxidation) under operation conditions, where the concentration of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   decreases and   $  Sn^{4+}  $   moieties form and create   $  Sn^{2+}  $   vacancies, leading to high defect density and fast degradation. As-formed   $  Sn^{4+}  $   in Sn-perovskite acts as an electron trap site and causes nonradiative recombination. # 4. Conclusions

In summary, this work highlights a new approach to overcome the limitation of environment-friendly alternatives of lead-based PSCs, i.e. fabrication of lead-free Sn-based PSCs using   $  FA_{0.8}MA_{0.2}SnI_{3}/rGO-Sn  $   QDs composite in active layer. The metallic Sn QDs are oxidized and generate abundance of   $  Sn^{2+}  $  , resulting in more stable operation of the composite-based champion device by suppressing the   $  Sn^{2+}/Sn^{4+}  $   oxidation. Compared to the Sn-PSC without Sn QDs the composite-based champion device showed 55% increase in power conversion efficiency. This remarkable improvement of device performance is attributed to strong suppression of   $  Sn^{2+}/Sn^{4+}  $   oxidation as well as reduction of trap state density and recombination. More interestingly, the champion device shows a remarkable reproducibility and stability improvement in both ambient air and inert atmospheres over 30 days, showing a potential for the development of lead-free perovskite solar cells.
