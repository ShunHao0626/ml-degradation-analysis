# High Performance Inverted Perovskite Solar Cells by Reducing Electron Capture Region for Electron Transport Layers

Shengnan Zuo, Xuejie Zhu, Jiangshan Feng, Ziyu Wang, Cong Zhang, Chenyu Wang, Xiaodong Ren, Shengzhou (Frank) Liu, and Dong Yang*

Keywords: perovskite solar cell, electron transport layer, electron capture region, relative permittivity, recombination

Abstract: The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of inverted perovskite solar cells (i-PSCs) is lower than the normal structures. The low efficiency is mainly ascribed to the inferior properties of commonly-used [6,6]-phenyl C61 butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) electron transport layers (ETLs) such as complexity in achieving high quality films, low electron mobility, imperfect energy level for electron extraction, and large electron capture region. Herein, we develop the bulk heterojunction (BHJ) ETLs, which compose of PCBM and conjugated polymer materials. The electron mobility of BHJ film is enhanced by more than 3 times compared to pristine PCBM, and its Fermi level is close to the conduction band of perovskite. The high electron mobility and suitable energy level result in efficient electron extraction. Further, the electron capture region of BHJ film significantly decreases to  $ 1.20 \times 10^{-18} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3} $  from  $ 3.70 \times 10^{-17} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3} $  for pristine PCBM due to increased relative permittivity (enhanced to 5.32 from 3.66 for PCBM), leading to reduced trap-assistant recombination at perovskite/ETL interface. Organic-inorganic halide perovskite materials have rapidly became appealing candidates for photovoltaics due to its outstanding properties such as optimal band gap, large absorption coefficient, extremely low trap density, high charge carrier mobility, small exciton binding energy, long electron/hole diffusion length, and so on.  $  ^{[1-9]}  $   The lab certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite photovoltaics is as high as 24.2% within just ten years through optimizing device structures and fabrication technologies.  $  ^{[10-20]}  $   The perovskite devices are divided into normal (n-i-p) and inverted (p-i-n) architectures, which depend on the deposition order of carrier transport layer.  $  ^{[21]}  $   The normal device is comprised of mesoporous and planar structures, determined by the morphology of electron transport layers (ETLs).  $  ^{[22]}  $   The commonly-used compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs often need high temperature treatment regardless of mesoporous and planar perovskite devices, limiting their transition. Although there are some reports on relatively low temperature ETLs such as   $  SnO_{2}  $ , the devices with low temperature ETLs display severe current density-voltage (J-V) hysteresis.  $  ^{[23,24]}  $   The extra process is required to treat the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETLs or   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanoparticles is adopted to suppress/eliminate this hysteresis,  $  ^{[11,21]}  $   increasing the fabrication cost and complexity.

Inverted perovskite solar cells (i-PSCs) have been developed to overcome this issue involved with normal structure devices. This type of i-PSCs shows the hysteresis-free and does not need high temperature ETLs, indicating the huge potential application. However, the efficiency is too low compared to normal structure of perovskite devices. The most commonly-used ETL in i-PSC is a small organic molecule of [6,6]-phenyl C61 butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM), which has some defects to restrain the efficiency of i-PSCs. The thin PCBM layer hardly forms a continuous film on rough surface over large perovskite grains, resulting in severe nonradiative recombination due to the direct contact between cathode and perovskite absorber.[25] Thermal evaporation of LiF or bathocuproine to fill the cavies in PCBM thin layer to solve this problem,[26,27] that adds not only complications of fabrication process but also cost. Further, PCBM has the low electron mobility and imperfect energy level, impeding the electrons transport. Worse still, the small relative permittivity of PCBM generates a large electron capture region, which is the volume occupied by the positive traps, causing severe nonradiative recombination at perovskite/ETL interface. There have been reports on blending PCBM to produce more photogenerated carriers,[28-34] but small relative permittivity due to the intrinsic property of PCBM are still the challenge.

In our previous work, we used n-type polymer doped PCBM as the ETLs to improve the performance of i-PSCs.  $  ^{[35]}  $   In present study, we develop the efficient bulk-heterojunction (BHJ) films as the ETLs, which couple with PCBM and conjugated polymer materials of poly[N-9'-heptadecanyl-2,7-carbazole-alt-5,5-(4',7'-di-2-thienyl-2',1',3'-benzothiadiazole)] (PCDTBT). The BHJ film not only acts as an effective ETL but also produces the photogenerated charges. The experimental results reveal that this BHJ film can completely cover the perovskite surface to avoid direct contact between cathode and absorber. The BHJ film has higher electron mobility and perfect energy level compared to pristine PCBM, leading to effective electron extraction in i-PSCs. What's more, the relative permittivity of BHJ film

The outstanding properties of BHJ film including good film formation, high electron mobility, desired energy level and small electron capture region inspire us to fabricate i-PSCs. Figure 2a shows the structure of inverted perovskite device. Wherein fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) is employed as the anode, poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) acts as the hole transport layer,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  plays the absorber, pristine PCBM or BHJ film is used as the ETL, and silver is selected as the cathode. The cross sectional SEM images of complete devices are shown in Figure S4. The pristine PCBM thin layer presents the cavities, while BHJ film can fully cover the perovskite surface, in good accordance with the top view SEM measurements. Figure S5 shows the  $ J - V $  curves of i-PSCs with the different ratio of PCDTBT and PCBM, and the parameters are listed in Table S1. The perovskite device based on pristine PCBM gives the lowest efficiency, which is caused by some openings of PCBM thin layer, leading to severe charge recombination at perovskite/PCBM interface owing to direct contact between perovskite and cathode.[39] The efficiency is enhanced with increasing the weight ratio between PCDTBT and PCBM, and the optimal value is 0.25. Beyond the threshold of 0.25, the PCE decreases since the properties of BHJ film dominated by PCDTBT, which demonstrates inferior electron selectivity. The  $ J - V $  curves of the devices based on different thickness of BHJ ETLs were also measured, as shown in Figure S6, and the parameters are summarized in Table S2. The i-PSC with thin BHJ ETL (12 nm) shows the lowest PCE, which is likely due to the inferior coverage. While the thick BHJ film (43 nm) would generate the large series resistance ( $ R_{\mathrm{s}} $ ), leading to lower efficiency. The optimized thickness of BHJ ETL is 28 nm, and the following researches fix on this thickness. The hysteresis of i-PSCs with different ETLs was investigated by scanning at reverse and forward directions, as shown in Figure 2b. Figure 2d shows the PCE histogram of 20 individual devices with different ETLs, and the key parameters are summarized in Table S3 and S4. In conclusion, the PCE of inverted perovskite device is enhanced to  $ 18.84\% $  using BHJ ETL, enhanced by about  $ 15\% $  compared to pristine PCBM. Meanwhile, the inverted perovskite cells exhibit excellent stability due to the more hydrophobic BHJ films. This work opens up a feasible path to improve the efficiency of i-PSCs by interface engineering.

# Experimental Section

Materials: Methylamine solution (33 wt.% in absolute ethanol), hydroiodic acid (HI) (57 wt% in water),  $ \gamma $ -butyrolactone (GBL, 99%) and Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) were purchased from Aladdin.  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  (99.9985%) was purchased from Alfa Aesar. PCBM (99.5%), PCDTBT (average  $ \mathrm{M_w} $  100,000-140,000), PEDOT:PSS (AI4083) and silver (> 99.99%) were purchased from Sigma Aldrich.

$  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  synthesis:  $  CH_{3}NH_{2}  $  and HI solution were mixed with the ratio of 1.2:1 in a round bottom flask and kept stirred at 0 °C for 3 h. The solvents were removed by rotary evaporator at 60 °C. The precipitate was dissolved in absolute ethanol, and then precipitated was obtained by adding diethyl ether. Above step was repeated until obtaining the purified  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $ .

Finally, the white powder was dried at 60 °C in a vacuum oven for 24 hours.

Perovskite device fabrication: Glass/FTO was cleaned by acetone, isopropyl alcohol and ethanol succession by ultrasonic bath for 15 min, and dried by flowing nitrogen. The cleaned glass/FTO substrates were treated by ultraviolet-ozone machine for 15 min. PEDOT:PSS was filtered by a 0.22 μm filter, and then spin-coated on glass/FTO substrates at 4500 rpm for 1 min. The samples were annealed at 120 °C for 30 min in ambient air. After cooling down to room temperature, the samples were transferred into glovebox to deposit perovskite absorbers. 646.8 mg PbI₂ and 222.6 mg CH₃NH₃I were dissolved in 1 mL GBL and DMSO (7:3, volume/volume) mixed solvent. The solution was filtered by a 0.45 μm filter prior to use. The precursor solution was spin coated on glass/FTO/PEDOT:PSS substrates by anti-solvent method. The spin process consists of consecutive two steps: firstly, the spin rate was 1000 rpm for 20 s. Secondly, the spin rate was 4000 rpm for 45 s. At the last 20 second during the second step, 200 μL chlorobenzene was drop. The film s were annealed at 95 °C for 10 min in a glove box. After that, 20 mg/mL PCBM chlorobenzene solution and different weight ratio of PCBM and PCDTBT mixed solution were spin-coated on perovskite films using different spin rates. Finally, 100 nm-thick Ag was thermally evaporated using metal mask. The device area was defined by a metal mask with an aperture area of 0.096 cm².

Characterization: The  $ J-V $  curves of devices were measured by a Keithley 2400 source under ambient air. The IPCE was performed on the NREL measurement system, and the light source was a  $ 300\mathrm{W} $  xenon lamp. The performance of inverted perovskite solar cells (i-PSCs) is significantly improved using bulk-heterojunction electron transport layers. High efficiency originates from reduced trap-assistant recombination owing to shortened electron capture region, high electron mobility and suitable energy level of electron transport layers.
