# ABSTRACT

In sequential deposition method of lead-halide perovskite material, the   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer morphology and remnant   $  PbI_{2}  $   play an important role in enhancing the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the perovskite solar cell. However, humidity level affects the   $  PbI_{2}  $   and perovskite film morphology, resulting in defect sites and recombination centers on the surface and within the bulk of the material, thus impeding the overall device performance and stability. To address this, we report incorporation of tetrahydrofuran (THF) additive in   $  PbI_{2}  $  -DMF precursor solution, to improve the quality of   $  PbI_{2}  $   thin film and to prevent the water interaction directly with   $  PbI_{2}  $   under high humidity environment. The O-donor THF interacts with   $  PbI_{2}  $   resulting in the homogeneous, dense and pinhole-free layer as compared to   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer without additive. The Perovskite layer so obtained from pinhole-free   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer is compact and smooth resulting in a significant reduction of defects/traps. The device fabricated with modified perovskite ~ 50% humidity atmosphere, resulted in 15% efficiency with high reproducibility. Moreover, the THF modified non-encapsulated perovskite device retains its 80% PCE after exposing to 50% relative humidity for 20 days. Our result demonstrates the strategy to fabricate perovskite solar cells with reproducible efficiency in high humidity atmosphere viable for large-scale production.

KEYWORDS: tetrahydrofuran (THF), two-step deposition, pinhole-free  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , perovskite solar cells, high humidity, long-term stability

# INTRODUCTION

This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved

With the exceptional optoelectronic properties such as high absorption coefficient $ ^{[1]} $ , ambipolar carrier mobility $ ^{[2]} $ , low lying valance band and long carrier diffusion length $ ^{[3]} $ , perovskites have attracted a lot of research attention. After the pioneering work on perovskite solar cells by Kojima et al. $ ^{[4]} $ , the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells, which have been assembled in various device architectures such as planar (free of mesoporous layer) $ ^{[5]} $ , mesoporous $ ^{[6]} $  and meso-superstructure architectures (employing insulating Al $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  as a scaffold) $ ^{[7]} $  has rocketed to more than 23% $ ^{[6]} $  in just 10 years. Such rise in PCE is due to concerted research efforts towards various deposition techniques which include one-step deposition, sequential deposition $ ^{[8]} $ , vapor deposition $ ^{[9]} $ , solvent engineering $ ^{[10]} $ , compositional engineering $ ^{[11]} $ , intermolecular exchange process $ ^{[12]} $ . Generally, the quality of perovskite is highly sensitive to the surrounding atmosphere (humidity) and therefore perovskite layer is deposited either in nitrogen-filled glove boxes and/or in dry conditions which is not suitable for practical applications $ ^{[13]} $ . Among various deposition techniques, sequential deposition method $ ^{[8]} $  is one of the facile approach for depositing perovskite film as in this the perovskite layer can be formed at relatively high humidity (20% > humidity > 60%) $ ^{[14]} $  atmosphere.

It is well known that in the sequential deposition method, the morphology of the final perovskite layer profoundly depends on the quality of   $  PbI_{2}  $   film.  $  ^{[8, 12]}  $   Hence, in mesoporous architecture, using the two-step deposition process, several techniques have been applied to engineer   $  PbI_{2}  $   morphology including the controlled crystallization by producing   $  PbI_{2}  $  -DMSO complex  $  ^{[12]}  $  , anti-solvent dripping over the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film  $  ^{[15]}  $  , pre-heating technique  $  ^{[16]}  $  , mixed solvent process for smooth perovskite layer with regular grain size  $  ^{[17]}  $   and incorporation of additives such as of   $  H_{2}O^{[18]}  $  , etc. crafting the route for high efficiency.

Previously, Umadisingu et al., showed direct evidence and reported that remnant   $  PbI_{2}  $   mostly reside at the perovskite/  $  TiO_{2}  $   mesoporous layer interface  $  ^{[19]}  $  . This excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   content in   $  TiO_{2}  $   mesoporous has a beneficial effect on device performance as it helps to abolish

recombination at the interfaces  $  ^{[19]}  $  . Additionally, it also helps to enhance the long-term stability of the cell by protecting the perovskite from UV-sensitive   $  TiO_{2}  $    $  ^{[20]}  $  . However, in sequential deposition technique, the   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer shows non-uniform morphology with a large number of pinholes when deposited in high humidity condition which eventually results in non-uniform perovskite layer  $  ^{[14]}  $   generating the defect sites which are detrimental to the device performance and long-term stability of the cell. In order to enhance the quality of the perovskite layer, in addition to device fabrication in high humidity environment (by sequential deposition process), developing compact pinhole-free   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer is necessary. To address this, we report a facile method by incorporating tetrahydrofuran (THF) as an additive into the   $  PbI_{2}  $  -DMF precursor solution, through which compact pin-hole free   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer was obtained and the resulting perovskite containing device (fabricated in 50% humidity atmosphere) showed enhancement in PCE up to 15% with high reproducibility owing to the elimination of defect sites/recombination centers.

# 2. EXPERIMENTAL

# 2.1 PSC fabrication

FTO-coated transparent conductive glass sheet (17Ω sq  $  ^{-1}  $  , Nippon, 1.1 mm thickness) was patterned by etching with Zn powder and Hydrochloric acid (HCl, 3 M, Sigma-Aldrich), followed by cleaning with 2% Hellmanax soap solution (Sigma-Aldrich), deionized water and ethanol for 15 minutes each with ultra-sonication. Before depositing compact layer, cleaned FTO plates were exposed to UV ozone to remove the traces of organic residues for 10 min. The TiO  $  _{2}  $  compact layer (CL) was deposited from the precursor solution of concentration 0.15 M and 0.3 M titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) (75 wt% in

This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved

isopropanol, Aldrich) in isopropanol (Wako). Initially, 0.15 M solution was spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 30 sec followed by annealing at 125 °C for 5 min. The same procedure was followed twice to deposit 0.3 M and finally, the substrates were annealed at 550 °C for 30 minutes. TiCl₄ (80 mM) treatment was performed at 70 °C for 60 minutes followed by annealing at 550 °C for 30 minutes. The mesoporous layer was obtained by spin-coating a commercially available nanocrystalline TiO₂ paste (Dyesol, 18NR-T) at 4000 rpm for 30 sec followed by annealing at 550 °C for 30 min in a muffle furnace. Perovskite layer was deposited by the route of sequential deposition[8], for standard samples (without THF additive) initially 1M PbI₂ (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.999%) solution in DMF (Sigma-Aldrich anhydrous, 99.8%) was spin-coated on the mesoporous substrate at 6500 rpm for 30 sec. followed by drying at 70 °C for 30 min. On the other hand, smooth PbI₂ film was obtained by adding THF volume ratio of 1:10 (THF:DMF) with DMF-PbI₂ precursor solution (1M) and after stirring for 15 minutes the additive containing solution was spin-coated following the same procedure as mentioned above. After cooling down, the PbI₂ films were dipped in methylammonium iodide solution in 2-propanol (10 mg/ml) for 50 sec. to obtained perovskite films. The perovskite coated substrates were rinsed in 2-propanol and were dried using spin coater at 4500 rpm for 10 sec. followed by heating at 70°C for 30 minutes. The hole transporting material (HTM) was prepared by dissolving 84 mg of spiro-OMeTAD, 14.053 μl of 4-start-butyl pyridine (TBP, Aldrich), 27.013 μl of a stock solution of 520 mg/ml of lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)-imide in acetonitrile in 1 ml of chlorobenzene. HTM solution was spin-coated over the obtained perovskite films at 3000 rpm for 30 sec. After spiro-OMeTAD deposition, the substrates were kept in dark for overnight oxidation. The fabrication procedure was completed by depositing 80 nm Au as back contact electrode via thermal evaporation. The active area of the obtained cell was 0.09 cm².

# RESULT AND DISCUSSION

As mentioned, the perovskite layer is drastically affected by the quality of   $  PbI_{2}  $   under layer and thus obtaining uniform   $  PbI_{2}  $   under layer is critically important. Primarily there are two factors which govern the uniformity of   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer; crystallization process  $  ^{[21]}  $   and proper dissolution of   $  PbI_{2}  $   into the main solvent.  $  ^{[22]}  $   In sequential deposition  $  ^{[8, 23]}  $   of perovskite, DMF is used as a solvent to dissolve   $  PbI_{2}  $ , however presence of the non-negligible amount of water in the   $  PbI_{2}  $   leads to its improper solubility in precursor solution at the molecular level.  $  ^{[22]}  $   This can directly impact the crystallization process, consequently, give rise to non-uniform   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer with a large number of pinholes.  $  ^{[24]}  $   Herein, we found that a small amount of   $  PbI_{2}  $   is soluble in THF (as shown in Figure1b). The absorbance spectra of   $  PbI_{2}  $   dissolved in THF (Figure 1a) shows a characteristic peak at around 365 nm which is different from the

This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved

absorption band of DMF-PbI $ _2 $  (at  $ \sim $ 325 nm) and PbI $ _2 $  thin film (510 nm $ ^{[25]} $ ), indicating interaction of THF with PbI $ _2 $  and formation of new adduct; (PbI $ _2 $ -THF $ _x $ ). Although the exact mechanism of the PbI $ _2 $ -THF $ _x $  complex formation in the present work is unclear, we suspect it is formed by binding of lead (Pb $ ^{2+} $ ) with O-donor moiety present in THF (Lewis base). After adding THF as an additive into the DMF-PbI $ _2 $  solution, the color of the solution changed as shown in Figure1b. The UV-Vis spectra showed two characteristic absorption peaks at 365 nm and 325 nm compared to non-additive PbI $ _2 $ -DMF solution, showing single absorption peak at 325 nm. This indicated that, after adding THF as an additive, precursor solution contains both PbI $ _2 $ -THF and PbI $ _2 $ -DMF adduct.

On the other hand, the additive guided  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  layer showed uniform pinhole-free morphology (Figure 2b). We suspect that formation of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -THF $ _x $  intermediate adduct allows the better dissolution of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  into the main solvent DMF through which smooth, uniform and pinhole-free layer were obtained, however, a detail investigation is required and is under progress. The optical absorption spectra, obtained by UV-vis spectroscopy, of the perovskite film with and without THF additive, shows a sharp absorption band at 780 nm which is in consistence with the previous reports (Figure 3a). Moreover, it can be noted that the absorbance of the “THF-additive” and “non-additive” guided perovskite films is retained over the entire visible range with no difference in the perovskite thickness (Figure 2e, 2f), which also reflects that THF-additive guided procedure did not have any detrimental effect on perovskite film formation. The XRD diffraction patterns (Figure 3b) of both the “THF-additive” and “non-additive” film showed peaks at   $  2\theta = 14.03^{\circ}  $  ,   $  20.05^{\circ}  $  ,   $  26.42^{\circ}  $  ,   $  31.70^{\circ}  $  ,   $  33.66^{\circ}  $  ,   $  40.45^{\circ}  $   and   $  43.12^{\circ}  $   corresponding to the planes [110], [202], [310], [312], [224] and [314] of tetragonal MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   respectively. Hence, no structural difference between the two films signified no detrimental effect of THF-addition on PbI  $  _{2}  $   and perovskite formation. Instead, slightly higher peak intensity (  $  2\theta = 14.03^{\circ}  $  ) in the latter case implied better crystallinity of the film (Figure S1). In addition, remnant PbI  $  _{2}  $   was found in both films. Advantageously, the addition of THF produced a smooth PbI  $  _{2}  $   layer (Figure 2b).

In order to check the influence of tuned perovskite morphology on the device performance, photovoltaic characterization of the devices fabricated by the procedure mentioned in the experimental section was performed. Figure 4a and Figure 4b displays the current density-voltage (J-V) curves of the champion devices of all the cases studied and measured under light (100 mW/cm²) and dark respectively. From Figure 4a and box plot (Figure S2 and Figure 4e), it was clear that perovskite devices obtained from THF-additive route showed enhancement in short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) and open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) leading to an overall enhancement in PCE as compared to standard perovskite (without additive) based devices. Furthermore, the PCE box plot (Figure 4d) shows the distribution of efficiency of THF-additive guided perovskite-based devices in a narrower range, implying its high reproducibility. This performance enhancement can be due to the elimination of defect states and uniform perovskite layer as evident from PL intensity mapping and steady-state PL plots. Incident photon to conversion efficiency (IPCE) of the best performing THF-additive devices showed slightly improved photon-to-current conversion than the no-additive cells over the entire wavelength range (Figure 4c). Even though the IPCE was improved in the entire range, the improvement was much higher in the shorter wavelength regime compared

to longer wavelength regime (Figure 4c). The IPCE of perovskite solar cells in the shorter wavelength regime <500 nm is strongly influenced by the interface layer and interface quality of devices whereas the longer wavelength regime, >500 nm, is strongly dependent on the quality of perovskite material and interfaces  $  ^{[27]}  $  . This clearly indicates that the interface layer and the interface quality of the THF guided perovskite devices improved resulting in reduced recombination and enhanced charge transport property. According to the previous report  $  ^{[19]}  $  , the remnant   $  PbI_{2}  $   (in the sequential deposition of perovskite) mostly resides at the interface of perovskite and mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer. Corroborating the point with our IPCE results indicates that the THF treated uniform pinhole-free   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer (surrounding   $  TiO_{2}  $   mesoporous layer) promoted better interfacial connection and prevented charge carrier recombination leading to an overall enhancement in the device performance. Integrated current, calculated from IPCE plot, showed   $  18.9\ mA/cm^{2}  $  ,   $  21.9\ mA/cm^{2}  $   for standard, and THF guided devices respectively which are well matching with the   $  J_{sc}  $   from the device J-V characteristic plots.

As discussed above, a large number of pinholes in non-additive based   $  PbI_{2}  $   morphology have been observed (Figure 2a). From this, it can be suspected that the distribution of the remnant   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer, at the interface of   $  TiO_{2}  $ /perovskite, is non-uniform due to non-uniform and pinhole containing   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer. This gives rise to non-uniform perovskite layer (as evident from 2D PL mapping) and poor perovskite/  $  TiO_{2}  $   interfacial connection (Figure 4c) creating defects within the bulk of perovskite and at the interfaces. These defects can cause charge carrier recombination in bulk and at the interfaces, thus lower device performance. On the other hand, for the THF guided perovskite device, the defects are significantly reduced which can be attributed to compact perovskite layer and efficient charge transport across the interfaces (reduced interfacial recombination) as evident from IPCE results. Suppressed recombination in the THF-additive case was also supported by electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) (Figure 5a) and open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) decay (OCVD) (Figure 5b) measurement. The EIS Nyquist plots (Figure 5a) show three semicircles, of which the first one in high-frequency region is small and hidden by the second bigger semicircle, but could be confirmed from the Bode plot (Figure S3b). Hence, three resistance-capacitance (RC) components and one series resistance constitute the equivalent circuit (shown in Figure S3a). The first semicircle (at high frequency) of the Nyquist plot corresponds to charge transfer at back contact electrode interface (i.e. spiro-OMeTAD and Au interface)  $  ^{[28]}  $  . The second arc appearing in the intermediate frequency range is attributed to the recombination in perovskite and/or perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD interface  $  ^{[29]}  $   while the third low-frequency arc corresponds to charge transfer at the   $  TiO_{2}  $ /perovskite interface. The larger second semicircle observed in the THF-treated sample in comparison to the reference (non-treated) sample testified less recombination in the former case, which was also supported by slower   $  V_{oc}  $   decay in the THF-additive guided perovskite

Finally, we performed the stability test of the perovskite devices obtained from the additive and non-additive guided route as shown in Figure 6. THF additive based non-encapsulated perovskite devices showed enhance stability when exposed to the ambient atmosphere (relative humidity of  $ \sim 50\% $ ) compared to non-additive based devices when stored under the same condition. It is reported that perovskite layer with defect states tends to degrade rapidly[1, 23] which can be the reason for rapid degradation in the device performance in the non-additive case while the enhanced stability in THF additive case can be credited to the formation of compact perovskite layer, enhanced crystallinity, and reduced defect states. The obtained result indicates that in mesoporous architecture, obtaining smooth and pinhole-free  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  layer is critically important in order to obtain a perovskite layer with fewer defects and voids. This uniformity in the perovskite layer can assist smooth interfacial connection with its neighboring layers leading to efficient carrier extraction with which improved device performance can be obtained when fabricated in higher humidity condition. # CONCLUSION

We developed a strategy to obtain a uniform, pinhole-free  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  layer by adding THF as an additive to DMF- $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  precursor solution. The O-donor solvent THF coordinates to the  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  cation in the solution, and thus, controlling crystallization of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . Advantageously, it also acts as a water absorber to trap water molecules from the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  solution which helps in further dissolution of lead iodide in DMF and prevents the direct interaction with water while spin coating under high humidity atmosphere. Perovskite layer obtained from THF additive based  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  layer was rather crystalline as depicted from XRD with fewer non-radiative defect sites and vacancies in contrast to

This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved

perovskite layer without any additive based  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film, as confirmed by PL intensity mapping and steady-state plots. THF guided perovskite devices (fabricated in  $ 50\% $  humidity atmosphere) showed enhancement in all the device parameters, resulting in overall PCE enhancement. Impedance spectroscopic and open-circuit voltage decay measurement results reveal reduced recombination in THF guided perovskite devices. Additionally, long-term stability test (without encapsulation) showed additive based perovskite devices are quite stable when exposed to a relative  $ 50\% $  humidity atmosphere. Our present result demonstrates a strategy to develop quality perovskite layer in high humidity atmosphere with augmented efficiency viable for large scale production.
