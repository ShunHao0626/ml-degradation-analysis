# ABSTRACT

In order to minimize the environmental pollution and realize large-scale production, green chemicals are very necessary. In this work, we report the impact of ethyl acetate (EA) antisolvent treatment on the performance of fully air-fabricated planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs). The proposed spin-coating approach with the antisolvent treatment was compared with the sequential spin-coating deposition process. Our findings demonstrated that the antisolvent treatment with green EA enhances the optoelectronic features and morphology of the fabricated methylammonium lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{MAPbI}_3) $  film. Also, the charge transfer is enhanced, and hence, the interfacial recombination is significantly suppressed, resulting in boosted power conversion efficiency of  $ 17.75\% $  for the best PSC with enhanced thermal and ambient stability.

Thus, fabricating defects-free perovskite film with high-quality has been considered as one important challenge to obtain efficient and more stabilized PSCs [10]. Besides, the major difficulty in developing highly effective PSCs is accomplishing smooth perovskite morphologies and high crystalline structure, which involve accurate control of the process and skilled operating personnel [11].

Many metal oxides were employed as an electron transfer layer (ETL) in the PSCs such as   $  TiO_{2}  $  , ZnO,   $  SnO_{2}  $  ,   $  ZrO_{2}  $   and so on [12–15]. Among them, ZnO ELTs, which are easy to prepare and have unique optoelectronic merits, provided PVs with high PCE [16, 17]. Nevertheless, the air-processed PSCs stability constructed based on this ETL needs to be enhanced. To this end, several techniques for manufacturing perovskite films have been proposed [18]. Among the different routes to fabricate perovskites, for example inkjet printing, thermal evaporation, spin-coating, and so on, the most common and widely employed is the antisolvent modification approach [13, 19–21]. For one-step process,   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   are dispersed to produce the mixed perovskite solution, followed by depositing on a photoelectrode. This method is considered to be better in terms of step and time consuming [22, 23]. However, apart from the toxicity of lead material, organic solutions, particularly antisolvent employed in one-step fabrication approach, are mostly highly toxic, which is also a main hinder for the commercialization [24]. Hence, in order to minimize the environmental pollution and realize large-scale production, a green solvent engineering is very necessary.

Not only to prepare efficient and stable PSCs free from the glovebox, but also to develop eco-friendly production is expected. Here, we reported a spin coating of the perovskites mixture uses a green antisolvent modification to promote the removing of the host-solvent and initiate crystallization of the perovskites. The antisolvent engineering approach relying on the use of green EA provides slow nucleation of perovskite with obtained dense and high crystallinity. Interestingly, the champion cell with a FTO/ZnO/perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au structure yields an optimal PCE of 17.75%, which is one of the best PCE of completely ambient-processed ZnO-

planar device without employing of the class of an antisolvent.

# Experimental part

# Materials

ZnO powder was prepared by an approach described in the literature   $  [25, 26]  $  . Dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%), methylammonium iodide (MAI), chlorobenzene (CB, 99.9%), and lead iodide (PbI  $  _{2}  $  ) were provided from Dyesol. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 98%), spiro-OMeTAD, EA (99.8%), and 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP) were provided from Sigma-Aldrich. Fluorine-doped SnO  $  _{2}  $   (FTO, 5 Ω/sq.) glass was obtained from Solaronix. Note that all other materials were provided from Merck.

# Solution preparation

For ETL solution, 100 mg of ZnO was dissolved in 50 ml of CB by stirring for 15 min at 25 °C. PbI  $  _{2}  $  with 458 mg and 65 mg of DMSO were added in 500 mg of DMF and mixed for 60 min at 30 °C. MAI (40 mg) was dispersed in 2 ml of isopropanol by stirring for 15 min at 25 °C. To prepare the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskite mixture of one-step antisolvent process, 485 mg of PbI  $  _{2}  $  and 155 mg of MAI in 2 ml of DMF and DMSO at 4:1 v/v. The hole transfers later (HTL) solution was synthesized by mixing 70 mg of spiro-OMeTAD, 27.4  $  \mu  $  l of TBP, and 17  $  \mu  $  l of bis(trifluoromethane)-sulfonimide lithium in 2 ml of CB.

# PSCs fabrication

FTO glasses were cleaned using detergent, followed by sonicating sequentially in distilled water, isopropyl alcohol, and acetone, then dried at 50 °C. The ZnO dispersion was deposited onto the FTO at 4000 rpm for 40 s, and annealed at 110 °C for 30 min. To prepare two-step perovskite,   $  Pb_{2}  $   solution was deposited onto ZnO electrode at 4000 rpm for 30 s and heated at 90 °C for 25 min. After   $  PbI_{2}  $   deposition, MAI precursors were deposited above   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer at 4000 rpm for 30 s and then baked at 85 °C for 15 min. In the case of one-step fabrication,   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   precursor solution was fabricated onto the synthesized electrode via a typical green antisolvent process. The mixed   $  PbI_{2}  $ :MAI solution was spin-coated on the ZnO

# Characterizations

The surface morphologies of the perovskites were characterized with FESEM (TESCAN, Mira 3) images. X-ray diffraction spectra (XRD) of the perovskite film were examined employing a Bruker, D8 advance X-ray diffraction spectrometer. The absorption measurements of the perovskites were investigated via an Analytic Jena, Specord 250 spectrophotometer. The photoluminescence (PL) response of perovskites was recorded via a Teifsanje, FL-Ar-2015 spectrometer system. The photovoltaic characteristics of the PSCs under simulated irradiation of AM 1.5 were studied with a Keithley Model 2400 under simulated irradiation of  $ 100\mathrm{mW / cm^2} $ . The devices were covered with an effective area of  $ 0.12~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ . The I-V test was conducted for 10 devices per variable. To measure the IPCE measurements of the devices, a Newport IPCE device was used. The Nyquist measurements were recorded employing electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (Zennium 400147).

# Results and discussion

Herein, a simple spin-coating approach with two procedures was used to fabricate planar  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  based solar cells, as demonstrated in Fig. 1. Firstly, sequential spin-coating deposition process was conducted by, respectively, coating  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and MAI solutions over ZnO ETL. For comparison, a green antisolvent of EA was used to fabricate one-step  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film without humidity control. In this content, several measurements were performed to make deeper insights in the effect of deposition process on the PSC performance.

To probe the effect of EA antisolvent modification on the air-processed  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film, the series of measurements were carried out. Figure 2a depicts UV-vis

spectra of MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite deposited with two spin-coating methods. As shown, the UV-vis absorption of EA-based MAPbI $ _3 $  layer is stronger than that of MAPbI $ _3 $  deposited with two-steps method within 500–680 nm, harvesting more light for highly efficient PSC. We also determined band gaps of perovskite films from absorption spectra [27]. The band gaps of the perovskite layer were found to be the same value of 1.575 eV from their Tauc plots (Fig. 2b). The steady-state PL patterns of PSCs are revealed in Fig. 2c, the PL peak of perovskite treated with EA antisolvent distinctly suppressed, further indicating that the better charge transfer is presented in the PSCs after EA treatment and signifying less recombination processes [1]. Moreover, with using one-step method, the FWHM showed decreasing tendency from 36 to 29 nm. The FWHM of PL peak could be related with the mean size of grains and their distribution. With the utilization one-step method, the mean size of perovskite grains was significantly increased. The FESEM tests were used to measure the average size and the size distribution of the perovskite (Fig. 3c). Considering that the perovskite film fabricated with one-step has the narrower FWHM but has the broader size distribution. The same findings were also reported for inorganic CsPbBr $ _3 $  perovskite, where the average size of CsPbBr $ _3 $  seemed has more impact on the FWHM, regardless of the compositional variation. [28] Also, the surface defects could result in the broadening of the FWHM of the PL emission peak, due to disorder of the band structure by trap states, and as well, reduce the photoluminescence. [29] Fig. 2d illustrates the XRD patterns of the perovskites fabricated without and with the green EA antisolvent. The characteristic peaks emerged at  $ 2\theta = 14.12^{\circ} $ ,  $ 28.63^{\circ} $ , and  $ 31.89^{\circ} $  can be related to the (110), (220), and (310) facets, respectively, implying the development of MAPbI $ _3 $  layer with a tetragonal phase in the case of both MAPbI $ _3 $  samples [7]. Most important, we detect that the intensity of the main (110) plane for the MAPbI $ _3 $  with EA modification is remarkably improved, consistent with the better crystallinity. Also, it can be seen that diffraction signal of PbI $ _2 $  ( $ 12.57^{\circ} $ ) appeared in two-steps-based MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite. The PbI $ _2 $  peak was clearly reduced after EA treatment, indicating that the perovskite film formed without unwanted PbI $ _2 $  residual.

# Conclusion

In brief, with a PSC architecture of FTO/ZnO/perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD/Au, highly efficient and stable low-temperature solution-processed PSCs were fabricated in open air. The high quality perovskites with preferential crystal orientation, low density trap states and, less charge recombination were achieved via green EA antisolvent treatment. Based on EA-based perovskite as photoactive layers, the planar PSC yields a highest PCE of 17.75%. This is one of the highest recorded PCE of ambient-fabricated PSC-based ZnO ETL without using of the class of CB antisolvent. In addition to the high PCE, the unencapsulated cell displays good long-term stability, maintaining 90% of its initial performance after kept in air environment for over 50 days. Also, the EA-based PSC aged at 80 °C for 30 days maintains 88% of the original PCE, exhibiting remarkable thermal stability.
