# Abstract

The recent rapid rise in power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has attracted worldwide extensive research. However, the PSC applications are limited by their poor stability due to perovskite degradation in moisture. We used a fullerene amine interlayer in planar PSCs to reduce the interface barrier between ETL and metal electrode and resist the moisture. The utilization of fullerene amine interlayer allowed for the enhancement of PSCs' performance, showing a highest power conversion efficiency (PCE)  $ >17.2\% $  with small hysteresis. More importantly, the air stability of PSCs with fullerene amine was improved: the unpackaged devices stored in air condition can keep their high performance with no obvious PCE loss in  $ 10\% $  humidity and  $ >90\% $  of the initial PCE in  $ 45\% $  humidity after 20 days.

# Introduction

Organic metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been intensively studied in recent years, whose power conversion efficiency (PCE) record has already reached more than

22.1% by now [1,2]. These revolutionizing solar cells possess excellent properties such as convenient fabrication, strong light harvesting ability, high carrier mobility, tunable band gap and low non-radiative carrier recombination [3,4]. However, practical PSC applications are limited by their poor stability, due to perovskite degradation and interface/device architecture instability [5].

In all device architectures, inverted P-I-N planar PSCs have attracted more and more attention because of their simple fabrication and small hysteresis effect [6,7]. Emergent P-I-N planar PSCs have yielded efficiency as high as  $ 20.3\% $  [8], which is almost comparable to that of conventional N-I-P mesoporous PSCs [9]. In P-I-N planar PSCs conductive poly (3,4-ethylene dioxythiophene): poly (4-styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT: PSS) is generally used as a hole transport layer (HTL) material. However, PEDOT: PSS strongly restricts the device's lifetime and performance due to its hydroscopic nature and low work function (WF) [10,11]. Solution-processed inorganic counterparts such as CuSCN [12,13], NiO [14,15],  $ \mathrm{CuI}_2 $  [16], and  $ \mathrm{Cu_2O} $  [17], with an appropriate WF, have been proposed as promising alternatives to PEDOT: PSS for high device performance and stability among which NiO with intrinsic p-type semiconducting nature has exhibited remarkable performance and stability [18].

It is worth to note that interface barriers usually exist between metal cathode (such as Al, Ag) and existing electron transport layer (ETL) in planar PSCs, due to the mismatch of energy band [19,20]. Some interfacial layers, including LiF [21], Ca [22], BCP [23], PEIE [19], and functionalized fullerene derivatives [24], have been utilized to reduce the interface barrier between ETL and metal electrode to enhance the device performance. However, most of interlayer materials may have bad influence on the stability of devices, due to their weakness in air condition (moisture or/and  $ \mathrm{O}_2 $ ). Therefore, the selection of a proper interlayer material should be beneficial for the long-term stability of PSCs in air. Metal oxides including  $ \mathrm{TiO_x} $  [18,25] and ZnO [26,27], have been introduced as an ETL or interlayer against water and oxygen degradation. Meanwhile, some hydrophobic organic semiconductor materials like

$ \mathrm{F - C_{60} \& bis - C_{60}} $  [28], tertiary and quaternary alkyl ammonium [29] and insulating polymers [8], have also been used to improve the air stability of devices. These hydrophobic interlayers can effectively isolate the humidity and enhance the stability of the perovskite device. In addition, it is known that the incomplete coverage of ETL has negative effect on the PCE and stability of devices [6,26]. For most of organic ETL materials, the film thickness could not be too thick for achieving high device performance due to the limitation of their low carrier mobility. Thus, it is still a big challenge to spin-coat a thin and full coverage ETL on perovskite films with a small roughness.

Recently, fullerene derivatives such as PCBB-2CN-2C8 [30] and DMAPA- $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  [31-32] have been successfully employed as interlayer to enhance device performance by reducing charge recombination loss. Also, our previous studies have demonstrated that [6, 6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid 2-((2-(dimethylamino)ethyl) (methyl)amino) ethyl ester (PCBDAN) can be universally used as an interlayer to improve the performance of polymer solar cells [33-35]. Here, our present study focuses on the fabrication of efficient and stable PSCs under air conditions, through introducing PCBDAN as an interlayer between ETL and metal electrode. As a result, the utilization of PCBDAN interlayer can not only effectively increase the PCE of devices, but also improve the stability of devices due to its hydrophobic property.

# Results and discussion

Our device architecture of PSCs is similar to the common P-I-N planar PSCs, by using NiO and PCBM as the HTL and ETL, respectively. To investigate the effect of PCBDAN on the performance of PSCs, PCBDAN was inserted as an interfacial layer between PCBM and Ag electrode. Figure 1a shows the molecular structure of PCBDAN and the configuration of a PSC. The PCBDAN is an amine group-modified PCBM derivative which has been used as a good cathode interfacial material since it has higher electron mobility than PCBM [36]. In our

devices, the PCBDAN could act as an interfacial dipole layer to reduce the energy barrier and tune the WF of the Ag electrode. The energy diagram of the device with PCBDAN modification is shown in Figure 1b. The WFs of Ag and PCBDAN/Ag were measured by a Kelvin probe force microscope (KPFM). The result shows that the WF of Ag decrease from 4.64 to  $ 4.14\mathrm{eV} $  after modification using PCBDAN (Figure S1), which is more compatible with the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of PCBM  $ (-4.2\mathrm{eV}) $ . The lowered electrode WF is helpful to facilitate improved electron transport from the PCBM to Ag layer by reducing the contact barrier. The SEM cross-sectional micrograph of device with PCBDAN treatment is shown in Figure 1c. It can be seen that the ultra-smooth perovskite film is fully covered by a very thin and uniform PCBM/PCBDAN layers. The thicknesses of the PCBM and PCBDAN are 40 and  $ 15\mathrm{nm} $ , respectively.

Figure 2a shows the top-view SEM image of a perovskite film in our devices. The grains of films are quite uniform, with an average size of  $ \sim 300\mathrm{nm} $ . The AFM image of the top surface of perovskite film shows that the perovskite fabricated in atmosphere are ultra-smooth, with a root-mean-square (RMS) roughness of approximately  $ 5.2\mathrm{nm} $  (Figure 2d). Since the precursor solvent with the anti-solvent allows rapid nucleation/crystallization before obvious growth of perovskite grains, a uniform, fine-grained, and ultra-smooth perovskite film could be attained [37]. The ultra-smooth and uniform perovskite film is beneficial to develop a thin and full coverage ETL in the following step of device fabrication. Both PCBM and PCBDAN layers prepared by spin-coating on the perovskite film are pinhole free, as displayed in Figure 2b and c, respectively. The RMS roughness of the surface is only approximately  $ 1.4\mathrm{nm} $  after spin-coating a PCBM solution  $ (12.5\mathrm{mg~mL}^{-1}) $ , which is probably the lowest recorded roughness in present literatures (Figure 2e). Furthermore, the ultra-smooth PCBM is helpful to obtain a thin and uniform PCBDAN layer, whose RMS roughness could reach approximately  $ 1.0\mathrm{nm} $  (Figure 2f). The full coverage of PCBM/PCBDAN layers can not only

Figure 3a shows current density–voltage (J-V) curves of the devices with and without PCBDAN, measured under one simulated sun illumination. The device without PCBDAN has short circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 19.85 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.02 V and fill factor (FF) of 68%, yielding a PCE of 13.8%. It can be seen that the PCBDAN interlayer can enhance all parameters of PSC. The value of   $  J_{sc}  $   increases from 19.85 to 20.71 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $   from 1.02 to 1.08 V, and FF from 68% to 77% (Table 1). Therefore, a PCE of 17.2% can be obtained for the device with PCBDAN, with an increase of 25% compared with the device without PCBDAN. More importantly, the devices with PCBDAN show quite small hysteresis (Figure S2). We have performed a series of comparative experiments to confirm these effects of PCBDAN on the device performances. Figure 3b shows the histogram PCEs of devices with and without PCBDAN. The average PCE of the devices with PCBDAN is 16.3%, which is much higher than that of the devices without PCBDAN (13.0%). The statistics for the parameters (  $  J_{sc}  $  , FF,   $  V_{oc}  $  ) for these two kinds of devices also show the same trend, as presented in Figure S3.

The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra and integrated   $  J_{sc}  $   of various devices are shown in Figure 3c. The EQE of the device with PCBDAN is higher than that of the device without PCBDAN. The photocurrents integrated from the EQE data are 20.6 and 19.5 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   for the device with and without PCBDAN, respectively. Integrated   $  J_{sc}  $   obtained from EQE spectra are in good agreement with the J-V results. The steady-state   $  J_{sc}  $   and PCE of the device with PCBDAN at a constant bias of 0.90 V were measured under AM 1.5 G 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $   illuminations (Figure 3d). As a result, a steady-state   $  J_{sc}  $   of approximately 18.9 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and a stabilized output power of 17.0% can be achieved, respectively.

Dark current measurement is an effective method to evaluate charge carrier loss through the leakage pathways and the recombination of free carriers in devices during operating condition. Thus, the J-V characteristics were measured under dark conditions, as shown in Figure 4a. It is known that the J-V characteristics in dark can be described by the Shockley diode equation [38]:

$$
J = J _ {0} \left[ \exp \left(\frac {q V}{\eta k T}\right) - 1 \right]
$$

(1)

where   $  J_{0}  $   denotes the saturation current density,   $  \eta  $   the ideality factor, k the Boltzmann constant and T the temperature. The dark J-V characteristics (Figure 4a) show typical diode behavior, with a leakage contribution at low voltages and an exponential regime at intermediate voltages, with a transition to the space-charge-limited regime at the built-in voltage (  $  V_{bi}  $  ). The ideality factor can be independently determined from the slope of the exponential regime of dark J-V characteristics on a semi-logarithmic plot and can be determined by the equation (2).

$$
\eta = \left(\frac {k T}{q} \frac {\partial \ln J}{\partial V}\right) ^ {-}
$$

(2)

The  $ \eta $  approaches unity in the ideal diode, whereas carrier recombination can change the ideality factor up to a value of 2 [39,40]. According to the equation (2), the ideality factor of the device with PCBDAN has a value of 1.58, which is significantly lower than that of the device without PCBDAN (2.10), indicating that carrier recombination is reduced (Figure S4) [41,42]. According to equation (1), it can be obtained that the  $ J_0 $  of the device without PCBDAN was  $ 8.29\times 10^{-6}\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , which is two orders of magnitude larger than that of device with PCBDAN ( $ 2.01\times 10^{-8}\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ ). This decrease in  $ J_0 $  further indicates that carrier recombination is suppressed by the use of PCBDAN [43]. To address the origin of the improvement in  $ V_{oc} $  by PCBDAN modification, we obtain a commonly used equation (3) for  $ V_{oc} $  by reforming the equation (1).

These results show that PCBDAN can significantly improve  $  V_{oc}  $  by minimizing interface related recombination. Furthermore, the series resistances of the devices with and without PCBDAN are 1.83 and 12.86  $  \Omega  $  cm $  ^{-2}  $ , respectively. This is definitely attributed to the decrease of interface barrier for electron transferring between PCBM and Ag due to the PCBDAN modification. Therefore, the FF of devices with PCBDAN gets increased. The decrease of interface barrier at the PCBM/PCBDAN/Ag can also be clarified by the fabrication of electron-only devices with the following device structure: FTO/ZnO/PCBM with and without PCBDAN/Ag. As shown in Figure 4b, the electron current density injected from the side of Ag electrode shows an increase of about 1.8 times in the case of PCBDAN modification, while the one from the FTO/ZnO electrode remains the same for both devices. This improvement in electron current density suggests that the modification with PCBDAN reduces the injection barrier at the PCBM/Ag interface.

Except for the high photovoltaic efficiency, the most important issue is the stability of PSCs under air condition from the viewpoint of practical application. The unpackaged devices with and without PCBDAN were intentionally exposed in air environment at  $ \sim 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  with relative humidity of  $ \sim 45\% $  for 30 days. Figure 5a shows the evolution of PCEs for the devices with and without PCBDAN. The PCE of the device without PCBDAN has lost  $ \sim 60\% $  within 30 days (Figure S5 and table S1). However, the device with PCBDAN shows much better stability and its PCE decrease only  $ 9\% $  after 20 days and  $ 15\% $  within 30 days (Figure S5 and Table S2). photovoltaic parameters like  $  V_{oc}  $ ,  $  J_{sc}  $ , and FF versus time are summarized in Figures 5b–d. It can be seen that the  $  V_{oc}  $  has a slight increase during the initial period of storage in both types of devices (Figure 5b). The phenomenon is mainly caused by the reduction of charge traps in the perovskite film due to an aging effect, as reported previously [44]. Meanwhile, the WF of PCBM would decrease with time in air [45], which is more compatible with the LUMO (-3.9 eV) of perovskite, and therefore also beneficial for an increase of  $  V_{oc}  $ . Interestingly, no significant decrease in  $  J_{sc}  $  is found in our device with PCBDAN, whereas the  $  J_{sc}  $  of device without PCBDAN shows an obvious decrease (Figure 5c). In order to better understand the reason, X-ray diffraction (XRD) was used to characterize the various samples. From the XRD spectra, a small signal around 12.6° related to PbI $ _2 $  can be seen for both kinds of fresh devices which may be attributed to the thermal decomposition of perovskite during the heat process in atmosphere (Figure S7) [46]. Note that appropriate excess of PbI $ _2 $  in perovskite could be beneficial for the device performance because of the elimination of pervoskite surface states by PbI $ _2 $  [47,48]. With delaying the storing time in air, the PbI $ _2 $  peak for the device without PCBDAN becomes more and more pronounced, and the perovskite film would completely be decomposed into PbI $ _2 $  after 120 days (Figure S7). The decomposition of the perovskite layer should play the key role in the decrease of  $  J_{sc}  $  for the device without PCBDAN. Encouragingly, no significant change of PbI $ _2 $  peaks can be observed for the device with PCBDAN with storing time, even after 120 days. It suggests that the utilization of PCBDAN can significantly prevent the decomposition of perovskite film in the devices. The decomposition of the perovskite layer in the device without PCBDAN would further result in the damage of the PCBM layer and Ag electrode, which can clearly be seen from the SEM cross-sectional micrographs (Figure S8). However, the SEM cross-sectional micrographs of the devices with PCBDAN show undamaged perovskite and PCBM/PCBDAN layers. It is believed that the damage of active–layers and the direct contact between various layers in the device without PCBDAN will cause the reduction of device shunt resistance and the increase of device series

Figure 6 shows the comparison on PCEs of various devices stored in different humidity for 20 days. When stored in dry-air atmosphere with  $ 10\% $  humidity, the devices with and without PCBDAN show good stability, with  $ 0.5\% $  and  $ 3.8\% $  loss of the initial PCEs after 20 days, respectively (Figure S9). It can be seen that the humidity has a strong influence on the stability of PSCs. With the humidity increasing, the stability of both devices gets worse, but the device with PCBDAN has much better stability than the one without PCBDAN. This result demonstrates that introducing a PCBDAN layer can effectively protect the PSC from water corrosion. To better understand the enhancement of the device stability, the contact angles of PCBM and PCBDAN were measured, as shown in Figure 7a. It is clearly seen that the PCBDAN is more hydrophobic than the PCBM, which can delay moisture penetration into the perovskite film. We have dropped the water on the surface of perovskite/PCBM and perovskite/PCBM/PCBDAN, respectively. The stability of the device could be reflected by the change in color over time. The result shows that the perovskite covered by bare PCBM have been decomposed completely within 2 min after dropping water, whereas the perovskite covered by PCBM/PCBDAN has better stability with slight color change at the same time (Figure 7b). Another fact for this point is from the result of a destructive experiment. We have intentionally immersed various devices in water for 1 min (Figure S10). The device with PCBDAN shows highly stable since it has a negligible decrease in PCE from  $ 15.9\% $  to  $ 15.6\% $  (Figure 7c). However, the PCE of the device without PCBDAN has dropped from  $ 13.0\% $  to  $ 6.6\% $  due to the decrease in  $ J_{sc} $ , which must be caused by the decomposition of perovskite in water (Figure 7c). All these results have further confirmed the protective effect of a hydrophobic PCBDAN layer on the devices, beneficial for the stability of devices.

# Conclusions

In summary, PSCs with high efficiency and air stability for the unsealed devices have been achieved by utilizing a hydrophobic PCBDAN interlayer under air conditions. The PCBDAN layer is inserted in between the conventional PCBM ETL and metal electrode, which cannot only reduce the interface barrier through facilitating an efficient electron transport, but also protect the perovskite film and other device-active-layer from the damage of moisture in air. The maximum efficiency of PSC with PCBDAN is 17.2%, much higher than that of devices without PCBDAN. The PSCs with PCBDAN keep their high performance with no obvious PCE loss in 10% humidity and >90% of the initial PCE in 45% humidity after 20 days. These results pave a way to the low-cost fabrication of PSCs with high efficiency and stability in air, with big significance for the practical application of PSCs in photovoltaics.

# Materials and equipment

# Device fabrication

The FTO were cleaned sequentially in deionized water, cleaning fluid, acetone, ethanol under sonication for 5 min, respectively. Then the substrates were treated with UV-Ozone machine for 20 min. The 20 nm NiO layer without doping was deposited by spray pyrolysis following a procedure previously reported in the literature [18]. The 461 mg of PbI $ _2 $ , 159 mg of CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I, and 78 mg of DMSO (molar ratio 1:1:1) were mixed in 600 mg of DMF solution and then spin coated as described in reference [49], but using 5000 rpm as the rotate speed. The perovskite films were heated at 70 °C for 1 min and 100 °C for 10 min on a hotplate respectively. After several minutes, a chlorobenzol solution of PCBM (12.5 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ ) was spin-coated on the top of perovskite film at the rotation speed of 2000 rpm for 40 s and then heated at 70 °C for 30 s. The 2-propanol solution of PCBDAN (0.5 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ ) was spin-coated on the top of PCBM at the rotation speed of 1000 rpm for 60 s. At last, 100 nm thick Ag contacts were deposited under high vacuum (<1.0×10 $ ^{-3} $  Pa). For all devices, the active area is 10 mm $ ^2 $ . The entire perovskite film fabrication process was performed in the air with 20–50% humidity. All the devices were unencapsulated and measured in atmosphere.

# Highlights

- A highest PCE  $ >17.2\% $  was achieved by using PCBDAN as interlayer in planar PSCs.

- The unpackaged devices with PCBDAN can keep high performance without any PCE loss in  $ 10\% $  humidity and  $ >90\% $  of the initial PCE in  $ 45\% $  humidity after 20 days.

- The stability of PSCs with PCBDAN was improved due to the hydrophobic property.

- The present finding paves a way to the low-cost fabrication of PSCs with high efficiency and stability in air.
