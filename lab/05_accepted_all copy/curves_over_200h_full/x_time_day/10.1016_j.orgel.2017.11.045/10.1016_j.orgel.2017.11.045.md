ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

ABSTRACT: The commercialization of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been impeded by their structural instability and susceptibility towards moisture. We demonstrate the fabrication of mixed cation lead mixed halide  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.8}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.2}\mathrm{PbI}_{2.68}\mathrm{Br}_{0.32} $  perovskite solar cells with long-term stability by addition of the  $ \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2} $  additive. The introduction of  $ \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2} $  improves the crystallinity of perovskite to promote the generation of high-quality film. The perovskite films with  $ \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2} $  exhibit large grain-size, less pinholes, reduced trap density and prolonged charge carrier lifetime. The PSCs with  $ 2\mathrm{mol}\% $   $ \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2} $  fabricated in ambient environment have showed notable power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 17.0\% $  with less hysteresis and remarkably high moisture stability. The PCE of unsealed devices maintained  $ 85.1\% $  of the initial value under ambient environment ( $ 60\pm 5\% $  RH) after 45 days.

KEYWORDS: large grain-size, long-term moisture stability, mixed cations and mixed halide perovskite, lead thiocyanate additive, solar cell

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted much attention as a new promising light-harvesting device due to the easy fabrication process, low production cost and superb photovoltaic performance [1-4]. Methylammonium lead iodode  $ (\mathrm{MAPbI}_3) $  is the most widely studied material and high PCE over  $ 20\% $  has been achieved. However,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  suffers from moisture and structural instability, which influences the long-term device stability and restricts its practical application [5, 6].

To improve the stability of the devices, mixed cations and/or halides have been introduced for strengthening the stability of neat perovskite [7-9]. Michael et al. employed a triple Cs/MA/FA cation perovskite to achieve high efficiency perovskite solar cells with a PCE of  $ 21.1\% $  and  $ 18\% $  after 250 hours in the glove box [10]. Yang et al. fabricated MA/FA lead mixed halide perovskite which had achieved the PCE of  $ 17\% $ , and investigated that  $ \mathrm{Br}^{-} $  can reduce trap density when partly replaces  $ \Gamma $  [11]. Lee et al. fabricated perovskite solar cells employing  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.9}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.1}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  to obtain PCE of  $ 16.5\% $  and retained  $ 70\% $  of initial value after 220 hours [12]. However, most of the reported mixed cations and/or halides perovskite solar cells were fabricated and operated under  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $  atmosphere, it is difficult to achieve stable unsealed devices with higher PCE when fabricated under ambient environment [7, 12, 13].

The high quality of perovskite films with less pinholes and grain boundaries is important to achieve high efficiency and stability of the devices [14-16]. The electronic trap states caused by the pinholes and grain boundaries could influence the lifetime of charge carrier and reduce the photoluminescence (PL) yield because of the enhanced non-radiative recombination [17, 18]. Perovskite films with large grain-size and less electronic trap states can be obtained by tuning the

speed of nucleation and growth of crystal [19, 20]. Introduction of additives have been proved to be an effective approach to improve the morphological and electronic properties of perovskite films. PMMA was added to promote heterogeneous nucleation and slow down crystal growth, resulting in smooth perovskite films with excellent electronic quality and hence high PCE of  $ 21.01\% $  [21]. Our group reported the tuning of crystal-growth of perovskite using 2-pyridylthiourea additive to achieve a PCE of  $ 18.2\% $ . The PCE of devices with 2-pyridylthiourea decreased by only  $ 8\% $  after 30 days under ambient atmosphere  $ (55 \pm 5\% \mathrm{RH}) $  [22]. Recently,  $ \mathrm{SCN^{-}} $  was introduced into organic-inorganic perovskite films to enlarge their grain size and improve stability against moisture.  $ \mathrm{NH_4SCN} $  additive was employed into the pure  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  to enlarge the grain size of  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  thin films and PSCs achieved the PCE of  $ 11.44\% $  with improved stability [23]. Lead thiocyanate  $ (\mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2}) $  additive could improve crystalline quality of perovskite film [24] and their stability against moisture [25].  $ \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2} $  additive was added in the  $ \mathrm{FA_{0.8}Cs_{0.2}PbI_3} $  perovskite precursor solutions and achieved the remarkable PCE of more than  $ 18\% $ , the films also show larger perovskite grains, and the PCE decreased to  $ 96.5\% $  when stored in a dry box after 10 days [26].

Herein, we reported the fabrication of  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.8}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.2}\mathrm{PbI}_{2.68}\mathrm{Br}_{0.32} $  with  $ \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2} $  as additive under ambient environment by a simple one-step spin coating process. The influence of  $ \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2} $  as on the optoelectronic properties, film morphology and microstructure, photovoltaic performance as well as device stability of  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.8}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.2}\mathrm{PbI}_{2.68}\mathrm{Br}_{0.32} $  were investigated. With  $ 2\mathrm{mol}\% $   $ \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2} $ , the champion PCE of  $ 17.0\% $  has been achieved and the PCE of unsealed devices maintained  $ 85.1\% $  of the initial value under ambient environment ( $ 60\pm 5\% $  RH) after 45 days.

# 2. Experimental Section

# 2.1.Materials.

Formaminium iodide (FAI), lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2, 99.9985\%) $ , 4-tert-butylpyridine (tbp), lithium bis (trifluoromethylsulphonyl) imide (Li-TSFI) and spiro-OMeTAD were purchased from Luminescence Technology Corporation. Lead bromide  $ (\mathrm{PbBr}_2) $ , cesium iodide (CsI,  $ 99.999\% $ ) and n-butanol were purchased from Aladdin. Lead thiocyanate  $ (\mathrm{Pb(SCN)}_2, 99.5\%) $  was obtained from Aldrich. Titanium diisopropoxide bis (acetylacetonate) was obtained from Alfar Aesar. Those materials were used as received. The Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) conducting glasses were purchased from Nippon Sheet Glass Corporation.

# 2.2. Perovskite solution preparation.

The pristine perovskite solution was prepared by dissolving FAI (0.8 mmol), CsI (0.2 mmol),  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  (0.84 mmol) and  $ \mathrm{PbBr}_2 $  (0.16 mmol) in  $ N,N $ -dimethyl formamide (DMF)/dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). The perovskite solution with  $ \mathrm{Pb(SCN)}_2 $  was prepared through adding different contents of additive  $ \mathrm{Pb(SCN)}_2 $  (0, 1, 2, 3, 4 mol%) to the pristine perovskite solution, the solution was stirred for 4 hours at room temperature before used.

# 2.3. Device fabrication.

FTO conducting glasses were washed by continuous ultrasonic treatment in detergent, acetone, deionized water and anhydrous ethanol each for 30 min, respectively. The compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was deposited on the cleaned substrate through spin-coating the titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) diluted in n-butanol (1:16 v/v) at 2000 rpm for 30 s, followed by sintering at 500°C for 30 min in air. The mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was deposited by spin-coating the diluted   $  TiO_{2}  $

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

paste at 4000 rpm for 30 s, followed by sintering at 500 °C for 30 min. The perovskite films were deposited by one-step anti-solvent spin-coating method. The prepared perovskite solutions were spin-coated on the mesoporous TiO₂ layer at 2000 rpm for 9 s and followed 5000 rpm for 30 s. During the second spin-coating step, a certain amount of chlorobenzene (CB) was dried onto the substrate. The film was subsequently heated on a hotplate at 100 °C for 60 min. After cooled down, the hole-transporting material was deposited on the top of perovskite film by spin-coating at 2000 rpm for 20 s. The hole-transporting material solution was prepared through dissolving 72.3 mg spiro-OMeTAD in 1 mL chlorobenzene with the addition of 30 μL tbp, 18 μL Li-TSFI (520 mg/mL). All the above processes was conducted under ambient environment. Gold electrode of 80 nm thickness was deposited as electrode through thermally evaporation under high vacuum.

# 2.4. Characterization.

The images of perovskite solar cells were characterized with a scanning electron microscope (SEM, HitachiS-4800, Japan). X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were recorded on the Mini Flex 600 using CuKal x-ray source. Ultraviolet–visible (UV-vis) absorbance spectra was obtained with a Thermo Evolution 300 UV/Visible-spectrometer. The composition of final film was characterized by XPS system (PHI5000VersaProbe). Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra was confirmed on Edinburgh Instruments (FLS 980, UK) collected at 780 nm after excitation at 405 nm. The current density-voltage (J-V) curves were measured with Keithley 2400 Source Meter, the scan rate was 50 mV/s, a mask of 0.1 cm $  ^{2}  $  was employed to confirm the effective area of solar cells, the illumination of the PSCs was adjusted at 100 mW/cm $  ^{2}  $  (AM 1.5G) made with an Oriel solar simulator 91160A. The lab-made IPCE testing system was employed for incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE).

# 3. Results and discussion

To verify the composition formula of stable   $  ABX_{3}  $   perovskite, theoretical calculation of the Goldschmidt tolerance factor (t) and factor (  $  \mu  $  ) of corner-sharing   $  [BX_{6}]^{4-}  $   octahedral was conducted. The perovskite will form high stable cubic structures when the tolerance factor and octahedral factor falls in the range of 0.8–1.06 and over 0.41 [27], respectively. These indexes can be calculated from the ionic radius using equation (1-2) [24].

$$
t = \frac {r _ {A} + r _ {X}}{\sqrt {2} (r _ {B} + r _ {X})}
$$

$$
\mu = \frac {r _ {B}}{r _ {X}}
$$

where  $ r_{\mathrm{A}}, r_{\mathrm{B}} $ , and  $ r_{\mathrm{X}} $  are the effective ionic radii for A, B, and X anions, respectively. In this work, the formula of the mixed cations and mixed halides perovskite is  $ \mathrm{FA_xCs_{1 - x}Pb(I_yBr_{1 - y})_3} $ , so the effective tolerance factor  $ (t_{\mathrm{eff}}) $  and octahedral factor  $ (\mu) $  can be forecasted by equations (3-6) [24].

$$

The ionic radii of  $ \mathrm{FA^{+}} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2 + }} $ ,  $ \Gamma^{-} $  and  $ \mathrm{Br^{-}} $  are 2.53, 1.67, 1.02, 2.20 and  $ 1.96\AA $ , respectively [28]. In consequence, the optimal composition formula with high stability is  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.8}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.2}\mathrm{Pb(I_{2.68 / 3}Br_{0.32 / 3})_3} $  with  $ t_\mathrm{eff} $  of 1.003 and  $ \mu $  of 0.469, which indicated the prepared perovskite could form cubic structure with high stability.

The full width at half maximum (FWHM) for the diffraction peak at 2θ of 14° is shown in Figure 1b, it reaches a local minimum at 2 mol % contents of the Pb(SCN)₂, revealing larger grain size and better crystallinity of the film. The PbI₂ peak at 12.6° is observed as Pb(SCN)₂ introduced, appropriate excess PbI₂ is considered to improve solar cell performance because it can passivate the surface of perovskite and reduce the recombination trap states to accelerate charge injection [29]. As the Pb(SCN)₂ content is increased to 3%, the CsPbI₃ peak at 2θ of 9.84° appears, which reduces the film phase purity and is prejudicial for the device performance [30]. It is worth noting that, no diffraction peak for leftover Pb(SCN)₂ phase is detected in the whole Pb(SCN)₂ content range studied, indicating that Pb(SCN)₂ added in the precursor does not change the original structure of perovskite [23]. Full XPS survey of the perovskite film in Figure S1 shows no signal from S energy level, but when tested the bulk composition with EDS in Figure S2, there shows a very small amount of residual S element. As the excitation volume is deeper in EDS (~1μm) compared with that in XPS (~5 nm), it suggests that a trace of SCN group is distributed in the bulk while it is possible that thiocyanate ion on the surface evaporates during the annealing process [25, 29, 31]. And the EDS mapping of S element (Figure S7) has shown that S distributes everywhere in the films with Pb(SCN)₂ additive.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we demonstrated PSCs with high efficiency, less hysteresis and improved stability by employing   $  FA_{0.8}Cs_{0.2}PbI_{2.68}Br_{0.32}  $   with   $  \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2}  $   additive. It shows that the   $  \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2}  $   additive can efficiently enlarge grain size and smooth surface of film. With content of 2 mol %   $  \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2}  $   additive, the perovskite films exhibit larger absorption enhancement and longer free carrier lifetime, the champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) is 17.0 % under AM 1.5G illumination. The solar cells with   $  SCN^{-}  $  show notable stability against moisture, the PCE of unsealed devices maintains approximate 85.1 % of the initial value when tested under ambient environment of 60±5 % relative humidity in the dark after 45 days. Our work concluded that by adding   $  \mathrm{Pb(SCN)_2}  $ , FA and Cs lead mixed halide PSCs can achieve both higher PCE and excellent stability, which may offer a pathway for fabricating stable and efficient perovskite solar cells.
