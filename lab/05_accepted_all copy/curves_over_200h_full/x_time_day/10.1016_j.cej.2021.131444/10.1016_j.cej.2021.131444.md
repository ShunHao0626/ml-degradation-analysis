# 1. Introduction

Hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been paid widespread attentions due to their outstanding photoconversion performance and high compatibility with low-cost solution preparation   $  [1]  $  . Various attempts have been adopted to improve the efficiency of PSCs and the certified record small area device has reached to 25.5%   $  [2]  $  . The PSCs also possess high potential as the next-generation photovoltaics for varied applications, such as the semitransparent, flexible and tandem devices   $  [3-5]  $  . Comparing with inverted PSCs, regular architecture PSCs demonstrated relative higher efficiency and applicable for varied electron transport layers (ETLs), such as   $  TiO_{2}  $  , ZnO and   $  SnO_{2}  $   etc.   $  [6-9]  $  .   $  TiO_{2}  $   is normally prepared with a high temperature and the catalytic effect under illumination will corrode the perovskite (PSK) layer affect devices

Chemical bath deposited tin oxide film (CBD  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ ) has been demonstrated as an amazing electron transporting layer for high photoconversion efficiency (PCE) hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) over  $ 25\% $ . The current CBD process is normally studied for small area devices. A rapid batch production of large-area CBD  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  film with high and uniform performance is essential for potential upscaling of perovskite solar module. Herein, we carried out a modified batch CBD process, a double chemical bath deposition (DC), for batch fabrication of large-area  $ (400~\mathrm{cm}^2) $   $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  films and realized a homogenized PCE with a variance  $ (V_{\mathrm{a}}) $  of 0.127, comparing to that of control sample of  $ V_{\mathrm{a}} $  at 2.833. We further introduced mercaptosuccinic acid (MSA) at the interface of  $ \mathrm{SnO_2}/ $  perovskite for convenient energy band alignment and defects passivation, which significantly improved the PCE, stability and homogeneity of the devices. As a result, the mix-cation planar PSCs reaches  $ 21.24\% $  with further reduced  $ V_{\mathrm{a}} $  of 0.073. A high efficiency of  $ 22.54\% $  was obtained for the narrow band gap FA-based perovskite device. These results suggest high potential of this modified CBD route for large area and high efficiency perovskite module preparation.

In a word, selecting a suitable modification layer to modify the interface between PSK and  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  is an effective method to obtain high-efficiency and stable PSCs.

# 2. Results and discussion

Chemical bath deposition (CBD) of   $  SnO_{2}  $   has been demonstrated to be an efficient method for preparing high efficiency PSCs. We designed a batch produced CBD route to prepare of large area   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film (10 pieces, 20 cm ×20 cm). The Fig. 1a are the schematic   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film prepared on small substrate (SS) and large substrate (LS) by (CBD). Then, the fabrication steps of PSCs (FTO/  $  SnO_{2}  $  /PSK/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au) are illustrated in Fig. 1b and c. The as-made DC-  $  SnO_{2}  $   can be used as the substrate to prepare a large-area perovskite solar module (Fig. 1d).

We surprisingly found that the PSCs prepared based on the LS-2.5 h-SnO $ _2 $  presents much lower efficiency and larger variance (PCE 15.82%,  $ V_a $  2.833) comparing to that of SS-2.5 h-SnO $ _2 $  (PCE 19.72%,  $ V_a $  0.331), as shown in Fig. 2a and Table 1. Then we modified the single chemical bath deposition (SC) time of LS-SnO $ _2 $  ETLs (Fig. 2b, Fig. S1 and Table S1). The J-V parameter of the devices SC-1.5 h-SnO $ _2 $  is widely distributed (PCE 15.94%,  $ V_a $  7.533). This may be due to the insufficient film coverage in short-term SC, resulting in low efficiency and wide distribution of efficiency. The devices based on SC-4.5 h-SnO $ _2 $  demonstrate the best efficiency and variance (PCE 19.41%,  $ V_a $  0.593). Continuing to extend the SC time, the devices efficiency (average PCE is 17.70 ± 1.080%) become lower and the distribution slightly wider (PCE 18.78%  $ V_a $  1.167). In order to further explore the influence of CBD time on the uniformity of LS-SnO $ _2 $ , we tested the conductivity of 20 pieces of 3 cm $ ^2 $  SnO $ _2 $  film from LS-SnO $ _2 $ . The detailed calculation method was described in the support information. The dynamic light scatter meter (DLS) measurements suggest that particle sizes of CBD solutions were actually varied during the deposition time. To further reduce the variance of the PCE, we carried out a double chemical bath deposition (DC) method (Fig. 1c) with the same solution and substrate. The pH of DC-4.5 h solution at different deposition depth is narrowly dispersed with a smaller pH   $  \sim  $  1.61 comparing to SC-4.5 h samples (Fig. 2d) [24]. The homogeneity of large area   $  SnO_{2}  $   film at different CBD depth was also studied. As shown in Fig. 2e and f and Table S3, these is obvious that the conductivity of SC-  $  SnO_{2}  $   film gradually increases with the increasing of CBD depth (  $  \sim  $  0.00633 mS cm  $  ^{-1}  $   (d1) to   $  \sim  $  0.00889 mS cm  $  ^{-1}  $   (d4)), which is not observed in the DC-  $  SnO_{2}  $   samples. The PCE of the devices SC-  $  SnO_{2}  $   film shows positive gradient, which is increased from   $  \sim  $  17% (d1) to 19% (d4) with the variance of 0.593. These results indicate inhomogeneity may occur in the CBD solution due to particle sedimentation or variation of constitution during the CBD process. According to previous pH results of DC-4.5 h solution at different depths (Fig. 2d), we guess that the DC method can solve the problem of the unevenness. As shown in Fig. 2e and f and Table S4, we pleasantly find that the conductivity of the DC-  $  SnO_{2}  $   film and the variance of device efficiency are reduced to 0.00884 mS cm  $  ^{-1}  $   and 0.00072, respectively. The overall devices average efficiency of DC-  $  SnO_{2}  $   is 19.22

In Fig. S9, the average roughness (Ra), obtained from atomic force microscope (AFM) tests, are 30.912 nm (  $  DC-SnO_{2}  $  ) and 31.484 nm (  $  DC-SnO_{2}-MSA  $  ), respectively, which indicates that the roughness of the film is almost unchanged before and after the MSA treatment. The SEM morphology of the PSK film shows that   $  DC-SnO_{2}-MSA  $   sample has slightly larger grains (Fig. 3a and Fig. S10). Comparing with   $  DC-SnO_{2}/PSK  $  , the absorption of   $  DC-SnO_{2}-MSA/PSK  $   has no obvious change, implying that the additional modification layer has no effect the absorption of the PSK layer (Fig. 3c). As shown in Fig. 3d, the MSA show characteristic peaks at around   $  1697\ cm^{-1}  $   (-C=O) and   $  2564\ cm^{-1}  $   (-HS) [29]. The effect of MSA coating on the  $ \mathrm{DC - SnO_2} $  were further studied using steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and transient resolution photoluminescence (TRPL) to investigate the charge transfer and recombination dynamics of the PSK film. As shown in Fig. 6a, comparing to  $ \mathrm{DC - SnO_2} $  sample, the PL intensity of the  $ \mathrm{DC - SnO_2 - MSA} $  sample is reduced, which reveals enhanced electronic extraction and reduced charge recombination. In addition, comparing with the control PSK film, the PL peak of  $ \mathrm{DC - SnO_2 - MSA / PSK} $  exhibits a blue shift of  $ 1\mathrm{nm} $ , indicating the trap state is passivated by Lewis acid-base reaction between the -HS of MSA and the  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2 + } $  in the PSK [28]. As shown in Fig. 6b and Table S7, the PSK on  $ \mathrm{DC - SnO_2 - MSA} $  shows shorter lifetimes ( $ \tau_{1} $  of 52.0 ns,  $ \tau_{2} $  of 120.8 ns) than that on  $ \mathrm{DC - SnO_2} $  ( $ \tau_{1} $  of 64.9 ns,  $ \tau_{2} $  of 157.4 ns), suggesting more efficient electron transfer, in good agreement with the energy level analysis [43,44]. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was employed to gain further insights in charge transfer and recombination. The Nyquist plot of the devices based on  $ \mathrm{DC - SnO_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{DC - SnO_2 - MSA} $  ETLs were measured at  $ 1\mathrm{V} $  under 1 Sun illustration (Fig. 6c). Generally, in EIS analysis, the series resistance ( $ R_{\mathrm{S}} $ ) represents the resistance of the device's electron transmission, and the recombination resistance ( $ R_{\mathrm{REC}} $ ) refers to the resistance recombination of the carrier. Obviously, comparing with the devices based on  $ \mathrm{DC - SnO_2} $ , the devices with  $ \mathrm{DC - SnO_2 - MSA} $  has a smaller  $ R_{\mathrm{S}} $  of  $ 5.27\Omega $  and a high  $ R_{\mathrm{REC}} $  of  $ 53.82\Omega $  (Table S8). A small  $ R_{\mathrm{S}} $  is beneficial for electron extraction, and a large  $ R_{\mathrm{REC}} $  is good for resisting charge recombination, which is consistent with the observations discussed [45]. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, we report a modified CBD method to massively produce the uniform large area   $  SnO_{2}  $   film (20 cm × 20 cm) for high efficiency perovskite solar cells. A novel double chemical bath deposition (DC) route was demonstrated to significantly enhance the uniformity of the batch prepared   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETLs. The performances and uniformity of the devices were improved from PCE of 15.82% (  $  V_{a}  $   2.833) to 19.69% (  $  V_{a}  $   0.127). Moreover, the mercaptosuccinic acid (MSA) was first introduced to passivate the interface of   $  SnO_{2}/PSK  $   to further enhance the performances. Through the esterification reaction of MSA with   $  SnO_{2}  $   and the coordination of MSA with lead ions, the interface defects and the band alignment gap between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and PSK were remarkably reduced. The humidity and heat stability of the device were also accordingly improved. Thus, the average PCE of MSA modified device was 20.69% ± 0.270 and the uniformity was further improved (PCE 21.24%,   $  V_{a}  $   0.073). A high efficiency of 22.54% was also obtained for FA-based perovskite solar cells, implying the modified DC method exhibits high potential for massive preparation of transportation layers for high-performance large size perovskite solar modules.
