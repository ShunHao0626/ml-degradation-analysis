# Introduction of Fluorine Into spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene]-Based Hole Transport Material to Obtain Sensitive-Dopant-Free, High Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Kunpeng Guo,  $  ^{*}  $   Min Wu, Shaomin Yang, Zongtao Wang, Jie Li, Xiaozhong Liang, Fang Zhang, Zhike Liu,  $  ^{*}  $   and Zhongqiang Wang

# 1. Introduction

Despite the substantial development of efficient hole transporting materials (HTMs) for high-performance perovskite solar cells (PSCs), optimization of the HTMs to sensitive-dopant-free HTMs for high efficient PSCs with prominent stability have rarely been reported. Herein, a low-cost fluorinated spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene] based HTM termed 2mF-X59 is designed and synthesized. In comparison with its reported nonfluorinated counterpart X59, 2mF-X59 shows lowered highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level, improved hole mobility, and hydrophobicity. Aided by 2,3,5,6-tetrafluoro-7,7,8,8-tetracyanoquinodimethane (F4TCNQ) to further lower the HOMO level of 2mF-X59 and improve its hole transfer, the optimized 2mF-X59 based PSCs show a maximum power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 18.13\% $  without the use of any sensitive-dopants (e.g., lithium salt/4-tert-butylpyridine), which is comparable to the Spiro-OMeTAD based PSCs  $ (18.22\%) $  with sensitive dopants. More importantly, the sensitive-dopant-free 2mF-X59 based PSCs maintain  $ 95\% $  of their initial performance for more than  $ 500\mathrm{h} $  under air exposure, showing much better long-term stability than control PSCs based on Spiro-OMeTAD with sensitive dopants. This is the first case that a sensitive-dopant-free HTM is demonstrated in PSCs with a high PCE  $ (>18\%) $  and good stability by optimizing the literature HTM. This work could pave a new way to develop low-cost sensitive-dopant-free HTMs for highly efficient and stable PSCs for practical applications.

Solution-processed organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been demonstrated to be the promising candidates to replace the silicon-based photovoltaic technology, which is ascribed to their low-cost fabrication processing, and remarkable power conversion efficiency (PCE) boosting. Contributed to the delicate device design, interfacial engineering and energy levels modification, the PCE of PSCs has been dramatically improved from 3.8 to  $ 23\% $  within 10 years.[1-3] However, the instability of PSCs is still a challenge issue for the PV technology to meet commercial applications. In general, to realize a high PCE, the PSCs usually employ a sandwich-type configuration, composed a layer of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  electron acceptor, followed by a light harvesting material (LHM) that is coated with perovskite organometal halide  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbX}_3 $  ( $ X = Cl $ , Br, I), then a layer of hole transporting material (HTM), and a metal back electrode. Thus, the properties of the HTM are crucial for improving PCE and stability of PSCs.

In terms of improving the hole mobility, modifying the energy levels, increasing the thermal stability and simplifying the preparation for HTMs, much effort has been made to develop various HTMs with novel molecular configurations. To date, the spiro-type 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) represented the most successful commercial HTM, and the record PCEs for PSCs of >20% have been realized through the perovskite composition and device optimization.[8,9] However, the complex synthetic steps and hard purification render Spiro-OMeTAD with a high-cost preparation and limit large-scale application.[10] To address these issues, HTMs with linear and star-shaped structures have been developed and explored in PSCs. For example, by cross-linking the benzotrithiphene core with p-methoxytriphenylamines, Molina-Ontoria et al.[11] demonstrated a star-shaped HTM (BTT-4) with enhanced conductivity, thus giving an impressive PCE of 19%. Nevertheless, the spiro-type structure based PSCs still dominate the record PCEs. Most recently, using fluorene replace part of methoxyphenylamines in Spiro-OMeTAD to tune the energy level and increase the thermal stability, Lee et al. reported a champion PSC with a novel  $  N^2,N^2,N^7,N^7  $ -tetrakis(9,9-dimethyl-9H-fluoren-2-yl)- $  N^2,N^2,N^7,N^7  $ -tetrakis(4-methoxyphenyl)-9,9'-spirobi[fluorene]-2,2',7,7'-tetraamineas HTM and a PCE of 23.2%.[2] Notably, all of the above mentioned HTMs used in PSCs need p-type dopants addition, such as bis-(trifluoromethane)sulfonimdie lithium salt (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) to increase the mobility or conductivity.[12-16] However, such dopants are sensitive to moisture, leading to decomposition of the perovskite materials and instability of PSCs.[17-20] In this context, great research interests have focused on the development of sensitive-dopant-free HTMs in PSCs.[21-25]

Generally, there are two major approaches to obtain sensitive-dopant-free HTMs. One is to explore novel molecular structures, which are different to those of doped HTMs in literatures. For example, D-A structures, obtained by rationally combining different donor (D) units and acceptor (A) units, have shown the efficiency in molecular design toward sensitive-dopant-free HTMs. Nazeeruddin et al.  $  ^{[26]}  $  synthesized a series of star-shaped D-  $  \pi  $  -A molecules as sensitive-dopant-free HTMs for PSCs, combining with a elaborative perovskite material as LHM, the PSCs exhibit PCEs of up to 18%. However, most of the D-A type sensitive-dopant-free HTMs reported to date still required multistep synthesis, which limited their potential large-scale applications in future due to the raised costs.  $  ^{[21,27-30]}  $  The other is the optimization of high-efficient doped HTMs in literatures into sensitive-dopant-free HTMs. Obviously, the later one is of great energy conservation to the development of sensitive-dopant free-HTMs for stable PSCs, but challenged in promising molecular

optimization strategy with low-cost synthesis toward high PCE (e.g., exceed 18%) in PSCs application.

As mentioned above, the HTMs can also function as a block layer on the surface of LHM to protect the perovskite material from moisture corrosion. Therefore, in addition to improving the hole mobility and matching energy levels, hydrophobicity is also an important factor which should be considered seriously when making an optimization of HTMs in literatures into sensitive-dopant free HTMs with high performance.  $  ^{[29,31]}  $   Fluorination would be a very promising molecular design strategy to optimize HTMs for further PSCs performance improvement, because fluorinated organic molecules have exhibited the fascinating advantages such as increasing the materials hydrophobicity, down-shifting the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level of the HTMs, improving the crystallinity of LHMs, and facilitating charge transport.  $  ^{[32-35]}  $   Meanwhile, to satisfy both low-cost preparation and high PCE in PSCs, spiroannulated spiro-[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene] (SFX) core would be particularly well suited as a scaffold, as its facile one-pot synthetic approach and outstanding properties in PSCs.  $  ^{[9,19]}  $   Recently, Sun et al.  $  ^{[36]}  $   synthesized a SFX-based HTMN',N',N'',N''-tetrakis-(4-methoxyphenyl)spiro[fluorene-9,9'-xanthene]-2,7-diamine (X59, Scheme 1) within two steps, and obtained an impressive PCE of 19.8% in PSCs with the sensitive Li-TFSI and tBP addition, which qualified SFX-based HTMs as low-cost candidates for high efficient PSCs.

To identify our envision that the fluorination of SFX as an excellent strategy for realizing efficient sensitive-dopant-free HTMs of PSCs, in this work, by using SFX as the core moiety, we designed and obtained a novel sensitive-dopant-free HTM (2mF-X59) by fluorination of X59, which are readily prepared via two step reactions from commercially available raw materials, as shown in Scheme 1. The lab synthesis and purification cost of 2mF-X59 was estimated to only 21.9 \$ g $  ^{-1}  $  (Table S6, Supporting Information), which showed possibility for commercial production. Further investigations indicated that 2mF-X59 possessed a decreased HOMO level, as well as increased hole mobility and hydrophobicity compared with its nonfluorinated counterpart X59, indicating it as anticipated sensitive-dopant-free HTM for efficient and stable PSCs. As a result, the CH $  _{3}  $ NH $  _{3}  $ PbI $  _{3}  $  solar cells based sensitive-dopant-free 2mF-X59 show a PCE of 15.45%, which is 3.9-fold higher than that of PSCs based on X59 (3.95%). To further optimize the performance of PSCs with 2mF-X59 as HTM, a strong electron acceptor F4TCNQ was added into 2mF-X59 to further lower its HOMO level and improve its interfacial hole transfer. As a result, by using the optimized 2mF-X59 with 4% weight ratio of F4TCNQ as HTM, the PSCs exhibit a remarkable PCE of 18.13%, which is comparable to the PCE (18.22%) of PSC based Spiro-OMeTAD HTM with the commonly used sensitive-dopants of Li-TFSI and tBP. Most importantly, the PSCs with F4TCNQ (4wt%)-optimized-2mF-X59 showed much better long-term stability than that of PSCs based on Spiro-OMeTAD HTMs with sensitive-dopants, maintaining 95% of their initial efficiency under air exposure for more than 500 h. This work paves the way to develop low-cost and sensitive-dopant-free HTMs for highly efficient and stable PSCs toward commercial applications.

# 2. Results and Discussions

As depicted in Scheme 1, the synthetic route of the fluorinated SFX-based HTM is quite facile. First, the key fluorine containing core moiety 2,7-dibromo-3',6'-difluoro-spiro(fluorene-9,9'-xanthene) (2Br2F-SFX) was prepared via one-pot cyclization reaction of the commercial available 2,7-dibromo-9-fluorenone and 3-fluorophenol using methanesulfonic acid as catalyst.  $  ^{[36]}  $   Subsequently, Buchwald-Hartwig amination of the 2Br2F-SFX and 4,4'-dimethoxydiphenylamine in the presence of palladium-catalysts afforded the target compound 2mF-X59.  $  ^{[37]}  $   The detailed synthetic information and full chemical characterization of the intermediate and target product were fully accessible in the Supporting Information. 2mF-X59 exhibited good solubility in common organic solvents such as dichloromethane, chloroform, and chlorobenzene.

To understand the configuration and frontier molecular orbital distribution of 2mF-X59 better, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were firstly carried out using the Gaussian 09 program at the B3LYP/6-31G*level. The optimized molecular geometry of 2mF-X59 is shown in Figure S1, Supporting Information. It was found the dihedral angles between the peripheral 4,4'-dimethoxydiphenylamine substituents and the SFX core were 67.9° and 68.1°. Meanwhile, the fluorene rings in SFX tilt toward one side, significantly reducing the intersection angle between the fluorene and xanthene to 85.13°. Such results indicate 2mF-X59 exhibits highly twisted configuration. The electron distribution of the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of 2mF-X59 is mainly localized on the peripheral 4,4'-dimethoxydiphenylamine substituents and the centered fluorene ring, while the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of it slightly shifted from 4,4'-dimethoxydiphenylamine substituents to the fluorene ring, as shown in Figure S2, Supporting Information. This result indicates the donor character of 2mF-X59 stems from the conjugation between the two 4,4'-dimethoxydiphenylamine substituents.

The structural analysis of 2mF-X59 single crystal was further performed to disclose its real conformation. Similar to X59, the fluorene ring is perpendicular to the xanthene ring with a torsion angle of 84.28°, as illustrated in Figure 1a. This value is consistent well with its calculated result. 2mF-X59 was found to

crystallize in the triclinic space P-1 (Table S5, Supporting Information). There are four independent molecules in one unit cell. Only weak intermolecular interactions, including C–H…F (d1 2.594 Å), (d2 2.611 Å), (d3 2.702 Å); C–F…C (d4 3.121 and 3.127 Å), and C–H…π (d5 2.849, 2.829, and 2.719 Å), were detected by carefully inspection (Figure 1b). Meanwhile, the molecules form antiparallel packing with the phenyl rings of 4,4'-dimethoxydiphenylaminesubstituents of the adjacent molecule stacked in a malposed pattern; and therefore, no typical π–π intermolecular interactions were observed in the crystal structure (Figure 1c). As a result, the nonplanar molecular structure of 2mF-X59 endow it with good solubility in common organic solvents, which is indicative of its practicability for solution-processed PSCs.

Thermal stability of 2mF-X59 was investigated by thermogravimetric (TGA) and different scanning calorimetry (DSC) analysis under nitrogen atmosphere. As illustrated in Figure S3, Supporting Information, the TGA analysis reveals that 2mF-X59 possesses a high decomposition temperature (  $  T_{d}  $  ) of 410.7°C. Meanwhile, a high glass transition temperature (  $  T_{g}  $  ) at 113.0°C for 2mF-X59 was observed from DSC analysis. Both   $  T_{d}  $   and   $  T_{g}  $   of 2mF-X59 are close to those of X59 (  $  T_{d}=416^{\circ}C  $  ,   $  T_{g}=105^{\circ}C  $  ). These results indicate that 2mF-X59 hold good thermal stability, as well as slightly influence of introducing fluorine atoms in molecular structure on the thermal stability.

The UV–vis absorption and fluorescence spectra of X59 and 2mF-X59 in dilute tetrahydrofuran (THF) at room temperature are shown in Figure S4 Supporting Information, and the corresponding spectroscopic parameters are summarized in Table 1. Both of X59 and 2mF-X59 exhibit strong absorption peaks in the near visible region (350–400 nm), which can be assigned to a more localized   $  \pi-\pi^{*}  $   in peripheral 4,4'-dimethoxydiphenylamines and centered aromatic rings. The absorption maximum (  $  \lambda_{abs}  $  ) was observed at 385 nm for 2mF-X59, which is slightly blue-shifted in comparison to X59 (  $  \lambda_{abs}=387  $   nm), as is expected due to the introduced electron withdrawing fluorine atoms in the molecular structure. From the absorption onsets, we could obtain the optical band gaps (  $  E_{g}  $  ) of X59 and 2mF-X59 were 2.87 and 2.82 eV, respectively. To investigate the effect of fluorination in HTM molecules on charge-transport properties, carrier mobility was determined by a space charge limited current (SCLC) method. In the present work, hole-only devices with the structure of ITO/PEDOT: PSS/HTM/MoO $ _3 $ /Al using different HTMs were prepared, respectively for measurements. The hole mobility values were extracted by fitting the dark current to the model of a single carrier SCLC, which was described by the Mott-Gurney law. As shown in Figure 2c and Table 1, the hole mobilities for X59 and 2mF-X59 show a similar order of magnitude, however, a slightly enhanced hole mobility up to  $ 7.14 \times 10^{-5} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $  was observed for the 2mF-X59, which is 1.26- and 1.08-fold higher than that of X59 ( $ 5.67 \times 10^{-5} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ ) and commercially available HTM Spiro-OMeTAD ( $ 6.62 \times 10^{-5} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ ), respectively. # 3. Conclusions

In summary, we applied the synergistic molecular optimization of HTM for high efficient PSCs with prominent stability in this work. A new fluorinated SFX-based compound 2mF-X59 was successfully designed and synthesized via two steps. The newly developed 2mF-X59 exhibits a down-shifted HOMO level, improved hole mobility and hydrophobicity relative to its nonflurinated counterpart X59. As a result, the 2mF-X59 based

PSC without using any dopants yields a PCE of 15.45%, which is much higher than that of the nonflurinated counterpart X59 based PSC (3.95%) under the same condition. Furthermore, by introducing an insensitive fluorinated electron acceptor F4TCNQ into 2mF-X59 to lower the HOMO level of HTM, polish up the morphology of HTM film, and improve hole extraction and charge dissociation from perovskite to HTM, an impressive PCE of 18.13% is obtained from the F4TCNQ (4wt %)-optimized-2mF-X59 based PSCs. which is comparable to that of the sensitive-dopants-based Spiro-OMeTADHTM (18.22%). Most importantly, as a low-cost HTM, the 2mF-X59 based PSCs showed much better long-term stability than the commercial HTM Spiro-OMeTAD based PSCs. This work demonstrates that rational molecular optimization is of great importance to improve the PCE and stability of PSC toward commercial applications.
