# Interfacial Bridge Using a cis-Fulleropyrrolidine for Efficient Planar Perovskite Solar Cells with Enhanced Stability

Chengbo Tian, Kebin Lin, Jianxun Lu, Wenjing Feng, Peiquan Song, Liqiang Xie, and Zhanhua Wei*

Fullerene derivatives, especially after purposely functionalization, have potential to efficiently passivate interfacial defects between perovskites and electron transport layers. In this work, a fullerene derivative with amine functional group, 2,5-diphenyl   $  C_{60}  $   fulleropyrrolidine (  $  DPC_{60}  $  ), is synthesized and employed as an interfacial layer between a perovskite and   $  SnO_{2}  $   in planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs). The cis-configuration and the specific amine group of   $  DPC_{60}  $   effectively enhance the chemical interaction between the perovskite and   $  DPC_{60}  $ , promoting the passivation of perovskite defects at the interface. The suitable energy level of   $  DPC_{60}  $   and the improved conductivity of the   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   film facilitate the electron extraction from the perovskite layer. As a result, PSCs incorporated with   $  DPC_{60}  $   reach a PCE of 20.4% with high reproducibility, which is much higher than that of the bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   based devices (18.8%). Furthermore, the hydrophobic   $  DPC_{60}  $   layer suppresses heterogeneous nucleation and improves the crystallinity of the perovskite film, resulting in better device stability, retaining 82% of its initial efficiency after 200 h of 1 sun continuous irradiation and thermal ageing (55 ± 5 °C).

Metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have fascinated the academic community in the past decade due to their inherently high-quality optical and electronic properties.  $  ^{[1-4]}  $   Since the first report in 2009, PSCs are moving toward high performance in an incredible rate, and now the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of the single-junction PSCs have been rocketed to 24.2%.  $  ^{[5-12]}  $   The high performance of PSCs are realized exclusively using high quality electron transport layers (ETLs), including   $  TiO_{2}  $  ,   $  BaSnO_{3}  $  , and   $  SnO_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[10,11,13]}  $   Among these ETLs,   $  SnO_{2}  $   is considered as one of the most promising alternative ETL materials for high performance PSCs due to its high optical transparency in visible region, excellent electron mobilities, low-temperature processed methods, better UV-resistance properties and less photocatalytic activity than other ETLs.  $  ^{[14,15]}  $   Despite the aforementioned advantages,   $  SnO_{2}  $ /perovskite interfaces still own some drawbacks, such as inefficient charge

In fact, some groups have already tried to modify   $  SnO_{2}  $   with fullerenes and have achieved improvements on device performance.  $  ^{[29-32]}  $   For instance, Jen and coworkers reported a strategy using a   $  C_{60}  $  -SAM modified   $  SnO_{2}  $   film in PSCs and obtained a PCE of 15% with negligible hysteresis.  $  ^{[31]}  $   Fang and coworkers employed the extensively utilized PCBM to modify   $  SnO_{2}  $   and prepared a device with an efficiency of 19.12%.  $  ^{[32]}  $   However, there are some issues need to be addressed for the reported fullerene derivatives applied in PSCs. For example,   $  SnO_{2}  $   modification with   $  C_{60}  $  -SAM is time-consuming and inconvenient for future commercial production. PCBM can be dissolved in N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF) or dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) during perovskite deposition, leading to poor device performance reproducibility. Therefore, it is highly desirable to design and synthesize new fullerene derivatives with low solubility in perovskite solvents to improve reproducibility and device performance.

In this work, we report the design and synthesis of a pyrrolidine-functionalized  $ C_{60} $  derivative with a cis-configuration, 2,5-diphenyl  $ C_{60} $  fulleropyrrolidine  $ (\mathrm{DPC}_{60}) $ , and its device performance as a modification layer between  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and perovskite film in PSCs. For convenience, we denote  $ \mathrm{DPC}_{60} $  modified  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  as  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{DPC}_{60} $ . The low solubility of  $ \mathrm{DPC}_{60} $  in the perovskite solvent makes it hard to be removed during perovskite deposition, which is critical for realizing high reproducibility of device performance. extraction and interface recombination, due to the existence of defect states and energy level mismatching at their interface.  $  ^{[15-18]}  $   It is necessary to propose a strategy to eliminate these issues.

$  C_{60}  $  and its derivatives, especially phenyl- $  C_{61}  $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM), have been reported as excellent interfacial modifiers of ETL/perovskite in PSCs. $ ^{[19,20]} $  It has been demonstrated that not only the conduction band (CB) of a monoadduct fullerene derivative (such as PCBM) is well-matched with that of perovskites, but the fullerene derivative can efficiently passivate the interfacial defects of ETLs and the perovskite layers. $ ^{[21-27]} $  Therefore, the modification of the  $  SnO_{2}  $  layer using fullerene can simultaneously enhance the extraction of electrons and passivate the defects at the interface of perovskite/ETLs, thereby improving the performance of the PSCs. $ ^{[28]} $

DPC  $  _{60}  $  not only serves as an efficient ETL for the PSCs but efficiently decreases the energy loss during electrons passing through the ETL/perovskite interface. The compact crystal-packing structure allows DPC  $  _{60}  $  to have high electron mobility, thus enhancing the electron extraction capability of the SnO  $  _{2}  $ /DPC  $  _{60}  $  film. The chemical interaction between perovskite layer and DPC  $  _{60}  $  promotes the passivation effect at the ETL/perovskite interface. Meanwhile, the high hydrophobicity of DPC  $  _{60}  $  suppresses heterogeneous nucleation and improves the crystallinity of the perovskite film. As a result, using DPC  $  _{60}  $  as a modification layer, PSC reaches a maximum PCE of 20.4%, which is higher than that of the bare SnO  $  _{2}  $  based devices. Furthermore, the SnO  $  _{2}  $ /DPC  $  _{60}  $  based PSC shows higher stability in comparison with the SnO  $  _{2}  $  based device, which could retain 82% of their initial efficiency after 200 h of illumination with a thermal ageing temperature of 55 ± 5 °C.

The PSC with configuration of FTO/SnO $ _2 $ /DPC $ _{60} $ /perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD/Au is displayed in Figure 1a. The SnO $ _2 $  layer is deposited on the ITO substrate in a low temperature process using a SnO $ _2 $  nanocrystals dispersion aqueous solution. $ ^{[33,34]} $  Subsequently, a thin DPC $ _{60} $  modified layer is spin coated on the SnO $ _2 $  ETL. The perovskite film with composition of FA $ _{0.81} $ MA $ _{0.10} $ Cs $ _{0.04} $ PbI $ _{2.55} $ Br $ _{0.40} $  is deposited on the SnO $ _2 $ /DPC $ _{60} $  ETL by an antisolvent based one-step method. As reported previously, the CB of SnO $ _2 $  is about  $ -4.5 $  eV, which is lower than that of the perovskite materials. $ ^{[16]} $  The mismatched energy level between SnO $ _2 $  and perovskite layer may result in an unfavorable electron extraction efficiency at the SnO $ _2 $ /perovskite interface, which hinders the improvement of device efficiency. $ ^{[15-17]} $  In addition, fullerene ETLs cannot effectively block holes as efficient as metal oxide ETLs due to their relatively small bandgaps and mismatched level of HOMO, $ ^{[32]} $  which will result in more severe carrier recombination and low device performance (Figure S1, Supporting Information). Therefore, as illustrated in Figure 1b, we propose to synthesize a fullerene derivative, DPC $ _{60} $ , and deposit it on the surface of SnO $ _2 $  to improve the device performance. DPC $ _{60} $  as a monoadduct C $ _{60} $  derivative could have a well-matched CB with perovskite layer, which will improve the interfacial energy level alignment between the DPC $ _{60} $  based ETL and the perovskite layer, thereby improving the electron extraction capability of

As shown in Figure 2a, a simple synthetic method to prepare DPC $ _{60} $  is presented. The crystals of DPC $ _{60} $  are obtained from its toluene solution by solvent evaporation. It is expected that the high hydrophobicity of SnO $ _2 $ /DPC $ _{60} $  film can suppress heterogeneous nucleation and reduce the grain boundary defects of perovskite. As investigated by XRD patterns (Figure 4b), the full width at half maximum of the perovskite (110) peak on SnO $ _2 $ /DPC $ _{60} $  is narrower than that on the bare SnO $ _2 $  substrate, indicating that the high hydrophobicity of the SnO $ _2 $ /DPC $ _{60} $  film promotes the crystallization of perovskite and forms larger crystals. The top-views and cross-sectional scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of the corresponding perovskite films are also provided in Figures S8 and S9 in the Supporting Information. The average grain size of the SnO $ _2 $ /DPC $ _{60} $  based perovskite film is around 272.9 nm, which is much larger than that of the bare SnO $ _2 $  based perovskite film (246.2 nm), which is consistent with the XRD results. The improved perovskite quality is expected to enhance the device efficiency and stability. Besides, it can be clearly seen that the excess PbI $ _2 $  in the perovskite precursor solution is formed on the surface of the perovskite film. This phenomenon has also been reported in previous reports, $ ^{[45]} $  which demonstrates that excess PbI $ _2 $  in the perovskite precursor is located at the grain boundaries near the interface of the perovskite/hole transport layer for the one-step antisolvent solution method.

To explore the electron extraction capability of   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   and the bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   films, the steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) experiments are conducted. As shown in Figure 4c, the neat perovskite film shows the strongest PL intensity with a peak at 778 nm. While both of   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   based perovskite films show significant PL quenching, indicating efficient electron extraction capability of the both ETLs. Compared with the bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   based perovskite film, the   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   based perovskite film exhibits much lower PL intensity, indicating that the   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   has a more efficient electron extraction capability than that of the bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   film. This can be attributed to the suitable energy level and high electron affinity of the   $  DPC_{60}  $ . Figure 4d shows the time-resolved PL spectra of perovskite,   $  SnO_{2}  $ /perovskite and   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $ /perovskite films. As summarized in Table S2 in the Supporting Information, two decay times (the fast decay lifetime   $  \tau_{1}  $   and the slow decay lifetime   $  \tau_{2}  $ ) are obtained by fitting the experimental data with biexponential decay function.  $  ^{[46,47]}  $   The fast decay is attributed to the quenching of carriers by the ETLs, and the slow decay is originated from the radiative recombination.  $  ^{[48]}  $   The perovskite film,   $  SnO_{2}  $ /perovskite film and   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $ /perovskite film yield a decay time of 88.4, 11.0, and 8.2 ns, respectively. The fastest PL decay dynamics of   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $ /perovskite film indicates that   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   ETL offers better electron extraction/transport capability than the bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   film.

The performances of the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{DPC}_{60} $  and the bare  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  based devices are studied as follows. Considering that the device performance should be sensitive to the thickness of  $ \mathrm{DPC}_{60} $  modification layer, we first explore the influence of different concentration of  $ \mathrm{DPC}_{60} $  solution on the device performance, and achieve the highest device efficiency with a  $ \mathrm{DPC}_{60} $  concentration of  $ 3\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  (Figure S10 and Table S3, Supporting Information). We select  $ \mathrm{DPC}_{60} $  with a concentration of  $ 3\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  for further study. For direct comparison, PCBM is also incorporated into the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  based devices, and the devices efficiency is relatively inferior than that of  $ \mathrm{DPC}_{60} $

based devices (Figure S11 and Table S4, Supporting Information), indicating that the structure characteristic of fullerene derivative is critical to improving the device performance. Figure 5a provides the J-V curves with forward and reverse scans of the best-performing devices based on SnO₂/DPC₆₀ and the bare SnO₂ ETLs, respectively. The SnO₂/DPC₆₀ based device shows negligible hysteresis in J-V curves with different scanning directions, while the bare SnO₂ based device exhibits an obvious hysteresis. The elimination of hysteresis of SnO₂/DPC₆₀ based device could be attributed to the effective electron extraction at ETL/perovskite interface.  $  ^{[49,50]}  $   The key photovoltaic parameters of the best-performing devices are summarized in Table S5 in the Supporting Information. The SnO₂/DPC₆₀ based devices yield a maximum PCE of 20.4% with an open-circuit voltage (VOC) of 1.14 V, a short-circuit current density (JSC) of 23.0 mA cm⁻², and a fill factor (FF) of 77.7%. In contrast, the bare SnO₂ based devices yield a relatively inferior performance with a maximum PCE of 18.8%, a VOC of 1.11 V, a JSC of 22.5 mA cm⁻², and a FF of 75.5%. We also record the J-V curves of the SnO₂ and SnO₂/DPC₆₀ based devices using the commonly-used perovskite composition (CsMAFA: Cs₀.₀₅(MA₀.₁₇FA₀.₈₃)₀.₉₅Pb(I₀.₈₃Br₀.₁₇)₃) (Figure S12, Supporting Information). The SnO₂/DPC₆₀ based device shows much higher efficiency than that of the SnO₂ based device using CsMAFA perovskite solution, which is consistent with the results with the perovskite precursor solution of FA₀.₈₁MA₀.₁₀Cs₀.₀₄PbI₂.₅₅Br₀.₄₀. The improved VOC of SnO₂/DPC₆₀ based devices is attributed to the improved interfacial energy level alignment and the passivation effect of DPC₆₀, which suppresses carriers recombination at ETL/perovskite interface. The elevated JSC of SnO₂/DPC₆₀ based devices is attributed to the improved electron extraction at ETL/perovskite interface.

Both efficiencies of the   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   and the bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   based devices are consistent with the values from the corresponding J-V curves. To better compare the performance of the different ETLs based devices, the statistical photovoltaic parameters of 20 separate devices are provided in Figure S14 in the Supporting Information. The average PCE of   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   based devices is much higher than that of bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   based devices. The narrower distribution of the PCEs shows that the   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   based device performance is more reproducible than the bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   based devices. The   $  J_{SC}  $   value of the both devices is verified by the incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectrum. Figure 5b shows the corresponding IPCE curves of the   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   and the bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   based devices. The stability of the unencapsulated devices are also evaluated using the MPP tracking method under continuous irradiation in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere with a thermal ageing temperature of   $  55 \pm 5  $   °C. It should be noted that this temperature is due to the heating effect from the continuous illumination, no cooling unit is adopted in our experiment. After 200 h, the   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   based device retains 82% of its initial efficiency, while the PCE of the bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   based device drops to 51% (Figure 5d). The enhanced stability of   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   based PSCs is attributed to the chemical interaction between perovskite and   $  DPC_{60}  $ , as well as the improved crystallinity of perovskite film.

A functionalized cis-fulleropyrrolidine with amine functional group,   $  DPC_{60}  $  , is synthesized and employed as a modification layer between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite layer in PSCs. The   $  DPC_{60}  $   possesses many advantages for improving device performance of PSCs such as appropriate energy levels, relatively fast electron mobility, chemical interaction with perovskite layer, and low solubility in the perovskite solvent. As a result, PSCs based on   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   reach up to a maximum PCE of 20.4%, higher than those of bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   based devices. Meanwhile, the   $  SnO_{2}/DPC_{60}  $   based device shows a higher performance reproducibility in comparison with the bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   based device. Furthermore, the hydrophobic   $  DPC_{60}  $   suppresses heterogeneous nucleation

and improves the crystallinity of the perovskite film, resulting in increased device stability. In summary, our results demonstrate the potential to improve PSCs device performance and stability simultaneously by using functional fullerene derivatives as interfacial bridge of ETL/perovskite.

# Experimental Section

Synthesis of   $  DPC_{60}  $  .   $  DPC_{60}  $   was synthesized following a reported procedure with modification.  $  ^{[36]}  $     $  C_{60}  $   (72 mg, 0.10 mmol) was dissolved in chlorobenzene (20 mL) under sonication for 5 min, then 2 equiv. of benzylamine (21.4 mg, 0.20 mmol) and 7 equiv. of Benzaldehyde (74.2 mg, 0.70 mmol) were added. The flask was wrapped with aluminum foil and refluxed for 24 h under a nitrogen atmosphere. The crude reaction mixtures were directly poured onto a silica gel column, and   $  CS_{2}  $   was used to separate the non-reacted   $  C_{60}  $  , then a toluene/  $  CS_{2}  $   (1:1) mixture was employed to obtain the pure   $  DPC_{60}  $  . Yield 46%.

Device Fabrication: Perovskite solar cells with the configuration of ITO/  $  SnO_{2}  $  or  $  (SnO_{2}/DPC_{60})  $ /perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD/Au were fabricated on ITO-coated glass substrates with a resistivity of 7  $  \Omega  $  cm  $  ^{-2}  $ . Prior to fabrication, the patterned ITO glass substrates were ultrasonic cleaned sequentially with detergent, deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol for 15 min for each step. Then the ITO glass substrates were dried with nitrogen gas and treated using UV-ozone cleaning for 30 min.
