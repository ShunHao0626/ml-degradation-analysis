# Multifunctional Chemical Linker Imidazoleacetic Acid Hydrochloride for 21% Efficient and Stable Planar Perovskite Solar Cells

Jiangzhao Chen, Xing Zhao, Seul-Gi Kim, and Nam-Gyu Park*

Chemical interaction at a heterojunction interface induced by an appropriate chemical linker is of crucial importance for high efficiency, hysteresis-less, and stable perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Effective interface engineering in PSCs is reported via a multifunctional chemical linker of 4-imidazoleacetic acid hydrochloride (ImAcHCl) that can provide a chemical bridge between  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and perovskite through an ester bond with  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  via esterification reaction and an electrostatic interaction with perovskite via imidazolium cation in ImAcHCl and iodide anion in perovskite. In addition, the chloride anion in ImAcHCl plays a role in the improvement of crystallinity of perovskite film crystallinity. The introduction of ImAcHCl onto  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  realigns the positions of the conduction and valence bands upwards, reduces nonradiative recombination, and improves carrier life time. As a consequence, average power conversion efficiency (PCE) is increased from  $ 18.60\% \pm 0.50\% $  to  $ 20.22\% \pm 0.34\% $  before and after surface modification, respectively, which mainly results from an enhanced voltage from  $ 1.084 \pm 0.012\mathrm{V} $  to  $ 1.143 \pm 0.009\mathrm{V} $ . The best PCE of  $ 21\% $  is achieved by  $ 0.1\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  ImAcHCl treatment, along with negligible hysteresis. Moreover, an unencapsulated device with ImAcHCl-modified  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  shows much better thermal and moisture stability than unmodified  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ .

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) employing organic-inorganic metal halide hybrid perovskites as light-harvesting materials have received amazing attention due to high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and low-cost solution-processable procedure,  $  ^{[1]}  $  since a PCE of 9.7% was achieved based on solid-state PSCs using solid spiro-MeOTAD hole transporting material (HTM) (spiro-MeOTAD = 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene).  $  ^{[2]}  $  Recent technologies can lead to a certified PCE of 24.2%,  $  ^{[3]}  $  including perovskite film quality optimization, additive engineering, interface engineering, and device structure engineering. To date, to the best of our knowledge, efficient PSCs with the PCE exceeding 22% were fabricated based on mesoporous n-i-p

For example, self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) (3-aminopropyltriethoxysilane (3-APTES),  $  ^{[26]}  $   and 4-pyridinecarboxylic acid (PA)  $  ^{[27]}  $  ) formed on   $  SnO_{2}  $   led to improved PCE. Recently, a PCE of 21.43% was obtained by modification of   $  SnO_{2}  $   with a zwitterionic compound (3-(1-pyridinio)-1-propane sulfonate), which resulted in an improved PCE and thermal stability.  $  ^{[12]}  $   Additionally, Bu et al. incorporated a KOH passivation layer at   $  SnO_{2}/perovskite  $   interface in planar PSCs, which facilitated perovskite grain growth and passivated the interface defects.  $  ^{[28]}  $   The above successful examples strongly indicate that interfacial engineering with multifunctional interface modifiers is imperative to improve further PCE and stability of a   $  SnO_{2}  $  -based PSCs.

In the present work, we demonstrate a simple and effective versatile interface modification strategy in the   $  SnO_{2}  $  -based planar PSCs, where chlorine-containing imidazole ionic liquid (IL) 4-imidazoleacetic acid hydrochloride (ImAcHCl) is employed to modify the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETM. The basis for selection of this interface link molecule is because multifunctional effects are expected. Carboxylic acid in ImAcHCl can react with the hydroxyl group on the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   to form ester bonding via esterification.  $  ^{[27,29]}  $   Lone pair on the nitrogen of imidazole group in ImAcHCl is expected to passivate defects of the perovskite layer. It was reported that pyridinium cation and amino cation could effectively passivate the Pb-I antisite defects or cation vacancy.  $  ^{[12,30]}  $   Yang et al. reported the surface modification of   $  TiO_{2}  $   by 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium tetrafluoroborate ionic liquid, which resulted in dramatically increasing PCE and eliminating hysteresis in planar PSCs,  $  ^{[31]}  $   which however might not be controllable because this ionic liquid could not be able to bond chemically to   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface in the absence of proper functional group. Besides, Tan et al. demonstrated a contact passivation strategy in planar PSCs with improved PCE and stability, which suppressed nonradiative recombination because deep level trap states at   $  TiO_{2}  $ /perovskite were reduced by interfacial Cl.  $  ^{[7]}  $   We have been motivated to find multifunctional chemical linker to improve the interface between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite. We have found an interfacial modifier having two functional groups, carboxylic acid and amine, and chloride which is ImAcHCl. Modification of   $  SnO_{2}  $   with ImAcHCl improves open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) significantly and thus eventually increases PCE from 19.5% to 21%. Moreover, increased device stability and reduced hysteresis are also realized by the multifunctional chemical linker.

The device configuration employed in the present work is   $  FTO/SnO_{2}/(FAPbI_{3})_{0.95}(MAPbBr_{3})_{0.05}/spiro-MeOTAD/Au  $   as shown in Figure 1a, where MACl additive was added into perovskite precursor solution to prepare high-quality perovskite films based on the previous report.  $  ^{[1h]}  $   In order to further reduce interfacial nonradiative recombination and accordingly improve the device performance, ImAcHCl is used to modify the   $  SnO_{2}/perovskite  $   interface (Figure 1b), where molecular structure and electrostatic potential (ESP) map of ImAcHCl are depicted in Figure S1a,b, in the Supporting Information, respectively. The gradual color change from red to blue in ESP map indicates the gradual increase in electron density from -COOH group (  $  127.378\ kJ\ mol^{-1}  $  ) to imidazole ring (  $  659.322\ kJ\ mol^{-1}  $  ), which implies that ImAcHCl molecule has high dipole moment and is suitable to be utilized to modify the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $  .  $  ^{[12]}  $   In Figure 1b, carboxylic acid in ImAcHCl is expected to form ester bonding with   $  SnO_{2}  $   via esterification reaction and imidazolium moiety will interact with iodide in the perovskite layer.

To confirm the existence of ImAcHCl on the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   film, EDS mapping of FTO/  $  SnO_{2}  $  /ImAcHCl film was performed (Figure S2, Supporting Information). Cl and N elements are obviously observed, which clearly demonstrate the existence of ImAcHCl on the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   film. In order to further confirm the existence of ImAcHCl and understand the interaction between ImAcHCl and   $  SnO_{2}  $ , X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS) measurements are performed (Figure 1c–g, see full spectra in Figure S3 in the Supporting Information). The occurrence of Cl 2p (Figure 1c) and N 1s (Figure 1d) again confirms the existence of ImAcHCl at the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   film. As illustrated in Figure 1e, Sn   $  3d_{5/2}  $   (486.9 eV) and Sn   $  3d_{3/2}  $   (495.3 eV) of the bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   film are reduced to 486.7 and 495.1 eV for the ImAcHCl treated   $  SnO_{2}  $   film, respectively, indicative of a chemical interaction between   $  SnO_{2}  $   film and ImAcHCl.  $  ^{[32]}  $   As revealed by O 1s spectra in Figure 1f,g, an asymmetric broad peak is observed for both untreated   $  SnO_{2}  $   and treated   $  SnO_{2}  $   films, which can be deconvoluted into two peaks. The peak at about 530.6–530.8 eV is assigned to the lattice oxygen on the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   film and the other peak at 531.2–531.9 eV is attributed to the chemisorbed oxygen atoms or hydroxyl groups on the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   film.  $  ^{[32,33]}  $   It is easily found that the intensity of lattice oxygen reduces while the intensity of adsorbed oxygen increases after ImAcHCl modification, which is indicative of esterification reaction between -OH at the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   film and -COOH on ImAcHCl. Figure S4 in the Supporting Information shows FTIR spectra of   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}  $ -IL films along with ImAcHCl. ImAcHCl sample exhibits characteristic peaks at around 1700 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   (-C=O) and 1213 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   (-C-O-), which are shifted to 1653 and 1226 cm  $  ^{-1}  $ , respectively.  $  ^{[11]}  $   The above FTIR results again confirm esterification reaction between -OH at the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   film and -COOH on ImAcHCl, which is consistent with XPS results.

Steady-state photoluminescence (SSPL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) are measured to understand radiative recombination and carrier life time (Figure 4 and Table S7 in the Supporting Information). PL intensity decay curves are fitted using a bi-exponential decay equation of  $  I(t) = I_0 + A_1 \exp(-t/\tau_1) + A_2 \exp(-t/\tau_2)  $ , where  $  \tau_1  $  and  $  \tau_2  $  represent fast and slow decay time constant, respectively.[21b,d,23a] Average time constant ( $  \tau_{\text{ave}}  $ ) is calculated using relation of  $  \tau_{\text{ave}} = (A_1 \tau_1^2 + A_2 \tau_2^2)/(A_1 \tau_1 + A_2 \tau_2)  $ .[21d] As displayed in Figure 4a, it is evidently seen that PL intensity from glass side (light incident on glass side) is slightly higher than that from perovskite film side when the same sample (glass/FAMA or glass/ImAcHCl/FAMA) is illuminated on different sides. This difference in PL intensity could be related to different defect contents. PL intensity of the glass/ImAcHCl/FAMA sample is markedly higher than that of the glass/FAMA reference sample, regardless of incident sides, which is in good accordance with significantly enhanced PL lifetimes from 616.14 to 812.45 ns (Figure 4b and Table S7, Supporting Information). This strongly suggests that ImAcHCl layer at SnO $ _2 $ /perovskite interface can effectively passivate the parasitic defects from perovskite through imidazolium cation or Cl $ ^- $  anion and thereby suppress interface nonradiative recombination,

eventually resulting in improved   $  V_{oc}  $  . Similar defect passivation effect has been reported in the previous work.  $  ^{[7,12,30,31]}  $   After ImAcHCl interface modification, electron extraction ability is improved in comparison with   $  SnO_{2}  $   control film as confirmed by reduced PL lifetime from 68.42 to 26.35 ns, as shown in Figure 4c,d and Table S8 in the Supporting Information. Improved electron extraction could originate from improved band alignment and increased conductivity after ImAcHCl treatment for   $  SnO_{2}  $   film, which might partially contribute to the slightly increased   $  J_{sc}  $  .

Space-charge-limited current (SCLC) technique is usually used to quantitatively evaluate the defect density of perovskite films.  $  ^{[12,20,21b,d,28]}  $   Figure 5a,b illustrates the typical dark I-V curves for electron-only devices (FTO/SnO  $  _{2}  $   or SnO  $  _{2}  $  -IL/perovskite/PCBM/Ag) and the defect density is estimated (Table S9, Supporting Information). There are three regions: the Ohmic region at low bias exhibiting linear relation between current and electric field, the trap-filled limited region at intermediate bias region showing a sharp increase in current, and a trap-free SCLC region at high bias. It is well known that the trap density levels are continuously filled in the trap-filled limited region and all the traps are filled until trap-filled limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ) as bias increases. The trap density can be calculated using an equation of   $  n_{\mathrm{t}} = (2\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}V_{\mathrm{TFL}})/(eL^{2})  $  , where   $  \varepsilon  $   is the dielectric constants of perovskite,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity, e is the elementary charge, and L is the thickness of perovskite film.   $  V_{TFL}  $   is obtained by fitting the dark I-V curves. # Experimental Section

Materials: Formamidinium iodide (FAI) (FA:  $ \mathrm{HC(NH_2)_2} $ ) was synthesized according to the previous reports.[21b,23a] Methylammonium bromide (MABr, MA = CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ ) and methylammonium chloride (MACl) were synthesized by adopting the same method for synthesizing FAI. All the other materials were purchased from commercial sources and used as received without further purification. SnO $ _2 $  aqueous colloidal solution

(15 wt% in   $  H_{2}O  $  ), lead (II) iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  , 99.9985%), and lead (II) bromide (  $  PbBr_{2}  $  , 99.999%) were purchased from Alpha Aesar. ImAcHCl (98%), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.9%), dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%), chlorobenzene (99.9%), acetonitrile (99.8%), and diethyl ether (99.7%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-MeOTAD) was purchased from Merck.

Device Fabrication: FTO-coated glass substrates (Pilkington, TEC-8, 8 Ω sq  $  ^{-1}  $  ) were etched by laser and then ultrasonically cleaned for 15 min with detergent, deionized water, and ethanol, sequentially. After FTO substrates were exposed to UV ozone (UVO) for 30 min, the 1.67 wt% SnO  $  _{2}  $  aqueous colloidal solution, which was prepared by diluting 15 wt% SnO  $  _{2}  $  aqueous colloidal dispersion with deionized water, was spin coated on the FTO-coated glass substrates at 3000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed on a hot plate in ambient air at 150 °C for 25 min. After the film cooled down to room temperature, the resultant SnO  $  _{2}  $  film was treated by UVO for 30 min. For modifying SnO  $  _{2}  $  film, an ImAcHCl solution in ethanol (concentration was varied: 0.05, 0.1, 0.5, and 1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) was spin coated on as-prepared SnO  $  _{2}  $  film at 5000 rpm for 30 s followed by annealing at 90 °C for 10 min. 1.5 M FA  $  _{0.95}  $  MA  $  _{0.05}  $  PbI  $  _{2.85}  $  Br  $  _{0.15}  $  perovskite precursor solution was prepared by dissolving FAI (0.2451 g), PbI  $  _{2}  $  (0.6569 g), MABr (0.0084 g), PbBr  $  _{2}  $  (0.0275 g), and MACl (0.0330 g) in DMSO (0.111 mL) and DMF (0.889 mL), where concentration of precursor solution was calculated with respect to the content of Pb  $  ^{2+}  $ . The perovskite precursor solution was spin coated on the as-prepared unmodified or ImAcHCl modified SnO  $  _{2}  $  film at 4000 rpm for 20 s, where 500 μL diethyl ether was dripped at 10 s before ending the program. The as-spun Lewis acid–base adduct film was annealed at 150 °C for 15 min. After cooling down to room temperature, 20 μL of the spiro-MeOTAD solution was spin coated on the as-prepared perovskite layer at 3000 rpm for 30 s.
