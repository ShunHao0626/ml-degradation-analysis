# Abstract

In this work, we systematically investigated the properties of three kinds of  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  films prepared by magnetron sputtering either NiO or Ni target under direct current (DC) or radio frequency (RF) power without any preheating. Those  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  films were found to be different in composition ratios, lattice strain and energy levels, thus showing different optical and carrier transport properties. By RF power source, sputtering Ni target in pure  $ \mathrm{O}_2 $  atmosphere led to  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  film with relatively good crystallization and surface properties compared with other two types of  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  films. These  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  films were further introduced into  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  based planar p-i-n heterojunction perovskite solar cells (PSCs) as the hole transport layers (HTL). It is revealed that PSCs based on  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  HTL prepared by sputtering Ni target in pure  $ \mathrm{O}_2 $  atmosphere achieved a highest power conversion efficiency of  $ 16.3\% $  with negligible hysteresis. # 1. Introduction

Nickle oxide (NiO) is a direct wideband gap semiconductor. Due to its promising properties such as ultraviolet (UV) transparent conductivity, natural p-type semiconductivity and low resistivity, it has been widely studied and applied in the fields of UV detectors, gas sensors, electrochromic display devices, heterojunction light emitting diodes and laser diodes [1-5]. NiO films are usually nonstoichiometric and show Ni-defective states [6].

Recently, organic-inorganic halide perovskites solar cells (PSCs) emerged as a promising photovoltaic technology and a remarkable highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 22.1\% $  has been demonstrated based on mesoscopic PSCs [7]. Mesoscopic PSCs usually employ a mesoscopic layer sintered at high temperature as a scaffold, which increases the fabrication cost and limits their compatibility with flexible substrates [8, 9]. Therefore, as an alternative structure, planar heterojunction configuration (regular n-i-p structure or inverted p-i-n structure) devices which can be entirely fabricated at low temperature are actively studied [9-12]. In planar heterojunction devices, the charge transport materials play vital roles in electron/hole

extraction.

As an intrinsic wide-gap p-type semiconductor,  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  film recently has attracted extensive attention for its application in PSC field. It has been widely reported that  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  can be served as ideal hole transporting material owing to its low cost, chemical stability and suitable energy level [9]. Due to the higher conduction band relative to  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  (MAPbI₃),  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  prevents unfavorable carrier leakage from the MAPbI₃ layer to the electrode. Furthermore,  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  is an inorganic material which features good stability. In this regard,  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  is much superior to conventional organic hole transport layers (HTLs) such as poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) which is prepared from an acidic solution and can accelerate the decomposition of MAPbI₃ [13].

Diverse methods are adopted to fabricate  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  films including magnetron sputtering [14, 15], sol-gel deposition [16], vacuum evaporation [17], atomic layer deposition [18] and pulsed laser deposition [19]. Among these methods, magnetron sputtering shows advantages of high depositing rates, easy control over the composition of the deposited films and low cost in the industry [20, 21]. Moreover, it can completely apply to low-temperature processes which meet the demands of fabricating flexible devices [22]. Therefore, magnetron sputtering has recently become a prior approach to prepare  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  as a HTL for PSCs. Some studies regarding magnetron sputtered  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  HTLs for PSCs have been reported. For example, Cui et al. [23] reported PSCs with RF reactive magnetron sputtered  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  achieved PCE of  $ 9.8\% $ ; Wang et al. [24] demonstrated that by introducing the RF sputtered  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  and optimizing the oxygen

# 2.1 Materials Preparation

The  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -DMSO@DMF precursor solution was prepared by dissolving both the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  (99.9985%, Alfa Aesar) and DMSO (purity  $ 99.9\% $ , Sigma-Aldrich) into DMF (purity  $ 99.8\% $ , Sigma-Aldrich) at the ratio of  $ 1.3\mathrm{mol} / \mathrm{L} $ . The methyl ammonium iodide

(MAI, >99.5%, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp) of 60 mg was dissolved into 1 mL isopropyl alcohol (IPA, Aladdin) to form the ultrahigh concentration (0.337 mol/L) MAI/IPA solution. The [6,6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp) of 30 mg was dissolved into 1mL Chlorobenzene (Sigma-Aldrich). A saturated bathocuproine (BCP, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp) /IPA precursor solution was obtained by adding excess BCP into 1mL IPA at temperature of  $ 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ .

# 2.2 Fabrication of the  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  Electrode Interlayer

Three different  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  thin films were sputtered on fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) substrates without intentional heating using a Denton Vacuum DESKTOP PRO sputter deposition system (Denton Vacuum LLC). Two of them were deposited by reactive DC/RF magnetron sputtering techniques from an Ni target (50 mm in diameter and 3 mm in thickness) and the other was deposited by DC magnetron sputtering from an NiO target (atomic ratio: 1:1, 50 mm in diameter and 3 mm in thickness, binding with 3 mm-thickness copper backplane). Both of the Ni target and NiO target were of high purity (99.99%). Prior to the sputtering of each film, the base pressure in the chamber was pumped down to less than 1E-6 Torr. The target was pre-sputtered for 15 min to remove the contamination layer if any on the surface of the target. Over all these three sputtering operations, pure oxygen was introduced as the sputtering gas. The total sputtering pressure and power were constantly kept at 1E-2 Torr and 100 W, respectively (see Table 1). The distance between the target and substrates was approximately 8 cm. All

# 2.3 Device Fabrication

The planar-heterojunction hybrid PSCs were fabricated in standard arrangement by sandwiching a planar layer of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  between the  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  hole transport layer and the PCBM electron transport layer. The counter layer was made of a bathocuproine (BCP) exciton blocking film and silver (Ag). Prior to the device fabrication, the pre-patterned FTO/glass substrates (NSG Corp,  $ 14\Omega/\square $ ) were rinsed with detergent and then sequentially ultrasonic cleaned in de-ionized water, acetone and ethanol.

After depositing the   $  NiO_{x}  $   layers, the methyl ammonium lead iodide (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  ) layer was prepared using a continuous dripping method which had been detailed described in our previous work [26]. Briefly, substrates loaded with 30   $  \mu  $  L   $  PbI_{2}  $  -DMSO@DMF precursor solution were set to spin at 4000 rpm for totally 45 s and the MAI/IPA solution was dripped on the spinning substrates at the 25th second. To ensure   $  PbI_{2}  $   completely transform into   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ , the samples were heated on a hot plate at 120 °C for 20 min. Subsequently, both the PCBM/chlorobenzene and the saturated BCP/IPA precursor solutions were spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 25 s after a filter process. All these procedures were implemented inside an Argon-filled glove box at 25 °C.

# 2.4 Characterization

The current density-voltage (J-V) characteristics of devices were measured using a Keithley 2400 digital source meter under simulated AM 1.5G solar irradiation at 100 mW/cm² in ambient condition. The J-V characteristics were recorded by reverse and forward scan in the range from -0.1 V to +1.2 V with a scan rate of 50 mV/s. XRD results were acquired using a Rigaku Utima IV XRD system. Transmittance and absorbance measurements were carried out by Shimadzu UV-2550. Field emission scanning electron microscope (SEM) images were obtained using SUPRA 55 FESEM system. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) data were obtained using XPS ESCALAB 250, the C 1s peaks were calibrated at 254.8 eV. The Valance band (VB) spectra were measured with a monochromatic He I light source (21.21 eV) and a VG Scienta R4000 analyzer. A sample bias of -5 V was applied to observe the secondary electron cutoff (SEC). Photoluminescence (PL) measurements were carried out by Hitachi F7000 fluorescence spectrometer. The electronic impedance spectroscopy (EIS) test of the devices was carried out without illumination in the range from 100 Hz to 1 MHz using EG&G VersaSTAT 3. All the characterized NiO $  _{x}  $  samples were deposited on FTO substrates, their thicknesses were controlled to be 100 nm for XRD analysis and 50 nm for XPS and UPS analysis.

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was applied to identify and analyze the chemical states and stoichiometries of oxygen and nickel on the surfaces of these three different  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  films. The Ni 2p3/2 region comprises the main peak, the shoulder, and its satellites at 854.0, 855.5 and  $ 861.0\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. The relevant energy regions (852-858 eV), as showed in Fig. 3 (a), were satisfactorily fitted by 3 peaks corresponding to the electronic states of  $ \mathrm{Ni}^{2+} $  (peak 1 at  $ 853.8\mathrm{eV} $ ) in the cubic rock-salt structure, NiOOH (peak 2 at  $ 855.2\mathrm{eV} $ ) and  $ \mathrm{Ni}^{3+} $  (peak 3 at  $ 856.2\mathrm{eV} $ ) [29]. Hydroxylation is indeed a ubiquitous phenomenon for oxide materials including  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $ . The peak 2 of nickel hydroxide was observed because all samples have been exposed to ambient air and their surfaces were hydroxylated. To some extent, the hydroxyl contents can weaken the connection between the element of Ni and O and result in worse interface property and stability of the perovskite film which unfolds decomposable nature in moisture [29, 30]. From Fig. 3 (a), the detailed proportions of  $ \mathrm{Ni}^{2+}/\mathrm{NiOOH}/\mathrm{Ni}^{3+} $  were estimated to be 28/26/46 in a- $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  film, 26/30/44 in b- $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  film and 24/33/43 in c- $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  films. It is observed that a- $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  film presents lower proportion of hydroxyl groups while c- $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  presents the highest, which is also verified by the O 1s spectra presented in Fig. 4 (b). The more  $ \mathrm{Ni}^{3+} $  ions are created in  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  film, the more holes can exist in the film since  $ \mathrm{Ni}^{3+} $  ions serve as acceptors to affect the conductive property of the  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  film. Films of a- $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  and b- $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  exhibit higher surface coverage of  $ \mathrm{Ni}^{3+} $  owing to their higher intensity and preferential in-plane orientation of the (1 1 1) planes. The created  $ \mathrm{Ni}^{3+} $  ions are also reported to act as color centers which would affect the optical properties of  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  films [18, 29]. It can be seen in Fig. A2 that  $ 20\mathrm{nm} $  thick films of a- $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  and b- $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  films exhibit average transmittances above  $ 80\% $  in the wavelength range of visible light from  $ 400\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 800\mathrm{nm} $ . The differences of energy levels can result in great differences of the hole injection processes from perovskite layer to  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  layer [33]. Because the VB of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer is approximate -5.43 eV, the hole injection from the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  layer into c- $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  layer might be not efficient. From the J-V curves scanned with different directions in Fig. 5 (c), we can observe negligible hysteresis which still exists as a controversial but nonnegligible issue in PSCs field. Recently more and more results have supported the proposal that the hysteresis originates from ion migration within the perovskite layer. When an external field is applied, the ions move

# 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, by changing the sputtering targets and power sources, three various magnetron sputtering techniques were employed to prepare  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  films applied in planar PSCs as hole transport layers. The properties of these three  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  HTLs were well studied and compared. It is found that the  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  properties are very sensitive to the composition proportion of nickel and oxygen. With more oxygen atoms introduced, the RF reactive sputtered  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  exhibited more oxygen interstitial defects and resulted in a

22

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

more suitable energy level, which significantly improved its carrier transport property in PSCs. Compared with the  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  sputtered from NiO/Ni targets with DC power applied, the RF reactive sputtered  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  HTLs performed best in planar PSCs, leading to a champion cell with PCE of  $ 16.3\% $ . Furthermore, the  $ \mathrm{NiO}_x $  based devices also show remarkable reproducibility and stability. This work provides a pathway towards high efficiency PSCs and also contributes to the further research in flexible and commercial PSCs.
