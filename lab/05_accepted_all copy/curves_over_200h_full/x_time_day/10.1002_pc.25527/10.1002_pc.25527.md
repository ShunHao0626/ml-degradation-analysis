# Preparation of high-efficiency perovskite solar cells via doping Ag into CuO nanofibers as hole buffer layer

Zhishan Zhuang  $  ^{1}  $   | Linlin Qiu  $  ^{1}  $   | Lika Dong  $  ^{1}  $   | Yue Chen  $  ^{1}  $   | Zhudan Chu  $  ^{1}  $   | Xiangyu Ma  $  ^{1}  $   | Pingfan Du  $  ^{1,2,3}  $   | Jie Xiong  $  ^{1,2}  $

# Abstract

Ag/CuO nanofibers (Nfs) with a diameter of 650 nm were successfully prepared via electrospinning Ag doped CuO nanofibers and then calcined at   $  450^{\circ}  $  C for 2 hours. By using Ag/CuO Nfs and PEDOT:PSS as the hole transport layer, an optimum perovskite solar cell power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 11.6% was achieved, which was 33% higher than that of the original perovskite solar cells (8.7%). The devices showed a high PCE because of the high cavity mobility of the Ag/CuO Nfs/PEDOT:PSS films. At the same time, the Ag/CuO Nfs could also slow down the hydrolysis and corrosion of the perovskite by acidic PEDOT:PSS, and increase the environmental stability of the solar cells.

# KEYWORDS

$ \mathrm{Ag / CuO} $  nanofibers, electrospinning, hole transport layer, perovskite solar cells

# 1 | INTRODUCTION

Perovskite solar cells have gained a lot of interest in the field of solar cells because of their high absorption coefficients, broad wavelength absorption range, and long carrier diffusion lengths and lifetimes. In particular, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of lead halide perovskite solar cells has increased from 3.8% to more than 24.2%.  $  ^{[1,2]}  $   This astonishing improvements in efficiency indicates that perovskite solar cells could move into the mainstream for the development of solar cells. The

structure of perovskite solar cells can be divided into a positive structure and an inverted structure. Among the two, the inverted planar heterojunction perovskite solar cell (PSC) has attracted broad attention because of its simple fabrication process.

In inverted planar hetero junction PSCs, poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) is the most commonly employed hole transport layers (HTLs), delivering outstanding photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{[3]}  $   However, perovskite solar cells based on the PEDOT:PSS HTL have higher efficiencies but lower open-circuit

Hence, the device performance decreases rapidly, which limits the use of PEDOT:PSS as a HTL. To ensure high performance devices, PEDOT:PSS, which is commercially manufactured, is generally used, however, it has a high price and the costs for battery preparation increases. These problems limit the commercialization of perovskite solar cells.

Compared with organic materials, inorganic p-type semiconductor materials have the advantages of good chemical stability, low costs, and a wide band gap. Hence, they have been introduced into the solar cells HTL system. As a hole transport material, there were used in dye-sensitized solar cells and organic polymer solar cells,  $  ^{[5-9]}  $   and later in reverse perovskite solar cells. At present, there are many inorganic hole extraction materials for perovskite solar cells, such as copper iodide (CuI),  $  ^{[10]}  $   Copper oxide (CuO),  $  ^{[11]}  $   cuprous oxide (Cu  $  _{2}  $  O),  $  ^{[12,13]}  $   cuprous thiocyanate (CuSCN),  $  ^{[14-19]}  $   and nickel oxide (NiO).  $  ^{[4,17,20-26]}  $   Among them, the valence band of CuO is 5.4 eV, which matches with CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $   very well and has been directly spin-coated for use as a HTL. The unique morphology of one-dimensional (1D) nanostructures provides a continuous path for charge transport. Among the 1D structures, nanofibers of metals and their oxides combine the excellent conductivity of metals and metal oxides and the size effects of nanowires, which can be applied for the preparation of high-performance perovskite solar cells. Nanofiber preparation methods include the template method, the hydrothermal method, the self-assembly method, and the electrospinning method. Among them, electrospinning has the advantages of low cost, simple equipment operations, various spinnable materials, and a high efficiency.  $  ^{[27,28]}  $   At present, it is the only effective method to prepare nanofibers directly and continuously. Compared with spin-coated films, electrospun nanofibers have larger specific surface areas, higher conductivities, and lower charge carrier recombination. Furthermore, the addition of Ag can improve the conductivity of the HTL.  $  ^{[29]}  $

In this step, Ag/CuO-Nfs with an average diameter of   $  \sim  $  650 nm were prepared on FTO conductive glass substrates by electrospinning and then calcined at high temperature. At present, PSCs with high efficiency can be successfully prepared in the ambient environment (45%-55% humidity, 20°C). Glove boxes may be an obstacle to the future growing up of PSCS, which is no longer needed. Finally, by using Ag/CuO Nfs with different coverages as an anodic modification layer, a maximum PCE of 11.6% was achieved, which was significantly improved compared with the reference device (8.7%).

# 2 | EXPERIMENT

# 2.1 | Reagents and materials

PEDOT:PSS,   $  PbI_{2}  $  , and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (MAI) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. PCBM was supplied by OPV Tech Co. FTO (12   $  \Omega/sq.  $  ) was purchased from Heptachroma. CoPolyvinyl pyrrolidone (PVP) and   $  CuNO_{3}  $   were obtained from Macklin. Others were supplied by Aladdin.

# 2.2 | Materials preparation

The precursor solution of Ag/CuO Nfs modified layer was synthesized from ethanol and PVP with a mass ratio of 10:1. The precursor solution of electrospinning for the Ag/CuO Nfs comprised  $ 0.55\mathrm{g} $  PVP  $ (\mathrm{Mw} = 1300000) $ ,  $ 0.3\mathrm{g} $   $ \mathrm{CuNO}_3 $ ,  $ 0.03\mathrm{g} $   $ \mathrm{AgNO}_3 $ , and  $ 1\mathrm{mL} $  Glacial acetic acid  $ (\geq 99\%) $  in  $ 5\mathrm{mL} $  ethanol  $ (\geq 99\%) $ . A PEDOT:PSS solution was prepared with  $ 5\mathrm{mL} $  PEDOT:PSS and  $ 0.25\mathrm{mL} $  isopropyl alcohol. To form the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  precursor solution,  $ 0.922\mathrm{g} $   $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and  $ 0.349\mathrm{g} $   $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  were dissolved in  $ 0.9\mathrm{mL} $   $ N,N $ -dimethylformamide and  $ 0.1\mathrm{mL} $  dimethyl sulfoxide. A PCBM solution was prepared by dissolving  $ 15\mathrm{mg} $  PCBM in  $ 1\mathrm{mL} $  chlorobenzene.

# 2.3 | Substrate production

The solar cells were prepared on conductive glass (NSG10) substrates doped with tin fluoride (FTO). Before depositing different layers, the glass was placed in soap water, acetone, and isopropanol for 15 minutes, and then ultraviolet ozone was used for 15 minutes to clean the glass. For the synthesis of the Ag/CuO Nfs, we transferred the Ag/CuO precursor solution to a 10 mL eletrospinning glass syringe. By controlling the spinning time, different coverages of the electrospun fibers could be obtained on the FTO substrates. In the process of electrospinning, the spinning speed was 0.6 mL h  $  ^{-1}  $  , the receiving distance was 14 cm, and the spinning voltage was 14 kV. Finally, the nanofibers were dried at 80°C for 1.5 hours and annealed at 450°C for 1 hour in a muffle furnace. Next, the PEDOT:PSS solution was coated at 5000 rpm for 30 seconds to form a thin layer.

# 2.4 | Preparation of perovskite solar cells

In order to form a perovskite film at room humidity (typically between  $ 35\% $  and  $ 55\% $ ), the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  precursor solution was spin-coated onto the substrate at 4000 rpm

# 2.5 | Characterization

The photocurrent density-voltage (J-V) curves of the perovskite solar cells were measured by a Keithley Model 2400-SCS Source Meter under AM 1.5 illumination. The device was irradiated with a xenon arc having a light intensity of less than  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $  AM  $ 1.5\mathrm{G} $  as a light source (CHF-XM 35 to  $ 500\mathrm{W} $ , Beijing Changtuo Technology Co., Ltd.). The crystal structure was investigated by X ray measurement (XRD, Thermo ARL-X'TRA, America) and  $ \mathrm{CuK}\alpha $  ( $ \lambda = 1.5418\AA $ ). Energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS, Ultra55, ZEISS, Germany) was used to analyze the element composition and content. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were performed with an electrochemical workstation (Im6ex/Zahner). The UV-Vis absorption was measured with a UV-Vis spectrophotometer (Mapada P4).

This result could also be confirmed by the diameter distribution of Ag/CuO Nfs. The statistical distribution of diameter is calculated by using the measurement software Image-Pro, as shown in Figure 3. It could be concluded that the fibers were Ag/CuO Nfs composed of Ag and CuO. Compared with the standard device, as the spinning time increased, the number of Ag/CuO Nfs increases, and the  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $ ,  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $ , FF, and PCE all increased first and then decreased. The relatively poorer solar cell performance with lower Ag/CuO Nfs may be due to the lower coverage and poorer hole

extraction ability. As the coverage of Ag/CuO Nfs increased, the average   $  J_{sc}  $   increased from 18.5 to 21.7 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and then declined to 17.4 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  . When t = 40 seconds, the PCE achieved an optimal average of 11.6%, which was an increase of more than 33% compared to the reference device. As the coverage of Ag/CuO Nfs further increased, the performance of the perovskite solar cell were lowered because of the increased series resistance and the decreased perovskite light absorption. The results show that the Ag/CuO Nfs had an optimal coverage and could feasibly improve the efficiency of PSCs as an anode buffer layer.

Finally, we investigated the stability of PEDOT:PSS and Ag/CuO Nfs/PEDOT:PSS devices. All the cells were tested unsealed in ambient air (45%-65% humidity, 25°C). As shown in Figure 9, devices prepared from Ag/CuO Nfs/PEDOT:PSS had a better stability than the PEDOT:PSS. The devices based on Ag/CuO Nfs/PEDOT:PSS retained over 65% of their initial PCE even after 1 month. Using pure PEDOT:PSS as a HTL had a negative impact on the long-term stability of the device.
