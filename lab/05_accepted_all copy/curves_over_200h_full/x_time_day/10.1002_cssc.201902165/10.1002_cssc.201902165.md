# Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) have attracted significant attention owing to the exceptional photophysical properties of perovskite materials, such as the adjustable optical band gap, $ ^{[1]} $  excellent absorption coefficient, $ ^{[2]} $  charge-carrier diffusion lengths, $ ^{[3,4]} $  the potential for low-cost solution processing $ ^{[5]} $  and power conversion efficiency (PCE), which has been improved from 3.8% $ ^{[6]} $  to over 25% $ ^{[7]} $  within 10 years.

Electron-transporting layers (ETLs) and hole-transporting layers (HTLs) are always required for the construction of high efficiency PVSCs.  $  ^{[8]}  $   ETLs of titanium dioxide (TiO  $  _{2}  $  ) are widely used in n-i-p PVSCs owing to the low-cost and high-performance.  $  ^{[9-12]}  $   Compared with mesoporous TiO  $  _{2}  $   films and compact TiO  $  _{2}  $   films, TiO  $  _{2}  $   nanoparticles (NPs) films have the advantages of adjustable band gap, low temperature annealing and no need for hydrolysis.  $  ^{[13-15]}  $   Surface engineering on TiO  $  _{2}  $   NPs ETL can further decrease the interface recombination and facilitate carrier transport.  $  ^{[16-19]}  $   Chemical modification on TiO  $  _{2}  $   NPs through chlorine-capping,  $  ^{[20]}  $   Er-doping,  $  ^{[21]}  $   and amino functionalization  $  ^{[14]}  $   can form a passivation interface between TiO  $  _{2}  $   NPs ETL and the perovskite, and the resulting n-i-p devices based on spiro-OMeTAD have PCEs of 20.1%, 14.06% and 21.33% by

For example, the use of an ETL prepared from TiO $ _2 $  NPs/[6,6]-phenyl-C71-butyric acid methyl ester (PC $ _{70} $ BM) in n-i-p PVSCs based on spiro-OMeTAD results in the simultaneous formation of a PC $ _{70} $ BM passivation layer, resulting in a maximum PCE of 17.2%. $ ^{[22]} $  Compared with chemical modification and physical doping, the use of interface modification layers (IMLs) provides more freedom in material choice and device optimization. An IML between a TiO $ _2 $  NPs ETL and a perovskite can act as a hole-blocking layer, passivation layer and surface smoothing layer to facilitate carrier transport and perovskite crystallinity. In this work, thin films of poly[[9,9-bis[3'-(N,N-dimethyl)propyl]-2,7-fluorene]-alt-2,7-(9,9-dioctylfluorene)] (PFN) and polystyrene (PS) were used as IMLs of TiO₂ ETL for the first time. # Effects of particle size and thickness of   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs ETL on device performance

The particle size not only affects the photoelectric properties of   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs, but also the surface morphology of   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs ETL.   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs with an average size of 6, 10, 20 and 40 nm (Figure 1) were obtained by changing the reaction time and dispersion conditions. The PVSCs with a structure of ITO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/perovskite/MEH-PPV/MoO  $  _{3}  $  /Ag were fabricated to study the size effect of   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs. A 50 nm thick   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs ETL was used in all devices. As shown in Figure 2a, device performances of PVSCs changed with increasing particle size of   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs from 6 to 40 nm. # Effects of IML on the PVSCs based on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs

Based on the work on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs ETL, further optimization was performed by inserting an IML between the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs ETL and the perovskite layer.  $  ^{[36]}  $   The device structure of the PVSCs (ITO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/IML/perovskite/MEH-PPV/  $  MoO_{3}/Ag  $  ) is shown in Figure 3a. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectrometry (UPS) spectra of ITO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs, ITO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PFN and ITO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PS are shown in Figure 3b. The deposition of   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs on the ITO substrate led to a shift in the photoemission onset toward a higher binding energy (17.50 eV), indicating the formation of an interfacial dipole shifting the vacuum level upward, and the resulting work function (WF) of ITO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs was 3.80 eV (WF can be determined by the difference between the photon energy (21.20 eV) and the binding energy). The photoemission onset of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs film further shifted toward a lower binding energy upon the deposition of the PFN and PS, increasing the WF to 4.10 and 4.40 eV, respectively. The band diagram in Figure 3c is shown to illustrate the function of insulating PS and semiconducting PFN as the IML of   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs ETL. The WFs of ITO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PFN and ITO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PS aligned well with conduction band of the perovskite, which in principal can promote electron extraction and transport.  $  ^{[37]}  $   In addition, a thin insulating layer of PS allows the transport of photogenerated electrons from the perovskite to the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs by tunneling, and also blocks the photogenerated holes from the perovskite to the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs, which reduces their recombination at the contact between the perovskite and ETL.  $  ^{[27]}  $

A summary of three performance parameters from 20 separate devices is displayed in Figure S4. In general, an improvement in  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $ ,  $  J_{\text{SC}}  $  and FF was observed for devices containing PFN IMLs and the more distinct enhancement was observed for devices containing PS IMLs. The  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $  of a PVSC is determined by multiple factors, including surface defect/trap of selective contact, microstructure and defect states in the perovskite, mobility, affinity and the space charge of charge transport layers, energy levels of the selective contact and surface disorder of surfacing materials.[38] The improved  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $  and FF of the PVSCs based on TiO $ _2 $  NPs/PFN and TiO $ _2 $  NPs/PS was attributed to the optimized interface contact at the ITO/ETL interface and ETL/perovskite interface after the IML insertion as well as reduced nonradiative recombination at the interface, which leads to more efficient charge transfer.[39] The improvement of the  $  J_{\text{SC}}  $  of TiO $ _2 $  NPs/PFN and TiO $ _2 $  NPs/PS devices was attributed to the decreased recombination of holes and electrons and the optimized perovskite layer. The highest FF among three kinds of devices demonstrated the perfect interface modification effect of PS on TiO $ _2 $  NPs. As an insulating polymer, PS can be a superior surface modifier to the famous surface modifier PFN, which brings many advantages such as low cost and high stability.  $  J-V  $  curves of the devices in the dark state are shown in Figure 3e. TiO $ _2 $  NPs/PS-based devices had the lowest dark current, which indicated that the insulating PS effectively reduced the trap states density and blocked hole injection, resulting in improved FF and  $  J_{\text{SC}}  $ . Three champion devices presented broader external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra from 300 to 800 nm (Figure 3f). There was a clear increase in photoresponse of the TiO $ _2 $  NPs/PS-based device in the whole spectrum range. The integrated photocurrents of the PVSCs based on TiO $ _2 $  NPs, TiO $ _2 $  NPs/PFN and TiO $ _2 $  NPs/PS were 19.44, 19.78 and 20.28 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , respectively, which were consistent with the measured  $  J_{\text{SC}}  $  of 19.92, 21.37 and 21.50 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  under simulated solar light. Hysteresis is a common problem in perovskite solar cells. The origin of hysteresis is attributed to charge-trapping, ion migration, ferroelectric effects, dynamic trapping and detrapping processes.[40-42] In Figure S5, clear  $  J-V  $  hysteresis was observed in all devices. There was a trend of gradual decrease in  $  J-V  $  hysteresis for the devices based on TiO $ _2 $  NPs, TiO $ _2 $  NPs/PFN and TiO $ _2 $  NPs/PS, which was attributed to the optimized interface contact of ETL/IML/perovskite.

XRD patterns of   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs (Figure S6a) include the characteristic peaks of anatase type   $  TiO_{2}  $   at 30, 35, 50, 60.  $  ^{[43]}  $   For the functional layers prepared from solution layer by layer, the crystalline phase of the perovskite film is affected by the under layer. Similar XRD profiles containing the diffractions of (101), (012), (021), (202), (211), (122), (024) and (131) planes of the perovskite lattice were observed for the perovskite films on   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/IML and pure   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs (Figure S6b), which confirmed the formation of the black phase of the perovskite.  $  ^{[44-46]}  $   There was a gradual increasing tendency of the XRD intensities of the lattice planes (202), (211) and (024) for the perovskite formed on   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs,   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PFN and   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PS, indicating

that the use of PFN and PS IMLs benefits the crystallization of the perovskite.

The morphologies of the perovskite films formed on   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs with and without an IML were studied by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and atomic force microscope (AFM). Clear grain boundaries with a grain size from 50 to 400 nm were evident in the SEM image of the perovskite formed on pure   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs (Figure 4a). Clear holes were observed between the perovskite grains, which are potential carrier traps during the photovoltaic process. In contrast, larger crystalline domains of perovskite were observed after the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs were modified by PFN or PS (Figure 4b,c); less boundaries and traps can contribute to promoting charge transport and reducing the carrier recombination. Moreover, compared with the perovskite prepared on   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PFN, larger grains, less boundaries and traps were observed in those prepared on   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PS, which agreed with the best device performance of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PS-based devices. Similar features were observed in the corresponding AFM images (Figure 4d–f). The root-mean-square (RMS) roughness of the perovskite film formed on   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs,   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PFN and   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PS was 10.50, 9.12, and 8.06 nm, respectively.

The water contact angles of   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs,   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PFN, and   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PS before and after   $  O_{2}  $   plasma were obtained. As shown in Figure S7,   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PFN and especially   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PS exhibited a much lower surface energy than   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs under the same conditions. The increased grain size of the perovskite crystal on   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PFN and   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs/PS thin film might be related to the lower number of nuclei generated on the polymer-modified   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs with a low surface energy.  $  ^{[47]}  $

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were employed to evaluate the internal electrical properties of the PVSCs. The Nyquist plots of the PVSCs were measured under dark conditions (Figure 5a), and well fitted by using an equivalent circuit (inset). The circuit includes a series resistance  $ (R_{\mathrm{s}}) $ , a contact resistance  $ (R_{\mathrm{co}}) $  and a recombination resistance  $ (R_{\mathrm{rec}}) $ .[48] In the models, the  $ R_{s} $  corresponds to the resistance from wiring, electron extraction and transport at high frequency and it strongly depends on the properties of the ETLs. In the high frequency range,  $ R_{\mathrm{co}} $  reflects the charge transport properties and  $ R_{\mathrm{rec}} $  represents the recombination resistance at low frequency.[5,49] Extracted after fitting of the EIS spectra, the  $ R_{\mathrm{rec}} $  of the devices based on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs/ PFN and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs/PS was 64.6, 70.7 and  $ 80.2\mathrm{k}\Omega \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $ , respectively. The  $ R_{\mathrm{co}} $  of the devices based on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs/PFN and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs/PS was 2.8, 2.1 and  $ 1.8\mathrm{k}\Omega \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $ , respectively. The higher  $ R_{\mathrm{rec}} $  and lower  $ R_{\mathrm{co}} $  of the devices with PFN and PS demonstrated that the IML enhances the electron transport and inhibits the electron-hole recombination at the ETL/perovskite interface. The distribution of FF in Figure S4c was consistent with the  $ R_{\mathrm{rec}} $  and  $ R_{\mathrm{co}} $  of the different devices. The downward trend of the recombination rate for the devices based on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs/PFN and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs/PS implied less loss of  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ [39,50] which could have contributed to the higher  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of the latter two devices.

The reproducibility of the PVSCs was checked by using 20 separate devices (Figure 6a). Most of the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs/PFN-based devices gave PCE values above  $ 13\% $ . The  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs/PS-based devices gave PCE values of approximately  $ 15\% $ . The high reproducibility of the devices based on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs/PS was attributed to the high molecular weights of MEH-PPV and PS, which resulted in high quality and controllable films. Good ambient stability is a key performance index for a solar cell, so we performed a device stability experiment by storing the encapsulated PVSCs in ambient conditions  $ (25^{\circ}\mathrm{C}) $  with the humidity at approximately  $ 30\% $ . As shown in Figure 6b, the device without IML showed a clear decline within 5 days and the PCE was only approximately  $ 40\% $  of its original value after 17 days. # Conclusions

PFN and PS are effective surface modifiers for  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs ETLs by suppressing carrier recombination at the interface and improving crystalline quality of the perovskite. Fortunately, the cheap plastic PS demonstrated superior properties of hole-blocking, surface smoothing and passivation, which were attributed to the high-quality film and its insulating characteristics.  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs-based PVSCs containing a PS IML and MEH-PPV HTL displayed a promising PCE of  $ 15.09\% $  with a  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of  $ 1.05\mathrm{V} $ , a  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  of  $ 21.50\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , and a FF of  $ 66.76\% $ . A PCE of  $ 17.13\% $  with an ultrahigh  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of  $ 1.18\mathrm{V} $  was attained with the device containing  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  NPs, PS, and spiro-OMeTAD. Moreover, the stabilities of the devices and the perovskite can be enhanced by introducing nondoped polymeric functional layers. This work provides valuable information about interface engineering of ETLs, as well as the use of cheap and stable polymers in high efficiency PVSCs.

# Synthesis of  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  nanoparticles

The synthesis of   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs was based on a modification of previously reported procedures.  $  ^{[43,53,54]}  $   First, anhydrous titanium tetrachloride (2 mL) was added into a beaker containing anhydrous ethanol (8 mL) cooled in an ice water bath and equipped with a magnetic stirrer. The mixed solution was then transferred into a flask containing anhydrous benzyl alcohol (40 mL). The solution was held at 85 °C under stirring until its color changed from yellow to translucent (small particle size) or milky (big particle size). Then the solution was subsequently washed by adding anhydrous ethanol and diethyl ether followed by centrifugation (5000 rpm for 5 min) three times. The dry purified   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanocrystals were dispersed in anhydrous ethanol at a concentration of approximately 5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   by the addition of titanium(diisopropoxide) bis(2,4-pentanedionate) (500 μL mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ); the obtained solution was transparent and had a slight yellow color.
