# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic halide Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been considered as the most fascinating solar cell field, because of their exciting photovoltaic performance and facile device fabrication process   $  [1-6]  $  . The PSCs have an incredible enhancement in the Power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.5% (certified) over the past few years owing to their preferable and outstanding optoelectronic properties of perovskite   $  [7]  $  . However, it is still a great challenge to obtain long-term stable and highly efficient PSCs, which is a major bottleneck for commercialization   $  [8,9]  $  . Firstly, perovskite materials are highly sensitive, which easily degrade under exposure to oxygen, moisture and Ultraviolet (UV) light   $  [10-16]  $  . Additionally, the perovskite films are prepared by solvent processing method, which prone to form defects on the film surface and at the grain boundaries. These defects further accelerate the degradation of perovskite and serve as non-radiative charge recombination centers to yield a seriously negative impact on the device performance. Therefore,

Perovskite films are apt to format defects on surface and in grain boundaries, which results in inferior photovoltaic performance. Moreover, perovskite materials are sensitive to Ultraviolet (UV) light and are easily damaged under UV light illumination, accelerating the degradation of Perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Therefore, it is significant to design and develop effective defect passivation and UV inhibitors for efficient and stable PSCs. Herein, a small molecular material 2,5-bis(perfluorophenyl)thiazolo[5,4-d]thiazole  $ (\mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz}) $  was designed and introduced into the perovskite precursor solution. The research manifested that  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  is a bifunctional additive, acting as both defect passivation and Fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) agents. Through FRET action, the spectrum response in the short wavelength region and the device stability under UV light illumination were remarkably enhanced. Moreover, the  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $ -treatment can significantly decrease the trap state density of perovskite film. Correspondingly, the  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $ -treated PSC device exhibited an impressive Power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 21.38\% $ , which is much higher than the control device  $ (19.27\%) $ . Meanwhile, the unencapsulated  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $ -treated PSC device exhibits long-term stability, maintaining their initial PCE of  $ 90.4\% $  up to 30 days under relative humidity of  $ 45 - 55\% $ .

developing effective strategies for achieving high performance and stable PSCs is highly appreciated.

Fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) strategy is proved to be an efficient way to simultaneously improve efficiency and stability   $  [17–20]  $  . The researchers found by using a down-conversion material to absorb the UV light and then generate the visible light that can subsequently utilized by perovskite, the Incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) in UV region could be greatly enhanced, leading to higher short-current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ). At the same time, due to the UV filtering action of the down-conversion material, the UV stability of PSC can also be improved. However, the fluorescence spectrum of the ever reported down-conversion materials is narrow, limiting the enhancement of PCE.

solution engineering is considered one of the most effective techniques, because it is still the simplest, and low-cost [23,24]. The introduction of additives is a typical approach for perovskite precursor solution engineering. Usually, these additives interactions with the perovskite component, which slows down the crystallization rate and reduces the formation of defects. Till this day, a various kind of additives has been utilized, such as inorganic salts [25,26], ammonium salts [27], organic small molecules[28], fullerene derivatives[29], perylene derivatives [30], polymers [31], ionic liquids [32,33] and graphene derivatives [34]. Among these, organic small molecules are gained much attention because of their definite molecular weight, good solubility, and tunable molecular structures. Liu et al. employed 3-(Decyldimethylammonio)-propane-sulfonate inner salt (DPSI) as an additive in perovskite precursor solution to interact with lead, which resulted in improved crystallinity of perovskite and trap density reduced 23 folds compared with the control device [23]. Wang et al. utilized 2-hydroxy-4-methoxybenzophenone as a sunscreen molecule to regulate the defect passivation and protect the PSC device from UV light [35]. With this sunscreen molecule, the PSC device exhibits an improved PCE of  $ 23.09\% $  accompanied with excellent UV stability. Moreover, in recent days, fluorinated organic materials, which are commonly hydrophobic, are widely utilized to passivate perovskite surfaces to enhance the device performance and stability, due to the strong hydrogen bond or ionic bond interaction of fluorinated atoms with hydrogen or lead [27,36,37].

Based on this concern, we developed a novel bifunctional organic material 2,5-bis(Perfluorophenyl)thiazolo[5,4-d]thiazole (termed  $ \mathrm{PFP_2TTz} $ ) and successfully utilized it as an additive in perovskite precursor solution. The research results demonstrated that the  $ \mathrm{PFP_2TTz} $  containing fluorine and nitrogen groups which easily interact with  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2+}} $ , assists the formation of better crystallized perovskite film with suppressed trap density. More importantly, the fluorescence spectrum of  $ \mathrm{PFP_2TTz} $  is wide and drastically overlaps with the perovskite absorption spectrum, resulting in the efficient FRET between  $ \mathrm{PFP_2TTz} $  and perovskite. Furthermore, the  $ \mathrm{PFP_2TTz} $  treatment also significantly increases the hydrophobicity of perovskite. With the optimization, the  $ \mathrm{PFP_2TTz} $  treated PSC obtained a higher PCE of  $ 21.38\% $  with negligible hysteresis, compared to the control device  $ (19.27\%) $ . The long-term device stability, especially under UV light illumination is significantly boosted after

PFP  $  _{2}  $  TTz treatment, retaining its original PCE 90.4% up to 30 days under relative humidity of 45–55%.

The detailed molecular structure of  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  and device structure is shown in Fig. 1a and 1b, respectively. It consists of a thiazolo[5,4-d] thiazole core with pentafluorobenzene end-capping units. The synthetic route of  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  was depicted in Scheme S1 and experimental details are discussed in supporting information (SI). The  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  have multiple fluorine units and nitrogen atoms, which can strongly interact with  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $ , in turn, influence the crystallization of perovskite. Therefore, we firstly tested the X-ray diffraction (XRD) of perovskite film with and without  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  as additive in precursor solution. As shown in Fig. 1c, after  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  treatment, the full width at half-maximum (FWHM) of the (110) peak decreases from 0.204 to 0.199 without any shift, and the intensity ratio of the (110) plane to the (220) plane increases from 1.529 to 1.576, demonstrating the  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  treatment effectively enhances crystallinity of perovskite without entering perovskite lattice. To further confirm the interaction of  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  with perovskite, we tested the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) of perovskite film and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  film with and without  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  additive. The relevant data were shown Fig. 1d-f and Fig. S1. For the control perovskite film, the binding energy peaks of  $ \mathrm{Pb}4\mathrm{f}_{5/2} $  and  $ \mathrm{Pb}4\mathrm{f}_{7/2} $  is measured to be  $ 138.51\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ 143.31\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. With the  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  treatment, the binding energy peaks of  $ \mathrm{Pb}4\mathrm{f} $  shifted towards low binding energy states (138.38 eV and  $ 143.18\mathrm{eV} $ ), demonstrating a strong electrostatic interaction between  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  and  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $ . To further elucidate the detailed actions, as shown in Fig. 1e-f and S1b, the XPS spectra of N 1s, F 1s and S 2p were further analyzed. For the N 1s spectra, to avoid the impact from  $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $  and  $ \mathrm{FA}^+ $ , the XPS measurement was conducted with  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  and  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} + \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  samples. Due to electron donating nature of N atom (see Fig. 1a), the  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} + \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  sample exhibited lower binding energies of  $ 399.64\mathrm{eV} $  than neat  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  (400.52 eV), revealing that the N atom interact with  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  through the Lewis base-acid action. For the F 1s spectra, the characteristic binding energy peak shifted towards high binding energy to  $ 687.74\mathrm{eV} $ , with decreased peak intensity after interacting with perovskite. Different from the N atom, the F atom is easily to withdraw electrons, so it showed opposite shift compare with N atom. The effects of  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  treatment on surface morphology and roughness was investigated using scanning electron microscope (SEM) and atomic force microscope (AFM) studies. As shown in Fig. 2, the  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  treated perovskite film shows a smoother surface, more homogeneous morphology than pristine perovskite. With the  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  treatment, the surface roughness decreases from  $ 42.2\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 34.1\mathrm{nm} $ . Moreover, the  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $ -treated perovskite exhibited larger average grain size of  $ 394\mathrm{nm} $  than pristine perovskite  $ (194\mathrm{nm}) $ . The distribution of  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  on perovskite was further elucidated through energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) analysis. As shown in Fig. 2b and S3, for  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  treated perovskite, the  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  well distributed over the perovskite in the grain boundaries, detecting as flocculent thin film

at the edges of the perovskite grains.

To further understand the surface passivation effect of  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  on perovskite, we measured the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra of perovskite film with and without  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  treatment. The  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $ -treated perovskite film showed higher PL intensity with an obvious blue shift than the control sample, which is caused by the decrease of spontaneous non-radiative recombination from the trap states [40]. As a result, the hysteresis, which is commonly observed in  $ J-V $  curves of reported PSC, could be suppressed [41,42]. Fig. 3b shows the time-resolved PL decay curves for the control and  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $ -treated perovskite samples. The curves were fit using a biexponential decay function, yielding a fast decay ( $ \tau_1 $ ) and a slow decay ( $ \tau_2 $ ) component. Compared with the control device, all photovoltaic parameters, especially the   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF, have been greatly enhanced with the addition of   $  PFP_{2}TTz  $   in perovskite precursor solution. The hike of   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF can mainly ascribe to the restricted charge recombination during the working process, which results from the improvements of perovskite film quality as discussed above. The built-in potential (  $  V_{bi}  $  ), Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) and light intensity dependence measurements further confirmed this deduction. As shown in Fig. 3d, the   $  V_{bi}  $   value is improved from 0.92 to 0.98 V with   $  PFP_{2}TTz  $   treatment. The increased   $  V_{bi}  $   is beneficial to enhance charge separation, extraction and transport properties, in turn, minimize the voltage loss and render higher   $  V_{oc}  $   [44,45]. The   $  PFP_{2}TTz  $ -treated PSC displayed a higher recombination resistance of 574 Ω than the control device (86 Ω, see Fig. 4d), which implies reduced charge recombination at the perovskite/HTL interface. From the light intensity dependence testing results (see Fig. 4e-f), we observed that the   $  PFP_{2}TTz  $ -treated device shows a higher slope value α of 1.01 than the control device (α = 0.94), denoting the   $  PFP_{2}TTz  $   treated device efficiently enhanced charge extraction properties. Moreover, the   $  PFP_{2}TTz  $   treated device shows a smaller slope of 1.71 (kT/q) than that of the control device slope 1.95 (kT/q). The smaller slope value of   $  PFP_{2}TTz  $   treated device was attributed to the lower trap state at the film, and demonstrated the non-radiative recombination was suppressed.

The slight increase in   $  J_{sc}  $   may induce from the enhanced response to solar spectrum in 300–500 region. The first reason for the enhancement is the improved absorption of perovskite in 300–500 nm region due to the passivation of crystal defects (see Fig. S5b) [46]. The other reason can be attributed to the FRET between PFP₂TTz and perovskite. The UV–vis absorption of perovskite and the emission spectrum of PFP₂TTz are displayed in Fig. S5a. PFP₂TTz exhibited a strong emission band at 300–780 nm on being excited, which substantially overlaps with the entire absorption spectrum of perovskite. The overlap of absorption of perovskite and the emission spectra of PFP₂TTz make the FRET between PFP₂TTz and perovskite possible. The PFP₂TTz molecule absorbs light at 300–500 nm and simultaneously generate fluorescence overlapping with the absorption spectrum of the perovskite. The subsequent absorption of the fluorescence by perovskite successfully enhanced the response of PSC in 300–500 nm wavelength region, correspondingly, resulting in higher   $  J_{sc}  $  .

Apart from high efficiency, long-term device stability is one of the crucial factors for further commercialization. The defect passivation is manifested to be beneficial for device long-term stability, and the down conversion through FRET is reported to be helpful for enhancement of UV-stability. To better evaluate the PFP  $  _{2}  $  TTz treatment on device stability, we tested the unencapsulated device with and without PFP  $  _{2}  $  TTz as additive under a relative humidity of 45–55% over 30 days. The normalized PCE of the device as a function of days is shown in Fig. 5. In dark condition, it's observed that the PFP  $  _{2}  $  TTz-treated device shows

better stability, maintaining 90% of its original PCE after 30 days, while the control device kept only 51% of the original performance. The improved stability in dark condition mainly results from the well restricted defects as discussed above, and the ascended hydrophobicity as shown in Fig. S6. The pristine perovskite shows a water contact angle of   $  45.8^{\circ}  $  , whereas the   $  PFP_{2}TTz  $  -treated perovskite shows a higher water contact angle of   $  58.3^{\circ}  $  . To gain insights into the influence of   $  PFP_{2}TTz  $   treatment on UV-stability of PSC, the unencapsulated PSC device was measured under 365 nm UV light irradiation from both the glass and the counter electrode side in humidity of 45–55%. It is well-known the perovskite materials are highly sensitive, which can easily decompose under UV light, especially with the appearance of   $  TiO_{2}  $   as electron transport material [16,47]. Consequently, the control device showed quickly attenuation in PCE. While, for the   $  PFP_{2}TTz  $   treated devices, benefiting of   $  PFP_{2}TTz  $   molecules convert UV to visible light through FRET action, the UV-stability was improved and maintained its initial PCE of 87% and 79% for the glass and device side, respectively.

# 2. Conclusions

In summary, we introduced the bifunctional additive  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  to the perovskite precursor, which achieved passivation defects and FRET action. The trap state density of  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $ -treated films decreased by 2.1 folds compared to pristine perovskite. Meanwhile, the  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $  also effectively filters UV light and enhances the utilization of short wavelengths light. Consequently, the optimized  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $ -treated device exhibited an impressive PCE of  $ 21.38\% $  which is much higher than control device  $ (19.27\%) $ . Benefits of this FRET action, the  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $ -treated molecule significantly enhanced UV stability. The unencapsulated  $ \mathrm{PFP}_2\mathrm{TTz} $ -treated device exhibits excellent stability and maintains  $ 90.4\% $  of its initial PCE after aging for 30 days. This work guides the design and development of multifunctional additive molecules for the sake of simultaneous enhancement of efficiency and stability of PSCs.
