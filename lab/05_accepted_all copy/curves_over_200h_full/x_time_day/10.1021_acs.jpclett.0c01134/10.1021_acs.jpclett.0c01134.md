# ABSTRACT

The phase instability of cesium lead halide perovskite is still a substantial challenge hindering its application. A 2D-3D all-inorganic  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PbI_2Cl_2 - CsPbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}} $  perovskite solar cell was successfully developed to address this issue. The 2D  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PbI_2Cl_2} $  phase distributed among the grain boundaries of the 3D  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Br}_x $  grains. The existence of  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PbI_2Cl_2} $  effectively facilitated the (100) preferential crystal orientation of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_{2.5}\mathrm{Br}_{0.5} $  crystals, promoting the carrier transport. The smooth transition region between the  $ (003)_{2\mathrm{D}} / / (001)_{3\mathrm{D}} $  interface indicated the formation of a 2D-3D heterostructure. Due to the improved crystal quality, high uniformity and repeatability, the efficiency of the solar cells with areas of  $ 0.09~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ ,  $ 1~\mathrm{cm}^2 $  and  $ 2~\mathrm{cm}^2 $  significantly improved to  $ 15.09\% $ ,  $ 12.74\% $ , and  $ 10.01\% $ , respectively. The PCE retained  $ 95.3\% $  of the initial efficiency after 60 days in a nitrogen atmosphere at room temperature and  $ 80\% $  of the initial efficiency at humidity of  $ 70\pm 10\% $  RH under continuous heating at  $ 80^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 12\mathrm{h} $ .

Keywords:  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PbI_2Cl_2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_{3 - x}Br_x} $ , stability, all-inorganic perovskite solar cells, heterojunction

Halide perovskites have emerged as one of the most promising next-generation photovoltaic materials because of their remarkable photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{1-2}  $   Numerous studies have focused on improving the performance of the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites (MAPbX  $  _{3}  $   or FAPbX  $  _{3}  $  ).  $  ^{3-4}  $   To date, the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has reached 25.2%.  $  ^{5}  $   Despite the great achievements, the thin perovskite film is very susceptible to degradation upon exposure to moisture, heat, oxygen and light.  $  ^{6-7}  $   Two-dimensional (2D) perovskite was regarded as one of the effective potential alternatives to further solve the stability issue. The so-called Ruddlesden-Popper 2D perovskite, in which a larger hydrophobic cation, such as phenyl ethylammonium (PEA)  $  ^{8}  $  , n-butylamine (BA)  $  ^{9}  $   or 2,2-(ethylenedioxy)bis(ethylammonium) (EDBE)  $  ^{10}  $  , was used as a spacer for isolating the 3D perovskite, can inhibit ion migration and improve overall stability.

Although many strategies have been reported to partly effective in increasing the stability of the hybrid perovskite materials exposed to moisture, the intrinsic thermal instability arising from the volatility of the organic components is unavoidable.  $  ^{11-12}  $   The replacement of organic cations with inorganic Cs cations was regarded as a potential solution to this issue.  $  ^{13-14}  $   Cesium lead halide perovskites,   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   (X = I, Br or Cl), have attracted considerable attention due to their suitable bandgap, excellent charge-carrier mobilities and high thermal stabilities, even at temperatures greater than 400 °C.  $  ^{15-18}  $   In the past few years, many researchers have attempted to improve the performance of the all-inorganic perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{19-20}  $   Specifically, Zhao et al.  $  ^{19}  $   employed a surface treatment method with choline iodide to eliminate the pinholes and

XRD patterns revealed the coexistence of the 2D  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PbI_2Cl_2} $  and 3D  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}} $ . Based on the EBSD results, the 2D  $ \mathrm{Cs_2PbI_2Cl_2} $  phase distributed among the grain boundaries of the cubic 3D  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}} $  grains. The smooth transition region between 2D and 3D crystals indicated the formation of a 2D-3D heterostructure between the  $ (003)_{2\mathrm{D}} / / (002)_{3\mathrm{D}} $  interface. The defects and non-radiative recombination have been strongly suppressed through the perfect lattice match and clean heterogeneous interfaces between the 2D and 3D crystal grains. The changes in the morphology and structure of the films as a function of CsCl concentration were monitored to further investigate the role of   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   in the   $  CsPbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}-Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   films. As depicted the SEM images shown in Figure 3a, the film showed an improved morphology and increased coverage when 0.1 M CsCl was added compared with the 3D control sample (Figure 1c). As the CsCl concentration increased to 0.15 M, the film was completely covered. In particular, the second phase (  $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $  ) began to appear around the grain boundaries. When the CsCl concentration further increased to 0.2 M, the second phase started to appear inside the grains, but was concentrated at the grain boundaries. Based on the XRD results (Figure 3b), the intensity of the (100) and (200) peaks assigned to cubic   $  CsPbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}  $   increased significantly as the CsCl concentration increased. As shown in Figure S4a, a further increase in the CsCl concentration to 0.3 M may reduce the quality of the   $  CsPbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}  $   crystal. The higher magnification images of the XRD patterns shown in Figure S4c (Figure S4d) confirm the existence of   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $  . The preferred orientation of the   $  CsPbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}  $   crystal (Figure 1h) and the increased intensities of the characteristic   $  CsPbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}  $   diffraction peaks collectively confirm the significantly improved quality of   $  CsPbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}  $   crystals in the presence of   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $  . Therefore,   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   plays a crucial role in regulating the growth of the 3D   $  CsPbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}  $   perovskite, which is attributed to the good lattice matching between the   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   and   $  CsPbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}  $   crystals. In addition, the construction of the 2D-3D heterojunction within the film also plays a key role in improving the stability of the solar cells. As illustrated in Figure 4g, the PCE of the 2D-3D PSC retained 95.3% of the initial efficiency after 60 days of incubation in a nitrogen atmosphere at room temperature. However, the 3D PSC only retained 30% of the initial PCE within 30 days. Moreover, the construction of the 2D-3D heterojunction significantly improved the thermal and moisture stability of the PSC, as illustrated in Figure 4h and Figure 4i.
