# Introduction

Halide perovskites with a hybrid organic-inorganic stoichiometry have emerged as one of the most promising next-generation photovoltaic materials. Although the power conversion efficiency of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has exceeded 23%,  $  ^{1}  $  the expensive metal electrode (Au or Ag) and the ambient-unstable hole transport layers are unsuitable for the low-cost and large-scale production of PSCs and thus may obstruct their commercialization.  $  ^{2,3}  $  Various technological approaches have been proposed to overcome the instability problem, among which carbon based PSCs may be one of the most promising attempts to improve the stability because of their stable carbon electrode and the removal of hole transport layer.  $  ^{4,5}  $  The carbon based PSC devices using titanium dioxide (TiO  $  _{2}  $  ) as the electron transport layer (ETL) commonly require an exceedingly high-temperature sintering process (about 500 °C). These cells also suffer from conspicuous decline of performance under light illumination, aside from the notorious current-voltage hysteresis.  $  ^{4-21}  $  Although TiO  $  _{2}  $  has good electron selective properties, its sensitivity to the surface adsorption of oxygen

# Solution-Processed Electron Transport Layer of n-Doped Fullerene for Efficient and Stable All Carbon Based Perovskite Solar Cells

Junshuai Zhou, $ ^{a} $  Jie Hou, $ ^{a} $  Xia Tao, $ ^{a} $  Xiangyue Meng $ ^{*ac} $  and Shihe Yang $ ^{*bc} $

Carbon-based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) without hole transport layer (HTL) have shown great prospect for practical applications because of their high stability and low cost. However, almost all of the carbon based PSCs reported so far are fabricated using titanium dioxide   $  \left(\mathrm{TiO}_{2}\right)  $   as the electron transport layer (ETL) that requires a rather high-temperature sintering process (500 °C). Moreover, they also suffer from obvious voltage-current hysteresis and conspicuous performance decline under operating conditions. Herein, we report a new type of low-temperature all carbon based PSCs using solution-processed hexamethonium bromide (HMB)-doped   $  C_{60}  $   as ETL, which achieved a high power conversion efficiency (PCE) of over 16% without the above-mentioned hysteresis. It is shown that doping of   $  C_{60}  $   with HMB carries several outstanding advantages, including faster charge transport, enhanced electron mobility, and the decreased electron trap density. More encouraging is the remarkable stability of the PSCs fabricated with HMB-doped   $  C_{60}  $   ETL, which can maintain nearly 90% of their initial performance after continuous operation for 338 hours at their maximum power point under one sun illumination in ambient air.

Organic electron transport layers, such as fullerenes, have been verified to achieve effective electron extraction when interfaced with the perovskite layers,  $  ^{22-26}  $   and they tend to reduce UV-induced degradation of the solar cell compared with the devices using inorganic   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL.  $  ^{22}  $   The Buckminsterfullerene   $  C_{60}  $   exhibits an electron mobility of 1.6 cm  $  ^{2}  $   V s  $  ^{-1}  $  , which is much higher than that of PCBM (6.1 × 10  $  ^{-2}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V s  $  ^{-1}  $  ).  $  ^{27}  $   Vacuum-deposited   $  C_{60}  $   electron transport layers have been successfully used to replace   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL,  $  ^{23-26}  $   but the method of vacuum-deposition needs expensive equipment and great energy consumption. Solution-preparation of   $  C_{60}  $   ETL may be a desirable process for fabricating PSCs. But solution-processed   $  C_{60}  $   ETL requires to be made into a film thick enough to ensure complete coverage, which however may hinder electron transport. To improve the electron mobility and electrical conductivity of fullerenes, one of the most efficient ways is to utilize n-type doping technology by increasing the free electron concentration through efficient electron transfer from the n-type dopant to the host material.  $  ^{28-31}  $

Herein, we demonstrate an n-doped ETL by doping  $  C_{60}  $  with hexamethonium bromide (HMB) to fabricate efficient and stable all carbon based perovskite solar cells with the structure of ITO/ $  C_{60}  $ / $  CH_3NH_3PbI_3  $ /Carbon. We achieved a power conversion efficiency of 16.03%, much higher than that of the PSC with non-doped  $  C_{60}  $  ETL (12.22%). Moreover, the PSCs-based on HMB-doped  $  C_{60}  $  maintained 81.9% of their initial PCE after storage in ambient condition at room temperature over 60 days and 90% of their initial performance after continuous operation for 338 hours in air directly determined from the maximum power point (MPP) tracking. # Results and Discussion

The wetting capability of the perovskite precursor solutions on the fullerene films plays an important role in shaping the morphology of the perovskite film. The   $  C_{60}  $   and PCBM we used are both commercially available fullerene materials. By comparing the contacting angles of dimethylformamide (DMF) on the   $  C_{60}  $   and PCBM films (Fig. 1a,b), we found that the contact angles of DMF on the   $  C_{60}  $   film is 11.8°, which is obviously lower than that of PCBM (43.9°), indicating the better spreadability of the perovskite precursor solution on the   $  C_{60}  $   film. The smaller contacting angle on the   $  C_{60}  $   film is propitious to form more smooth and compact perovskite layer, resulting in higher photovoltaic performance. Therefore,   $  C_{60}  $   was chosen as the ETL for PSCs, other than PCBM.

To improve the electron mobility and electrical conductivity, the   $  C_{60}  $   film was doped by HMB. Electron transfer between HMB and adjacent fullerene core during the film evolution induces in situdoping of the fullerene   $  C_{60}  $   thin-film (Fig. 1c). The incorporation of bromide anions into fullerene can induce effective n-doping via anion-induced electron transfer and thereby substantially increase film conductivity.  $  ^{32}  $   Electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectra was performed to investigate the doping effect as shown in Fig. 1d. Furthermore, we investigated the photo- and humidity stability of the PSC devices based on HMB-doped   $  C_{60}  $   ETLs without encapsulation. The long-term stability of PSCs is closely

related to the ETL/perovskite interface binding strength and interfacial charge accumulation during operation.  $  ^{40}  $   The PSC devices based on HMB-doped   $  C_{60}  $   maintained 81.9% of their initial PCE after storage in ambient condition (RH 20%-30%) at room temperature for over 60 days, whereas those with   $  C_{60}  $   retained 78.7% of their initial efficiency (Fig. 6a). # Conclusions

In conclusion, we reported a new type of low-temperature all carbon based perovskite solar cells using solution processed HMB-doped  $  C_{60}  $  as ETL with the device structure of ITO/HMB-doped  $  C_{60} / \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 / \mathrm{Carbon}  $ . Such solar cells have achieved power conversion efficiencies of over  $  16\%  $  without hysteresis, which compare favorably with that of the PSC based on solution processed  $  C_{60}  $  without doping (12.22% with hysteresis). The excellent device performance is attributed to the outstanding advantage of HMB-doped  $  C_{60}  $ , including a smoother surface, faster electron transport, lower electron recombination, enhanced electron mobility, and the decreased electron trap-state density of the perovskite layer. Moreover, the devices exhibit good humidity stability and photo-stability, which maintained  $  81.9\%  $  of their initial PCE after storage in ambient condition at room temperature over 60 days and  $  90\%  $  of their initial performance after continuous operation for 338 hours in air directly determined from the MPP tracking. Finally, the large-area PSC device ( $  4~\mathrm{cm}^2  $ ) fabricated by employing HMB-doped  $  C_{60}  $  as ETL also achieved a considerable high PCE of over  $  10\%  $ , again the highest value for this kind of PSCs. This study provides a feasible route for the fabrication of highly efficient and stable all carbon based perovskite solar cells at low temperature.

Dichlorobenzene was spin-cast at 3000 rpm for 30 s on the LTQ substrate and dried at 100°C for 10 min. The HMB-doped C $  _{60}  $  film was also tested by mixed 2.5%, 5%, 10.0% molar ratio HMB with the C $  _{60}  $  solution (15mg ml $  ^{-1}  $ ) in the presence of 1,2-Dichlorobenzene and 2-Chlorophenol mixed solvent (volume ratio 3:1). The perovskite(CH $  _{3}  $ NH $  _{3}  $ PbI $  _{3}  $ ) active layer was fabricated on the C $  _{60}  $  or HMB-doped C $  _{60}  $  films by using Lewis base adduct method. $ ^{34} $  To prepare a CH $  _{3}  $ NH $  _{3}  $ •PbI $  _{2}  $ •DMSO (molar ratio 1:1:1) adduct solution, 159 mg of CH $  _{3}  $ NH $  _{3}  $ l, 461 mg of PbI $  _{2}  $  and 78 mg of DMSO were dissolved in 600 mg of DMF solution in a N $  _{2}  $  condition with stirring overnight. The prepared solution was deposited by spin-coating on the C $  _{60}  $  or HMB-doped C $  _{60}  $  films at 4000 rpm for 20 s with 0.5 ml of diethyl ether dripping method during the process, which causes rapid vaporization of DMF. After that, the perovskite film was annealed at 100 °C for 10 min to make a uniform and dense MAPbI $  _{3}  $  film. The carbon paste was bladed on the perovskite film directly and baked at 100 °C for 20 min.

# Characterizations

The EPR spectra were recorded at room temperature using a JEOL FA-200 EPR spectrometer. The contacting angles of DMF on the   $  C_{60}  $   and HMB-doped   $  C_{60}  $   films were measured using a contact angle measurement (OCA 15pro). The AFM height images were obtained with a Bruker Multimode 8 atomic force microscope in tapping mode. The cross-sectional and surface images of the ETL films, perovskite films and the fabricated perovskite solar cells were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (JEOL J-7800F). The crystalline structure of the perovskite films was characterized by powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) using Cu Ka radiation (  $  \lambda=0.15406  $   nm) on a Rigaku D/max-2500 VB2+/PC diffractometer in the 2θ range of   $  10^{\circ}\sim60^{\circ}  $   at a scanning rate of   $  5^{\circ}  $   min  $  ^{-1}  $  . The optical property of the   $  C_{60}  $   and HMB-doped   $  C_{60}  $   films on the ITO glass was determined by using the transmission spectra recorded on a UV-vis spectrophotometer (Rerkin Elmer Lambda 950 UV/VIS). Photoluminescence spectra were obtained on the Edinburgh Instruments FLS 980 with the range of wavelength from 700 nm to 850 nm by exciting at 470 nm. The current density-voltage (J-V) characteristics were measured by a solar simulator (Oriel Sol3A) with the Keithley 2400 Source Meter under AM 1.5G. The J-V curves were measured in an ambient atmosphere with a scanning rate of 50 mV s  $  ^{-1}  $  . The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectrum was measured by a EQE system (Oriel instruments) with 150 W Xenon lamp (USHIO, Japan) as a source of monochromatic light. The photo-stability test at continuous MPP operation under 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , AM 1.5G illumination was carried out in an ambient atmosphere by using an Electrochemical Workstation (Zennium 400147) to fix the voltage at   $  V_{MPP}  $   and then track the current output. The   $  V_{MPP}  $   at maximum power point was determined from the J-V curve. A 420-nm cutoff UV-filter was applied in front of the perovskite solar cells during the photo-stability tests. The EIS measurements were measured by applying a bias of the open-circuit voltage with frequencies ranging from   $  10^{6}  $   Hz to 100 Hz. The IMPS and IMVS measurements were recorded using a CIMPS system (PP211, Zahner).
