# Europium and Acetate Co-doping Strategy for Developing Stable and Efficient   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   Perovskite Solar Cells

Shaomin Yang, Huan Zhao, Yu Han, Chenyang Duan, Zhike Liu,* and Shengzhong (Frank) Liu*

All-inorganic perovskite solar cells have developed rapidly in the last two years due to their excellent thermal and light stability. However, low efficiency and moisture instability limit their future commercial application. The mixed-halide inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite with a suitable bandgap offers a good balance between phase stability and light harvesting. However, high defect density and low carrier lifetime in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskites limit the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc} < 1.2~V  $  ), short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc} < 15~mA~cm^{-2}  $  ), and fill factor (FF < 75%) of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite solar cells, resulting in an efficiency below 14%. For the first time, a   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite is doped by   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   to obtain a high-quality inorganic perovskite film with a low defect density and long carrier lifetime. A high efficiency of 15.25% (average efficiency of 14.88%), a respectable   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.25 V, a reasonable   $  J_{sc}  $   of 15.44 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and a high FF of 79.00% are realized for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cells. Moreover, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cells with   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   doping demonstrate excellent air stability and maintain more than 80% of their initial power conversion efficiency (PCE) values after aging in air (relative humidity: 35–40%) for 30 days.

# 1. Introduction

In recent years, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved rapid and significant development, in which the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs has exceeded 24%.  $  ^{[1]}  $   However, with the intrinsic volatility of organic cations such as methylammonium (MA  $  ^{+}  $  ) and formamidinium

CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite film, the defects and trap states inside the bulk perovskite film and/or at the interface can be reduced and/or passivated and the V $ _{oc} $ ,  $ J_{SC} $ , and PCEs of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs will be increased. $ ^{[7]} $  But beyond efficiency, long-term stability is another challenge for the development of inorganic CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs. The stability of the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs depends on a number of potential degradation factors. $ ^{[8]} $  In particular, a photoactive  $ \alpha $  phase of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite can be easily transformed into an undesirable non-perovskite  $ \delta $  phase under humidity at room temperature, causing a serious efficiency degradation. $ ^{[9-11]} $  Therefore, improving the long-term stability of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs is equally important.

Previous studies have shown that in a typical ABX $ _3 $  (A: Cs, Rb; B: Pb, Sn; X: Cl, Br, I) inorganic perovskite structure, smaller B-site and/or X-site substituted ions can improve the Goldschmidt tolerance factor, which is conducive to the formation of a stable photoactive  $ \alpha $  phase perovskite. $ ^{[12]} $  For this purpose, a series of doping strategies have been carried out by incorporating some smaller metal cations into the perovskite lattice to modulate photoelectronic properties and improve the phase stabilities of inorganic perovskite materials. For instance, some works proposed to partially substitute B-site cations (Pb $ ^{2+} $ , 120 pm) with smaller lanthanide ions such as Ce $ ^{3+} $  (103 pm), Eu $ ^{3+} $  (95 pm), Sm $ ^{3+} $  (111 pm), Er $ ^{3+} $  (88 pm), and so on in the inorganic perovskites. $ ^{[9,13-15]} $  It demonstrated that the addition of lanthanide ions can effectively improve the photovoltaic

More recently, By partially substitute   $  Pb^{2+}  $   with   $  Eu^{3+}  $  , a stable   $  \alpha  $  -phase CsP-  $  bi_{2}Br  $   perovskite was obtained at room temperature, showing a maximum PCE of 13.71%.  $  ^{[16]}  $   All of these findings demonstrated that smaller   $  Eu^{3+}  $   can be doped into the inorganic perovskites to improve their photovoltaic performance and stability.  $  ^{[15]}  $

The quality of the perovskite film is also an important factor to influence the performance of the PSCs. Passivation treatment by introducing a functional anion can adjust the growth of the perovskite crystal and effectively passivate the defects of perovskite films, such as   $  F^{-}  $  ,   $  SCN^{-}  $  , and so on. Recently, some studies have reported that the addition of   $  Ac^{-}  $   anions can enhance the device efficiency significantly which will adjust the growth of perovskite crystals and control surface morphology to obtain uniform, dense, high-quality perovskite films.  $  ^{[17-20]}  $   Currently, Zeng et al. realized a stable and photoactive   $  \alpha  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   by doping   $  \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_{2}}  $   at low temperature. It is demonstrated that   $  Ac^{-}  $   ions can strongly coordinate with   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   to stabilize the   $  \alpha  $  -phase by reducing the formation energy and making the grain size smaller. Finally, a champion efficiency of 12.00% is obtained for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with   $  \mathrm{Pb(Ac)_{2}}  $   addition.  $  ^{[21]}  $

In this study, we present an innovative   $  Eu^{3+}  $   and   $  Ac^{-}  $  co-doping strategy by introducing europium acetate (  $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $  ) into inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution for the first time. A stable   $  \alpha  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film is obtained, which exhibits enhanced optical properties, long carrier lifetime, and reduced defect densities. More importantly, after optimizing the doping concentration of   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $ , the as-fabricated inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs exhibited enhanced photovoltaic performance and long-term stability in ambient environment. Finally, we achieve a champion PCE of 15.25% for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br(Eu(Ac)_{3})  $   PSCs. In particular, the   $  V_{oc}  $   is increased to 1.25 V, which means that energy loss (  $  E_{loss}  $  ) is effectively reduced after doping   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $ . Moreover, the characterization of air stability also indicates that the   $  Eu^{3+}  $   and   $  Ac^{-}  $  co-doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs have a preferable long-term stability, and the device efficiencies can still remain at 82% of their initial values after exposure to air (relative humidity: 35–40%) for 30 days.

# 2. Results and Discussion

X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film doped with different concentration of   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   were measured to identify the change of perovskite crystal structure, and the result is shown in Figure 1a. The diffraction peaks at   $  14.6^{\circ}  $   and   $  29.5^{\circ}  $   are assigned to the (100) and (200) crystal planes of   $  \alpha  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite, respectively, which is consistent with the previous reports about   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films.  $  ^{[22-24]}  $   The intensity of the diffraction peaks was slightly enhanced with increasing of   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   doping concentration, indicating that the crystalline quality of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films was improved by   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   doping (Figure S1, Supporting information). Figure 1b exhibits an enlarged view of the (200) diffraction peaks. The peaks were slightly shifted to higher   $  2\theta  $   values with the incorporation of   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $ , indicating the contraction of crystalline lattice. The partial replacement of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   (119 pm) by smaller   $  Eu^{3+}  $   (95 pm) and   $  I^{-}  $   (220 pm) by smaller   $  Ac^{-}  $   (162 pm) increases the tolerance factor of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite,  $  ^{[25,26]}  $

which facilitates the formation of a stable   $  \alpha  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite.  $  ^{[27-29]}  $

As shown in the Figure 1c, the UV–vis absorption spectra measurements were applied to characterize the optical property of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films without and with   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant. The absorption intensity of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film with   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant was enhanced marginally compared to that of the control perovskite film. We ascribe the enhanced absorption to the increased crystallinity of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films. The absorption edge of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films without and with   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant was hardly changed. To investigate the influence of   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant on the bandgap of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, Tauc plots were plotted based on the UV–vis absorption spectra (Figure 1d).  $  ^{[6,30,31]}  $   The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films without and with   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant exhibited the same band gap of 1.91 eV, indicating that doping   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   had a negligible influence on the bandgap of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films.

The charge recombination and extraction behaviors in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films without and with   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant were investigated by the steady photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements. As shown in Figure 1e, the PL intensity of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films were notably reinforced with the increasing of doping concentration of   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $ , which indicated that the quality of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film was improved by dramatically decreasing non-radiative recombination. TRPL spectra are shown in Figure 1f and fitted with a double-exponential decay function. The extracted life-time values (  $  \tau  $  ) and weight factors (A) are summarized in Table S1, Supporting Information.  $  ^{[32]}  $   The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film with 1 mol%   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant shown the long fluorescence lifetime and the corresponding   $  \tau_{ave}  $   was 7.93 ns, which is much longer than that of the control sample (3.04 ns). The prolonged fluorescence lifetime may be ascribed to the reduced defect density which suppressed non-radiative recombination in the perovskite film, as well as the increased crystallinity of the inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film. The increased PL intensity and prolonged fluorescence lifetime may benefit from the excellent electronic properties of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films caused by the   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant.  $  ^{[33,34]}  $

The morphology of perovskite film is crucial for improving the performance of photovoltaic devices.  $  ^{[35]}  $   In order to further investigate the effects of   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pervoskite films, the morphology of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films were observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), as shown in Figure 2a–e. For inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films, when the doping concentration was increased from 0 to 0.5 mol%, significant grain refinement effect was observed, which is probably due to the addition of   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   introducing more nucleation sites.  $  ^{[36]}  $   A smaller grain is more likely to be the ideal morphology as it stabilizes the   $  \alpha  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskites due to its higher surface energy.  $  ^{[37,38]}  $   When the doping concentration is more than 0.5 mol%, there are more evident grain boundaries existing in the perovskite films and it seems that the small grains have a tendency to combine and form the large grains. The cross-sectional SEM images are shown in Figure 2f–j, the thickness of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite layer about 400 nm did not change remarkably after 0–1 mol%   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   doping. In order to characterize the effect of   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   doping on the photovoltaic performance of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs, we conducted some tests on the photoelectric performance of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. Figure 3a illustrates the device structure of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs which consisting of   $  FTO/TiO_{2}/CsPbI_{2}Br(Eu(Ac)_{3})/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au  $ , wherein the   $  TiO_{2}  $   film deposited on the FTO glass is employed as the electron-transport layer (ETL), the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film is fabricated without and with   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant as the active absorbing layer, the Spiro-OMeTAD film is the hole-transport layer (HTL) and the Au coating is the top anode. The photocurrent–voltage (J-V) curves for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with different concentration of   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant are presented in Figure 3b and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters are listed in Table 1. The control device without   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   doping exhibited a PCE of 14.26%, a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.18 V,   $  J_{sc}  $   of 15.36 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , and a fill factor (FF) of 78.16%. When   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   was incorporated, all the photovoltaic parameters of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs were significantly increased. The PCE of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC with an optimized doping ratio of 0.5 mol% was improved to 15.22%, with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.25 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 15.44 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and a FF of 79.00%. The   $  V_{oc}  $   is obviously increased from 1.18 to 1.25 V (Figure S4, Supporting Information), which means the energy loss in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs is effectively reduced.

In order to understand the increase of   $  V_{oc}  $  , capacitance–voltage (  $  C^{-2}-V  $  ) measurements were performed to further investigate the change of the built-in electric field of the CsP-bI  $  _{2}  $  Br PSCs with the Eu(Ac)  $  _{3}  $   dopant. Figure S5, Supporting Information, shows the   $  C^{-2}-V  $   plots for the CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br PSCs, from which the built-in potential was obtained based on the Mott–Schottky equation of   $  C^{-2}=(2(V_{\mathrm{bi}}-V))/(A^{2}e\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}N_{\mathrm{A}})  $  , where C is the capacitance under applied voltage (V),   $  V_{bi}  $   is the built-in potential, V is the applied voltage, A is the device area,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative permittivity,   $  e_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity, and   $  N_{A}  $   is the carrier concentration.  $  ^{[47,48]}  $   The   $  V_{bi}  $   for the PSC made with

0.5 mol% Eu(Ac)  $  _{3}  $   dopant is 1.02 V, which is larger than 0.95 V as obtained for the control device. The improved built-in potential (0.07 V) is equal to the increased   $  V_{oc}  $   of CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br PSCs, thus it is the main reason for the improved   $  V_{oc}  $   for CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br PSCs with the Eu(Ac)  $  _{3}  $   dopant. The relative higher   $  V_{bi}  $   of CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br PSCs with 0.5 mol% Eu(Ac)  $  _{3}  $   dopant provides an enhanced driving force to separate the photogenerated charge carriers as well as an extended depletion region to suppress electron–hole recombination.  $  ^{[33,49,50]}  $

Figure 3c shows the corresponding external quantum efficiency (EQE) of the control and 0.5 mol% Eu(Ac) $ _{3} $ -doped CsPbI $ _{2} $ Br PSCs. The integrated  $ J_{\text{sc}} $  values from the EQE spectra of CsPbI $ _{2} $ Br PSCs without and with Eu(Ac) $ _{3} $  doping are 15.20 and 15.40 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , respectively, which agree well with the  $ J_{\text{sc}} $  values extracted from  $ J-V $  curves. The stabilized power output of the devices is shown in Figure 3d. While maintaining an external bias near the maximum power output point (1.02 V), the stabilized photocurrent for the 0.5 mol% Eu(Ac) $ _{3} $ -doped CsPbI $ _{2} $ Br PSC is 14.82 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , corresponding to a stabilized efficiency of 15.11% well matched with the  $ J-V $  sweep measurement. In contrast, the control CsPbI $ _{2} $ Br device shows relatively inferior and unstabilized photocurrent and efficiency of 13.66 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  and 13.39% bias at the maximum power output point (0.98 V), respectively. The results indicate that the doping of Eu(Ac) $ _{3} $  is beneficial to the illumination stability of the CsPbI $ _{2} $ Br device. $ ^{[51]} $

Figure S6, Supporting Information, shows the reverse-and forward-scanned J-V curves of the control and 0.5 mol%   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $  -doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. # 3. Conclusions

In summary, we introduced   $  Eu^{3+}  $   to partly substitute   $  Pb^{2+}  $   in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite structure, which is conducive to stable   $  \alpha  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite structure. The introduction of functionalized   $  Ac^{-}  $  is beneficial to obtain high-quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film. Therefore,   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant aims to improve the crystallinity, electrical, and optical properties of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film and the stability of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC simultaneously. Besides, what is more important is that the increased built-in potential proves the increase of   $  V_{oc}  $ , which is beneficial to the reduction of energy loss and the improvement of device performance. The champion inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC with 0.5 mol%   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant shows an supreme PCE and   $  V_{oc}  $   of 15.25% and 1.25 V, respectively. Furthermore,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   devices with 0.5 mol%   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   dopant demonstrate superior stability in comparison to the control   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   devices, which remains 82% of their initial values after aging in air for 30 days. The simultaneous substitute of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   with a smaller   $  Eu^{3+}  $   cation and   $  I^{-}  $  with a smaller   $  Ac^{-}  $  anion provides a feasible route for further optimization of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with respect to morphology, electronic properties, and stability toward efficient and reliable photovoltaic industry.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials:   $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)_{2}  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}(DMSO)_{2}  $   adducts were synthesized in accordance with the previously reported method.  $  ^{[5]}  $   CsI

(99.99%) was purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Europium acetate (Eu(Ac)  $  _{3}  $  , 99.99%) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich.

Device Fabrication: The inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs were fabricated with a structure of   $  FTO/TiO_{2}/CsPbI_{2}Br/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au  $  . FTO glass substrates were successively cleaned with acetone, isopropanol, and distilled water in an ultrasonic bath for 30 min, respectively. Then the substrates were dried by   $  N_{2}  $   flow and treated with UV-ozone for 10 min to raise the hydrophilicity.   $  TiO_{2}  $   films as the electron-transport layer (ETL) were prepared by chemical bath deposition on prepared FTO substrates.  $  ^{[57]}  $   The substrates were immersed in 200 mL aqueous solution with 4.5 mL Titanium tetrachloride for 60 min at 70 °C, followed by washing with distilled water and annealing at 200 °C for 30 min.  $  ^{[58]}  $   The control   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite precursor solution was prepared according to the reported procedure.  $  ^{[5]}  $     $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $  -doped perovskite precursor solution was prepared by adding   $  Eu(Ac)_{3}  $   into the control   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite precursor solution with different doping ratio. Then the perovskite precursor solutions were spin-coated on   $  TiO_{2}  $   film by two-step process at 1000 rpm for 10 s and then at 3000 rpm for 40 s. After that, the precursor films were heated at 120 °C for 10 min and 200 °C for 2 min to obtain black   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films. The hole-transport layer was prepared by spin-coating Spiro-OMeTAD solution (90 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) doped with 36 μL t-BP and 22 μL Li-TFSI (520 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) solution in acetonitrile at 5000 rpm for 30 s onto   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films. Finally, a gold layer (  $  \approx  $  80 nm) was thermally evaporated as the top electrode using a shadow mask to form a device active area of 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  .
