# Tuning the crystal-growth of perovskite thin-films by adding 2-pyridylthiourea additive for highly efficient and stable solar cells prepared in ambient air

Mengna Sun  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Fei Zhang  $  ^{a,b,*}  $  , Hongli Liu  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Xianggao Li  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Yin Xiao  $  ^{a,b}  $  , Shirong Wang  $  ^{a,b,*}  $

Here, we report a rapid and simple process to prepare the perovskite solar cells in ambient air by adding 2-pyridylthiourea in the precursor solution. 2-pyridylthiourea can not only promote the conversion of the  $ 2\text{-D PbI}_2 $  to the tetragonal  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  crystals, but also improve the quality of the perovskite absorber layer via enhancing the uniformity and the size of the crystal grains. The perovskite solar cells with adding 2-pyridylthiourea at the concentration of  $ 0.5\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  exhibited a remarkable overall power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 18.2\% $ , which is among the highest PCE of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ -based devices fabricated in ambient air. It also showed an  $ 18\% $  increase and less hysteresis compared with the cells without additives. Importantly, the devices with 2-pyridylthiourea show relatively better stability compared to reference devices when aged under ambient air of  $ 55\pm 5\% $  relative humidity in the dark and under  $ 65^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  after 30 days. The presented results clearly qualify that 2-pyridylthiourea can be one of additive candidates for tuning the morphology of perovskite thin-films, which may be a new direction for large-scale production of PSCs.

# Introduction

Hybrid lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have got substantial attention as the next-generation commercial photovoltaic materials due to their simple structure, low fabrication cost and outstanding photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{1-4}  $   In recent years, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs have increased from 3.8 % to more than 20 %.  $  ^{5-10}  $   Although the PCE of the perovskite solar cells have achieved over than 20 %, the current–voltage hysteresis, as well as moisture and thermal stability are still important issues to be solved.

Since hybrid lead halide perovskite is an ionic crystal, the perovskite absorber layer has been reported to be influenced to humidity and heat in air.  $  ^{[11,12]}  $   Without long-term device stability, PSCs are difficult to be commercially used.  $  ^{[13-15]}  $   The conventional perovskite solar cells are made up of lead halide perovskite   $  (\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{PbI}_{3})  $   absorber layer, which is deposited on the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer, and spiro-OMeTAD is used as the hole-transporting layer with TBP and LiTFSI as additives.  $  ^{[16]}  $   Traditional attempts had been made to improve the stability of perovskite solar cells, such as modification of the perovskite sensitizer,  $  ^{[17-20]}  $   as well as modification of the additives in the hole-transporting layer  $  ^{[21-24]}  $   and the application of dopant-free or additives-free hole-transporting materials.  $  ^{[25-28]}  $   Within modification of the perovskite, the easiest way is adopting some additives into perovskite solution precursor.

The role of the additives is major in improving the morphology and crystallinity of the perovskite absorber layer to control the film quality of the perovskite absorber layer.  $  ^{29-37}  $   For example,   $  NH_{3}SO_{3}  $   have been used as additive to improve the performance of the solar cells by controlling the morphology and crystallinity of the perovskite film, where the PCE was enhanced from 13.08 % to 16.02 %, and the devices without encapsulation exhibit respectable long-term stability.  $  ^{34}  $   PEG as the polymer scaffold was employed for the solar cells to achieve the highest PCE of 16 % and the cells with PEG displayed strong humidity resistant.  $  ^{37}  $   In addition, other additives had been explored to improve the photovoltaic performance, such as 1,8-diiodooctane (DIO),  $  ^{30}  $   polyethylenimine (PEI),  $  ^{35}  $   fullerene derivatives  $  ^{31}  $   and LiCl  $  ^{32}  $   etc. All the additives were aimed to improve PCE and long-term stability, but the reported PCE is not high, few above 18 %.  $  ^{34,36,37}  $   Moreover, it is also a big challenge to improve the humidity stability and thermal stability of the devices at the same time, which depends on the high quality film of the perovskite. Thus, to fabricate a uniform, continuous and compact perovskite layer is very important factor for the photovoltaic performance and the long-term stability of the perovskite solar cells.

Herein, we firstly address the use of 2-pyridylthiourea as the additive in the fabrication of the PSCs in ambient air via a simple one-step spin-coating method. 2-pyridylthiourea can act as N-donor, as well as S-donor to coordinate with   $  PbI_{2}  $  , resulting in reducing the speed of perovskite crystal formation, which can produce the uniform, continuous and compact perovskite films with larger crystal grains.  $  ^{38}  $   The optical and electrical properties can be improved due to the improvement of the crystallinity and morphology of the perovskite absorber layer. Compared to the control device, the PCE of devices with 2-pyridylthiourea additive is obviously improved from 15.5% to 18.2%. The champion device with adding 2-pyridylthiourea

# Experimental

# Materials

Substrates are FTO conducting glass (<15 Ω/square) achieved from Nippon Sheet Glass Corporation. spiro-OMeTAD and PbI₂ was obtained from Luminescence Technology Corporation. Titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) and 2-pyridylthiourea was purchased from Alfar Aesar. N-butanol, methylamine solution (40 % in methanol) was obtained from Aladdin. Dimethyl Sulphoxide (DMSO) and N,N'-dimethylformamide (DMF) were purchased from J&K chemical Corporation. CH₃NH₃I (MAI) was synthesized according to literatures.  $  ^{39}  $   All the materials were used as received without any other purification unless other-wise specified.

# Characterization

The image of a cross section of a solar cell was measured on a scanning electron microscope (SEM, XL30S-FEG, FEI, USA). X-ray diffraction (XRD) data was recorded on the Mini Flex 600 in the range  $ 2\theta = 10 - 60^{\circ} $  using CuKa1 radiation. Ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) absorption spectrum was measured with a Thermo Evolution 300 UV/Visible spectrometer. Fourier transfer infrared spectra (FTIR) was recorded as a KBr disk on a Thermo NICOLET380 spectrometer. Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra was confirmed on Edinburgh Instruments (FLS 980, UK) collected at  $ 775~\mathrm{nm} $  after excitation at  $ 485~\mathrm{nm} $ . Illumination of the solar cells was set at 100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $  (AM 1.5G) made with an Oriel solar simulator 91160A, the current density-voltage  $ (J - V) $  characteristics were recorded with Keithley 2602 SourceMeter, a mask of window of  $ 0.16~\mathrm{cm}^2 $  was applied to confirm the effective area of the solar cells. The incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) was obtained with a lab-made IPCE testing system.

# Solar cell fabrication

FTO-coated glass substrates were washed by detergent, after rinsing with distilled water, the substrates were further cleaned with deionized water and then with ethanol. In the end, the substrates were placed in air to dry. A compact layer of   $  TiO_{2}  $   was deposited on the cleaned substrate by spin-coating the titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) in n-butanol (v:v=1:16) at 2000 rpm for 30 s, soon after by heating at 500 °C for 30 min in air.  $  ^{40}  $   The mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer can be obtained by spin-coating the commercial   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste diluted in the ethanol (w:w=1:6) on the compact layer at 4000 rpm for 30 s, the substrate was sintering at 500 °C for 30 min. The   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   absorber layer was obtained through anti-solvent method  $  ^{6}  $   and the precursor solution was prepared from 1.35M   $  Pb^{2+}  $   with different concentration of 2-pyridylthiourea in the mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO, the volume ratio of DMF/DMSO is 4:1. The spin-coating procedure was performed by 2000 rpm for 9 s followed with 5000 rpm for 30 s. At 21 s before the last spin-coating

step, 180 μL of chlorobenzene (CB) was pipetted onto the substrate. Thereafter, the substrate was put onto a hot plate for 1 hour at 100 °C. Subsequently, the hole-tranporting material (HTM) was deposited on the top of perovskite by spin coating at 4000 rpm. for 20 s. The spiro-OMeTAD solutions were prepared dissolving the spiro-OMeTAD in 1 mL chlorobenzene at concentration of 72.3 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  with the addition of 30 μL 4-tert-butylpyridine, 18 μL of 520 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulphonyl)imide acetonitrile solution.  $  ^{28}  $  All these process are prepared in ambient air. At last, gold electrode of 80 nm thickness of was deposited on the top of the HTM layer to get the back contact through thermally evaporation under high vacuum.

Figure 2a showed the UV-Vis absorption spectra of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   absorber films prepared with the different concentration of 2-pyridylthiourea in the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   precursor solution on the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   film. Compared to the pristine   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film, with the increasing concentration of the 2-pyridylthiourea, the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film showed obvious absorption enhancement in almost the whole visible-light region, reaching the maximum value at the concentration of 0.5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in precursor solutions, which probably originates from the contribution of its impact on the improved surface morphology and more uniform crystal formation of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  .⁴⁴ With more increase of the concentration of 2-pyridylthiourea, there is significant decrease of absorbance which can results from the decrease of the crystal quality.³¹,³²,⁴⁵

The good thin film uniformity suggests a homogenous nucleation of the perovskites on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface.  $  ^{46}  $   In order to confirm the influence of the 2-pyridylthiourea on the crystallinity and surface morphology of the perovskite thin films,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   thin films with 2-pyridylthiourea at different concentration had been made. Figure 2b-f shows the top SEM images of the perovskite absorber films. The morphology of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film is affected by adding the 2-pyridylthiourea obviously. There are some visible pinholes in the perovskite absorber films without 2-pyridylthiourea and the crystal grains are small. With the addition of 2-pyridylthiourea, the morphology of the active layer can be effectively improved. The perovskite films are more uniform and smooth and the grains become larger. The pinholes may be due to the rapid reaction between the organic iodide and   $  PbI_{2}  $  . Due to the weak

coordination between DMSO and   $  PbI_{2}  $  , the DMSO is easy to escape from the   $  PbI_{2}  $   framework. This often leads to insufficient perovskite grain growth. With the addition of 2-pyridylthiourea, the reaction can be retarded to obtain the uniform and dense perovskite films with high quality.  $  ^{47,48}  $   At 0.1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   of 2-pyridylthiourea, the perovskite film was compact and smooth. Moreover, the needle-like pores on the surface decreased, the quality of the film was improved. When the content of 2-pyridylthiourea was increased to 0.5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , the crystal grain of perovskite became larger and the arrangement between the grains became more dense. In addition, the perovskite thin film exhibits good uniformity, with no pinholes. The significant effect on the morphology of the perovskite films may be attributed to the interactions between   $  PbI_{2}  $   and 2-pyridylthiourea during the growth of the perovskite, which correspondingly slow down the speed of perovskite formation process.  $  ^{30,48}  $   However, when we further increase the amount of 2-pyridylthiourea to 1.0 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   and 3.0 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , the perovskite film became coarser for the non-compact arrangement of the crystal grains. This conclusion is well consistent with UV-Vis absorption properties as shown in Figure 2a.

The morphology of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  thin films with and without 2-pyridylthiourea was studied by AFM, as shown in Figure 3a-b. It can be found that with 2-pyridylthiourea, the small root mean squared (Rms) roughness of the perovskite film is  $ 2.63~\mathrm{nm} $ , on contrast, without 2-pyridylthiourea, Rms is  $ 13.86~\mathrm{nm} $ . The result showed that with the addition of 2-pyridylthiourea, the perovskite thin film can be more smooth and uniform, which is benefit for the effective charge transport and can achieve higher photovoltaic performance.

The FTIR spectra is carried out to investigate how 2-pyridylthiourea tunes the crystal-growth of the perovskite thin-films. Figure 3c showed the spectra for the mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO with  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , 2-pyridylthiourea,  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2\cdot 2 $ -pyridylthiourea, and 2-pyridylthiourea-containing perovskite precursors at different molar ratio. The stretching vibrations of  $ C = S $  and  $ C = N $  for pure 2-pyridylthiourea lie at  $ 785~\mathrm{cm^{-1}} $  and  $ 1600~\mathrm{cm^{-1}} $ , respectively. The stretching vibrations of  $ C = S $  and  $ C = N $  is blue-shifted to  $ 783~\mathrm{cm^{-1}} $  and  $ 1598~\mathrm{cm^{-1}} $  respectively for the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2\cdot 2 $ -pyridylthiourea solution due to the interaction with  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , and then further shifted to lower wavenumber for the 2-pyridylthiourea-containing perovskite precursors. The blue-shift in the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2\cdot 2 $ -pyridylthiourea solution is due to the partial electron cloud migration from  $ C = S $  and  $ C = N $  to S-Pb and N-Pb.[38,48] While, the 2-pyridylthiourea-containing perovskite precursor can form the adduct of MAI-PbI₂·2-pyridylthiourea caused by the interaction between 2-pyridylthiourea and/or iodide (I-) and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , which may be result the further blue-shift of the stretching vibrations of  $ C = S $  and  $ C = N $ .[38] In addition, with the increase of the additive amount, both  $ C = N $  and  $ C = S $  is further shifted to lower wavenumber due to the enhanced coordination between the 2-

pyridylthiourea and   $  PbI_{2}  $  . All the results can indicate that 2-pyridylthiourea can coordinate with the   $  PbI_{2}  $  , which is benefit for the perovskite formation process. We reason that this interaction not only stabilizes the surface of perovskite nanocrystals, but also prevents decomposition of perovskite into   $  PbI_{2}  $  . The EDS data in the Figure S1 shows 2-pyridylthiourea was evenly distributed in the perovskite film. The XRD of the perovskite films with 2-pyridylthiourea at different concentration is shown in Figure 3d, all the films have the main diffraction peaks of 14.67°, 28.91°, and 32.46°, which are assigned to the (110), (220), and (310) face of the perovskite crystal structure, respectively.  $  ^{49}  $   It is clear that with 2-pyridylthiourea additive, the XRD peak centred at 14.67°, 28.91°, and 32.46° increased, as well as the peak centred at 13.2° corresponded to the (001) face of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   disappeared. The peaks which can be assigned to the perovskite structure reach the highest at the concentration of 0.5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  . With more increase of the concentration of 2-pyridylthiourea, there is significant decrease, which is mainly due to the decrease of the crystal quality. All the results are consisted with enhancement of crystallinity and orientation of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   grains.

The durable XRD tests of the thin films with and without 2-pyridylthiourea had also been conducted. It is clear that the thin film with 2-pyridylthiourea show better stability under thermal pressure as well as humidity. As shown in Figure 3e-f, the XRD peaks located at  $ 13.2^{\circ} $  assigned to the (001) face of the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , the intensity of the peak can represent the ratio between the peak correspond to the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and the peak of the (110) face of the perovskite. For the film with 2-pyridylthiourea, there is no obvious peak of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  both under humidity and thermal pressure. In contrast, for the film without 2-pyridylthiourea, the intensity of the peak gradually increased and finally achieved the  $ 72\% $  and  $ 27\% $  compared to reference peak located at  $ 14.67^{\circ} $  after aging under the same condition which can attribute to the degradation from  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  to  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , respectively.[50,51] As a results, the colour of perovskite films with 2-pyridylthiourea remained persistent, while the perovskite films without 2-pyridylthiourea faded from black to yellow, which can be observed from Figure S2. These results indicate 2-pyridylthiourea can enhance the stability by modification which is benefit to the long-term stability for the cells. Besides, the contact angles ( $ \theta $ ) between perovskite thin films and  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  were carried out to confirm the hydrophobicity, which can provide a rational for its greatly enhanced stability against degradation in humid air.[10,52,53] As shown in Figure S3, the value of  $ \theta $  for perovskite thin film with 2-pyridylthiourea is  $ 65.07^{\circ} $ , higher than the value of the film without additive ( $ 44.07^{\circ} $ ). With 2-pyridylthiourea, the  $ \theta $  can be effectively enhanced, which is favourable for the long-term stability, the results are corresponding to the XRD data in Figure 3e.

To investigate the mechanism of the improvement of performance by adding 2-pyridylthiourea, steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film with and without 2-pyridylthiourea were exhibited in Figure 4a-b. With adding 2-pyridylthiourea, the steady-state PL spectrum of perovskite film displayed a blue shift by  $ 7\mathrm{nm} $ , which suggests the fewer surface defects close to the top surface of perovskite film.[54] We also investigated TRPL measurement on the FTO substrate with and without 2-pyridylthiourea. Figure 4b shows the TRPL decay measurement and the stretched exponential decay lifetimes are obtained by fitting the data with bi-exponential decay function,  $ Y = A_{1}\exp (-t / \tau_{1}) + A_{2}\exp (-t / \tau_{2}) + y_{0} $ .[55] In this formula,  $ A_{1} $  and  $ A_{2} $  are relative amplitudes, while  $ \tau_{1} $  and  $ \tau_{2} $  represent the lifetimes of the fast and slow recombination, respectively. The film without 2-pyridylthiourea showed fast and slow phase lifetimes of  $ \tau_{1} = 11.83 $  ns

(fraction   $  A_{1}=0.97  $  ) and   $  \tau_{2}=157.84  $   ns (fraction   $  A_{2}=0.03  $  ) while the film with 2-pyridylthiourea exhibits fast and slow phase lifetimes of   $  \tau_{1}=21.27  $   ns (fraction   $  A_{1}=0.89  $  ) and   $  \tau_{2}=388.18  $   ns (fraction   $  A_{2}=0.11  $  ). This indicates a lower defect concentration and hence superior electronic quality for the 2-pyridylthiourea-containing perovskite film, consistent with the higher   $  V_{oc}  $   of the corresponding PSC.  $  ^{56}  $

Figure 4c exhibits the cross sectional SEM image based on   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film with 2-pyridylthiourea of 0.5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  . Figure 4d displays the J-V curves of PSCs with and without 2-pyridylthioure under AM 1.5G illumination and the corresponding photovoltaic data are summarized in Table 1. The control device based on pristine perovskite has a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 21.13 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.06 V, a fill factor (FF) of 0.69, and a resulting PCE of 15.5 %. In contrast, 2-pyridylthiourea-containing perovskite-based devices exhibit significant improvements in both   $  J_{sc}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF. The 2-pyridylthiourea-containing perovskite-based solar cells present the best performance with PCE of 18.2 %,   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.03 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.10 V, and FF of 0.75. We ascribed the improvement of performance based on 2-pyridylthiourea-containing perovskite mainly to improved light-harvesting ability, charge transport, the reduction of defect and recombination loss, as well as the improvement of the smooth and continue perovskite absorber layer,  $  ^{30}  $   which is due to the coordination of 2-pyridylthiourea and PbI  $  _{2}  $  .  $  ^{57,58}  $   Besides, there is a commonly observed hysteresis phenomenon in PSCs which may be associated with the changes of the defect state in the active layer, ion transport, or other changes happened in the interface between the charge collection layer and the active layer, as well as the ferroelectric effect and other factors.  $  ^{59,60}  $   The device's hysteresis effect can be reduced obviously by the addition of 2-pyridylthiourea due to decrease of spontaneous radiative recombination between trap.  $  ^{44}  $

The stabilized power output was measured in the Figure 4e, the PCE was stable after 30 s. The IPCE spectrum of the cells based on the corresponding perovskite layers are presented in the Figure 4f. By integrating the IPCE spectrum, the   $  J_{sc}  $   was 21.70 and 20.59 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   for the devices with and without 2-pyridylthiourea, respectively, indicating the results are in good agreement with the   $  J_{sc}  $   values confirmed from the J-V curves. The reproducibility of the device performance was also evaluated by characterizing about 10 cells. The cells demonstrate excellent reproducibility by the narrow statistical distribution of the photovoltaic metrics shown in Figure 5.

# Conclusions

In conclusion, we show an excellent additive (2-pyridylthiourea) for fabricating highly efficient and stable perovskite solar cells in ambient air. With the additive, the morphology and crystallinity had been improved obviously, which is benefit to enhance of the performance for the solar cells. With the addition of 2-pyridylthiourea, a PCE of  $ 18.2\% $  with less hysteresis can be obtained, which is  $ 18\% $  higher than that of the devices without 2-pyridylthiourea. Moreover, especially with regard to long-term stability, the devices with 2-pyridylthiourea exhibit better stability compared to reference devices when aged under ambient air of  $ 55 \pm 5\% $  relative humidity in the dark and under  $ 65^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  after 30 days. The result may pave a new way for fabricating stable and efficient perovskite solar cells.
