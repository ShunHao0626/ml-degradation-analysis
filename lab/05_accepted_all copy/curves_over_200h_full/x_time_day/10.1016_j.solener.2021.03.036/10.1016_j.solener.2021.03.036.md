# 1. Introduction

In recent years, organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted much attention due to high power conversion efficiency (PCE), low cost, and a simple preparation process (Chen et al., 2017; Im et al., 2014; Im et al., 2011; Jung et al., 2019; Kim et al., 2012; NREL, 2019; Saliba et al., 2016; Xu et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2014). In recent reports, the PCE of PSCs has surpassed  $ 25\% $ , thus overtaking the extant PCE record of crystal silicon solar cells, which is around  $ 26\% $ . The excellent performance of PSCs is closely related to the use of high-quality perovskite film with high light absorption, long carrier diffusion length, suitable bandgap, and low exciton separation energy (Kojima et al., 2009; Jung and Park, 2015; Kazim et al., 2014; Kim et al., 2016). With improvements in the preparation process and material optimisation, PSC efficiency is close to the theoretical limit (Wu et al., 2019). Further, cost-effective improvement of solar cells is becoming increasingly important to promote the development of photovoltaics.

PSC performance is generally defined by three main parameters: the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ), short-circuit current (  $  J_{SC}  $  ), and fill factor (FF). Compositional engineering with the addition of organic and inorganic cations is regarded as an effective way to improve the performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, exploration of triple-mixed cations in PSCs is still in its initial stage. In this study, triple cations of  $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $ ,  $ \mathrm{FA}^+ $ , and  $ \mathrm{Gua}^+ $  are mixed into perovskite and moulded into a 3D structure by the two-step deposition method, thus proving the feasibility of the triple-cation-based perovskite layer. Through the results of X-ray diffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscope (SEM), and Photoluminescence (PL) tests,  $ \mathrm{Gua_y(FAMA)_1 - yPbIxCl_{3 - x}} $  perovskite crystallisation was observed. The triple-mixed, cation-based perovskite film exhibits improved crystal quality, larger grain size, and fewer trap densities, all of which are positive for final power conversion efficiency (PCE) and stability. Based on the optimised  $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $  and  $ \mathrm{FA}^+ $  component ratio, the PSC showed the best performance with a Gual doping concentration of  $ 10\% $ . The introduction of Gual further improves the performance of devices by the passivation of defects and suppression of nonradiative recombination. The final PSCs displayed the best PCE  $ (21.3\%) $ , as well as enhanced stability.

Compositional engineering with the addition of organic and inorganic cations is an efficient method of improving   $  V_{OC}  $   and FF by reducing traps in the perovskite film (Ono et al., 2017; Santander et al., 2007; Prochowicz et al., 2019). Formamidinium (  $  FA^{+}  $  ), cesium (  $  Cs^{+}  $  ), rubidium (  $  Rb^{+}  $  ), and guanidinium (  $  Gua^{+}  $  ) have been used in perovskite materials, and significant progress has been made in terms of rapid production of perovskite film. For example, in Zhang's work, 10%   $  FA^{+}  $  was doped into methylammonium lead iodide (  $  MAPbI_{3}  $  ), which improved the perovskite crystallinity as well as all solar cell parameters; this also allowed attainment of high PCE (over 20%). Similarly, the addition of   $  Cs^{+}  $  and   $  Rb^{+}  $  cations into   $  ABX_{3}  $   perovskite produced the same lifting effect by reducing the trap states in the perovskite crystals (Prochowicz et al., 2018a,b). In Yang's work, Gua  $  ^{+}  $  was proved to have passivation effects on devices performance through reduction of defect activation energy and enhancement of carrier lifetime (Marco et al., 2016). In Alexander's work, Gua  $  ^{+}  $  was observed to take the place of MA  $  ^{+}  $ ; it formed a 3D GuaMAPbI  $  _{3}  $  structure, which widened the exploration of cations with a radius beyond the tolerance limit. Therefore, most of the reported works focus on the defect passivation and recombination suppression and ignore the mechanism of GuaI in perovskite crystallization process. Some of the reports exhibit that the perovskite grain size is improved with the adding of guanidinium ionic while not giving detailed explain about the phenomenon. Further, some results of the above studies are contradictory, and limited by preparation process the previously reported efficiency of GuaI-based devices is not high enough; additionally, the study of GuaI-based PSCs should be further carried out. In this work, triple cations of   $  MA^{+}  $  ,   $  FA^{+}  $  , and   $  Gua^{+}  $  are mixed into perovskite and made to form a 3D structure by the two-step deposition method, which proves the feasibility of the triple-cation-based perovskite layer. Through the results of XRD, SEM, and PL tests,   $  Gua_{y}(-FAMA)_{1-y}PbI_{x}Cl_{3-x}  $   perovskite crystallisation was observed. The triple-mixed cation-based perovskite film exhibits better crystal quality, larger grain size, and fewer trap densities, all of which has a positive effect on the final PCE and stability. When the concentration of GuaI continues to increase, the 3D perovskite structure is destroyed with a decrease in light absorption capacity. The introduction of GuaI further improves the performance of PSCs by the passivation of defects and suppression of nonradiative recombination. The final PSC displayed the best PCE (21.3%), as well as enhanced stability.

# 2. Results and discussion

PSCs are forward n-i-p structures, which are prepared by the

following steps: First, the glass/ITO substrate was ultrasonically cleared using decon 90, DI water, and alcohol for  $ 20\mathrm{min} $ . The substrate was then blow-dried and UV-treated. The treated substrate was then spin-coated by the electron transport layer of tin oxide  $ (\mathrm{SnO}_2) $ , the light absorption perovskite layer of  $ \mathrm{Gua_y(FAMA)_{1 - y}PbI_xCl_{3 - x}} $ , and the hole transport layer of Spiro-OMeTAD. Finally, the silver electrode was deposited on the devices. The final product is shown in Fig. 1. The detailed steps are shown in the supporting information section. The perovskite film was prepared by the two-step method, with  $ \mathrm{PbX_2} $   $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{PbCl}_2) $  deposited on the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  substrate, followed by spin-coating of GuaI, FAI, and MAI ternary mixed solution, as shown in Fig. 2a. The optimal proportion of MAI and FAI is based on our previous work (Chen et al., 2019; Pang et al., 2020). Therefore, the optimisation of the perovskite film is mainly based on the changing of GuaI rates. To investigate the optimal rate of GuaI in the compound perovskite, the concentrations of GuaI were set as  $ 0\% $ ,  $ 5\% $ ,  $ 10\% $ ,  $ 20\% $ ,  $ 30\% $ , and  $ 40\% $ . The statistical parameters acquired from the GuaI-doped solar cells are shown in Fig. 1b and Fig. S1. The  $ \mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{SC}} $  exhibited an increasing trend until  $ 10\% $  GuaI is doped and then sharply decreased with further increase in GuaI. The change in  $ \mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{OC}} $  varied from that of the  $ \mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{SC}} $ , which increased constantly. However, the rising extent gradually slowed down when the GuaI ratio was over  $ 10\% $ . The FF revealed a similar trend to that of  $ \mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{SC}} $ . Finally, PCE showed the highest value when the GuaI ratio was  $ 10\% $ . The PCE increased from an average of  $ 18\% $  for  $ \mathrm{FAMAPbI_xCl_{3 - x}} $  solar cells to  $ 19\% $  for the  $ \mathrm{Gua_{0.05}(FAMA)_{0.95}PbI_xCl_{3 - x}} $  and finally  $ 20.5\% $  for  $ \mathrm{Gua_{0.1}(FAMA)_{0.9}PbI_xCl_{3 - x}} $  mixed system. Fig. 1c displays the best devices with and without GuaI. For the GuaI ratio of  $ 10\% $ , the best PCE was  $ 21.3\% $  with  $ \mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{SC}} = 23.8~\mathrm{mA / cm^2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{OC}} = 1.14\mathrm{V} $ , and  $ \mathrm{FF} = 0.78 $  higher than other ratios. However, the performance of GuaI-based devices, when the GuaI ratio is over  $ 20\% $ , is far lower than those without GuaI, as shown in Fig. 1b. The IPCE and corresponding integrated currents of the best devices are shown in Fig. 1d. The PSCs exhibited high and similar photon-to-current conversion efficiencies in the visible light band. The corresponding integrated current densities are  $ 22.9~\mathrm{mA / cm^2} $  and  $ 23.5~\mathrm{mA / cm^2} $  for the GuaI ratio of  $ 0\% $  and  $ 10\% $ , which are in agreement with the high IPCE. The preparation of the perovskite films is shown in Fig. 2a. GuaI is doped into the perovskite film by the two-step method. Fig. 2b and 2e show the photographs and UV–vis spectra of different ratios of GuaI-containing perovskite films. The colour of the annealed perovskite films shows a clear change from black (GuaI = 0%) to reddish-brown (GuaI = 40%) with different ratios of GuaI doping. This may be caused by a reduction in light absorption ability and an increase in the bandgap. In Fig. 2e, all perovskite films exhibit the absorption edge at 790 nm, indicating similar bandgaps of 1.57 eV. It can be inferred that GuaI has little effect on bandgaps. The perovskite films exhibit close light absorption with GuaI ratios lower than 10% and decrease with an increase in the GuaI ratio, which coincides with the changing trend of   $  J_{SC}  $   in Fig. 1. To acquire insight into the GuaI-doped perovskite structure, XRD is carried out as shown in Fig. 2c. All the films demonstrate the diffraction intensity of the peaks at approximately 14.1° and 28.3°, corresponding to the (110) and (220) planes of the cubic perovskite phase, respectively. Peak intensities gradually increased in the range of 0 – 10% and decreased in the range of 10 – 40%. The incorporation of GuaI cations had a different influence on the reflection peak intensity depending on the GuaI ratios. Furthermore, some new diffraction peaks appeared at 8.5° and 11.3°, which are attributed to the 1D GuaPbI₃ phase; these are in agreement with previous reports (Jodlowski et al., 2016). The formation of new phases is mainly due to the different precipitation kinetics of MA-based, FA-based, and GuaI-based hybrids (Zhang et al., 2019). In Fig. 2d, the magnification of the XRD peaks at the (110) and (220) lattice planes indicate a slight peak shift to lower angles with increasing GuaI content. In accordance with Bragg's law:   $  2\mathrm{dsin}\theta = n\lambda  $  , where a smaller   $  \theta  $   leads to a larger interplanar distance (d), the peak shift to lower angles represents an expansion of the unit cell volume, indicating the incorporation of the larger Gua⁺ cation by the formation of Gua₃(FAMA)₁₋ᵧPbIXCl₃₋ₓ perovskite. Based on the XRD

images in Fig. 2d and e, we can draw the following conclusions: Moderate GuaI doping (y < 0.2) is helpful for the perovskite crystallization through incorporation into the crystal and formation of 3D Gua  $  _{y}  $  (-FAMA)  $  _{1-y}  $  PbI  $  _{x}  $  Cl  $  _{3-x}  $  perovskite. Excessive GuaI cannot be incorporated into the crystalline network and leads to a phase-separated 1D GuaPbI  $  _{3}  $  with a Gual rate over 0.2.

In the above results, the perovskite films show a higher-intensity GuaI content of 10%, indicating improved crystallisation. To analysed the results, the SEM, PL, and TRPL tests were implemented (Fig. 3a – d). Both films grew with full coverage of substrates. The grain sizes ranged between 400 and 700 nm for the perovskite film without GuaI doping, as shown in Fig. 3a; with 10% GuaI doping, they significantly increased, and ranged between 600 and 1300 nm. This larger average grain size came from the expansion of the crystal unit with the incorporation of GuaI. We suspected that guanidinium ionic is helpful to form more stable mesophase and prolong the crystallization time of perovskite films. On the one hand, the longer crystallization time of guanidinium ionic reduces the number of initial crystal- nucleus, which is beneficial to improve the crystal growth rate, and the perovskite crystals tend to grow into larger particles. On the other hand, during the annealing process, guanidinium ionic is gradually squeezed to the grain boundary and enriched, which greatly increases the interface energy of perovskite crystals. In order to reduce the free energy of the system, these high interfacial energies provide the driving force for the behavior of large-size grains swallowing small-size grains. During the annealing process of perovskite films, Ostwald ripening mechanism or directional bonding growth mechanism dominates the growth process of perovskite grains. We think that the grain size of perovskite is increased by the two effects aroused by the adding of GuaI. And, larger average grain size is primarily associated with better performance, with fewer grain boundaries and fewer trap densities (Yang et al., 2016; Yun et al., 2017). This is confirmed by the PL and TRPL images shown in Fig. 3c and 3d. It is apparent that the perovskite film with 10% GuaI displays a higher intensity than the pure film without GuaI, indicating a lower nonradiative

The PL decay curves were fitted to a single-exponential rate law:  $  \mathrm{Y} = \mathrm{Aexp}(-\mathrm{t} / \tau_1) + \mathrm{y}_0  $ , where A is the relative amplitude and  $  \tau_1  $  is the lifetime of the recombination. The lifetime of the perovskite film increased from  $  0.4~\mu \mathrm{s}  $  to  $  1.2~\mu \mathrm{s}  $  for the incorporation of  $  10\%  $  GuaI, indicating that higher radiation recombination occurred in the GuaI-based perovskite film. Both the improved PL intensity and extended lifetime support that radiative recombination was enhanced and nonradiative recombination was restrained with the addition of  $  \mathrm{Gua^{+}}  $  cations into the perovskite lattice. Such has a positive effect on carrier transport and results in an increase in  $  V_{OC}  $  similar to previous reports (Chen and Park, 2019; Rajagopal et al., 2018). The surface roughness of the perovskite film without GuaI is  $  22.6~\mathrm{nm}  $  higher than that of the  $  10\%  $  GuaI-based film (15.6 nm), as shown in Fig. S2. The smoother surface of the Gual-based perovskite film is helpful for the deposition and interface contact of the HTL, resulting in better carrier transportation.

From Fig. 4a, it can be observed that the steady output current densities of PSCs with and without  $ 10\% $  GuaI at the maximum power point are  $ 21.4~\mathrm{mA / cm^2} $  and  $ 20.8~\mathrm{mA / cm^2} $ , respectively. The PCEs calculated by the steady output current densities are  $ 20.7\% $  (with GuaI) and  $ 18.7\% $  (without GuaI). These are similar to the values obtained from the JV curves and thus indicate the reliability of our results. As shown in Fig. 4b, the current density of PSCs without GuaI is one or more orders of magnitude higher than that of the GuaI-based device under dark conditions, indicating that current leakage of the GuaI-based PSC is inhibited. The lower dark current density implies that light-induced photocurrents could be collected efficiently rather than internally compounded, which is helpful for improving solar cell performance (Chen et al., 2016). Nyquist plots of PSCs with and without GuaI are shown in Fig. 4c; these were carried out under light conditions with a forward bias of  $ 0.8\mathrm{V} $ . The devices with GuaI exhibited a larger radius than those without GuaI, indicating that a larger Rrec (recombination resistance) and lower charge recombination occurred in the GuaI-based device. It can be assumed that the restrained charge recombination originated from perovskite bulk passivation by the  $ \mathrm{Gua^{+}} $  cations, which had a positive effect on improvement of open-circuit voltage. The Mott-Schottky (M-S) results (Fig. 4d) may provide strong support for the restrained charge recombination and improved  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $ . Based on the interception of the extrapolated  $ 1 / C^2 - V $  straight line with the V-axis, it can be determined that the built-in voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{bi}}) $  of the GuaI-based devices is  $ 1.25\mathrm{eV} $  higher than those without GuaI doping  $ (1.20\mathrm{eV}) $ . This larger  $ V_{\mathrm{bi}} $  will produce a larger built-in electric field in the GuaI-doped device, which is closely related to the larger  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $ . In conclusion, these results confirm that the improved performance of GuaI-based devices is due to the suppression of charge recombination and the enhancement of the built-in electric field. These improvements are related to the trap densities and the space-charge-limited currenttest which was executed

under dark conditions with the structure of ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /perovskite/PCBM/Ag. In Fig. S3, the trap density (Nt) is determined by the trap-filled limit voltage (V $ _{TFL} $ ) based on dark JV curves (Yin et al., 2018). The V $ _{TFL} $  value of the Gual-based devices is lower than that of pure PSCs, indicating fewer trap densities in the Gual-doped perovskite film. To further study recombination and trap density, a comparison of V $ _{OC} $  versus light intensity is shown in Fig. 4e for solar cells with and without GuaI under light conditions. From the linear fitting of V $ _{OC} $  and logarithm of light intensity, the slopes of PSCs with and without GuaI are 1.28 and 1.92  $ K_B T/q $ , respectively ( $ K_B $  is the Boltzmann constant,  $ T $  is the absolute temperature, and  $ q $  is the basic charge). The smaller slope of the GuaI-based device indicates that there are fewer traps in the Gual-based sample.

The long-term stability of PSCs with and without 10% Gual is tested at 25 °C and 25% humidity. The solar cells were tested for 35 days, neither were encapsulated shown in Fig. 5. The Gual-based devices exhibited better stability, with 97% of the original value which is higher than that without Gual (75%), indicating the enhanced long-term stability of the Gual-doped devices. The light soaking stability in air environment of unencapsulated devices with Gual is also better than that without Gual in Fig. S4a,b. Furthermore, with the adding of Gual perovskite solar cells exhibit the less hysteresis shown in Fig. S4c. The Gual helps to inhibit hysteresis by the better perovskite film quality.

# 3. Conclusions

In conclusion, triple-mixed cations in perovskite films can produce a positive effect on the optimisation of perovskite crystallisation with increasing grain size, decreasing trap densities, and restraining recombination. All of these promote the improvement of   $  V_{OC}  $  , FF, and PCE. The best solar cell had a high PCE of 21.3% with   $  V_{OC} = 1.14  $   V,   $  J_{SC} = 23.8  $   mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , FF = 0.78. The solar cells with the triple-mixed cations exhibited better apparent stabilities under the conditions of the light soaking and long-term storage environment. And, the devices with the adding of GuaI also exhibit the less hysteresis due to the improvement of the perovskite crystallisation. Here, the Gua  $  _{y}  $  (FAMA)  $  _{1-y}  $  PbI  $  _{x}  $  Cl  $  _{3-x}  $   perovskite is prepared by the two-step method, resulting in the improvement of stability and the PCE, thus proving the effectiveness of triple-mixed cations and also pointing out a simple way to further optimise PSCs.
