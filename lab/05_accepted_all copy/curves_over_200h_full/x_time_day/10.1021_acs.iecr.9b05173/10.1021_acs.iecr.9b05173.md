# Magnetite as Inorganic Hole Transport Material for Lead Halide Perovskite-Based Solar Cells with Enhanced Stability

Fatemeh Ansari, $ ^{\dagger} $  Masoud Salavati-Niasari, $ ^{*,\dagger} $  Omid Amiri, $ ^{\ddagger} $  Noshin Mir, $ ^{\S} $  Bahram Abdollahi Nejand, $ ^{\parallel} $  and Vahid Ahmadi $ ^{\perp} $

$  ^{\ddagger}  $  Department of Chemistry, College of Science, University of Raparin, Rania 46012, Kurdistan Region, Iraq

$  ^{§}  $  Department of Mechanical and Nuclear Engineering, Virginia Commonwealth University, Richmond, Virginia 23284, United States

$  ^{1}  $  Institute of Microstructure Technology, Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, 76344 Eggenstein-Leopoldshafen, Karlsruhe 76131, Germany

$  ^{1}  $  School of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Tarbiat Modares University, Tehran 14177-13116, Iran

ABSTRACT: A low-cost and stable perovskite solar cell using solution-processed nanostructured   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   as a potential hole transport layer (HTL) was reported for the first time. The Hall effect measurement performed on   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   film showing hole-type carriers with a mobility of   $  0.269\ cm^{2}\ V^{-1}\ s^{-1}  $   confirmed that   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   could be a potential hole-transporting material (HTM) candidate for perovskite solar cells. This value is higher than the reported one for other inorganic HTMs such as CuSCN (  $  0.01-0.1\ cm^{2}\ V^{-1}\ s^{-1}  $  ). The prepared device using   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   shows a maximum power conversion

efficiency (PCE) of  $ 15.42\% $  and long-term durability up to 30 days. This inorganic HTM is introduced as a strong and low-cost substitution to the expensive complex materials in the fabrication of perovskite solar cells. The device is more robust and stable in the moisture atmosphere in comparison to the fabricated devices using Spiro-OMeTAD as an organic HTM.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Recently, three-dimensional (3D) semiconductor perovskites with a general formula of ABX3, where A can be methylammonium   $  \left(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\right)  $   or formamidinium   $  \left(\mathrm{NH}=\mathrm{CHNH}_{3}\right)  $   ion  $  ^{1-3}  $   and cesium,  $  ^{4}  $   B is Pb or Sn, and X is a halogen ion such as I  $  ^{-}  $ , Br  $  ^{-}  $ , or Cl  $  ^{-}  $ ,  $  ^{5}  $   have attracted significant interest. Due to their great electrical and optical properties, they are potential candidates for different applications including solar cells,  $  ^{6,7}  $   light-emitting diodes (LEDs),  $  ^{8,9}  $   photodetectors,  $  ^{10,11}  $   and lasers.  $  ^{12,13}  $   Perovskites are considered as ideal photovoltaic materials in solar cell because of their broad light absorption,  $  ^{14,15}  $   low exciton binding energy (  $  \sim2  $   meV), direct band gap,  $  ^{16}  $   and long carrier lifetime (  $  \sim270  $   ns). In a typical device, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) employ a thin perovskite absorber layer (  $  \sim300  $   nm) between an electron transport layer (ETL) and a hole-transporting layer (HTL). For the majority of these devices, choosing an efficient electron transport material (ETM) and hole-transporting material (HTM) for extracting electrons and holes from absorber, respectively, has a high impact on PSC efficiency. TiO  $  _{2}  $ ,  $  ^{17,18}  $   ZnO,  $  ^{19}  $   and [6,6]-phenyl-C  $  _{61}  $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) have been used widely as an efficient electron-extracting layer.  $  ^{20}  $   The most commonly used HTM is 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-N,N-di-para-methoxyphenylamine-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD), which is a p-type material showing excellent properties that make it an ideal organic HTM for

deposition by spin coating.  $  ^{20}  $   However, the use of organic hole conductors such as Spiro-OMeTAD,  $  ^{21-23}  $   poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS),  $  ^{20,24}  $   poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P  $  _{3}  $  HT), and poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)-amine] (PTAA)  $  ^{25,26}  $   may represent a considerable ban in solar cells due to their high cost and low stability and durability. The low durability in organic HTM could be due to the presence of a hygroscopic lithium salt in many organic HTMs.  $  ^{27}  $   Additionally, using additive, 4-tert-butylpyridine,  $  ^{28}  $   causes degradation of perovskite structures. Also, the organic HTMs with conjugated systems can be degraded through exposing to oxygen and light.  $  ^{29}  $   Therefore, material selection and engineering for HTM is one of the significant challenges in perovskite solar cells to design appropriate, low-cost, and durable devices. In this regard, inorganic HTMs such as NiO,  $  ^{30,31}  $   CuI,  $  ^{32}  $     $  Cu_{2}O  $  ,  $  ^{33}  $   and CuSCN  $  ^{34}  $   were reported as alternatives to organic HTMs. Recently, Liu et al. have achieved 14.11% efficiency and high thermal stability by applying NiO as HTL.  $  ^{35}  $

The efficiency and durability of the prepared devices using   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   as an inorganic HTM with optimization of the film thickness are compared to those of the devices employing the conventional organic Spiro-OMeTAD HTM.

# 2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

2.1. Preparation of the Substrate and Mesostructure Layer. FTO-coated glass substrates are patterned by Zn powder and 2 M HCl solution etching. To prepare a   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer, 50   $  \mu  $  L of titanium di-isopropoxide bis-(acetylacetonate) (0.6 mL of titanium acetylacetonate in 8 mL of ethanol)  $  ^{46}  $   was sprayed on FTO substrates previously cleaned by a sequential sonication treatment in a 2% Hellmanex solution, acetone, and ethanol and was heated to 450 °C for 30 min. Then, a mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was prepared by spin-coating a diluted   $  TiO_{2}  $   dispersion in ethanol (1 g of   $  TiO_{2}/6  $   mL of ethanol) at 5000 rpm for 15 s followed by annealing at 450 °C for 30 min.

2.2. Deposition of Perovskite Layer. To deposit a uniform layer of perovskite on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , spin coating of a solution of  $ 1.4\mathrm{M} $  methylammonium iodide (MAI) and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) on the FTO/m- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  substrates at  $ 1000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 10~s $  and  $ 4000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 30~s $  was performed, and the deposited perovskite thin film underwent a sintering step at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 1\textrm{h} $ . The process of preparation of the solution of perovskite was under argon and deposition of the perovskite was under nitrogen.

2.3. Synthesis and Deposition of   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   Layer. For deposition of   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   electron barrier layer on the as-prepared perovskite layer, the   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   nanoparticles were synthesized by the ultrasonic approach.  $  ^{47,48}  $   In a typical synthesis,   $  FeCl_{2}  $ ,   $  FeCl_{3}  $ , and ethylenediamine (  $  C_{2}H_{8}N_{2}  $  ) (1 M) were mixed in a 1:2 proportion of   $  Fe^{2+}/Fe^{3+}  $   in deionized (DI) water under ultrasonic waves (60 W) for 5 min under nitrogen atmosphere. Appearance of a black precipitate confirmed the synthesis of   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $ . The synthesized   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   nanoparticles were dispersed in 2-propanol (30 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) and were spin-coated on the sintered perovskite layer at 3500 rpm for 30 s. For fabrication of the control cell, the conventional HTM was deposited via spin-coating 72 mg of Spiro-OMeTAD dissolved in 1 mL of chlorobenzene, with two additives, including 17.5   $  \mu  $  L of lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (0.0184 M) and 28   $  \mu  $  L of 4-tert-butylpyridine (0.0659 M). Spin coating was carried out at 4000 rpm for 20 s. Au electrodes were thermally evaporated on both devices as the final step.

2.4. Thin-Film and Device Characterization. A solar simulator (AM1.5G, 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , Sharif solar simulator, Iran) was used to illuminate the solar cell under ambient temperature and air conditions (humidity, ca. 20%). The light source was a 450 W xenon lamp (Oriel) equipped with a SchottK113 Tempax sunlight filter (Praezisions Glas & OptikGmbH) to match the emission spectrum of the lamp to the AM1.5G. Before each measurement, the exact light intensity was determined using a calibrated Si reference diode equipped with an infrared cutoff filter (KG-3, Schott). Current–voltage measurement was performed by applying an external potential bias to the cell while recording the generated photocurrent with a digital source meter (Keithley Model 2400), without device preconditioning. All measurements were conducted using a nonreflective metal mask with an aperture area of 0.16 cm  $  ^{2}  $   to define the active area (0.4 cm × 0.4 cm = 0.16 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) of the device and to avoid light scattering through the sides.

The microstructure and morphology of the deposited films, field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM, S416 Hitachi), and atomic force microscopy (AFM, Veeco PCResearch) were used. The phase structure and crystal size of films were also investigated by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Philips Expert-MPD). The XRD was performed in the   $  \theta-2\theta  $

Based on the FE-SEM results (Figure 5), when only one layer of   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   nanoparticles is coated, the perovskite surface is not uniformly covered. As a result, due to considerable recombination in the interfaces of Au and perovskite layer, the fill factor (FF) and open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) are low (FF: 0.51,   $  V_{OC}  $  : 0.968 V) although a high short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) is obtained (  $  J_{SC}  $  : 20.3 mA). As no treatment has been conducted after deposition of   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   thin film, there will be a high carrier-transporting resistance due to a higher amount of grain boundaries in   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $  .

Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) quenching experiments of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film with   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   or Spiro-OMeTAD as HTMs are conducted to investigate the efficiency of charge extraction of each studied HTL. In a PL experiment, high-intensity peaks show that the recombination of electrons and holes has occurred. However, the quenched PL peaks could be attributed to the reduced recombination probability in the interface of perovskite and the HTL layer. As illustrated in Figure 7b, the   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   film exhibits more efficient PL quenching than Spiro-OMeTAD, suggesting its better hole extraction capability from perovskite absorber. This shows that   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   has this potential to be used as an effective HTL layer; however, the lower   $  J_{SC}  $   of the device with   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   compared to that of the Spiro-OMeTAD-based cell shows that more studies are required to evaluate the effect of the grain boundaries and defects present in the HTL on final charge collection.

To estimate the electrical properties of the introduced HTM, Hall effect measurement was performed on nanostructured   $  Fe_{3}O_{4}  $   film spin-coated on nonconductive glass, and the results are summarized in Table 2. # 4. CONCLUSIONS

$ \mathrm{Fe}_3\mathrm{O}_4 $  MNPs have been demonstrated as a good, efficient, and durable HTM in optoelectronic applications such as perovskite solar cells compared to conventional organic HTMs like Spiro-OMeTAD and common inorganic HTMs like CuSCN and NiO. Interesting optoelectronic performances are achieved by a room-temperature deposition of  $ \mathrm{Fe}_3\mathrm{O}_4 $  on the perovskite layer as an inorganic HTM. The prepared devices show high durability and PCE, which makes them outstanding in the fabrication of low-cost, durable, and efficient perovskite solar cells.
