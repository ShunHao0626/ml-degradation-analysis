# SUMMARY

The electron-transporting layer (ETL) in perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is playing a vital role in improving the power-conversion efficiency (PCE). To date, the certified highest 23.2% efficiency has been reported only for normal (n-i-p) type PSCs. Therefore, in the present investigation the high-quality Ga:TiO  $  _{2}  $  compact layer ETLs (c-Ga:TiO  $  _{2}  $ ) are prepared by simple spin-coating technique and the influence of Ga cation doping in c-TiO  $  _{2}  $  ETL is studied. Systematic study in this work revealed that the Ga cation doping significantly influences the current density, open-circuit voltage (V  $  _{OC}  $ ), and fill factor (FF), and reduces the charge recombination of the perovskite devices. Optimized devices exhibit a PCE >19.57% ±0.5% with >200 days of air stability for 1% Ga doping. The final efficiency of the champion cell independently certified at the KITECH is 18.89% with V  $  _{OC}  $  of 1.138 V, FF of 78.54%, and short-circuit current density of 21.72 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ .

# INTRODUCTION

Organometal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) represent the most promising low-cost photovoltaic technology to arouse significant interest due to their competitive power-conversion efficiency (PCE) with Si-solar cells.  $  ^{1,2}  $   To date, certified peak efficiency of 23.2% for n-i-p and 26.3% for perovskite-silicon tandem solar cells has been demonstrated.  $  ^{3-5}  $   This champion 22.1% PCE is achieved through different compositions of MAPbX/FAPbX (herein MAPbX stands for methylammonium lead halide and FA stands for formamidinium lead halide), mixing of a variety of cations such as Rbl and CsI, and using different hole-extraction layers (herein HTMs, HTLs, or HELs). PSCs are classified into different types based on their device architecture, namely, conventional, planar, mesoscopic, and inverted, and on their electron- and hole-transporting layer position. However, to date the highest certified efficiency has been demonstrated only for the regular mesoscopic type. Therefore, researchers worldwide are interested in regular-type mesoscopic (n-i-p) device architecture. Among the various electron-transporting layers (ETLs), thus far compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   (c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) and mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   (mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) ETLs have shown certified efficiency. In addition, c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   can be used for both planar and mesoscopic types. However, the   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer still suffers from defect trap levels, which hamper electron transport that simultaneously affects the charge-collection efficiency.

The electron mobility can be improved by suitable metal doping. Therefore, various metals such as Mg, $ ^{6} $  Sn, $ ^{7} $  Nb, $ ^{8} $  Ta, $ ^{9} $  Al, $ ^{10} $  Li, $ ^{11} $  graphene, $ ^{12} $  and Au $ ^{13} $  have been used as an effective dopant for TiO $ _{2} $  ETL. # Progress and Potential

The perovskite solar cell (PSCs) is a promising low-cost photovoltaic technology to compete with conventional Si photovoltaics. The photovoltaic parameters and its stability are determined by the type of electron- and hole-transporting materials, perovskite composition, and deposition method. However, the electron-transporting layer (ETL) with excellent electronic properties is the key parameter in improving performance. Therefore, the justification behind this work is mainly the search for alternative ETL for normal-type n-i-p PSCs, because of the interesting excellent electronic properties of gallium-doped  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL followed by deposition of highly uniform  $ (\mathrm{FAPbI}_3)_{0.85}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_3)_{0.15} $  perovskite thin-film deposition via a solvent-engineering process. Adopting this approach with best composition, we reported state-of-the-art power-conversion efficiency  $ >19.5\% $  with  $ >95\% $  stable performance even after 200 days.

Recently, Ru doping has been used for   $  c-TiO_{2}  $   with 15.7% PCE for planar n-i-p architecture.  $  ^{[15]}  $   However, to date Ga doping in   $  c-TiO_{2}  $   has not been studied. Abate et al. demonstrated the Ga is another potential candidate for   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETLs for UV stable PSCs with 16.4% PCE.  $  ^{[16]}  $   These authors suggested that   $  Ga^{3+}  $   cation doping plays a key role in decreasing the trap-state density in   $  SnO_{2}  $ . Liu et al. implemented Ga in compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   by a low-temperature process and used it for planar n-i-p PSCs to reduce the trap states in   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL film with 16.51% efficiency.  $  ^{[17]}  $   Umar et al. demonstrated that nano-diatom-like   $  Ga-TiO_{2}  $   can be used as an efficient ETL for planar n-i-p PSCs with 6.58% efficiency.  $  ^{[18]}  $   Although both studies suggested that Ga doping in   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL for PSCs may help to improve the charge transport by reducing surface traps, there are currently no reports of   $  Ga:TiO_{2}  $   ETL being used to improve state-of-the-art mesoscopic PSCs. In addition, this particular trivalent   $  Ga^{3+}  $   dopant has attracted much attention due to its non-changeable optical properties of   $  TiO_{2}  $   phase and improved photoelectric properties.  $  ^{[19,20]}  $   Chandiran et al. used a   $  Ga_{2}O_{3}  $   subnanometer tunneling layer by atomic layer deposition (ALD) onto mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   and used these   $  Ga_{2}O_{3}@mp-TiO_{2}  $   modified electrodes for dye-sensitized solar cell application with 4% PCE and highest recorded open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.098 V, although the thickness of   $  Ga_{2}O_{3}  $   had a substantial effect on   $  V_{OC}  $   and extended up it to 1.118 V, which results from reduced charge recombination at the short-circuit.  $  ^{[21]}  $   On the same topic, recently Ma et al., used   $  Ga_{2}O_{3}  $   as a tunneling nanolayer into the interface between the metal electrode and electron-transport passivation layer by ALD for p-i-n type PSCs and demonstrated 19.86% PCE.  $  ^{[22]}  $   This   $  Ga_{2}O_{3}  $   acts as an outstanding dense permeation barrier to suppress the carrier recombination and hinder contact metal diffusion. Therefore,   $  Ga_{2}O_{3}  $   played a key role in the device performance as well as the substantial stability improvement. However, to our best knowledge, Ga doping in   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer ETL has not yet been studied for mesoscopic PSCs. Regarding the crystal structure of   $  TiO_{2}  $ , the replacement of   $  Ti^{4+}  $   with any cation is relatively easier than substituting   $  O^{2-}  $   with any other anion due to the difference in the charge states and ionic radii. In this investigation, we have studied for the first time the influence of Ga doping in c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   for PSCs while maintaining the regular mesoscopic n-i-p device architecture. These trivalent   $  Ga^{3+}  $   ions can occupy the tetravalent   $  Ti^{4+}  $   ions by producing oxygen vacancies. Besides, these oxygen vacancies that stem from   $  Ga^{3+}  $   ions are positively charged due to the charge balance of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   system. This system facilitates effective hole blocking and reduces the recombination rate, effectively yielding a fill factor (FF) of >0.78 and   $  V_{OC}  $   of >1.146 V. We optimized the Ga precursor concentration to produce hysteresis-less efficient (  $  FAPbI_{3})_{0.85}(MAPbBr_{3})_{0.15}  $   PSC devices having a champion PCE of 19.57% with better stability. This enhancement was attributed to the increased conductivity of the c-  $  Ga:TiO_{2}  $   ETL. Furthermore, we showed that   $  Ga:TiO_{2}  $   has much higher stability compared with conventional c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL.

Next, we investigated the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) measurements to estimate the electron trap-state density and charge-carrier mobility in the perovskite films by recording the current-voltage (I-V) characteristics of electron-only devices.  $  ^{[28,29]}  $   In the present investigation, we used the electron-only single-carrier device having FTO/c-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /perovskite/PCBM/Ag and FTO/c-Ga:TiO  $  _{2}  $  /perovskite/PCBM/Ag device architectures. The curves of current values versus electric fields in logarithmic scale from single-carrier devices are shown in Figure S5. It is clear that the I-V plot is divided into three different regions, namely linear ohmic region, trap-filled region, and SCLC regime. The nature of the ohmic contact region illustrates perfect interfaces between ETL and perovskite. Therefore, when trap density states are negligible, this interface is capable of injecting enough charges from electrode to semiconducting materials to SCLC. The trap-filled limit voltage (V  $  _{TFL}  $  ) can be determined by the following equation:  $  ^{[30]}  $

$$
V _ {\mathrm{TFL}} = \frac {e n _ {\mathrm{t}} L ^ {2}}{2 \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0}},
$$

(Equation 3)

where e is the elementary charge of the electron,   $  n_{t}  $   is the trap-state density, L is the thickness of the perovskite film,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative dielectric constant of perovskite, and   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity. Here, we used   $  \varepsilon = 28.8  $   for   $  (\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3})_{0.85}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})_{0.15}  $   composition.  $  ^{31-35}  $   From Figure S4, we calculated the   $  V_{TFL}  $   values of mixed-halide perovskite based on c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   and c-Ga:  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs as 0.465 and 0.450 V, respectively. From the above logarithmic I-V analysis, the reduced calculated trap densities (  $  n_{t}  $  ) from   $  2.97 \times 10^{15} cm^{-3}  $   to   $  2.86 \times 10^{15} cm^{-3}  $   revealed the improved charge-extraction ability of c-Ga:  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs.

The hysteresis behavior of fabricated devices was carried out with a 50-ms scanning delay (Figure 3D). The champion cell exhibits PCE values of 19.58% (J  $  _{SC}  $  = 21.88 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , V  $  _{OC}  $  = 1.139 V, FF = 0.79) and 18.72% (J  $  _{SC}  $  = 21.86 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ , V  $  _{OC}  $  = 1.138 V, FF = 0.75), while the reference cell exhibited 15.89% and 15.26% for the reverse and forward scans, respectively, indicating negligible hysteresis compared with c-TiO  $  _{2}  $  ETL. \mathrm{HI} = \frac {\int \int_ {\mathrm{SC}} ^ {\mathrm{OC}} \left(J _ {\text {Reverse Scan}} (V) - J _ {\text {Forward Scan}} (V)\right) \mathrm{d} V}{\int_ {\mathrm{SC}} ^ {\mathrm{OC}} J _ {\text {Reverse Scan}} (V) \mathrm{d} V}
$$

(Equation 4)

Here,  $ \int_{\mathrm{SC}}^{\mathrm{OC}}J_{\mathrm{Forward}\mathrm{Scan}}(V)\mathrm{d}V $  and  $ \int_{\mathrm{SC}}^{\mathrm{OC}}J_{\mathrm{Reverse}\mathrm{Scan}}(V)\mathrm{d}V $  are the area under the  $ J - V $  curves measured under illumination from forward scan and reverse scan, respectively.

Our results revealed that the HI value of the c-Ga:TiO  $  _{2}  $  -based device is 2-fold smaller than conventional c-TiO  $  _{2}  $   based ETL (Table 3). The 1% c-Ga:TiO  $  _{2}  $  -based device achieved slightly higher EQE values than reference cell. This negligible difference arises due to low PCE intensity during measurements.

# DISCUSSION

We have developed a   $  Ga^{3+}  $   cation-doped modified   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL without compromising optical transmittance for PSCs that can reduce the optical loss. The substitution of a   $  Ga^{3+}  $   cations into   $  Ti^{4+}  $   sites creates an oxygen vacancy, which serves as an electron trap and effectively enhances the lifetime analyzed for chemical analysis. The developed Ga:  $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs are highly uniform and pinhole free, and can effectively improve the   $  V_{OC}  $   and FF of PSC devices. Optimization of the Ga doping level will improve the PCE from 15% to >19% with better air stability. The c-Ga:  $  TiO_{2}  $   EEL-based PSCs show good stability over 100 days and retain >95% PCE even after 200 days. Therefore, we suggest the developed method as a promising approach to improve the
