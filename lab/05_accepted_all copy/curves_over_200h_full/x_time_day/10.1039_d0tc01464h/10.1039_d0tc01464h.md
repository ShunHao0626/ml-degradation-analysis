# An Efficient and Stable Tin-Based Perovskite Solar Cells Passivated by Aminoguanidine Hydrochloride

Qingxia Fu $  ^{a}  $ , Xianglan Tang $  ^{a}  $ , Dengxue Li $  ^{a,b}  $ , Lu Huang $  ^{a}  $ , Shuqin Xiao $  ^{a,b}  $ , Yiwang Chen $  ^{a,c}  $ , Ting Hu $  ^{*a,b}  $

It is challenging to simultaneously fabricate high-performance and stable Sn-based perovskite device owing to the inferior Sn-based perovskite film and the fast oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $  . Herein, a simple approach was employed by adding an additive of aminoguanidine hydrochloride (  $  NH_{2}GACI  $  ) in perovskite precursor solution to prepare efficient Sn-based perovskite solar cells. The higher film quality was obtained due to the formation of strong hydrogen bonding between halide ions in the perovskite and   $  NH_{2}GACI  $  , which helps to passivate the defects and lessen   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation. In addition,   $  NH_{2}GACI  $   addition can effectively adjust the energy level alignment between Sn-based perovskite and adjacent layer to facilitate the charge transportation. The perovskite device modified with   $  NH_{2}GACI  $   achieved a power conversion efficiency up to 7.3%, retaining 90% of its initial efficiency after 30 days in a nitrogen glovebox without encapsulation.

# Introduction

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) have become research hotspot because of their remarkable power conversion efficiency (PCE), low cost and facile fabrication processes in the past few years  $ ^{1,2} $ . Recently, the lead (Pb)-based PVSCs delivered the recorded PCE of over 25.2%. However, the toxicity of Pb severely prohibits commercial applications of PVSCs, which impels to develop less Pb or Pb-free based PVSCs $ ^{3,4} $ . Other environmental friendly elements, such as Sn, Ge, Bi, Sb, etc have been proposed to replace  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{5-12} $ . Among them, Sn-based perovskite is supposed to be a potential candidate with narrower optical bandgaps and higher charge-carrier mobilities than that of Pb-based perovskite $ ^{13,14} $ . Unfortunately, it is challenging to fabricate compact and well-crystalline Sn-based perovskite film $ ^{15} $ . In addition, Sn-based PVSCs suffer from the fast oxidation of  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  to  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{4+} $  upon air exposure or even in glove box with a trace amount of oxygen, resulting in Sn vacancies in the perovskite lattice $ ^{16} $ . For these reasons, Sn-based PVSCs exhibit inferior device photovoltaic performance (especially low open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{OC} $ ) and poor environmental stability) $ ^{17} $ . In an attempt to improve the PCE and stability, extensive methods have been directed to passivate the defects of the Sn-perovskite films. More recently, some organic molecules with  $ -\mathrm{NH}_2 $  functional group, such as butylammonium ( $ \mathrm{BA}^+ $ ) $ ^{18} $  and phenylethylammonium ( $ \mathrm{PEA}^+ $ ) $ ^{19} $ , guanidinium ( $ \mathrm{GA}^+ $ ) $ ^{20} $ , 5-ammoniumvaleric acid ( $ 5-\mathrm{AVA}^+ $ ) $ ^{21} $  have been also exploited to optimize the perovskite device performance. For instance, PEAI could simultaneously improve the crystallinity of perovskite films

and reduce   $  Sn^{4+}  $   content with an efficiency of up to 9.0%  $  ^{19}  $  . Furthermore,   $  GA^{+}  $  was incorporated in formamidinium tin triiodide (  $  FASnI_{3}  $  ) perovskite to adjust the energy levels of perovskite layer, achieving a record PCE of 9.6% with superior stability  $  ^{20}  $  . In addition to ammonium cations, chlorine anion has a positive influence on the morphological evolution of the perovskite films  $  ^{21-23}  $  . Until now, GACI has been employed in Pb-based PVSCs as additive to optimize the quality of perovskite film  $  ^{21}  $  , but there is no report about the application of GACI in Sn-based PVSCs.

In this work, aminoguanidine hydrochloride (NH $ _2 $ GACl) additive was induced in the Sn-based perovskite. The GA cation has been reported as a cross-linker between neighbouring perovskite grains due to the presence of ammonium groups $ ^{24} $ . Bulky organic cations with multiple ammonium functional groups could form more strong interaction with the Sn-I frameworks and suppress the formation of pinholes during film fabrication process $ ^{20} $ . Hence, NH $ _2 $ GACl effectively produce a passivation effect on the perovskite by forming hydrogen bonding with perovskite, which could reduce defects states in the Sn-perovskite film and retard the oxidation of Sn $ ^{2+} $ . In this case, charge transportation was improved, resulting in an increased PCE and better stability, which will be demonstrated and explained in the following content.

Besides the crystallinity, similar improvement was predicted in the PL measurement. The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra and time-resolved photoluminescence (Tr-PL) decay tests of the perovskite films were implemented on glass to further verify perovskite film quality and excited-state property. In Figure 2a, the

perovskite films with   $  NH_{2}GACI  $   showed stronger PL peak intensity than that of pristine film, which resulted from the less pinhole and higher quality in the modified perovskite film. The pristine perovskite film exhibited a short carrier lifetime of 8.15 ns (Figure 2b). By comparison, the perovskite films with   $  NH_{2}GACI  $   present longer carrier lifetime of 12.06 ns, which could reduce the carriers recombination and facilitate charge transportation  $  ^{21, 28}  $  . In addition, the surface potential has been measured by using Kelvin-probe force microscopy (KPFM) to prove the effects of   $  NH_{2}GACI  $   on surface defects passivation of perovskite films  $  ^{29}  $  . As shown in Figure 2c, the perovskite films modified with   $  NH_{2}GACI  $   (17.90 mV) present higher surface potential than that of pristine film (8.29 mV), indicating the reduced surface defects and enhanced hole transportation, which further lessen the opportunity of the recombination. To further investigate charge-carrier mobility and trap-state density of the perovskite films by   $  NH_{2}GACI  $   passivation, the hole mobility was measured by using the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) model (Figure 2d). The Mott-Gurney law J = 9/8εε₀μV²/L³ was used to estimate the mobility.  $  ^{30}  $   By calculation, the hole mobility of   $  NH_{2}GACI  $   modified perovskite device was 3.42 × 10⁻³ cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹, which was higher than that of the pristine device (2.52 × 10⁻³ cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹). Moreover, the following equation was applied to calculate the trap-state density  $  ^{26}  $  .

$$
\mathrm{N} _ {t} = \frac {2 \varepsilon_ {0} \varepsilon_ {r} \mathrm{V} _ {T F L}}{q L ^ {2}}
$$

In this formula,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity.   $  \varepsilon_{r}  $   is the relative dielectric constant of perovskite film (  $  \sim34  $  , determined by the impedance spectroscopy).   $  V_{TFL}  $   is the onset voltage of the trap-filled limit region. q is the elemental charge. L is the thickness of the perovskite film. The calculated trap densities decreased from   $  7.12 \times 10^{16} \, cm^{-3}  $   to   $  6.11 \times 10^{16} \, cm^{-3}  $   in perovskite layer after   $  NH_{2}GACl  $   being induced. The increased charge carrier mobility and reduced trap state density was mainly attributed to the passivation effect of   $  NH_{2}GACl  $   molecular, which helps to decrease charge recombination and increase FF.

To probe the interaction between   $  NH_{2}GACl  $   and perovskite,   $  {}^{1}H  $   NMR measurements of   $  NH_{2}GACl  $ , perovskite and   $  NH_{2}GACl  $ -perovskite mixture solutions in deuterated N, N-dimethylformamided  $  _{7}  $   (DMF-  $  d_{7}  $  ) solvent were performed (Figure S7). Compared with the chloride ions, the iodide anions with lower electronegativity can inductively extract less electron density out of the protons, thus leading to an upfield shift in the resonance signals.  $  ^{35}  $   Consequently, the N-H hydrogen signal underwent an upfield shift of 0.42 ppm, suggesting that the hydrogen-bonding interaction between this ammonium end group and halide ions has changed from N-H/Cl  $  ^{-}  $  to N-H/I  $  ^{-}  $ . Through this interaction, the passivation effect of   $  NH_{2}GACl  $   molecular was realized to enhance the morphology and the stability of the perovskite film.

Moreover, the energy-level diagram of PVSCs was displayed in Figure 4b. The valence band maximum (VBM) were determined on analysis of the ultraviolet photoelectron spectrometer (UPS) measurements (Figure S8a), and the conduction band (CB) were derived on the VBM with the corresponding optical bandgap. The optical bandgap was calculated from the absorption spectra in Figure S8b. As shown in Figure 4b, it was obviously observed that the perovskite films with 0.05 mol NH₂GACI addition have more favorable energy level alignment with the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level of PEDOT:PSS, which was expected to reduce the energy barrier at the PEDOT:PSS/perovskite interface and thus suppress interface recombination.  $  ^{5, 36}  $   Finally, the J-V curves of the PVSCs with different concentrations of NH₂GACI were demonstrated (Figure 4c) and the corresponding parameters were illustrated in Table S1. The pristine PVSCs showed a low PCE of 4.92%. In contrast, the best PCE was improved to 7.30% with higher   $  J_{SC}  $   and   $  V_{OC}  $   value upon adding NH₂GACI (0.05 mol) (Device active area is 0.04 cm².). The increased   $  J_{SC}  $   stem from the morphological enhancement in the perovskite film with fewer Sn vacancy defects and better charge transport property. Moreover, the increased   $  V_{OC}  $   value was attributed to the favorable energy level alignments between the hole transportation and perovskite layer  $  ^{37}  $  . However, the electron transporting layer (PCBM) is probably not the best electron transport layer with imperfect Lowest Unoccupied Molecular Orbital (LUMO) energy level, resulting in the low   $  V_{OC}  $    $  ^{38}  $  . Therefore, more suitable electron transport layer with shallower energy level can be selected to improve   $  V_{OC}  $ , for example, indene-C60 bisadduct (ICBA), which brings a larger maximum attainable voltage and suppresses iodide remote doping that reduces interface carriers recombination. The PCE dropped rapidly with addition of excess NH₂GACI (0.1 mol), which was ascribed to the unfavorable morphology of perovskite films. So far, the photovoltaic performance of the Sn-perovskite solar cells was relatively low due to the poor quality of Sn-perovskite films and the oxidation of Sn²⁺. Therefore, all the present studies are aimed at small area device (Table S2). Moreover, we have tried fabricated device with larger area. However, it is very difficult to prepare high-quality tin-based perovskite film on a large scale, resulting in lower photovoltaic performance and poor repeatability.

Finally, 100 nm Ag was deposited by thermal evaporation to complete the device.

Characterization: Keithley 2400 was used to characterize the current density-voltage (J-V) curves. The currents were measured under a solar simulator (Enli Tech,  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $ , AM 1.5 G irradiation). The light intensity was calibrated by means of a KG-5 Si diode with a solar simulator (Enli Tech, Taiwan). Devices were stored and tested in the nitrogen-filled glovebox. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (SU8020) and atomic force microscopy (AFM) (MultiMode 8-HR (Bruker)) images were recorded to test the morphology of perovskite films. X-ray diffraction (XRD) spectra were carried out by using X-ray diffractometer (Rigaku D/Max-B). The ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) spectra were characterized on UV-2600 spectrophotometer (SHIMADZU). The photoluminescence (PL) spectra and Time-resolved photoluminescence (Tr-PL) spectra were measured by an Edinburgh instruments FLS920 spectrometer. Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) was taken by nanoscope multimode (Bruker). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were performed on a ESCALAB 250 XPS with Al (Ka) X-ray source (1,486.6 eV). Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) were collected on Thermo Fisher Scientific with He I (21.22 eV) emission line. All the experiments of Sn-perovskite films without encapsulation were done under ambient conditions.

# Conclusions

In summary,   $  NH_{2}GACI  $   was introduced into the Sn-based perovskite as additive to improve device performance and long-term stability successfully. The incorporation of   $  NH_{2}GACI  $   not only efficiently improved the quality of perovskite film with decreased trap density and carriers recombination but also significantly tuned energy level of perovskite layer to facilitate the charge transportation. In addition,   $  NH_{2}GACI  $   retarded the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   by suppressing formation of Sn vacancies. As a result, the PCE of PVSCs was considerably promoted from 4.72% to 7.3% and higher stability was obtained with 90% PCE retained after being stored in a glove box for 30 days without encapsulation. Hence, this work provides a simple route for the fabrication of efficient Sn based PVSCs.
