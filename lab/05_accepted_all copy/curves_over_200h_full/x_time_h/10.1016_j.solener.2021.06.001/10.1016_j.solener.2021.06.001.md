# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are promising photovoltaic materials that have attracted the attention of a wide range of researchers. The certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) has increased from  $ 3.8\% $  to  $ 25.5\% $  in the past few years (Kojima et al., 2009, National Renewable Energy Laboratory, Research Cell Record Efficiency Chart). This is related to many advantages of perovskite materials, such as long carrier life (Sun et al., 2018), high absorption coefficient and adjustable band gap (Sun et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2019), etc. Although perovskite materials have high defect tolerance, it is still difficult to reach the theoretical limit efficiency (Li et al., 2018). High-quality films are essential for achieving efficient and stable PSCs. There are some defects in the perovskite light-absorbing layer, which hinder the device performance further improvement, such as unbalanced carrier transport and serious carrier recombination (Snaith, 2018; Edri et al., 2014; Wolff et al., 2019; Xiang Yan; Zhuang Jia; Ma Zhu; Lu Honglin; Xia Haoran; Zhou Weiya; Zhang Tao; Li Haimin, Mixed-phase Mesoporous TiO2 Film for High Efficiency Perovskite Solar Cells, CHEMICAL RESEARCH IN CHINESE UNIVERSITIES, 2019). The defects of grain boundaries and film surface are harmful to the efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Defect passivation is an effective strategy to improve the performance of PSCs. In this work, a silane coupling agent 3-aminopropyltrimethoxysilane (APMS) was used as an additive of perovskite light-absorbing layer to passivate defects, thereby significantly improving charge transport, reducing charge recombination and improving device performance. The amino group in APMS can not only form a coordinate bond with the uncoordinated Pb in the perovskite, but also form a hydrogen bond with I (N-H…I). The crystallinity and uniformity of perovskite grains were improved obviously. Therefore, the addition of APMS greatly reduces the defects of perovskite and the power conversion efficiency (PCE) increased from 18.85% to 20.72% with less hysteresis. Furthermore, a proper carbon chain can be hydrophobic to improve the moisture stability of the perovskite. The PCE of the device remains 60% of the initial device after being placed at a humidity of 50–60% for 400 h. Therefore, this work proves that the use of silane coupling agents as additives is a promising strategy to achieve efficiency and stable PSCs.

Many studies have shown that solvent engineering (Yi et al., 2020), interface engineering (Jeon et al., 2014), and additives (You et al., 2016; Kim et al., 2016) are beneficial to improve PSCs performance. The amino groups coordinate with  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  ions or form hydrogen bonds with iodine ions to reduce defects. Amino-containing silanes have been widely used by researchers in solar cells to improve performance. Lao et al. used 3-aminopropyltrimethoxysilane (APTS) to modify photoelectrodes to prepare dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSC). In this paper, a cheap silane coupling agent, 3-aminopropyltrimethoxysilane (APMS) was selected for the perovskite light absorption layer. This is the first time that APMS has been used in the  $ \mathrm{Cs}_{0.05} $  ( $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.85} $   $ \mathrm{MA}_{0.15]0.95} $  Pb ( $ \mathrm{I}_{0.85}\mathrm{Br}_{0.15})_3 $  mixed-cation perovskite active layer. Due to APMS contains a hydrophobic carbon chain, it can prevent water from invading the perovskite. In addition, the  $ -\mathrm{NH}_2 $  group interacts with Pb or I ions to inhibit ion migration and passivate defects (Yang et al., 2016; Li et al., 2015). The addition of APMS enhances the crystallinity and increases grain size of perovskite film. Furthermore, the passivation of APMS effectively reduces the defect density and improves the carrier lifetime of PSCs. As a result, the PCE increased from  $ 18.85\% $  to  $ 20.72\% $  and the open circuit voltage increased from  $ 1.121\mathrm{V} $  to  $ 1.150\mathrm{V} $ . Furthermore, the cross-linked device reduces hysteresis and enhances stability. We provide a simple and effective strategy to achieve efficient and stable PSCs, which is of great significance.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

3-Aminopropyltrimethoxysilane (APMS, 99.5%) was purchased from Aladdin. The SnO₂ solution was purchased from Alfa Aesar (15% in H₂O). Lead iodide (PbI₂, 99.99%), bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)-imidelithium salt (Li-TFSI), 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP), formamidinium iodide (FAI, 99.5%), methylammonium bromine (MABr, 99.5%), lead bromine (PbBr₂, 99.99%), 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-4-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD, 99.8%) and cesium iodide (CsI, 99.9%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Chlorobenzene (CB, 99.9%), dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.9%), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.9%) and acetonitrile (99.9%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich.

# 2.2. Perovskite solar cell fabrication

Firstly, FTO (NSG, TEC-6–8   $  \Omega  $   sq  $  ^{-1}  $  ) ultrasonic treated for 15 min with cleaning solution, deionized water, acetone, absolute ethanol in sequence and UV ozone for 15 min. The SnO  $  _{2}  $   solution was mixed with water in a ratio of 1:5, then spin-coated on the FTO substrate at 3000 rpm for 30 s, and annealed at 150 °C for 30 min. Then UV ozone for 15 min and transfer to a glove box filled with N  $  _{2}  $  . Subsequently, the mixed perovskite were prepared by dissolving 507 mg PbI  $  _{2}  $  , 172 mg FAI, 22.4

mg MABr and 81.18 mg PbBr  $  _{2}  $  in 1 mL mixture of DMF and DMSO (4:1, volume ratio), and then 50  $  \mu  $  L CsI (1.5 M) was added to perovskite precursor solution. The perovskite precursor solution containing different amounts of APMS (0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 mg/mL) was spin-coated on SnO  $  _{2}  $  with a two-step procedure of 1000 rpm for 10 s and 5000 rpm for 30 s. During the process, 120  $  \mu  $  L chlorobenzene was dripped onto the perovskite film when the end of the program at 10 s, and immediately annealed at 100 °C for 45 min. After cooling to room temperature, the hole transport layer was spin-coated onto the perovskite surface for 30 s at 4000 rpm by dissolving 72.3 mg Spiro-OMeTAD, 29  $  \mu  $  L of tBP and 17.5  $  \mu  $  L of Li-TFSI stock solution (520 mg Li-TFSI in 1 mL acetonitrile) in 1 mL chlorobenzene. Finally, 90 nm Ag electrode was deposited by a magnetron sputtering deposition system (JGP-450A, Shengyang) through a mask to define the effective active area of the device (0.08 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ).

# 2.3. Characterization

The crystallinity of the film was tested with an X-ray diffractometer (XRD, DX-2700, Dandong). The surface morphology of the films were tested by scanning electron microscope (SEM, Germany ZEISS EV0MA15) and atomic force microscopy (AFM, Keysight 7500). The absorption of the film was tested with an UV–Vis spectrophotometer. (UV-2600, SHIMADZU). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, KRA-TOS, AXIS Ultra DLD) analysis was used to characterize the composition of the perovskite. FTIR spectra were measured by Nicolet 6700. The PL and TRPL spectra were obtained by a FLS980 (Edinburgh Instruments). The photocurrent density–voltage (J-V) curve of the PSCs were tested using a solar simulator (Zolix SS150) under a standard 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  simulated AM 1.5 G irradiation with an electrochemical workstation (CHI660D). The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectrum of PSCs were obtained by the EQE measurement system (PVE 300, Bentham, Inc).

In order to analysis the trap state density of the perovskite film, the dark current–voltage (J-V) curve based on the space charge limited current (SCLC) model with the FTO/SnO  $  _{2}  $  /Perovskite/PCBM/Ag device structure were measured as shown in Fig. 6a-b. At low bias voltages, the current and voltage are linearly related, indicating an ohmic response. As the bias voltage in the middle region increases, the injection current increases significantly, indicating that the trap state is completely filled. The bending point between these two areas is defined as the trap filling limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ), and the calculation formula of the trap density (  $  N_{d}  $  ) is as follows (Liu et al., 2019):

$$
V _ {T F L} = \frac {e N _ {d} L ^ {2}}{2 \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0}}
$$

Where  $  e  $  presents the elementary charge,  $  L  $  is the thickness of the perovskite layer,  $  \varepsilon_0  $  is the vacuum permittivity,  $  \varepsilon  $  is the relative dielectric constant of the perovskite film (Bube, 1962). The  $  V_{\mathrm{TFL}}  $  of the control device is 0.31 V, and the  $  V_{\mathrm{TFL}}  $  of the APMS-modified perovskite is 0.14 V. The electron trap density of the control perovskite film is  $  7.58 \times 10^{15} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}  $ , while the electron trap density of the APMS passivation device is  $  3.43 \times 10^{15} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}  $ . It shows that the addition of APMS can suppress surface defects, while the reduced trap density will increase the  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  and FF of the PSCs (Fan et al., 2018; Li et al., 2019). The dark  $  J-V  $  curve of the control perovskite and APMS passivated device as shown in Fig. 6c. The dark saturation current density of APMS modified perovskite is smaller than that of the control device. The reduction of dark current shows that the series resistance of the APMS passivation device is reduced, which can prevent current leakage and increase  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  and FF (Wang et al., 2018). # 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, we introduced a low-cost silane coupling agent APMS as an additive to the perovskite precursor, which is conducive to defect passivation and significantly improves the efficiency and stability of PSCs. The addition of APMS enhances the crystallinity and increases grain size of perovskite film. The characterization methods of SCLC, PL and TRPL show that the addition of APMS effectively reduces the defect density and improves the carrier lifetime. Therefore, the champion PCE of the perovskite device with APMS added reaches  $ 20.72\% $  with the  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  of 1.150 V. Furthermore, the hysteresis of the APMS passivation perovskite device is reduced and the stability is also improved. These results indicate that APMS is a promising passivation material for high-performance stable PSCs. This simple strategy will further promote the large-scale commercial development of high-performance PSCs.
