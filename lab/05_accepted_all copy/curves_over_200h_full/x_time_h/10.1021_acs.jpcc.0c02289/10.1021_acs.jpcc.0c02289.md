# ABSTRACT

Formamidinium (FA) based perovskite solar cells (PSC) shows enhanced stability comparing to their methylammonium (MA) based counterpart. However, its stability needs to be further enhanced for the potential commercial applications. We demonstrate here that the high-quality thin film can be obtained for FA-based perovskite with remarkably enhanced moisture stability using a confined-pressure annealing (CPA) processing and controlling the amount of Cs introduced into the  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  perovskite precursor solution. Without additional 2D cation additives, surface passivation or encapsulation, no phase degradation was observed for the  $ \mathrm{Cs_{0.1}FA_{0.9}PbI_3} $  perovskite under a humidity of  $ 70\mathrm{RH}\% $  over 500 hours. The unencapsulated device maintained over  $ 70\% $  performances for 500 hours comparing to  $ < 80\mathrm{h} $  of the conventional processed devices, which is the comparable to the outstanding moisture stability of the 2D/3D perovskites. The FA-based perovskite grain size was promoted to over 1 micrometer with a high static water contact angle up to  $ 112^{\circ} $ . The surface cations composition was varied with the concentrated FA cation species at surface while homogenized Cs/FA distribution inside the film. The trapped state density and carrier recombination rate were also reduced with a high fill factor of  $ 82\% $  and the efficiency over  $ 20.23\% $ . This work suggests the intrinsic stability of the 3D perovskite could be further enhanced with adjusting of the surface compositions during the crystallization process.

# Introduction

Hybrid organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite materials have attracted wide attentions as a revolutionary material due to their superior semiconducting properties and low temperature processability for potential low cost optoelectronic applications.  $  ^{1-4}  $   Recently, the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) for perovskite solar cell (PSC) has surged to 25.2%.  $  ^{5}  $   However, due to the intrinsic ionic characteristics of hybrid perovskite film, the stability of devices especially in operating conditions is a critical concern in perovskite-based photovoltaics.  $  ^{6-9}  $   It has become manifested that perovskite films generally begin to degrade from the defects at surfaces and grain boundaries, which are more susceptible to degradation when contact with water and oxygen.  $  ^{10-12}  $   This conclusion is supported by the single crystalline PSC with dramatic improved stability comparing to their multi-crystalline counterparts.  $  ^{13}  $   Therefore, minimizing the density of defective locations, such as grain boundaries areas and handling the surface dangling bonds are very important to achieve high performance and device stability. Various post-processing methods, such as pressure-induced crystallization, solvent annealing, chemical vapor recrystallization, Ostwald ripening and two-dimensional liquid cage annealing were usually used to repair crystal defects and reconstituted crystal structure with dramatic enhanced grain size.  $  ^{14-19}  $   It needs to be noted that the current methods are very rarely reported for methylammonium (MA) free perovskite due to significance of MA assisted grain growth.

# Results and discussion

The perovskite was first deposited on a substrate using the green anti-solvent ethyl acetate  $  ^{51}  $  and then placed in a small container with set pressure controlled by the amounts of nitrogen, which is shown in Figure 1a and details of the preparation can be found in experimental process. We recognized that, in conventional annealing process (CA) (Figure 1b), the open system accelerates the evaporation of solvents, which in hence elevate migration energy of perovskite species for grain-growth. Moreover, the perovskite surface in the CA is susceptible to the gas components of a glove box or the humidity in the air, result in reduced repeatability occasionally in our laboratory. Thus, we fabricated the high quality  $  FA_{x}Cs_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $  perovskite films in a convenient measure, named as the confined pressure annealing (CPA). Within a confined chamber, the solvent escaping is slowing down due to the dramatically increased saturate pressure. This is also preventing the volatilization of FA species which slows down the nucleation growth and contributes to the increase in grain size in following heat-treatment. This process is resembling to solvent assisted annealing (SA), but SA requires precise control of the amount of additional DMSO.  $  ^{16}  $  In contrast, CPA has the effect of increasing grain size and is easier to handle.

We surprisedly found that after CPA the sample achieved superior humid stability comparing to the CA prepared perovskite films. The linear region at the low bias voltage corresponds to the ohmic-type response. As the bias voltage in the middle region increases, a significant increase in current injection is considered to be the trap filling process. The intersection between these two regions is defined as the trap-filling limit voltage ( $ V_{\mathrm{TFL}} $ ), so the trap density in the devices can be calculated according to the following equation: $ ^{21} $

$$
