# 2D   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   Nanosheets for Holistic Passivation of Inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   Perovskite Solar Cells for Improved Efficiency and Stability

Shaomin Yang, Weiduan Liu, Yu Han, Zhike Liu,  $  ^{*}  $  Wenjing Zhao, Chenyang Duan, Yuhang Che, Haoshuang Gu, Yuebin Li,  $  ^{*}  $  and Shengzhong (Frank) Liu  $  ^{*}  $

Inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have gained enormous research interest due to their excellent thermal and light stabilities. However, their unsatisfactory power-conversion efficiency and poor intrinsic phase stability remain roadblocks to their further development. Herein,   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   nanosheets (NSs) with the Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) structure are synthesized, and an NSs/  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ /NSs heterostructure is employed to enhance both the stability and efficiency of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cells. The novel   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs can not only passivate the top and bottom surfaces of the perovskite film and top surface of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   film but also enhance the stability of the perovskite film. Based on the heterostructured NSs/  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ /NSs inorganic perovskite film, the efficiency of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs is improved from 15.02% to 16.65%. Moreover, the unencapsulated   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   devices with the NSs/  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ /NSs heterostructure sustain over 90% of their original efficiencies after being exposed to ambient conditions (  $  \approx25\ ^{\circ}C  $   and   $  \approx35\%\ RH  $  ) for 648 h. Both the UV-light-soaking stability (100 mW cm  $  ^{-1}  $   365 nm UV light) and thermal stability (  $  T=85\ ^{\circ}C  $  ) of the optimized devices are dramatically improved in comparison with their counterparts with only a 3D active layer. Therefore, this work promotes the application of RP inorganic perovskite nanocrystals in a range of perovskite optoelectronic devices.

Recent research shows that the interface state in a PSC is the main factor affecting the stability and performance of the device.  $  ^{[8-11]}  $   Therefore, in order to enhance the efficiency and stability of inorganic PSCs, engineering of the different interfaces (such as the electron transport layer (ETL)/CsPbX  $  _{3}  $   interface, hole transport layer (HTL)/CsPbX  $  _{3}  $   interface) is essential and has been developed in separate works.  $  ^{[12-16]}  $   Inorganic

perovskite nanocrystals (NCs) consisting of abundant elements and possessing tunable energy levels, excellent crystallinity, and high stability are very attractive for both elemental doping and interface engineering in the inorganic   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   PSCs.  $  ^{[17]}  $   For example, inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   NCs have been used

# 1. Introduction

All-inorganic   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   (X = I, Br, and Cl or their mixture) perovskites have attracted great attention for their excellent optoelectronic properties and superior thermal stability.  $  ^{[1,2]}  $   Among

them,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   possesses a high stability for preparing in air  $  ^{[3,4]}  $   and a suitable bandgap for solar-spectrum absorption,  $  ^{[5,6]}  $   so it is considered to be an excellent candidate for fabricating efficient all-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, the black-phase   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   (cubic   $  \alpha  $  -phase) is unstable under ambient conditions and tends to convert into the most stable non-perovskite   $  \delta-CsPbI_{2}Br  $   phase, which poses a major hurdle in the path toward commercial viability. Furthermore, despite the rapid development of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs, their efficiencies achieved to date are still unsatisfactory, leaving much room for further improvement.  $  ^{[7]}  $

Computational modeling and experimental observations indicated that   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   is a novel inorganic 2D perovskite possessing a standard Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) phase and has ultrahigh carrier mobilities (  $  1.39 \times 10^{4}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $   for holes and   $  9.39 \times 10^{3}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $   for electrons), a direct band gap, small effective masses, a low exciton binding energy and excellent ambient/thermal stability, which ensure its potential application in optoelectronic devices.  $  ^{[22]}  $   More importantly, 2D inorganic   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   is more robust under extreme conditions (high temperature/humidity) and has better optoelectronic properties than the organic–inorganic hybrid 2D perovskites (such as   $  PEA_{2}PbI_{4}  $   and   $  BA_{2}PbI_{4}  $  ) because of the elimination of large organic interlayer spacers.  $  ^{[23,24]}  $   However, it has attracted less attention compared to its hybrid 2D perovskite counterparts due to the scarcity of experimental evidence. Li et al. synthesized all-inorganic   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   perovskite bulk and proved that   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   has excellent stability against humidity and temperature.  $  ^{[25]}  $   In addition,   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   also has a good lattice match with   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   (1.6%) for constructing a 2D-3D heterostructure.  $  ^{[23]}  $   However, to the best of our knowledge, the synthesis of 2D   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NCs and their application in   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   PSCs have been never reported.

Herein,   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs were synthesized based on a modified protocol  $  ^{[17,26]}  $   and creatively employed as a multifunctional interface optimization material to modify the interfaces between the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film and charge transport layers for improving the efficiency and stability of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. First,   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs are deposited onto a   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer by a simple coating method, which can passivate the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface and promote the charge transfer at the   $  TiO_{2}/CsPbI_{2}Br  $   interface. Second, the   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer deliver a Cl-doping gradient in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite, which enhances the stability of the perovskite film by increasing its formation energy. Third, a thin layer of   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs is coated onto the perovskite layer through an antisolvent process, which can change the morphology of the perovskite film, passivate the trap states via downward diffusion of elemental Cl and align the energy levels at the interface. Fourth, ligands on the   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs increase the perovskite film's surface hydrophobicity to inhibit moisture penetration. As a result, the   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $ -NSs-modified inorganic device achieves a photoelectric conversion efficiency (PCE) of 16.65%, which is among the best ever reported for a   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cell. In addition, the   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $ -NSs-based inorganic PSCs exhibit high stability under UV, air, and heat conditions. Therefore, this work demonstrates the promising potential of inorganic RP   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $

NSs as effective interface passivation agents for realizing high-performance optoelectronic devices in the future.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs were synthesized using the modified protocol described in the Experimental Section.  $  ^{[17,24]}  $   The Cs-oleate solution was swiftly injected into a reaction solution that contained   $  PbI_{2}  $ ,   $  PbCl_{2}  $ , 1-octadecene (ODE), oleic acid (OA), and oleylamine (OLAM) to react at 160 °C for 10 min. The reacted solution was quenched by cooling the flask in an ice-water bath. The obtained   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs were directly washed via centrifugation and dispersed in toluene or hexane. The morphology of the   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs is depicted in the transmission electron microscopic (TEM) images in Figure 1a and Figure S1, Supporting Information, which manifests that the   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   perovskite has regular sheet morphology with a mean size of ≈29 nm and a mean thickness of ≈5 nm. Figure 1b shows the high-resolution TEM image of   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs with a lattice distance of 0.399 nm, corresponding to the (110) crystal facet of the tetragonal structure. A selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern of   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs is displayed in Figure 1c, for which all the diffraction rings can be indexed according to the lattice planes of the RP   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   perovskite, which confirms the high purity of the synthesized   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs film are shown in Figure 1d, where all diffraction peaks are matched well with the tetragonal structure of the RP   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   perovskite.  $  ^{[27]}  $   To confirm the environmental stability of   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs, the   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs films were stored in ambient air (≈25 °C and ≈35 RH%) for one month, after which another XRD test showed no sign of degradation or new phases. Elemental mapping results shown in Figure 1e confirm the elemental composition of the   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs. Figure 1f presents the UV–vis absorption and PL spectra of   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs in toluene. An absorption edge was obtained at 415 nm and, according to the Tauc curve of its absorption spectrum (Figure 1g), the band gap (  $  E_{g}  $ ) of the   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs was calculated to be ≈3.02 eV, which is in accordance with the literature.  $  ^{[25]}  $   A strong and broad PL emission peak appeared in the 400–550 nm wavelength range. The inset in Figure 1f is a photograph of the synthesized   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs in toluene under illumination. The   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs exhibited strong blue emission when excited by UV light (  $  \lambda = 375  $   nm), which means that   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs can convert UV light to visible light. The conduction band and valence band edges of the   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs were determined by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) as -2.20 and -5.22 eV, respectively (Figure S2 and Table S1, Supporting Information).

To study the electron mobility of   $  TiO_{2}  $   films without and with NSs modification, electron-only devices based on an architecture of   $  FTO/TiO_{2}  $   (or   $  TiO_{2}/NSs  $  )/Au were fabricated for measuring the current density–voltage (J-V) curve in dark. As shown in Figure 2d, J-V curves were fitted using the Mott–Gurney equation   $  J = 9\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{r}\mu V^{2}/8L^{3}  $  , [33] where J is the current density,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity (  $  \varepsilon_{0} = 8.854 \times 10^{-12}  $   F m  $  ^{-1}  $  ),   $  \varepsilon_{r}  $   is the dielectric permittivity,   $  \mu  $   is the electron mobility, V is the applied voltage of the device, and L is the thickness of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   film (40 nm). [34] The calculated values of the electron mobility are   $  1.58 \times 10^{-3}  $   and   $  1.96 \times 10^{-3}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   S  $  ^{-1}  $   for pure   $  TiO_{2}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}/NSs  $   films, respectively, demonstrating the effectively promoted electron transport in the   $  TiO_{2}  $   film. Furthermore, the conductivities of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   films were also calculated from the slopes of the J-V curves according to the equation   $  I = \sigma AL^{-1}V  $  , [35] where A and L represent the active area (9 mm  $  ^{2}  $  ) and thickness (40 nm), respectively. As shown in Figure 2e, the conductivity was increased to   $  4.46 \times 10^{-3}  $   mS cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for the   $  TiO_{2}/NSs  $   film from   $  1.74 \times 10^{-3}  $   mS cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for the pure   $  TiO_{2}  $   film.

Generally, a reduction in the number of film defect states contributes to the improvement of carrier mobility and conductivity. Therefore, we calculate the electronic defect state density of   $  TiO_{2}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}/NSs  $   films according to the equation   $  n_{\mathrm{trap}} = (2V_{\mathrm{TFL}}\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0})/(eL^{2})  $  , [36] where e is the elementary charge   $  (1.6 \times 10^{-19}\mathrm{C})  $  , and   $  V_{TFI}  $   is the trap-filled limit voltage, which can be obtained from Figure 2f. It is notable that the   $  n_{trap}  $   of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   film is   $  2.28 \times 10^{17} cm^{-3}  $  , which is significantly higher than   $  1.63 \times 10^{17} cm^{-3}  $   of the   $  TiO_{2}/NSs  $   film. Taken together, the reduced defect states and enhanced mobility and conductivity are working together to facilitate the interface charge transfer and enable the solar cells to have a higher power output.

To explore in depth the recombination dynamics of the charge carriers in perovskite films, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL (TRPL) decay were measured at the same excitation wavelength (510 nm) and intensity. As demonstrated in Figure 3c, the SSM and DSM films exhibited stronger PL emission with   $  \approx  $  1.3 and twofold enhancements, respectively, relative to the control perovskite film. Meanwhile, the emission peaks are slightly blue-shifted. Hence, the significantly enhanced PL intensity indicates that the NSs modification passivated defects in the perovskite film and largely suppressed the surface nonradiative recombination. TRPL spectra are shown in Figure 3d and fitted with a double-exponential decay function. Therein, the relatively fast decay component (  $  \tau_{1}  $  ) represents the charge trapping process, while the much slower decay component (  $  \tau_{2}  $  ) was assigned to the carrier recombination processes.  $  ^{[43]}  $   The decay lifetime (  $  \tau  $  ) and fractional amplitudes of the decay components (A) are summarized in Table S3, Supporting Information. The   $  \tau_{1}  $   was increased from 15.78 ns for the control film to 21.28 ns.

for the SSM perovskite film and then to 70.41 ns for the DSM perovskite film, suggesting a slower trapping process, which can be ascribed to a lower density of available charge trap states. This result implies that the charge trapping in NSs-modified films was greatly suppressed, which can be attributed to the decreased number of surface vacancy defects.  $  ^{[44-46]}  $   The SSM and DSM perovskite films exhibit long fluorescence lifetimes, and the corresponding values of   $  \tau_{ave}  $   were 17.07 and 68.22 ns, respectively, of which the fluorescence lifetime of the DSM perovskite film has been increased tremendously compared with that of the control film (12.78 ns) (Figure S11 and Tables S4 and S5, Supporting Information). Certainly, the increased PL intensity and prolonged fluorescence lifetime may be ascribed to the reduced defect density which suppressed non-radiative recombination in the perovskite film as well as to the increased crystallinity of the inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film. These PL data are consistent with the XRD results of the perovskite films, manifesting that the NSs modification of perovskite film can effectively improve film quality. Figure 4h presents the Nyquist plots of the devices without and with NSs modification. According to the literature, the weak Pb–I bond in perovskite film is prone to breakage under UV light illumination, leading to severe degradation at the ETL/perovskite interface.  $  ^{[58]}  $   Here, we exposed the control and NSs-modified devices to 365 nm UV light with a high power of 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $   for 200 h (Figure 5d) to investigate their UV light stability. The results show that the PCE of the control device exhibited a sharp drop to 30% of its original value within 200 h. Encouragingly, the DSM devices still retained over 82% of the original PCE. The improved UV light stability of these devices can be attributed to the following two aspects: First, as mentioned with reference to

Figure 1a,   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs can convert UV light to visible light, so they can greatly reduce the intensity of UV light reaching the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite, thus attenuating UV-light-accelerated device degradation.  $  ^{[59]}  $   Second, the Cl-ion doping in the perovskite lattice enhances the structural stability of the perovskite and protects it from degradation induced by the photocatalytic effect of   $  TiO_{2}  $   under UV light illumination.  $  ^{[52,58]}  $

Furthermore, the thermal stability of the devices was also evaluated. As shown in Figure 5e, the devices were heated at 85 °C for 70 h to test the thermal stability. Surprisingly, the DSM device retained 71% of its initial performance, considerably higher than the 26% remaining for the control devices, demonstrating superior thermal stability. Therefore, the improvements of perovskite film quality and interface stability have contributed greatly to optimizing the stability of the device.  $  ^{[60]}  $

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, we demonstrate a holistic interface engineering strategy that achieves high PCE with high stability in inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. The strategy utilizes novel RP inorganic   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs to achieve double-side passivation of 3D   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films by forming a 2D/3D/2D heterostructure. It is found that the novel 2D   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs can be used as interface- and bulk-passivation agents to modify the surface of   $  TiO_{2}  $   and undersurface of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, deliver elemental dopants across the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, and modify the upper surface hydrophobicity/band structure of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film. As a result, a champion PCE of 16.65% is obtained, which is one of the best efficiencies reported for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cells so far, along with much-enhanced UV, air, and thermal stabilities. # 4. Experimental Section

Materials: Cesium carbonate (  $  Cs_{2}CO_{3}  $  , 99.9%), lead(II) chloride (  $  PbCl_{2}  $  , 99.99%), lead(II) iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  , 99.999%), OLAM (OLAM, 80–90%), oleic acid (OA, 90%), 1-octadecene (ODE, 90%), and hexane (98%, with molecular sieves, water   $  \leq  $   50 ppm) were purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Co., Ltd.   $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)_{2}  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}(DMSO)_{2}  $   adducts  $  ^{[61]}  $   were synthesized in accordance with the previously reported method. The   $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}(DMSO)  $   powders were obtained by annealing   $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)_{2}  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}(DMSO)_{2}  $ , respectively, in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 24 h. Cesium iodide (Csl, 99.99%) and lead acetate (  $  PbAc_{2}  $  ) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) and dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) were purchased from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co. Ltd.

Preparation of Cs-Oleate Precursor Solution: 0.4 g   $  Cs_{2}CO_{3}  $   and 1.75 mL OA were loaded into a 3-neck flask and dissolved in 15 mL ODE. The flask was degassed and dried under vacuum at 120 °C for 40 min and then heated under   $  N_{2}  $   to 150 °C until all   $  Cs_{2}CO_{3}  $   reacted with OA.

Synthesis of   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs: For a standard reaction, 0.1 mmol   $  PbI_{2}  $  , 0.1 mmol   $  PbCl_{2}  $  , 5 mL ODE, 0.125 mL OA, and 0.5 mL OLAM were loaded into a 3-neck flask and degassed under vacuum for 30 min at 120 °C. Then the temperature was raised to 160 °C under   $  N_{2}  $   and held for 10 min, after which 0.5 mL of Cs-oleate solution was swiftly injected. After 10 s, the reaction was quenched by cooling the flask in an ice-water bath. The   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs were directly washed via centrifugation (6500 rpm for 5 min), followed by redispersion in 5 ml hexane.

Device Fabrication: FTO glass substrates were sequentially cleaned with acetone, isopropanol, and distilled water in an ultrasonic bath for 30 min and then dried with   $  N_{2}  $  . The clean FTO glass substrates were then treated with UV-Ozone for 10 min prior to the deposition of a   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL. The   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was deposited by immersing FTO glass substrates in 200 mL aqueous solution with 4.5 mL titanium tetrachloride for 60 min at 70 °C then rinsed with distilled water and annealed at 200 °C for 30 min. The NSs-coated   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL was prepared by spin-coating the as-synthesized NSs toluene solution with different concentrations (1, 1.25, 2.5, and 5 mmol L  $  ^{-1}  $  ) at 2000 rpm for 30 s. The 0.9 M   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution was prepared by dissolving   $  PbBr_{2}(DMSO)  $  ,   $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)  $  , Csl, and   $  PbAc_{2}  $   (molar ratio = 1:1:2:0.015) in DMF and DMSO (4:1 v/v). The resulting perovskite precursor solution was spun at 1000 rpm for 10 s, followed by 3000 rpm for 30 s. Afterward, the film was annealed at 35 °C for 5 min, 120 °C for 10 min, and 180 °C for 4 min to obtain the perovskite layer. Similarly, the as-prepared NSs solutions with different concentrations were deposited on the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film at 2000 rpm for 30 s followed by thermal annealing at 120 °C for 10 min to form the   $  TiO_{2}/NSs/periovskite/NSs  $   structure. The HTL was prepared by spin-coating Spiro-OMeTAD solution (90 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) doped with 36 μL t-BP and 22 μL Li-TFSI (520 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) solution in acetonitrile at 5000 rpm for 30 s onto   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films to produce a   $  TiO_{2}/NSs/periovskite/NSs/Spiro-OMeTAD  $   architecture. Finally, an 80-nm Au electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation through a shadow mask to form a device active area of 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  .

Characterization: XRD patterns for   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs were obtained on a Bruker D8 Advanced diffractometer (Cu KαI radiation, X-ray wavelength of 1.54 Å); all samples were prepared by drop casting a concentrated solution on a zero diffraction quartz wafer. The TEM images for   $  Cs_{2}PbI_{2}Cl_{2}  $   NSs were taken on a JEM2100F at an accelerating voltage of 200 kV; the samples were prepared by drop-casting diluted NSs solutions onto 200-mesh carbon-coated copper grids. XRD patterns for perovskite film were performed on a D/MAX 2400 diffractometer. The absorption spectra of perovskite films were measured using a UV-vis NIR spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer, Lambda 950). PL (excitation at 510 nm) spectra were measured using a FLS980 spectrometer

(Edinburgh Instruments Ltd), and TRPL spectra were measured with a PicoQuant FluoQuant 300. SEM images of perovskite films were obtained by field-emission SEM (HITACHI, SU-8020). XPS and UPS of the perovskite films were carried out using a photoelectron spectrometer (ESCALAB250Xi, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The J-V curves of the inorganic PSCs were measured using a Keithley 2400 source under AM1.5G illumination at 100 m Wcm  $  ^{-2}  $  . The EQEs of the PSCs were recorded using a QTest Station 2000ADI system (Crowntech Inc.). EIS analysis was performed on a Zahner Electrochemical Workstation. TOF-SIMS characterization was carried using a TOF-SIMS 5 IONTOF. Water contact angles were measured using a DataPhysics OCA 20.
