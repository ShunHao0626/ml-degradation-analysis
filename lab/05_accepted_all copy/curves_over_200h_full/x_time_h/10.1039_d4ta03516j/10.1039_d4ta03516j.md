# Rational design of one-dimensional triarylamine-based covalent organic frameworks for perovskite solar cells with improved efficiency and stability†

Jun He,  $  ^{ab}  $   Longfei Yuan,  $  ^{ab}  $   Sixiao Gu,  $  ^{ab}  $   Jiaxv Bai,  $  ^{ab}  $   Yaxin Li,  $  ^{ab}  $   Shirong Wang,  $  ^{ab}  $   Dewang Li  $  ^{*bcd}  $   and Hongli Liu  $  ^{*ab}  $

Covalent organic frameworks (COFs) have emerged as compelling interface optimizer candidates for competent perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, there is a notable absence of rationally designed COFs tailored for perovskite and hole transporting layers (HTLs). Herein, an unreported one-dimensional (1D) triarylamine-based covalent organic framework (COF)   $  \{[(TPA)_{2}(TPB)_{1}]_{-C=N-}\}  $   with active amino groups was first synthesized. To anchor COF on the perovskite surface and manipulate the interface hole transportation, this 1D COF was salified with hydrogen halides to produce   $  \{[(TPA)_{2}(TPB)_{1}]_{-C=N-}-X\}  $   (COF-X; X = Cl, Br, and I). The ammonium terminals   $  (-NH_{3}^{+})  $   can coordinate with perovskite via strong ionic interaction. The backbone units of triphenylamine (TPA) and N,N,N',N'-tetraphenylbenzidine (TPB), renowned for their superior hole extracting and transporting properties, can enhance the hole mobility of the perovskite film from 1.02 to 3.72 cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $  . Consequently, the optimal device achieved a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 23.58% and an impressive open-circuit voltage (V  $  _{OC}  $  ) of 1.181 V. Additionally, the unencapsulated devices retained 90.8% and 89.2% of their initial efficiencies after atmospheric storage over 4800 hours and continuous illumination over 500 hours, respectively.

# 1 Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) as the most promising photovoltaic (PV) technology have been widely investigated over the past decade.  $  ^{1-6}  $   In a typical PSC configuration, the perovskite absorber layer is sandwiched between two carrier transporting layers (CTLs) to achieve photogenerated carriers' separation and transportation.  $  ^{7,8}  $   However, the interfaces between these functional layers always accumulate substantial defects and suffer from inefficient carrier transfer,  $  ^{9-12}  $   which not only hinders the achievement of excellent photovoltaic performance but also induces the degradation of devices during long-term operation.  $  ^{13-15}  $   Given the necessity for stability and efficiency in commercialization, there is an immediate requirement to develop multifunctional interface modifiers for high-performance PSCs.  $  ^{16-21}  $

To address the challenges associated with the interface between bulk perovskite and the hole transporting layer (HTL), various organic substances were incorporated into PSCs to passivate interfacial defects and regulate hole transport. Small organic molecules with reactive groups can covalently bond with perovskite to reduce surface defects.  $  ^{22,23}  $   Oversized ammonium salts facilitate the formation of ordered two-dimensional (2D) or quasi-2D perovskites for manipulating good energy level alignment with three-dimensional (3D) perovskites.  $  ^{24,25}  $   Bulk organic molecules possessing   $  \pi  $  -conjugated units are beneficial for efficient hole shuttling between the perovskite and HTL.  $  ^{26}  $   Cross-linked supramolecular complexes and polymers with chelating groups can inhibit ion diffusion and lead leakage in devices.  $  ^{27,28}  $   Therefore, the development of interface materials from small molecules to multifunctional polymers with an orderly extended structure can effectively alleviate defect-assisted non-radiative recombination, enhance more efficient hole extraction and transportation, and even favor the efficiency and stability of PSCs.

Covalent organic frameworks (COFs) are crystalline organic polymer monomers connected into one-dimensional (1D), 2D and 3D networks by strong covalent bonds.  $  ^{29-31}  $   Diverse building blocks and reticular chemistry endowed COFs with designable structures,  $  ^{32,33}  $   adjustable functions,  $  ^{34}  $   and superior carrier mobilities,  $  ^{35}  $   which led to extensive research and application in the field of photoelectronics,  $  ^{36,37}  $   such as photocatalysis,  $  ^{38,39}  $   organic field effect transistors,  $  ^{40}  $   lithium–organic batteries,  $  ^{41}  $

In recent years, COFs have also been competitive candidates for functional materials in PSCs due to their  $  \pi  $ -conjugated skeleton, donor–acceptor (D–A) fragments and tremendous functional groups,  $  ^{43-47}  $  which can regulate perovskite crystallization,  $  ^{48}  $  enhance photon-generated charge transfer,  $  ^{49,50}  $  passivate surface defects,  $  ^{44,51}  $  and inhibit halogen ion diffusion.  $  ^{52}  $

However, those COFs applied in PSCs are confined to either 2D or 3D skeleton structures with poor dispersion and limited exposed active sites. 1D COFs due to their tremendous active groups and more liable modifiability  $  ^{53-60}  $  have the potential to strongly bond with perovskite and effectively realize carrier transport. In this work, a novel 1D ribbon-like COF  $  \{[(TPA)_{2}(TPB)_{1}]_{C=N-}\}  $  with an active amino group  $  (-NH_{2})  $  and a full-triarylamine backbone was constructed,  $  ^{60}  $  and then it was converted into hydrogen halide acidified COFs  $  \{[(TPA)_{2}(TPB)_{1}]_{C=N-X}\}  $  (abbreviated as COF-X; X = Cl, Br, or I) as interface modifiers for PSCs. In  $  \alpha  $ -phase formamidine lead iodine (  $  \alpha  $ -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $ ) PSCs, the COF-I with acidified amine functionalities (  $  -NH_{3}^{-}I^{+}  $ ) located at the edge of COFs can reinforce the bond interaction with uncoordinated Pb on the perovskite surface, which effectively passivates these defects and decreases the trap density (  $  N_{t}  $ ) of perovskite films from  $  6.16 \times 10^{15}  $  to  $  2.25 \times 10^{15}  $  cm  $  ^{-3}  $ . Triphenylamine (TPA) and  $  N,N,N',N'  $ -tetraphenylbenzidine (TPB) backbone units excellently facilitate carrier transport and the perovskite film achieves a balanced hole mobility of  $  3.72  $  cm  $  ^{2}  $  V  $  ^{-1}  $  s  $  ^{-1}  $ . Owing to the synergetic effect of COF-I on perovskite, the target device achieved a PCE of 23.58% with an enhanced open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $ ) of 1.181 V. Notably, the target PSCs without encapsulation can retain 90.8% of their initial efficiency after 4000 h of storage and 89.6% of their initial efficiency after 500 h of illumination tracking under maximum power point-tracking conditions. This work motivates exploration of innovative multifunctional materials to solve interface issues in PSCs and paves the path for design of function-directed COFs.

# 2 Results and discussion

# 2.1 Synthesis and characterization of COFs

Considering the ionic characteristics of perovskite  $  ^{61}  $  and the efficient hole transporting requirement of the HTL,  $  ^{62}  $  a 1D COF  $  \{[(TPA)_{2}(TPB)_{1}]_{-C=N-}\}  $  was synthesized via Schiff-base condensation of tris(4-aminophenyl)amine (TPA-NH  $  _{2}  $ ) and 4,4',4'',4''-([1,1'-biphenyl]-4,4'-diylbis(azanetriyl))tetrabenzaldehyde (TPB-CHO, Fig. S1 and S2†) with a 2:1 ratio. Subsequently, salification of  $  \{[(TPA)_{2}(TPB)_{1}]_{-C=N-}\}  $  with hydrohalide acids (HX, X = Cl, Br, and I) was performed to achieve the final products  $  \{[(TPA)_{2}(TPB)_{1}]_{-C=N-X}\}  $  (abbreviated as COF-X; X = Cl, Br or I), as depicted in Fig. 1a.

The optimally crystalline  $ \{[(\mathrm{TPA})_2(\mathrm{TPB})_1]_{-C = N}\} $  was obtained under solvothermal conditions in a solvent mixture of nitrobenzene and mesitylene (v/v = 3:1) with 0.2 volume% trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) as the catalyst after heating for  $ 72\mathrm{h} $  at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ . The obtained orange powders were Soxhlet extracted in ethanol and tetrahydrofuran for  $ 24\mathrm{h} $ , respectively. By screening modulators (aniline, p-toluidine, 4-fluoroaniline, and 4

trifluoromethylaniline) and their adding amounts, the 20 equivalent anilines can greatly improve the crystallinity of  $ \left[\left(\mathrm{TPA}\right)_2\left(\mathrm{TPB}\right)_1\right]_{\mathrm{C} = \mathrm{N}-} $  (Fig. S3 and S4†). Two building blocks and  $ \left[\left(\mathrm{TPA}\right)_2\left(\mathrm{TPB}\right)_1\right]_{\mathrm{C} = \mathrm{N}-} $  were first characterized using the Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra in Fig. 1b. TPA-NH $ _2 $  and TPB-CHO present the absence of characteristic amine groups ( $ \nu_{\mathrm{NH_2}} = 3413 $  and  $ 3341~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ ) and aldehyde groups ( $ \nu_{\mathrm{C=O}} = 1696~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ ), respectively. In  $ \left[\left(\mathrm{TPA}\right)_2\left(\mathrm{TPB}\right)_1\right]_{\mathrm{C} = \mathrm{N}-} $ , the formation of the imine linkages is supported by the appearance of the stretching vibration absorption peak at  $ \nu_{\mathrm{C=N}} = 1623~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ . Importantly, the FT-IR spectrum also substantiates the presence of free amine functionalities in COFs at  $ \nu_{-\mathrm{NH}} = 3033~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ . The formation of imine bonds was further supported by  $ ^{13}\mathrm{C} $  crosspolarization magic angle spinning (CP-MAS) NMR spectroscopy. The signal at  $ 158~\mathrm{ppm} $  demonstrates the formed imine linkages. Noteworthily, the presence of the peak at  $ 146~\mathrm{ppm} $  corresponds to the  $ \alpha $ -carbon atoms of the amino phenyl group (Fig. S5†).

The crystal structure of   $  \left[\left(\mathrm{TPA}\right)_{2}(\mathrm{TPB})_{1}\right]_{-\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{N}-}  $   was elucidated using the powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) pattern using Reflex in Accelrys Materials Studio software. Pawley refinement assigned a space group of Cmcm and cell parameters of a = 54.54 Å, b = 20.54 Å, c = 11.23 Å, and   $  \alpha = \beta = \gamma = 90^{\circ}  $  , with factors of   $  R_{p} = 3.71\%  $   and   $  R_{wp} = 2.70\%  $   (Fig. 1c and Table S1†). The diffraction peaks of   $  \left[\left(\mathrm{TPA}\right)_{2}(\mathrm{TPB})_{1}\right]_{-\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{N}-}  $   at   $  3.21^{\circ}  $  ,   $  4.28^{\circ}  $  ,   $  6.46^{\circ}  $  ,   $  8.57^{\circ}  $  ,   $  9.69^{\circ}  $  ,   $  11.64^{\circ}  $  ,   $  13.78^{\circ}  $  ,   $  16.41^{\circ}  $  , and   $  20.99^{\circ}  $   were assigned to the corresponding d-spacings of 27.27 Å, 19.22 Å, 13.63 Å, 10.26 Å, 9.09 Å, 7.57 Å, 6.41 Å, 8.38 Å, and 4.22 Å, which further corresponded to the planes of (200), (110), (400), (020), (600), (021), (330), (112), and (322), respectively. These results indicate the highly periodic crystal structure of   $  \left[\left(\mathrm{TPA}\right)_{2}(\mathrm{TPB})_{1}\right]_{-\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{N}-}  $  . As demonstrated in Fig. S6,†   $  \left[\left(\mathrm{TPA}\right)_{2}(\mathrm{TPB})_{1}\right]_{-\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{N}-}  $   has a 1D ribbon-like structure with rhombic pore channels and these chains are joined together by the hydrogen bonding between aromatic hydrogens and imine linkages. Viewed from different positions, the crystal structure of   $  \left[\left(\mathrm{TPA}\right)_{2}(\mathrm{TPB})_{1}\right]_{-\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{N}-}  $   exhibits a dumbbell-like shape, as shown in Fig. S7.† It is worth mentioning that the PXRD patterns of   $  \left\{\left[\left(\mathrm{TPA}\right)_{2}(\mathrm{TPB})_{1}\right]_{-\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{N}-}\mathrm{X}\right\}  $   (abbreviated as COF-X; X = Cl, Br or I) exhibit the same intense diffraction peaks as those of   $  \left\{\left[\left(\mathrm{TPA}\right)_{2}(\mathrm{TPB})_{1}\right]_{-\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{N}-}\right\}  $   at   $  3.2^{\circ}  $  ,   $  4.3^{\circ}  $  ,   $  6.5^{\circ}  $   and   $  8.6^{\circ}  $   (Fig. 1d), which indicates that the structures and crystallinity of COF-X still maintain after hydrohalide salification. The clear diffraction peaks at around   $  22.0^{\circ}  $   in COF-X have a π-π interface distance of 0.4 nm. The inserted halogen atoms might fasten these ribbon chains and enhance the non-covalent interactions between aromatic skeletons.

The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) image of   $  [(TPA)_{2}(TPB)_{1}]_{C=N-}  $   in Fig. 2a exhibits a lamellar morphology with a size of approximately 500 nm. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images in Fig. 2b and S8† revealed that   $  [(TPA)_{2}(TPB)_{1}]_{C=N-}  $   has an obvious nanoribbon structure. The high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) images clearly emerge lattice fringes indicating the well-ordered structure of   $  [(TPA)_{2}(TPB)_{1}]_{C=N-}  $   (Fig. 2c). The fast Fourier transform (FFT) image of the selective region displayed a square structure signifying the orthorhombic crystal system of   $  [(TPA)_{2}(TPB)_{1}]_{C=N-}  $  .

# 2.2 Fabrication and characterization of perovskite films

(Fig. S14†). As demonstrated in Fig. 3a, the COF-I suspension was added to formamidinium iodide (FAI)/methylamine hydrochloride (MACl)/IPA solution and drop-coated on the top of the PbI₂ film. To evaluate the storage stability of PSCs, the unencapsulated devices were stored under ambient conditions with a relative humidity (RH) of 30–40% at 25 °C. The target PSC retained 90.8% of its initial efficiency after storage for 4800 hours, while the one for the control device retained only 37.6% (Fig. 6e). In addition, the morphology of perovskite films after storage in air with RH 60–70% at 25 °C for 1000 hours was measured by SEM. The target perovskite film maintained clear crystal grains without pinholes (Fig. S30†). But the control one had tremendous PbI₂ crystals as well as pinholes (Fig. S31†). The energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) mappings detected higher Pb content in the control perovskite film, which indicated that perovskite suffered from severe degradation. The higher C element content in the target perovskite film evidences that COF-I capped on the surface of perovskite. The operation stability was tested under continuous 1 sun illumination (100 mW cm⁻² white LEDs) in a flowing inert argon atmosphere at 25 °C. The target PSC without encapsulation retained a PCE of 20.86% (initial PCE of 23.38%) over 500 hours of maximum power point tracking (MPPT), as shown in Fig. 6f, whereas, the PCE of the control PSC decayed to 10.32% (initial PCE of 21.14%). The contact angle measurement of the corresponding films was performed to announce the reason for the improved stability of the PSC after incorporation of COF-I (Fig. S32†). The contact angles of water droplets on the surface of the control and target perovskite films were 60.5° and 75.6°, respectively. The more hydrophobic surface is favourable for repelling water penetration into the perovskite film, which slows down the degradation of perovskite after the incorporation of COF-I. Meanwhile, the contact angle of the hydrophobic chlorobenzene droplets on the control and target perovskite films decreases from 15.5° to 7.1°, indicating improved compatibility between the perovskite and HTL. These results demonstrate that the rationally designed 1D COF as an efficient interface modifier can enhance the photovoltaic performance and comprehensive stability of n-i-p PSCs.

# 3 Conclusion

In summary, aiming at improving the performance of PSCs, salified 1D  $ \left[(\mathrm{TPA})_2(\mathrm{TPB})_1\right]_{-\mathrm{C} = \mathrm{N}} - \mathrm{X} $  ( $ \mathrm{X} = \mathrm{Cl} $ , Br, and I) were meticulously designed and successfully synthesized. COF-I as an interface modifier between  $ \alpha $ -FAPbI₃ perovskite and the HTL can not only effectively bond with perovskite using terminal ammonium groups to passivate interfacial defects, but also effectively facilitate hole extraction and transportation through

full-triphenylamine backbones to achieve carrier balance in devices, which can also improve the crystallinity of perovskite films and manipulate energy level alignment. This modification method led to the target device yielding an optimal PCE of  $ 23.58\% $  with an impressive  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of 1.18 V. Furthermore, the stability of the unencapsulated device has been improved, which retained  $ 90.8\% $  and  $ 89.2\% $  of its initial efficiencies after atmospheric storage for 4800 hours and continuous illumination for 500 hours, respectively. Our results shed valuable light on the development of function-directed COFs and provide an effective avenue to explore multifunctional interface materials for more efficient and stable PSCs.
