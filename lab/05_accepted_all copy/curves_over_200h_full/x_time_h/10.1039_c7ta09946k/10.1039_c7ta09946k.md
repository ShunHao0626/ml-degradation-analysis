# Abstract

Certified power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have increased to an impressive value of 22.1%. The most efficient perovskite solar cells have the n-i-p device architecture and use 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N'-di-pmethoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) as the hole transport material (HTM). However, there exists microscopic inhomogeneity that is detrimental to the long-term performance of the solar cells, primarily as a result of hygroscopicity of lithium bis((trifluomethyl)sulfonyl)amide (Li-TFSI) dopant. Here, we report a strategy for reducing heterogeneity by using an organic-inorganic integrated hole transport layer (HTL) composed of the solution-processable conjugated polymer FBT-Th4 and copper oxide (Cu $ _x $ O). The optimized PSCs show significant performance enhancement with power conversion efficiency up to 18.85% from a reverse voltage scan and stabilized champion efficiency of 18.24% with negligible hysteresis. Moreover, we observe a significant enhancement of the long-term stability of perovskite solar cells under high humidity of 70-80% in air.

# Introduction

Recently, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have emerged as a very promising alternative to existing photovoltaic technologies and have reached certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of over 22%.  $  ^{1-14}  $   However, one of the hurdles which can potentially prevent the commercialization of this technology is the stability of the devices under high humidity conditions. One of the critical concerns is the instability of iodo plumbate based perovskite materials themselves, which quickly degrade to   $  PbI_{2}  $   when exposed to moisture and heat.

This intrinsic instability of perovskite is the most dominant factor affecting stability of PSCs. Many attempts have been made to enhance its intrinsic stability by partial substitution (Br for I or Cs for FA) or layered 2D structure. $ ^{10,15} $  Michael Gratzel have shown solar cells employing the composition  $ \mathrm{Cs}_{0.2}\mathrm{FA}_{0.8}\mathrm{PbI}_{2.84}\mathrm{Br}_{0.16} $  yield high average PCEs of over  $ 17\% $  exhibiting negligible hysteresis and excellent long term stability in ambient air. They also elucidated this remarkable behavior using first principle computations, the stabilization of the perovskite phase by mixing the A-cations stems from entropic gains and the small internal energy input required for the formation of their solid solution. $ ^{16} $  Layered 2D halide perovskite structures accommodate bulkier and more hydrophobic organic cations, resulting in superior moisture stability, Mohammad Khaja Nazeeruddin and colleagues combine 3D and 2D

To date, the most efficient perovskite solar cells are still fabricated with an n-i-p device architecture. In this context, 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N'-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) or a triarylamine polymer (PTAA) have been developed as the hole-transporting material (HTM) for PSCs. $ ^{9,14,17-19} $  However, neat organic HTMs suffer from low hole mobility and low conductivity $ ^{20} $ , and therefore require doping to perform efficiently. To improve the conductivity and mobility of spiro-OMeTAD film, lithium bis((trifluoromethyl)sulfonyl)amide (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine ( $ t $ BP) have been generally doped in the precursor solution. However, these dopants or additives degrade the perovskite slowly and it is documented that LiTFSi absorbs moisture in the air. $ ^{21,22} $  Furthermore, the requirement for oxygen exposure thus makes it difficult to precisely control the exact fraction of spiro-OMeTAD. More importantly, during the oxidation of the HTL, pinholes may form on the surface of spiro-OMeTAD. These pinholes may cause direct contact between Au top electrode and perovskite layer and slow moisture penetration may happen.

Semicrystalline 5,6-difl uorobenzothiadiazole based conjugated polymer, FBT-Th4 has been extensively used as a p-type semiconductor in organic field-effect transistors and polymer donor due to properties such as high hole mobility, as well as excellent thermal and chemical stability.  $  ^{43-45}  $   More intriguingly, it is a novel polymer with different functionalities that have not been previously checked in perovskite optoelectronic devices: i) it displays appropriate electrochemical Highest Occupied Molecular Orbital (HOMOs) and chemical processability with excellent cooperation with perovskite; ii) high hole mobility are demonstrated without any additives at room temperature; and iii) they are hydrophobic for the fabrication of the devices. Although FBT-Th4 have high hole mobility and reasonable solubility in organic solvents by itself, it displays low LUMO (or electron affinities), which are not always very effectively in blocking photoseparated electron. Hence, to minimize undesired recombination losses at the interfaces, pristine copper oxide  $ (\mathrm{Cu}_x\mathrm{O}) $  thin films have been successfully incorporated between Au and FBT-Th4. To date, there are no reports on PSCs devices employing FBT-Th4 as HTM.

Herein, we successfully fabricated PSC devices containing solution processable FBT-Th4 and vacuum evaporated copper oxide (  $  Cu_{x}O  $  ) nanocrystals integrated as robust hole transport materials (HTMs) for PSCs. Through careful interface modification, we achieve stabilized power conversion efficiencies greater than 18% at 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  illumination and significantly enhance the long-term stability of perovskite solar-cells at high humidity.

# 4. Charge transfer studies

In order to investigate the hole extraction ability of spiro-OMeTAD and integrated FBT-Th4/Cu $ _x $ O, we compared the steady-state and time-resolved PL spectra of the corresponding perovskite films. As presented in Figure S8, pristine perovskite film shows a strong luminescence peak at 768 nm, which is in good accordance with the bandgap of MAPbI $ _3 $ . When we introduced spiro-OMeTAD HTL on top of perovskite film, the PL peak intensity decayed obviously, which proved that the carrier recombination was suppressed at the interface or in perovskite film. Remarkably, sample based on FBT-Th4/Cu $ _x $ O HTL

The results confirmed the hybrid FBT-Th4/Cu $ _x $ O HTL was more efficient in reducing charge recombination and extracting charge carriers to metal contact than spiro-OMeTAD, which is the most important reason for the  $ Jsc $  improvement. Meanwhile, the blue shift of PL peak indicated a suppressed spontaneous radiative recombination and a lower trap density, which is probably the origin of the enhanced  $ Voc $ . Figure 4(a) shows the TRPL decays of the neat perovskite and perovskite films processed with different HTMs. The experimental data are fitting with bi-exponential decay function, and the fitting parameters are summarized in Table S4. The neat perovskite film exhibits an average decay time of 45.1 ns. After modifying the surface of perovskite with spiro-OMeTAD, the sample exhibits much faster PL quenching, with an average decay time of 16.9 ns, indicating efficient hole extraction from perovskite to spiro-OMeTAD. The perovskite/FBT-Th4/Cu $ _x $ O film exhibits even faster PL quenching with an average decay time of 12.4 ns, indicating faster carrier extraction and transfer to the back contact through FBT-Th4/Cu $ _x $ O HTM. The separated photocarriers can either recombine via surface trap states or be swept out of the device for electrical power generation. # Conclusions

To conclude, we reveal that FBT-Th4/Cu $ _x $ O HTMs are extremely promising candidates for replacing the spiro-OMeTAD as the hole-transporting layer. The integrated FBT-Th4/Cu $ _x $ O HTM is superior at injecting photo-generated carriers from perovskite to top Au electrode. Such HTL successfully blocks any accessible pathways of water molecule permeation and thus protect the perovskite films underneath from moisture-caused damage. Unlike Li-TFSI doped spiro-OMeTAD, the FBT-Th4/Cu $ _x $ O obviously increases the stability of PSCs devices. Our champion devices deliver stabilized power conversion efficiencies of 18.2%
