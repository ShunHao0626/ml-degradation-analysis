# ABSTRACT

All-inorganic perovskite cesium lead iodide/bromide (CsPbI $ _2 $ Br) is considered as a robust absorber for perovskite solar cells (PSCs) due to its excellent thermal stability that guarantees its long-term operation stability. Efficient CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs are available when obtaining low energy loss, which needs efficient charge generation, less charge recombination, and balanced charge extraction. However, numerous traps in perovskites hinder the photon-electron conversion process. Herein, hierarchical manipulation of charge recombination is proposed for CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs featuring low energy loss. Non-selective trap reduction and selective halogen vacancy passivation are performed by using 2,2'-(ethylenedioxy) diethylamine and phenylbutylammonium iodide for bottom and top contacts, respectively. Due to all-around suppressed charge recombination, balanced charge extraction and suppressed hysteresis are realized. The champion PSC achieves an open-circuit voltage of 1.30 eV, a fill factor of 80.2%, and a power conversion efficiency of 16.6% that is 28.6% higher than that of the reference device. Moreover, the thermostability of PSCs is simultaneously enhanced due to the limited defect-assisted degradation.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Metal halide perovskite-based photovoltaic technology has gained remarkable achievements in the past decade.  $  ^{1-8}  $   The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of over 25% has been obtained for organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs), which is approaching to the record efficiency of monocrystalline silicon photovoltaics.  $  ^{9}  $   However, the application of PSCs is hindered by the easy-degradation feature of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite when it is exposed to heat, light, and moisture.  $  ^{10-15}  $   The stability improvement of the PSCs lags far behind its efficiency. The emerging of all-inorganic perovskite materials paves a new way for robust PSCs. Without the existence of organic components, all-inorganic perovskite semiconductor exhibits the superior phase stability against heat and moisture.  $  ^{16-20}  $

Among all-inorganic perovskite materials, cesium lead iodide/bromide (CsPbI $ _2 $ Br) is rather attractive due to its suitable bandgap for visible light absorption and excellent thermal stability. $ ^{21-23} $  It is reported that no phase transition is observed in CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite even under 300  $ ^{\circ} $ C continuous heating. $ ^{24,25} $  Despite the outstanding thermostability, the device performance of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br PSCs is still suffering from significant energy loss. The large bandgap of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br leads to insufficient absorption of incident light that with wavelength beyond 650 nm. $ ^{26} $  Photons with energy smaller than the bandgap have no contribution to the photocurrent. In addition to optical energy loss, the device performance of PSCs is limited by the electrical energy loss during the extraction process of the photogenerated carriers. Moreover, halogen vacancies are selectively passivated by iodide in PBAI. Moreover, the long-term stability is largely enhanced after hierarchical perovskite manipulation because of the suppressed defect-assisted phase degradation.
