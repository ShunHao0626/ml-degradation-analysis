# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells with their archetypal structure   $  ABX_{3}  $   (where A and B are cations and X is a halogen) have emerged as one of the most promising photovoltaic technology due to its sudden increase in efficiency in a matter of a decade [1].

At present, the most researched area for perovskite solar cell (PSC) is its long-term stability which has not made any spectacular gains as has been the case with power conversion efficiency (PCE). There are some examples where perovskites have achieved long term stability ranging to 6–7 months. For example, Bai et al. (2019) reported perovskite solar cells which retained 80% of their initial efficiencies of around   $  \sim  $  19% for around 5200 h under continuous stimulated full spectrum sunlight [2]. Another study by Yang et al. (2019) reported an efficiency of 21.1% for PSC that managed to retain   $  \sim  $  96% of their initial efficiencies for up to 1200 h [3]. Baranwal et al. (2018) have simulated thermal stress conditions for MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   PSCs with carbon based HTLs at 100 °C for up to 7000 h wherein the cause of degradation was due to deactivated CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}^{+}  $   [4]. Since the inception of perovskites in the light absorbing region of solar cell, perovskite solar cell (PSC) have come a long way from a mere power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 3.8% in 2009 to 25.2% in 2019. The sudden growth in efficiency is riddled with instability issues caused by myriad extrinsic and intrinsic factors like moisture, heat, cell configuration etc., which would prove difficult for commercial scalability of the cells. Like other commercially established photovoltaic cells, PSCs have to pass certain stress tests before it can be deemed commercially viable. In the present study, we have focussed on three main types of stress testing on perovskite solar cells, 1) Damp heat test (i.e. solar cells were exposed to high humidity (RH>85%) and high temperature (85 °C) simultaneously, 2) Thermal stress (sudden increase and decrease in ambient temperature with a ramp rate of 65 °C/h) and 3) Outdoor aging under natural diurnal conditions for 504 h. Our results show that the PSCs are more susceptible to accelerated degradation under damp heat test due to the combined effect of heat and moisture retaining less than 1% of initial PCE. Under lower humidity levels, they managed to retain ~70% of initial PCE. Moreover, cells had ~37% of initial PCE towards 504 h of outdoor exposure. Temperature dependence on resistances of the cell was also studied under thermal stress conditions. Our results and analysis would be helpful for those interested in developing or designing a consensus on testing PSCs before paving its way for commercialization.

The IEC (International Electrotechnical Commission)-61215 stipulates the standards for design and approval of terrestrial photovoltaic modules for long-term operation commercially [9]. The IEC standard entails a rigorous set of detailed sequential stress tests to determine if the cells are capable of withstanding prolonged exposures to conditions described in its scope. The commercially established technologies like Si and GaAs have passed the full IEC 61215. Thus, a PSC should at least, at minimum, pass IEC 61215 tests before it can be commercially scalable. As the perovskite solar cell technology matures, there is a requirement of more stress tests to account for specific behaviour of perovskite such as hysteresis and reversible losses [10,11].

The current IEC standard on photovoltaics on the IEC website dates back to 2016 [12]. It is not freely accessible and is available only in an industrial context. # 2. Experiments and method

A series of accelerated degradation study of PSCs with different hole transport layers (P3HT, Spiro-OMeTAD and a blend of P3HT and PEDOT:PSS) based on IEC conventions was carried out. The cells had a standard configuration of FTO/TiO  $  _{2}  $  /Perovskite layer/HTL/Carbon as shown in Fig. 1.

The configuration was chosen after careful consideration of number of studies   $  [13–15]  $  . Studies have shown that   $  TiO_{2}  $   when used as an ETL (Electron Transport Layer) in PSCs has exhibited pinhole-free and dense film with highly crystalline structure   $  [13,14]  $  . There is a reduction in the number of recombination of carriers at perovskite and FTO interfaces when   $  TiO_{2}  $   is used with mixed cationic perovskites. Also,   $  TiO_{2}  $   is still a widely popular choice in different device architectures as an ETL layer   $  [15–17]  $  .

The choice of hole transport layer was also made keeping in mind the practical consideration of facile preparation, availability, price, versatility, JV characteristics and a number of studies on previous literature   $  [18–21]  $  . Polymeric hole transport layer, P3HT (Poly (3-hexylthiophene-2,5-diyl) was chosen as one sample set of PSCs because of its high charge density due to its dense polymeric network   $  [18,22,23]  $  ). Also, P3HT can be applied easily on PSC with less modification using anti-solvents like chlorobenzene which makes it a potential candidate for commercial scalability.

PEDOT:PSS (poly (3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrene sulfonate) has the highest conductivity among thiophene based long chain polymer [24]. It has been extensively used in research based on thin-films, nanowires and screen-printing due to its high conductivity. The combination of PEDOT:PSS and P3HT as HTL in PSC has been shown to increase optoelectronic performance of PSC. A blend of P3HT and PEDOT:PSS in the ratio of 80:20 (P3HT:PEDOT:PSS) was chosen based on previous study [23].

Fig. 1. Schematic diagram of thin film configuration of PSC used in all experiments.

Spiro-OMeTAD (N2,N2,N2',N2',N7,N7,N7',N7'- octakis (4- methoxyphenyl)-9,9'-spirobi [9H-fluorene]-2,2',7,7'-tetramine) was chosen as another 'control' sample set as is the case in a number of studies related to PSC. It is the most studied hole transport layer in context of PSC.

The mixed cation perovskite was prepared through optimization through our previous research work [25]. The perovskite material was tuned by the addition of FA and Ce. The mixed cation used was of chemical formula of  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.55}\mathrm{MA}_{0.25}\mathrm{Ce}_{0.2}\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  instead of conventionally used  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ . In all set of experiments mixed cation perovskite configuration was used.

The commonly used working electrode in context to PSCs are Au or Ag which are very expensive and it serves as a big impediment in commercialization of PSCs due to its high cost. Moreover, the use of Ag and Au involves thermal evaporation process performed under vacuum using expensive equipment. There is also the problem of diffusion of gold from back electrode when operating at a temperature above   $  70\ °C  $   leading to poor optical performance [26]. Ag also creates corrosion problems due to formation of silver halide when it comes in contact with perovskite layer. Researchers have therefore, focused on the use of cheaper electrode alternatives like carbon and its derivatives like graphene, carbon nanotubes etc. [27–30].

From a commercial viewpoint, PSCs with carbon-based electrodes are the most promising candidates. They are cheap, thermally stable for a long period of time, water-resistant and are commercially scalable with processes like inkjet printing, screen printing, drop-casting etc. [31]. Therefore, in the present study we have chosen carbon as our working electrode.

# 3. General overview

The three aging tests were performed to study the degradation which are mentioned as below:

1) Outdoor aging test

In this test, all the PSCs were kept normal to the surface and exposed to sun under ambient conditions for a period of 504 h. The JV characteristics were measured at a specified time (in this case 12:30 pm.). The ambient conditions varied with prevailing weather conditions at the location with humidity varying from 30% to 55% and temperature from 22 °C to 37 °C.

2)Damp and damp heat test

Here, all the PSCs were exposed to high humidity  $ (>85\%) $  and high temperature  $ (85^{\circ}\mathrm{C}) $  conditions which gave rise to accelerated degradation conditions due to the combined effect of heat and moisture.

3) Thermal stress test

To investigate the effect of changing temperature on PSC, thermal stress test was performed based on IEC convections. The PSCs were

# 5. Outdoor aging test

For the outdoor aging tests, all the three types of cells were exposed to natural diurnal cycles of sunlight at Surat, India (21°09'49.6"N 72°46'58.8"E). The testing was done continuously for a period of 504 h. Continuous measurement of JV characteristics in outdoor premises was not possible, so the efficiencies for the cells were measured intermittently every day at same time (12:30 pm). The JV characteristics for the three test samples over the course of 504 h are tabulated in Table 1.

The initial current density is maximum for P3HT (Fig. 2). It can be observed that P3HT based cells managed to retain 37% of the initial current density even after 504 h. Contrastingly, Spiro-OMeTAD could retain only 6% of the initial current density. As reported in many other studies, Spiro-OMeTAD based cells perform well initially but degraded faster under environmental stressors. In our previous study, it was observed that Spiro-OMeTAD based cells had the greatest loss of efficiency under constant illumination and heat stressors compared to P3HT and HTL-free cells [25].

The outdoor aging test gives an insight on how the cells may degrade overtime under ambient conditions. The weather at the time was mostly sunny with an average relative humidity of about 43%. The same results might not be obtained during overcast conditions. Since, solar cells typically give about 10–25% of their optimum performance on cloudy days. Moreover, the cells used in the experiments were not encapsulated. The JV characteristics (Table 1) did not show any huge differences in the open circuit voltages for all the three test samples.

To understand the relationship between phase transition and thermally induced degradation we performed EIS on freshly prepared PSCs and PSCs during thermal stress test. EIS was performed at temperatures of   $  35\ °C  $   and   $  95\ °C  $   with respect to thermal cycle as shown in Fig. 10.

The configuration which was used to fit data was R (RC) (RC) model which is conventionally known as the RC model   $  [18,35,36]  $  . This model was chosen among other different models available, as this model had better fit than other models (RCPE model, Garcia model)   $  [36,37]  $  . RC model obeys double semi-circle law, where each RC element denotes a semi-circle dome.   $  R_{1}  $  ,   $  C_{1}  $   and   $  R_{2}  $  ,   $  C_{2}  $   takes into the account, the resistance and capacitance offered by mobility of electrons and recombination charges respectively.   $  R_{s}  $   denotes the series resistance offered by electrical contacts, metallic wires etc.

The EIS for selected samples sets from Spiro-OMeTAD, P3HT and P3HT and PEDOT:PSS is shown in Fig. 12. One common characteristic observed in all the EIS figures is that the dome shaped curve for recombination resistance depreciates considerably. Recombination occurs when some electrons instead of moving from the ETL (Electron Transport Layer) to perovskite layer moves in the opposite direction contributing lower charge density at the perovskite interface. The EIS curves show that the resistances offered to recombination is lowered considerably after each test.

The EIS curve for Spiro-OMeTAD shows a single dome shaped curve instead of archetypal two dome shaped curve (Fig. 12c). In our previous study, we got the same single dome shaped curve for carbon-based PSC

with Spiro-OMeTAD as hole transport layer [23]. However, the equivalent circuit was different in both the cases because of the absence of carbon monolithic substrate in the present study. Herein, the fitting curve gave an equivalent RC circuit. The series resistance  $  \mathbb{R}_s  $  was very less which indicates that less resistance is offered by external metallic contacts or wire (Table 5). The recombination resistance values  $  \mathbb{R}_1  $  decreases after thermal stress. The PSCs after thermal stress were fitted with the same equivalent circuit to compare the values of resistances before and after stress test. Since, the equivalent circuit model was based on freshly prepared samples the fit had a higher percentage of error for resistance values after thermal stress.

The EIS curves for P3HT based PSCs did not give a perfect semi-circular dome curve for the sample that was selected (Fig. 12a). The dome-shape was rather less pronounced in this case. However, the closest equivalent circuit was RC circuit. All the other models gave a very high percentages of error. The series resistances offered by P3HT was higher (34.56 Ω) than that of Spiro-OMeTAD (5.32 Ω) (Table 6). The values of recombination resistance were much higher (almost double than Spiro-OMeTAD based PSCs). This is not surprising as P3HT being a polymeric hole transport layer has a higher charge carrier density than that of small organic molecule based HTL like Spiro-OMeTAD. Even the recombination values for P3HT post thermal test was almost double than that of Spiro-OMeTAD.

# 8. The role of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  in degradation: analysis of samples from all three tests

The XRD samples of the champion PSC with P3HT-PEDOT:PSS as HTL is shown in Fig. 16a) from each degradation study. The unaged sample exhibited a very small peak for  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . All the samples used for stress testing showed a rise in  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  peak (shown as dotted lines) at around  $ 2\theta \sim 12.5^{\circ} $  (Fig. 16a). The characteristic peak for perovskite crystal was also observed at around  $ 2\theta \sim 14 $  in the same figure (shown as dotted lines). The peak for damp heat test was the highest. This shows that the amount of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  produced over the course of degradation was highest in the case of damp heat test. It was least during outdoor aging (almost half of damp heat test). The corresponding  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  peaks at  $ \sim 12.5 $  for all the PSC samples with different HTLs is shown in Fig. 16. b. The peaks are highest for Spiro-OMeTAD based PSCs and lowest for P3HT-PEDOT:PSS based PSCs. This shows that  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  based degradation is more pronounced in the presence of moisture and heat. The thermal stress test sample did not give a peak as high as damp heat test. Thus, it can be said that temperature have less pronounced effect on  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  based degradation than moisture. Also, Spiro-OMeTAD based cells suffer the most in heat and moisture-based stressors which could be due to its hygroscopic nature by the presence of dopants like LiTFSI and TBP.

LiTFSI is hygroscopic in nature and easily adsorbs water molecule on its surface leading to agglomeration and TBP further accelerates this degradation process by leaving some residual water molecules on the surface when its evaporated.

There are a number of studies which have studied perovskite-based degradation due to   $  PbI_{2}  $   in presence of moisture [38–40]. In the presence of high humidity (RH>85%), the perovskite layer containing FA and MA reacts with water molecule to form a complex hydrate phase and   $  PbI_{2}  $  . At very low humidity levels, the perovskite layer readily completely degrades into complex phase containing   $  PbI_{2}  $  . The humidity levels during outdoor aging were seldom above 50%. Thus, the peaks were lower for outdoor aging test than damp heat test and thermal stress test.

# 9. Conclusions and future outlook

The present study involved testing of perovskite solar cells under various IEC based convections namely, outdoor aging, damp heat test and thermal stress test using different hole transport layers (P3HT, Spiro-OMeTAD, and a blend of PEDOT:PSS and P3HT). The scope of this study involved studying behaviour of cells during accelerated aging tests through simultaneous measurement of JV characteristics, data -fitting of ZZ Nyquist plots through equivalent circuit fitting.

Under outdoor aging test, the fill factor did not show any marked drop in values for all test cases for the initial time period. After a week, Spiro-based PSCs which is known to have better film-forming properties depicted a sudden drop in the values of fill factor. The current density values for both the polymeric hole transport layers (P3HT and P3HT: PEDOT:PSS) were higher. In fact, P3HT managed to retain  $ 37\% $  of its initial power conversion efficiency towards  $ 540\mathrm{h} $ . Current density is observed to have a big impact on the power conversion values, a common theme observed in our previous studies. It should be noted that the values of outdoor aging can vary considerably depending on the location and weather conditions. In contrast, both sets of polymeric HTLs showed an increase in charge carrier traps with increase in temperature beyond phase transition. These charge carrier traps are detrimental in the long-term stability of the cells. The samples of PEDOT:PSS and P3HT showed large peaks for  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  presence in XRD denoting accelerated degradation in damp heat test.

Researchers have focussed on IEC based testing (based on silicon cells) as there is no international standard agency that has laid out the groundwork into effect for standardisation. At present, there is no consensus among scientists for standardised testing of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Lastly, this study would help researchers to identify the degradation and failure modes associated with perovskite solar cells under their early stage of development.
