# Quinoidal 2,2',6,6'-Tetraphenyl-Dipyranylidene as a Simple and Efficient Dopant-Free Hole Transporting Material for Stable and Cost-Effective Perovskite Solar Cells

Chao Shen, $ ^{1\#} $  Marc Courté, $ ^{1\#} $  Anurag Krishna, $ ^{1} $  Shasha Tang, $ ^{1} $  and Denis Fichou. $ ^{1,2,3*} $

Abstract: We report on the use of 2,2',6,6'-tetraphenyldipyrynlidene (DIPO-Ph4), a large quinoidal planar π-conjugated heterocycle, as a simple, easy-to-synthetize and efficient dopant-free hole-transporting material into perovskite solar cells (PSCs). PSCs using pristine DIPO-Ph4 show photon conversion efficiencies up to 10.1%, that is higher than with dopant-free spiro-OMeTAD (5.1%). DIPO-Ph4-based PSCs exhibit a Jsc of 19.52 mA/cm² and a Voc of 0.933 V, values that are superior to dopant-free spiro-OMeTAD and comparable to doped spiro-OMeTAD. These excellent performances find their origin in a higher HOMO level (-4.74 eV) and a much higher hole mobility (2×10⁻² cm²/V.s). Finally, PSCs based on DIPO-Ph4 possess a superior long-term PCE stability under white light illumination as compared to doped spiro-OMeTAD based devices when tested over 600 hours in a relative humidity of ≈30%.

# Introduction

Organic–inorganic halide perovskites, in particular methyammonium lead iodide   $  \left(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{PbI}_{3}\right)  $  , have recently emerged as highly efficient and low-cost materials for photovoltaic applications.  $  ^{1-4}  $   In the last few years the development of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been unprecedented with power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) exceeding 20%.  $  ^{5-8}  $   In current PSC devices spiro-OMeTAD is the most commonly used hole-transporting material (HTM).  $  ^{9}  $   However, its high cost due to a non-trivial synthesis combined with the need to mix it with various dopant materials, is a major hindrance towards cost-effective PSCs. Then it is mandatory to develop alternative HTMs having good transport properties and being easy to synthesize. A number of conjugated polymers, organic small molecule and inorganic

HTMs have been reported.  $  ^{10-12}  $   Organic small molecule HTMs are preferred because they offer some unique advantages such as the possibility to tune their electronic properties by chemical design, few-steps and low-cost synthetic pathways, solution processability, good stability, and often are environmentally friendly. Most of reported HTMs are inferior to spiro-OMeTAD but no HTM has yet clearly emerged in terms of cost, efficiency and stability. Various classes of HTMs based on thiophene,  $  ^{13}  $   carbazole,  $  ^{14}  $   triazatruxene,  $  ^{15}  $   star-shaped molecules,  $  ^{16}  $   fluorinated indolo[3,2-b]indole,  $  ^{17}  $   or triptycene,  $  ^{18}  $   have been recently investigated in PSCs. However, it appears that a number of these HTMs possess a non-planar molecular conformation thus inducing large intermolecular distances in the solid state, a typical example being HTMs based on triarylamines.  $  ^{19-22}  $   As a result, these HTMs suffer from a low hole mobility or conductivity in their pristine form that limits device performance.  $  ^{23,24}  $

One promising strategy to improve charge transport is to use small   $  \pi  $  -planar conjugated molecules which assemble into   $  \pi  $  -stacks in the solid state thus exhibiting favorable intermolecular electronic coupling.  $  ^{25}  $   Only a few planar organic molecules have been reported as dopant-free HTMs in PSC, showing modest performances. For example, Kazim et al. reported on the use of pristine TIPS-pentacene with a maximum efficiency of 11.51%.  $  ^{26}  $   Recently, the Zou's group reported on the use of rubrene in a planar inverted configuration PSC device with a PCE as high as 15.83%.  $  ^{27}  $   Besides, two types of quinoidal small planar molecules have also been reported as HTMs in PSCs, namely alkylated TTF derivatives (PCE=11.03%),  $  ^{28}  $   and 3,6-di(2H-imidazol-2-ylidene)cyclohexa-1,4-diene (PCE=11.6%).  $  ^{29}  $

A widespread strategy to improve charge transport in HTMs consists in adding dopants of various kinds such as Li-bis(trifluoro-methanesulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI), tert-butylpyridine (t-BP) and tris(2-(1H-pyrazol-1-yl)pyridine)cobalt(III) (FK 102).  $  ^{30-32}  $   However, these dopants are hydrophilic, which compromises solar cell stability considering that the perovskite active layers are susceptible to moisture-induced degradation.  $  ^{33}  $   Finally, the use of dopants implies an additional step in the fabrication process and thus increases the final cost of the devices. To date, only a few dopant-free HTMs have been reported which all are inferior to the ubiquitous spiro-OMeTAD in its doped form in terms of device performance and are often synthesized via complex and costful chemical procedures.  $  ^{34-40}  $   Hence, it is mandatory to design novel stable dopant-free HTMs having appropriate properties.

In the solid state the large quinoidal planar   $  \pi  $  -conjugated structure of the molecule leads to a marked intermolecular face-to-face   $  \pi  $  -stacking which is beneficial to charge transport. We recently reported on the hole mobility of DIPO-Ph  $  _{4}  $   thin films as measured in field-effect transistors (FETs) that we recorded to be as high as   $  \mu_{h}=2\times10^{-2}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $  /V.s with on/off ratio of the order of   $  10^{4}  $  .  $  ^{44}  $   Surprisingly, in this study we observed that at low voltages in the range 0.0–3.0 V the Id/Vg transfer characteristics of these FETs present a clear hysteresis typical of a resistive memory effect. This memory effect was also observed by conducting AFM at low-voltages and repeated write-read-erase-read cycles performed at low frequency properties through simple and cost-effective pathways reveal a non-volatile memory effect in the form of high-resistance and low-resistance states.

Furthermore, we also recently demonstrated that DIPO-Ph $ _4 $  acts as an efficient hole collecting interfacial layer in organic solar cells based on P3HT:PCBM. $ ^{45} $  Prominently, vacuum-deposited DIPO-Ph $ _4 $  thin films are constituted of densely packed and vertically aligned crystalline nano-needles, a morphology which increases the contact area with the adjacent active photovoltaic material and hence the rate of charge collection at this interface. Finally, note that in an early study we reported on the use of the sulfur analog of DIPO-Ph $ _4 $ , namely DITPY-Ph $ _4 $ , as a hole-collecting interlayer in OSCs. $ ^{46} $

We report here on the use of DIPO-Ph $ _4 $  as a highly efficient and cost-effective HTM in perovskite solar cells. We show in particular that mesoscopic PSCs using DIPO-Ph $ _4 $  as the HTM possess a high short-circuit current  $ J_{SC} $ =19.52 mA/cm $ ^2 $ , an open circuit voltage  $ V_{OC} $ =0.933 V and a power conversion efficiency (PCE) as high as 10.1%. The simple chemical structure of DIPO-Ph $ _4 $  combined with a facile three-step synthesis (Scheme S1) starting from inexpensive materials represent a major advantage in view of large-scale device development.

Overall, the excellent electronic properties of DIPO-Ph  $  _{4}  $  thin films make it a potential candidate as an efficient hole-transporting materials in perovskite-based solar cells.

# Conclusion

The quinoidal dipyrylidene DIPO-Ph  $  _{4}  $  has been inserted as a dopant-free hole transport material in perovskite solar cells. DIPO-Ph  $  _{4}  $  based PSCs show PCEs up to 10.1%, that is two times higher than those obtained with undoped spiro-OMeTAD (5.1%), still retaining Jsc and Voc comparable to those of doped spiro-OMeTAD based devices. Moreover, DIPO-Ph  $  _{4}  $  devices show a higher stability over 600 hours at a relative humidity of 30%. However, further optimization of the materials preparation and device parameters could probably lead to even higher performance in terms of efficiency and stability. Its simple chemical synthesis and high hole mobility make DIPO-Ph  $  _{4}  $  a potential candidate to substitute the more expensive spiro-OMeTAD in PSCs. This work also lays the ground for a strategy to design and develop novel dipyrylidene-based HTMs for stable and cost-effective organic solar cells.

# Experimental Section

# Fabrication of the PSCs

Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO, <14 Ω/square, 3 mm thick) substrates were etched to form the desired pattern and subsequently cleaned by sonication in 2% Hellmanex aqueous solution made of deionized water and ethanol. A thin compact layer of TiO₂ as a blocking layer between FTO and perovskite was deposited by atomic layer deposition (ALD). The mesoporous TiO₂ layer composed of 30 nm particles was deposited by spin coating at 6000 rpm for 30 seconds using a commercial TiO₂ paste (Dyesol DSL 30 NRD) diluted in ethanol (2:7, weight ratio). After drying at 70°C, the TiO₂ films were gradually heated to 500°C, baked at this temperature for 30 min and cooled down to room temperature. Then, the organic-inorganic perovskite CH₃NH₃PbI₃ was deposited by a single-step method with solvent treatment.³ Following the spin-coating step, the samples were annealed at 100°C for 30 min. DIPO-Ph₄ is deposited on perovskite by evaporation under high vacuum (P=10⁻⁴ Pa) at a slow rate of 0.2 Å s⁻¹ as monitored by a quartz microbalance (Inficon, X TM/2). For spiro-based devices, spiro-OMeTAD was dissolved in chlorobenzene (100 mg/ml) respectively and spin-coated on top of the perovskite layer. Additives such as Li-TFSI, t-BP and tris(2-(1H-pyrazol-1-yl)pyridine)cobalt(III) (FK 102) were added to the above solution according to a protocol described in the literature.⁷ Finally, all devices were placed in a thermal evaporator for gold (Au) deposition via shadow masking. The thickness of the Au electrode was 100 nm and active area of either 0.2 cm² or 0.07 cm² were defined by the overlap of TiO₂ and Au.

# Material and device characterizations

The photocurrent-voltage characteristics of the solar cells were measured with an electrochemical workstation (Zahner zennuim, Germany) under AM 1.5G solar simulator (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , Newport, calibrated with a silicon reference cell). The incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) was measured with a 300 W Xenon lamp and a monochromator controlled by TracQ Basic software (QEPVSI-b Measurement System, Newport). The TiO  $  _{2}  $  thickness is determined by Alpha-Step IQ surface profiler (KLA-Tencor). The morphology was characterized with a field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM FEI SIRION). Absorbance spectra were measured by a UV-VIS spectrophotometer (Shimadzu, UV-2501PC).
