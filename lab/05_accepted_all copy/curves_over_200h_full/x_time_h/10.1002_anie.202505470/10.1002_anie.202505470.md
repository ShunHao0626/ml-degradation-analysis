# Metalized Porphyrin-Based COFs for Conductive Porous Layers in Perovskite Solar Cells to Enhance Electron Injection, Defect Passivation, and Lead-Protection

Zhengyan He  $  ^{+}  $  , Tianxiang Luan  $  ^{+}  $  , Qilin Wei, Liang Wang, Dan Huang, Haibo Chen, Ivan Yu Dmitriev, Peizhou Li,  $  ^{*}  $   Shufang Zhang,  $  ^{*}  $   and William W. Yu  $  ^{*}  $

Abstract: In perovskite solar cells (PSCs), the introduction of an intermediate layer to bridge the transport and photoactive layers has become a key strategy for enhancing carrier extraction efficiency. However, traditional approaches are often limited by the seesaw effect, making it challenging to achieve an optimal balance between electron transport and defect passivation. In this study, we employed a  $ \mathrm{Cu^{2+}} $ -loaded metalized porphyrin-based covalent organic framework (Cu-Por-COF) as a conductive porous layer (CPL) at the perovskite bottom interface. Experimental results showed that a Cu-Por-COF coverage of  $ 19\% $  significantly enhanced electron transport and effectively suppressed long-distance electron diffusion. Moreover, the carefully designed Cu-Por-COF provided abundant active sites, which improved the film-forming quality of the perovskite layer, thereby facilitating the beneficial synergy of electron injection and defect passivation. The n-i-p type devices achieved a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 25.41\% $  ( $ 0.09~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ ) and  $ 21.99\% $  ( $ 1.01~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ ). Using Cu-Por-COF to stabilize the perovskite crystal structure, the unencapsulated devices retained over  $ 81\% $  of its initial efficiency after  $ 2000\mathrm{h} $ . Additionally, Cu-Por-COF effectively chelated lead ions and thus enhanced the environmental sustainability of PSCs.

# Introduction

The certified efficiency of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has reached 27.0%.  $  ^{[1]}  $   This remarkable efficiency gain is closely tied to the multiple improvements including the interface layer engineering.  $  ^{[2-5]}  $   Efforts have been extensively applied on the interface between the transport layer and the photosensitive layer. Low-conductivity organic materials,  $  ^{[6,7]}  $   low-dimensional perovskite materials,  $  ^{[8,9]}  $   and halide inorganic compounds  $  ^{[10,11]}  $   have been introduced to significantly boost the performance and stability of PSCs.

Those materials typically offer good solubility as intermediate layers, which facilitates their deposition via spin coating. Dielectric inorganic materials such as   $  ZrO_{2}  $   and   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   have

been used in PSCs. However, due to the sensitivity of the electrical tunneling effect to the dielectric layer thickness, improper thickness control results in a significant drop in the fill factor (FF).  $  ^{[12-14]}  $   Therefore, achieving a balance between passivation and charge transport, particularly in optimizing the open circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) and FF, presents a major challenge. This issue is also relevant to organic or halogenated inorganic passivation layers, where there is an inherent trade-off between carrier transport and defect passivation.  $  ^{[15,16]}  $   To effectively balance electron transport and defect passivation, Xu et al. proposed a porous insulator contact (PIC) structure, consisting of   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   with random nano-sized openings and adjustable thickness. This design effectively mitigated non-radiative recombination through the action of soft acid and hard base salts, facilitated efficient electron injection, and contributed to defect passivation.  $  ^{[18]}  $

However, several challenges remain in the application of oxide materials to PSCs. First, the processing of oxide materials is complex, and certain oxides may exhibit excessive vacancy defects, which can hinder the efficient transport of charge carriers.  $  ^{[19-21]}  $   Additionally, while oxide materials can enhance electron transport in some cases, they often fail to significantly improve the film quality or the structural stability of the perovskite layer.  $  ^{[22,23]}  $   Therefore, there is an urgent need to design new conductive porous/mesoporous materials at the molecular level. These materials should exhibit excellent electron transport properties, effectively passivate defects, and offer partial lead ion protection for PSCs, thereby enhancing both the stability and performance of the devices. Covalent organic frameworks (COFs) have emerged as highly promising functional materials for PSCs due to their periodic structure, regular pore architecture, exceptional thermal stability, and unique optoelectronic properties.  $  ^{[24-26]}  $   COF materials, with their strong conjugation and   $  \pi-\pi  $   interactions, exhibit exceptional charge transport properties, effectively facilitating the efficient migration of charges at the interface.  $  ^{[27]}  $   Furthermore, the functional groups within COFs can be tailored to meet specific needs, preserving excellent photoelectric performance while enabling robust intermolecular interactions. As a conductive porous/mesoporous interlayer, COF materials hold significant promise in balancing electron transport and defect passivation.  $  ^{[28,29]}  $

In this study, we designed a highly stable COF material, Cu-Por-COF, as a conductive porous layer (CPL) in PSCs. Cu-Por-COF consists of porphyrin coordinated with   $  Cu^{2+}  $   ions and pyrene diimidazole groups. The semiconductor properties of Cu-Por-COF arise from its unique   $  \pi-\pi  $   stacking interactions and the presence of   $  Cu^{2+}  $   ions, which work synergistically to enhance its electron transport capabilities. Experimental results demonstrate that Cu-Por-COF exhibits excellent structural stability, effectively mitigating the impact of vacancy defects on charge carriers while promoting efficient electron transport. Additionally, the abundant active sites (C=N, N-H) within Cu-Por-COF facilitate cation coordination and compensate for anion vacancies, thereby passivating defects in the perovskite layer. Leveraging the synergistic effects of electron injection and defect passivation, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of Cu-Por-COF-based perovskite solar cells reached 25.41%, accompanied by outstanding stability. Notably, Cu-Por-COF also plays a crucial role in preventing lead ion leakage, thus paving the way for the development of environmentally friendly and sustainable PSCs.

# Results and Discussion

We successfully synthesized imidazole-linked Cu-Por-COF materials (Figure 1a) using metalized Cu-5,10,15,20-tetra(4-

benzaldehyde)porphyrin (Cu-p-Por-CHO), pyrene-4,5,9,10-tetraketone (PyTO), and ammonium acetate (NH $ _{4} $ OAc) as reactants via a three-component Debus-Radziszewski reaction. $ ^{[30,31]} $  Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images revealed that Cu-Por-COF exhibited a well-defined layered structure, confirming the successful synthesis of the 2D Cu-Por-COF (Figure S1). Energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) mapping analysis (Figure S2) further demonstrated that the elements C, Cu, and N were uniformly distributed within the Cu-Por-COF structure. Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy showed the characteristic C≡N stretching vibration peak at 1604 cm $ ^{-1} $ , indicating the formation of an imidazole bond (Figure S3). Additionally, a new peak at 1003 cm $ ^{-1} $  was observed, which can be attributed to the coordination between the Cu ions and the polyazide center in the Cu-porphyrin compound, further confirming that Cu remains embedded within the COF framework even after the washing treatment. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) of Cu-Por-COF (Figures S4, S5) revealed Cu doublet peaks at 935.02 and 955.13 eV, corresponding to the coordination of Cu ions in the Cu-porphyrin structure, thereby verifying the successful retention of the coordinated Cu. Meanwhile, comparing the  $ ^{13} $ C NMR spectra of Cu-Por-COF with the model compound, signals at 126.6, 142.1, and 160.7 ppm were assigned to the carbon atoms of the imidazole group (Figure S6), $ ^{[30,32]} $  providing strong evidence for the presence of the imidazole structure in Cu-Por-COF.

The crystallinity of the Cu-Por-COF was confirmed by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD). Based on the theoretically predicted AA and AB stacking models of Cu-Por-COF (Figures S7, S8), the PXRD spectrum of the experimentally synthesized powder sample closely matches the AA stacking model (Figure 1b). In the low-angle region, the diffraction peaks exhibit a one-to-one correspondence, which is fully consistent with our previously reported results for Cu-Por-COF.  $  ^{[31]}  $   Above results confirm the successful synthesis of Cu-Por-COF and demonstrate that its   $  \tilde{E}  $   units exhibit an AA stacking columnar structure, providing an effective alignment pathway for directional carrier transport. Compared to the unmetallized COF previously synthesized,  $  ^{[30]}  $   the Cu-Por-COF material demonstrates significant advantages. First, the Cu-Por-COF with   $  Cu^{2+}  $   ions as the loading center enhances the material's conductivity, facilitating electron transport. Second, the metallized Cu-Por-COF exhibits excellent chemical and thermal stability, attributed to the robustness of its metal centers, which is essential for enhancing the long-term stability of PSCs. Finally, the metalized structure of Cu-Por-COF not only boosts its charge transfer capability but also strengthens the interfacial interaction with the perovskite layer through its surface enriched with functional groups. This interaction helps mitigate carrier recombination, thereby increasing the overall performance of the PSCs.  $  ^{[17,18]}  $

To further investigate the electrical properties of Cu-Por-COF, Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) was employed to analyze its surface potential. As shown in Figure 1c, the untreated Cu-Por-COF powder exhibits an aggregated, packing structure with particle sizes exceeding  $ 1\mu \mathrm{m} $ . This interaction lowers the   $  E_{Fermi}  $   of   $  SnO_{2}  $   and strengthens the built-in electric field. The alignment of the Fermi levels and the enhancement of the built-in electric field improve electron mobility, reduce carrier recombination, and promote efficient electron extraction and transport.  $  ^{[37]}  $   Therefore, we propose introducing Cu-Por-COF as a novel n-type CPL at the bottom interface of PSCs to enhance the device's photovoltaic conversion efficiency.

From a theoretical perspective, we further demonstrate the outstanding functionality of the synthesized Cu-Por-COF material. Using Gaussian 16 software for density functional theory (DFT) calculations, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of the intrinsic electronic properties of Cu-Por-COF. The Cu-Por-COF was modeled using the porphyrin (Por) and pyrene diimidazole (Py) components as minimal repeating units. The electrostatic surface potential (ESP), shown in Figure 1e, reveals a distinct distribution of the ability to gain and lose electrons between molecules. Combined with the main distribution of the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of Cu-Por-COF (Figure 1f,g), it was observed that these orbitals were predominantly concentrated on the C=N and N–H groups, respectively. This corresponds precisely to the electron donor (red region) and electron acceptor (blue region) areas in the ESP. These findings demonstrate that the HOMO and LUMO distributions of Cu-Por-COF align with the regions of electron donation and acceptance, respectively. This result indicates that photoexcited charges in Cu-Por-COF facilitate efficient spatial separation through intermolecular charge transfer, promoting electron transport and reducing carrier recombination.  $  ^{[26,38]}  $   In addition, we further investigated the interaction between Cu-Por-COF and FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite using Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) calculations. The results, shown in Figure 1h–j, reveal that the binding energies of Por and Py to FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   with the (010) crystal plane orientation are -1.03 and -0.78 eV, respectively, while the bonding energy (  $  E_{b}  $  ) of Cu-Por-COF to FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   is -1.96 eV. These findings indicate that the synergistic effect of the Por and Py components significantly enhances the interaction between Cu-Por-COF and FAPbI  $  _{3}  $ . Furthermore, the defect formation energy (  $  E_{f}  $  ) of oxygen vacancies (  $  V_{O}  $  ) in SnO  $  _{2}  $   is 2.43 eV, whereas the   $  E_{f}  $   increases to

4.33 eV after the introduction of Cu-Por-COF (Figure S12). This increase further demonstrates that the functional groups in Cu-Por-COF can effectively inhibit the formation of oxygen vacancies through intermolecular interactions.  $  ^{[39]}  $

The experiment further confirmed the interactions between Cu-Por-COF and   $  SnO_{2}  $  , as well as between Cu-Por-COF and the perovskite. As shown in Figure S13, upon combining with   $  SnO_{2}  $   or the perovskite, the stretching vibration peak of the C=N group in Cu-Por-COF and the new signal peak of the -Cu-C-N-group shifted from 1604.0 and   $  1003.0\ cm^{-1}  $   to lower wavenumbers, respectively.  $  ^{[40]}  $   At the same time, it was found that the -C-NH-C-vibration peak in the perovskite film also shifted significantly to lower wavenumbers after the introduction of Cu-Por-COF (Figure S13b). These shift suggests that the functional groups of Cu-Por-COF interact with   $  SnO_{2}  $   or the perovskite layer, likely due to the changes in the electron cloud density of these groups caused by electronic interactions, which in turn affect their vibration frequencies.  $  ^{[31]}  $   X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) further confirmed that the Sn 3d peak in   $  SnO_{2}  $   shifted by 0.23 eV to higher binding energy (Figure S4a). This shift may result from the formation of coordination bonds between the C=N group and the   $  Sn^{4+}  $   ion, or from electron transfer between Cu-Por-COF and the oxygen vacancies, which in turn alters the binding energy of the Sn 3d peak.  $  ^{[41]}  $   Additionally, the appearance of the Cu 2p signal on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   surface indicates that Cu-Por-COF has been successfully deposited onto the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   (Figure S14b). Simultaneously, the XPS peaks of Pb 4f and I 3d shifted by 0.15 eV to higher binding energies (Figure S14c,d). This shift can be attributed to the coordination between the C=N group and   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions, where the C=N group forms a coordination bond with   $  Pb^{2+}  $  . Additionally, hydrogen bonding between the -C-NH-group and   $  I^{-}  $  was observed. This hydrogen bonding results from the interaction between the nitrogen and hydrogen atoms in the -C-NH-group and the   $  I^{-}  $  ion, which enhances the electron density of   $  I^{-}  $  and facilitates the interaction.  $  ^{[42]}  $   In summary, the combination of experimental data and theoretical calculations shows that Cu-Por-COF effectively passivates   $  Pb^{2+}  $   and   $  I^{-}  $  defects, improves the quality of the perovskite film, and enhances device performance.

Thanks to the functional groups incorporated into the molecular design of the Cu-Por-COF and its inherent optoelectronic properties, we aim to confine the long-range electron diffusion by constructing a CPL, thereby reducing the probability of carrier recombination (Figure 2a). We expect that on the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  surface, Cu-Por-COF with a specific coverage selectively extracts electrons through its n-type semiconductor properties and  $ \pi -\pi $  stacking effect (Figure 2b). This facilitates direct electron passage through the Cu-Por-COF layer, enhancing electron transport and reducing the diffusion length within the perovskite layer. Additionally, the excellent stability of Cu-Por-COF suppresses non-radiative recombination at the perovskite bottom interface during electron transfer, further lowering the likelihood of carrier recombination. By depositing Cu-Por-COF materials with different concentrations on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   surface, we used AFM to observe the morphology changes of Cu-Por-COF at different concentrations. As the Cu-Por-COF concentration increases, AFM reveals the coarsening and vertical growth of clusters (Figures 2e-g and S10–S18), with the corresponding Cover rising from 6% at 0.2 mg   $  ml^{-1}  $   to 78% at 1.5 mg   $  ml^{-1}  $  . Additionally, the exposed   $  SnO_{2}  $   surface in the AFM image shows a clean and flat background, suggesting that Cu-Por-COF has successfully formed a CPL on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   surface. Next, we deposited the perovskite active layer, and the cross-sectional view (Figures 2h-j, and S19) reveals that the interface size between the perovskite and Cu-Por-COF layers is consistent with the results observed in the AFM. More importantly, we observed that the perovskite film quality was optimal at a Cu-Por-COF concentration of 0.4 mg   $  ml^{-1}  $  . This can be attributed to the ability of the functional groups in Cu-Por-COF to effectively passivate defects in the perovskite film, and the introduction of the Cu-Por-COF enhances the crystallinity of the perovskite film. Therefore, Cu-Por-COF with a concentration of 0.4 mg   $  ml^{-1}  $   holds significant research potential, which may substantially improve the performance of PSCs.

We investigated the performance of perovskite films incorporating 0.4 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  Cu-Por-COF. AFM and SEM characterization revealed a significant increase in the grain size of the perovskite film (Figures 3a and S20). This result further supports the notion that Cu-Por-COF enhances the crystallization quality of the perovskite film. Based on the in situ KPFM scans obtained via AFM (Figure 3b), we observed a significant reduction in potential fluctuations on the surface of the Cu-Por-COF-based perovskite film. This indicates a substantial decrease in charged defects (both positive and negative) on the film surface.  $  ^{[43]}  $  These results demonstrate that the introduction of Cu-Por-COF effectively passivates defects in the perovskite film, and the interaction between functional groups and the perovskite helps inhibit ion migration. The grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) results further confirm that the crystallinity of the perovskite film improves upon the introduction of Cu-Por-COF (Figure 3c). Additionally, the excess PbI  $  _{2}  $  peak near  $  q_{xy} = 0.9  $  in the Cu-Por-COF-based perovskite film is significantly reduced. Excessive PbI  $  _{2}  $  can lead to the formation of poor phase interfaces, which negatively impact the uniformity and long-term stability of the film.  $  ^{[44]}  $  The reduction of PbI  $  _{2}  $  content helps to optimize the structure of perovskite films, thereby improving their optoelectronic properties and device stability.

The crystal structures of the perovskite films, both with and without the introduction of Cu-Por-COF, were investigated using high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM). As shown in Figure 3d,e, both films exhibit clear crystal orientation, indicating the high quality of the prepared perovskite films. In the absence of Cu-Por-COF, the lattice spacing of the perovskite film is measured at  $ 0.312\mathrm{nm} $ , which aligns closely with the theoretical value for the (220) crystal plane of the perovskite cubic phase.[45] Upon the introduction of Cu-Por-COF, the (220) crystal plane spacing of the perovskite film decreases to  $ 0.303\mathrm{nm} $ , which

corresponds to a slight shift of the diffraction peak toward a smaller angle. This shift could be attributed to Cu-Por-COF either reducing the tensile strain during perovskite crystallization or facilitating the substitution of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions with smaller-radius   $  Cu^{2+}  $   ions, both of which may induce subtle changes in the lattice structure. To confirm that the introduction of Cu-Por-COF reduces the tensile strain in the perovskite layer, we employed grazing incidence X-ray diffraction (GIXRD) to analyze the stress changes in the perovskite film (Figure 3f,g). As the tilt angle (  $  \psi  $  ) of the instrument increases, the diffraction peak of the (220) crystal plane in the control perovskite film shifts progressively to the left, indicating an increase in interplanar spacing and greater tensile strain in the film.  $  ^{[46]}  $   However, after the introduction of Cu-Por-COF, the shift in the diffraction peak is notably reduced. Linear fitting of the GIXRD data reveals that the slope (in absolute value) for the control film is 0.41, significantly higher than the value of 0.20 observed after the introduction of Cu-Por-COF (Figure 3h). This suggests that Cu-Por-COF effectively alleviates the residual strain in the perovskite film,  $  ^{[47]}  $   thereby enhancing the film stability.

Next, we investigated the possible substitution of   $  Cu^{2+}  $   ions in Cu-Por-COF for   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions in perovskite to affect the lattice structure. Using secondary time-of-flight mass spectrometry (TOF-SIMS) measurement (Figure S21), we observed a clear distribution of   $  Cu^{2+}  $   ions within the perovskite bottom layer. Theoretical calculations (Figures S22, S23) show that the   $  E_{b}  $   of   $  Cu^{2+}  $   in the porphyrin group is -2.17 eV, while the   $  E_{b}  $   of   $  Cu^{2+}  $   replacing   $  Pb^{2+}  $   at the perovskite surface termination is -2.15 eV, indicating a negligible difference. These results indicate that a small amount of   $  Cu^{2+}  $   may migrate from Cu-Por-COF and replace   $  Pb^{2+}  $   in the perovskite lattice, or occupy possible Pb vacancies (  $  V_{Pb}  $  ). Given the relatively low bond energy of the Pb–I bond in the perovskite (approximately 1.47 eV),  $  ^{[48]}  $   this migration process is feasible. Moreover, we hypothesize that the substituted uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions may chelate with the functionalized groups (e.g., C≡N) in Cu-Por-COF, which is supported by our experimental results mentioned above. Therefore, the limited substitution of   $  Cu^{2+}  $   and the balance between metal ions do not disrupt the structure of Cu-Por-COF. On the contrary, due to its intact framework structure, Cu-Por-COF can serve as a CPL to enhance device performance, which will be discussed in detail below.

The space charge limited current (SCLC) test was used to measure the defect density in the perovskite layer. A pure electronic device with the  $ \mathrm{FTO / SnO_2 / Perovskite / PC_{61}BM / Ag} $  structure was fabricated, and the perovskite film was tested both with and without the introduction of Cu-Por-COF (Figure 5d and Table S2). The defect density  $ (N_{\mathrm{t}}) $  of the perovskite film was determined using the following formula:

$$
N _ {t} = \frac {2 \varepsilon_ {r} \varepsilon_ {0} V _ {T F L}}{\mathrm{e} L ^ {2}}
$$

where, e, L,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $  , and   $  \varepsilon_{r}  $   represent the electron charge, film thickness (L = 800 nm), vacuum dielectric constant, and relative dielectric constant of the perovskite film (  $  \varepsilon_{r} = 32  $  ), respectively.  $  ^{[55]}  $   The   $  N_{t}  $   of the device without Cu-Por-COF and

with Cu-Por-COF were   $  2.44 \times  $   and   $  2.03 \times 10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  , respectively. After introducing Cu-Por-COF, the reduction in defect density is attributed to its passivation effect on the perovskite, further demonstrating that Cu-Por-COF effectively balances electron transport and defect passivation. This also prevents voltage loss caused by the increased CPL/perovskite contact interface.  $  ^{[17,18]}  $   Additionally, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) in the dark state further confirmed that the introduction of Cu-Por COF led to a decrease in the charge transfer resistance (  $  R_{tr}  $  ) of the device, indicating an enhancement in electron transport capability.  $  ^{[56,57]}  $   The increase in charge recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) suggests that Cu-Por COF effectively passivated film defects (Figure 4e and Table S3), which is consistent with the findings from the SCLC analysis.

The impact of air environment preparation conditions on device performance and stability should not be overlooked.  $  ^{[65,66]}  $   We fabricated devices under ambient air conditions throughout the entire process, with the Cu-Por-COF-based device achieving a maximum efficiency of 24.87% (Figure S34 and Table S9). In the molecular design, we selected monomers containing  $ -\mathrm{C} = \mathrm{N}- $  and  $ -\mathrm{NH}- $  functional groups. # Conclusion

We successfully demonstrated a novel Cu-Por-COF material as a porous layer in PSCs. Embedding functionalized Cu-Por-COF at the bottom of the perovskite film significantly enhances electron collection efficiency while effectively hinders hole transport. With the introduction of Cu-Por-COF functional groups, Cu-Por-COF as a CPL achieves an optimal balance between electron transport and defect passivation, leading to a significant improvement in device performance, with the PCE of the device prepared in the air reaching 25.41%. Additionally, the   $  Cu^{2+}  $   ions in Cu-Por-COF stabilize the perovskite lattice, which helps the device retain excellent stability under various conditions. More importantly, using Cu-Por-COF as a CPL demonstrates excellent lead ion absorption capacity, making it a simple, scalable technology that enhances both the efficiency and stability of PSCs while also meeting environmental requirements. We anticipate that the optoelectronic properties of Cu-Por-COF as a CPL will also pave a way for its potential application in light-emitting devices.
