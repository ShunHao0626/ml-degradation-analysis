# Highly Efficient Organic Hole Transporting Materials for Perovskite and Organic Solar Cells with Long-Term Stability

Saripally Sudhaker Reddy, Kumarasamy Gunasekar, Jin Hyuck Heo, Sang Hyuk Im, Chang Su Kim, Dong-Ho Kim, Jong Hun Moon, Jin Yong Lee, Myungkwan Song,* and Sung-Ho Jin*

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have recently attracted considerable attention due to their excellent photovoltaic performance and facile/low-cost production process. Researchers worldwide have demonstrated that power conversion efficiency (PCE) is one of the greatest assets of PSCs, particularly of those based on methylammonium lead halides (CH₃NH₃PbX, MAPbX₃, where X = I, Br, or Cl). As direct band gap materials, perovskites have high extinction coefficients, good absorption, ambipolar charge transport, and large free carrier diffusion lengths. These properties allow perovskites to exhibit a strong light harvesting ability across the UV-visible region of solar spectrum.  $  ^{[1-12]}  $   A dramatic enhancement of PCE has been observed in PSCs by incorporating hole transporting materials (HTMs) between the perovskite and metal electrode. This increase in PCE was attributed to the improvement in charge extraction and mitigation of undesired recombination at the interface, as reported by Yu and Sun and Jen and co-workers.  $  ^{[13,14]}  $   In this regard, the design and synthesis of new and efficient HTMs is the most promising way to achieve high PCEs in PSCs. Yang et al. and Park et al. have reported PCEs of ≈19% and 19.7%, respectively, using 2,2',7,7'-tertrakis(N,N-p-dimethoxyphenylamino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) as an HTM and lead-based

perovskite as a light harvester.  $  ^{[15,16]}  $   However, aside from its potential HTM features, the complex synthesis, high cost, and low charge carrier mobility of spiro-OMeTAD limit its potential for commercialization. Furthermore, when polymer-based HTMs, such as poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) and poly(triarylamine) (PTAA), with appreciable PCEs were used, the device stability, reproducibility, and synthetic complexity have been identified as key limitations.  $  ^{[10,17-19]}  $   Although several types of HTMs have been designed and synthesized, PCEs of only 10%–15% was achieved in PSCs.  $  ^{[20-31]}  $   Therefore, the realization of high device performance will require the design and development of alternative efficient small molecule (SM) HTMs.  $  ^{[13,32]}  $

The promising yield, high purity, defined molecular structure, and better batch-to-batch reproducibility of SM HTMs have greatly increased the performance of bulk heterojunction inverted organic solar cells (BHJ IOSCs) in addition to their pivotal role in PSCs.  $  ^{[33]}  $   However, considerable room for improvement remains in terms of efficiency and stability through the use of new HTMs in PSCs and BHJ IOSCs. Recent reports have demonstrated that the systematic molecular design strategies enable the use of a single material for dual applications.  $  ^{[34-37]}  $   Therefore, the development of novel SM HTMs that exhibit suitable characteristics for applications as both a stable PSC and BHJ IOSC with promising PCEs is challenging. No SM HTMs with a PCE and stability comparable to those of spiroOMeTAD and PEDOT:PSS have yet been reported.

Here, we demonstrate the hole transporting ability of two organic SM HTMs, namely, 7-(9,9'-spirobifluorene-2-yl)-N-(7-(9,9'-spirobifluorene-2-yl)-9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluoren-2-yl)-N-(4-(9H-carbazol-9-yl)phenyl)-9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluoren-2-amine (CzPAF-SBF) and 7-(7'-carbonitrile-9,9'-spirobifluorene-2-yl)-N-(7-(7'-carbonitrile-9,9'-spirobifluorene-2-yl)-9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluoren-2-yl)-N-(4-(9H-carbazol-9-yl)phenyl)-9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluoren-2-amine (CzPAF-SBFN), as highly efficient PSCs and BHJ IOSCs with an excellent long-term stability and reproducibility. In particular, the CzPAF-SBF-based devices have exhibited remarkable device performance in PSCs and BHJ IOSCs with maximum PCEs of 17.21% and 7.93%, respectively, which are superior to those of standard spiro-OMeTAD and PEDOT:PSS, respectively. In addition, CzPAF-SBF has shown excellent stability in PSCs and BHJ IOSCs. This study reveals that a systematic design strategy of SM HTMs can enhance the PCE and stability of PSCs and BHJ IOSCs.

To gain insight into the geometrical configuration and electronic distribution of CzPAF-SBF and CzPAF-SBFN, we performed density functional theory (DFT) calculations with B3LYP functional and 6-311+G** basis sets using a suite of Gaussian 09 programs.  $  ^{[41]}  $   As shown in Figure 1c, the electron density of the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) is primarily localized on fluorene moieties and the phenyl group adjacent to the amino group. However, the lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (LUMOs) of CzPAF-SBF and CzPAF-SBFN are different. The LUMO electron density of CzPAF-SBF is distributed over the   $  \pi  $  -conjugated system through the fluorene and spirobifluorene moieties, whereas CzPAF-SBFN shows a significant ICT from fluorene to spirobifluorene moieties due to the electron-withdrawing CN group. In addition, the HOMO/LUMO energy levels were calculated to be -5.11/-1.75 and -5.29/-2.24 eV for CzPAF-SBF and CzPAF-SBFN, respectively. Thus, the calculated HOMO-LUMO energy gaps of CzPAF-SBF and CzPAF-SBFN were 3.36 and 3.05 eV, respectively, and were close to the experimentally determined values of 2.89 and 2.76 eV. The HOMO-LUMO energy gap difference is attributed to the difference in the LUMO energy level rather than in the HOMO energy level.

To experimentally determine the HOMO and LUMO energy levels, we conducted cyclic voltammetry (CV) experiments for both CzPAF-SBF and CzPAF-SBFN in   $  CHCl_{3}  $   solutions. As illustrated in Figure S3 (Supporting Information), both CzPAF-SBF and CzPAF-SBFN underwent irreversible oxidation processes and exhibited oxidation peaks at 1.07 V. The HOMO and LUMO of CZPAF-SBF were estimated to be -5.26 and -2.37 eV, respectively, compared with -5.27 and -2.57 eV for CzPAF-SBFN. The device architectures and energy level diagrams of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  -based PSCs and BHJ OSCs are shown in Figure 1d–g. The HOMO energy levels of both CzPAF-SBF and CzPAF-SBFN are well matched with   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   (-5.43 eV).  $  ^{[6]}  $   Thus, good charge separation and charge transfer at the interface is expected. When compared to the HOMO of the benchmark HTM spiro-OMeTAD (HOMO, -5.22 eV), the HOMO energy levels of the two synthesized HTMs are slightly deeper, which was attributed to the introduction of phenyl and fluorene rings around the N-atom, where the HOMO density was located. Furthermore, the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) primarily depends on the difference between the quasi-Fermi level of zinc oxide (ZnO) and the HOMO level of the HTM (CzPAF-SBF/CzPAF-SBFN/spiro-OMeTAD) for PSCs. Consequently, a higher   $  V_{oc}  $   value is expected for PSCs when using the two synthesized HTMs.

To investigate the hole transporting properties of CzPAF-SBF and CzPAF-SBFN, the hole-only devices were fabricated with configurations of ITO/MoO $ _3 $ /CzPAF-SBF, CzPAF-SBFN or spiro-OMeTAD/MoO $ _3 $ /Ag and were evaluated using the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method (Figure S4, Supporting Information). The current density–voltage ( $ J-V $ ) characteristics were recorded under dark conditions. At higher voltages, assuming Ohmic injecting contacts and trap-free transport, the measured current is only space–charge limited, and the charge carrier mobility can be estimated using Mott–Gurney's equation.

Specifically,   $  J = (9/8)\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}\varepsilon_{0}\mu(V^{2}/L^{3})  $  , where   $  \varepsilon_{r}  $   is the dielectric constant (  $  \varepsilon_{r} = 3  $  ),   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the permittivity of free space, L is the thickness of an HTM layer,   $  \mu  $   is the charge mobility, and V is the voltage drop across the device. The extracted hole mobilities of pristine CzPAF-SBF and CzPAF-SBFN were   $  2.18 \times 10^{-4}  $   and   $  1.05 \times 10^{-4}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $  , respectively, which were comparable to or higher than that of spiro-OMeTAD (  $  1.80 \times 10^{-4}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $  ). However, the hole mobilities of Li-salt-doped CzPAF-SBF, CzPAF-SBFN, and spiro-OMeTAD are   $  1.27 \times 10^{-3}  $  ,   $  5.07 \times 10^{-4}  $  , and   $  9.28 \times 10^{-4}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $  , respectively. Consequently, the higher hole mobility of CzPAF-SBF improved the device efficiency compared with that of CzPAF-SBFN and spiro-OMeTAD. The lower mobility of CzPAF-SBFN was attributed to the introduction of an electron-withdrawing CN group on SBF. In addition, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was performed under 1 sun illumination on PSCs configured with CzPAF-SBF, CzPAF-SBFN, and spiro-OMeTAD as HTMs to investigate hole/charge transport in these PSCs (Figure S4c,d, Supporting Information). The results were fitted to an appropriate equivalent circuit model that has been previously reported.  $  ^{[42]}  $   We extracted and analyzed the fitted EIS spectra parameters. Thus, a recombination loss process was identified at low frequency, and the high frequency was attributed to the diffusion of holes through HTMs. The recombination rate is inversely proportional to the recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) obtained from the fittings and is represented in Figure S4c (Supporting Information). The   $  R_{rec}  $   value followed an exponential decrease with increasing bias voltage. Specifically,   $  R_{rec} = \eta \times \exp\left(-\frac{eV}{AK_{B}T}\right)  $  , where   $  \eta, e, V, A, k_{B}  $  , and T correspond to the preexponential factor, elementary charge, bias voltage, ideality factor, Boltzmann constant, and absolute temperature, respectively.  $  ^{[43]}  $   At the applied low and high voltages, the order of   $  R_{rec}  $   is   $  R_{rec}  $   (CzPAF-SBF) >   $  R_{rec}  $   (spiro-OMeTAD) >   $  R_{rec}  $   (CzPAF-SBFN), which provides longer free charge carrier lifetimes for better charge transport, improves performances of PSCs and explains the   $  V_{oc}  $   trend (  $  V_{OC}  $   (CzPAF-SBF) >   $  V_{OC}  $   (spiro-OMeTAD) >   $  V_{OC}  $   (CzPAF-SBFN)). Moreover, we estimated the hole conductivity (  $  \sigma_{HTM}  $  ) of HTMs from the transport resistance (  $  R_{HTM}  $  ) shown in the inset of Figure S4d (Supporting Information). Specifically,   $  R_{HTM} = L/A\sigma_{HTM}  $  , where L is the HTM layer thickness and A is the active film area. Conductivity increases with an increase in applied voltage due to the higher hole density gradient in HTM, which is generated via hole injection from the perovskite material.  $  ^{[43]}  $   The order of conductivities for HTMs is   $  \sigma_{CzPAF-SBF} > \sigma_{spiro-OMeTAD} > \sigma_{CzPAF-SBFN}  $  , which correlates with device performances of the respective HTMs, as shown in Table 1.

The total thickness of PSCs was  $ \approx $ 500 nm as they consisted of a dense 50 nm ZnO layer, a 350 nm MAPbI $ _3 $  layer, and a 40 nm HTM layer that was uniformly capped. Finally, a  $ \approx $ 60 nm thick Au electrode was deposited via thermal evaporation.

The obtained results were fitted to the equivalent circuit model, which is shown in the inset of Figure 3c. In general, a semicircle on the Nyquist

plot is associated with charge transfer across the electrode interface. Moreover, the semicircle diameter strongly depended on the HTM in BHJ IOSCs. In this circuit,   $  R_{s}  $   represents the ohmic resistance, including the electrodes and bulk resistance in the active layer.   $  R_{ct}  $   is related to the interface charge transport process, and the constant phase element (CPE) suggests a non-ideal capacitor behavior. The   $  R_{s}  $ ,   $  R_{ct}  $ , and CPE observed values are presented in Table S6 (Supporting Information). This result leads to an increased   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF, which enhances the photovoltaic performance.

In conclusion, we have designed and synthesized two facile SM HTMs, CzPAF-SBF and CzPAF-SBFN, through coupling reactions with an N-atom serving as a linker, phenylcarbazole and fluorene groups as the main scaffold, and SBF/SBFN as end cappers. The two HTMs showed suitable energy levels, high hole mobility, and good solubility. These unique characteristics support the potential application of these materials as HTMs in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -based PSCs and PTB7:PC  $  _{71}  $  BM-based BHJ IOSCs, and the device characterization results indicated high PCEs. In particular, CzPAF-SBF exhibited an outstanding device performance compared with CzPAF-SBFN, spiro-OMeTAD, and PEDOT:PSS in both PSCs and BHJ IOSCs. The CzPAF-SBF-based PSCs delivered a high PCE of 17.21% under AM 1.5G illumination at an intensity of 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , which was superior to that of the widely used HTM spiro-OMeTAD (16.67%) under the same conditions. CzPAF-SBF showed excellent device stability in both PSCs and BHJ IOSCs. Furthermore, these HTMs are reproducible, and their characteristics render them commercially viable. When applied to BHJ IOSCs, CzPAF-SBF exhibited a PCE of 7.93%, which was higher than that of a standard HTM PEDOT:PSS (7.74%). The promising PCE results based on the design strategy proposed herein will open new and practical avenues for the development of new SM HTMs based on phenylcarbazole/fluorene and SBF derivatives for efficient, stable, and reproducible PSCs and BHJ IOSCs.
