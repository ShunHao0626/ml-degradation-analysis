Abstract: Conventionally, the hydroscopic nature of Li-TFSI and low boiling point of t-BP are considered as the primary limitations of hole transport layer (HTL), ultimately affecting the power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term stability of perovskite solar cell (PSC). To better stress these problems, a dual functional dopant termed PFPPY is reported. The in-depth operating mechanism of PFPPY with Spiro-OMeTAD, its profound effects on overall photovoltaic performance and device physics are systematically investigated. It is observed PFPPY can simultaneously take place of t-BP and FK209 in conventional HTL. By employing PFPPY as dopant cooperating with Spiro-OMeTAD, a higher PCE of  $ 21.38\% $  is achieved, compared with the reference device based on t-BP and FK209-doped Spiro-OMeTAD  $ (19.69\%) $ . More importantly, the unencapsulated PFPPY-doped device shows greatly improved stability, maintaining over  $ 90\% $  of its initial PCE after  $ 600\mathrm{h} $  in  $ 40 - 50\% $  RH. These findings provide a new strategy to optimize the HTL composition for efficient and stable PSCs.

# Main Text

During the past decade, perovskite solar cell (PSC) keeps attracting intense research attention due to low-cost, easy fabrication process and rapid improvement in power conversion efficiency (PCE) from  $ 3.8\% $  in 2009 to  $ 25.2\% $  in 2019.[1-11] The rapid boom of PSC technology was derived from inherent unique organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite properties such as large absorption coefficient, tuneable band gaps, long charge carrier diffusion length, and high charge carrier mobility. [12-21] Although the photovoltaic performance of PSC devices are very close to crystalline silicon solar cells, higher PCE and better long-term stability are still urgently desired for the further commercialization. Many researches manifested that the commonly used hole transport layer (HTL) is a double-edged sword for highly efficient PSCs.[22, 23] On the one hand, the introduction of HTL can facilitate the hole extraction, restrict the charge carrier recombination during device working process and avoid the corrosion of metal electrode; but on the other hand, the intrinsic properties of additive and dopant in HTL increase device instability. For instance, the liquid additive 4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP) would volatilize during the HTL preparation and vacuum evaporation processes, leaving many pinholes on HTL surface. The moisture can easily penetrate HTL through these pinholes and then degrade perovskite.[22] Meanwhile, the corrosion nature of t-BP to perovskite make the stability even worse.[24] The hydroscopic nature of bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI) also limits the device stability. For commonly used p-type dopant tris(2-(1H-pyrazol-1-yl)-4-tert-butylpyridine)-cobalt(III)Tris(bis-(trifluoromethylsulfo

nyl)imide)) (FK209),[25] it hardly improves the potential instability of HTL, moreover, the high cost and complicated synthesis process are another unfavourable factors for its large-scale application. Hence, the compositional engineering of HTL is imperative for highly stable and efficient PSC.

At present, many research groups expended a considerable amount of attention on developing novel hole transport materials (HTMs), unfortunately, merely reported HTMs could fully prevail over Spiro-OMeTAD.[26-33] With the dopant-free HTMs, although the humidity stability can be greatly enhanced, the achieved PCEs are still lagging.[28-31] Few groups focus the research on HTL composition engineering, which would be one of the main sources to prompt the instability of device.[34] Based on the above considerations, to diminish the side effects of t-BP and decrease the cost of HTL, in this work, a novel dual effective dopant PFPPY was reported. At one hand, the newly designed PFPPY represents a favourable  $  p  $ -type property, enhancing the hole extraction and transport properties of Spiro-OMeTAD. On the other hand, PFPPY can passivate surface defects of perovskite, and segregate perovskite from moisture vapor. Through composition engineering, the PSCs adopting new HTL composition of Spiro-OMeTAD+Li-TFSI+15% PFPPY (15% is defined as molar ratio relative to Spiro-OMeTAD) showed a remarkable  $  PCE  $  of 21.38%, which is much higher than that of the control PSCs (19.69%). Most importantly, the PSC adopting newly developed HTL exhibits an excellent stability, maintaining 90% of its initial  $  PCE  $  after aging test under ambient condition for 600 hours.

The detailed structure of PFPPY is shown in Figure 1a. The novel organic small molecule dopant PFPPY was synthesized by simple one-step reaction with low-cost raw materials as shown in scheme S1. The synthesized PFPPY was confirmed by FT-IR,  $ ^{1} $ H NMR,  $ ^{13} $ C NMR,  $ ^{19} $ F NMR and HRMS spectroscopy. 1,2,3,4,5-pentafluoro-phenyl were introduced on N-position of 1,4-dihydropyrrolo[3,2-b]pyrrole (PPY) to weaken its electron donating ability, rendering a deep-lying the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level, and making PFPPY be a p-type dopant. The UV-Vis absorption spectra of PFPPY was measured in DCM solution as shown in Figure 1b. The first absorption peak locating at  $ 241\mathrm{nm} $  is attributed to  $ \pi-\pi^{*} $  transition and the second absorption peak observed at  $ 318\mathrm{nm} $  results from the intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) between PPY to pyridine unit, which is supported by density functional theory (DFT) calculations (see Figure S1). The HOMO energy level distributes on 1,2,3,4,5-pentafluoro-phenyl-substituted PPY moiety, while the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy level delocalizes over the whole molecular skeleton and charge density obviously increased on the electron deficient units. The ICT properties are beneficial to enhance the charge carrier mobility. The optical band gap ( $ E_{g}^{\mathrm{opt}} $ ) of PFPPY was calculated to be  $ 3.25\mathrm{eV} $  from absorption onset. As shown in Figure 1c, PFPPY displayed an  $ E_{1/2}^{\mathrm{PFPPY}} $  at  $ 0.640\mathrm{V} $  vs  $ Fc/Fc^{+} $  ( $ E_{1/2}^{\mathrm{ferrocene}} $ :  $ 0.193\mathrm{V} $  vs  $ \mathrm{Ag/AgNO_3} $ , see supporting information Figure S1a). Correspondingly, the HOMO and LUMO energy levels of PFPPY were calculated to be  $ -5.60\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ -2.35\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively, by using the equations:  $ E_{\mathrm{HOMO}} = - (E_{1/2}^{\mathrm{PFPPY}} - E_{1/2}^{\mathrm{ferrocene}} + 5.1)\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ E_{\mathrm{LUMO}} = (E_{\mathrm{HOMO}} + E_{\mathrm{g}})\mathrm{eV} $ . The experimentally calculated HOMO level is very close to theoretically calculated value of  $ -5.58\mathrm{eV} $  (DFT method). The PFPPY showed a more deep-lying HOMO level compared with

As decisive factors for photovoltaic performance, the conductivity and hole mobility of HTL with different composition were measured by using two-probe electrical conductivity method and space charge limited current (SCLC) method, respectively (see Figure 2c, 2d, S3 and S4), and the relative results data were represented in Table 1 and S1. The conventionally used HTL with composition of Spiro-OMeTAD+Li-TFSI+t-BP+FK209 serves as the control HTL. The pristine Spiro-OMeTAD and the control HTL showed relative low conductivity of  $ 1.41 \times 10^{-6} \mathrm{~S} \cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-1} $  and  $ 2.73 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~S} \cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ , respectively. Figure S5 shows that the perovskite/HTL samples display significantly quenched PL emission. Furthermore, the  $ 15\% $  PFPPY dopant based HTL showed a higher PL quenching effect compared with the control HTL. This results clearly indicate that the interfacial hole transfer between  $ 15\% $  PFPPY dopant based HTL and perovskite is more efficient. The morphology and uniformity of the PFPPY dopant based HTL is further investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM), as shown in Figure 3. The control HTL shows lots of pinholes and rough surface, while the PFPPY dopant based HTL shows more homogeneous, pinhole-free and smoother surface. The PFPPY dopant based HTL film exhibits a lower root-mean-square roughness  $ (R) $  of  $ 7.7\mathrm{nm} $ , compared with the control HTL film of  $ 8.2\mathrm{nm} $ . The introduction of PFPPY is benefit for obtaining better morphology and quantity films without pinholes, which is crucial for restricting charge recombination and enhancing the device stability.

We conducted Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR) to in-depth investigate the interaction between Li-TFSI and PFPPY (Figure S7). From the results we can see that the strong pyridine stretching frequency located at  $ 1372~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $  is remarkably suppressed with addition of Li-TFSI. This result suggests that the pyridine unit of PFPPY may interact with  $ \mathrm{Li^{+}} $  to form PFPPY-Li-TFSI complex, further facilitating to inhibit the aggregation of Li-TFSI.[23] We also performed Raman spectroscopy to explore the composition interaction of the whole HTL (Figure S8). For pristine Spiro-OMeTAD, the characteristic peaks of fluorene units are mainly located at  $ 714.93~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ ,  $ 734.88~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $  and  $ 755.31~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ .[40] For the PFPPY dopant based HTL, the secondary characteristic peak of fluorene units is slightly shifted from  $ 734.88~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $  to  $ 732.20~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $  and the peaks located at  $ 755.31~\mathrm{cm}^{-1} $  is dramatically enhanced, which is also detected in the case of control HTL. These results indicate that the composition interaction occurred in the PFPPY dopant based HTL is similar to the control HTL.

The charge collection and recombination behaviour of control and PFPPY based PSCs have been evaluated through light intensity measurements and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements, as shown in Figure 5 and Figure S12. The EIS testing results show that  $ 15\% $  PFPPY based PSC exhibits larger recombination resistance  $ (73\Omega) $  than control device  $ (39\Omega) $ , suggestion the charge recombination at perovskite/HTL surface is well restricted. By plotting the double logarithmic graph, and through linearly fitting the data for  $ J_{sc} $  as a function of light intensity in Figure 5a, it is observed that PFPPY based device shows larger  $ \alpha $  value of 1.00, than the control device  $ (\alpha = 0.95) $ , indicating that PFPPY based device possesses more efficient charge extraction probability from the perovskite layer to the HTL. To evaluate the recombination at the interface between perovskite/HTL, we plot the  $ V_{oc} $  as a function of lnI (see Figure 5b). By linearly fitting the data points for both devices, it is found that PFPPY based device shows lower slope value  $ 1.23(\mathrm{kT / q}) $  compared with  $ 1.34(\mathrm{kT / q}) $  for control device. Lower slope value hints the suppression of non-radiative bimolecular recombination in the PFPPY based device.

maintaining about 90% of its original PCE after 600 hours, while the reference device kept only 54% of the original performance. As shown in Figure S13, we measured the contact angle of water droplets on the HTL with different composition. The PFPPY dopant based HTL displayed a slightly larger contact angle of 93.48° than the control HTL (90.90°), indicating the better hydrophobicity of PFPPY dopant based HTL. Furthermore, to evaluate the effect of PFPPY dopant on the stability of perovskite, we measured the UV-Vis absorption spectra of different HTLs coated perovskite film stored under ambient conditions with 40-50% RH before and after 600 h aging test (see Figure S14). The absorption spectrum of control film shows a significant decrease compared with the PFPPY dopant based one after 600 h aging test due to the degradation of perovskite. The better stability of PFPPY dopant-based PSC device may mainly derived from the less pinholes in the HTL film, which better isolate the moisture from perovskite. Moreover, the stability under illumination (1sun) in air condition with white LED light near MMP was also conducted. The normalized PCEs of typical devices as a function of the aging time are shown in Figure S15. The 15% PFPPY based device retained approximately 83% of its initial PCE after 120 h light soaking tests, whereas the control device with t-bP+FK209 retained only approximately 49% of initial PCE under the same conditions. The results indicate the newly developed additive is also helpful for illumination stability.

In summary, we conducted a research work on HTL composition engineering by introducing a newly developed dopant PFPPY instead of traditional components t-BP and FK209. The incorporation of PFPPY greatly improved the film morphology,

We believe that the new developed PFPPY is promising dopant in HTL for obtaining stable and highly efficient PSC, and the research results pave a new path to construct efficient and stable PSCs.

# Highlight

- A 1,4-dihydropyrrolo[3,2-b]pyrrole (PPY) core units based small molecular material termed PFPPY is reported.

- PFPPY is a dual functional material, it can simultaneously work as p-type dopant and defect passivator.
