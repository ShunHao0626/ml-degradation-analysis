Abstract: Perylene diimide based small molecules are widely used as intermediates of liquid crystals due to the high planarity and electron mobility. In this work, a small molecule tetrachloro-perylene diimide (TCI-PDI) replacing  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  served as electron-transporting material (ETM) for the planar perovskite solar cells (PVSCs). Among hole-transporting materials of PVSCs, poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT) endows the devices highest stability and reproducibility. Therefore, the PVSCs with the structure of ITO/electron-transporting layer/perovskite/P3HT/MoO $ _3 $ /Ag was used to value the performances of new ETMs. The reference devices with compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and P3HT display a reasonable power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 12.78\% $ . While the PVSCs using TCI-PDI as ETM exhibit a promising PCE of  $ 14.73\% $ , among the highest values of the literatures reported on the PVSCs using undoped P3HT. Moreover, TCI-PDI based devices display higher stability than that of the devices based on compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  due to the superior perovskite quality.

# Introduction

The study on organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) have made tremendous progress over the past few years, due to the exceptional photo-physical properties of perovskite materials such as high panchromatic absorption, $ ^{[1]} $  charge carrier diffusion lengths, $ ^{[2-4]} $  the potential for low-cost solution fabrication processing, and power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the PVSCs has been boosted from 3.8% $  ^{[5]}  $  to over 23%. $ ^{[6-7]} $  Most highly efficient PVSCs utilize a p-type layer of spiro-OMeTAD in the n-i-p device configuration. Besides additives and oxidization are needed, the spiro-based PVSCs are thermally unstable and much sensitive to the fabrication condition. By contrast, poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT), another popular used hole-transporting material (HTM), $ ^{[8-11]} $  has superior merits in stability and reproducibility due to its high film-formation ability and electron mobility. $ ^{[12-13]} $  Unfortunately, undoped P3HT is rarely used in the PVSCs containing new perovskite and electron-transporting materials (ETMs), $ ^{[14-15]} $  maybe contributed to the pure requirement on high PCE. But on the other hand, the stable and reproducible P3HT based device can also offer valuable data for the evaluation of new materials for the PVSCs. In the PVSCs, electron-transporting layer (ETL) is a general concept, which can perform the function of electron-injection, electron-transporting, hole-blocking and surface modification. $ ^{[16]} $  At present, undoped P3HT based PVSCs present the maximum PCE of 13.7%. $ ^{[17]} $  Therefore, there are huge spaces for the further improvement of P3HT based device with other ETMs.

A large proportion of high efficiency PVSCs reported so far utilize the conventional n-i-p device configuration with the titanium dioxide (TiO $ _2 $ ) or stannic oxide (SnO $ _2 $ ) as ETLs. $ ^{[18-20]} $  However, the preparation of those inorganic n-type semiconducting metal oxide ETLs involves high-temperature sintering, especially the mesoporous TiO $ _2 $  scaffold requiring more than 450 °C to sinter, $ ^{[21-22]} $  which has negative effects on mass production and flexible device architecture. In order to circumvent these drawbacks, n-type organic semiconductors have performed the function as ETMs of the PVSCs, for the formation of low-temperature, solution processed ETLs.

Compared with the organic HTMs for PVSCs,  $  ^{[23-26]}  $   organic ETMs for high-performance PVSCs are studied much less. Even so, the promising candidates of organic ETM for PVSCs are developed rapidly. Fullerene and its derivatives phenyl-C61-butyric-acid-methyl ester (PCBM)  $  ^{[27-28]}  $   are naturally used as ETMs of the inverted

Among those non-fullerene ETMs, perylene diimide (PDI) based ETMs have been attracted intensive attention due to the high electron nucleophilic potential and strong electron-receiving power.  $  ^{[44-51]}  $   Benefited from the rigid, planar p-skeletons conducive to ordered molecular packing, PDI is a popular skeleton unit of liquid crystal molecules. This rich resource of new ETMs for the PVSCs needs to be exploited more widely.

In this work, an intermediate of liquid crystals, 1,6,7,12-tetrachloro-N,N'-dioctyl-3,4,9,10-perylenetetracarboxylic diimide (TCI-PDI) is used as ETM of the PVSCs for the first time, in which a n-i-p structure of ITO/ETL/perovskite/P3HT/MoO₃/Ag is applied. With four chlorines and N,N-dioctyl on PDI unit, TCI-PDI exhibits suitable energy levels, high thermal stability and electron mobility. It is more interesting that perovskite films formed on TCI-PDI ETL display higher crystalline and large crystalline domains. All those endow the device based on TCI-PDI ETL a promising PCE of 14.73% and high stability, which is superior to that of the reference device using compact TiO₂ ETL significantly.

# Results and Discussion

TCI-PDI was synthesized according to our the previous work.  $  ^{[52]}  $   There are four chlorines on the bay of perylene core, which serve as electron-withdrawing unit to decrease the electron cloud density of conjugate system (Figure 1a). As a new ETM of the PVSCs, its absorption, thermostability, electrochemical and charge transporting characteristics are checked firstly. UV-vis absorption spectra of TCI-PDI film and solution are plotted in Figure 1b. The absorption spectra of TCI-PDI in solution mainly consist of   $  \pi-\pi^{*}  $   electronic transitions associated with a series of characteristic vibronic structures from 400-750 nm,  $  ^{[53-54]}  $   which are 0-0, 0-1, and 0-2   $  \pi-\pi^{*}  $   electronic transitions with the absorption peaks at 518, 485 and 425 nm, respectively. The absorption spectra of thin film show broadening and red-shifted absorption bands, in which the relative intensities of the vibronic structures are quite different from those of the solution sample. These changes in spectroscopic features indicate a significant molecular interaction of TCI-PDI in the solid state like the   $  \pi-\pi  $   stacking action. Bandage of TCI-PDI is estimated to be 1.98 eV by the absorption edge of the film.  $  ^{[55]}  $   The decomposition temperature (  $  T_{d}  $  ) of TCI-PDI is confirmed to be 358 °C at 5% weight-loss, which is obtained from thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) curve (Figure S1).

Frontier energy levels of TCI-PDI were obtained from cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements (Figure S2). The onset of the irreversible reduction wave  $ (\varphi_{red}) $  is  $ -0.65\mathrm{eV} $ , and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LOMO) level can be estimated to be  $ -4.15\mathrm{eV} $ , according to an empirical formula  $ [E_{\mathrm{HOMO}} = -e(E_{\mathrm{ox}} + 4.80)(\mathrm{eV})] $ . Highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy level of TCI-PDI is determined to be  $ -6.31\mathrm{eV} $ , according to the optical band gap and LOMO energy level. Charge transport property of TCI-PDI was studied with space charge limited current (SCLC) method. Electron only devices of ITO/ZnO/TCI-PDI/LiF/Al were fabricated for obtaining electron mobility of TCI-PDI. J-V characteristics curve of electron-only device is shown in Figure S3. For the functional layers prepared from solution layer by layer, crystalline phase of perovskite film will be affected by the under layer, which can be checked from X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns (Figure 2a). There are similar XRD profiles of the perovskite film on both ETLs, which confirm the formation of the black phase of the perovskite containing the diffractions of (101), (012), (021), (202), (211), (122), (024) and (131) planes of the perovskite lattice.  $  ^{[59-61]}  $   In addition, XRD intensities of lattice planes (202) and (024) of perovskite film prepared on TCI-PDI are higher than those of perovskite film on   $  TiO_{2}  $  , indicating that TCI-PDI ETL benefit the crystallization of perovskite, which is helpful for device performance.

The PVSCs with a structure of ITO/ETL/Perovskite/P3HT/MoO $ _3 $ /Ag were fabricated and characterized under 100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $  illumination. In order to study the effect on TCI-PDI film from perovskite precursor solution, the mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO (4:1), the solvent of perovskite precursor, is used to wash the annealed TCI-PDI film. The annealed TCI-PDI film cannot be washed away by the solvent. UV-visible absorption spectra of TCI-PDI film before and after being washed by the mixed solvent of DMSO and DMF is shown in Figure S4. Thickness of TCI-PDI was changed by using different spin-coating speeds, which was measured with step profiler and UV-vis absorption spectroscopy (Figure S5). The thickness below 50 nm is not shown clearly in the step meter. Therefore, the TCI-PDI thicknesses of 20 nm, 25 nm and 35 nm are obtained by using step meter and UV-vis absorption spectroscopy together. First, a 50 nm TCI-PDI is confirmed by a step meter, and then the other thicknesses are obtained based on the ratio between their relative absorption intensity to the 50 nm TCI-PDI. As shown in Figure 2b and Table 1, TCI-PDI based devices exhibit a peak PCE of 14.73% with a  $ V_{OC} $  of 1.07 V, a  $ J_{SC} $  of 17.86 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and a FF of 76.77%, which is among the highest performances of the PVSCs using undoped P3HT as HTM (Table S1). The thickness of TCI-PDI affect the device performances of the PVSCs obviously. The optimized thickness of TCI-PDI was about 25 nm. The further increase of thickness resulted in a lower PCE due to decreases in FF and  $ J_{SC} $ . The reference devices with TiO $ _2 $  exhibit the maximum PCE of 12.78% with a  $ V_{OC} $  of 1.03 V, a  $ J_{SC} $  of 20.85 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  and a FF of 59.22%, which is a reasonable PCE value of the PVSCs with P3HT HTL and compact TiO $ _2 $  ETL. In general,  $ V_{OC} $  of the PVSCs is determined by the gap between the quasi-Fermi level of holes in HTL and that of the electrons in ETL. The little higher  $ V_{OC} $  and dramatically improved FF of the PVSCs based on TCI-PDI maybe come from a better energy alignment at the ITO/ETL interface and ETL/perovskite as well as reduced non-radiative recombination at TCI-PDI/perovskite interface, which lead to the more efficient charge transfer. To investigate the carrier transport processes at the perovskite/ETL interface, we performed electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) on the PVSCs based on TCI-PDI and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  (Figure 3a). The curves were fitted with an equivalent circuit model consisting of a series resistor  $ R_{s} $ , a charge transfer resistance  $ (R_{ct}) $  and a constant phase element (CPE) at the interface between ETL and the perovskite layer.[65] The  $ R_{ct} $  is related to the interface charge transport[66] and the  $ R_{s} $  is related to the resistance of the ITO, external wires, and other additional contributions, which can be obtained from the starting point at the real part of the Nyquist plot.[67-69] A  $ R_{s} $  of  $ 38\Omega $  in the device based on TCI-PDI is lower than that of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  based device  $ (73\Omega) $ , contributing to the higher FF and lower loss in  $ V_{OC} $ . TCI-PDI based device presents an obviously lower  $ R_{ct} $   $ (2.4\times 10^{5}\Omega) $  than that of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  based device  $ (3.4\times 10^{5}\Omega) $ , which again demonstrates a more efficient extraction/transport of electron at the interface. To further understand the charge transfer behavior in our devices, steady-state photoluminescence (PL) measurement was conducted on perovskite films prepared on different ETLs. All the perovskite films with the same thickness around  $ 560~\mathrm{nm} $  were prepared on ITO/ETL substrates. As presented in Figure 3b, more effective PL quenching of perovskite is observed from TCI-PDI/perovskite, which indicates that carriers created in the excited perovskite layer should be efficiently extracted.

Morphologies of the perovskite films formed on different ETLs were studied by using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and atomic force microscope (AFM). SEM image (Figure 4a) of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite displays clear grain boundaries with grain sizes from  $ 50~\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 300~\mathrm{nm} $ . On the contrary, larger crystalline domains of perovskite from  $ 100~\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 1000~\mathrm{nm} $  are demonstrated on TCI-PDI (Figure 4b), which are helpful for facilitating the charge transport and decreasing the carrier recombination. Water contact angels of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and TCI-PDI before and after  $ \mathrm{O}_2 $  plasma are studied from water contact angel test. TCI-PDI displays lower surface energy than  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  in both conditions (Figure S7). The increased grain size of the perovskite crystal on TCI-PDI thin film might be related to the lower number of nuclei generation on TCI-PDI surface with low surface energy.[70] AFM images of the perovskite on the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and TCI-PDI are shown in Figure 4c and 4d. Surface modification effect is the other important function of ETL for decreasing current leakage and attaining better ohmic contact with cathode. Stability of solar cells is the most important property for the application. Therefore, stability of the PVSCs based on different ETLs needs to be checked. The devices encapsulated by UV curable adhesive were stored in ambient conditions (25 °C) with the humidity around 30%. In Figure 5, TCI-PDI based devices show remarkable stability, maintaining over 90% of their original PCEs after storage of more than 720 hours. While the reference devices with   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL display a decreased PCE of 30% within 720 hours. The more stable PVSCs based on TCI-PDI is attributed to better crystallinity and larger crystalline domains of the perovskite film. The high-quality and pinhole-free film perovskite film can prevent moisture from penetrating into perovskite layer, suppressing the decomposition of the perovskite film and improving the device stability.

# Conclusions

In this study, a simple small molecule TCI-PDI containing a perylene tetracarboxylic diimide unit with four chlorines in the meso-position is demonstrated to be an efficient ETM of the PVSCs. A promising PCE of  $ 14.73\% $  is exhibited in the device with a structure of ITO/TCI-PDI/perovskite/P3HT/MoO₃/Ag, which is among the highest performances achieved from the PVSCs using undoped P3HT HTL. Compare with the reference device based on TiO₂ ETL, the little higher  $ V_{OC} $  and dramatically improved FF of the PVSCs based on TCI-PDI maybe come from a better energy alignment as well as the reduced nonradiative recombination at TCI-PDI/perovskite interface. The superior crystalline characteristics of perovskite formed on TCI-PDI are confirmed by XRD, SEM and AFM measurements. Benefiting from larger crystalline grain domains and less grain boundaries of perovskite film, the PVSCs based on TCI-PDI show much better stability with little degradation after 720 hours in air. With this work, we believe more ideas will be stimulated to rich the materials family of PDI based HTMs.

# Materials and reagents

DMF (99.9%), DMSO (99.9%), anhydrous 2-propanol (IPA, 99.99%), Acetonitrile (99.9%) and chlorobenzene (99.9%) were purchased from J&K Chemistry. # Fabrication of perovskite solar cells

The perovskite solution was prepared by dissolving PbI $ _2 $  (1.1 M), FAI (1.0 M), PbBr $ _2 $  (0.22 M), and MABr (0.2 M) in a anhydrous solvent DMF :DMSO = 4:1 (volume ratio) added CsI (1.5 M) in DMSO according to a volume ratio = 95:5. The devices were encapsulated in glass by UV-curable resin in the glove box, then measurements and storage were taken in air.

# Measurement and characterization

Current-voltage (J-V) curves were measured under 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  irradiation by using the simulated solar light from Newport Orial solar 3A simulator and recorded with a Keithley 2400 source meter. Active areas of the solar cells were defined with a black mask of about 0.04 cm  $  ^{2}  $ . EQE spectra of the solar cells were analyzed by Enlitech QE-R011 (Enlitech Co. Ltd., Taiwan) measurement system. UV-Vis absorption spectra of the films were obtained on a Shimadzu UV-2600 spectrometer. CV measurements were conducted using a three-electrode cell under N  $  _{2}  $  atmosphere with CHI 660 electrochemical workstation. TGA was recorded on a Mettler-Toledo TGA/SDTA 851e thermal apparatus (Switzerland). EIS was measured by a CHI 660 electrochemical workstation (CH Instruments Inc.) in the frequency range between 100 and 10  $  ^{5}  $  Hz with perturbation amplitudes of 700 mV. The tests were calibrated with the standard ferrocene/ferrocenium (Fc/Fc  $  ^{+}  $ ) redox system with the assumption that the energy level of Fc/Fc  $  ^{+}  $  is 4.8 eV under vacuum. XRD spectra of the perovskite films were recorded on a Bruker, D8-Advance X-ray diffractometer with Cu-Ka radiation,  $  \lambda = 1.54056  $  Å, 40 kV, 40 mA from 2θ = 10° to 50° at a scan rate of 0.02° s  $  ^{-1}  $  at room temperature under ambient conditions. SEM (SEM, JEOL JSM-6700F) and AFM (AFM, Bruker Dimension Edge SPM System) was performed to characterize the surface morphology of different films. Water contact-angle measurements were conducted using a contact angle tester (KRÜSS DSA25).

TCI-PDI

With four chlorines and N,N-dioctyl on perylene diimide (PDI) unit, tetrachloro-PDI (TCI-PDI) exhibits suitable energy levels, high thermal stability and electron mobility, and also induces better crystalline phase of perovskite with large crystalline domains. Due to these merits of electron-transporting material, TCI-PDI endows the PVSCs higher photovoltaic performances and stability than the device based on compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ .
