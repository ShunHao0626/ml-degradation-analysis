# Engineering of the Back Contact between PCBM and Metal Electrode for Planar Perovskite Solar Cells with Enhanced Efficiency and Stability

Shaobing Xiong, Meng Yuan, Jianming Yang, Jingnan Song, Xuewen Guo, Xiang Li, Bo Li, Xianjie Liu, Chungang Duan, Feng Liu,* Mats Fahlman, and Qinye Bao*

The cathode interface plays a critical role in achieving high-performance fullerene/perovskite planar solar cells. Herein, the simple molecule Isatin and its derivatives are introduced at the back contact [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM)/Al as a cathode modification interlayer. It is revealed that the Isatin interlayers facilitate electron transport/extraction and suppress electron recombination, attributed to the formation of negative dipole potential steps and the passivation of the interfacial trap density. The average power conversion efficiencies of the resulting devices are significantly improved by  $ 11\% $  from  $ 17.68\% $  to  $ 19.74\% $ , with an enhancement in all device parameters including short-circuit current, open-circuit voltage, and fill factor. The hysteresis index is found to disappear. In addition, such interlayer enhances device stability under ambient conditions compared to the control devices due to suppression of moisture-induced degradation of the perovskite films. These findings provide a comprehensive understanding of the engineering of the back contact between PCBM and the metal electrode to improve efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells.

The electrode interface has a crucial influence on device performance, governing the charge extraction and transport behaviors between the perovskite layer and the electrode. The incorporation of an interlayer at an electrode interface is often regarded as an effective strategy to adjust energy level alignment, improve carrier extraction, and eliminate recombination loss at interfaces, all of which boost device efficiency.  $  ^{[19-23]}  $   The interlayer also affects the crystallization process and morphology of the perovskite film. High-quality polycrystalline films with enlarged grains and decreased trap state density can be successfully formed when deposited on, e.g., more hydrophilic interlayers.  $  ^{[24,25]}  $   Moreover, the environmental stability of perovskite solar cells can remarkably be enhanced via interlayer modification, if the interlayer prevents moisture, oxygen, and metal electrode diffusion into the bulk of the cell.  $  ^{[26-28]}  $

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells have attracted tremendous attention in the past few years due to their high solar-energy conversion efficiency, low-cost processing, and potential for large-scale manufacturing.  $  ^{[1-5]}  $   The confirmed excellent optoelectronic properties such as strong light-absorbing ability, small exciton binding energy, long carrier diffusion length, and high charge mobility render the perovskite materials an enormous potential for photovoltaic applications.  $  ^{[6-8]}  $   Attributed to the substantial effort focused on compositional, morphological, and interfacial engineering, the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite-based solar cells has reached over 24%.  $  ^{[9-15]}  $   Generally, a planar perovskite heterojunction photovoltaic device is composed of a perovskite layer as light absorber sandwiched between electron and hole selective contacts, which

To further explore the role of Isatin-Cl and Isatin interlayers, the interfacial electronic structures are measured by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS). Figure 4a shows UPS spectra of MAPbI $ _3 $ , MAPbI $ _3 $ /PCBM, Al, and Al modified by Isatin and Isatin-Cl, respectively. The left panel exhibits the secondary electron cutoff utilized to derive the work function (WF), and the right panel highlights the valence band region referenced to the Fermi level ( $ E_{\text{F}} $ ). The WF of the perovskite film prepared on F4TCNQ:PTAA/ITO substrate is estimated to be 4.90 eV, and the valence band maximum (VBM) position relative to  $ E_{\text{F}} $  is determined to be 0.6 eV in a logarithmic intensity scale, $ ^{[49,50]} $  see Figure 4b. After depositing PCBM, an upward vacuum level shift of 0.13 eV is observed as the WF increases to 5.03 eV, where the VBM of PCBM lies at 0.93 eV below  $ E_{\text{F}} $ . The WF of the bare Al electrode is 4.28 eV, which would create an energy barrier for injection into (bulk) transport states in the adjacent PCBM (the energy difference between the Fermi level of the electrode and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of the PCBM). $ ^{[51]} $  After inserting the thin Isatin and Isatin-Cl coated on Al, the values of WF significantly reduce to 3.65 and 3.41 eV, respectively, which are below the LUMO level of PCBM and thus benefits the electron transport. It indicates that the interlayers can effectively tune the surface energetics of the metal electrode with the formation of an interfacial negative dipole between PCBM and Al. The interfacial dipole originates from double dipole steps induced by the positive charged N $ ^+ $  interaction with the opposite image charge in Al as per previous reports. $ ^{[52,53]} $  Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy measurements confirmed that there is no strong interaction between Isatin (Isatin-Cl) and PCBM (Figure S6, Supporting Information). The charge transfer gap of MAPbI $ _3 $  and PCBM are 1.7 and 2.0 eV; $ ^{[54,55]} $  respectively. According to the energy level diagrams drawn from the UPS in Figure 4c, the electron extraction barrier effectively decreases as the diminished work function of the cathode and the  $ E_{\text{F}} $  below the conductive band minimum of PCBM, which is favorable for electron extraction from PCBM to the cathode.

The introduction of Istain and Istain-Cl interlayers also improves the device stability. In Figure S7 in the Supporting Information, the water contact angle on the pristine PCBM is   $  73.3^{\circ}  $  . With the two interlayer modifications, the contact angles greatly increase to   $  90.1^{\circ}  $   and   $  80.2^{\circ}  $  , respectively. The improved hydrophobicity can help suppress the attack of moisture on the perovskite films and the interlayers thus act also like protection layers. The long-term durability of devices without encapsulation held at room temperature under ambient atmosphere at a relatively low humidity (  $  \approx45\%  $  ) is further monitored. Figure 5 shows the normalized PCE decay as a function of storage time. The interlayer-modified devices remain above 85% of the initial PCE after 350 h, however, the PCE of the reference device with the only PCBM/Al cathode quickly drops to 45% for the same period. These results confirm that the Isatin and Isatin-Cl interfacial interlayers protect the perovskite solar cell device from rapid degradation.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: PTAA, F4TCNQ, and lead iodide (PbI  $  _{2}  $  , 99.99%) were provided by Polymer Light Technology Corp. Methylammonium iodide (MAI, 99.5%) was purchased from Greatcell Solar Ltd. PCBM was obtained from Nano-c Ltd. N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.8%), IPA (99.5%), and chlorobenzene (CB, 99.5%) were received from Acros Organics.

Device Fabrication and Characterization: ITO glass substrates were cleaned by sonication method following the reported procedures and dried in an oven, then they were exposed to UVO for 15 min. PTAA solution was prepared in CB with a concentration of 2 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  and doped with 25wt% F4TCNQ, and the PTAA solution was filtered by 0.22  $  \mu  $  m filter before using. The PTAA layer was deposited on ITO substrate at 5000 rpm for 30 s and heated at 150 °C for 10 min. DMF was spin coated on PTAA layer to improve its wettability. The MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  precursor was prepared by dissolving 1.08 mol PbI  $  _{2}  $  and 1 mol MAI in 1 mL DMF/DMSO (volume ratio, 9:1) mixed solvent. The perovskite film was spin coated onto PTAA layer at 4000 rpm for 30 s. 150  $  \mu  $  L CB was dripping at the center of the substrate after 5 s of the spin coating. The perovskite film was then annealed at 100 °C for 10 min on the hotplate. PCBM layer was subsequently fabricated from 20 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  solution on the top of the perovskite layer at 1000 rpm for 40 s. Isatin-Cl or Isatin in IPA with different concentrations was spin coated on the PCBM film at 4000 rpm for 30 s without annealing process. Finally, 100 nm Al was thermal evaporated at the pressure of 2 × 10  $  ^{-6}  $  Torr. The active area of each device is 0.05 cm  $  ^{2}  $ . The current density–voltage (J-V) curves of the devices were tested inside N  $  _{2}  $  filled glovebox, using a Keithley 2400 system under the illumination of AM 1.5G (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $ ). The EQE spectra were recorded by a CrownTech quantum efficiency measurement system (QTesT 1000ADX). The illumination intensity of solar simulator was calibrated by a silicon reference cell. EIS was performed via Precision impedance analyzer (Agilent 4294A).

Film Characterization: XRD was performed to check film crystallinity with monochromatic Cu Kα radiation on analytical apparatus (Empyrean). The morphologies of the perovskite films were measured through an SEM (SEM Carl Zeiss, Merlin). The UV-vis absorption spectrum was achieved via a Lambda 750 instrument in Perkin Elmer model. The steady-state PL spectrum was detected by Horiba Jobin-Yvon Ihr550. TRPL characterization was realized by a time-correlated single photon counting system (PicoHarp 300). UPS measurements were performed in an ultrahigh vacuum surface analysis system with the base pressure of a sample analysis chamber   $  \approx5\times10^{-10}  $   mbar. UPS used the Hel (21.2 eV) as the excitation source and had an energy resolution of 50 meV.
