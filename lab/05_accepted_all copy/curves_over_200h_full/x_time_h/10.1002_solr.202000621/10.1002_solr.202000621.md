Keywords: perovskite solar cell, butyl acrylate additive, defect passivation, moisture stability, thermal stability

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are a new class of photovoltaic devices, which have attracted increasing interests due to high efficiency, low cost and simple fabrication process. Although a remarkable enhancement in the power conversion efficiency (PCE) has been achieved during the past decade, the stability issue, including high water/heat sensitivity and UV light-induced degradation, has become one of the main obstacles for practical applications of PSCs. Herein, hydrophobic butyl acrylate with C=O and C=C groups is reported as a new additive to enhance the PCE and moisture/thermal stability of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  -based PSC, which can improve the quality of perovskite film by promoting the defect passivation, construct a suitable energy level alignment and increase the charge carrier lifetime in PSCs. Such an additive can be easily introduced into PSCs without the need of thermal treatment, which is typically required by other additives, causing negative effect on the efficiency. The   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  -based PSC with butyl acrylate additive shows a superior PCE of 20.0%, 19% higher than that of the unmodified device (16.8%) as well as much improved moisture/thermal stability. # 1. Introduction

Because of their high efficiency, low cost and easy fabrication, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have become one of the most rapidly developing solar cells with great practical application potential.  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   Actually, within a short period of twelve year after the first report in 2009, the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of PSCs has increased from 3.8% to more than 25%,  $  ^{[4,5]}  $   reaching the PCEs of state-of-the-art commercial silicon-based solar cells.  $  ^{[5]}  $   The PSCs display several advantages over well commercialized silicon-based solar cells, which suffered from expensive raw materials, complicated fabrication processes and hazardous by-product in the fabrication of silicon. Unfortunately, the poor long-term stability of available PSCs becomes the biggest hurdle for their commercialization. During the past, considerable efforts have been devoted to boost the efficiency.  $  ^{[6-10]}  $   Recently, the improvement of their operational stability has been received more and more attention.  $  ^{[11-13]}  $

In organic-inorganic ABX $ _3 $  halide perovskites, A-site could be CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3^+ $  (MA $ ^+ $ ), NH $ _2 $ CHNH $ _2^+ $  (FA $ ^+ $ ) and cesium (Cs $ ^+ $ ) and B-site is typically Pb $ ^{2+} $  and Sn $ ^{2+} $ , while X-site could be I $ ^- $ , Br $ ^- $  and Cl $ ^- $ . The structural stability of halide perovskites is closely related to their components. It is believed that the black phases of Cs $ ^+ $ - and FA $ ^+ $ -based perovskites are thermodynamically unstable at room temperature while the tetragonal black phase of MA $ ^+ $ -based perovskites show better structural stability at room temperature. $ ^{[14-18]} $  Therefore, from the overall consideration of efficiency and intrinsic stability, MA $ ^+ $ -based PSCs are more preferred for the commercialization purpose. However, the practical application of MAPbI $ _3 $ -based PSCs is still hurdled by their high heat and water sensitivity. For example, MAPbI $ _3 $  can be easily decomposed to MAI and PbI $ _2 $  at high temperature (> 80 °C), $ ^{[19]} $  while water can easily degrade the perovskite structure by forming hydrogen bonding with MA $ ^+ $ , which is much easier than that with PbI $ _2 $ . issue for halide perovskites, which originates from the hygroscopic nature of amine groups in the halide perovskites.  $  ^{[20]}  $

Up to now, many research groups have tried functional additives to enhance the stability of MAPbI $ _3 $ -based PSCs against moisture and heat. $ ^{[21-25]} $  For example, hydrophobic fluorinated perylenediimide (F-PDI) was incorporated into MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite to enhance both the PCE and moisture/thermal stability due to the efficient defect passivation and the promoted charge carrier transport at grain boundaries because of the chelation effect between the carbonyl of F-PDI and non-coordinating Pb in the perovskite. $ ^{[21]} $  However, it suffered from several drawbacks such as non-uniform film morphology and poor interaction between the F-PDI additive and the halide perovskite, which may have negative effects on the operational stability of PSCs. Many attempts have also been made to improve the quality of perovskite film by utilizing various specific functional groups that have the capability of interaction with perovskite. The interaction between the S=O or C=O bond in the additives and Pb in the perovskite can lead to the passivation of the defect and/or the controlled perovskite crystallization process. $ ^{[22-25]} $  For example, Li et al. have introduced a hydrophobic and cross-linkable trimethylolpropane triacrylate (TMTA) additive into perovskite films to enhance the moisture stability of MAPbI $ _3 $ -based PSCs. $ ^{[26]} $  It was found that the C=O and C=C groups in TMTA enhanced the chemical anchoring to grain boundaries of the perovskite films and promoted the crosslinking polymerization after thermal processing, respectively. As a result, TMTA not only passivated the defects in the perovskite films via crosslinking perovskite grains but also inhibited the water penetration because of its hydrophobicity. However, to promote the polymerization for TMTA, a step of high-temperature treatment at 140 °C was needed, which caused a degradation of MAPbI $ _3 $  film, leading to a lower PCE of the device. $ ^{[26]} $  An additive with similar functions to TMTA without the requirement of thermal treatment for polymerization is thus highly attractive, which can improve both the water/heat tolerance and efficiency of MAPbI $ _3 $ -based PSCs.

Herein, for the first time, a new hydrophobic butyl acrylate (BA) with a conjugated system based on C=O and C=C groups was proposed as an additive for   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite, which can enhance both the moisture/thermal stability and PCE of PSCs without the requirement of detrimental high-temperature treatment step. Such improvement was assigned to the efficient defect passivation, increased charge carrier lifetime, more suitable energy level alignment and hydrophobic nature to inhibit the water penetration, which are closely related to the conjugated system based on C=O and C=C groups in BA. This work provides some useful guidelines for the rational design of high-efficiency and stable   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  -based PSCs, promoting the commercialization of this technology.

# 2. Results and Discussion

For comparison, we also tried a C=C free butyl propionate (BP) as additive for the MAPbI $ _3 $  film. For both BP and BA functional additives, they were added into the perovskite precursors at a fixed volume percentage of 2% for the fabrication of corresponding MAPbI $ _3 $ -based films. In addition, the same to the fabrication process for additive-free MAPbI $ _3 $  film, both the solar cells with MAPbI $ _3 $ -BP and MAPbI $ _3 $ -BA layers were fabricated by a standard process as reported in the literature without any further modification. $ ^{[27]} $  The perovskite-based layers were spin-coated and annealed at the same temperature of 100 °C for 5 min. It should be mentioned that, for other additives, in order to promote the polymerization of the additives, a higher thermal treatment was usually adopted in literature, for example, 140 °C for 10 min, for TMTA. $ ^{[26]} $  As mentioned, the thermal treatment at high temperature could lead to the decomposition of perovskite, thus negatively affecting the cell efficiency. In addition, the F-PDI additive was added in the antisolvent step in the fabrication process of solar cells, which led to an inferior distribution of F-PDI additives in perovskite films as well as poor interaction between the additive and the perovskite due to the short contact time. $ ^{[21]} $  As a result, a relative inferior stability was achieved by the device with F-PDI additive although it showed improved moisture and thermal stability as compared with the

For example, large grains and fewer grain boundaries can reduce the recombination of charge carriers in compact perovskite films. It is widely accepted that the transport capability and recombination behavior of photogenerated charge carriers as well as the defect amount (trap density) of the perovskite films played crucial roles on the PCEs of PSCs. A larger charge carrier diffusion length is beneficial for the charge carrier diffusion and transfer, inhibiting the charge carrier recombination in PSCs. If the charge carrier diffusion distance is not long enough, the photogenerated electrons and holes will recombine first before being transported to the ETL and HTL in PSCs, respectively, causing a reduction in the PCEs of PSCs. The defects of PSCs are considered as recombination centers, which reduced the transfer and extraction efficiencies of charge carriers in PSCs, leading to a larger current leakage, thereby reducing the PCEs of PSCs. Dark current refers to the current generated in PSCs with no incident sunlight under reverse bias conditions. The generation of dark current in PSCs is generally caused by detrimental defects in the device, acting as the recombination centers to capture electrons and holes to recombine. When the electrons and holes are captured by the defects in PSCs, the dark current was formed due to the movement of charge carriers. As shown in Figure 4b, the device with the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -BA perovskite layer displayed much smaller current densities compared with the other two devices within the voltage range of -0.4 to 0.8 V under dark conditions, suggesting that the photoinduced charge carriers transported efficiently through the charge transporting layers in the PSC with MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -BA which led to a lower leakage in the current density.  $  ^{[37]}  $   The above results demonstrated that BA additive effectively reduced the recombination of the photoinduced electrons and holes in PSCs, leading to much improved PCEs of the corresponding devices.

Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) was also employed to further understand the transport properties of photogenerated charge carriers after the incorporation of BP or BA additive. The TRPL decay lifetimes were estimated by biexponential fitting based on the results shown in Figure S5 (Supporting Information) and the corresponding  $  \tau  $  values were presented in Table S3 (Supporting Information). It was found that the average  $  \tau  $  values of MAPbI $ _3 $ -BA (55.4 ns) and MAPbI $ _3 $ -BP films (47.6 ns) were much higher than that of MAPbI $ _3 $  (25.3 ns), suggesting the charge carrier extraction capability was improved by the addition of BA and BP with C=O groups, especially for the BA additive with a conjugated system. A larger  $  \tau  $  value means a larger diffusion length of photogenerated charge carriers in PSCs, which is beneficial for achieving a better transfer/extraction efficiency of charge carriers and a higher photocurrent density, thus enhancing the PCEs of PSCs.

The Pb and I mapping images were overlapped and it was found that there was an obvious aggregation of Pb elements in MAPbI $ _3 $ , which was assigned to the metallic Pb species. $ ^{[48]} $  However, such aggregation of Pb elements was suppressed after the introduction of BA or BP additive, due to the defect passivation effect of C=O group in the additives. It is well known that there are numerous defects existing in the grain boundaries of organic-inorganic halide perovskite films due to the existence of non-coordinating Pb and iodide vacancy in the perovskite film, $ ^{[49]} $  which are considered as trap centers for the non-radiative recombination of photoinduced charge carriers, leading to inferior PCEs and thermal/moisture stability of PSCs. $ ^{[50,51]} $  Furthermore, the degradation of halide perovskite under high-temperature and humid conditions can be propagated inward from the grain boundaries to the interior of the perovskite grains, which were extremely harmful to the efficiency and stability of the devices. $ ^{[52]} $  It is well accepted that the non-coordinating Pb in the halide perovskite is a Lewis acid, which can coordinate with C=O in Lewis base-based additives by sharing electrons in the Lewis base. The suppression effectiveness of Pb-based defects depends on the strength of the coordination bond between non-coordinating Pb and C=O group as well as the electron donating capability of Lewis base-based additives. $ ^{[24,53]} $  The higher electron donating capability means a higher electron cloud density of the formed coordination bond between C=O group and non-coordinating Pb species. $ ^{[47]} $  As mentioned above, the C=C group in BA additive enhanced the electron donating capability of C=O group in BP by forming a higher electron cloud density. As a result, the BA additive showed a much superior defect passivation capability to that of BP additive due to the conjugated system of C=O and C=C groups, which can protect the MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite films from degradation in humid and high-temperature conditions.

The high water and heat sensitivities of organic-inorganic halide perovskites were considered as one of challenges for the commercialization of PSCs and the moisture in the air and high temperature showed great effects on the stability of perovskite. It is well known that  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  can be easily decomposed to MAI and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  at high temperature ( $ >80^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ), while the water can also easily degrade the perovskite structure of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  by forming hydrogen bonding with  $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $ , which is much easier than that with  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . The PCEs of various devices after the storage in an ambient condition (temperature of  $ 20 - 30^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and relative humidity of  $ 30 - 40\% $ ) were recorded as a function of time as shown in Figure 6a. After  $ 384\mathrm{h} $ 's storage, the PSC with pristine  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  degraded to  $ 46\% $  of its initial PCE. The PSCs with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BP and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BA perovskite films showed much better moisture stability due to the hydrophobic nature and the primary PCEs remained  $ 76\% $  and  $ 90\% $  after  $ 648\mathrm{h} $ 's storage, respectively. Based on the detailed parameters of PSCs in the moisture stability test as shown in Figure S8 (Supporting Information), it was found that the PCE degradation of PSC was mainly attributed to the decrease in  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  and FF but especially  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  due to the loss of sunlight absorption capability caused by the perovskite decomposition in humid atmosphere. The static contact angle of water on the perovskite film surface was reported to be correlated with the moisture stability of the devices and a higher static contact angle of water means a better moisture tolerance.[54] As shown in Figure S9 (Supporting Information), the surface water contact angles of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ ,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BP and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BA films are  $ 40^{\circ} $ ,  $ 47^{\circ} $  and  $ 57^{\circ} $ , respectively, suggesting the improved hydrophobicity of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite films after the introduction of BA or BP additives. The increased hydrophobicity of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BP and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BA films partially contributed to the improved moisture stability of the corresponding devices under ambient conditions.

The thermal stability of the devices with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ ,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BP and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BA perovskites at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and relative humidity of  $ \sim 55\% $  in an ambient atmosphere was also tested. Since Spiro-OMeTAD HTL was very sensitive to heat, we fabricated HTL-free PSCs with a configuration of  $ \mathrm{FTO / TiO_2 / perovskite / carbon} $  for the thermal stability tests. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, a new hydrophobic BA additive was employed for  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -bsaed PSCs to enhance the stability and PCE, which effectively suppressed the defect amount, reduced the charge carrier recombination, and constructed a suitable energy level alignment at the same time. The device with the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BA perovskite layer delivered a PCE of  $ 20.0\% $ , superior to those of pristine device  $ (16.8\%) $  and the PSC with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BP  $ (18.0\%) $ , suggesting the crucial roles of C=O groups and the conjugated system in enhancing the photovoltaic performance of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based PSCs. PSCs with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BA and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BP perovskites also displayed improved moisture/heat tolerance as compared with the pristine device due to the compact morphology and smaller surface roughness of

More specifically, the device with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BA maintained  $ \sim 90\% $  of its original PCE after  $ 648\mathrm{h} $ 's storage in the air with a relative humidity of  $ 30 - 40\% $  while the pristine device and PSC with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BP retained only  $ 76\% $  and  $ 46\% $  after  $ 648\mathrm{h} $ 's and  $ 384\mathrm{h} $ 's storage under the same conditions, respectively. As for the thermal stability, the device with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BA retained  $ \sim 52\% $  of its initial PCE after  $ 240\mathrm{h} $ 's storage in the air at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  with a relative humidity of  $ \sim 55\% $  while the devices with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -BP maintained only  $ \sim 34\% $  and  $ 38\% $  after  $ 144\mathrm{h} $ 's and  $ 192\mathrm{h} $ 's storage under the same conditions, respectively. Due to the gentle nature of the additive, this work proposed a facile and useful strategy to simultaneously improve the PCE and moisture/thermal stability of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based PSCs, which may accelerate the commercialization of this technology.

Materials: All materials and reagents are used as received without any purification. PbI $ _2 $  (99.8%, Alfa Aesar), Li-TFSI (99.95%) and 75% titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate in isopropoxide (99.999%) are purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.9%), chlorobenzene (anhydrous, 99.9%), N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.9%) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP, 96%) are purchased from Aladdin. Cell Fabrication: The etched FTO glasses (resistance ≈ 7 Ω sq $ ^{-1} $ , light transmittance ≥ 80%) were ultrasonically treated with acetone, water, isopropanol and ethanol for 15 min, respectively, which were finally treated by oxygen plasma cleaner for 10 min. For the fabrication of compact TiO $ _2 $  layer (c-TiO $ _2 $ ), bis(pentane-2,4-dionato-O,O')bis(propan-2-olato)titanium/butyric alcohol mixed solution was spin-coated on the FTO substrate at 2500 rpm for 30 s with a subsequent annealing at 500 °C for 30 min. To fabricate the mesoporous TiO $ _2 $  layer (mp-TiO $ _2 $ ), TiO $ _2 $  paste/ethanol mixture (1: 6, w/w) was then spin-coated on the surface of c-TiO $ _2 $  at 4000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at 500 °C for 30 min.
