# Abstract

The commercialization process of metal hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is hindered by the poor quality of perovskite films, imperfect carrier extraction, and inferior stability. Organic or inorganic molecules were widely used to overcome these limitations. Herein, 5-(ethylthio)-1H-tetrazole (ETT) is applied for the deposition of perovskite films with high crystallinity and smooth surface. Due to the interaction between the lone pair electrons of tetrazole and  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2+}} $ , the surface defects of perovskite films were passivated, thus leading to facilitated charge transport and inhibited carrier recombination. The improved crystallinity and defect passivation enabled a power conversion efficiency over  $ 20\% $  in the ETT-modified inverted  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  PSC device,

# 1. Introduction

Metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have revolutionized the photovoltaic field owing to their low-cost fabrication and the excellent optoelectronic properties of perovskite semiconductors including large absorption coefficient [1], high carrier mobility [2], long carrier diffusion length [3], and tunable optical bandgap [4]. The certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has been raised to  $ 25.5\% $  [5] with efforts from alternative charge transfer layer [6], interface engineering [7-9], novel film deposition techniques [10, 11], and new device structural design [12]. However, this technology still faces the problems of poor quality perovskite films, imperfect carrier extraction, and inferior stability [13-19]. The quality of perovskite thin-films plays a critical role in the PSCs performance [20-22]. The polycrystalline nature of perovskite means that there are many defects in the films, which could induce ion migration and non-radiative recombination loss [23-25]. Moreover, grain boundaries and surface defects such as cation vacancy are deemed as the main channel for moisture and oxygen permeation, thus accelerating the decomposition of perovskite [26].

Various multifunctional additives, such as polymers [27, 28], alkaline metal cations [29, 30], ionic liquids [15], and Lewis acids/bases [31], have been utilized to reducing the defects and improving the moisture tolerance of PSCs. Generally, these additives can be classified into sulfur (S)-donor ligands, nitrogen (N)-donor ligands, oxygen (O)-donor ligands, and phosphor (P)-donor ligands. Thereinto, N-donor ligands showed great potential in the perovskite crystallization control and defects passivation [32]. It has been recognized that the halide migration in PSCs generated vacancies and interstitial defects to form positive charged  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2+}} $  defects on the surface of perovskite films, leading to undesirable charge recombination and performance deterioration [33]. Molecules with N-donor ligands could passivate these unsaturated  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2+}} $  defects and reduce surface recombination [34]. For instance, 1-adamantylamine (ADA) [35] and diethylenetriamine (DETA) [36] were applied to passivate the surface defects of perovskite layers. Grätzel et al. used bifunctional molecular modulators of mercapto-tetrazolium, and phenylammonium to passivate the perovskite surface defects as well

as a structure-directing modulator through the interaction with  $ \mathrm{FA_xCs_{1 - x}PbI_3} $  perovskite [37]. In addition, Choy et al. demonstrated that triazolium-based ionic liquids can improve stability by functionalizing perovskite film with hydrophobic molecule. The self-assembled monolayer (SAM) formation of water-soluble triazolium ionic liquids on a hydrophilic perovskite surface renders a humidity-stable perovskite film [38]. Therefore, it is highly desired to utilize molecular engineering of azoles derivatives to modulate the perovskite crystallization and defect chemistry.

In this work, 5-(ethylthio)-1H-tetrazole (ETT) was applied for the deposition of high quality perovskite films due to the specific molecular structure of ETT. The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy confirmed the interaction between ETT and   $  Pb^{2+}  $  . The crystallinity and surface roughness of perovskite films have been improved with ETT modification. Meanwhile, the surface defects of perovskite films have been passivated and the carrier recombination has been mitigated in the corresponding PSC devices. With an inverted structure of   $  FTO/NiO_{x}/MAPbI_{3}/ETT/PC_{61}BM/PEI/Ag  $  , a PCE of 20.49% was achieved after ETT modification. While stored in the ambient conditions with 50~60% relatively humidity for 600 h, the unencapsulated devices with ETT maintained 90% of initial PCE. Our work provides a facile route to regulate the crystallization and defect chemistry of perovskite films to improve the performance and humidity stability of PSCs.

# 2.2 Device fabrication

PSCs were fabricated with a p-i-n inverted architecture of FTO/NiO $ _x $ /MAPbI $ _3 $ /ETT/PC $ _{61} $ BM/PEI/Ag. The fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) substrates (Nippon sheet glass, 15  $ \Omega\cdot $ sq $ ^{-1} $ ), were cleaned by a sequentially ultrasonic with detergent, deionized water, ethanol, acetone, and isopropanol for 20 min, respectively. After that, the substrates were dried by nitrogen stream and treated with UV ozone for 15 min. Then, a thin layer of NiO $ _x $  was deposited by spin-coating and annealed at 350°C for 1 h in an oven. A solution of 0.5 M nickel acetate tetrahydrate dissolved in ethylene glycol with ethylenediamine was used. Then NiO $ _x $  substrates were transferred into a nitrogen-filled glovebox. The perovskite precursor solution was prepared by dissolving 1.35 M PbI $ _2 $ :MAI (1:1) in NMP:DMF=9:8 v:v. ETT was added in the precursor solution with various concentrations. Perovskite film was spin-coated in a one-step procedure at 3000 rpm for 70 s, and 150 μL of CB was dropped onto the substrate in the last 20 s, then the films were annealed on a hot plate at 100 °C for 10 min. Afterward, a thin layer of PC $ _{61} $ BM (20 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  in CB) and PEI (1:500 wt% in IPA) was spin-coated, respectively. Finally, a 100 nm thick silver electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation under high vacuum condition. An active area of 0.096 cm $ ^2 $  was defined by a metal mask.

# 3. Results and discussion

The effect of ETT on surface roughness and morphology of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films were probed by AFM and SEM. As depicted in Figure 1a and 1b, the pristine perovskite film exhibited a root mean square (RMS) of  $ 29.35\mathrm{nm} $ , while the RMS of ETT-modified perovskite film was reduced to  $ 13.21\mathrm{nm} $ , implying that perovskite film was more uniform after ETT modification. Top-view SEM images showed that uniform and pinhole-free perovskite films with an average grain size of  $ 142.1\mathrm{nm} $  were observed upon ETT modification, larger than that of the pristine  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film ( $ 89.4\mathrm{nm} $ , Figure S2). Large grain size and well-crystallized perovskite films were prerequisites for a high PSC device performance, which can be explained by the fact that the defects usually accumulated at the grain boundaries of the perovskite films. Large grains with less grain boundary would suppress the non-radiative recombination due to the less localized charge traps. The crystallinity of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films was further recorded by XRD measurements. As displayed in Fig. 1c, peaks at  $ 14.28^{\circ} $ ,  $ 28.60^{\circ} $  and  $ 32.02^{\circ} $  can be indexed to the (110), (220) and (310) lattice planes of the orthorhombic  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite [39]. No obvious shifted after ETT doping revealed that ETT didn't participate in the forming of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  crystal lattice, thus no change in the bandgap of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ . Moreover, the absence of the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  peak and high diffraction intensity

The steady-state PL and TRPL spectroscopy were conducted to investigate the charge transfer characteristics of the perovskite films. As depicted in Fig. 5a, the PL intensity of the ETT-modified perovskite film was significantly higher than that of the control film, suggesting suppressed nonradiative recombination [47, 48]. Moreover, the PL peak was shifted to a lower wavelength from  $ 777~\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 771~\mathrm{nm} $  after ETT modification, indicating effective defects passivation after ETT modification [1]. Additionally, TRPL spectra were displayed in Fig. 5b. Obviously, ETT-modified perovskite film exhibited a longer lifetime than the control film. Bi-exponential equation was used to fit the decay curves, and the fitting results were listed in Table S3. A fast decay lifetime  $ \tau_{1} $  was attributed to non-radiative recombination at the interface, while a slow decay lifetime  $ \tau_{2} $  is correlated to the radiative recombination in the bulk perovskite [49]. A negligible difference was observed in  $ \tau_{2} $  for both films, but longer  $ \tau_{1} $  was observed in ETT-modified  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film, demonstrating that ETT modification could passivate the perovskite defects and inhibit the non-radiative recombination at the interface. Since permittivity is related to the power law changes in conductivity and the Kramers-Kronig relationships affect the dielectric loss [50], relative permittivity-ramp rate curves were tested (Figure S8). With an electron-only device structure of  $ \mathrm{FTO / SnO_2 / MAPbI_3 / PC_{61}BM / Ag} $ , the ETT-modified film exhibited higher relative permittivity as the ramp rate changes than control perovskite film, denoting higher conductivity and less dielectric loss in ETT-modified perovskite.

To elucidate the origin of PCE enhancement after ETT modification, the relationships between   $  V_{oc}  $   and incident light intensity were probed to expound the recombination dynamics within PSCs. As shown in Fig. 5c, by plotting   $  V_{oc}  $   as a function of incident light intensity, a smaller slope of   $  1.10 k_{B}T q^{-1}  $   was observed after ETT modification, while the control device showed a slope of   $  1.20 k_{B}T q^{-1}  $  , implying trap-assisted Shockley–Read–Hall monomolecular recombination was less involved in ETT-modified device [51]. Such reduced recombination was consistent well with the increase of   $  V_{oc}  $  . TPC measurement was further resorted to probe the charge transport dynamics(Figure S9). A decay time of 1.37   $  \mu  $  s was obtained in the ETT-modified

This confirmed that ETT-modified perovskite had better charge transport efficiency. # 4. Conclusions

In summary, a tetrazole modulator of ETT was implemented to improve the crystallinity and surface roughness of perovskite films due to the interaction between tetrazole and   $  Pb^{2+}  $  . The ETT modification induced a   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF enhancement in the corresponding devices, thus yielding a champion PCE over 20%. While stored under the ambient conditions with 50~60% relatively humidity, the unencapsulated ETT-modified device exhibited excellent stability with a 90% retaining of initial performance after 600 h. Therefore, our work provides a versatile path to modulate the perovskite crystallization and improve the stability of corresponding PSCs.
