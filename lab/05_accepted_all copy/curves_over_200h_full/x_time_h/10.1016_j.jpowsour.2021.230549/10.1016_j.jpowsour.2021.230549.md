# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted considerable research interest due to their excellent photoelectric properties, such as long carrier diffusion length, excellent light harvesting, low exciton binding energy, dual carrier transmission characteristics, and high defect tolerance   $  [1-6]  $  . Over the past decade, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has boosted from 3.8% in 2009 to certified 25.5% in 2020 through interface modification, compositional and additive engineering. Therefore, the PSCs have become the greatest potential candidates for the next generation thin solar cells   $  [7-11]  $  .

# ABSTRACT

The electron transport layer (ETL) is an essential part in perovskite solar cells (PSCs), which plays a crucial role in electron-transport and hole-blocking. However, the defects and band-energy-mismatch at ETL/perovskite interface accelerate the interfacial carrier recombination, leading to severe voltage losses and hysteresis effect. Herein, a thin ethanolamine (EA) modification layer is inserted between the stannic oxide  $ (\mathrm{SnO}_2) $  and perovskite layer to enhance the performance of PSCs. The results show that the hydroxyl group in EA can coordinate with the uncoordinated Sn atom of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  to reduce the oxygen vacancy defects, and the amino group in EA can passivate the uncoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  defects of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2/ $  perovskite interface, thus inhibiting the interfacial carrier recombination. Meanwhile, the energy alignment between  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and perovskite layer is better-matched after EA modification, ensuring more efficient electrons transport and minimizing energy losses. Consequently, the EA-based device exhibits an optimized PCE of  $ 20.44\% $  with an open-circuit voltage  $ (\mathrm{V_{oc}}) $  of  $ 1.134\mathrm{V} $ . In addition, the air stability of EA-based device exhibits great superiority to the control device under the same storage conditions. This interface engineering based on EA provide a feasible and novel strategy to fabricate the PSCs with improved efficiency.

As is known, the ETL plays a key role in electron-transport and hole-blocking. The most common ETL materials are the semiconductor oxides, such as titanium dioxide (TiO $ _2 $ ), zinc oxide (ZnO), and SnO $ _2 $  [12–16]. However, the poor chemical stability between the ZnO and perovskite is a dilemma in organic-inorganic halide PSCs [17]. Meanwhile, the high sintering temperature (>450 °C) and poor ultraviolet stability of TiO $ _2 $  hinders its further development [18]. Recently, SnO $ _2 $  has been regarded as an ideal ETL material for planar-structure PSCs due to its high optical transmittance, high electron mobility, matched energy level with the adjacent perovskite, and excellent stability [19–24]. Therefore, the reduced oxygen vacancy defects in   $  SnO_{2}  $  , less interface defects, and more suitable band alignment are the important contributors for the improvement of device performance. The KCl significantly suppressed the carrier recombination, contributing to a significant increase for  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  and a decrease for hysteresis [32]. Chang et al. introduced an amino acid self-assembled layer on  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  surface to change the lattice mismatch between  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  ETL and perovskite layer. The more suitable interfacial energy level alignment accelerated the carrier extraction and transport, thus improving the short-circuit current ( $ \mathrm{J_{sc}} $ ) of PSCs [34]. Tu et al. employed a long chain fullerene derivative to modify the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  surface. The fullerene modification layer can passivate the oxygen vacancy defects of  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  surface, thus accelerating the extraction and transfer of electrons [35]. Although various interfacial modification layers have been applied to passivate the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ /perovskite interface, the use of these interfacial passivation layers is difficult to control the electrical properties of  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ , regulate the energy level alignment of  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  and passivate the interfacial defects simultaneously. Therefore, it is necessary to find a kind of interface modification material to achieve multi-functional modification, thus enhancing the performance of PSCs.

In various interface modification materials, ethanolamine (EA) is one of the most effective interface modification material, which can reduce the contact barrier of ETL/perovskite and passivate interface defects   $  [19,36]  $  . On one hand, the hydroxyl group of EA can combine with the under-coordinated metal atoms of metal-oxide ETL to decrease the

formation of oxygen vacancy defects. On the other hand, the amino group of EA can effectively passivate the uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   defects of ETL/perovskite interface [9,36]. Cho et al. introduced a EA modification layer at the   $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface. They found that the insertion of EA can reduce the energy barrier of   $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface and the defects in the metal oxide, thus accelerating the electron transport and inhibiting the interfacial carrier recombination [36].

In this work, we presented a facile and efficient interfacial modification method by inserting a thin EA modification layer into the   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface to passivate the oxygen vacancy defects of   $  SnO_{2}  $   surface, and inhibit the interfacial carrier recombination. Meanwhile, the energy alignment between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite is improved after EA modification, which can ensure more efficient electrons transport and reduced voltage losses. Eventually, the 2% (weight ratio of EA to 2-methoxy ethanol) EA-based device exhibits an optimized PCE of 20.44% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.134 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 23.22 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and a FF of 77.62%. The control device exhibits a PCE of 18.17% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.100 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.61 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and a FF of 73.05%. Therefore, the efficiency improved 12.5% after EA-modification. In addition, the stability of the 2% EA-based device exhibits significant improvement than that of control device. After 1000 h of storage in the air (relative humidity is ~25%), the 2% EA-based device maintained 78% of its initial efficiency. While the control devices suffered a rapid efficiency decrease and only retained 55% of its initial efficiency after 760 h. This work highlights the importance of   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface modification, and provides an effective strategy for preparing the high-performance PSCs.

# 2.2. Devices fabrication

First, we cleaned the ITO with detergent, deionized water, ethanol, acetone, isopropanol sequentially and blow-dry with a nitrogen gun. Before spin-coating, we treated the ITO substrates by ultraviolet ozone (UVO) for 30 min. The ratio of   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloidal dispersion to deionized water was 1:6 (volume ratio). Next, we spin-coating the diluted   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloidal dispersion at 4000 rpm for 30 s and then annealing on a hot plate at 150 °C for 30 min in ambient air to prepare the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL. The EA solution was diluted by 2-methoxy ethanol at different weight ratio (EA: 2-methoxy ethanol = 1%, 2% and 3%) and spin-coated at 5000 rpm for 30 s on ITO/  $  SnO_{2}  $   substrates followed by annealed at 130 °C for 10 min. After cooling down the room temperature, the substrates were transferred into a nitrogen glove box immediately. The preparation method of hole transport layer (HTL) was consistent with that reported in previous literature [24]. In addition, the preparative technique of perovskite films and Ag electrode was reported in our previous literature [37]. The effective area of the devices was 0.11 cm  $  ^{2}  $  .

# 2.3. Device characterizations

The ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) of films are recorded by a Thermo Fisher Escalab 250Xi spectrometer. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) of films is measured by a PANalytical Empyrean diffractometer equipped with a Cu Kα radiation. The optical absorption spectra and transmission spectra of films are measured by the ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) spectrophotometer (Shimadzu UV-1800). The Atomic-force microscopy (AFM) of films are analyzed by a Bruker Dimension Icon. The morphology of films are scanned using the Gemini SEM 300 field emission scanning electron microscopy. The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra are obtained by a fluorescence spectrophotometer (Edinburgh Instruments Ltd, FLS980). The current-voltage (J-V) curves of devices are obtained by a Keithley 2400 source meter, and an AM 1.5G solar simulator (Newport, 2612A) are used as light source. The incident photon-to-current efficiency (IPCE) measurement is implemented on a Newport Instruments system (Newport-74125). The computer-controlled electrochemical workstation (CHI760E, Chenhua) is used to measure the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS).

As is known, the energy level mismatch between the ETL and perovskite interface can lead to severe voltage losses in PSCs [30,34]. As shown in Fig. 1c, d and Fig. S2, the UPS measurements exhibit the influence of EA modification layer on energy band structure of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  films. The cut-off binding energy  $ (\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{cut - off}}) $  of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{EA} $  films are 17.57 and  $ 17.54\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. According to the equation, i.e.,  $ \mathrm{E_F} $  (Fermi level)  $ = \mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{cut - off}} - 21.22\mathrm{eV} $ , the calculated  $ \mathrm{E_F} $  of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{EA} $  films are  $ -3.65 $  and  $ -3.68\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. Moreover, the energy gaps  $ (\mathrm{E_F} - \mathrm{E_{VBM}} $  (valence band maximum)) of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{EA} $  films are estimated to be 3.98 and  $ 3.99\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. Therefore, the  $ \mathrm{E_{VBM}} $  values of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{EA} $  films are  $ -7.63 $  and  $ -7.67\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. Fig. 1e shows the absorption spectra (insert) and the corresponding Tauc-plots of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{EA} $  films. It is shown that the deposited EA layer has negligible impact on the absorption spectra, where the bandgap  $ (\mathrm{E_g}) $  of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{EA} $  films are estimated to be  $ 3.99\mathrm{eV} $ . According to the  $ \mathrm{E_{VBM}} $  and  $ \mathrm{E_g} $  value, the  $ \mathrm{E_{CBM}} $  (conduction band minimum) value of the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{EA} $  films are calculated to be  $ -3.64 $  and  $ -3.68\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. It is worth noting that the  $ \mathrm{E_{CBM}} $  decreased by  $ 0.04\mathrm{eV} $  after EA modification, indicating more favorable energy level alignment between the ETL and perovskite layer, which can ensure more efficient electron transport to reduce the voltage loss. As shown in Fig. 2, the XPS spectra are measured to analyze the

surface elements and chemical states of   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}/EA  $   films. Fig. 2a exhibits the full XPS spectra of   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}/EA  $   films, indicating the presence of Sn, O, and N elements. Moreover, the high-resolution N 1s, Sn 3d, and O 1s XPS spectra are shown in Fig. 2b–d. The   $  SnO_{2}/EA  $   film exhibits an obvious N 1s peak at 400.04 eV, which is absent in the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film, confirming the existence of amino group of EA molecules. In addition, the N elements are uniformly distributed on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   surface demonstrated by the EDS mapping, as shown in Fig. S4. The Sn 3d peaks of   $  SnO_{2}  $   film are located at 531.68 and 530.49 eV, representing the lattice oxygen (Sn–O) and vacancy oxygen (  $  O_{v}  $  ) of   $  SnO_{2}  $ , respectively [38–40]. After EA modification, the Sn 3d peaks slightly shift to higher binding energy, i.e., 531.89 and 530.63 eV, which indicates that the Sn atoms are bound with a more stable chemical state [19,35]. The hydroxyl group attached to the end of EA can combine with the under-coordinated Sn atoms to form a Lewis adduct [35]. In other words, the EA modification can significantly decrease the oxygen-vacancy defects of   $  SnO_{2}  $   surface, which can also be confirmed by the O 1s XPS spectra. The O 1s XPS spectra of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}/EA  $   films have been analyzed by XPS Gaussian distribution. As shown in Fig. 2d, the peak area ratios of   $  O_{v}  $   decreases, whereas the peak area ratios of   $  O_{Sn}  $   increases after EA passivation. Therefore, the relative amount of each O state in the whole O 1s spectra can be estimated and the corresponding parameters are listed in Table 1. As expected, the   $  O_{Sn}  $   content increases from 72.59% to 81.04%, while the   $  O_{v}  $   content decreases from 27.41% to 18.96% after EA passivation. The less oxygen-vacancy defect is beneficial to the decrease of interfacial carrier recombination.

As reported in literature, the main sources of hysteresis of PSCs are interface defects, ion migration and accumulated unbalance charge

The steady-state PCE of control and 2% EA-based devices are 17.21% and 19.23%, further confirming that EA modification can effectively improve the PCE of PSCs.

On one hand, EA can effectively passivate the oxygen-vacancy defects of the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  surface through the formation of a Lewis adduct between the hydroxyl group in EA and the under-coordinated Sn atoms in  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  [35]. In other words, the EA modification can significantly decrease the oxygen-vacancy defects of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  surface, suppressing the formation of defect states on  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  surface [36]. On the other hand, the amino group in EA can effectively passivate the uncoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  on the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2/ $ -perovskite interface, thus greatly decreasing the amount of interface defects [19]. Therefore, the introduction of EA remarkably reduces the recombination of carriers at the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2/ $ -perovskite interface, enabling the realization of highly efficient PSCs.

The decrease of defects density can also be demonstrated by the space-charge limited current (SCLC) measurements of electron-only devices [27,40]. Fig. 7c shows the SCLC measuring result, where the knee point voltage of the slopes is related to the trap-filled limit. According to the reported literature, the trap-state density  $ (\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{traps}}) $  can be calculated by the equation [46].

$$
\mathrm{N} _ {\text { traps }} = \frac {2 \mathrm{V} _ {\mathrm{TFL}} \varepsilon_ {0} \varepsilon}{\mathrm{eL} ^ {2}}
$$

(1)

where  $ \varepsilon_0 $  is vacuum permittivity,  $ \varepsilon $  is relative dielectric constant of perovskite, e is elementary charge, L is the perovskite film thickness,  $ \mathrm{V_{TFL}} $  is trap-filed limit voltage. Based on the devices without/with EA possess the identical  $ \varepsilon_0 $ ,  $ \varepsilon $ , e and L, therefore, the value of  $ \mathrm{V_{TFL}} $  is used to estimate the  $ \mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{traps}} $ . As shown in Fig. 7c, the values of  $ \mathrm{V_{TFL}} $  of the control and  $ 2\% $  EA-based devices are 0.217 and  $ 0.127\mathrm{V} $ , respectively. Therefore, the calculated  $ \mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{traps}} $  of control device is  $ 2.36\times 10^{15} / \mathrm{cm}^3 $ , which is higher than the  $ 1.33\times 10^{15} / \mathrm{cm}^3 $  for the  $ 2\% $  EA-based device. The results demonstrate that the insertion of EA can effectively passivate defect states, inhibiting the carrier non-radiative recombination and leading to higher  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $ . Fig. 7d gives the dark J-V curves of control and  $ 2\% $  EA-based device, the non-radiative recombination of carrier can also be evaluated by the reverse saturation current density. The dark current density of  $ 2\% $  EA-based devices is lower than the value of control device, which means the insertion of EA reduces the leakage current density. In general, the lower leakage current corresponds to lower non-radiative recombination.

To investigate the transfer dynamics of carriers at  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2/ $  perovskite interfaces, the EIS measurements are implemented for the control and  $ 2\% $  EA-based devices, as shown in Fig. 7e. The Nyquist plots of the control and  $ 2\% $  EA-based devices are measured under dark condition and the equivalent circuit model is shown in Fig. S8. The equivalent circuit model is formed by series resistance  $ (\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{s}}) $ , transfer resistance  $ (\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{tr}}) $  between the transport layer and perovskite interfaces, and recombination resistance  $ (\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{rec}}) $  [47]. As reported in previous literature, the high-frequency component represents the  $ \mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{tr}} $  and the low-frequency component corresponds to the  $ \mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{rec}} $  [48]. The  $ \mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{tr}} $  of EA-based device (53.97 kΩ) is smaller than that of control device (89.65 kΩ), indicating the introduction of EA lead to more efficient electrons transport at the ETL/perovskite interface. The  $ \mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{rec}} $  of EA-based device (19191 kΩ) is larger than that of control device (15371 kΩ), which means fewer charge recombination. Moreover, the stability is a crucial parameter to evaluate the performance of PSCs. Therefore, the long-term air stability test of the un-encapsulated control and  $ 2\% $  EA-based devices was conducted, as shown in Fig. 7f. The  $ 2\% $  EA-based device exhibit a better environmental stability, which maintained  $ 78\% $  of its initial efficiency after  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  aging in air with  $ 25\% $  relative humidity, while the control device suffered a rapid efficiency decrease and only retained  $ 55\% $  of its initial efficiency after  $ 760\mathrm{h} $ . The improvement of air stability of PSCs should attribute to the decrease of defects states [21].

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, a simple and effective EA passivation method is developed to fabricate the high quality planar PSCs. The insertion of EA between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite leads to better energy alignment, less interfacial defects, thus resulting in higher   $  V_{oc}  $   and   $  J_{sc}  $  . Eventually, the 2% EA-based device exhibit an optimized PCE of 20.44% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.134 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 23.22 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and a FF of 77.62%. In addition, the air stability of 2% EA-based device exhibits significant increase. Without encapsulation, the 2% EA-based device maintain 78% of its initial efficiency after 1000 h in ambient (relative humidity is ~25%). This work demonstrates that the PCE of PSCs can be further enhanced by optimizing the interfacial energy alignment and trap density.
