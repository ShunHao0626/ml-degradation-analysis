All-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite material has attracted increasing attention because of the unique advantages (e.g., wide band-gap (  $  \sim1.7eV  $  ), simple component with organic-free and single-halide, and ideal thermal stability) for perovskite-based tandems (e.g., perovskite/Si). Inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs are more compatible with commercial silicon bottom cells in view of the fabrication of monolithic perovskite-based tandems. However, realizing high-efficiency inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   devices is a challenge. The   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite deposited on the hole transporting layer features a p-type semiconductor characteristic. When contacting the ETL, i.e., [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (i.e., PCBM), a large energy level mismatch between   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   and PCBM leads to the formation of a huge electron transfer barrier. In this work, we developed a surface n-type band bending strategy to mitigate the energy loss at the perovskite/ETL interface. Post-deposition treatment of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   with propylamine hydrochloride not only caused a p to n-type transition at the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   surface but induced a favorable energy level alignment with ETL to accelerate the separation and extraction of the electrons. An efficiency of 20.17% with a high FF of 0.84 was achieved for inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs, which is the highest efficiency reported on inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs.

# Surface N-type Band Bending for Stable Inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   Perovskite Solar Cells with over 20% Efficiency

Shuo Wang, $ ^{*,a} $  Ming-Hua Li, $ ^{*,a} $  Yanyan Zhang, $ ^{a} $  Yan Jiang, $ ^{b,*} $  Li Xu, $ ^{c} $  Fuyi Wang $ ^{a,d} $  and Jin-Song Hu $ ^{a,d,*} $

Inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells offer merits for tandem and indoor photovoltaics. Comparing to the regular structure (n-i-p), realizing high-efficiency inverted devices is still challenging due to severe energy losses at the contact of p-type perovskite and n-type electron transporting layer (ETL). Herein, we develop a surface n-type band bending strategy to mitigate such energy loss. It is discovered that post-deposition of propylamine hydrochloride induces a p to n-type transition at the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   surface to accelerate the separation and extraction of the electrons while passivating the surface defects. Consequently, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   devices reaches record-high values of 20.17% (19.67% on average) under 1-sun equivalent illumination and 38.93% under indoor LED light (1000 lux, 307   $  \mu  $  w cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). Moreover, such inverted devices maintain ~97% PCE after light soaking for 792 h at the open circuit condition while the control degraded to ~11% in 552 h.

# Introduction

Metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have become the most promising next-generation photovoltaics (PVs).  $  ^{1-3}  $   Among the various components, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites have been intensively investigated with the record power conversion efficiency (PCE) reaching 25.7% for single-junction devices and 31.3% for perovskite/Si tandem solar cells.  $  ^{4}  $   However, The volatile organic components (such as MA and FA) in these hybrid perovskites are thermal instability channels of the devices. Outgassing of hybrid perovskites has been observed under external stresses such as heat (80 °C in the case of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   and 95 °C in the case of FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ).  $  ^{5-8}  $   What's more, hybrid wide bandgap perovskites (i.e., ~1.7 eV) are mainly composed of FA/MA cations and I/Br anions. Phase separation (halogens

or cations) extremely hinders their long-term operation stability.  $  ^{9, 10}  $   All-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite exhibits a wide bandgap of 1.7 eV, which is ideal for tandem  $  ^{11}  $   and indoor photovoltaic applications.  $  ^{12}  $   Compared with hybrid wide bandgap perovskites, all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite is composed of a single halogen (I') and cation (Cs'). Therefore, no phase separation occurs during the device operation. In addition, the complete substitution of volatile organic components with   $  Cs^{+}  $  eliminates thermal instability channels of devices.  $  ^{13}  $

In recent years, black phase of  $ \gamma $ - and  $ \beta $ -CsPbI $ _3 $  films were successfully obtained and developed towards stable and high-performance CsPbI $ _3 $  PSCs. $ ^{14-16} $  Most of these studies focused on the regular structure (i.e., n-i-p structure). Many strategies such as components management, $ ^{17-22} $  molecular regulation (bulk) $ ^{23-29} $  and surface engineering, $ ^{30-35} $  have been developed to boost the PCE of regular structured CsPbI $ _3 $  PSCs over 21%. $ ^{29} $  However, a key limitation of the regular structured PSCs is that they are not compatible with commercial bottom cells (such as Si with a p-i-n structure and Copper Indium Gallium Selenide (CIGS) with a p-n structure) in view of the fabrication of monolithic perovskite-based tandems, $ ^{36-40} $  which are believed to be the next big thing in PVs. From this perspective, it is a necessity to develop inverted CsPbI $ _3 $  PSCs. Herein, we developed a surface n-type band bending strategy to mitigate the energy loss at the perovskite/ETL interface. By post-deposition treatment of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   with propylamine hydrochloride (PACl), a p to n-type transition at the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   surface was achieved to present a favorable energy level alignment with ETL for accelerating the separation and extraction of the electrons. The chloride ion also passivated the surface defects. As a result, the Voc and FF of inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   devices increased significantly from 0.97 V to 1.13 V and 0.70 to 0.84, respectively. The PCE rose from 14.43% to 20.17% under 1-sun equivalent illumination, which is the highest value for inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs so far. The device also exhibited a record-high PCE of 38.93% under indoor LED light (1000 lux, 307   $  \mu  $  w cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). Importantly, such inverted devices maintained ~96% of the original PCE after storing in   $  N_{2}  $   environment for 1032 h and ~97% of the original PCE after light soaking for 792 h while the control devices without treatment sharply degraded to ~11% PCE in 552 h.

The   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films were deposited on the dopant-free poly [3-(4-carboxybutyl)thiophene-2,5-diyl] (P3CT) HTL by a one-step spin-coating process. A two-step annealing process was adopted to promote the decomposition of dimethylamine hydroiodide (DMAI) and enhance the crystallinity of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $  . PCBM was spin-coated onto the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   as the ETL. The deposition process was shown in Fig. S1. The inverted devices could be obtained with the structure of FTO/P3CT/  $  CsPbI_{3}  $  /PCBM/BCP/Ag (labeled as "control"). Surface energy level structure has shown to severely affect the carrier extraction and the efficiency of the inverted devices using hybrid perovskites.  $  ^{52-55}  $   However, its impact on inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   devices is barely understood. Herein, ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurements were conducted to reveal the energy position of valence-band maximum (VBM) and fermi level of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   and PCBM films (Fig. S2, S3). Besides, UV-vis spectra and the Tauc plot were employed to obtain the bandgap and the conduction band minimum (CBM) of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   and PCBM films (Fig. S4, S5). Based on these analyses, a schematic energy-level diagram at the perovskite/ETL interfaces was plotted in Fig. 1A. The fermi level of the control   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films was close to the VBM (0.74 eV), indicating the p-type nature.  $  ^{47}  $   When contacting PCBM, the   $  \triangle E_{CBM}  $   of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   and PCBM was 0.35 eV, the large energy level mismatch may lead to the formation of the huge energy-level gap and serious non-radiative recombination in the interface.

To mitigate the energy loss at the   $  CsPbI_{3}/PCBM  $   interface, a thin layer of PACI was deposited atop   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films (the films and devices were labeled as “w PACI”). The surface termination could be changed and an n-type surface may be obtained with the coverage of PACI.  $  ^{52}  $   The schematic illustrations were exhibited in Fig. 1B. We proposed that the   $  Cl^{-}  $  would substitute   $  I^{-}  $  to form the Pb-Cl bond on the surface of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $ , and the propylamine (  $  PA^{+}  $  ) would cap on the upper surface of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   to passivate the surface defects (i.e., under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   defect). The photo-generated electron extraction capability at the   $  CsPbI_{3}/PCBM  $   interface was investigated by steady-state photoluminescence (PL). Indeed, the PL intensity was quenched

significantly after PACI post-deposition treatment, suggesting enhanced carrier electron extraction capability (Fig. 1C). Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (TOF-SIMS) measurement was performed to reveal the distribution of PACI. The intensities of  $ \mathrm{Cl^-} $  and  $ \mathrm{PA^{+}} $  signals were significantly decreased after the first few seconds of sputtering, indicating that both the  $ \mathrm{Cl^-} $  and  $ \mathrm{PA^{+}} $  ions were located on the surface of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  films (Fig. 1D). UPS measurements were carried out on the sample with PACI and the surface energy level structure was plotted in Fig. 1E. In contrast to the control sample, the surface of the sample with PACI converted to an n-type nature. This observation suggested a p to n-type transition from  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  bulk to the surface. The surface n-type band bending characteristic facilitated the transport of electrons from the bulk to the surface. More interestingly, the PACI induced a favorable n-type doping level, resulting in a negligible  $ \triangle E_{\mathrm{CBM}} $  between  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  and PCBM, eliminating the electron extraction gap and allowing smooth electron transfer at the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3/\mathrm{PCBM} $  interface.

Morphology of the control and w PACI films were characterized by a scanning electron microscope (SEM), both showing compact nature without a pinhole (Fig. 2A, B). In the case of the w PACI film, PACI adsorbed onto the surface of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film uniformly. Moreover, energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) mapping indicated the uniform distribution of PACI (Fig. S6). XRD measurements were carried out to verify the crystal structure of the control and w PACI perovskite films (Fig. 2C). Both of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films were tetragonal y-phase (Fig. S7).  $  ^{15, 56}  $   Neither low dimensional nor secondary phase appeared between   $  3^{\circ}-10^{\circ}  $   for the w PACI sample, indicating that the   $  PA^{+}  $  mainly capped onto the surface of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film rather than doped into the crystal lattice (Fig. S8).  $  ^{57}  $   We further conducted XPS measurements to analyze the surface chemical states of the control and w PACI films. The Pb 4f spectra were shifted from 142.95 eV and 138.10 eV to higher binding energies of 143.30 eV and 138.40 eV. This could be ascribed to the formation of Pb-Cl bond (Fig. 2D).  $  ^{17, 33}  $   Besides, no N 1s signal was detected in the control film (Fig. 2E), indicating that the all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   was successfully prepared without DMAI

Furthermore, steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra were measured on the control and w PACI samples (Fig. 3A). The control CsPbI3 film presented a weak emission peak at 728 nm. In contrast, the w PACI film showed a substantially increased peak intensity, which is an indication of the reduced non-radiative recombination. A small blue shift of PL peak to 726 nm was observed. This could be attributed to the incorporation of Cl  $  ^{-}  $  and the suppression of shallow defects.  $  ^{58}  $  Carrier lifetimes were extracted from time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra (Fig. 3B and table S2). The w PACI films exhibited a longer lifetime (63.25 ns) than the control film (14.87 ns), which is another piece of evidence verifying the passivation effect of PACI.

Electrical characterizations were carried out to understand the defect density and carrier recombination kinetics. A hole-only device (FTO/P3CT/CsPbI $ _3 $ /PACl/Ag) was firstly established to evaluate the defect density through space-charge limit current (SCLC) measurement (Fig. 3C). Trap-filled limit voltage ( $ V_{TFL} $ ) decreased from 0.343 V to 0.153 V. The defect density ( $ N_{trap} $ ) could be obtained by the following equation: $ ^{59} $

$$
N _ {t r a p} = \frac {2 \varepsilon_ {0} \varepsilon V _ {T F L}}{e L ^ {2}}
$$

(1)

where   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $  , e,   $  \varepsilon  $   and L represent the vacuum permittivity, electron charge, relative dielectric constant and film thickness, respectively.   $  N_{trap}  $   was significantly reduced from   $  1.66\times10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $   to   $  7.39\times10^{14}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  , agreeing with the XPS results. The defect passivation effect mainly originated from the formation of Pb-Cl in the perovskite crystal lattice.

Mott-Schottky curves were also used to evaluate the build-in potential (  $  V_{bi}  $  ) according to the reference  $  ^{60}  $   (Fig. 3D). The   $  V_{bi}  $   of the w PACI device (1.08 V) was larger than that of the control device (0.95 V), implying that the driving force for carrier separation was enhanced and the electron-hole recombination was reduced after treatment of PACI.  $  ^{61}  $   Furthermore, the w PACI sample demonstrated a smaller dark current than the control sample. (Fig. 3E).  $  ^{33}  $

relationship could be calculated according to the following equation:  $  ^{59}  $

$$

The ideality factor of the control device was calculated to be 1.54 kT/q, which was obviously larger than that of the w PACI device (1.31 kT/q), indicating the reduction of the defect-induced recombination or preferential recombination at the interface.  $  ^{62}  $

Furthermore, we prepared the inverted  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  PSCs based on the structure of FTO/P3CT/ $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3/ $  PACI/PCBM/BCP/Ag to evaluate the photovoltaic performance. A cross-sectional SEM image of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  device with PACI was shown in Fig. S10. The concentration of PACI was first optimized (Fig. S11 and table S3). Device PCE increased from  $ 14.43\% $  for the control sample to  $ 20.11\% $  for the sample with  $ 1\mathrm{mg/mL} $  PACI, which exhibited the highest  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  of 1.15 V. The champion PSC was further pushed to a PCE of  $ 20.17\% $  with a high FF of 0.84 via optimization of the fabrication process (Fig. 4A, table S4).

To the best of our knowledge, this is the highest PCE among the reported inverted  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  PSCs (Fig. 4B and table S5). The hysteresis behavior was studied and results were presented in Fig. S12 and table S6. A severe J-V hysteresis could be detected for the control device because of the unfavorable interface condition. In contrast, the w PACl device exhibited negligible hysteresis with a reverse PCE of  $ 19.97\% $  and a forward PCE of  $ 19.31\% $ . EQE spectrum and integrated current density of the w PACl device were shown in Fig. 4C. The current density obtained from the EQE spectrum was  $ 20.67\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $ , which is in good agreement with the  $ \mathsf{J}_{\mathsf{sc}} $  value obtained from the J-V measurement (below  $ 5\% $  deviation).

The average PCE of the w PACI devices reached  $ 19.67\% $ , which is much higher than the control devices  $ (13.23\%) $ . # Conclusions

In summary, a surface n-type band bending strategy was developed for the inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs to mitigate the energy losses at the   $  CsPbI_{3}/ETL  $   interface. UPS and IPES measurements suggested that the post-deposition treatment of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   with PACI caused a p to n-type transition at the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   surface. Such transition favored the energy level alignment between   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   and ETL and thus accelerated the separation and transportation of the electrons, as indicated by PL spectra. Systematical characterization indicated that the surface defects were simultaneously passivated by the formation of Pb-Cl bond during the PACI treatment. Benefiting from these improvements, the fabricated inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   devices achieved the record-high PCE of 20.17% (19.67% on average) under 1-sun equivalent illumination and 38.93% under indoor LED light (1000 lux, 307   $  \mu  $  w cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). Moreover, the inverted device with PACI treatment maintained ~97% of the original PCE after light soaking for 33 days at the open circuit condition while the control device sharply decayed to ~11% PCE in 23 days. These results suggested that the present surface N-type band bending strategy paved the way for the development of high-efficient inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   devices.
