# SUMMARY

All-inorganic perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) are attracting considerable attention due to their promising thermal stability, but their inferior power-conversion efficiency (PCE) and moisture instability are hindering their application. Here, we used a gradient thermal annealing (GTA) method to control the growth of  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br crystals and then utilized a green anti-solvent (ATS) isopropanol to further optimize the morphology of  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film. Through this GTA-ATS synergistic effect, the growth of  $ \alpha $ -CsPbI $ _2 $ Br crystals could be precisely controlled, leading to a high-quality perovskite film with one-micron average grain size, low root-mean-square of 25.9 nm, and reduced defect density. Pero-SCs based on this CsPbI $ _2 $ Br film achieved a champion scan PCE of 16.07% (stabilized efficiency of 15.75%), which is the highest efficiency reported in all-inorganic pero-SCs. Moreover, the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br pero-SC demonstrates excellent robustness against moisture and oxygen, and maintains 90% of initial PCE after aging 120 hr under 100 mW/cm $ ^2 $  UV irradiation.

# INTRODUCTION

Since 2009, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) have made a tremendous progress in power-conversion efficiencies (PCEs), which have increased from 3.8% to 23.2%.  $  ^{1-9}  $   However, poor long-term stability, partly due to the thermally unstable and intrinsically volatile nature of organic components, is a barrier to their commercialization. Replacing the organic with an inorganic component is probably the ultimate solution for addressing the thermal instability issue.

Recently, all-inorganic cesium lead halide perovskites (CsPbX $ _3 $ , X = Cl, Br, I) have attracted considerable attention due to their thermochromic properties, excellent charge-carrier mobilities, and high thermal stabilities, where a stable phase can be retained even at temperatures exceeding 400°C. $ ^{10-12} $  Among the various components, CsPbBr $ _3 $ , as the first reported inorganic absorber in pero-SCs, has been fully exploited and demonstrated to have excellent stabilities, including thermal stability, $ ^{13} $  photostability (even under UV illumination), $ ^{11} $  and moisture resistance. $ ^{14} $  The best CsPbBr $ _3 $  PCE has reached  $ \sim $ 10% to date. $ ^{14} $  However, its 2.3-eV wide bandgap dramatically limits its absorption and further photovoltaic performance improvement. In contrast, another inorganic perovskite, CsPbI $ _3 $ , has a favorable bandgap of 1.73 eV and is regarded hopefully as an all-inorganic perovskite material. # Context & Scale

All-inorganic perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) develop rapidly due to their excellent thermal stability. However, their lower efficiency and humidity instability limit their application.   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   materials with a suitable bandgap offer a good trade-off between stability and light harvesting. For the first time, we precisely control   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   crystal growth by a synergistic effect of gradient thermal annealing (GTA) and anti-solvent (ATS). We demonstrated a high-quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film and realized a record efficiency of 16.07% (stabilized efficiency of 15.75%). These   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films-based pero-SCs also showed excellent robustness against moisture, oxygen, and UV light. Therefore, we believe that our results would provide significant progress in the field of all-inorganic pero-SCs, in particular for promoting their efficiency and stability toward commercialization. Thus, this work would be interesting to a wide readership across the optoelectronics community.

Although a series of strategies such as doping,  $  ^{17}  $   passivating,  $  ^{10,18}  $   synthesizing quantum dots,  $  ^{19-21}  $   and reduced-dimensional assistance  $  ^{22}  $   were carried out to stabilize the   $  \alpha  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $ , its intrinsic inferior phase stability with a low Goldschmidt tolerance factor (0.81) makes future application difficult.  $  ^{23}  $   By partially replacing an   $  I^{-}  $  ion with a   $  Br^{-}  $  ion, the mixed-halide   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   shows an increased Goldschmidt tolerance factor (0.84)  $  ^{24}  $   and improved stability of the   $  \alpha  $   phase, opening a bright future for highly stable all-inorganic pero-SCs.  $  ^{16,25}  $   However, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs generally showed a relatively low open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC} < 1.2~V  $  ) and a low short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC} < 14~mA/cm^{2}  $  ), resulting in a poor PCE typically below 12%. The unfavorable   $  V_{OC}  $   and   $  J_{SC}  $   are mainly ascribed to non-radiative recombination losses both inside the bulk perovskite film and at the interfacial contacts, owing to a considerable defect density and mismatched band alignment in the devices.  $  ^{25-31}  $   To overcome these problems, an interface engineering strategy was employed to passivate the surface defects of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film and optimize the band alignment of the devices; the resulting pero-SCs showed a significantly improved   $  V_{OC}  $   in the range of 1.19–1.32 V, but sacrificed fill factor (FF) and   $  J_{SC}  $ , indicating an unfavorable charge separation/transportation due to the existing deep trap states in the bulk   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ .  $  ^{26,27}  $   Various metal cations were doped into the host lattice for reducing defects and passivating the deep trap states of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ .  $  ^{29,30,32-34}  $   Both the FF and   $  J_{SC}  $   were significantly increased, which results in a better PCE of 13.47%. Several approaches to control   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   growth, including optimal annealing temperature,  $  ^{31,35}  $   controllable   $  \alpha  $ -CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br phase transition time,  $  ^{36}  $   and solvent control growth,  $  ^{15}  $   have also been proposed and can effectively reduce the density of defects in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films. By these strategies, incorporating a bilayer electron-transporting layer achieved a high PCE of 14.6%,  $  ^{37}  $   a precursor engineering process could push the PCE to a value of 14.78%,  $  ^{38}  $   and further employing temperature-assisted crystallization process a record PCE of 14.81% was realized.  $  ^{39}  $   These works confirmed that   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film quality dominates the related device performance. Nevertheless, the inferior   $  V_{OC}  $   and   $  J_{SC}  $   compared with theoretical values indicate that non-negligible defects still exist in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films.  $  ^{25,40,41}  $   Additionally, the relative thin thickness (200–350 nm) of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film limits the absorption of perovskite, which is another key factor resulting in a lower   $  J_{SC}  $ . Therefore, there is still plenty of room for further improving the PCE of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs by exploiting a high-quality and thick perovskite film with a low defect density and recombination center.

Here, we report a synergetic method consisting of gradient thermal annealing (GTA) and anti-solvent (ATS) treatment to precisely control the growth of crystals and thus construct a high-quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film. We found that the thermal annealing process determined the crystallization of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , while solvent type in the ATS process has a great influence on film uniformity and morphology. When processing the films via GTA-ATS with isopropanol (IPA) as anti-solvent, a high-quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film with a low defect density was obtained. We fabricated the n-i-p type pero-SCs using this   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film as a photoactive layer achieving a PCE of 16.07% (stabilized efficiency of 15.75%), which is the highest efficiency reported in all-inorganic perovskite solar cells to date. The device can also retain 95% of its initial efficiency after 1,000 hr of storage in an air atmosphere with a 30% relative humidity (RH) at room temperature. This GTA-ATS method can address the ambient-air stability and even the photostability issues of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cells.

To fine-tune the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film morphology further, we first performed an in situ investigation of the morphology evolution of perovskite film growth during the GTA treatment. As shown in the upper photographs in Figure 1B, during the pre-annealing process at 50°C the color of the as-prepared films gradually, unevenly changed from colorless to light brown from the periphery to the center, indicating a non-uniform nucleation grown process accrued. After the subsequent annealing processes at 100°C for 1 min and 160°C for 10 min, the films in periphery region show some inhomogeneous morphology with obvious stripes and bright color (Figure 1C) in comparison with that in the central region (Figure 1D), which are consistent with their related scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images (Figures 1E and 1F). This inhomogeneous morphology may arise from the strong coordination of DMSO with the precursor and the solvent edge effect whereby the residual DMSO-containing perovskite precursor is more likely to accumulate at the edge of the substrate during the thermal annealing process. Note that the pre-annealing temperature being obviously lower than the boiling point of DMSO would enhance this effect owing to DMSO's low volatility, providing enough time to accumulate.

To eliminate this edge solvent effect, we proposed an ATS concept to wash away the residual DMSO by a low-boiling-point solvent once the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor film formed. Toluene, which had a boiling point of 110.6°C and a viscosity of 0.59 mPa·s, was selected as the ATS. We dropped the toluene onto the as-prepared   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film before stopping the spinning. This process could rapidly extract the residual DMSO solvent and formed homogeneously dispersed   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor. # High-Quality  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  Films Improve Photovoltaic Performance

The prepared   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films were used as photoactive layers to fabricate pero-SCs with an n-i-p structure of indium-tin oxide (ITO)/c-  $  TiO_{2}/CsPbI_{2}Br/2,2',7,7'  $  -tetrakis(N,N-di-4-methoxyphenylamino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD)/Au, and the related device structure is shown in Figure 2K. Capacitance-voltage measurement was then employed to understand the influence of reduced intrinsic defect density on charge transport properties. Figure 5D shows the capacitance-voltage   $  (C^{-2}-V)  $   plots for the devices. The built-in potential was obtained according to the Mott-Schottky equation  $  ^{53}  $     $  C^{-2}=2(V_{bi}-V)/(A^{2}e\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}N_{A})  $  , where   $  V_{bi}  $   is the built-in potential, V is the applied voltage, A is the device area, e is the charge of the element,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative permittivity,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity, and   $  N_{A}  $   is the carrier concentration. This behavior indicates that a perovskite film with significant reduction in intrinsic defect density could improve the overall charge dynamic, which is a critical factor for achieving high   $  V_{OC}  $   and simultaneously enhancing other photovoltaic parameters.

# Conclusion

In summary, we developed a GTA-ATS method for precisely controlling the crystallization and morphology of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films. We found that the residual solvent in the precursor film plays an important role in determining the quality of the final perovskite films. An initial GTA process can slow down the evaporation rate of the residual DMSO, which can effectively suppress nucleation aggregation and facilitate the formation of high-purity   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br phase. The ATS process, by selecting the suitable low boiling point and viscosity anti-solvent IPA, can homogeneously "freeze" the perovskite precursor onto the substrate and reduce the solvent edge effect to improve the morphology of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, because IPA can extract the residual DMSO and has a weak coordination interaction with the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor. A uniform   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br film with compact crystals, large grain sizes, and negligible grain boundary morphology was realized by the GTA-ATS-IPA treatment. The pero-SCs based on this GTA-ATS-IPA film demonstrated a high PCE of 16.07% (stabilized efficiency of 15.75%), and benefited from less defect density

# EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES

# Characterization

The PCE values of the pero-SCs were measured under an illumination of AM 1.5G (100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) using an SS-F5-3A solar simulator (AAA grade, 50 × 50 mm  $  ^{2}  $  photo-beam size) of Enli Technology. The light intensity was corrected by a standard silicon solar cell. EQE spectrum was obtained by a solar cell spectral response measurement system (Enli Technology, QE-R3011). XRD patterns were collected using X'Pert Pro MPD (PANalytical). PL spectrum was obtained by a FLS980 (Edinburgh Instruments). Optical microscope images were taken by Leica DM40000M. SEM images were collected on an SU8010 produced by Hitachi, where the electron beam was accelerated at 5 kV. EDS images were also obtained by an SU8010 produced by Hitachi, where the electron beam was accelerated at 15 kV. Absorption spectrum was tested using a Cary Series UV-vis-NIR Spectrophotometer. Atomic force microscopy images were obtained using a Park XE-120 (Park Systems, Suwon, Korea) atomic force microscope. The UV irradiation was provided by UV resource (PORTA-RAY 400R), whose intensity is 100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $  . C  $  ^{-2}  $  -V spectra were measured using Zahner Ennium Electrochemical Workstation.

# Device Fabrication

Commercially available ITO glass (with ITO thickness of   $  \sim  $  200 nm) was sequentially cleaned with deionized water, acetone, ethanol, and IPA. For the electron-transporting layer, the c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was deposited on ITO substrate by spin-coating at 3,000 rpm for 30s and then annealed at 150°C for 30 min in air, after which the second c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was deposited under the same conditions. The samples were transferred into a nitrogen-filled glovebox for preparation of the perovskite layer. The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution was prepared by dissolving stoichiometric-ratio   $  CsI  $ ,   $  PbBr_{2}  $ , and   $  PbI_{2}  $   in DMSO with a 1.3 M solution. Before using it, the solution was stirred at 70°C for 2 hr and the precursor solution was filtered by a 0.45-  $  \mu  $  m PTFE membrane. For the GTA film, the precursor solution was spin-coated on the c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   at 3,000 rpm for 30 s. It was then transferred onto a hotplate for the GTA process: (1) 50°C for 1 min; (2) 100°C for 1 min; (3) 160°C for 10 min. For the GTA-ATS-Tol or GTA-ATS-IPA films, toluene or IPA was dropped onto the as-cast precursor film before stopping (after 20-s spin-coating) and followed another continuous 10-s spin-coating. The prepared precursor film was then treated with a GTA process and formed the resulting   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film. For deposition of hole-transporting material, Spiro-OMeTAD was dissolved in chlorobenzene to prepare Spiro-OMeTAD solution with a concentration of 80 mg/mL, then 35   $  \mu  $  L of lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide in acetonitrile (260 mg/mL) and 30   $  \mu  $  L of 4-tert-butylpyridine was added into the Spiro-OMeTAD solution. The mixture was coated onto the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film at 3,500 rpm for 30 s to form a Spiro-OMeTAD hole-transporting layer. Finally, the counter electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation of 80-nm-thick gold under a pressure of   $  5 \times 10^{-5}  $   Torr. The active area was 0.1 cm².
