# Abstract

The limited ultraviolet (UV) absorption of the common  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  electron-transporting layers (ETLs) and the trap states density as well as imperfect contact at  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite interface have been proposed as one of the main obstacles for realizing long-term photostability and high power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). To address this issues, an advanced and universal interface engineering has been employed to block UV irradiation on perovskite films and to improve the interface contact with perovskite layer as well as decrease the trap states of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  via chemical

Journal Pre-proofs

bonding by modifying an efficient UV absorber of 2,2'-methylenebis (4-tert-octyl-6-benzotriazole phenol) (UV-360) on the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETLs. Additionally, the triazole groups in UV-360 combine with the uncoordinated cations of perovskite to reduce the defects at  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite interface, and a large-grained perovskite film with low grain boundaries is also formed on the UV-360 modified  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETLs owing to the reduction of perovskite nucleation sites. As a result, the modification of UV-360 on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  greatly filtrates the UV attack on perovskite photosensitive layer and suppresses the interfacial charge recombination as well as promotes charge extraction. Finally, the carbon-based  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  PSC tailored with UV-360 modified  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETLs free of encapsulation achieves a champion PCE up to  $ 9.61\% $  with super-stability under long-term light soaking as well as UV illumination, high temperature and high humidity conditions in air. Our work provides a new perspective to achieve PSCs with high efficiency and stability by introducing UV-absorption functional materials.

Keywords:  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  perovskite solar cells, Interface Engineering, Ultraviolet filtration, Suppressed charge recombination, Photostability

# 1. Introduction

When converting photovoltaic technology from research theory to commercial products, low cost, high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and stability are three key indicators for examining their technical feasibility in addition to other factors, such as low toxicity and energy recovery time, high-throughput production technology to manufacture effective large-area modules, etc. In the field of photovoltaic technology, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have made great achievements with a certified  $ 25.2\% $  PCE

9

Journal Pre-proofs

of organic-inorganic hybrid devices owing to their excellent photoelectric properties including remarkably long carrier mobility and diffusion lengths, wide spectral response and high light absorption coefficient etc, which is comparable to monocrystalline silicon solar cells [1-6]. With the development of manufacturing technology and device structural materials, the cost of preparing PSCs has been continuously reduced. However, notably, how to improve the long-term stability of PSCs has become the most difficult problem in commercial applications [7].

As timely as rain, perbrominated   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   halide has quickly attracted widespread attention owing to its superior moist heat stability in air atmosphere, and revived hope for the commercial development of PSCs. According to previous reports, the devices with an architecture of FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /m-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  /Carbon retained 79% of the original PCE under 90% RH at 25 °C over 130 days and almost unchanged efficiency under 0 RH at 80 °C over 40 days [8], which is unmatched by any hybrid PSCs. However, while appreciating long-term tolerance toward humidity and heat, its low PCE and poor photostability have become urgent problems. In addition to the inherent narrow spectral absorption range (<550 nm) of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   resulted from its high band gap (2.3 eV), the serious charge recombination at the charge-transporting layer/  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   interface and/or within   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film is one of the main reasons for the low PCE of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs [9-11].   $  TiO_{2}  $   is a well-known electron-transporting layers (ETLs) that widely used in PSCs due to its outstanding advantages, such as suitable band gap width, corrosion resistance, non-toxicity and low cost, etc [12,13]. However, many defects such as oxygen vacancies of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   film arising from photocatalytic reaction and its

rough surface morphology not only lead to low electron extraction and transfer rates at  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite interface, but also cause imperfect interface contact with perovskite layer to indirectly affect the perovskite film quality deposited on the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , which exacerbates charge recombination and the efficiency loss [13]. Besides, the limited ultraviolet (UV) light absorption of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETLs makes the perovskite structure easy to degradation under long-term illumination, which significantly destroys the photostability of PSCs [14]. Although the destruction of perovskites by UV irradiation has been widely confirmed, there is little research and effect on improving the long-term stability of devices under UV irradiation. Chander et al. deposited a  $ \mathrm{YVO_4:Eu^{3+}} $  down-shifting nano-phosphor layer on the nonconductive surface of fluorine doped tin oxide glass to reduce the perovskite degradation induced by UV light and actually enhanced the stability of PSCs under UV light soaking, but the serious charge recombination inside the devices was not addressed and thereby the obtained PCE of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ -PSCs was only about  $ \sim 8.11\% $  [15]. Therefore, it is essential to suppress charge recombination by modifying the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite interface and reduce the UV irradiation on perovskite for improved PCE and photostability of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  PSCs.

At recent, it has been reported that the implementation of contact-passivation strategies can effectively reduce interfacial charge recombination. For instance, Shih et al. introduced glycine to modify the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite interface and achieved the effect of simultaneously enhancing the interface contact and reducing the defect states density of the perovskite film, as a result the PCE of the modified PSCs boosted from 8.35 to  $ 12.02\% $  [16]. Ma et al. successfully performed contact passivation treatment at the

4

Journal Pre-proofs

$ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite interface with NaCl-doped  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  approach for the more perfect interface contact, significantly promoting charge transfer and suppressing charge recombination [17]. Jiang et al. studied the introduction of  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  layer at the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /pervoskite interface and found that Cl anions could improve the contact between  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and perovskite through interfacial coupling, while ammonium cationic could passivate the anions defects within the perovskite film to enhance the film quality, thereby greatly improving the PCE of PSCs [18]. Zhang et al. reported that the capping of dopamine on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanoparticles not only reduced the oxygen vacancies and suppressed the deep trap states of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  for improved UV tolerance of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  films, but also passivated the defects on perovskite surface, leading to an enhanced PCE and full-sun illumination stability of PSCs [19]. All the results demonstrate that the contact-passivation technology can remarkably reduce the charge recombination at  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /pervoskite interface by passivating defects and improving interfacial contact and thereby enhance device PCE.

Hence, inspired by the suppression of charge recombination realized by contact-passivation method and the reduction of UV irradiation on perovskite, a creative and meaningful strategy is first proposed to introduce the UV absorber layer of 2,2'-methylenebis (4-tert-octyl-6-benzotriazole phenol) (UV-360) to modify the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  surface for simultaneously enhancing UV absorption, reducing the defects at  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite interface and improving the interface contact as well as the quality of the formed perovskite film. As an interface modifier, the hydroxyl groups in the UV-360 can passivate the oxygen vacancies on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  surface through chelating effect and

5

Journal Pre-proofs

the triazole groups tend to combine with uncoordinated cations of the perovskite film to decrease the trap states at the interface of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite, which greatly suppresses charge recombination and efficiency loss. Meanwhile, the smooth morphology of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  after modifying UV-360 improves the interface contact with perovskite, and the decrease of the wettability of modified  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film reduce the nucleation locations of  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  precursor to fabricate a high-quality perovskite film with large-grains, resulting in an acceleration of charge extraction and transportation. As a result, a champion PCE as high as  $ 9.61\% $  is achieved for PSCs with the architecture of FTO/c- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /m- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /UV-360/ $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $ /Carbon, yielding an increase by  $ 49.22\% $  in comparison with  $ 6.44\% $  efficiency of the control device. Moreover, the modified devices exhibit extremely high long-term stability under different environmental conditions, especially the PCE maintains  $ 87\% $  and  $ 94\% $  of the original efficiency under UV light soaking for  $ 300\mathrm{h} $  and simulated sunlight illumination for  $ 600\mathrm{h} $ , respectively. Our work also demonstrates that this interface engineering strategy is applicable and effective to PSCs based on other organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1 Materials and Reagents

Both reagents and materials in this work were purchased from the specific supplier and were not purified before use. The synthesis method of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  paste was consistent with our previous work [8].

# 2.2 Preparation of   $  TiO_{2}  $   photoanodes

FTO conductive glass etched by hydrochloric acid and zinc powder was washed

6

Journal Pre-proofs

with deionized water, absolute ethanol, acetone, and isopropanol in order. An ethanol solution containing 0.5 M titanium isopropoxide and 0.5 M diethanol amine was deposited on the FTO glass surface at 7000 rpm for 30 s, and then annealed at 500 °C for 2 h to successfully obtain the compact TiO₂ (c-TiO₂) film. The prepared TiO₂ paste was spin-coated on the FTO/c-TiO₂ sample at 2000 rpm for 30 s, and annealed at 450 °C for 0.5 h in a muffle furnace to obtain the mesoscopic TiO₂ (m-TiO₂) layer. Next, the FTO/c-TiO₂/m-TiO₂ sample was immersed in an aqueous solution of 40 mM of TiCl₄ at 75 °C for 0.5 h, rinsed successively with water and absolute ethanol, then finally annealed at 450 °C for another 0.5 h. Until now, the preparation of TiO₂ photoanode had been successfully completed. In this work, the chlorobenzene solution containing different concentrations of UV-360 (0.5, 1 and 3 mg mL⁻¹) was deposited on TiO₂ photoanode at 2000 rpm for 30 s and heated at 100°C for about 10 min to achieve multiple functional modifications simultaneously.

# 2.3 Assembly of inorganic perovskite solar cells

The   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite films were fabricated by a multi-step solution-processed technology. Firstly, 90 uL of DMF solution containing   $  PbBr_{2}  $   (1.0 M) was spin coated onto the pre-heated (90 °C for 15 min) FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /m-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /with or without UV-360 film at 2000 rpm for 30 s, followed by drying at 90 °C for 0.5 h to fully evaporate the DMF solvent. Then, 0.07 M of CsBr methanol solution was spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 30 s, and heated at 250 °C for about 5 min. After repeating the above deposition of CsBr onto the prepared   $  PbBr_{2}  $   film for seven times, the high-quality   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite film was produced. Finally, conductive carbon paste was knife-coated on the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $

# 2.4 Measurements and characterizations

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra of all samples were performed on the PHI-5000C ESCA system (Perkin Elmer) with MgKα radiation (hv = 1253.6 eV). The infrared absorption peak position and peak intensity of each functional group of UV-360, TiO₂+UV-360, UV-360+CsBr and UV-360+PbBr₂ were obtained by a commercial FTIR system (Cary 680, Agilent). The top-view as well as cross-sectional SEM images and EDS mapping images of various films were characterized by a field-emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM, SU8220, Hitachi). Ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) absorption and transmission spectra of FTO/c-TiO₂/m-TiO₂/with and without UV-360 films and the absorption spectra of various perovskite films were measured by Meipuda UV-3200 spectrophotometer. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were conducted on PHILIPSPW1800 diffractometer (X-ray Source: Cu-Ká radiation, λ = 1.5406 Å). Both J-V curves of devices were obtained by CHI660E electrochemical workstation, the simulated solar light intensity under AM 1.5 G solar light condition was 100 mW cm⁻². The photostability of PSCs was measured under the bias voltage at the maximum power point (V_MPP) with simulated AM 1.5G (100 mW cm⁻²) solar continuous illumination. The specific process was: the device was first tested under

8

Journal Pre-proofs

continuous illumination at  $  V_{\mathrm{MPP}}  $  over 600 h to obtain a steady-state photocurrent density ( $  J_{\mathrm{MPP}}  $ ) curve, then the corresponding steady-state output PCE was calculated according to the formula:  $  \mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{MPP}} = (V_{\mathrm{MPP}} \times J_{\mathrm{MPP}} / 100) \times 100\%  $ . The UV lamp ( $  \lambda = 340  $  nm) with an intensity of 15 mW cm $  ^{-2}  $  was used to continuously irradiate the PSCs to measure the stability of long-term UV light illumination. The IPCE spectrum was characterized on a power source (Newport 300 W xenon lamp, 66920) in the wavelength range of 300-600 nm. The trap states density of  $  \mathrm{CsPbBr_3}  $  films for the electron-only devices were recorded from 0 to 7 V under dark condition with a space-charge limited current (SCLC) model. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were conducted at  $  V_{\mathrm{OC}}  $  under dark condition from 1 MHz to 0.1 Hz. The time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) decay spectrum and steady-state PL spectrum of various samples were performed on a Horiba spectrometer excited at 500 nm laser and a FluoroMax-4 spectrofluorometer excited by 385 nm laser, respectively.

The extremely high defects on the surface of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and perovskite films is the main reason for the serious charge recombination at the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ /perovskite interface. In this work, by modifying UV-360 on the surface of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETLs, the characteristic groups contained in UV-360 can simultaneously passivate defects on the surface of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and perovskite films through chemical bonding to improve charge extraction and separation. As shown in Fig. 1a, the intense photocatalytic effect of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  under UV irradiation in

control sample can generate holes to extract electrons from Br⁻ anion in perovskite, thereby resulting in the decomposition of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   (Stage I) [14,21,22]. In addition, the oxygen vacancies on the surface of   $  TiO_{2}  $   film formed by long-term UV irradiation and the uncoordinated ions (  $  Cs^{+}, Pb^{2+}  $  ) defects of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film are responsible for the serious charge recombination and photo-generated electrons loss at the interface (Stage II). As a result, the charge recombination and the photo-decomposition of perovskite structure in the modified sample can be significantly suppressed to enhance the comprehensive performance of the PSCs. # 4. Conclusions

28

Journal Pre-proofs

In summary, we have launched a novel strategy to reduce the UV irradiation on perovskite film and improve the charge extraction by introducing the UV-360 absorber at the ETLs/perovskite interface. The oxygen vacancies on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and the surface trap states density of perovskite film are respectively passivated by the hydroxyl and triazole groups of UV-360, resulting in a reduced interfacial defects and charge recombination. A good interfacial contact is formed between  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and perovskite film after UV-360 modification, and a large-grained perovskite film with low grain boundaries is also fabricated on the UV-360 modified  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , which enhance the charge extraction and transfer. As a result, a champion PCE of  $ 9.61\% $  for the PSCs with an architecture of FTO/c- $ \mathrm{TiO_2/m - TiO_2/UV - 360 / CsPbBr_3 / Carbon} $  is obtained, which is increased by  $ 49.22\% $  compared with  $ 6.44\% $  efficiency for the control device. In addition, the modified device exhibits a remarkably improved long-term stability in various environmental conditions, especially under continuous illumination and UV light soaking. Our work provides a new and universal idea of modifying UV-absorption functional materials at ETLs/perovskite interface to decrease the transmission of UV light and suppress the charge recombination for fabricating efficient and photostable PSCs.
