# Suppressing Defects-Induced Nonradiative Recombination for Efficient Perovskite Solar Cells through Green Antisolvent Engineering

Wenzhan Xu, Yu Gao, Wenjie Ming, Fang He, Jingzhou Li, Xu-Hui Zhu, Feiyu Kang, Jiangyu Li,* and Guodan Wei*

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites have attracted considerable attention due to their superior optoelectronic properties. Traditional one-step solution-processed perovskites often suffer from defects-induced nonradiative recombination, which significantly hinders the improvement of device performance. Herein, treatment with green antisolvents for achieving high-quality perovskite films is reported. Compared to defects-filled ones, perovskite films by antisolvent treatment using methylamine bromide (MABr) in ethanol (MABr-Eth) not only enhances the resultant perovskite crystallinity with large grain size, but also passivates the surface defects. In this case, the engineering of MABr-Eth-treated perovskites suppressing defects-induced nonradiative recombination in perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is demonstrated. As a result, the fabricated inverted planar heterojunction device of ITO/PTAA/Cs $ _{0.15} $ FA $ _{0.85} $ PbI $ _{3} $ /PC $ _{61} $ BM/Phen-NADPO/Ag exhibits the best power conversion efficiency of 21.53%. Furthermore, the corresponding PSCs possess a better storage and light-soaking stability.

It is noteworthy that most of high-performance PSCs reported so far are based on mixed monovalent cations perovskite thin films, deposition of which required the strategy of antisolvent engineering.  $  ^{[11]}  $   Antisolvent engineering has been demonstrated to be effective in tuning the crystal nucleation and grain growth.  $  ^{[12]}  $   However, the amount of antisolvent required for achieving high efficiency is large and more seriously, most of which, such as carcinogenic solvent chlorobenzene and toluene, are toxic.  $  ^{[13]}  $   Obviously, toxicity is incompatible with practical industrial development, which has gradually become a major obstacle for the low-cost solution process upscaling. In this regard, the development of toxicity-free green antisolvent engineering for high performance PSCs is very attractive.

In this study, we chose green antisolvent of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}Br  $   (MABr) in ethanol (MABr-Eth) to modify perovskite   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $  , where   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $   is used due to its high efficiency and stability in PSCs.  $  ^{[25,26]}  $   On the one hand, MABr treatment enhances the resultant perovskites crystallinity with large grain size. On the other hand, MABr passivates the surface defects. In addition, MABr reacts with   $  PbI_{2}  $   from the decomposition of   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $   by Eth washing. In this case, it is demonstrated that the engineering of MABr-Eth-treated perovskites do suppress defects-induced nonradiative recombination. As a result, the fabricated inverted planar heterojunction device of ITO/PTAA/  $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}/PC_{61}BM/Phen-NADPO/Ag  $   exhibits the champion PCE of 21.53% with a much better storage and light-soaking stability.

The deposition process of perovskite   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $   thin films via the method of one-step spin-coating with antisolvent (x mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   MABr in Eth, x = 0, 1, 2, 5) treatment is displayed in Figure S1 of the Supporting Information, adding dropwise antisolvent of 80   $  \mu  $  L during last 10 s of the spin-coating process on top of the precursor films. Afterward, the films were thermal annealed at 100 °C for 1 h. Here noting that perovskite or PSCs treated by Eth referred as Eth-Perovskite or Eth-PSCs and perovskite or PSCs treated by MABr-Eth referred as MABr-Eth-Perovskite or MABr-Eth-PSCs. To study the influence of MABr in Eth treatment on the optical properties of various perovskite films, absorption of which was conducted. As shown in Figure 1a, no change in absorption profile for all perovskite films implies the treatment of MABr would not affect the perovskite crystal structure, while blue-shifted absorption were observed after treatment, which may be due to reaction of MABr with PbI  $  _{2}  $  . Further, to investigate the charge carrier recombination behavior and charge carrier dynamics in perovskite films, steady state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved

photoluminescence (TRPL) were conducted in Figure 1a and Figure S2 (Supporting Information), respectively. It is clear that about 6, 10, and  $ 14\mathrm{nm} $  blue-shifted PL peak observed from  $ 1\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  MABr-Eth-Perovskite,  $ 2\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  MABr-Eth-Perovskite and  $ 5\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  MABr-Eth-Perovskite, respectively, which are consistent with the results from the absorption. In addition, the MABr-Eth-Perovskite exhibits an obvious increased PL intensity compared with Eth-Perovskite, implying a significant suppressed nonradiative recombination due to MABr passivates the defects.[27] TRPL results of samples were fitted by biexponential decay function with PL lifetime  $ (\tau_{i}) $  and amplitudes  $ (A_{1}) $ , followed by the equation of  $ \gamma = \gamma_0 + A_1e^{-1 / \tau_1} + A_2e^{-1 / \tau_2} $  in Figure S2 and corresponding parameters are summarized in Table S1 of the Supporting Information.  $ \tau_{1} $  is closely related to nonradiative recombination by defects and  $ \tau_{2} $  as a component of radiative recombination from perovskite.[28,29] As a result, the increase in  $ \tau_{1} $  for MABr-Eth-Perovskite is attributed to the lower defect density. The increase in  $ \tau_{\mathrm{ave}} $  (11.31 vs 14.73 ns) is consistent with the improved film quality by MABr-Eth treatment. This result implies reduced possibility of defects induced recombination.

X-ray diffraction (XRD) for various perovskite thin films before and after thermal annealing are carried out to study the effect of MABr on crystal structure of perovskite. Figure 1b and Figure S3 (Supporting Information) showed that peaks at   $  14.8^{\circ}  $  ,   $  21.2^{\circ}  $  ,   $  28.6^{\circ}  $  , and   $  30.6^{\circ}  $  , corresponding to the (110), (012), (220), and (310) crystal planes, respectively, of the all perovskite thin films, demonstrating prepared thin films retain perovskite phase structure, which is favorable for promoting device performance. Interestingly,   $  2 \, mg \, mL^{-1}  $   MABr-Eth-Perovskite thin film exhibits the strongest peak intensity in (110) of crystal planes, which indicates higher crystal orientation along the (110) direction.  $  ^{[30]}  $   However, a peak at   $  12.8^{\circ}  $   detected from the Eth-Perovskite film (both before and after thermal annealing) is likely to due to the peak of decomposed   $  PbI_{2}  $   (Eth could decompose perovskite to   $  PbI_{2}  $  ), which agrees with the previous report.  $  ^{[13]}  $   Further, a smaller content   $  PbI_{2}  $   is also detected from the   $  1 \, mg \, mL^{-1}  $   MABr-Eth-Perovskite thin film before thermal annealing, which is due to insufficient enough MABr to react with   $  PbI_{2}  $  . Table S2 of the Supporting Information showed the XRD intensity ratio of (110) plane of perovskite with   $  PbI_{2}  $   for perovskite thin film before and after thermal annealing. It was observed the intensity ratio was enhanced from 0.23 to 11.90 for Eth-Perovskite and 1.96 to 13.35 for   $  2 \, mg \, mL^{-1}  $   MABr-Eth-Perovskite before and after thermal annealing, respectively, which implies that MABr promotes perovskite crystallinity. The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements of Eth-Perovskite,   $  2 \, mg \, mL^{-1}  $   MABr-Eth-Perovskite and   $  5 \, mg \, mL^{-1}  $   MABr-Eth-Perovskite films were conducted in Figures S4–S6 of the Supporting Information, respectively. Table S3 of the Supporting Information summarizes the atoms content of each perovskite films, which confirms that Br is detected in 2 and   $  5 \, mg \, mL^{-1}  $   MABr-Eth-Perovskite films, and more Br with increasing the MABr concentration, implying that Br ion retains on the perovskite films, which is likely to passivate the surface defects by MABr filling the defects states and/or react with   $  PbI_{2}  $   to form MAPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br.

In order to calculate the electron trap densities of the MABr-Eth-Perovskite film, the electron-only device with a device structure of ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /Perovskite/PC $ _{61} $ BM/Ag is fabricated and characterized. The electron carrier trap densities of the perovskite thin films are estimated from the trap-filling limited (TFL) region based on the Mott–Gurney law. $ ^{[45,46]} $  Figure S10 of the Supporting Information presents the  $ I-V $  curves of electron-only devices. The electron trap densities ( $ n_t $ ) of the perovskite thin films is estimated according to the equation of  $ V_{\text{TFL}} = \frac{en_t L^2}{2\varepsilon c_0} $ , where  $ V_{\text{TFL}} $  is the onset voltage of the TFL,  $ L $  is the thickness of the perovskite thin film,  $ \varepsilon $  is relative dielectric constant of perovskite ( $ \varepsilon $  is assumed to be 32), $ ^{[47]} $  and  $ \varepsilon_0 $  is the vacuum permittivity. Therefore,  $ n_t $  of Eth-Perovskite thin film and MABr-Eth-Perovskite thin film are calculated. It is found that the electron trap density in MABr-Eth-Perovskite thin film ( $ 1.29 \times 10^{16} \text{ cm}^{-3} $ ) is reduced by more than twofold than those in Eth-Perovskite thin film ( $ 2.95 \times 10^{16} \text{ cm}^{-3} $ ).

Figure 5a and Figure S11 (Supporting Information) show the   $  V_{OC}  $   and   $  J_{SC}  $   dependent on the steady-state light intensity (I) for investigation of the charge carrier recombination in PSCs, respectively. The slope (S) of   $  V_{OC}  $   versus   $  \ln(I/I_{0})  $   for MABr-Eth-PSCs is 1.592   $  k_{B}T/q  $ , where   $  k_{B}  $   is the Boltzmann's constant, T is the temperature, and q is the elementary charge, while 1.805   $  k_{B}T/q  $   of slope was achieved for Eth-PSCs. The S of 1   $  k_{B}T/q  $   suggests the absence of defects-induced recombination. The larger S deviating from 1, the more defects-induced recombination.  $  ^{[48,49]}  $   The MABr-Eth-PSCs exhibit S closer to 1 compared with the Eth-PSCs, arising from the suppressed nonradiative recombination. The power law dependence of   $  J_{SC}  $   upon I can be fitted to   $  J_{SC} \propto I^{\alpha}  $ . The exponential factor   $  \alpha  $   is close to 1, there is no space-charge limit effect.  $  ^{[50]}  $   The   $  \alpha  $   of 0.97 for MABr-Eth-PSCs is larger than 0.93 for Eth-PSCs, indicating more balanced charge carrier extraction due to suppressed defects-induced recombination.  $  ^{[51]}  $

To further evaluate the charge carrier lifetime in fully operating devices, the transient photovoltage (TPV) of PSCs was conducted.  $  ^{[56-58]}  $   Upon the illumination of pulse light, the photocarriers were generated to create free electrons and holes as well as the generation of photovoltage. Once the incident light was off, the voltage started to decay due to the instantaneous nonradiative recombination of electrons and holes at possible defect sites. As shown in Figure 5c, the charge carrier decay time is 6.2   $  \mu  $  s for MABr-Eth-PSCs, which is much longer than 4.8   $  \mu  $  s for Eth-PSCs. The longer decay time indicates that the MABr-Eth-treated active layer of   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $   has slower surface charge recombination, attributing to improved film quality with less nonradiative recombination sites. Meanwhile, the longer decay time offers more chance for charge carriers could be extracted from perovskite to their respective electrode, substantially reducing the carrier recombination. To note, the improved film quality and crystallinity of MABr-Eth-treated   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $   could reduce transport resistance and enhance the charge collection efficiency.

In conclusion, we reported a facile but highly efficient approach of antisolvent engineering for improving the efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells through suppressing nonradiative recombination herein. Our strategy of MABr-treated perovskites delivers PSCs with the best PCE of 21.53%, and better storage and light-soaking stability. The MABr here, on the one hand, is compensating for the loss of FAI via reacting with   $  PbI_{2}  $  , from the decomposition of perovskite; On the other hand, MABr passivates the surface defects. After perovskite film being treated by antisolvent of MABr-Eth, which shows improved crystallinity and morphology with highly full coverage, thereby reducing transport resistance and consequently increasing FF. The TRPL measurement shows MABr-Eth-treated   $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite films have longer charge carrier decay time, indicating improved film quality with less nonradiative recombination sites which is beneficial for charge collection efficiency. TAS shows a lower density of surface defect states after perovskite being passivated by MABr, which lowers down the probability of charge carriers being captured, enhancing the   $  J_{SC}  $  . Additionally, MABr-Eth-PSCs exhibit a higher   $  EQE_{EL}  $   which makes sure a reduced   $  \Delta V_{OC,nonrad}  $  , and therefore an enhanced   $  V_{OC}  $  . These results confirmed the defects-induced nonradiative recombination has been substantially suppressed for MABr-Eth-PSCs, and further our work signifies successfully the importance of rationally choosing green antisolvent for PSCs to achieve improved performance and stability.

Fabrication of Perovskite Solar Cells:  $ \sim 10 $  nm PTAA layer is spin-coated onto precleaned and UV-ozone-treated ITO-coated glass substrates from PTAA solution with concentration of  $ 2\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  at  $ 6000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 20~s $  followed with thermal annealing at  $ 110^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 10\mathrm{min} $  on the hotplate in Ar atmosphere. The perovskite layer is spin-coated on the top of PTAA with a spin-speed of  $ 1500~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 8\mathrm{s} $  and  $ 5000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for  $ 40~s $  continuously, followed with thermal annealing at a temperature of  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  on the hotplate for  $ 1\textrm{h} $ . It is worth mentioning that Eth was dropped onto the as-cast perovskite precursor film with last  $ 8\mathrm{s} $  finishing the spinning-perovskite films. For the MABr-Eth-PSCs, MABr-Eth solution was spin-coated on the top of as-cast perovskite precursor film. Eth-Perovskite film has a thickness of  $ 450~\mathrm{nm} $ , which is smaller than  $ 470~\mathrm{nm} $  of MABr-Eth-Perovskite film. For deposition of electron extraction material,  $ \mathrm{PC}_{60}\mathrm{BM} $  ( $ 20\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  in CB) of  $ 80~\mathrm{nm} $  and Phen-NADPO ( $ 0.5\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  in IPA) of  $ 5\mathrm{nm} $  were spin-coated layer by layer. Finally, the counter electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation of  $ 100~\mathrm{nm} $  thick Ag under a pressure of  $ 2\times 10^{-6} $  mbar. The active area was measured to be  $ 0.06~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ .

Characterization of Perovskite Thin Films: UV–vis–NIR absorption and PL spectra of thin films were recorded on an HP 8453 spectrophotometer and FLS920 spectrofluorimeter (Edinburgh Instruments), respectively. A 150 W, ozone-free xenon arc lamp was used in PL measurements. SEM images were obtained by using a field emission scanning electron microscope (JEOL-7401). Thicknesses of thin films were measured by Dektak 150 surface profilometer. The XRD measured on a desktop diffractometer (D8 Advance, Bruker, Germany) with a Cu Kα source at the range of 10°–90° (D8 Advance; Bruker, Germany). The XPS measurements are performed on an ESCALAB MK II (VG Co., Ltd, England) spectrometer, with Al Ka excitation radiation under ultrahigh vacuum condition. c-AFM-based measurement was performed on Asylum Research Cypher AFM with/without the built-in LED sheds light on the sample from the top. The broadband fs-TA measurements were carried out on a Heliospump-probe system (Ultrafast Systems LLC) coupled with an amplified femtosecond laser system (Coherent, 35 fs, 1 kHz, 800 nm). The probe pulses (from 380 to 800 nm) were generated by focusing a small portion (around 10 μJ) of the fundamental 800 nm laser pulses into a 1 mm CaF₂. The 365 nm pump pulses were generated from an optical parametric amplifier (TOPAS-800-fs). TA curves as a function of delay time were fitted to a biexponential decay functions of γ = γ₀ + A₁e⁻¹/τ₁ + A₂e⁻¹/τ₂.

Characterization of Perovskite Solar Cells: The J-V characteristics of PSCs were measured by a Keithley model 2400 source measure unit (Newport, Oriel AM 1.5 G, 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). The light intensity of 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  was calibrated by a silicon reference cell. The EQE is obtained by using EQE measurement system. The cells were connected to a digital oscilloscope (Tektronix TDS 3052C) and the input impedance of the oscilloscope was set to 1 MΩ and 50 Ω, respectively, for monitoring the charge density decay. The EQE  $  _{EL}  $  of the PSCs were obtained using a fiber-optic spectrometer (Ocean Optics, QE Pro), where a constant current density was provided by a Keithley 2400 digital sourcemeter. The transient photovoltage was measured under 0.3 sun background illumination. An attenuated laser pulse (500 nm) was used as a small perturbation to the background illumination on the device. The laser-pulse-induced photovoltage variation was smaller than 5% of the V  $  _{OC}  $  produced by the background illumination.
