# 1. Introduction

In recent times, numerous electron transporting materials (ETMs) containing the metal oxides and their composites have been studied to produce the inexpensive and stable hybrid organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with incredible power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) (Mahmood et al., 2015a, 2017, 2018a; Yang et al., 2017; Luo et al., 2018; Mahmood et al., 2018b; Dong et al., xxxx; Mahmood et al., 2019; Rehman et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2013; McGehee, 2013). Since, these ETMs show a crucial role in transporting and extracting the photogenerated electrons from perovskite absorber layer to fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass substrate (Jeon et al., 2015; Yang et al., 2017). Thus, the development of novel ETMs as an electron transporting layer (ETL) in PSCs is a critical matter to explore. The photovoltaic communities have been mainly focused on the exploration of transition metal oxides namely  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{ZnO} $ ,  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{WO_3} $  and  $ \mathrm{ZnSO_4} $  as ETLs in perovskite devices (Mahmood et al., 2015b; Wu et al., 2016; Mahmood et al., 2018c; Bera et al., 2014; Singh et al., 2017; Murakami et al., 2017; Song et al., 2017). Most of these ETLs, needed high temperature sintering to achieve the electrically conducting phase, which hampers their feasibility in flexible electronics. # ABSTRACT

For perovskite solar cells (PSCs), the development of low cost, stable and efficient electron transport materials (ETMs) is of prime consideration. Here, we have introduced a scalable electrospraying technique for the direct deposition of hydrotermally synthesized two-dimensional (2D)  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheets ETMs, onto the fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass substarte to use in PSCs with reduces hysteresis, improved ambient stability and high efficiency. The PSCs fabricated with electrospray deposited  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheets demonstrate the maximum power conversion efficiency  $ (\mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{max}}) $  of  $ 16.17\% $  , that is comparable to the PCEs achieved from compact  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETM based PSCs. The current findings present a new route for the construction of stable and efficient PSCs based on a low-cost and solution- processed  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheets ETMs.

Hence, there is a real motivation behind the exploration of a stable and low temperature solution-processed novel ETMs for efficient PSCs.

Thin films of inorganic compounds having layered-structures such as transition metal dichalcogenide (TMDC), particularly   $  MoS_{2}  $   have recently drawn incredible attention of the perovskite researchers as a hole transport material, owing to their high carrier mobility and intrinsic band gap (George et al., 2018; Huang et al., 2017; Dasgupta et al., 2017; Kim et al., 2016). In addition, these films are highly flexible and facilitate rapid charge transport in a vertical path. Consequently,   $  MoS_{2}  $   can also be one of the talented ETM for stable PSCs if its work function can be controlled to a low enough value as compared to other ETMs such as   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}  $  . The employment of   $  MoS_{2}  $   thin films (composed of particulates) as an ETL in PSCs is limited to one report only (Singh et al., 2019). These typical   $  MoS_{2}  $   thin films were mostly synthesized along the surface direction, with a few of them were grown vertically as produced via microwave irradiation method. These films are not applicable for the larger area production of PSCs because of their complex fabrication method and slower charge transport in the vertical direction. In order to avoid the complex film formation challenges and low efficiency devices, there is great need to grow the two-dimensional (2D)  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheets at low temperature to facilitate the charge transport in the vertical direction efficiently. These 2D  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheets with novel structures mostly facilitate the ultra-fast charge transportation and mainly contain few single nanosheets (Karunadasa et al., 2012).

In the current work, we employed the thin films of 2D   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanosheets as the ETL in PSCs.   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanosheets were produced at low-temperature via hydrothermal technique followed by electrospray deposition of nanosheets layers directly over fluorine doped tin oxide (FTO) substrate. For comparison the hydrothermally grown nanosheets were also formed onto the FTO substrates via spin-coating method. The electrospraying method supports the layer-by-layer link of the nanosheets with the better surface coverage compared to spin-coating technique. The PSCs based on electrospray deposited ETLs of 2D   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanosheets exhibited the best PCE of 16.17%, which is superior than spin-coated nanosheets (PCE of 13.93%), possibly acknowledged to the uniform surface coverage of   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanosheets and larger surface in contact with the perovskite absorber layer for the rapid charge carrier extraction vertically. Interestingly, this study also demonstrates an inexpensive, low-temperature, and simplified route to fabricate the efficient and stable perovskite devices containing 2D   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanosheets as ETL with better morphology.

# 2. Experimental details

# 2.1.Materials

Ammonium molybdate (83.00%), hydrazine monohydrate (98%), Sulfur (99.98%), hydrochloric acid (37%), lead (II) iodide (99%), and methyl ammonium iodide were bought from Sigma-Aldrich and used as received. 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis[N-4-methoxyphenyl-N'-2-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'spirobifluorene (po-Spiro-MeOTAD) hole transport material (HTL) was obtained from 1-Material company.

# 2.2. Synthesis of  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheets via hydrothermal method

MoS  $  _{2}  $   nanosheets were synthesized via hydrothermal method (Peng et al., 2001) as reported elsewhere. In a typical hydrothermal method, 1 g of ammonium molybdate and 0.3 g of elemental sulfur were dissolved in 7 mL of hydrazine monohydrate and 12 mL of deionized (DI) water. These reactants were then added to Teflon lined 250 mL autoclave and heated for 48 h at 180 °C. After 48 h, the autoclave was cooled down to room temperature and the resulting black precipitates (with production yield exceeding 22 wt%) were thoroughly washed with water, dilute HCl (37%) and ethanol repeatedly. After that, synthesized MoS  $  _{2}  $   nanosheets were dried in vacuum at 60 °C for 5 h. Finally, the MoS  $  _{2}  $   nanosheets were well dispersed in ethanol and DI water solution for further use as an electrospray precursor.

# 2.3. Electrospray deposition of  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheets

Thin films of hydrothermally synthesized  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheets were deposited directly onto the fluorine doped tin oxide (FTO) glass (surface resistivity  $ \sim 10\Omega /\mathrm{sq} $  and transmittance  $ 83\% $  (visible)) via electrospraying technique (Hameed et al., 2019). FTO glass substrates were cleaned ultrasonically using ethanol, DI water and acetone each for  $ 15\mathrm{min} $ , respectively. The precursor for electrospray deposition of  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheets was  $ 8\mathrm{mL} $  of  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $   $ (2\mathrm{mg / mL}) $ , which was sonicated for  $ 20\mathrm{min} $  for  $ 1\textrm{h} $  to fully disperse the nanosheets.  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheets films were deposited by applying a bias of  $ 14\mathrm{kV} $  and precursor flowrate rate was  $ 1\mathrm{mL / h} $  by maintaining a constant distance of  $ 10\mathrm{cm} $  between the needle tip and FTO glass substrate. Consequently, the vertically aligned 2D  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheets were obtained over the surface of FTO glass

substrates.

# 2.4. Spin-coating deposition of   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanosheets

For comparison, thin films of   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanosheets were also prepared by spin-coating route. A few drops of well dispersed   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanosheets in ethanol and DI water mixture were poured on the FTO glass surface and spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 10 s.

# 2.5. Solar cells fabrication

The perovskite devices with the configuration of glass/FTO/MoS $ _2 $  nanosheets (ETL)/perovskite absorber layer/HTL/Au were fabricated in the N $ _2 $ -filled glove box. For the deposition of perovskite absorber layer, 1.2 M PbI $ _2 $  solution was formed in DMF:DMSO (6:4 v/v %) at 70 °C and another 45 mg/mL MAI solution was formed in isopropyl alcohol (IPA) at ambient conditions by stirring continuously. The MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite layer was deposited via recognized two-step spin-coating method (Singh et al., 2019). In a typical two-step process, at first, the prepared solution of PbI $ _2 $  was spin-coated onto the surface of MoS $ _2 $  nanosheets (ETLs) at 3000 rpm for 20 s, and dried using hot plate at 60 °C for 5 min. After that, the MAI solution was then spin-coated over the dried PbI $ _2 $  layer for 30 s, and finally, dried at 100 °C on a hot plate for 40 min for complete formation of uniform perovskite layer. The hole transporting layer (HTL) was formed by dissolving 72.3 mg of po-spiro-MeOTAD, 28.8 μL 4-tert-butylpyridine, 17.5 μL of a stock solution (520 mg mL, 1-lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulphonyl)imide in acetonitrile) in 1.0 mL anhydrous chlorobenzene. The resultant solution was spin-coated at 4000 rpm for 30 s to form a thin layer of hole extracting material over the perovskite absorber layer. Finally, Au (80 nm) anode was deposited using high vacuum (Amod thermal evaporator) using a shadow mask to produce the perovskite devices of 0.055 cm $ ^{2} $ .

# 2.6. Characterization of  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheets and perovskite devices

The surface morphologies of   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanosheets were obtained by using a scanning electron microscope (SEM) (FE–SEM, JEOL, JSM-7600F) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM, FEI Tecnai G2 F20, 200 kV). The development of the   $  MoS_{2}  $   crystal phase was conducted via an X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis with monochromatic Kα X-rays (Model M18XHF, Macscience Instruments). The photoluminescence (PL) spectra were obtained using a FluoroMax-4 Spectrofluorometer. Time constant for recombination (  $  \tau_{R}  $  ) of photo-generated electrons were obtained with the help of transient photocurrent-voltage spectroscopy setup. Ultraviolet photoemission spectroscopy (UPS) was used to obtain the UPS spectra. The current-density vs voltage (J-V) plots were measured with the help of a Keithley digital source meter (Keithley 4200). Both the backward and forward scans were conducted with a rate of 10 mV/s.

Further, the charge extraction and charge transport behavior of devices fabricated with   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanosheet ETLs deposited both by the spin-coating and electrospray processes is studied by steady-state Photoluminescence (PL) and the intensity-modulated photovoltage spectroscopy (IMVS). This type of faster PL quenching recognized as a better charge extraction through the interface between the perovskite and   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanosheet layers, which will further improve the   $  J_{SC}  $  . Moreover, from IMVS measurement (as exhibited in Fig. S3b), longer electron lifetime is shown by devices based on electrospray deposited   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanosheet ETLs, which will further inhibit the interfacial charge recombination and consequently, produced the PSCs with higher   $  V_{OC}  $  .

The long-term stability is also a critical stuff to explore for the future progress of PSCs. Fig. 5 displays the long-term stability data of PSCs based on electrospray and spin-coated  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheet ETLs kept under ambient conditions without any encapsulation. The PSCs constructed with electrospray deposited  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheet ETLs reveal the much improved cells stability (stays above  $ 93\% $  of initial value of PCE even after storage time of  $ 700\mathrm{h} $ ), compared to perovskite cell fabricated with spin-coated  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheet ETLs. The improved stability of PSCs can be allotted to unique and better morphology of electrospray deposited  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheet in terms of their highly porous nature and growth along the vertical direction. Since, these types of nanostructures promote the proper pore filling of ETLs with perovskite light absorber suppressing the decay of perovskite. Besides, such morphology with novel design supports the rapid carrier extraction and transfer over the ETL/perovskite light absorber interface, which also obstructs the movement of electrons deteriorating inside the devices.

# 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we have deposited the highly porous and vertically aligned thin films of  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheet ETLs (for stable perovskite solar cells) directly onto the FTO glass substrates with the help of a viable and scalable electrospray method. Electrospraying method assures the potential advantages in terms of its simplicity and ease of depositing various nanostructures with fine morphologies over the larger area compared to other conventional technique such as spin-coating. The perovskite devices fabricated by using electrospray deposited  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $

nanosheet ETLs exhibit a significant high PCE of  $ 16.09\% $  compared to the devices with spin-coated  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  nanosheet ETLs (with PCE of  $ 13.79\% $ ). We believe that the better morphology, rapid charge extraction at the MoS2/perovskite absorber interface and the good electrical conductance are the key factors for the enhancement of device performance. Our findings strongly propose that a fast, simplest and scalable electrospray technique could be a good alternative methodology for the deposition of ETLs with unique morphology and characteristics that will be one of the finest rational tactics to increase the device performance.
