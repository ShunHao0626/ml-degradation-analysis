# 1. Introduction

In recent years, inorganic perovskite   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   with suitable bandgap of 1.73 eV has aroused broad interests due to its unique properties, such as low defect density, long carrier diffusion length and high compositional stability under environmental stress.  $  ^{[1-4]}  $   The severe challenge, however, encountered by   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   is that it converts spontaneously from the photoactive   $  \alpha  $  -phase (“black phase”) to the undesired   $  \gamma  $   phase (“yellow phase”) especially in ambient moist environment at room temperature. Unfortunately, the commonly-used hole transporting materials (HTMs), such as 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-dip-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD), accelerate this notorious phase transition because they usually contains some hygroscopic p-type organic additives, such as lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) and tert-butylpyridine (tBP), for enhanced conductivity. Inorganic HTMs, such as CuSCN  $  ^{[5-6]}  $  and CuI,  $  ^{[7]}  $  with advantages of better stability and low costs have been reported in hybrid PSCs. However, it is still difficult for preparing uniform thin HTM, which hinders their large-scale application in PSCs.

Colloidal lead sulfide quantum dots (PbS QDs) is one of the most sufficiently researched semiconductor QDs due to their advantages of high absorption co-efficiency, multiple exciton generation effect and tunable band gap.  $  ^{[8-9]}  $   In this work, PbS QDs were applied as a hole-transporting layer and water-repelling barrier on   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films to fabricate all-inorganic PSCs for the first time. Basic physiochemical properties of as-synthesized PbS QDs were examined. Phase-stability improvement of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   after coating PbS QDs as HTM has been verified. Hole-transporting ability of PbS QDs and devices' moisture resistance improvement were discussed. Finally, the performance and stability of devices were monitored.

# 2. Results and discussion

These PbS QDs were synthesized by a hot-injection method.  $  ^{[10]}  $   Figure 1a and 1b show the transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images of PbS QDs, demonstrating a narrow size distribution with an average diameter of 3.1 nm. The lattice fringes with a distance of 0.34 nm are coincident with the d-spacing of the (111) plane of PbS. The crystal structure of PbS QDs was characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurement (Figure 1c). The main diffraction peaks located at 25.96°, 30.07°, and 43.06° could be well-indexed to the (111), (200), and (220) crystal planes of cubic (Fm-3 m) PbS phase (JCPDS card no. 05–0592). Scanning electron microscope (SEM) was utilized to examine the morphologies and structures of the resulting films. A uniform and compact film of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   could be observed in the top-view SEM image (Figure 2a). After depositing PbS QDs, the flaws and leaks of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   grains were repaired to form a smooth and uniform film (Figure 2b), which is in favor of the reduction of the leak current. The cross-section SEM image was presented in Figure S2 (Supporting Information), from which we could find the thickness of PbS QDs is about 5 nm. The inorganic perovskite   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   is highly sensitive to moisture.  $  ^{[11]}  $   Hence, the hydrophobic HTM is highly desired. To reveal the hydrophobicity of PbS QDs, contact angle measurement was carried out to the resulting films (Figure 2c and 2d). A   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film with Spiro-OMeTAD coated on it (denoted as   $  CsPbI_{3} + Spiro-OMeTAD  $  ) was also prepared for comparison. The contact angle of 1   $  \mu  $  L water droplet on   $  CsPbI_{3} + Spiro-OMeTAD  $   sample was 80.9°. Whereas, the contact angle of a   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film with PbS QDs coated on it (denoted as   $  CsPbI_{3} + PbS QDs  $  ) sample was 105.4°, exhibiting a much strong hydrophobicity due to the PbS-QDs-surface-capped hydrophobic long hydrocarbon chains of oleic acid.

Without PbS QDs capped, the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  films showed quite low phase-stability. The black  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  films turned into pale

yellow with an absorbance peak at  $ \sim 420\mathrm{nm} $  (Figure 3a) in less than  $ 48\mathrm{h} $  even in a drying box. However, the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 / \mathrm{PbS} $  QDs films exhibited significant phase-stability improvement. After storage for  $ 30~\mathrm{d} $ , the absorbance peak located at  $ \sim 725\mathrm{nm} $ , indicating a maintained black phase. The XRD patterns of the above two samples were coincident with the UV-vis spectra. The  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  samples demonstrated a strong peak at  $ \sim 10^{\circ} $  after less than  $ 48\mathrm{h} $ , which was the characteristic peak of  $ \delta $  phase. It is worthy to note that no peak of PbS QDs was found in  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3/ $  PbS QDs samples because the quantity of PbS QDs was too small to be detected. The improvement of phase stability was mainly ascribed to the hydrophobic (oil-wet) oleic acid ligand of PbS QDs. To identify which part of the PbS QDs work to improve the phase stability of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $ , we applied oleic acid in toluene  $ (8\mathrm{mg / mL}) $  to post treat on  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  films to investigate the stability improvement. The stability improvement of oleic acid treatment is similar with those treated by PbS QDs, as shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information). The  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  films and  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  films treated by oleic acid in the toluene were aged in a dry box for  $ 30\mathrm{d} $ . We find the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  films treated by oleic acid in toluene show much improved stability and could keep the black phase for at least  $ 30\mathrm{d} $ . This is because the oleic acid is hydrophobic. In Figure 4, we figured the illustration of PbS QDs with OA ligands working as HTM and water repelling barrier. In solar cells, PbS QDs could effectively transport holes from inorganic halide perovskite layer to the Au cathode. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was carried out to investigate the interfacial charge transfer processes in the PSCs, as illustrated in Figure 6c. The equivalent circuit used to analyze the EIS spectra was figured out in the inset graph of Figure 6c. The lower frequency arc is attributed to a charge recombination resistance  $ (R_{\mathrm{rec}}) $ , in parallel with a chemical capacitance  $ (C_{\mathrm{rec}}) $ . It is worthy to note that the  $ R_{\mathrm{rec}} $  increased from  $ 314\Omega $  to  $ 1669\Omega $  when PbS QDs were capped to the inorganic halide perovskite, which means the recombination process was reduced largely and the open-circuit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{oc}}) $  were improved sequentially. Stability test was conducted to

confirm the improvement of phase-stability (Figure 6d). We stored the two devices in a drying cabinet (<20% RH) and tested them in ambient air environment. Even in the drying cabinet, the PCE of PSCs with Spiro-OMeTAD dropped quickly with obvious phase transition to "yellow" phase after storage for 144 h. Its PCE dropped quickly, coincident with the previous report of δ-CsPbI₃ solar cells.  $  ^{[12]}  $   Nonetheless, the PSCs with PbS QDs as HTM were stable and remained 89% of initial PCE after storage for 504 h with no significant phase transition. The remarkable stability improvement was due to the hydrophobic oleic acid capped on PbS QD surfaces which are regarded as a water-repelling barrier to protect CsPbI₃ from phase transition.

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, PbS QDs synthesized by the hot-injection method have been successfully used as both hole transporting material and water-repelling barrier in all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs. The   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   with PbS QDs as HTM showed large phase-stability improvement and could remain the black   $  \alpha  $  -phase for at least 30 days without any encapsulation in air. The highest-performing device based on PbS QDs HTM with planar configuration of FTO/cp-  $  TiO_{2}/CsPbI_{3}/PbS  $   QDs/Au exhibited a PCE of 7.07%, which was comparable to those based on Spiro-OMeTAD HTM. After being stored for near 500 h in a drying cabinet, they could remain 89% of initial PCE with no significant phase
