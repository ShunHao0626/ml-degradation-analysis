# Nonpolar Solvent-Dispersible Alkylated Reduced Graphene Oxide for Hole Transport Material in n-i-p Perovskite Solar Cells

Jong-Jin Park, Minwoo Lee, Yunseul Kim, and Dong-Yu Kim*

Reduced graphene oxides (rGOs), despite their excellent electrical properties, have been seldom used as the solution-processed sole hole transport material (HTM) in perovskite solar cells (PeSCs) with an n-i-p structure, due to their low dispersity in orthogonal solvent of perovskite, such as chlorobenzene (CB). The limited rGO dispersion in nonpolar solvents precludes the formation of a fully covered film on top of the perovskite layer. A newly designed alkylated rGO (AF-rGO) is synthesized, which can be highly dispersed in a nonpolar solvent, for use as a solution-processed HTM on top of the perovskite layer in PeSCs. The AF-rGO can be dispersed in CB at a high concentration, and a fully covered AF-rGO film is successfully introduced on top of the perovskite layer as HTM through a simple solution process. The AF-rGO in the PeSC exhibits minimal series resistance and charge recombination and exhibits efficient hole extraction and transport abilities, resulting in a maximum power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 17%, which is the highest PCE of PeSCs with an n-i-p structure, which use graphene-based materials as the sole HTM. Moreover, devices with AF-rGO HTM exhibit improved ambient stability, exhibiting 89% of their original performance after 240 h of storage without encapsulation.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PeSCs) have attracted tremendous attention as a promising photovoltaic technology due to their outstanding properties, such as their low cost, straightforward synthesis, high absorption coefficient, tunable bandgap, and long charge-carrier diffusion length.  $  ^{[1-4]}  $   The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PeSCs has dramatically increased from 3.8% to over 25.5% in a very short period.  $  ^{[5,6]}  $   Hole transport materials (HTMs) have played a significant role in this progress, yielding high-performance devices by efficiently extracting and transporting photogenerated holes.  $  ^{[7,8]}  $

To date, most of the high-efficiency PeSCs with a conventional structure (n-i-p) have used small molecules or conjugated polymers as HTMs, such as 2,2,7,7-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobifuorene (spiro-OMeTAD) and poly-[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl) amine] (PTAA).  $  ^{[9-11]}  $   However, these materials have limited commercial potential due to the high costs of their complicated synthetic steps and purification processes. Moreover, these HTMs require an additional doping process with hygroscopic additives to improve their charge mobility and Fermi level alignment. This additional doping process causes stability issues and lowers the PeSC device reproducibility along slow and environment-dependent oxidation degrees.  $  ^{[4,12]}  $   Therefore, low-cost and dopant-free HTMs that can replace spiro-OMeTAD or PTAA-based HTMs are required for the successful commercialization of PeSCs.  $  ^{[13-15]}  $

However, typical rGOs exhibit limited dispersibility in nonpolar solvents due to the oxygen-containing functional groups on its surface and edges. The limited rGO dispersibility in nonpolar solvents precludes the formation of thick films that fully cover the perovskite layer of PeSCs via a solution process. To date, using rGO derivatives as a solution-processed HTM in the n-i-p structure of PeSCs requires either an additional HTM layer such as spiro-OMeTAD or combining them with another type of HTMs.  $  ^{[26-29]}  $   Therefore, it is necessary to develop an rGO that can be dispersed in a nonpolar solvent at a high concentration to successfully introduce rGO as the single layer HTM via a solution process in an n-i-p-structured PeSC without damaging the underlying perovskite layer.

In this work, we synthesized newly designed alkylated rGO using dodecyl amine (AF-rGO) with controlled polarity, which can be highly dispersed in a nonpolar solvent for use as a dopant-free and solution-processed sole HTM on top of the perovskite layer. The synthesized AF-rGO was sufficiently dispersed in CB at a high concentration (  $  \approx3 mg mL^{-1}  $  ), and the fully covered thick AF-rGO film was successfully formed on top of the perovskite layer as the sole hole transport layer (HTL) in the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -based PeSC with an n-i-p structure via a straightforward solution process without any additives. The AF-rGO HTM in the PeSCs exhibited minimal series resistance and charge recombination properties, and it demonstrated efficient hole extraction and transport abilities, resulting in a maximum PCE of 17%, which is the highest PCE of PeSCs with an n-i-p structure, which use graphene-based materials as the sole HTM. Moreover, devices with AF-rGO HTL exhibited improved ambient stability (30% humidity, room temperature), exhibiting 89% of their original performance after 240 h of storage without encapsulation.

# 2. Results and Discussion

To create highly concentrated nonpolar solvent-dispersible rGO, AF-rGO was prepared from GO through a two-step synthetic scheme, as shown in Scheme 1. In the first step, dodecylamine (DA) was introduced onto the GO surface (A-GO) to improve the dispersibility of the GO in nonpolar solvents. In the second step, A-GO was reduced to restore the electrical conductivity using 4-(trifluoromethyl)phenylhydrazine (TFPH) and to create the final product AF-rGO. The detailed synthetic procedure is described in the Experimental Section.

The surface chemical compositions of GO, A-GO, and AF-rGO were investigated using X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) to confirm the DA functionalization and GO reduction. Figure 1 displays the C 1s spectra for GO, A-GO, and AF-rGO. A high proportion of oxygen-containing functional groups was observed in the GO XPS spectrum. To confirm the charge extraction efficiency at the interfaces between the perovskite and HTMs (doped spiro-OMeTAD and AF-rGO), the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL (TRPL) were measured using an excitation wavelength of 375 nm and by depositing the HTMs onto the perovskite film. As shown in Figure 5a, the perovskite displayed a strong emission peak at   $  \approx790  $   nm. This emission peak was quenched by the deposition of doped spiro-OMeTAD and AF-rGO HTMs, indicating that the photogenerated holes in the excited perovskite layer were efficiently extracted by both HTMs. This result was further validated by TRPL analysis, as

shown in Figure 5b. The TRPL curves were fitted with a biexponential decay function using the Equation  $  I(t) = A1 \exp(t/\tau 1) + A2 \exp(t/\tau 2)  $ . There were two decay lifetimes: 1) the fast decay of  $  \tau 1  $ , which can be attributed to the carrier extraction from the perovskite to the HTM, and 2) the slow decay of  $  \tau 2  $ , which corresponds to the delayed recombination of the trapped charges in the bulk perovskite. $ ^{[22]} $  The detailed fitting parameters are summarized in Table S2, Supporting Information. The AF-rGO sample exhibited a  $  \tau 1  $  of 4.5 ns, a  $  \tau 2  $  of 18.2 ns, and an average  $  \tau  $  of 11.3 ns. Both the values of  $  \tau 1  $  and  $  \tau 2  $  of the AF-rGO sample were similar to those of the spiro-OMeTAD sample, which exhibited a  $  \tau 1  $  of 4.7 ns, a  $  \tau 2  $  of 18.8 ns, and an average  $  \tau  $  of 11.7 ns. To demonstrate the critical advantages of AF-rGO HTMs, a stability test was performed under ambient conditions of 30% relative humidity, room temperature, and no encapsulation. As shown in Figure 5d, the PeSCs with AF-rGO exhibited improved ambient stability, maintaining over 89% of their initial PCEs after storage for 240 h. In contrast, the spiro-OMeTAD-based PeSCs exhibited poor ambient stability. The inset photographs in Figure 5c display the PeSCs with different HTMs after storage for 240 h. As shown in these photographs, while the decomposition of the perovskite in the PeSC with spiro-OMeTAD was observed, the PeSC with AF-rGO did not exhibit apparent perovskite decomposition. The perovskite decomposition in the spiro-OMeTAD-based PeSC can be attributed to lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP), which are spiro-OMeTAD dopants.  $  ^{[43]}  $   Deliquescent and hygroscopic Li-TFSI and TBP attract moisture and can accelerate the decomposition of perovskite, resulting in reduced device stability. In contrast, the improved AF-rGO-based PeSC stability may result from nonuse of doping additives, along with the fully covered thick AF-rGO films and their higher

hydrophobicity.  $  ^{[44]}  $   To confirm the hydrophobic properties of the spiro-OMeTAD and AF-rGO films, the contact angles (CAs) of water droplets were measured on top of each film. The water droplet CAs were 62° and 79° for the spiro-OMeTAD and AF-rGO films, respectively (Figure S4, Supporting Information). This result indicates that the AF-rGO film exhibits higher hydrophobicity (lower water wettability) than that of the spiro-OMeTAD film, which can be ascribed to the nonpolar dodecyl alkyl chains and fluorine atoms  $  ^{[22]}  $   on the AF-rGO surface. Thus, the higher hydrophobicity of the dopant-free AF-rGO can effectively prevent moisture penetration into the perovskite layer, leading to improved device stability.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we successfully synthesized AF-rGO that can be dispersed in high concentrations of CB. The AF-rGO dispersion in CB was orthogonally coated onto the perovskite layer as the HTM via a solution process to form a fully covering thick film without any additives. The AF-rGO-based PeSC exhibited a maximum PCE of 17%, which is attributed to the efficient hole extraction and transport abilities and reduced recombination properties of the HTM. This PCE is comparable with that of the reference cell, which was based on the doped spiro-OMeTAD HTM, and the highest PCE of n-i-p PeSCs currently using graphene-based HTMs. More importantly, our device with the AF-rGO HTM demonstrates improved ambient stability compared with that of the doped spiro-OMeTAD-based PeSC, with

# 4. Experimental Section

Synthesis of AF-rGO: The AF-rGO was prepared using a two-step synthetic process. To prepare the A-GO, DA (Sigma-Aldrich) in ethanol (40 mL, 0.1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) was added to aqueously dispersed GO (20 mL, 0.2 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , Sigma-Aldrich). The mixture was then stirred at room temperature for 24 h. The resulting mixture was centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min, and the precipitate was washed with tetrahydrofuran (THF) to remove any remaining DA. To prepare the DA-rGO, TFPH (0.1 g) was added to the A-GO dispersed in a solvent mixture of ethanol and DI water (1:1 v/v ratio), and then, the mixture was stirred at 90 °C for 24 h. After the reduction was completed, the resultant mixture was centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min several times and then washed with THF to remove the remaining reductant. The washed AF-rGO was dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 6 h. Finally, the synthesized AF-rGO was dispersed in CB (3 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , Sigma-Aldrich) for use in the n-i-p PeSC HTM.

Fabrication of Planar PeSCs: The PeSCs were fabricated with a conventional structure (n-i-p) of ITO/SnO₂/MAPbI₃/HTM/Ag. To clean the PeSCs, the glass/ITO substrates (10 Ω sq⁻¹, AMG Technologies) were successively ultrasonicated with DI water, acetone, and isopropyl alcohol for 15 min. The cleaned substrates were then treated with oxygen plasma for 30 min after drying in an oven at 100 °C for 10 min. As the ETM, SnO₂ (TCI) diluted in DI water was spin-coated onto the substrate at 5000 rpm for 30 s, followed by thermal annealing at 100 °C for 10 min in the air. A 40 wt% perovskite precursor solution was prepared by mixing methylammonium iodide (GreatCell Solar Ltd.) with PbI₂ (TCI) at a molar ratio of 1:1.05 in anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide (Sigma-Aldrich). The perovskite precursor solution was deposited onto the SnO₂ layer at 5000 rpm for 30 s with a 3 s acceleration, and then, thermal annealing occurred at 125 °C for 5 min. As the HTM, AF-rGO dispersion (200 μL, 3 mg mL⁻¹ in CB) was coated onto the prepared perovskite layer using a dynamic spin-coating method at 3000 rpm.[16] As a reference HTM, spiro-OMeTAD with dopants (Li-TFSi and 4-t-butylpridine) was deposited onto the perovskite layer at 5000 rpm for 30 s and then oxidized in the air for 24 h. Finally, a 150 nm-thick Ag electrode was deposited using a thermal evaporator at a deposition rate of 1 Å s⁻¹ under ≈10⁻⁶ Torr.

Characterization and Measurements: The current density–voltage characteristics of the PeSCs were measured using a Keithley 2400 instrument/McScience solar simulator under   $  100 \, mW cm^{-2}  $   illumination (AM 1.5G). A calibrated silicon reference solar cell that was certified by the National Renewable Energy Laboratory was used to confirm the measurement conditions. The EQE was measured using a quantum efficiency measurement system (QuantX 300, Oriel). Cross-sectional images of the fabricated PeSCs and the layer thicknesses were obtained using SEM (S4700, Hitachi). XPS and UPS (AXIS-NOVA, Kratos) were performed using a monochromatized Al-Kα (1486.6 eV) and He I (21.2 eV) excitation source with an energy resolution of   $  100 \, meV  $   under   $  5 \times 10^{-8}  $   Torr, respectively.
