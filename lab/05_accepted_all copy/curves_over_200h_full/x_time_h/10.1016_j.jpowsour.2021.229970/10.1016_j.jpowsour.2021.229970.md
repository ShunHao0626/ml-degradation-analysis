# ABSTRACT

Low-cost and stable carbon-based hole-conductor layers have attained especial interest in perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Because the carbon-based hole-conductor-free PSCs usually have the serious problems of low power conversion efficiency (PCE) and obvious current hysteresis. Herein, we introduced CuSCN hole transfer layers (HTLs) with low pressure treatment (LPT) into carbon-based PSCs to improve the performance and reduce the hysteresis. After being coated, the CuSCN layers were immediately transferred to low-pressure environment for vaporizing the polar diethyl sulfide (DES) without destruction of perovskite films. Our champion device engineered through carbon-based PSCs with low-pressure-treated CuSCN HTLs achieved power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 13.05%, compared with 10.6% of that without CuSCN layer. Importantly, the incorporating CuSCN HTLs can build energy level cascade to obviously reduce the current hysteresis due to the balanced electrode-hole extraction ability. Moreover, the stability of the device was obviously improved due to the hydrophobic nature of CuSCN layer. While the low-temperature (  $  \sim  $  20 °C and 50 °C) volatilizing DES in CuSCN layer destroyed the perovskite films under room pressure, which reduced the PCE to 8.6% and 9.9%, respectively. Thereby, this work highlights the advantages of incorporating low-pressure-treated CuSCN HTLs into carbon-based PSCs with improving performance and reducing current hysteresis, which will promote the future industrial application.

Among which, the most common and successful device architecture can be represented as electron-transport-layer (ETL)/perovskite/hole-transport-layer (HTL)/top electrode. However, organic HTLs (spiro-OMeTAD) and noble metal electrodes (Au or Ag) are unstable and expensive, respectively [13-16]. More importantly, the spiro-OMeTAD HTL needs moisture-sensitive Li-TFSI and tert-butylpyridine dopants, which are helpful to improve conductivity, rather sensitive to external environment. In addition, the usual thermal evaporated deposition of noble metal electrodes is highly complicated, which contains energy consumptive process [17,18].

Similarly, carbon contacts appeared as an ideal candidate electrode materials for PSCs due to low cost advantages, suitable work function to noble metal (  $  \sim5  $   eV), excellent chemical, physical and thermal stability, and water-resistant property [20]. However, the PCE is still lower than those of spiro-OMeTAD/Au based structure devices [21,22]. Moreover, carbon-based PSCs without HTLs exhibit serious hysteresis under forward and reverse scanning direction, which mainly results from the unbalanced charge extraction ability at both perovskite/ETL and perovskite/carbon interfaces. Furthermore, the mismatched energy offset between perovskite valence band maximum and carbon Fermi level reduces effective hole extraction, which is primary origin for hysteresis, eventually limits performance of devices [23]. Based on above train of thought, inserting HTL with matched energy level to both perovskite valence band and the carbon Fermi level can enhance the PCE and reduce the hysteresis phenomenon of PSCs [24,25].

Copper(I) thiocyanate (CuSCN) is regarded as versatile p-type HTL material used in various photovoltaic fields due to remarkable light transmission, high chemical stability, and high hole mobility of   $  10^{-2}-10^{-1}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $   [26–28]. In 2014, it was first reported that CuSCN was introduced into PSCs with PCE of 6.4%. Since then, great efforts have been on various methods of depositing CuSCN layer for enhanced PSCs [29]. Typically, the CuSCN powder is dissolved in polar diethyl sulfide (DES), which is in-situ dynamically spin-coated on perovskite films [30]. As for Au electrode to avoid the possible chemical reaction with CuSCN layer, graphene oxide (rGO) was adopted between them for high-performance PSCs. For carbon-based PSCs, the inserted CuSCN HTL eliminated the hysteresis, but the device performance declined. It should be noted that DES is polar solvent, which may degrade the perovskite films during drying CuSCN process at low temperature [26,27,54]. To overcome these problems, an interfacial layer has been adopted, such as r-GO layer [31], NiOx nanoparticle layer [29], PEDOT:PSS [32] and PDMS [26]. However, the fabrication process is too tedious. Therefore, there is dire need to develop simple method, which can protect perovskite layer from the polar solvent DES.

In this scenario, low-pressure treatment (LPT) could be an effective approach to overcome the associated problems in CuSCN HTL. After being dynamically spin-coated on perovskite film, the CuSCN layer was quickly transferred into low-pressure box to vaporize the DES solution. It is interesting that the LPT can effectively remove the DES and enhance the quality of the perovskite films. In addition, it was confirmed that the low-temperature heating to remove DES could destroy the perovskite films under room pressure, even at room temperature. Thus, only the LPT of CuSCN HTL can enhance the PCE of the PSCs to 13.05% from 10.6% of that without CuSCN. While, the heating-treatment at 50 °C and at room temperature for drying CuSCN HTL would reduce the PCE of PSCs to 8.9% and 9.7%, respectively. Notably, after incorporating CuSCN layer the hysteresis phenomenon of the devices was reduced obviously due to the improvement of energy alignment and hole extraction between perovskite and carbon electrodes [33–35]. Meanwhile, the stability of the PSCs was obviously improved due to the hydrophobicity of the CuSCN HTL with LPT.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

Methylammonium iodide (MAI, 99.99%) and lead iodide (PbI  $  _{2}  $  , 99.99%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. CuSCN (99%) was purchased from Macklin. N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%), ethanol (99.5%), isopropanol (99.5%) and chlorobenzene (99.8%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.9%) was purchased from Sinopharm Group. Titanium(IV) isopropoxide (99.999%) and DES (98%) were purchased from Aldrich. TiO  $  _{2}  $   paste (18-NRT) was purchased from Deysol. F-doped SnO  $  _{2}  $  -coated (FTO) glass substrates (7   $  \Omega  $   cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , transmittance >85%) were purchased from Nippin Sheet Glass Co., Ltd.

# 2.2. Preparation of perovskite solar cells

Firstly, FTO glass substrates were etched using zinc powder and 2 M HCl solution for the designed pattern. Then, the patterned FTO substrates were ultrasonically cleaned with deionized water, acetone and ethanol for 15 min each. After being dried, the substrates were treated with UV-Ozone for 15 min to form the wettability surface. A   $  TiO_{2}  $   precursor was spin-coated on the clean patterned FTO substrates at 3000 rpm for 30 s, and annealed at 125 °C to obtain the compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   blocking layers. Here, the above precursor was composed of 370 μL titanium (IV) isopropoxide, 5.06 ml isopropanol, and 35 μl HCl solution (2 M). For   $  TiO_{2}  $   mesoscopic layer, the   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste was first diluted with ethanol (mass ratio of 1:7), then spin-coated on the compact layer at 5000 rpm for 30 s, and subsequently annealed at 125 °C for 10 min and 500 °C for 30 min in a muffle furnace.

The   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers were treated with UV-Ozone for 15 min, and immediately transferred into a   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glove-box. The perovskite precursor was prepared by dissolving 480 mg   $  PbI_{2}  $   and 159 mg MAI into a mixture solution of 640 ml DMF and 160 ml DMSO, which was spin-coated on   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers at 1000 rpm for 3 s and 4000 rpm for 20 s. During the second step at 10 s, 300   $  \mu  $  L chlorobenzene was dropped onto the spinning substrates to extract the solution. After being annealed on a hotplate at 75 °C and 105 °C for 10 min each, mirror-like black perovskite films were successfully achieved.

CuSCN solution was made up by dissolving 35 mg CuSCN powder into 1 ml DES under stirring for over 1 h. CuSCN solution (35   $  \mu  $  L) was dropped and covered on the perovskite films by dynamically spinning at 5000 rpm for 35 s. To evaporate the DES, the CuSCN films were treated by different methods: heating at 50 °C for 30 min (abbreviated to HT (50)-CuSCN), room-temperature resting for over night (abbreviated to RT-CuSCN), low-pressure treating at   $  10^{2}  $   Pa for 1 h (abbreviated to LPT-CuSCN). Finally, carbon paste was screen-printed on the films with or without CuSCN layers, and annealed at 105 °C for 20 min for the top electrodes.

# 2.3. Measurements and characterization

X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were characterized by Bruker D8 Advance X-ray diffractometer with Cu-Kα radiation (  $  \lambda = 1.5418 \AA  $  ). The morphology of the samples was observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, FEI NOVA NanoSEM 450). Absorption spectra were measured by UV–vis (PerkinElmer Lambda 650 S) spectrophotometer in the range from 400 to 800 nm. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy was measured by analyzer (AXIS Supra) with a monochromatic Al Kα X-ray source. The photoluminescence (PL) spectra were measured by an Edinburgh Instruments spectrometer (FLS980), attaching with a pulsed laser (less than 100 ps, 1 MHz, Wuhan Yangtze Soton Laser Co., Ltd, China) as excitation source with fixed wavelength of 450 nm. The samples were excited from the FTO side while as to study the photoluminescence decay kinetics in pristine and HTM containing samples. # 3. Results and discussion

The configuration of the carbon-based PSC with CuSCN HTL is shown in Fig. 1a. The   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact and mesoporous layers (marked as “c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ”, “m-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ”) were successively spin-coated on the commercial FTO substrate as the hole-blocking and electron-transport layers, respectively. Then the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) pervoskite layer as light harvester was deposited by one-step spin-coating method with antisolvent strategy. Next, the CuSCN layer was deposited by the dynamically spin-coating with the followed LPT to protect the pervoskite layer and ensure the effective hole extraction and collection at the carbon electrode. The energy level diagram of each layer in the device is plotted in Fig. 1b [36,37]. It should be noted that the energy offset is large between the valence band maximum of the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite layer and the Fermi level of the carbon electrode, which could impact the hole transfer behavior, leading to the imbalanced charge extraction and anomalous hysteresis phenomenon. The valence band maximum of CuSCN layer is between that of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   layer and the Fermi level of the carbon electrode, which can create an energy cascade for efficient hole extraction and collection.

However, the DES in CuSCN solution may degrade perovskite layer during depositing CuSCN film process. Thus the DES must be quickly evaporated without annealing, because the annealing may accelerate degradation of the perovskite films. LPT is an effective method to quickly vaporize the DES for protecting perovskite layer as shown in Fig. 1c. After the CuSCN solution was dynamically spin-coated on perovskite film, the substrate covered with CuSCN/DES films was

immediately transferred to the low-pressure environment. At low pressure, the boiling point of DES will reduce, and DES would evaporate at room temperature quickly, which achieves the purpose of avoiding destruction of perovskite films. In addition, the LPT may play a positive role for recrystallization of MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite films, which is similar as high-quality Cs $ _2 $ AgBiBr $ _6 $  film fabricated by low-pressure assisted solution processing [38].

Fig. 2a and b illustrates the top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film and the coated CuSCN film, respectively. In the perovskite film, the crystal grains with size of  $ 100 - 500\mathrm{nm} $  are closely connected without cracks, which achieves full broadband coverage of the underlying  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  mesoscopic layer. The size of grains and cracks of perovskite films are directly related to the efficiency of the device. The closely-connected large grains imply low defect density, which is beneficial for light absorption and carrier transport of the devices. Fig. 2b shows a dense CuSCN layer with LPT, which completely covers on the perovskite film. However, the surface of the film is fuzzy, mainly due to the low conductivity of CuSCN layer. In our future study, we will improve its conductivity.

Fig. 2c shows the cross section of the device with “Glass/FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $ /m-TiO $ _2 $ /MAPbI $ _3 $ /CuSCN/Carbon” configuration, where part of perovskite layer incorporates into the m-TiO $ _2 $  layer. The thickness of perovskite layer is approximate 500 nm, and the grains can cross the whole layer, which represents excellent crystallization and kindness for charge transfer. The thickness of CuSCN layer is about 50 nm on the MAPbI $ _3 $  layer, but there is no clear dividing line between them, indicating the surface of the perovskite layer went through a dissolved and recrystallized process due to the polar solvent DES. The top layer is carbon electrode with thickness of 15  $ \mu $ m. In addition, the water and oxygen may infiltrate the pores of the carbon electrode, but the dense CuSCN HTL can prevent them to decompose the perovskite layer [43].

After intercalating LPT CuSCN film, the hysteresis index (HI) of the devices is obviously decreased from 0.299 to 0.053 (Fig. S2). Usually, the  $ J - V $  hysteresis is induced by charge collection and transport related to charged defects and interfacial charge accumulation [51,52]. As demonstrated above, the LPT CuSCN-based device exhibits well-aligned energy level, which leads to the charge

transfer more efficient between interfaces. Moreover, the quality of perovskite films were increased by LPT, which leads to reduce the trap states. Thus, the HI was obviously reduced in the carbon-based PSCs with LPT CuSCN. The incident photon-to-current efficiency (IPCE) curves for the champion devices with and without LPT CuSCN in Fig. 6b demonstrate a response of wavelength ranging from 300 to 800 nm, which is in accordance with the absorption of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  thin film. The corresponding integrated J  $  _{SC}  $  values from the IPCE spectra are 18.79 and 21.01 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  for the PSCs with and without LPT CuSCN, respectively, which are close to those extracted from the J-V characteristics (Fig. 2a). From Fig. 6b, the improved IPCE happened in the whole region from 300 to 800 nm. As literature reported, the enhanced IPCE at the wavelength of 500–800 nm could be attributed to the well-aligned energy level and improved pervoskite quality. According to the observations, it can be concluded that the enhancement in photovoltaic parameters may be ascribed to the optimization of pervoskite crystallization and well-aligned energy level by incorporating LPT-CuSCN.

In addition, we examined the stability of the PSCs in air atmosphere at 15–25 °C and relative humidity of 40–65%. The normalized PCE (Fig. 6c) raised to a relatively stable state in initial days, which may ascribe to the enhanced interfacial interaction between carbon and the underlying layers [55]. After aging for 400 h, the periodically recorded PCE of devices with LPT-CuSCN can maintain more than 96% of its original value, while the CuSCN-free devices can only remain 86% of the original value. Importantly, the hydrophobic CuSCN layer is dense as seen from the SEM image, which can prevent water to infringe the perovskite films. Moreover, the enhanced perovskite films with high quality have low trap state density, which can delay the decomposition rate of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  .

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, the low-pressure treated CuSCN HTLs were successfully introduced into carbon-based PSCs to improve the performance and reduce current hysteresis. The LPT approach can not only vaporize the polar diethyl sulfide from CuSCN HTL but also enhanced the quality of perovskite films. Importantly, the incorporated CuSCN HTLs would build energy level cascade and improve hole transfer. Consequently, the carbon-based PSCs with LPT CuSCN HTLs achieved PCE of 13.05%, comparatively 23% higher than that without CuSCN layer. More importantly, the hysteresis was tremendously ameliorated after CuSCN incorporating due to the balanced electrode-hole extraction ability. While the low-temperature (50 °C) and room-temperature drying of CuSCN HTLs would destroy the perovskite films, which reduced the PCE of PSCs to 8.6% and 9.9%, respectively. Therefore, this work highlights the advantages of low-pressure-treated CuSCN layers as hole conductors in carbon-based PSCs to simultaneously improve performance and reduce hysteresis, which will promote the future industrial application.
