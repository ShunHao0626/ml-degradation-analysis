# ABSTRACT

Employing all-inorganic perovskite quantum dots (QDs) to treat the organic-inorganic perovskite film has well-documented as a serviceable tactics to improve the performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Whereas the inert molecules-coated QDs with zero dimension (0D) structure would limit further enhancement of the efficiency and stability of PSCs. Here we employ a conductive diammonium porphyrin (ZnPy-NH $ _3 $ Br) to treat CsPbBr $ _3 $  QDs coated on 3D perovskite film, thus constructing a stable 0D-2D perovskite capping layer. The generation of large-scale nanocube crystals by treating CsPbBr $ _3 $  nanocrystallites with ZnPy-NH $ _3 $ Br in solution demonstrates such an assembly strategy. The formed capping layer can achieve efficient charge transport and separation. As a consequence, the best efficiency of optimized device is up to 20.0%, which is superior to the control PSCs fabricated without modification (19.1%) and with pure CsPbBr $ _3 $  QDs modification (19.5%). More importantly, the porphyrin-treated CsPbBr $ _3 $  QDs based devices retain over 65% or 85% of their initial efficiency when placed at 85 °C or 45% humidity tracking for 1000 h, respectively. And also, with the QDs-Por incorporated, the device retained 85% of the original efficiency when illuminated at the AM 1.5 G for 450 h. Therefore, this work offered a facile avenue to modify perovskite film for fabricating the highly efficient and stable PSCs.

KEYWORDS:  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  quantum dots; Porphyrins; Ligand exchange; Protective layer; Perovskite solar cells

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Hybrid halide perovskite materials have catalyzed an exciting revolution in the photovoltaic fields by giving the continual breakthrough in photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{1-7}  $   The achieved certified efficiency of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) already reached to 25.2%.  $  ^{8}  $   To realize the commercialization of PSCs, the notoriously poor operational stability of PSCs should be further addressed. Especially, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite suffers from thermal degradation due to the evaporation of organic cation and the easy migration of iodide ions in perovskite lattice. In addition, the inferior interface contact can cause charge recombination and ions migration,  $  ^{9-11}  $   thus resulting in the destruction of PSCs. Several approaches, such as introducing functional molecules, quantum dots (QDs) and insulating buffer layers  $  ^{12-19}  $   between perovskite and hole transport layer, had been tried to address these issues.  $  ^{20}  $

Among all these strategies, all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   QDs with excellent thermal-stability and near-ideal energy level alignment  $  ^{21-24}  $   had been successfully utilized as functional components in PSCs. The introduction of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   QDs into chlorobenzene anti-solvent could prepare highly crystalline perovskite film, thus retarding charge recombination and improving charge transfer.  $  ^{21}  $   Moreover, the all-inorganic perovskite nanocrystals were used as the inter-layer between the absorber and hole transfer layer in PSCs to achieve better energy level matching.  $  ^{25}  $   The surface ligand of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   QDs is also play an important function in defect passivation of PSCs.  $  ^{26}  $   More importantly,   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   based PSCs possess superior

Porphyrin derivatives as a natural chromophore are usually emerged in photo-induced electron and energy transfer systems owing to their excellent photoelectric characteristics.  $  ^{32-34}  $   In this work, we employ a conductive diammonium porphyrin (ZnPy-NH  $  _{3}  $  Br) to treat the CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $   QDs coated on CsMAFA-based (MA = CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}^{+}  $  , FA = NH  $  _{2}  $  CHNH  $  _{2}^{+}  $  ) perovskite film, and thus a stable 0D-2D perovskite capping layer based on porphyrin-bridging CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $   QDs is constructed. Such a constructing strategy is also verified by the generation of large-scale nanocube crystals by treating CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $   nanocrystallites with ZnPy-NH  $  _{3}  $  Br in solution. # 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The pristine   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   nanocrystallites protected by oleylamine and oleic acid (OA) ligands were firstly prepared.  $  ^{[37]}  $   Since removing the protonated oleylamine in   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   nanocrystallite could facilitate the coordination of Br  $  ^{-}  $   from the   $  ZnPy-NH_{3}Br  $   with the positively charged metal center of QDs surface, OA was used to treat the obtained sample to prepare the OA capped   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   QDs.  $  ^{[37]}  $   The transmission electron microscope (TEM) observed the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   QDs being uniform nanocubes with an average size of 10.9 nm (of >200 particles, Figures S1 and S2). As-prepared   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   QDs were dispersed in cyclohexane, forming colloidal suspension for use in the treatment of perovskite film. The detailed treated method was illustrated in Figure 1a. Firstly, we coated   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   QDs on the 3D CsMAFA-based perovskite film (QDs-Modified film), then treating the as-prepared film by the   $  ZnPy-NH_{3}Br  $   to yield the desired film (QDs-Por-Modified film). To understand the effects of introduction of   $  ZnPy-NH_{3}Br  $ , the scanning electron microscope (SEM) tests were prepared. There was the formation of new layer after modifying with   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   QDs and   $  ZnPy-NH_{3}Br  $   (seen in Figure 1b-1d). The atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements further revealed the generation of rough morphology after modifying with QDs compared with pure perovskite film, whereas the film became smooth when treated by   $  ZnPy-NH_{3}Br  $   (Figure S3). The formed structure was further confirmed by X-ray diffraction (XRD) spectral (Figure 1e and Figure S4). The peaks at 15.2° and 30.5° were assigned to the 0D   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   QDs peak, and the 2D perovskite peaks  $  ^{[38]}  $   (7.4°, 9.8°, and 11.6°) were also observed in the QDs-Por-modified film. To further check the formation of 0D-2D  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  QDs-Por structure, we used  $ \mathrm{ZnPy - NH_3Br} $  to treat the as-prepared  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  QDs in dichloromethane solution (Figure 2a). This process was prepared under solution to demonstrate the presence of ligand exchange progress, which is different in the device progress. As displayed in Figure 2b-2c, firstly, the  $ \mathrm{ZnPy - NH_3Br} $  molecules are adhered on the surface of QDs via the coordination connection between the Br anions from the  $ \mathrm{ZnPy - NH_3Br} $  and the metal ions in QDs. With the increase of treated time, the  $ \mathrm{ZnPy - NH_3^+} $  as bridge units bind two different QDs together, and further yielding the large 0D-2D nanocrystals by the typical Ostwald ripening process. Finally, the uniform deposition of  $ \mathrm{ZnPy - NH_3Br} $  within the cube sample (Figures 2d-h and S7) was obtained, demonstrating that the  $ \mathrm{ZnPy - NH_3Br} $  can induce  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  QDs to yield 0D-2D perovskite capping layer based on porphyrin-bridging  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  QDs.

# 4. CONCLUSION

In summary, we used a diammonium porphyrin to treat   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   nanocrystals, and thus in-situ generating a 0D-2D covering layer on the top of 3D perovskite for PSCs. The generation of large-scale nanocube crystals by treating   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   nanocrystallites with   $  ZnPy-NH_{3}Br  $   in solution further demonstrated such a assemble strategy. The 0D-2D layer can efficiently inhibit charge recombination and accelerate charge transfer. Consequently, enhanced performance was obtained for the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   QDs-Por modified PSCs. The optimized QDs-Por modified cell possessed a PCE of 20.0% while the device based on pure perovskite is 19.1% or   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   modified is 19.5%.
