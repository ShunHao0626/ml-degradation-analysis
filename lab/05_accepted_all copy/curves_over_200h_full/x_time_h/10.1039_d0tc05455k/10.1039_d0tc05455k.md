# Introduction

In the past decade, organic-inorganic hybrid halide perovskites have aroused unprecedented attention as emerging photovoltaic materials due to their excellent photoelectric properties.  $  ^{1-4}  $   Furthermore the highest certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of conventional organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has skyrocketed from 3.8% to 25.5%,  $  ^{5,6}  $   showing great application potential in the field of photovoltaics. However, due to the presence of volatile organic components such as methylammonium (MA) and formamidinium (FA), traditional organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs always suffer from poor moisture and thermal stability,  $  ^{7-9}  $   which severely limits their commercial utility. The all-inorganic   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite solar cell (PSC) has received tremendous attention due to its favorable photoelectric properties and superior stability against moisture and heat attack. Nevertheless, the reported power conversion efficiency (PCE) of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSC is still far from its counterparts, mainly because of the inferior film crystallization and morphology of the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   light-absorbing layer, along with much trap defects and severe charge recombination. This study provides a feasible strategy for preparing a high quality perovskite film and improving the photovoltaic performance and long-term stability of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs, aiming at promoting the potential applications of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $  -based PSCs.

This dilemma mainly ascribes to the inferior quality of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films, in which lots of grain boundaries and compositional defects are inevitable from being produced during the typical solution process. It is acknowledged that the numerous grain boundaries and pinholes will impede the charge carrier transport and cause undesirable charge recombination, further lowering the whole device performance.

In order to fabricate high-quality   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite films, various strategies have been implemented such as interface modification,  $  ^{24-27}  $   ion substitution, intermolecular exchange,  $  ^{28,29}  $   and optimizing the substrate-preheating temperature.  $  ^{29}  $   In comparison, ion substitution is a simple but effective way to fabricate high-quality   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite films for controlling crystallization, releasing lattice strain, and passivating defects. Tan et al. used a smaller positive monovalent alkali metal ion   $  Li^{+}  $  to substitute the Cs-site to improve the crystallinity and morphology, and obtained a PCE of 9.25% of the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSC.  $  ^{30}  $   The substitution of the Pb-site has also been investigated by several research groups. For instance,   $  Mn^{2+}  $   and   $  Cu^{2+}  $   doped into   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   have been certified to reduce defect density and charge recombination of the perovskite films in comparison with their undoped counterparts, thus improving the PCE and long-term stability of PSCs.  $  ^{23,31}  $   Liang et al. fabricated the   $  CsPb_{0.9}Sn_{0.1}IBr_{2}  $   films via a two-step sequential deposition and the device achieved a high PCE of 11.33% with an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.26 V.  $  ^{32}  $   Subsequently, Li et al. further increased the content of   $  Sn^{2+}  $ , and the corresponding   $  CsPb_{0.75}Sn_{0.25}IBr_{2}  $   was prepared using a general one-step antisolvent method, and obtained a superior PSC performance. However,   $  Sn^{2+}  $   is easily oxidized to   $  Sn^{4+}  $   under air conditions, which is a huge ordeal for the long-term stability of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   doped devices. Therefore, it is necessary to explore a facile and effective approach for the preparation of large-grained   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   films with few crystal boundaries and trap defects, aiming to achieve high performance   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $ -based PSCs with satisfactory efficiency and stability simultaneously.

Herein, we incorporated   $  ZnBr_{2}  $   into a   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite precursor and prepared the   $  CsPb_{1-x}Zn_{x}IBr_{2}  $   perovskite films using a facile one-step spin-coating method.   $  Zn^{2+}  $   doping could adjust the crystallization of the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite film and ameliorate the interior morphology, which benefits to suppress charge recombination and reduce trap states. Moreover, the doping of   $  Zn^{2+}  $   could commendably regulate the energy band level of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $ , which improved the built-in potential and   $  V_{OC}  $   of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs. As a result, the optimized   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite film doped with 1%   $  Zn^{2+}  $   presented the most homogeneous and densely crystallized morphology along with the best optoelectronic properties. Consequently, the   $  CsPb_{0.99}Zn_{0.01}IBr_{2}  $ -based PSC achieved a champion PCE of 10.51% with a remarkable   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.28 V. Additionally, the   $  CsPb_{0.99}Zn_{0.01}IBr_{2}  $   PSCs without encapsulation retained 92% of their initial PCE values after 30 days of aging in air, and 81% of their initial PCE values after heating in nitrogen (  $  N_{2}  $  ) for more than 400 hours at 85 °C.

# Experimental

# Material preparation

All materials and reagents were used as received without any purification: cesium iodide (CsI, 99.9%, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.), lead bromide (PbBr  $  _{2}  $  , 99.9%, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.), zinc bromide(ZnBr  $  _{2}  $  , 99.9%, Aladdin), 2'2,7,7'-tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amine]-9,9'-spiro-bifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD, 99.9%, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.8%, anhydrous), chlorobenzene (CB, 99.9%, Aladdin), and isopropanol (IPA, 99.9%, Aladdin).

# Device fabrication

Firstly, FTO glass substrates were etched using Zn powder and 1.5 M HCl for expected patterns. The patterned FTO glass substrates were successively cleaned ultrasonically with deionized water, ethanol and acetone for 15 min and dried in nitrogen flow. Then, the dry substrates were treated with UV-ozone for 15 min before use. Subsequently, a homogeneous compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   (c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) layer (0.5 mol isopropyl titanate and 0.5 mol ethylene glycol amine dissolved in 1 mL IPA) was coated on the substrate. Then it was annealed in the air at 450 °C for 1 hour. After this, the c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate was immersed in 40 mM   $  TiCl_{4}  $   aqueous solution at 70 °C for 30 min. Finally, the treated c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate was cleaned with ethanol and annealed once in the air at 500 °C for 1 hour.

After annealing, the device was transferred to a nitrogen-filled glove box. The perovskite layer was obtained by spin-coating the 1 M   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   precursor (260 mg of CsI and 367 mg of   $  PbBr_{2}  $   were completely dissolved in 1 mL DMSO and stirred at 70 °C for 15 min.) on top of the c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate at 1000 rpm for 5 s and then 2500 rpm for 30 s. For   $  Zn^{2+}  $   doped   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $ , precursors were prepared by x% (x = 0.5, 1, 2) mol   $  ZnBr_{2}  $   substituting the same molar ratio of   $  PbBr_{2}  $ , maintaining the I/Br ratio unchanged. The operation of the   $  Zn^{2+}  $   doped   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   layer is the same as the one mentioned above. Then it is annealed at 250 °C step by step.

For the HTM layer, 72.3 mg Spiro-OMeTAD was absolutely dissolved in 1 mL CB, and then 28.5 μL of 4-tBP and 17.5 μL of Li-TFSI (520 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  in acetonitrile) were added into the Spiro-OMeTAD solution. The HTL was obtained by spin-coating Spiro-OMeTAD solution on top of the perovskite layers at 3000 rpm for 30 s.

Finally, approximately  $ 80~\mathrm{nm} $  thick silver electrodes were thermally evaporated by high vacuum thermal evaporation deposition onto the HTL. The active area of the device in this work was  $ 0.07~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ .

# Conclusions

In summary, we have incorporated   $  Zn^{2+}  $   into the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite structure by partial substitution of Pb. By doping an appropriate amount of 1%   $  Zn^{2+}  $   directly into the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   precursor, high-quality photoactive films with improved crystallinity and morphology with enlarged grain sizes and less pinholes can be successfully obtained. Simultaneously, the photoelectronic properties including favorable energy level alignment, decreased trap density, and the suppressed carrier recombination were achieved for the   $  CsPb_{0.99}Zn_{0.01}IBr_{2}  $   perovskite. Consequently, the all-inorganic PSC based on   $  CsPb_{0.99}Zn_{0.01}IBr_{2}  $   perovskite film displayed the highest PCE of 10.51% and   $  V_{OC}  $   up to 1.28 V, which were significantly improved compared with the pristine   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSC. In addition,   $  CsPb_{0.99}Zn_{0.01}IBr_{2}  $   PSCs also exhibit good long-term stability, retaining 92% of its initial efficiency after 30 days under atmospheric conditions and 81% of its initial efficiency after 400 hours of heating at 85 °C in a   $  N_{2}  $  -filled
