# ARTICLEINFO

Exploitation of novel inorganic hole-transport materials (HTM) is one of the most promising approaches that achieve stable and efficient perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, the low conductivity and large carrier transport barriers of the commonly used HTMs/perovskite system severely limit the device performance. Herein,   $  Mg^{2+}  $   doped   $  CuCrO_{2}  $   (M:CCO) nanocrystals are synthesized using a hydrothermal method and further adopted as the HTM for inverted PSCs. The hole conductivity of   $  CuCrO_{2}  $   is substantially enhanced upon ion doping. To address the anode/perovskite interfacial contact issues, the resultant M:CCO is designed to be a mesoporous structure atop an ultrathin compact   $  NiO_{x}  $   film (5 nm). This mesoscopic structured bilayer hole-transport layers (HTLs) with well-matched graded energy levels effectively shorten the charge transport pathway, reduce carrier transport barrier in HTL/perovskite interface, and improve the light harvesting efficiency. Combined with the design of a top electron-transport layer and the usage of methylammonium (MA)-free perovskites, a champion PCE of 21.64% has been achieved, which is among the highest efficiencies for inverted MA-free, cesium/formamidinium-based PSCs to date. Moreover, the unsealed device shows excellent long-term stability: more than 91% and 80% of their initial efficiencies were retained after light soaking under 1 sun illumination for 400 h and thermal aging at 85 °C for 1000 h, respectively. This study provides a potential strategy for developing highly efficient and stable inverted PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Hybrid organometallic halide perovskites, featured with low exciton binding energy, long carrier lifetimes and high defect tolerance, have drawn tremendous attentions in the fields of photovoltaics (PV), catalysis, detector, display and so on [1-8]. The power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) for single-junction perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with normal structure have increased dramatically from  $ 3.8\% $  to  $ 25.5\% $  within the past decade [9], which is comparable with the well-established silicon-based solar cells. Despite a huge improvement in PCE has been achieved, the unsatisfactory stability of perovskite film itself and traditional organic hole transport materials (HTM) against long-term light soaking, thermal excitation, and even humidity erosion greatly limited its future commercialization [10-12]. Among them,   $  CuCrO_{2}  $  , which was previously used as photocathode in p-type dye-sensitized solar cells [22], exhibits characteristics of hole transportation and UV-light blocking because of its high hole mobility (  $  10^{-2}  $   to   $  10^{1}  $     $  cm^{2}\cdot V^{-1}s^{-1}  $  ) and large bandgap (  $  \approx3.2  $   eV), showing great promise in highly stable PSCs [23,24]. Soon after, an azeotropic promoted approach has been proposed by Choy et al. to reduce the reaction temperature (  $  160\;^{\circ}C  $  ) of   $  CuCrO_{2}  $   nanocrystals. Nevertheless, the device efficiencies still lag far behind their organic counterparts, particularly the normal (n-i-p) structured devices [26–28]. The low intrinsic conductivity and/or unmatched energy levels of inorganic HTM were reported to be the important reasons that limit the device performance [29]. Therefore, many strategies have been adopted to improve the conductivity and regulate the energy levels of inorganic HTMs. Both element doping and synthesis method improvement can improve the physical and chemical properties of   $  CuCrO_{2}  $   materials to a certain extent, such as nanoparticle size, substrate coverage, transmittance, conductivity, and charge-carrier mobility [30]. However, it is still unable to completely eliminate the interstitials between the nanoparticles owing to the loose particle contact, and the carrier transport barrier in anode/perovskite interface because of the unmatched energy levels. This will leads to poor anode/perovskite interface contact, excessive charge accumulation and recombination, which severely limits the development of highly stable device [31]. It's thus highly desirable to develop novel strategy to address the above-mentioned issues. Herein, we first synthesize   $  Mg^{2+}  $   doped   $  CuCrO_{2}  $   (M:CCO) nanocrystals (NCs) using a traditional hydrothermal approach. The methylammonium (MA)-free, cesium/formamidinium (Cs/FA)-based perovskite (  $  Cs_{0.15}FA_{0.85}Pb(I_{0.9}Br_{0.1})_{3}  $  ) is then chosen as the light absorber to preclude the unstable component in perovskite film. As a result, the optimized device yields a champion PCE of 21.64%, which is among the highest efficiency thus far for inverted MA-free, Cs/FA-based PSCs. Meanwhile, more than 91% and 80% of their initial efficiencies have been remained even after long-term light soaking for 400 h and thermal aging at 85 °C for 1000 h, respectively, demonstrating superior photo- and thermal stability. This study offers a potential path for the future practical development of PSCs.

To overcome the poor interface contact between HTL and perovskite, the M:CCO was designed into mesoporous structure through adding pore-forming agent in the NCs solution according to our previously study [20]. The thickness of M:CCO film is critical to the device performance; we first subtly regulated the thickness parameter to pick out the optimal conditions. In addition to the microscopic carrier dynamics, the trap-assisted non-radiative recombination in the interface and/or the bulk is another factor influencing the device performance [52–54]. In principle, non-radiative charge recombination generally occurs through deep level traps or Auger recombination when the concentration of charge carriers is high [31]. The accelerated carrier transfer rate with respect to   $  NiO_{x}/mp-M:CCO  $   system can effectively avoid the charge accumulation, leading to a reduction in non-radiative recombination probability. The electrical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was then taken in the dark at open-circuit voltage to study the interface charge transport and accumulation characteristics, as shown in Fig. 4a. The result is fitted according to an equivalent circuit, as shown in the inset of Fig. 4a. The corresponding ohmic resistance (  $  R_{s}  $  ) in the high frequency mainly reflects the sheet resistance of electrodes and intrinsic resistance comes from the bulk materials, and the recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) assigned to the large arc at low frequency [20,37]. In this case, the mesoscopic device displays a lower   $  R_{s}  $   and higher   $  R_{rec}  $   (summarized in Table S5) compared to those of the planar one, suggesting that the designed   $  NiO_{x}/mp-M:CCO  $   HTL make a perfect interface contact and reduced resistance of interface charge transfer. It's reported that the interface charge accumulation will cause a large capacitance effect and hysteresis effect (Table 1 and Fig. S10) due to the dipole polarization of the interface electrons [55]. Although continuously increased capacitance near the low frequency region (1 Hz) can be clearly found for both devices in this study (Fig. S16), the capacitive effect of   $  NiO_{x}/mp-M:CCO  $   based device is visibly reduced as expected. We also measured   $  J_{SC}  $   and   $  V_{OC}  $   as a function of light intensity for the planar-M:CCO and   $  NiO_{x}/mp-M:CCO  $   based devices to probe the dynamics of charge-carrier recombination. A linear correlation for the power law dependence of   $  J_{SC}  $   with light intensity (  $  J \propto I^{0}  $  ) ranging from 10 to 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $   is shown in Fig. S17. The fitted values for both devices are all close to 1, indicative of negligible bimolecular recombination and no space charge effects for both devices [56]. A plot of   $  V_{OC}  $   versus logarithm of the incident light intensity is then obtained (Fig. 4b). The planar-M:CCO based device gives a slope of 1.39 kT/q, where k is Boltzmann's constant, q is the elementary charge, and T is the absolute temperature. The device based on   $  NiO_{x}/mp-M:CCO  $   exhibits a smaller slope of 1.23 kT/q, indicative of the reduced trap-assisted Shockley-Read-Hall recombination in the mesoscopic device [7]. The reduced slope also contributes to the increased FF, because the FF highly depends on the ideality factor [57]. The space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method was further used to examine the trap-state density in the perovskite films with different HTLs by constructing a hole-only device, as shown in Fig. 4c. Recently, device stability has become one of the biggest obstacles that limit the commercialization of PSCs, which is also the reason why we exploit novel inorganic HTM and MA-free perovskites. Thus, the long-term storage stability, thermal stability and light soaking stability of the devices are investigated. Fig. 5a and Table S6 show the main parameters for the unencapsulated planar-M:CCO and NiO $ _{x} $ /mp-M:CCO based devices under ambient conditions with relative humidity (RH) ranging from 20 to 80%. The PCE of NiO $ _{x} $ /mp-M:CCO retains more than 93.5% of its initial efficiency after two months storage, which is better than the control sample result (81.7%). Moreover, even if the unsealed devices subjected to thermal aging at 85 °C for 1000 h (Fig. 5b), over 81% of its initial efficiency is sustained for the NiO $ _{x} $ /mp-M:CCO based device, whereas the PCE of the planar-M:CCO has been drastically attenuated to 67% under the same condition. We then evaluate the photostability of the devices (white light LED, N $ _{2} $  filled glovebox, ~30 °C). As shown in Fig. 5c, the NiO $ _{x} $ /mp-M:CCO based device remains 91.2% of its original PCE after 400 h of irradiation. The improved device stability in the present work should be attributed to the follow reasons: 1) Exploitation of wide bandgap inorganic HTL and construction of novel device structure. On one hand, the inorganic HTL exhibit better intrinsic thermal stability than the traditional used organic HTL. On the other hand, the designed mesoscopic structure can increase the interfacial contact area and accelerate the carrier transfer, which will lead to improved device stability. 2) Use of stable MA-free perovskites. The MA-free, Cs/FA-based perovskites exhibit better thermal stability than that of the MA-containing perovskites, which can improve the stability of perovskite films fundamentally. 3) Enlarged crystal grains and reduced trap state density of perovskite film. The mp-M:CCO not only improves the crystal grains of perovskite film, but also shorten the carrier transport pathway and reduced the interfacial defects, which in turn reduced the degradation sites. These characteristics not only make a breakthrough on the device efficiency based on novel inorganic HTL, but also meet the increasingly stringent stability standards of PSCs.
