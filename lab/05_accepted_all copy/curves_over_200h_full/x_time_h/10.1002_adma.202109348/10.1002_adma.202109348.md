# High-Performance Ternary Perovskite–Organic Solar Cells

Tao Zhu, Lening Shen, Sangni Xun, Julio S. Sarmiento, Yongrui Yang, Luyao Zheng, Hong Li,  $  ^{*}  $   He Wang,  $  ^{*}  $   Jean-Luc Bredas,  $  ^{*}  $   and Xiong Gong  $  ^{*}  $

Perovskite solar cells in which 2D perovskites are incorporated within a 3D perovskite network exhibit improved stability with respect to purely 3D systems, but lower record power conversion efficiencies (PCEs). Here, a breakthrough is reported in achieving enhanced PCEs, increased stability, and suppressed photocurrent hysteresis by incorporating n-type, low-optical-gap conjugated organic molecules into 2D:3D mixed perovskite composites. The resulting ternary perovskite–organic composites display extended absorption in the near-infrared region, improved film morphology, enlarged crystallinity, balanced charge transport, efficient photoinduced charge transfer, and suppressed counter-ion movement. As a result, the ternary perovskite–organic solar cells exhibit PCEs over 23%, which are among the best PCEs for perovskite solar cells with p–i–n device structure. Moreover, the ternary perovskite–organic solar cells possess dramatically enhanced stability and diminished photocurrent hysteresis. All these results demonstrate that the strategy of exploiting ternary perovskite–organic composite thin films provides a facile way to realize high-performance perovskite solar cells.

# 1. Introduction

Hybrid organic-inorganic perovskites have emerged as promising photovoltaic materials due to their remarkable optoelectronic properties and the feasibility of cost-effective, high-throughput manufacturing of perovskite solar cells (PSCs).[1-3] Power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) over  $ 25\% $  have been reported for PSCs based on 3D perovskites through morphological manipulations of the perovskite photoactive layers and generic interface engineering of the electron and hole extraction layers.[1-4] However, 3D perovskites are sensitive

Here, we report a strategy to fabricate ternary perovskite–organic composite thin films, which contributes to increase photocurrent and stability, and suppress photocurrent hysteresis. By incorporating n-type, low-optical-gap conjugated organic molecules into the 2D:3D mixed perovskite layers, the resulting ternary perovskite–organic composites display extended absorption in the near-infrared (NIR) region, improved film morphology, enlarged crystallinity, balanced charge transport, efficient photoinduced charge transfer, and much reduced counter-ion movement. Based on joint experimental and computational studies including materials synthesis and characterization, device evaluation, and density functional theory (DFT) calculations, we reveal that these improvements can be primarily attributed to specific chemical interactions between the n-type organic molecules and the perovskites. As a result, PSCs based on such ternary perovskite–organic composite thin films exhibit a PCE of   $  23.06 \pm 0.70\%  $   with dramatically suppressed photocurrent hysteresis and enhanced device stability.

We start by considering the 2D:3D mixed composites as a reference point and then introduce the novel ternary composites that include n-type organic molecules. After a discussion of our computational results, we describe high-performance PSCs based on these ternary perovskite–organic composites.

# 3. Ternary Perovskite—Organic Composite Thin Films

To lessen the unbalanced character of charge transport, our strategy has been to incorporate n-type, low-optical-gap

O6T-4F  $  ^{[34-36]}  $  molecules (see molecular structure in Scheme 1B) into 2D:3D mixed perovskite composites, which leads to the formation of ternary perovskite–organic composites. Scheme 1C illustrates the formation of the ternary quasi-2D (4F-BEA)  $  _{x}  $  (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{1-x}  $ :3D MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ :O6T-4F composites. O6T-4F is chosen as electron acceptor since it displays high electron mobility, which we anticipate to contribute to balanced charge transport and to facilitate exciton dissociation within the ternary composites.  $  ^{[37-41]}  $  Remarkably, we find that the electron mobility upon formation of the ternary system is enhanced ≈10 times (Figures S6 and S7; Table S1, Supporting Information) and results in much better balanced charge transport (  $  \mu_{e}/\mu_{h}=0.86  $  ). Also, as shown in Figure 2A, O6T-4F possesses a strong absorption band extending from 550 to 1000 nm, which is complementary to that of the 2D:3D mixed perovskite composite. Therefore,  $  J_{SC}  $  is expected to increase in PSCs based on ternary perovskite–organic composite thin films.  $  ^{[40-42]}  $

Moreover, while a   $  0.190^{\circ}  $   full width at half-maximum (FWHM) of the (110) peak was observed in the 2D:3D mixed perovskite thin film, the ternary film has a corresponding FWHM of   $  0.120^{\circ}  $   (Figure S11, Supporting Information). Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy was carried out to investigate the possible chemical interactions between the perovskite components and the O6T-4F organic molecules; the results are shown in Figure 2E. The prominent peak at   $  1726 \, cm^{-1}  $   observed in the O6T-4F thin film corresponds to the stretching vibration of the carbonyl C=O bond. A new peak at   $  1705 \, cm^{-1}  $   is observed in the ternary perovskite–organic composite thin film and is ascribed to this stretching vibration. This decrease in the stretching vibrational frequency indicates that the strength of the C=O bond length is decreased, which suggests the presence of chemical interactions between the perovskites and the carbonyl groups of the O6T-4F molecules;  $  ^{[45]}  $   we specifically discuss these chemical interactions in the next section.

Figure 2F presents the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra of the 2D:3D mixed perovskite and ternary perovskite–organic composite thin films. Typical features associated with the C, N, I, and Pb atoms are observed in both systems (Figure S13, Supporting Information). The N 1s binding energy (BE) observed in the ternary film is shifted by   $  \approx+0.80  $   eV compared to that of the 2D:3D mixed film. Similar results are also observed for I 3d and Pb 4f, with a +0.44 eV BE shift for both I 3d and Pb 4f. These results are further indications of significant chemical interactions between the O6T-4F molecules and the perovskite lattices; importantly, such interactions could restrict ion migration and thus result in suppressed photocurrent hysteresis in the PSCs.  $  ^{[46-48]}  $

# 4. DFT Characterization of the Geometric and Electronic Structures at Grain Boundaries

The DFT calculations were carried out to understand the impact of the O6T-4F electron acceptor molecules on the structure and electronic properties of the 2D:3D mixed films, specifically at grain boundaries. The grain boundary model  $ \Sigma 5(012)^{[49]} $  containing two pairs of  $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $  and  $ \mathrm{I^{-}} $  vacancies ( $ \mathrm{V_{MA}} $  and  $ \mathrm{V_I} $ ) was used to describe the grain boundaries in bulk MAPbI $ _3 $ ;  $ \mathrm{V_{MA}} $  and

$  V_{1}  $  defects were purposely considered as they have low formation energies and are found to be shallow donors and acceptors, respectively.  $  ^{[50]}  $  To reduce computational costs while preserving the range of possible chemical interactions, we considered difluoride-substituted dicyanomethylene-indonane (DFIC, see chemical structure Figure S14 in the Supporting Information), as a representative O6T-4F fragment (see the DFT methodology in Section S3 of the Supporting Information). The optimized geometry of the  $  \Sigma5(012)  $ :DFIC system  $  ^{[49]}  $  (Figure 3A) shows that the electron acceptor moiety occupies the void associated with the  $  V_{MA}  $  and  $  V_{I}  $  pairs located within the MAI layer. The indonane plane of the DFIC acceptor moiety is nearly parallel with the  $  PbI_{2}  $  layer, with the shortest Pb-C distance at 3.14 Å (see the dotted line in Figure 3A). The most interesting feature is the appearance of an O–Pb chemical bond ( $  \approx  $ 2.68 Å) between the oxygen atom of the DFIC carbonyl group and a neighboring Pb atom in the  $  PbI_{2}  $  layer. Such an energy level alignment is consistent with the observation of enhanced PL quenching upon the incorporation of the organic acceptors.

# 5. Device Performance

The photovoltaic properties of the ternary perovskite–organic composite thin films have been investigated through the characterization of PSCs with a device structure corresponding to ITO/PTAA/perovskite/C $  _{60}  $ /BCP/Al. Figure 4A presents the  $  J-V  $  characteristics. The ternary-based devices have a  $  J_{\text{SC}}  $  of 26.04 ± 0.23 mA cm $  ^{-2}  $ , a  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $  of 1.08 ± 0.01 V, a FF of 0.82 ± 0.01; the corresponding PCE is 23.06 ± 0.70%, which is among the highest for PSCs with the p–i–n device structure. $ ^{[4,51,52]} $  Moreover, the photocurrent hysteresis index (HI) $ ^{[54]} $  is 0.035 in the ternary-based PSCs, down from a HI value of 0.104 in the mixed 2D:3D-based PSCs. We suggest that this

remarkable decrease in photocurrent hysteresis can be attributed to a reduced number of defects (see calculated trap densities in Section S1 of the Supporting Information) and the appearance of chemical interactions between the perovskite and O6T-4F (specifically,   $  Pb^{2+}  $   and C=O).  $  ^{[46-48]}  $

Figure 4B displays the PSC external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra. The ternary-based PSCs show a photocurrent response up to 1000 nm, which is consistent with the absorption spectrum of the O6T-4F thin film (Figure 2A), while the 2D:3D mixed perovskite devices exhibit no photoresponse beyond   $  \lambda > 800  $   nm. The photoresponse at   $  \lambda > 800  $   nm is attributed to efficient charge transport through the percolation pathways provided by O6T-4F.  $  ^{[34,35,40,42]}  $   Moreover, the significantly enhanced EQE in the NIR region is the signature of efficient exciton dissociation and charge transfer across the ternary active layers. The integrated photocurrents from the EQE spectra are consistent with the   $  J_{SC}  $   values observed from the J-V curves (Figure 3A). A   $  J_{SC}  $   of 0.63 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   observed from ternary-based PSCs at   $  \lambda > 800  $   nm is another piece of evidence for the positive impact of the O6T-4F contribution.

The statistical distributions of the measured PCEs of PSCs are shown in Figure 4C. Based on the t-distribution, a narrower confidence interval for the PSCs based on the ternary system points to a higher reproducibility compared to PSCs based on 2D:3D mixed perovskite composites.

Figure 4D presents the shelf-stability of the PSCs; here, the PSCs are unencapsulated but stored in a glove box with an   $  O_{2}  $   level of about 200 ppm and   $  \approx20\%  $   relative humidity. Remarkably, the ternary-based PSCs retain 50% of their initial PCE after   $  \approx1800  $   h, to be compared to   $  \approx1500  $   h for the PSCs based on the 2D:3D mixed systems. These results demonstrate that the incorporation of O6T-4F within the 2D:3D mixed perovskites can boost the PSC stability. Such enhanced shelf stability can be attributed to the improved film morphology induced by O6T-4F and the chemical interactions between the perovskites and O6T-4F molecules.

To better understand the factors contributing to the device performance, transient photocurrent (TPC) measurements were carried out to probe the photogenerated charge carriers.  $  ^{[46,53]}  $   As indicated in Figure 4E, the charge carrier extraction times are evaluated to be   $  \approx75  $   and   $  \approx50  $   ns for PSCs based on 2D:3D mixed perovskites and ternary perovskite–organic composites, respectively. A shorter charge carrier extraction time indicates a more efficient charge extraction process.  $  ^{[46,55]}  $   With the additional observation of slightly enhanced charge carrier densities (Figure S18, Supporting Information), this confirms that the ternary-based devices generate more photocurrent than the 2D:3D mixed systems.

# 6. Conclusion

We have reported high-performance perovskite solar cells based on ternary perovskite–organic composite thin films. The incorporation of O6T-4F within a 2D:3D mixed perovskite composite results in thin films with extended absorption in the NIR region, improved film morphology, increased crystallinity, better-balanced charge transport, more efficient photoinduced charge transfer, and reduced ion migration due to chemical interactions between the perovskites and O6T-4F. The combination of these properties leads to a PCE of   $  23.06 \pm 0.70\%  $  , with strongly reduced photocurrent hysteresis (HI = 0.035) and enhanced stability. All these results demonstrate that we have

developed a facile and promising way to realize high-performance perovskite solar cells by following a strategy based on ternary perovskite–organic composite thin films.
