# ABSTRACT

2D Ruddlesden–Popper perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with structure formulated as   $  R_{2}MA_{n-1}Pb_{n}I_{3n+1}  $   (R is the organic bulky cation) exhibit superior environmental stability, but their efficiency still lags behind that of their 3D counterpart, which is mainly limited by the insufficient light absorption and carrier transportation. Herein, we incorporated guanidinium (  $  GA^{+}  $  ) into the PEA at the "R" site to form mixed bulky cations and investigated the doping effect of   $  GA^{+}  $  on the photovoltaic performance of layered 2D perovskites. Our results show that incorporating   $  GA^{+}  $  into the "R" site of 2D PSCs exhibits a stronger and wider light absorption, faster charge carrier mobility, and better quality perovskite films. Consequently, both the short circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) and fill factor (FF) of the 2D PSCs were significantly improved. As a result, the energy conversion efficiency (PCE) of the (PEA,   $  GA_{2}MA_{4}Pb_{5}I_{16}  $   champion device achieved 15.27%, which is much higher than the 11.58% PCE of the single cation 2D PSCs. Also, the 2D PSCs with the mixed (PEA, GA) cations exhibit improved environmental stability. Our work provides important guidance to obtain efficient and stable 2D PSCs through bulky cation engineering and modulating the interlayer interaction in 2D perovskite.

# I. INTRODUCTION

3D perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have undergone rapid development in recent years due to their ideal bandgap, long charge diffusion length, and high defect tolerance. The highest energy conversion efficiency (PCE) currently certified has reached 24%.  $  ^{1}  $   However, the intrinsic stability issue of 3D perovskite impedes their practical applications.  $  ^{2}  $   It is urgent to improve the stability of the light absorption perovskite layer. Previous reports show that the layered Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) perovskite generally described by the formula   $  R_{2}MA_{n-1}Pb_{n}I_{3n+1}  $   (where R is the long hydrophobic bulky organic cation) exhibits a much better environmental stability than that of 3D PSCs.  $  ^{3-6}  $   However, the long-chain organic cations at the "R" site including butylammonium (BA  $  ^{+}  $  ), ethylenediammonium (EDA  $  ^{2+}  $  ), and phenylethylammonium (PEA  $  ^{+}  $  ) widely used in 2D perovskites are insulating materials and thereby impede the carrier transport between neighboring inorganic layers.  $  ^{7-9}  $   Meanwhile, due to the quantum confinement effect in low-dimensional materials,

the 2D RP perovskite exhibits a wider bandgap than 3D perovskite, which limit the light absorption.  $  ^{10,11}  $   These two factors are considered as the major reasons for the low efficiency of 2D PSCs.  $  ^{12,13}  $   Adjusting the crystal orientation of 2D PSC from the parallel direction toward the vertical direction has been known as an effective strategy to improve charge carrier transport. Previous studies have successfully improved the charge transport in 2D perovskites with different strategies including hot casting method, adding additives, and designing new bulky cations.  $  ^{14-19}  $   For example, Chen et al. reported that the preferential vertical orientation of 2D PSCs can be achieved by adding   $  NH_{4}SCN  $   additives, resulting in a high PCE of 11%.  $  ^{20}  $   Our group employed the new fluorinated PEA bulky cations to obtain a preferential vertical orientation of 2D RP perovskite and eventually achieved a high PCE over 17%.  $  ^{21}  $   Although the carrier transport can be enhanced by optimizing the crystal orientation, the relatively large bandgap of 2D perovskite leads to the insufficient light absorption and charge separation and thus limits the efficiency of 2D PSCs.  $  ^{22}  $

Herein, we incorporated the   $  GA^{+}  $  to replace part of PEA bulky cations at the R site to form the mixed cations for the RP type of 2D perovskite. The PCE of (PEA,   $  GA)_{2}MA_{4}Pb_{5}I_{16}  $   based PSCs with mixed cations significantly increased from 11.58% to 15.27% as compared to the single cation   $  PEA_{2}MA_{4}Pb_{5}I_{16}  $   reference device. Furthermore, we systematically investigate the effects of   $  GA^{+}  $  on the improved device performance by combining multiple optical, morphology, and photophysics characterizations. Our results highlighted the critical roles of rational design of bulky cations on the overall device performance for 2D RP PSCs.

Since 2D PSCs are known for their superior environmental stability, we compared the humidity stability of mixed cations and single cation 2D devices. The unencapsulated devices were stored under dark ambient air conditions with the relative humidity of 55%–60%. After 800 h, the PEA+GA devices can still maintain over 80% of its initial efficiency shown in Fig. 5. However, the efficiency of PEA devices drops to 65% of the initial efficiency. Clearly, PEA+GA mixed cation devices exhibit a significantly improved environmental stability compared to the referenced PEA devices, which may be related to the formation of the high-quality perovskite film.

# III. CONCLUSION

In conclusion, we incorporated guanidinium (GA $  ^{+}  $ ) into the PEA at the R site to form mixed bulky cations for RP type (R $ _2 $ MA $ _{n-1} $ Pb $ _n $ I $ _{3n+1} $ ) of 2D PSCs. Our results reveal that mixing GA $  ^{+}  $  with PEA cations at the R site significantly enhances the light absorption, improve film quality, and promote carrier transport in 2D PSCs. By optimizing the ratios of PEA/GA, we achieved a champion device efficiency of 15.27%, which is significantly higher than 11.58% of PEA single bulky cation devices. Additionally, incorporating GA $  ^{+}  $  improves the stability of 2D devices, which retain over 80% of their

initial efficiency after storing for 800 h under the ambient air condition. Our work highlighted the importance of a rational design of R bulky cations on the photovoltaic performance for 2D PSCs.
