# Bulk Heterojunction Perovskite Solar Cells Incorporated with  $ \mathrm{Zn_2SnO_4} $  Nanoparticles as the Electron Acceptors

Wenzhan Xu,  $  ^{\#}  $   Luyao Zheng,  $  ^{\#}  $   Tao Zhu, Lei Liu and Xiong Gong  $  ^{*}  $

Department of Polymer Engineering, College of Polymer Science and Polymer Engineering, The University of Akron, Akron, OH 44325, USA

# Abstract

Perovskite solar cells were developed very fast in the past decade, but hybrid perovskite materials with unbalanced charge carrier diffusion lengths were not fully addressed by either a conventional or a planar heterojunction device structures. In this study, high-performance perovskite solar cells with a bulk heterojunction device structure, where   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $   is blended with n-type high electron-mobility   $  Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   nanoparticle as the photoactive layer, are reported. Systematically studies indicate that the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}:Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   bulk heterojunction thin film possesses enhanced and balanced charge carrier mobilities, superior film morphology with enlarged crystal sizes, and suppressed trap-density. Photoluminescence and time-resolved photoluminescence studies further demonstrate that there is an efficient photo-induced charge carrier transfer between   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $   and   $  Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   nanoparticles. Thus, bulk heterojunction perovskite solar cells by the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}:Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   thin film exhibit over 21.07% power conversion efficiency, which is more than 12 % enhancement as compared to that (18.74%) observed from planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells by pristine   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $   thin film. Moreover, bulk heterojunction perovskite solar cells possess significantly suppressed photocurrent hysteresis, dramatically enhanced device stability and reproducibility. All these results demonstrate that high performance perovskite solar cells can be realized through bulk heterojunction device structure.

Keywords: Perovskite solar cells, bulk heterojunction device structure,  $ \mathrm{Zn_2SnO_4} $  nanoparticles, electron acceptors, device performance

# Introduction

In the last decade, there is a greatest attention on perovskite solar cells (PSCs) in both academic and industrial sectors.  $  ^{1-5}  $   Over 25% power conversion efficiency (PCE) has been reported from PSCs with a conventional device structure, where a mesoporous titanium dioxide (ms-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) scaffold layer serves as the electron extraction layer (EEL) for effectively transporting separated electrons from hybrid perovskite layer to the indium tin oxide (ITO) cathode.  $  ^{1-5}  $   However, the ms-  $  TiO_{2}  $   EEL was required to be sintered at high-temperature (over 500 °C),  $  ^{1,6}  $   which certainly restricted PSCs to be cost-effectively manufactured on the flexible substrates.  $  ^{7,8}  $   Alternatively, PSCs with a planar heterojunction (PHJ) device structure, which potentially can be processed on the flexible substrates, was developed to circumvent the problems from PSCs with a conventional device structure.  $  ^{1,6}  $   In PHJ PSCs, low-temperature solution-processed poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) was typically employed as the hole extraction layer (HEL) and solution-processed fullerene derivative typically served as the EEL.  $  ^{9-13}  $   PSCs with above two different device structures have been rapidly developed, however, the diffusion length of the hole is greater than that of the electron (  $  L_{eff,h}^{+}/L_{eff,e^{-}}>1  $  ) of hybrid perovskite was not fully addressed so far.  $  ^{1-5,12-15}  $

In order to address such unbalanced electron and hole diffusion lengths within hybrid perovskite materials, we firstly reported bulk heterojunction (BHJ) PSCs, where methylammonium lead halides was blended with fullerene derivatives for balancing charge transporting properties.  $  ^{[16,17]}  $   Further studies found that BHJ composites not only facilitate charge carrier extraction efficiency, but also optimize film morphology of photoactive layer, resulting in boosted PCEs.  $  ^{[16-23]}  $   However, fullerene derivatives were hardly homogenously mixed with hybrid perovskite materials due to its poor solubility in the solvent used for dissolving precursors for generating perovskite thin film.  $  ^{[16-23]}  $   Moreover, fullerene derivatives possess poor electron mobilities.  $  ^{[24]}  $   All these shortcomings restricted further boost device performance

The current densities versus voltages (J-V) characteristics of PSCs under different scan directions are shown in Figure 1c. The device performance parameters are summarized in Table 1. Under the scan rate of 0.6 V/s, PHJ PSCs by pristine   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $   thin film exhibit a short-circuit current (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 22.23 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , an open circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.06 V, a fill factor (FF) of 79%, and with a corresponding PCE of 18.74%. These device performance parameters are well agree with the reported values.  $  ^{1-6}  $   BHJ PSCs by the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}:Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   composite thin film, where the concentration of   $  Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   NPs is ~5% (by weight) in BHJ composite, exhibit a   $  J_{SC}  $   of 22.96 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.08 V, a FF of 80%, and with a corresponding PCE of 19.93%. BHJ PSCs by the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}:Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   composite thin film, where the concentration of   $  Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   NPs is ~10% (by weight) in BHJ composite, show a   $  J_{SC}  $   of 23.34 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.10 V, a FF of 82%, and with a resultant PCE of 21.07%. These results indicate an over 12 % improvement in PCE as compared with that from PHJ PSCs by pristine   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $   thin film. However, as the concentration of   $  Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   NPs is further increased to 15 %, BHJ PSCs by the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}:Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   composite thin film exhibit a decreased PCE (17.32%). The device performance of BHJ PSCs correlated with the ratios and particle sizes of   $  Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   NPs in the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}:Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   BHJ composite thin films are described in SI 2.

# Conclusion

In summary, we reported high-performance bulk heterojunction (BHJ) perovskite solar cells (PSCs), where   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $   was blended with high electron-mobility   $  Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   nanoparticles (NPs) electron acceptors as the photoactive layer. It was found that the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}:Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   BHJ composite thin films possess enhanced and balanced charge carrier mobilities, superior film quality and enlarged crystal sizes, suppressed trap-density, and reduced charge carrier extraction time. Photoluminescence and time-resolved photoluminescence studies demonstrated that there is an efficient photo-induced charge carrier transfer between   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $   and   $  Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   NPs. As a result, PSCs with a BHJ device structure by the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}:Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   thin film exhibited a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.10 V, a   $  J_{SC}  $   of 23.34 mA/cm², a FF of 82.1%, and with a corresponding PCE of 21.07%, all of which are higher than those (a   $  J_{SC}  $   of 22.23 mA cm⁻², a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.06 V, a FF of 79.4%, and with a corresponding PCE of 18.74%) from planar heterojunction PSCs by pristine   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $   thin film. In addition, BHJ PSCs by the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}:Zn_{2}SnO_{4}  $   thin film possesses dramatically reduced photocurrent hysteresis, improved device stability and reproducibility. All these results demonstrate that high performance perovskite solar cells can be realized through bulk heterojunction device structure.
