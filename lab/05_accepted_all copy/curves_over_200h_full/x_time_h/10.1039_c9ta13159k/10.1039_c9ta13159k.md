Abstract: Tin perovskite solar cells (TPSCs) as one of the most promising candidate  $  ^{45}  $  for lead-free PSCs have large potential to be industrialized in the future. However, the TPSCs have often been fabricated at an extremely low oxygen concentration to avoid the oxidation from  $  Sn^{2+}  $  to  $  Sn^{4+}  $ , which is not applicable to the large production. In this work, we construct a carbonylate antioxidant capping layer atop the perovskite surface via solvent engineering method to prevent the oxygen permeation into perovskite layer during the fabrication process of the device to enlarge the oxygen region from 0.1 ppm to 100 ppm. Meanwhile, a pinhole-free perovskite film with large grain size, reduced  $  Sn^{4+}  $  defects and long carrier recombination lifetime was fabricated. As a result, high-performance TPSCs could be achieved in a wide region of oxygen concentration with the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 9.47% at 0.1 ppm oxygen and 9.03% at 100 ppm oxygen, respectively; the device was stable, maintaining over 93% of its initial PCE after operating at the maximum power point under continuous light soaking (AM 1.5G, 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $ ) for 600 hours.

# Introduction

Lead halide perovskite solar cells as the next-generation photovoltaic technique have attracted much attention from all over the world.  $  ^{1-4}  $   However, the toxicity issue of the lead component makes it a hidden danger in industrialization.  $  ^{5,6}  $   Recently, environmental friendly tin perovskite materials have emerged as one of the most promising candidates in future industrialization due to their optimum optical bandgap from 1.2 eV to 1.4 eV, small exciton binding energy, and the high charge carrier mobility.  $  ^{7-10}  $   Despite these favorable optoelectronic properties, the tin perovskite solar cells (TPSCs) still show a power conversion efficiency (PCE) much lower than that of the lead analogue. This is mainly attributed to the fact that metastable   $  Sn^{2+}  $   in perovskite lattice is prone to be oxidized to p-type   $  Sn^{4+}  $   defects at the presence of oxygen during the device fabrication.  $  ^{11,12}  $   On the other side, these   $  Sn^{4+}  $   defects could also affect the crystallization of perovskite film and lead to unfavorable pinholes and vacancies that may act as recombination center for the charge carriers.  $  ^{13,14}  $

In order to suppress the generation of   $  Sn^{4+}  $   defects, some approaches have been attempted to suppress the instability of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   component. Antioxidant agents such as   $  SnF_{2}  $  ,   $  H_{3}PO_{2}  $  ,   $  N_{2}H_{5}Cl  $   and hydroxybenzene sulfonic acid were employed as sacrificial agent to reduce the   $  Sn^{4+}  $   defects within perovskite precursor.  $  ^{[11,15-17]}  $   On the other hand, the incorporation of large-size ammonium cations such as phenylethylammonium (PEA), formamidinium (FA), butylammonium (BA), and guanidinium (GA) could not only raise the formation energy of   $  Sn^{4+}  $   defect but also induce a higher crystallinity of the tin perovskite film.  $  ^{[18-24]}  $   However, there is still a lack of comprehensive study to

suppress the formation of  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{4+} $  during the whole process of devices fabrication, which could be the key to obtain efficient TPSCs at higher oxygen concentration. Furthermore, almost all of tin perovskite solar cells needed to be processed under an extremely low oxygen concentration, typically less than  $ 0.1~\mathrm{ppm} $ . Such a narrow processing window seriously restricts the scalable and industrial production of TPSCs.

Here, we introduced the 4-fluorobenzohydrazide (FBH) additive with low surface energy to form a carbonylate antioxidant capping layer at the FASnI $ _3 $  perovskite surface via solvent engineering method. This method effectively suppressed the generation of Sn $ ^{4+} $  during the entire fabrication process of the devices, including the deposition of tin perovskite film and subsequent charge transport layer and metal electrode. In addition, we found that the coordination between the carbonyl group in FBH and the Sn $ ^{2+} $  components could benefit the crystal growth in thermodynamically preferred orientation and lower the total Gibbs free energy of the crystallization process, resulting in a large and smooth perovskite grain with significant increase in the carrier recombination lifetime. Attributed to these benefits, high-performance TPSCs could be fabricated in a wide range of oxygen concentration, with the champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 9.47% at 0.1 ppm oxygen and 9.03 % at 100 ppm oxygen, respectively. Additionally, the device showed high operational stability, maintaining over 93% of its initial PCE after 600 hours continuous light soaking (AM 1.5G, 100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $ ) at the maximum power point.

# Results and Discussion

The preparation of perovskite film was conducted in a wide range of oxygen concentration condition from 0.1 ppm to 100 ppm (monitored by a digital detector in the glovebox, as shown in Fig. S1). Firstly, the precursor solution containing FAI,   $  SnI_{2}  $   and   $  SnF_{2}  $   were dropped onto the substrate for spin-coating process, FBH molecules were then introduced into the perovskite precursor by dripping a FBH-contained chlorobenzene anti-solvent to induce the nucleation and growth of perovskite crystal, following by the thermal annealing process to form   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite film capped with the FBH molecule (Fig. 1a). The formation of surface FBH capping layer is kinetically favorable due to the very low surface energy of the fluorobenzene unit on FBH,  $  ^{25}  $   since the surface energy tends to decrease spontaneously.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was employed to investigate the impact of FBH on surface morphology of  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  film (Fig. 1b), and the photographs of the samples were also depicted in Fig. S2. At a normal oxygen condition (0.1 ppm), the pristine  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  perovskite exhibited a polycrystalline and pinhole-free morphology with large surface roughness. In contrast, FBH-treated  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  perovskite showed a dense and smooth film with larger grain size, attributed to the fact that coordination effect between the C=O group and the  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  changed the morphology of precursor film from the homogeneous nanoparticles to the large randomly-distributed textures (Fig. S3), such a template effect could minimize the total Gibbs free energy of the

We also measured the environmental stability of control and FBH-based solar cells fabricated at 0.1 ppm   $  O_{2}  $   in ambient air (20% humidity), as shown in Fig. S14. The control device showed a serious degradation of PCE after a few hours measurement in both dark and light-soaking conditions. On the contrary, the FBH-based device showed a much slower PCE decay rate than that of the control one. Possible explanation was that the FBH capping layer can effectively forbid the oxygen absorption and permeation onto perovskite film, thus alleviate the destruction speed of the perovskite lattice. Finally, the operational stability of control and FBH-based solar cells with full encapsulation under 0.1 ppm   $  O_{2}  $   level was conducted under continuous light soaking (AM 1.5G, 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) at the maximum power point in air. The FBH-based cell maintained over 93% of its initial power output for 600 hours while the control device showed a 57% loss of its initial efficiency after 600-hours operation (Fig. 4e), indicating a significant improvement of the device operational stability after post-treatment with the FBH.

# Conclusion

In conclusion, we demonstrated a novel strategy to fabricate highly efficient TPSCs under a wide region of oxygen concentration by forming a carbonylate antioxidant capping layer atop the perovskite film via solvent engineering method. As a

We believed that this finding provide a new insight into the phase-stabilized mechanism of tin perovskite materials, and offer a guideline for the ambient production of high-efficiency tin photoelectric devices.

# Device fabrication

The ITO glass substrates were patterned and cleaned via sequential ultra-sonication in detergent, deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol for 30 min, respectively. Then the substrates were treated with UV light and ozone for 30 min before the deposition of the (PEG)-PEDOT: PSS layer.  $  ^{39}  $   The (PEG)-PEDOT: PSS solution was spin-coated onto the ITO substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s, and the coated substrate was then annealed at 150 °C for 20 min in air. After that, the substrates were immediately transferred into the glovebox. The perovskite precursor solution composed of 0.9 M SnI  $  _{2}  $  , 0.9 M FAI, 0.09 M SnF  $  _{2}  $   in DMSO was stirred for 3 h at room temperature. Then the prepared solution was spin-coated onto the (PEG)-PEDOT: PSS layer at 1000 rpm for 12 s and at 5000 rpm for 65 s, 140 uL chlorobenzene (CB) with or without FBH was dripped onto the perovskite film after 30 s of high-speed 5000 rpm process. Then the spin-coated perovskite films were annealed at 65 °C for 10 s and at
