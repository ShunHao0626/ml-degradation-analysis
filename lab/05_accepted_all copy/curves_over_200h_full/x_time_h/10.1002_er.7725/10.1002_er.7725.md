# A cascade bilayer electron transport layer toward efficient and stable Ruddlesden-Popper perovskite solar cells

Kyeong Su Kim  $  ^{1,2}  $   | Woosung Lee  $  ^{3}  $   | Jae Woong Jung  $  ^{1,2}  $

# Summary

Optimal interfaces play a key role in charge transport/recombination characteristics and minimized potential loss of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Currently,  $  n  $ -type oxide semiconductors are the most common electron transport layer (ETL) material, but the imperfect electronic structure, unfavorable band alignment, and corresponding poor interface of ETL/perovskite absorber remain a great challenge for achieving a high photovoltaic performance of PSCs. Here, we combine a fullerene derivative or a non-fullerene organic semiconductor with tin(IV) oxide ( $  \mathrm{SnO}_2  $ ) to promise much-improved surface and electronic structure of ETL interface in Ruddlesden-Popper perovskites (RPPs)-based photovoltaic devices. The organic semiconductors fill a bumpy surface of  $  \mathrm{SnO}_2  $  to make the surface smoother, which effectively avoids the shunt pathways at the interface of the ETL/RPP absorber layer, and thereby suppresses unintended electron-hole recombination. The organic-inorganic bilayered ETLs also improve the electronic structure of the  $  \mathrm{SnO}_2  $  surface, which provides favorable optoelectronic properties such as quick electron extraction and elongated carrier lifetime in the device. Moreover, the hydrophobic organic semiconductor is helpful in not only growing a uniform, compact, and largely grained RPP absorber layer but also improving the stability of the perovskite absorber layer. Benefiting from the optimized bilayer ETLs, the power conversion efficiency is obviously enhanced up to  $  10.17\%  $  in the RPP-based photovoltaic devices. More importantly, the bilayer ETLs allow improved long-term stability in ambient storage of the devices, providing a viable path to design practical interfaces of efficient and stable RPP-based PSCs.

# Highlights

- Hybrid bilayer electron transport layer is designed to enhance the efficiency of Ruddlesden-Popper perovskite solar cells.

- Phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester and ITIC are inserted as  $ n $ -type organic semiconductors onto  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  film for the bilayer electron transport layer.

- Tailored optoelectronic properties of the bilayer electron transport layers deliver  $ 10.17\% $  efficiency of Ruddlesden-Popper perovskite solar cells.

# 1 | INTRODUCTION

3-Dimensional (3D) organolead-trihalide perovskites   $  \left(\mathrm{APbX}_{3},\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{ammonium}\right.  $   cation, X=I, Br, or Cl) have emerged as a promising photoactive material due to its fascinating optoelectronic properties such as high absorptivity, shallow defect levels, and long carrier lifetimes, and the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) reported for perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have rapidly enhanced to   $  >25\%  $   in a single-junction device, which is competitive with silicon or inorganic counterparts.  $  ^{1-12}  $   Meanwhile, Ruddlesden-Popper perovskites (RPPs) are another interesting class of perovskite absorber described by a formula of   $  A^{\prime}_{2}A_{n-1}B_{n}X_{3n+1}  $  , where   $  A^{\prime}  $   and A is a large organic cation (eg, n-butylammonium) and a monovalent cation (eg, methylammonium), respectively.  $  ^{13-19}  $   Because the large organic cations isolate the perovskite layers efficient charge extraction and transport in the perovskite absorber layer is limited, so the RPP-based PSCs usually exhibited relatively inferior short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ), fill factor (FF) as compared to 3D APbX  $  _{3}  $  -type PSCs. To resolve this issue, early works have studied the manipulation of the crystal structure of RPP by varying the alkylammonium or monovalent cations and its impact on the photovoltaic properties of the device.  $  ^{20-22}  $   Later, novel processing strategies such as hot-casting, additives, solvent engineering have enabled the growth of vertically oriented RPPs to optimize electrical properties of the perovskite absorber layer, which achieved improved PCE up to 21% with controlled optoelectronic properties and morphological characteristics of RPP absorber layer.  $  ^{23}  $

Apart from the materials engineering of the perovskite absorber layer, modification of interfacial charge transport layers also plays an important role in determining the charge extraction and recombination kinetics of PSCs.  $  ^{24-28}  $   In particular, the moisture/oxygen permeability of the interfaces of the device can cause serious degradation of PSCs, so suitable selection of the optimal interfacial layers is critical to increasing PCE and stability of PSCs, simultaneously. Metal oxides such as titanium dioxide (TiO  $  _{2}  $  ) or tin(IV) oxide (SnO  $  _{2}  $  ) are typical electron transporting layers (ETLs) in the device with a regular (n-i-p) configuration due to their favorable energy band structure relative to the conduction band and valence band of perovskite absorber, decent electron conductivity, and high electrochemical stability.  $  ^{29-34}  $   However, such inorganic ETLs have not shown its full potential in PSCs due to poor surface and unstable interface at the ETL/perovskite absorber caused by surface trap density of the metal oxide surface. To resolve this issue, surface engineering routes of ETLs have been vigorously studied

in 3D perovskite-based PSCs. For example, a bilayer ETL using MgO nanoparticles and SnO  $  _{2}  $  where MgO contributed to the formation of uniform perovskite film and reduction of interfacial recombination under the illumination.  $  ^{35}  $  Despite many reports having emphasized the effectiveness of the multiple metal oxide-based cascade ETL interfaces, little attention has been paid to constructing organic-inorganic cascade ETL structure in 2D RPP PSCs. The n-type organic semiconducting molecules such as phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) or ITIC can be a promising passivation material because the electron-rich functional groups, such as hydroxyl, amino, and carbonyl groups might form coordination or bond formation with oxygen vacancies of metal oxides via Lewis acid-base interaction, which effectively fills deep trap level at the interface of perovskite/ETLs in PSCs.  $  ^{36-39}  $  In this respect, Lewis acid-base interaction of the n-type organic semiconducting molecules has been studied to passivate the imperfect surface of perovskite absorber layer, but its application in the passivation of metal oxide-based ETLs of RPP-based PSCs has not been thoroughly studied yet.

In the present work, the organic-inorganic hybrid bilayer structure is successfully designed as an ETL in RPP (BA $ _2 $ MA $ _3 $ Pb $ _4 $ I $ _{13} $ )-based PSCs to optimize optoelectronic properties at the interfaces of RPP-based photovoltaic devices. In the bilayer ETLs, we adopted  $ n $ -type organic semiconductors to improve interfacial properties of SnO $ _2 $ -based ETLs, and realized a robust cascade bilayer ETL in 2D RPP (BA $ _2 $ MA $ _3 $ Pb $ _4 $ I $ _{13} $ ) absorber layer. The results studied in this work confirmed that the organic semiconductors effectively passivate defective oxygen vacancies at the surface of the SnO $ _2 $  layer, attaining efficient electron transport and carrier recombination blocking within the devices. The cascade electron transport pathway achieved by the hybrid bilayer ETLs further facilitates charge transport and reduced the electron accumulation at the interfaces. As a result, the hybrid bilayer ETL is a beneficial structure to enhance the photovoltaic performance of the RPP-based photovoltaic devices with a PCEs up to 10.17%, which outperformed the single ETL (SnO $ _2 $ )-based device (PCE = 6.52%). In addition, the organic ETL layer on the SnO $ _2 $  afforded a more hydrophobic surface, resulting in the outstanding long-term stability of the device in the ambient atmosphere up to >300 hours.

# 3 | CONCLUSIONS

We employ two n-type organic semiconductors on the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   film to form a hybrid bilayer ETL of PSCs. The bilayer ETL effectively flattens the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL, which prevents the direct contact and shunt pathway of the cathode and the perovskite interfaces. Meanwhile, the organic semiconductors effectively lower the work function of the ETLs, which thus paves the way for quick and efficient electron transfer from the RPP absorber to the cathode of the device. The bilayer ETL also benefits the passivation of defective oxygen vacancies at the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL, resulting in suppressed charge carrier recombination at the interfaces of perovskite/ETL in the device. These synergetic effects for the bilayer ETL facilitate electron extraction and suppress carrier recombination in solar cell devices. The optimized device with   $  PCBM/SnO_{2}  $   bilayer ETL structure gives rise to a promising PCE of 10.17%, with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.08 V and Jsc of 16.23 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  . In addition, the organic molecule provides a hydrophobic surface, which affords enhancement in the long-term ambient stability of 2D PSCs. We thus believe that the successful application of the organic-inorganic bilayer ETLs will provide a foothold for stable and efficient 2D PSCs that are yet to be fully explored in RP PSCs. The development of hybrid bilayer ETLs moves us a step forward to the commercialization of efficient and practical RPP-based PSCs.

# 4 | EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

# 4.1 | Materials

All the chemicals and solvents used in this work were provided by Sigma-Aldrich, Alfa Aesar, TCI chemicals, and Greatcell Solar Materials. The precursor solution of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  (20 wt% in  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $ ) was diluted to 2.67 wt% by adding deionized (DI) water. The solution was filtered by polyvinylidene difluoride filters (pore size = 0.45 μm) vigorous stirring for 3 hours without heating. The precursor solution of RPP was prepared by dissolving butylammonium iodide, methylammonium iodide, lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2) $  with a molar ratio of 2:3:4 in anhydrous dimethyl formamide at a total solid concentration of 80 wt%. The precursor solution was filtered by PTFE-D filters (pore size = 0.2 μm) after vigorous stirring at room temperature. PCBM (99.9%) and ITIC (99.9%) were

dissolved in anhydrous chlorobenzene (CB) at a concentration of 0.5 and 0.1 wt%, respectively. Spiro-OMeTAD (sublimed grade) was dissolved in CB at a concentration of 90.9 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $ , and 23  $  \mu  $  L of bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonamide lithium salt solution in acetonitrile (540 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $ ) and 39  $  \mu  $  L of 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) were mixed in the solution.

# 4.2 | Fabrication of thin-film PSCs

The ITO-coated glass substrates (sheet resistance  $ < 20\Omega/ $  sq) were cleaned by sequential ultrasonication in water, acetone, and isopropanol. After the substrates were dried by  $ \mathrm{N}_2 $  blowing, and those were treated with UV- $ \mathrm{O}_3 $  for 30 minutes. The  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  layer was formed by spin-coating onto the ITO-coated glass at  $ 3000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for 30 seconds, followed by thermal annealing in an ambient atmosphere at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 1 hour. After the substrates were cooled to room temperature, the organic  $ n $ -type semiconductors were deposited onto the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  layer by spin-coating at 3000 and  $ 1500~\mathrm{rpm} $  for PCBM and ITIC, respectively, at room temperature. The RPP layer was formed by a hot-casting technique where the substrates and the precursor solution were heated at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , respectively, before spin-coating at  $ 5000~\mathrm{rpm} $ . Spiro-OMeTAD was deposited by spin-coating at  $ 2000~\mathrm{rpm} $  for 25 seconds for the hole transport layer. Finally, the gold electrode (thickness  $ = 80~\mathrm{nm} $ ) was evaporated to complete the device in a high vacuum system ( $ < 5\times 10^{-6} $  Torr). The photoactive area of the device was defined by the shadow mask to be  $ 10~\mathrm{mm}^2 $ .

# 4.3 | Characterizations

Absorption and transmittance measurements were carried out using a spectrometer (Cary 100, Agilent). PL and its decay of RPP film were measured by spectrofluorometer (FS-5, Edinburgh Instruments) and a picosecond diode laser (EPL-405) of  $ 405\mathrm{nm} $  wavelength. The crystalline property of RPP film was measured using a diffractometer (Miniflex-300, Rigaku). The surface topography of the film was characterized using an atomic force microscope (CoreAFM, Nanosurf) in tapping mode and scanning electron microscopy (S-4800, Hitach) at the Core Facility Center for Analysis of Optoelectronic Materials and Devices of Korea Basic Science Institute.  $ J - V $  curve of the device was obtained from a parameter analyzer (4200-SCS, Keithley) under simulated illumination with an AM 1.5G filter  $ (100\mathrm{mW} / \mathrm{cm}^2) $ , which was calibrated using an NREL-certified photodiode.
