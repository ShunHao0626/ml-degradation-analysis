# 1. Introduction

The organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite material   $  \left(\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{PbX}_{3},\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{Cl}^{-},\mathrm{Br}^{-},\mathrm{I}^{-}\right)  $   has received extensive attention because of the remarkable optoelectronic properties, such as large absorption coefficients, high charge carrier mobility and long charge carrier lifetimes [1-3]. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) increased from 3.8% to 23.3% in just a few years [4-9]. The planar perovskite solar cell is widely studied due to its simpler structure and facile preparation process compared to the mesoporous device architecture, according to the positions of the electron transport layer (ETL) and the hole transport layer (HTL) with respect to the transparent electrode, planar PSCs can be classified into the conventional n-i-p structure and the inverted p-i-n structure. # ABSTRACT

A series of tetraphenylethylene-diketopyrrolopyrrole (TPE-DPP) derivatives with different alkyl chains were synthesized and characterized. The effects of alkyl chains on the electronic property of these compounds and the performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) were investigated. Cyclic voltammetry results suggest that different alkyl chains have little influence on the molecular energy levels of these TPE-DPP derivatives. AFM (atomic force microscopy) images indicate that among the derivatives studied, TPE-DPP derivative with 2-butyloctyl (TPE-DPPC12) shows the most smooth surface morphology on top of the perovskite layer, ensuring a good charge selective contact. Time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) spectra indicate that the TPE-DPPC12 shows the best electron extraction ability. As a result, the device with TPE-DPPC12 interlayer exhibited an average power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 18.37\% $ , corresponding to an increase of  $ 17\% $  relative to the control device  $ (16.37\%) $ . In addition, considering the hydrophobic nature of TPE-DPPC12, the ambient stability of inverted PSCs incorporating the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer is also improved. This study provides an effective design strategy for electron extraction materials to achieve highly-efficient PSCs with improved stability.

Fang's group designed the benzobis-(thiadiazole) (B2F) derivative as electron extraction material [24], the potential Pb–S interaction between B2F and perovskite facilitates the electrons extraction and transporting. Recently, our group developed diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP) and isoindigo (ISO)-based electron extraction materials [25], and the corresponding device exhibited a high efficiency of 18.44%. In view of these examples, it is thus reasonable to anticipate that modifying the perovskite layer through interface engineering is promising to achieve highly efficient devices [26,27].

According to literature reports, the alkyl chains on organic molecules play an important role in affecting the charge carrier mobility of molecules and molecular self-assembly in the solid films   $  [28,29]  $  . The alkyl chains engineering in dye-sensitized solar cells, polymer solar cells, and organic field effect transistors have been extensively studied   $  [30–32]  $  . However, to date, only a little effort has been devoted to systematically studying how the alkyl chains on the charge transporting materials affect the performance of PSCs. Anders et al. designed a series of hole transport materials (HTM) with different side chain lengths   $  [33]  $  . Longer alkyl chains result in poor molecular ordering in solid film, which is detrimental to intermolecular hole transport. Yang et al. used a pyridine-functionalized fullerene derivative   $  C_{60}  $  -Py as an electron transporting material (ETM)   $  [34]  $  . The chain length of the N-alkyl group significantly affects the performance of   $  C_{60}  $  -Py, and the device with   $  C_{60}  $  -BPy achieves a high PCE of 16.83%. From these examples, we can see that different alkyl chains in the charge transporting materials can significantly affect the performance of PSCs. This motivates us to explore whether the alkyl chains on the interfacial material will exert significant influence on the charge extraction properties and finally affect the performance of the PSCs.

In our previous work, we designed and synthesized tetraphenylethylene-diketopyrrolopyrrole (TPE-DPP) and tetraphenylethylene-isoindigo (TPE-ISO)-based interfacial materials, heteroatoms such as N, S atoms in DPP and ISO can effectively passivate traps on the surface of perovskite, and the device with TPE-DPP interlayer exhibited better performance than that with TPE-ISO interlayer [25]. In order to enrich the study on interfacial materials for PSCs, and to address the importance of alkyl chains in interfacial materials, herein in this work we designed and synthesized a series of TPE-DPP derivatives with different alkyl chain lengths. We find that the different alkyl chains lengths on TPE-DPP derivatives leads to different surface morphology of the solid films, and also affect the electron extraction ability of the TPE-DPP derivatives. It turned out that the device using the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer obtained an average efficiency of  $ 18.37\% $ , with an open circuit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{oc}}) $  of  $ 1.05\mathrm{V} $ , a short-circuit current density  $ (J_{\mathrm{sc}}) $  of  $ 22.24\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  and a high fill factor (FF) of  $ 79\% $ . The average efficiency of devices using TPE-DPPC6, TPE-DPPC8, and TPE-DPPC16 interlayers are  $ 16.86\% $ ,  $ 17.89\% $ , and  $ 16.84\% $ , respectively. The control device without the interlayer exhibited an average PCE of  $ 16.37\% $ . Moreover, considering the hydrophobic property of the organic molecules, the ambient stability of inverted PSCs incorporating the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer is improved. These results demonstrate that the length of the alkyl chains affects the electron extraction ability of the interfacial materials, and highly efficient inverted PSCs could be achieved by rational design of interfacial materials.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Materials and instruments

Unless otherwise stated, all starting materials were purchased from commercial suppliers (Sigma–Aldrich and Energy Chemical) and used without further purification, including P3CT (Rieke Metals),   $  PbI_{2}  $   (p-OLED, >99.99%), MAI (p-OLED, ≥99.5%), PCBM (p-OLED, ≥99%),

$  C_{60}  $  (p-OLED), BCP (p-OLED), DMF (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.8%), DMSO (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.8%) and CB (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.8%). Tetrahydrofuran (THF) and toluene were freshly distilled over Na–K alloy under Ar atmosphere before usage. Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were obtained from a BRUKER AVANCE III 600 MHz NMR Instrument. MALDI-TOF HRMS was performed on a Bruker Autoflex instrument, using 1,8,9-trihydroxyanthracene as a matrix. UV/Vis absorption spectra were recorded on a Shimadzu UV-2450 absorption spectrophotometer. Fluorescence spectra were measured on a Hitachi F-4600 FL Spectrophotometer. The PL lifetimes were measured by a single photon counting spectrometer from Edinburgh Instruments (FLS920) with a Picosecond Pulsed UV-LASTER (LASTER377) as the excitation source. Field-emission SEM (FE-SEM) images were taken on JSM-7800F. AFM images were collected in air on a Bruker AFM using a tapping mode. Thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA) was performed on a TGA Q50 at a heating rate of  $  10^{\circ}\text{C min}^{-1}  $  under a nitrogen atmosphere. Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) measurement was performed on a DSC Q20 instrument at a heating rate of  $  10^{\circ}\text{C min}^{-1}  $  under a nitrogen atmosphere.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

The indium tin oxide (ITO) glass substrates were sequentially washed by sonication using detergent, deionized water, ethanol and acetone. The hole transporting layer P3CT-Na was formed on ITO substrates by spin coating at 4000 rpm for 60 s followed by annealing at 140 °C for 30 min. Then the samples were transferred into a N₂-filled glovebox. A CH₃NH₃PbI₃ precursor solution (1.4 M in DMF: DMSO mixed solution with a v/v of 4:1) was spin-coated in a two-step program at 400 and 5000 rpm for 3 and 30 s, respectively. During the second step, 200 μL of chlorobenzene was poured on the spinning substrate at 10 s after the start-up. Next, the as-spun perovskite layer was annealed on a hot plate at 60 °C for 1 min and at 80 °C for 2 min to drive off solvent and form the perovskite phase. Then TPE-DPP derivatives solutions in chlorobenzene were spin-coated (5000 rpm) onto the perovskite layer. To fabricate ITO/P3CT-Na/Perovskite/TPE-DPP series/C₆₀/BCP/Ag device, C₆₀ (40 nm) and BCP (6 nm) were evaporated under high vacuum on top of the interlayer. Finally, a 100 nm thick Ag electrode was deposited through a shadow mask. The active area of our device is 0.09 cm².

Hysteresis effect of the  $ J - V $  curves is measured by scanning from forward and reverse directions. As shown in Fig. 4b, the control device not containing the interlayer exhibits a small hysteresis, and further eliminated hysteresis is observed after deposition of the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer. Similarly, hysteresis effect of inverted PSCs based on the other three TPE-DPP derivatives is shown in Fig. S5 (ESI†), and negligible hysteresis is observed. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of the devices based on different interlayers is presented in Fig. 4c. For the TPE-DPPC12-based device and the control device, the integrated short-circuit current density values obtained from the EQE spectra were  $ 21.36\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  and  $ 20.27\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , which are consistent with the values obtained from the  $ J - V $  curves. Fig. 4d shows the  $ J_{sc} $  and PCE measured at the maximum power output point (MPP) of the champion device containing the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer. A stabilized PCE of  $ 18.61\% $  and  $ J_{sc} $  of  $ 21.03\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  were observed, confirming that the device has a stable output. In addition, the PCE distribution statistics of the control device and the device with TPE-DPPC12 interlayer are recorded, and the results are shown in Fig. S6 (ESI†). The respective standard deviations for the control devices and the devices with TPE-DPPC12 interlayer are both on the order of  $ 2\% $  of the PCE, indicating that both devices show good reproducibility. The results further demonstrate the excellent performance and good reproducibility of devices using the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer.

To further demonstrate that the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer has good electron extraction ability, steady state and time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) spectra were recorded. Fig. 5a shows the steady state PL spectra of the neat perovskite film, perovskite/TPE-DPPC12 and perovskite/TPE-DPPC12/ $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  films. The neat perovskite film on the quartz substrate exhibited a high PL intensity with a peak locating at about  $ 779\mathrm{nm} $ . After covering the perovskite film with TPE-DPPC12 interlayer, significant PL quenching was observed, indicating efficient electron extraction from perovskite to the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer. Further quenching was observed when  $ \mathrm{C}_{60} $  was coated on top of the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer, indicating faster electron extraction and

transport after depositing the   $  C_{60}  $   ETL. Fig. 5b shows the time-resolved PL spectra of neat perovskite, perovskite/TPE-DPPC12 and perovskite/TPE-DPPC12/  $  C_{60}  $   films. The fitted PL lifetimes for neat perovskite film, perovskite/TPE-DPPC12 and perovskite/TPE-DPPC12/  $  C_{60}  $   were 235.72 ns, 99.41 ns and 5.30 ns, respectively. The results suggest that spin-coating the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer on perovskite film facilitates electron extraction. Time-resolved PL spectra of the other TPE-DPP derivatives are shown in Fig. S8 (ESI†). Among the TPE-DPP series studied, perovskite/TPE-DPPC12 exhibits the shortest PL lifetime, indicating the more efficient electron extraction ability of TPE-DPPC12. Moreover, compared to the other derivatives studied in this work, a smoother surface morphology of the TPE-DPPC12 film can provide a better interfacial contact between the perovskite layer and the   $  C_{60}  $   electron transporting layer, which also contributes to a good electron extraction ability of TPE-DPPC12. Combining these factors, TPE-DPPC12 exhibits the best electron extraction ability among the TPE-DPP-based derivatives studied. The results are consistent with the excellent photovoltaic performances of the TPE-DPPC12-based device.

In order to study the long-term stability of PSCs incorporating the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer, device performances were investigated in air with a controlled humidity of 25–30%. As shown in Fig. 6, the PCE of the PSCs with TPE-DPPC12 interlayer maintained 80% of the initial value after 600 h, while the control device without interlayer maintained only 70% of the initial value. The device with TPE-DPPC12 interlayer exhibits better long-term stability than the control device, which is attributed to passivation of the traps on the perovskite film and hydrophobicity of the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer. The good hydrophobicity of the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer is beneficial for long-term stability, which can prevent the perovskite layer from being destroyed by moisture in the air. To address this issue, water contact angles of perovskite/TPE-DPPC12 and perovskite films were measured. As shown in the inset of Fig. 6, the contact angles of perovskite/TPE-DPPC12 and perovskite were 98.9° and 44.3°, respectively. Consequently, the hydrophobicity is prominently improved after deposition of the TPE-DPPC12 interlayer on top of the perovskite layer, and thus the stability of the corresponding PSCs is enhanced.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we synthesized and characterized a series of electron extraction materials based on TPE-DPP derivatives and applied them in inverted PSCs. These molecules have the same backbone structure but are different from the alkyl chain lengths. Although the different side chains do not affect the LUMO energy levels of these molecules, they have a significant effect on the surface morphology as well as the electron extraction ability of the TPE-DPP derivatives. Perovskite film covered by the TPE-DPP-based interlayers show a smoother film morphology than the neat perovskite film, ensuring a better interfacial contact between the perovskite layer and the   $  C_{60}  $   electron transporting layer, which also contributes to a good electron extraction ability. Moreover, the N, S atoms in DPP can effectively deactivate defects on the surface of perovskite, thereby enhancing the performance of PSCs. As a result, TPE-DPP derivative with 2-butyloctyl (TPE-DPPC12) offers the highest PCE of 18.93% among the derivatives studied. This work also provides an effective strategy for the design of interfacial materials.
