# Abstract

At present, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have shown superior photovoltaic performance with a high power conversion efficiency (PCE) over 22%. However, there are very few devices with high PCE and high stability simultaneously. In this study, we fabricated the PSCs made from   $  (\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3})_{0.85}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})_{0.15}  $   by using non-volatile Lewis base acetamide   $  (\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{CONH}_{2})  $   as the additive. Compared to the reference device, the device with 5 mg/mL   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   displays markedly improved PCE of 19.01% and less hysteresis, which are caused by the high-quality film with better crystal structure, evidently larger grain size and higher thickness. In addition, the device with   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $

exhibits better humidity and heat stability. The device without sealed can maintain about 70% and 50% of its starting PCE under around 50% and 80% relative humidity (RH) for 1000 h and 700 h, respectively. Meanwhile, the PCE of the unsealed device with   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   can retain about 80% and 60% at 60 °C and 85 °C for 200 h and 150 h, respectively. These results clearly show that   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   as an additive can promote the crystal growth and enhance grain size of perovskite thin films and introducing a suitable additive can realize to simultaneously improve the PCE and stability of PSCs.

Keywords: perovskite solar cells, acetamide additive, high performance, enhanced stability

# Introduction

Recently, perovskite materials based on the general form of ABX $ _3 $  (A= CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3^+ $ , HC(NH $ _2 $ ) $ _2^+ $  or Cs $ ^+ $ ; B =Pb $ ^{2+} $  and X = Cl $ ^- $ , Br $ ^- $  or I $ ^- $ ) have displayed outstanding optoelectronic properties (desirable band gap, high absorption coefficient, long exciton and charge diffusion lengths and low exciton binding energy), which have made them attractive for fabricating low-cost photovoltaic devices. $ ^{1-6} $  At present, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have gained high power conversion efficiency (PCE) over 22%, made them become the hope of the next generation of photovoltaic technology. $ ^7 $

Unfortunately, perovskite materials have been reported to suffer from instability upon exposure to moisture and heat, which has hindered the realistic popularization of PSCs.  $  ^{8-11}  $   Numerous attempts have been suggested to improve the stability of PSCs against moisture and heat, such as using the dopant-free or inorganic hole-transporting materials (HTM),  $  ^{12-14}  $   introducing the interfacial layer,  $  ^{15,16}  $   replacing the electron transport layer (ETL),  $  ^{17,18}  $   incorporating Rb and Cs cations,  $  ^{19,20}  $   and adding the

In recent several years, more and more additives have been used to improve the PCE and stability of PSCs by enhancing morphology and crystallinity.  $  ^{23-30}  $   For example, Lee et. al. reported using a bifunctional non-volatile Lewis base additive urea can retard crystal growth and enhance crystallinity, resulting in the improvement of PCE.  $  ^{31}  $   Fu et. al. introduced 3-hydroxypyridine molecules to dip the perovskite layer and greatly improved the long-term stability of solar cells.  $  ^{32}  $   Via adding 2-pyridylthiourea additive into the perovskite precursor solution, Sun et. al. prepared highly efficient and stable PSCs in ambient air due to the promoted crystal growth.  $  ^{33}  $   Wu et. al. found that an additive engineering strategy can improve the PCE and the durability of PSCs by modulating the film crystalline quality using an ionic-liquid additive of methylammonium acetate and a molecular additive of thio-semicarbazide.  $  ^{34}  $   Moreover, polyvinyl ammonium (PVAm·HI), polyvinyl alcohol (PVA), fullerene derivatives, etc. have been used as additives to enhance PCE and stability.  $  ^{35-38}  $   Whereas, it still retains a question to simultaneously improve the PCE and stability against humidity and temperature.

Herein, we firstly introduced a Lewis base acetamide (CH $ _3 $ CONH $ _2 $ ) as the additive to fabricate PSCs for improving the PCE and stability. It can be found that the addition of CH $ _3 $ CONH $ _2 $  promotes grain growth and enhances crystallinity of perovskite films, resulting in the improvement of grain size and film thickness, and then the enhancement of optical and electrical properties. The PCE of device with 5 mg/mL CH $ _3 $ CONH $ _2 $  is obviously enhanced from 16.44% (control device) to 19.01%. More importantly, the device with CH $ _3 $ CONH $ _2 $  exhibits outstanding humidity and heat stability. After over 1000 h and 700 h under 50% and 80% relative humidity (RH), it can retain about 70% and 50%

We measured the Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra to ascertain the interaction of C=O bond in   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   with   $  Pb^{2+}  $   in perovskite. The FTIR spectra of   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   and perovskite powders with and without   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   are shown in Fig. 1b and Fig. S1. It is clear that the characteristic peak of C=O bond in   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   appears at wavenumber of   $  1680\ cm^{-1}  $   (stretching vibration of C–O). Whereas, the perovskite sample with   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   exhibits characteristic peak of C=O bond at wavenumber of   $  1658\ cm^{-1}  $  . The existence and shift of the C=O stretching vibration peak from   $  1680\ cm^{-1}  $   in   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   to   $  1658\ cm^{-1}  $   in   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $ -treated perovskite indicate that the   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   has been incorporated into perovskite film and interacted with   $  Pb^{2+}  $   in perovskite. This interaction is beneficial to form the nucleation center around   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   and promote perovskite layer uniform growth. Furthermore, we also performed X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) of O 1s and Pb 4f of perovskite films with and without   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   as shown in Fig. S2. Compared to the film without   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $ , the Pb 4f peaks of the film with   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   exhibit obviously shift which clearly reveals the interaction between   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   and   $  Pb^{2+}  $   in perovskite.

From the above results, it can be clearly found that the films with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CONH}_2 $  have better crystal structure, larger grain size and the devices with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CONH}_2 $  present better photovoltaic performance and stability than that without  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CONH}_2 $ . However, the detailed reasons and precise mechanism are still unclear. Here, we supposed the possible mechanism as displayed in Fig. 7, which shows the schematic process of the perovskite crystal growth with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CONH}_2 $  and the structure of corresponding PSCs. As for high quality film with  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CONH}_2 $ , it is probably attributed to the reasons below: (1) Due to the solubilization effect, adding  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CONH}_2 $  can promote the dissolution of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  which can make  $ \mathrm{FA}^+ $  ( $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $ ) and  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  simultaneously and slowly separate out to form uniform and dense perovskite layer and decrease the crystallization rate of perovskite. (2) The electron pair on

oxygen atom of the C=O bond in   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   has a strong coordination with   $  Pb^{2+}  $ , which can form the nucleation centers around   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   and make perovskite layer slow and uniform growth. The slow crystallization implies high Gibbs free energy, which reduces the formation of nucleation centers.  $  ^{31}  $   As a result, perovskite crystals slowly and uniformly grow into high-quality film with large grain size around a small number of nucleation centers. To further analysis the reasons of improved stability, we supposed that: On the one hand, the high-quality perovskite layer has played a significant role; On the other hand,   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   exists around perovskite layer after annealing and interact with   $  Pb^{2+}  $   which can protect the perovskite layer from its decomposition due to water molecules infiltration and high temperature. Hence, the devices with high-quality perovskite films have superior photovoltaic performance and high stability by adding the   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $ .

# Conclusion

In conclusion, we have successfully introduced a Lewis base   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   additive into perovskite for promoting grain growth and enhancing crystallinity. It can be found that the perovskite films with   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   have better crystal structure, larger grain size, higher thickness and the improvement of optical and electrical properties. As a result, the device with 5 mg/mL   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   displays superior photovoltaic performance with improved PCE from 16.44% to 19.01% and less hysteresis. Especially, the unsealed device with   $  CH_{3}CONH_{2}  $   has enhanced moisture resistance under 50% and 80% RH for 1000 h and 700 h and better heat stability at 60 °C and 85 °C for 200 h and 150 h. This work presents that introducing a suitable additive into perovskite is an effective strategy for promoting perovskite films growth and further enhancing the photovoltaic performance and stability of PSCs.

All the chemicals and reagents were purchased from TCI, Sigma-Aldrich and Sinopharm, and were used as received without further purification. Formamidinium iodide (FAI) was prepared by reacting hydriodic acid (0.1 mol, 57wt% in water, sigma-aldrich) and formamidine acetate (0.1 mol, 98%, Sinopharm) in a round-bottom flask at 0 °C for 2~3 h with stirring. The solvent and excess hydriodic acid were removed by rotary evaporation at 60 °C under vacuum, leading to a white powder. After sufficient drying, the powder was further purified twice with ethanol and washed by diethyl ether, then the white solid was collected by air pump filtration. Finally, the powder was sufficiently dried at 60 °C for 12 h in a vacuum oven.

# Device fabrication

The devices were prepared on patterned FTO substrate which was cleaned by ultrasonication in detergent, ultrapure water and ethanol. Using air as a carrier gas, a compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was coated on the FTO by a spray pyrolysis method at 430 °C, the solution contained 0.6 mL titanium diisopropoxide and 0.4 mL bis(acetylacetonate) in 7 mL isopropanol. Then, the substrate was heated at 430 °C for 30-40 min. By diluting the   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste (30 nm, dyesol) using anhydrous ethanol (  $  TiO_{2}  $  :ethanol =1:5.5 by weight), a mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was spin-coated on the substrate at 4000 rpm for 20 s and then the substrate was annealed at 510 °C for 1 h. The perovskite layers were deposited on the mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer by one step spin-coating method in an air flowing glovebox (The relative humidity was remained about 20%.) at firstly 1100 rpm for 11 s, secondly 4600 rpm for 33 s. A moderate amount of chlorobenzene was carefully drop-added to the surface of the perovskite during the spin coating step 15 s before the end of the procedure. # Characterization

XRD patterns of the perovskite films were executed on an X'Pert MPD PRO (PANalytical). The 2θ range was 10–70° at room temperature. The spectral data in the 4000 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  -500 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   range were collected on a FTIR (Thermo Fisher IS50R, USA). XPS measurements were conducted on a Thermo ESCALAB 250Xi system. A high-resolution field-emission SEM (FE-SEM, sirion200, FEI Corp., Holland) with a Schottky Field Emission gun was used to record the perovskite film morphologies. UV–vis absorption spectra were measured by an ultraviolet–vis spectrophotometer (U-3900H, HITACHI, Japan). The measurement range was from 300 nm to 900 nm. Steady-state PL spectra were tested on a spectrofluorometer (photon technology international) and studied by the software Fluorescence. A standard 450 W xenon CW lamp with an exciting wavelength of 473 nm was used to excite the samples. J–V curves were executed on a solar simulator (Newport, Oriel Class A, 91195A) with a source meter (Keithley 2420) at 100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $   illumination AM 1.5G. Before the measurements, the setup was calibrated by a certified silicon solar cell (Fraunhofer ISE). The active area of the device was remained at 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $   by masking a black mask.
