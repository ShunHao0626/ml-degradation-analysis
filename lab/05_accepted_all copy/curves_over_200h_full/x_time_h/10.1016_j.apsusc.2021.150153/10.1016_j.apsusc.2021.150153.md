High power conversion efficiency (PCE) and good long-term stability are everlasting pursuit for perovskite solar cells. We present here   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite film deposited by vacuum co-evaporation of CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $  . By optimizing evaporation rate ratio of the two precursor and annealing temperature, perovskite films with large grain and high crystallinity are obtained. Furthermore, by regulating spinning speed of   $  TiO_{2}  $   precursor solution and thickness of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film, a best PCE of 9.43% is recorded under 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $   illumination, providing a significant enhancement in contrast with 6.26% of device based on spin-coating method. Additionally, the PCE of resulting perovskite solar cell without encapsulation remains at 96% of initial value after storing for 480 h under humidity of 40% in air. Our findings in this work deliver an effective strategy to fabricate high-performance inorganic perovskite solar cells.

# 1. Introduction

Solar cells based on organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite as an emerging field have aroused extensive attention owing to the excellent photoelectronic properties of perovskites and cost-effective fabrication in recent years   $  [1-3]  $  . Since power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 3.8% is obtained with   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbX_{3}  $   (X = Br, I) as sensitizer applied in dye-sensitized solar cells in 2009   $  [4]  $  , perovskite solar cell has reached a record-certified efficiency of 25.5% so far   $  [5]  $  , becoming a powerful competitor of silicon-based solar cells. Unfortunately, the terrible long-term stability under elevated moisture, temperature, oxygen and light exposure hinders commercialization of perovskite solar cells   $  [6-9]  $  . An effective strategy to improve operation stability of perovskite solar cells is to dope or substitute organic cations with inorganic counterpart, such as   $  Cs^{+}  $  . Device based on   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   with a desirable bandgap of   $  \sim1.7~eV  $   is recognized as the most potential all-inorganic perovskite solar cells, and the champion efficiency has beyond 20%   $  [10]  $  . However, the black perovskite phase of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   is unstable and easily transform to non-perovskite phase under room temperature owing to low tolerance.

factor. Alternatively, devices based on   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   exhibit excellent tolerance to moisture, heat and light [11]. In 2015, perovskite solar cells with structure of   $  FTO/c-TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}/HTM/Au  $   were reported firstly by Cahen and Hodes, and the best efficiency of 5.95% was obtained [12]. But the large bandgap of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   and serious defects in device block further development of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $ -based solar cells. To solve these problems, various strategies were taken to improve the PCE. P3HT was employed to modify the interface of   $  CsPbBr_{3}/carbon  $   in carbon-based device, reduced charge recombination and enhanced hole extraction increase PCE by 27% [13]. Lan and co-workers prepared smooth   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films with high crystallinity and suitable energy levels by adding   $  NH_{4}SCN  $   in precursor solution, achieving an optimized device efficiency of 8.47% [14]. Tang et al. adopted a multi-step solution process method to obtain high-purity perovskite film. With the modification of graphene quantum dots, solar cells displayed a PCE as high as 9.72% [15]. Subsequently, the same group refreshed the device efficiency to 10.85% by coupling alkyl-chain-regulated quantum dot hole-conductors and fluorescent electrode [16]. At present, the prevalent preparation method of perovskite solar cells is based on solution process, following toxicity of organic solvent and complex operation of anti-solvent   $  [17,18]  $  . Moreover, pin-holes are easily generated with solvent evaporation during spin-coating and annealing treatment, giving imperfect surface coverage. Compared with spin-coating, vacuum evaporation is a technique that precursor materials are sublimated under high vacuum and deposited onto the substrate, presenting perovskite films with high density, uniformity, and reproducibility   $  [19–22]  $  . Meanwhile, vacuum evaporation is a potential route to fabricate perovskite solar cells on commercial scale, as it has been proved by OLED industry. Sequential vacuum evaporation of   $  PbBr_{2}  $   and CsBr, and single-source evaporation of precursor mixture have been proved to be effective approaches to prepare high-performance   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  -based devices   $  [23,24]  $  . However, there are relative few studies about dual-source co-evaporation of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite films.

In this work, we successfully construct a solar cell incorporating a dual-source co-evaporated   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite absorber. By optimizing evaporation rate ratio of CsBr to   $  PbBr_{2}  $ , annealing temperature, thickness of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film and   $  TiO_{2}  $   electron transport layer, the resulting PCE of 9.43% is realized with device construction of FTO/  $  TiO_{2}/CsPbBr_{3}/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au  $ .

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Materials

CsBr (  $  \geq99.9\%  $  ),   $  PbBr_{2}  $   (>99.99%), Spiro-OMeTAD (  $  \geq99.8\%  $  ), TBP (>96%), Li-TFSI (>99%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corporation. All the other reagents were purchased from Aladdin Reagent (Shanghai, China).

# 2.2. Substrate preparation

The patterned FTO conductive glass (15   $  \Omega  $   sq  $  ^{-1}  $  ) was cleaned by sequential sonication in deionized water, acetone, ethanol, isopropanol for 15 min each. After drying by   $  N_{2}  $  , the FTO substrate was treated by UV/Ozone cleaning for 15 min. To fabricate compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer, an ethanol solution containing 0.5 M titanium isopropoxide and 0.5 M diethanol amine was spin-coated on the FTO substrate with various spinning speed (500–1100 rpm) for 30 s, followed by sintering in a muffle furnace at 500 °C for 30 min. Then, the   $  TiO_{2}  $  -coated FTO substrate was treated by UV/Ozone cleaning for another 10 min for standby.

# 2.3. Perovskite films fabrication

CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $  perovskite was deposited by vacuum co-evaporation of CsBr and PbBr  $  _{2}  $  via a vacuum evaporation system (Trovato 300C) integrated in a glovebox under  $  \sim10^{-7}  $  Torr. PbBr  $  _{2}  $  was evaporated at a constant rate of 0.5 Å s  $  ^{-1}  $ , and CsBr was simultaneously evaporated with different rate ratio to PbBr  $  _{2}  $  (1:3–3:1). The thickness of CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $  film (300–600 nm) was determined by respective thickness of two precursor materials. After evaporation, CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $  perovskite film was annealed at various temperature (250–320 °C) for 20 min in a N  $  _{2}  $ -filled glovebox.

For comparison,   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite film was also prepared by a multistep spin-coating method [25]. Firstly,   $  PbBr_{2}  $   solution (1 M in DMF) was spin-coated on   $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate at 2000 rpm for 30 s. After annealing at 90 °C for 1 h, CsBr solution (0.07 M in methanol) was spin-coated on   $  PbBr_{2}  $   film at 2000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 250 °C for 5 min. In order to obtain the similar thickness (  $  \sim  $  500 nm) as the co-evaporated film, the above process was repeated for 6 cycles. Finally, the obtained film was annealed at 250 °C for another 5 min.

# 2.4. Device preparation

To fabricate a whole perovskite solar cell device, a Spiro-OMeTAD solution was spin-coated on the annealed   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film with 3000 rpm for 30 s as hole transport layer under   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere. Spiro-OMeTAD solution was prepared by adding 35   $  \mu  $  L Li-TFSI acetonitrile solution (520 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) and 57.6   $  \mu  $  L TBP into 2 mL Spiro-OMeTAD chlorobenzene solution (80 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ). Finally, a 100-nm thick Au layer was deposited by vacuum evaporation. The active area of the device is 0.04 cm  $  ^{2}  $  .

# 2.5. Characterization

The crystal structures of the fabricated perovskite film were analyzed by X-ray diffractometer (XRD) (Rigaku, Ultima IV) with a Cu Kα radiation at 40 kV and 30 mA. The optical absorption properties of films were detected by UV-VIS-NIR spectrophotometer (Shimadzu, UV-3600). The top-view morphologies of the resultant films were collected by field-emission scanning electron microscope (SEM) (JEOL, JSM-7500F). The thickness of spin-coated perovskite film was detected by profile-system (Bruker, Dektak-XT). The steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra were recorded by a fluorescence spectrophotometer (Edinburgh, FLS980). The electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) of devices were characterized by electrochemical workstation (Autolab, PGSTAT 302N) performed between the range of 1 Hz–100 KHz under dark condition. The J-V characteristics were measured by a solar simulator (Newport, 94023A) with a Keithley 2420 source meter under AM 1.5G illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). The incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) was measured on a QE system (Newport).

# 3. Results and discussion

Fig. 1(a) shows schematic diagram of vacuum co-evaporation method to fabricate  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  film by CsBr and  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $ . CsBr and  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  are respectively placed in BN crucibles, the resulting stoichiometry and thickness of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  films can be accurate controlled by adjusting evaporation rate and thickness of CsBr and  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $ . As depicted in Fig. 1(b),  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  perovskite layer is deposited on an FTO conductive glass coated  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  electron transport layer, followed by spiro-OMeTAD as hole transport layer and Au electrode to construct a typical n-i-p structure.

Evaporation rate of precursor material is a critical parameter in the process of nucleation and growth for perovskite film. Here the relationship of quality for   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite film without annealing and evaporation rate ratio of   $  CsBr/PbBr_{2}  $   is discussed. The evaporation rate of   $  PbBr_{2}  $   remains unchanged at   $  0.5\ \AA\ s^{-1}  $  , and the film properties are regulated by varying the evaporation rate of CsBr. As shown in Fig. 2(a), a phase conversion occurs during the process of increasing evaporation rate of CsBr. When   $  CsBr/PbBr_{2} < 1:1  $  ,   $  CsPb_{2}Br_{5}  $   phase dominates the film. With keeping elevating the evaporation rate of CsBr,   $  CsPb_{2}Br_{5}  $   reacts with CsBr to produce   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   (PDF#18-0364) perovskite with high photovoltaic activity. Further investigation of the dependence of crystallinity of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite films with various annealing temperatures can be seen in Fig. 2(c). The characteristic peak positions of perovskite films are almost unchanged in the range of 260–320 °C. However, the preferred crystal orientation change from (110) to (200) after thermal annealing, demonstrating a more stable structure [29]. Moreover, with elevating annealing temperature, the diffraction intensities gradually increase, and reach the peak at 300 °C, indicating that the crystallinity of perovskite film has been enhanced. From the above, we can conclude that the optimum perovskite film can be prepared with evaporation rate ratio of   $  CsBr/PbBr_{2}  $   of 1:1 and annealing temperature of 300 °C, and we follow this procedure for the rest of our study.

As a vital component in a perovskite solar cell, electron transport layer has a significant effect on photovoltaic performance of the device. Here devices with structure of FTO/TiO $ _2 $ /CsPbBr $ _3 $ /Spiro-OMeTAD/Au are constructed. Fig. 3(a) shows the  $ J $ -V curves of the perovskite solar cells with different spinning speeds of TiO $ _2 $  precursor solution, and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table 1. The higher the spinning speed, the thinner the thickness is. When TiO $ _2 $  precursor solution is spin-coated at 1100 rpm, FTO substrate may be not completely covered, leading to a direct contact between perovskite and FTO, and inevitably creating serious leakage currents. If an excessive thick TiO $ _2 $  film is applied with spinning speed at 500 rpm, there will be a competition between TiO $ _2 $  and CsPbBr $ _3 $  perovskite film in light absorption, generating less charge carriers. Furthermore, electron diffusion path length will extend with thicker TiO $ _2 $  film, and increase the series resistance and probability of recombination. The optimum spinning speed of TiO $ _2 $  precursor solution is 700 rpm with the thickness of 65 nm determined by a step profiler, and a PCE of 9.35%, a short-current density ( $ J_{sc} $ ) of 8.90 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , an open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{oc} $ ) of 1.351 V, and a fill factor ( $ FF $ ) of 77.80% is obtained when applied in a cell device.

To further enhance performances of perovskite solar cells,   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films are vacuum evaporated with different thickness. The corresponding J-V characteristics are demonstrated in Fig. 3(b) and Table 2, respectively. Increasing the thickness of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film from 300 to 600 nm leads to a significant improvement of the   $  J_{sc}  $  . This is because the thicker the perovskite film, the more incident light it can absorb, thus generating more photocarriers and resulting a higher   $  J_{sc}  $  . We can also find that no further enhancement in   $  V_{oc}  $   and FF is observed with film thickness exceeds 400 nm, which is likely hindered by the increased charge diffusion path length and series resistance. After comparison tests, a champion efficiency of 9.43% can be reached with thickness of perovskite film at 500 nm.

Furthermore, space-charge-limited-current (SCLC) method is applied to evaluated trap density ( $ N_{\text{trp}} $ ). The dark  $ I-V $  curves of electron-only device with structure of FTO/TiO $ _2 $ /CsPbBr $ _3 $ /PCBM/Au is presented in Fig. 5(c). Generally,  $ N_{\text{trp}} $  can be calculated by the formula:  $ N_{\text{trp}} = 2\varepsilon\varepsilon_0 V_{\text{TFL}}/eL^2 $ , where  $ \varepsilon $  is the relative dielectric constant of perovskite,  $ \varepsilon_0 $  is the vacuum permittivity,  $ V_{\text{TFL}} $  is the trap-filled limit voltage,  $ e $  is the elementary charge, L is the thickness of perovskite film [32]. Because other parameters are same, co-evaporated perovskite with lower  $ V_{\text{TFL}} $  shows reduced trap density, which is in good agree with PL spectra.

EIS measurements are also carried out to understand carrier transport behaviors in perovskite solar cells and the Nyquist plots of devices based on various methods are shown in Fig. 5(d). Fitted from the low-frequency arc by a commonly used equivalent circuit model, the charge recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ), demonstrating the recombination of charge carriers in the perovskite layer [33,34], are 1.35 kΩ·cm  $  ^{2}  $   and 1.62

kΩ·cm  $  ^{2}  $  for devices based on spin-coated and vacuum co-evaporation. The larger  $  R_{rec}  $  indicates vacuum co-evaporation-based device suppresses the charge recombination more effectively, and hence enhances the device performance.

The  $ J - V $  curves of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  perovskite solar cells based on various preparation methods are provided in Fig. 6(a). On the basis of above discussions, the enhancement in photovoltaic performance for device applying vacuum co-evaporated  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  film can be ascribed to the effective charge transport and reduced trap-state density in perovskite solar cells.
