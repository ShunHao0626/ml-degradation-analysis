# Water-Based  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  Nanocrystal as Electronic Transport Layer for Operationally Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Yao Zhao, Zhengyuan Han, Wenke Zhou, Qi Li, Rui Fu, Dapeng Yu and Qing Zhao*

Y. Zhao, Z. Y. Han, W. K. Zhou, Q. Li, R. Fu, Prof. D. P. Yu, Prof. Q. Zhao

State Key Laboratory for Mesoscopic Physics, School of Physics, Peking University, Beijing 100871, China.

Collaborative Innovation Center of Quantum Matter, Beijing 100084, China. E-mail: zhaoqing@pku.edu.cn

Keywords:  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , green solvent, perovskite solar cells, interface passivation, operational stability

Abstract

Halide perovskite solar cells provide a new opportunity for next-generation photovoltaic applications. However, traditional low temperature solution-processed  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  electron transport layer based PSCs show an inferior stability than solar cells based on high temperature (typically  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ )  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , but high temperature process is energy consuming and not compatible with flexible device processing. Traditional  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanoparticles made from titanium tetrachloride dispersed in organic solvent usually have many organic molecules attached on its surface which would lead to the formation of deep level defect states during long-term operation. Here, an environmental-friendly, water-based Cl passivated  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanoparticles (W- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ ) was invented (W- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ ) and surface organic molecules were removed by vacuum-rotary evaporation process. W- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  based PSCs could reach  $ 20.5\% $  power conversion efficiency with reduced hysteresis and could maintain  $ 80\% $  of its initial performance after 500 hours continuous operation under 1-sun illumination at max power point. This improved performance is ascribed to the organic-molecules-free and Cl passivated surface. The water based  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanoparticles dispersion also offers a convenient and universal way to introduce other passivation agent to further improve the photovoltaic performance of PSCs.

ABX $ _3 $  (A=FA, MA, Cs; B=Pb, Sn; X = Cl, Br, or I) perovskite possesses many distinct properties, such as tunable band gap, long carrier diffusion length, long carrier lifetime and simple fabrication process, making it an ideal material for photovoltaic applications. $ ^{[1-9]} $  The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) increases from 3.8% to 24.2% within ten years. $ ^{[10-13]} $  Even though remarkable PCE has been reached nowadays, stability issue still remains the biggest challenge in perovskite based photovoltaic devices. PSC stability involves lots of factors: such as ion migration in perovskite, ultraviolet photocatalytic properties of electron transport layer (ETL) TiO $ _2 $  and unstable hole transport layer of Spiro-OMeTAD, etc. $ ^{[14-20]} $  Among them, imperfect interface between TiO $ _2 $  and perovskite is one of the key limiting factor for device stability due to their mismatched energy band and high defect density. $ ^{[21-24]} $  To solve this problem, interface passivation upon ETL and perovskite has been proposed. You et al. modified high temperature TiO $ _2 $  with biopolymer heparin sodium, 20.1% PCE and enhanced stability has been reached using MAPbI $ _3 $  as light absorption layer. $ ^{[25]} $  Generally, solar cells using high-temperature processed TiO $ _2 $  own superior stability and PCE than its low-temperature counterpart devices because TiO $ _2 $  have better crystallization state under relatively high temperature (typically 500°C). However, high-temperature processed ETL makes manufacture more complicated and inhibits the development of flexible modules and perovskite-based monolithic tandem devices. $ ^{[26-27]} $  Therefore, it is of great importance to modify low temperature processed TiO $ _2 $  nanoparticles with passivant to mitigate the inferior crystallization quality impact on PSCs' stability. For example, Zhou et al. modified TiO $ _2 $  nanoparticle with Yttrium and Titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) to improve PCE from 16.6% to 19.3%, with a rather inferior stability of PSC devices. $ ^{[28]} $  Zhou et al. modified TiO $ _2 $  with C $ _{60} $ , and flexible PSC with good stability has been achieved, while the PCE is only 16% due to the reduced J $ _{sc} $  from C $ _{60} $ 's light absorption effect. $ ^{[29]} $  Tan et al. modified TiO $ _2 $  nanoparticles with Cl ligand to optimize band structure of TiO $ _2 $  and 20.9% PCE has been fulfilled. $ ^{[30]} $  The as-prepared TiO $ _2 $  nanoparticles were dispersed

in methanol and chloroform mixed solvent, which is volatile, toxic and would have potential danger to experiment operator. In addition, this strategy has special requirements to the quality of titanium tetrachloride. For  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanoparticles processed from ordinary titanium tetrachloride, organic groups can be easily adsorbed onto nanoparticle surface during the preparation process, which has detrimental effect on the PSCs' stability.

Here, we develop a low-temperature, environmental-friendly water-based method for   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanocrystal dispersion and mange to remove organic molecules attached on   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanocrystal surface by vacuum-rotary evaporation process under 85°C. ETL made of this “green method” processed water-based   $  TiO_{2}  $   (W-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) show significantly improved operational stability than that made of   $  TiO_{2}  $   dispersed in methanol and chloroform mixed solvent (M&C-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) due to the greatly reduced organic molecule attached on   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface. These attached organic molecules were found to lead to the formation of deep level defect states during solar cell operation. Additionally, the stability of W-  $  TiO_{2}  $   based PSCs is much better than high temperature   $  TiO_{2}  $   (made by high temperature pyrolysis in 500°C) based PSCs due to Cl ligands’ passivation effect as Cl would adhere to   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanocrystal surface after the removal of organic molecules. The best performing W-  $  TiO_{2}  $   PSC reached a PCE of 20.5% and could maintain 80% of its initial PCE at maximum power point (MPP) tracking under 1 sun illumination over 500 hours operation. Overall, we invented a universal low-temperature method to prepare   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticles dispersed in water with Cl passivation, which is simple, clean, environmental-friendly, and has no specific request to the quality of   $  TiCl_{4}  $  .

We dissolve  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanoparticles into water and remove organic molecules with the help of vacuum-rotary evaporation under  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ . To prove that organic molecules have been effectively removed from W- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , a series of experiments have been carried out and the results are shown in Figure 1. M&C- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  turns to pure black when it was heated at  $ 300^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and when heating temperature exceeded  $ 500^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , black color faded to pale yellow gradually (Figure 1a). Pinhole-free TiO $ _2 $  film could avoid perovskite contact directly with ITO, which could improve the reproducibility of high performance PSC. Top-view SEM image of perovskite film deposited on W-TiO $ _2 $  and M&C-TiO $ _2 $  film indicates a similar pin-hole free morphology (Figure S2). Steady photoluminescence (PL) spectra (Figure 3a) show an obvious PL quenching when depositing perovskite on W-TiO $ _2 $  film. Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) results illustrate that the lifetime of the perovskite layer is reduced evidently when depositing on W-TiO $ _2 $  film (Figure 3b), indicating more efficient electron transfer from perovskite to W-TiO $ _2 $  film. To explore the reason of enhanced electron transportation between perovskite and W-TiO $ _2 $ , ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) was utilized to obtain the energy band information of TiO $ _2 $  films deposited on ITO substrates. Figure 3c shows that E $ _f $  (fermi level) – E $ _v $  (valence band) of W-TiO $ _2 $  and M&C-TiO $ _2 $  is 3.47 eV and 3.25 eV, respectively. Figure 3d provides work function (W $ _f $ ) information regarding W-TiO $ _2 $  and M&C-TiO $ _2 $ , which is 4.19 eV and 4.09 eV, respectively. E $ _v $  could be deduced by the equation of E $ _v $ =-W $ _f $ -(E $ _f $ -E $ _v $ ), and the bandgap E $ _g $  of TiO $ _2 $  is 3.4 eV (Figure S3), invariable for the same material at the same phase, so E $ _c $  could be calculated from E $ _c $ =E $ _v $ +E $ _g $ , which is -4.26 eV and -3.94 eV for W-TiO $ _2 $  and M&C-TiO $ _2 $ , respectively. One can also estimate that the conduction band of CsFAMA perovskite from Figure 3c,d and Figure S4, which is -4.27 eV. In principle, the deeper conduction band of W-TiO $ _2 $  could enhance charge transfer from perovskite to ETL. The possible band alignment between perovskite and TiO $ _2 $  is shown in Figure 3e. The difference of M&C-TiO $ _2 $  and W-TiO $ _2 $  band structure could well explain the reduced PL decay time and quenching effect. The energy barrier between W-TiO $ _2 $  and perovskite has been greatly reduced compared with M&C-TiO $ _2 $  and thus enhances charge transfer between ETL and perovskite. The higher content Cl, as Figure 2c illustrated, could passivate interface between ETL and perovskite, and thus optimize the band structure, as previously reported. $ ^{[30]} $

Planar type PSCs were fabricated in ITO/TiO $ _2 $ /perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au structure with two types of TiO $ _2 $  film. The J-V curves (Figure 4a) indicate a better photovoltaic performance of W-TiO $ _2 $  based PSC by showing a reverse-scan PCE of 20.5% (V $ _{oc} $  = 1.17 V, FF = 78%, J $ _{sc} $  = 22.5 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ) and a forward-scan PCE of 19.8% (V $ _{oc} $  = 1.15 V, FF = 76%, J $ _{sc} $  = 22.5 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ) compared to M&A-TiO $ _2 $  based PSC. The better photovoltaic performance of W-TiO $ _2 $  based PSC is coming from band structure induced enhancement of electron transportation and defect state passivation. A less hysteresis was also observed in W-TiO $ _2 $  based solar cells, which is thought to be from the Cl passivation induced band structure optimization between perovskite and TiO $ _2 $ . To check the performance reproducibility of M&A-TiO $ _2 $  and W-TiO $ _2 $  based PSCs, 30 devices were fabricated for each condition and the statistics of the PSC performance parameters are shown in Table 1 and Figure S5. The average PCE of M&A-TiO $ _2 $  based PSCs is 18.5 %, and W-TiO $ _2 $  based PSCs achieve a 19.9 % average PCE. External quantum efficiency (EQE) measurements also show a better performance of W-TiO $ _2 $  PSC: the integral current increases from 21.2 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  (M&C-TiO $ _2 $  PSC) to 21.8 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  (W-TiO $ _2 $  PSC), which might result from the enhancement of electron transportation and defect state passivation.

It was suggested to test the PSCs' operational stability performance under MPP conditions in  $ \mathrm{N}_2 $  atmosphere at  $ 25^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  to simulate the real working state of PSCs.[31] Figure 4c shows the initial 500 s MPP stability test for W- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  and M&C- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  based solar cell under 1-sun illumination. W- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  based PSC has higher continuous working PCE due to its reduced hysteresis and better interface contact between  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  and perovskite. The long-term operational stability of PSC under MPP condition and continuous light illumination shows a huge difference: PSC based on W- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  could maintain  $ 80\% $  of its initial PCE after 500 hours continuous working under 1-sun illumination, whereas M&C- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  based solar cell lost  $ 20\% $  of its initial PCE within 50 hours. W- $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  based PSC demonstrates a significantly enhanced operational stability in continuous working at MPP under 1 sun illumination. X-ray diffraction

Furthermore, we also conducted experiments of high temperature  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL prepared by pyrolysis to compare solar cell PCE and stability performance. The best device performances of planar devices using high temperature  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  as ETL shows a  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  of 1.13 V, a  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  of 21 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , a FF of 73 % and a PCE of 17.3 % under reverse scan (Figure S8a). On one hand, the reduced PCE is originated from the larger energy barrier between ETL and perovskite as Figure S9a-c illustrated. On the other hand, FTO substrate is much thicker (2.5 mm) than ITO substrate (0.2 mm), which would result in worse light absorption and reduced  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $ . Figure S8b suggests that PSC based on high temperature  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  can maintain 80% of its initial PCE over 80 hours stability test under 1-sun illumination, which has a better stability than that of M&C-TiO $ _2 $  based device (maintaining 80% of its initial PCE over 50 hours) but much worse compared to W-TiO $ _2 $  based PSCs. In conclusion, we develop a simple, environmental-friendly and low temperature method to disperse  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanocrystals in water as ETL in PSCs. In addition, this water-based  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanocrystals is independent to the quality of its precursor  $ \mathrm{TiCl_4} $ , which makes it possible for low-cost mass production. More importantly, water based  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanocrystals contain relatively high Cl content on surface, which could simultaneously optimize band structure between ETL and perovskite, and also effectively passivate the interface between ETL and perovskite for much reduced deep-level defect states. As a result, the PCE of W- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -based PSCs boosted to  $ 20.5\% $  and the operational stability is 500 hours under MPP tracking and continuous light illumination. The significances of this universal ETL fabrication method is passivation agent can be conveniently added since water is one of the most widely used solvents, which may open up effective ways to passivate the interface between ETL and perovskite to further improve the photovoltaic performance of PSCs.

# Experimental Section

M&C-TiO  $  _{2}  $  and W-TiO  $  _{2}  $  Nanocrystal Synthesis: The following experiments are all performed in fuming cupboard. Firstly, 4 mL TiCl  $  _{4}  $  (99% Alfa-Aesar/ TCI/ Energy Chemical) was injected very slowly into 16 mL ethanol in reaction vial cooled by ice-water with 5000 rpm stirring speed to avoid local overheating. 80 mL of anhydrous benzyl alcohol was added to the previous solution and stirred for 10 min. The result solution was sealed in a vial and heated at 80°C water bath for about 14 hours. The as-prepared TiO  $  _{2}  $  nanocrystals were then precipitated by the addition of 300 mL diethyl ether to the as-obtained solution and isolated by

centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 2 min. The nanocrystals were subsequently washed by anhydrous ethanol and diethyl ether (volume ratio 1:5), followed by a same centrifugation process. This washing procedure was repeated twice. The washed  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanocrystals were dispersed into anhydrous chloroform and anhydrous methanol (1:1 volume ratio) as M&C-TiO $ _2 $ . As for W-TiO $ _2 $ , the washed  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanocrystals were dispersed into ultrapure water and put it into rotary evaporator in  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 20 min to remove organic molecules attached on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  nanoparticles surface.

Solar Cell Fabrication: Firstly, we cleaned ITO substrates with acetone and isopropanol in an ultrasonic bath in sequence. These ITO were then spin-coated with as-obtained   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanocrystal solution at 3000 rpm for twice and annealed at 150°C for 30 minutes on a hotplate. For the perovskite layer, the   $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.81}MA_{0.14}PbI_{2.55}Br_{0.45}  $   precursor solution (1.4 M) was prepared with molar ratios of   $  PbBr_{2}/PbI_{2}  $   and MABr/FAI both fixed at 0.15:0.85, molar ratio of CsI/(FAI+MABr)=0.05:0.95, and the molar ratio of (FAI+MABr+CsI)/(PbI  $  _{2}  $  +PbBr  $  _{2}  $  ) was at 1:1. The perovskite films were deposited onto the   $  TiO_{2}  $   substrates with two-step spin-coating procedures. The first step was 2000 rpm for 10 s with an acceleration of 200 rpm/s. The second step was 5000 rpm for 30 s with a ramp-up of 1000 rpm/s. Chlorobenzene (140 μL) was dropped on the spinning substrate during the second spin-coating step. To form a thick and dense film, chlorobenzene was dropped on the precursor film within 3 seconds to allow the volatilization of DMSO through the entire precursor film. The substrate was then move to a hotplate and heated at 100°C for 30 min. The HTL spin-coating solution was prepared by using 1 mL Spiro-OMeTAD/chlorobenzene (80 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) solution with addition of 15 mL Li-TFSI/acetonitrile (200 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ), and 38 mL TBP. Finally, 60 nm gold was deposited as an electrode using a thermal evaporator under a pressure of 5·10  $  ^{-6}  $   Pa.

Photovoltaic characterization: J-V curves were measured by Agilent B2912 Series precision source/measure unit and a solar simulator (Solar IV-150A, Zolix). KG5-filtered Si reference cell was used to calibrate light intensity of source light. A black mask was used to define the cells' area. The J-V curves were tested from 1.8 V to -0.1 V with a scan velocity of 100 mV/s (voltage step of 10 mV and delay time of 200 ms). The long-term stability test at continuous MPP conditions and 1 sun, AM 1.5G illumination was carried out in a nitrogen-filled glovebox by setting the bias voltage to MPP and tracking the current output. The MPP was updated every 5 s by measuring the current response to a small perturbation in the potential. The active area of our PSC is 0.049 cm $  ^{2}  $ .

Perovskite film and solar cell characterization: The surface morphology of the perovskite thin film was characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (FEI 430) under an electron beam accelerated at  $ 5\mathrm{kV} $ . PL spectra were measured using a blue laser (470 nm in wavelength) on FLS 920 spectrograph. XPS measurements were conducted on a Thermo Fisher Scientific ESCALAB 250X system. Al Kα (1486.6 eV) was used as X-ray source and the analyzer was put at  $ 54.7^{\circ} $  relative to the source. UPS measurement is carried out on ESCALAB 250xi. The Valence band  $ (\mathrm{V_B}) $  spectra were measured with a monochromatic He I light source (21.2 eV) and a Thermo Scientific ESCALAB 250 XI analyzer. A sample bias of -5 eV was applied to observe the secondary electron cutoff (SEC). EQE measurement process: Firstly, the standard silicon solar cell was required to scan under laser 300-850 nm in Zolix Solar Cell Scan 100 IPCE Measurement System, and the instrument would automatically record data every 5 nm to set a standard. The to be tested device was put into a self-made box filled with nitrogen gas, and then the box was put into Zolix Solar Cell Scan 100 IPCE Measurement System. Laser position is adjusted to make sure a selected pixel was fully spotted under laser. The scan range was 300-850 nm, and the instrument automatically records data every 5 nm. Finally, the software would compare perovskite solar cell with standard silicon solar cell light response data to calculate the EQE response automatically.

A water-based   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanocrystal solution is developed to use as electron transport layer for perovskite solar cells, showing much reduced organic molecules and high Cl content on   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanocrystal surface, which effectively passivate the interface between   $  TiO_{2}  $   and perovskite layer with much reduced defects. Corresponding solar cells demonstrate 20.5% power conversion efficiency and 500 hour operational stability.
