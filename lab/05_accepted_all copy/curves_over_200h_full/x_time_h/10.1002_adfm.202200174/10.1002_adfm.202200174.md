# Stable  $ \alpha $ -FAPbI $ _3 $  in Inverted Perovskite Solar Cells with Efficiency Exceeding 22% via a Self-Passivation Strategy

Diwei Zhang,  $  ^{*}  $   Huidong Zhang, Huanxin Guo, Fangyuan Ye, Shuaijun Liu, and Yongzhen Wu  $  ^{*}  $

Formamidinium lead iodide (FAPbI $ _3 $ ) has endowed power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) up to 25.5% in regular-structured perovskite solar cells (PSCs) because of its optimal bandgap and enhanced thermal stability. However, the performance of FAPbI $ _3 $ -based inverted-structured PSCs is unsatisfactory. Herein, four kinds of commonly used hole transport materials (HTMs) are selected, including PEDOT:PSS, PTAA, NiOx, and MeO-2PACz, to study their impact on the methylamine chloride (MACl)-assisted one-step deposition of FAPbI $ _3 $  films. It is found that MeO-2PACz is the optimal substrate for stabilizing black-phase FAPbI $ _3 $  and the corresponding inverted-structured PSCs show the best photovoltaic performance. Nonetheless, the PCE is restricted by low open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{OC} $ ) due to non-radiative recombination caused by MACl residues. Therefore, homologous PbI $ _2 $  in situ passivation is implemented to passivate defects at grain boundaries. The addition of excess PbI $ _2 $  in precursor solution remarkably decreases charge trap densities and elongates carrier lifetimes. As a result, the optimized device achieves an impressive PCE of 22.13%, which is the highest efficiency of FAPbI $ _3 $  based on inverted-structured PSCs. Moreover, the best device exhibits free hysteresis and excellent long-term stability, maintaining 92% of the initial PCEs after 800 h aging under ambient conditions.

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) as a promising candidate for new generation photovoltaic technology have attracted extensive interest due to their outstanding photoelectric properties and low fabrication cost. The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has been boosted from barely 3.8% to a certified 25.5% in the past decade.  $  ^{[1-4]}  $   Generally, the main structure of PSCs can be divided into regular (n-i-p) and inverted (p-i-n) structures, according to the direction of the photocurrent.  $  ^{[5,6]}  $   However, it is worth noting that most of the certified efficiencies of over

To date, only a few studies have been reported that used   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   as the light absorber layer in inverted PSCs, and the PCE of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   PSCs was less than 21%.  $  ^{[16-18]}  $   The main bottleneck limiting the PCE of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $ -based inverted PSCs is intrinsic phase stability and the crystallization of   $  \alpha  $ -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $ .  $  ^{[19,20]}  $   In this regard, various effective strategies are proposed to stabilize black-phase of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   and improve the film crystallinity and morphology. For instance, the use of mixed cations,  $  ^{[21-23]}  $   anions,  $  ^{[24,25]}  $   ionic liquids (ILs),  $  ^{[26,27]}  $   hydrohalic acids,  $  ^{[28,29]}  $   Lewis base,  $  ^{[30,31]}  $   and others  $  ^{[32-37]}  $   are directly incorporated into the precursor solution to obtain the high-performance efficiencies. Apart from substitution strategy, chloride-based additives, especially methylamine chloride (MACl), have been widely studied recently.  $  ^{[18,38-43]}  $   Kim et al. have demonstrated that MACl is a good stabilizer for acquiring high-quality crystallization of black-phase   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   via the formation of an intermediate phase.  $  ^{[40]}  $   We note that all of these methods were developed based on regular structured PSCs. So far, only Xie et al. have used MACl for surface post-treatment to assist vertical recrystallization in   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films, and the efficiency of the   $  FAPbI_{3}  $ -based inverted device has improved to 20.6%.  $  ^{[18]}  $   However, the potential cationic residual of additive results in obstructed charge transport and increased bandgap.  $  ^{[44]}  $   Thus, how to stabilize black-phase   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   is the key to achieving stable and efficient inverted-structure PSCs.

Interestingly, among the reported perovskite materials, formamidinium lead iodide (FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) has been widely used in regular-structure devices because of its high absorption coefficient, superior thermal stability, and ideal band gap (1.48 eV) that is close to the Shockley-Queisser limit.  $  ^{[15]}  $  However, the outstanding performance of FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  could not be directly translated to efficient inverted-structure devices. In this work, we first fabricated the MACl-assisted black-phase   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films by one-step spin-coating methods on four different HTMs (PEDOT: PSS, NiOx, PTAA, and MeO-2PACz, shown in Figure 1a), and compared their device performance at different temperatures. After a screening experiment, we found that MeO-2PACz is the optimal substrate for stabilizing black-phase   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   and the corresponding inverted-structured PSCs show the best photovoltaic performance among the four HTM candidates. However, the device's low PCE and large   $  V_{OC}  $   losses are due to MACl residual. Subsequently, a facile and efficient passivation strategy of introducing excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   in precursor solution is proposed to passivate defects at grain boundaries (GBs) in   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   based inverted PSCs. Upon gradually increasing the amount of excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   in the precursor solution, a champion PCE of 22.13% is achieved at 5 mol% excess   $  PbI_{2}  $ , which is the highest efficiency of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   based inverted PSCs. Moreover, the 5 mol% excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   based devices exhibit free of hysteresis and long-term stability, maintaining 92% of the initial PCEs after 800 h aging under ambient conditions without encapsulation.

# 2. Results and Discussion

Figure 1a presents the structure of inverted PSCs with a device architecture of ITO/HTM/perovskite/PCBM/C $ _{60} $ /BCP/Ag. As well known, the optoelectronic performance of perovskite films is highly dependent on their crystallization quality, which is to a great extent determined by the properties of the substrate materials. The regular and inverted-structured PSCs adopt different substrate materials for perovskite deposition, which might be a critical origin to the remarkably different

photovoltaic performance of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite in regular and inverted-structured PSCs. However, the influence of substrate materials in inverted-structured PSCs (namely HTMs) on the deposition of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   has not been systematically studied to date. Therefore, we first select four kinds of commonly used hole transport materials (HTMs) to study their impact on the MACl-assisted one-step deposition of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   films. The zoom in Figure 1a visualizes the chemical structures of these HTMs covering different categories: hydrophilic polymer HTM of PEDOT: PSS,  $  ^{[45,46]}  $   hydrophobic polymer HTM of PTAA,  $  ^{[47,48]}  $   inorganic HTM of   $  NiO_{x}  $  ,  $  ^{[12,49]}  $   and a small organic molecule HTM of MeO-2PACz, which can form self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) on ITO substrate,  $  ^{[50,51]}  $   respectively.

We fabricated an inverted-structured device by using the four kinds of HTMs and PCBM/C $ _{60} $  as hole- and electron-transporting layers, respectively. The details on device fabrication can be seen in the Experimental Section. The light absorber of FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite films was prepared via a simple MACl assisted one-step spin-coating method. Briefly, the precursor solution consisting of stoichiometric FAI and PbI $ _2 $  in DMF/DMSO solution, with the additive of MACl (40 mol%), was spin-coated on HTM substrates, and chlorobenzene (CB) as anti-solvent was dripped during this process. We found that the annealing process for FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite films on these HTL substrates is significantly different from regular-structured PSCs. According to literatures, the optimal annealing conditions of FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite on inorganic electron transport layers like TiO $ _2 $  and SnO $ _2 $  is heating at 150 °C for 10–20 min. $ ^{[7,8]} $  However, the same fabrication conditions results in extremely low photovoltaic performance for inverted-structured PSCs. Therefore, we have systematically optimized the annealing conditions by decreasing the temperature from

In order to understand the huge difference in the photovoltaic performance of FAPbI $ _3 $  PSCs based on MeO-2PACz substrate compared to the PTAA, PEDOT:PSS, and NiOx, we carefully studied crystallization, optical properties, and morphology of perovskite films (annealed at 120 °C) by X-ray diffraction (XRD), ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) absorption spectroscopy and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) measurement, respectively. Figure S4, Supporting Information, shows UV-vis absorption spectra of FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite films deposited on the four kinds of HTM. It can be found that these perovskite films exhibited identical absorption profile, suggesting the same film thickness and light capture capability. The XRD measurement (Figure S5, Supporting Information) of these perovskite films also gave the same diffraction patterns with characteristic peaks at 14° and 28.1°, corresponding to the (100) and (200) crystal planes of α-FAPbI $ _3 $ , respectively. $ ^{[39,52]} $  Top-view SEM images (Figure S6, Supporting Information) of these perovskite films showed a relatively compact and uniform surface morphology, and the average grain size were approximately 1 μm. From the cross-sectional SEM images of these FAPbI $ _3 $  films (Figure S7, Supporting Information), it can be seen that the films were composed of one single and large-sized crystallites in the vertical direction, and there were no obvious pin-holes. There are no apparent differences in the morphology, crystallization, and optical properties, indicating that the four kinds of HTM substrates don't change the films' properties of FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite.

Surprisingly, we found that the stability of FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite was highly dependent on the substrate materials. As shown in Figure S8, Supporting Information, the initial black  $ \alpha $ -FAPbI $ _3 $  films based on PEDOT: PSS, NiO $ _x $ , and PTAA substrates decomposed apparently when stored in the air with relative humidity (RH) of 70 ± 2% for 6 hours. Take the PTAA based samples as an example, we recorded the XRD pattern (Figure S9a, Supporting Information) and UV-vis absorption spectra (Figure S10, Supporting Information) at different time intervals. The FAPbI $ _3 $  films underwent spontaneous transformation from  $ \alpha $ -phase to  $ \delta $ -phase during the storage. Notably, the perovskite film on MeO-2PACz substrate can maintain its black phase for a much longer time under the same conditions (Figure S9b, Supporting Information), which is in accordance with previous literature that demonstrated effective stabilization of black-phase FAPbI $ _3 $  by the free phosphate groups in MeO-2PACz. $ ^{[33]} $

The trap density of perovskite films based on different HTMs was further evaluated by the space charge-limited current (SCLC) measurement, based on hole-only devices with the structure of ITO/HTMs/FAPbI $ _3 $ /Sprio-OMeTAD/Ag. The details of calculation can be found in Experimental Section. As shown in Figure 1c and Figure S11, Supporting Information, the trap density of FAPbI $ _3 $  films on PEDOT:PSS, NiO $ _x $ , PTAA, and MeO-2PACz are calculated to be  $ 23.8 \times 10^{15} $ ,  $ 13.9 \times 10^{15} $ ,  $ 8.38 \times 10^{15} $ , and  $ 5.47 \times 10^{15} $  cm $ ^{-3} $  respectively. The lower trap density of FAPbI $ _3 $  films on MeO-2PACz is in good agreement with the improved PCE and films stability. Thus, after a screening experiment, it is found that MeO-2PACz is the most suitable substrate for preparing FAPbI $ _3 $  PSCs in inverted devices among the four HTMs candidates.

In spite of several advantages of the MeO-2PACz as the HTM for FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite, the best PCE of the inverted-structured device was only 15.55%, with a  $ V_{OC} $  of 0.92 V,  $ J_{SC} $  of 22.10 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  and  $ FF $  of 76.55%. The PCE is mainly restricted by low  $ V_{OC} $  performance. According to previous literature, the perovskite films deposited by the MACl-assisted one-step method might contain a certain amount of MACl residues, which could be defects and form nonradiative recombination centers to deteriorate device performance. $ ^{[53]} $  In order to verify that MACl is the reason for the poor device performance, the device performance of FAPbI $ _3 $  film with or without MACl was compared (Figure S12, Supporting Information). The results show that the addition of MACl will significantly reduce the  $ J_{SC} $  of the device, which can explain that the addition of MACl leads to the emergence of non-radiative recombination centers, which impedes the charge separation. Meanwhile, the FAPbI $ _3 $  film without MACl is easy to turns yellow in the air atmosphere. To determine whether the MACl is present in one-step deposited FAPbI $ _3 $  film, we used ion chromatography (IC) spectra to quantitatively analyze the MA $ ^+ $  and Cl $ ^- $  residue in the aqueous solution after soaking the MACl-assisted deposition of FAPbI $ _3 $  film for 10 min. Expectedly, an obvious MA $ ^+ $  (0.266 mg L $ ^{-1} $ ) (Figure 2a) and Cl $ ^- $  (0.41 mg L $ ^{-1} $ ) (Figure S13, Supporting Information) were detected, indicating MACl has remained in one-step deposited FAPbI $ _3 $  film. Furthermore, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was also employed to explore the evidence of residual MACl. The high-resolution XPS spectra of Cl 2p are shown in Figure S14, Supporting Information, which confirms the residue of the Cl element. We further found evidence of MACl residuals by the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of FAPbI $ _3 $  based PSCs at different annealing temperatures. As shown in Figure 2b, the normalized EQEs is observed in the entire wavelength for different annealing temperature. Onset wavelength of 827 nm for 150 °C is shifted to a shorter wavelength of 822 nm for 120 °C, which indicates that the badgap of FAPbI $ _3 $  is influenced by the residual additive in the annealed film. Meanwhile, the UV-vis and Tacu plot of absorption spectra exhibited a slight increase in band gap, which also confirmed the presence of additive residual in perovskite films (Figure S15 and S16, Supporting Information). Therefore, it is imperative to eliminate the effect of MACl residuals in such one-step deposited FAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite films for boosting the performance of inverted-structured PSCs.

Herein, a self-passivation strategy by increasing the stoichiometric ratio of   $  PbI_{2}  $   in precursor solution (control, 3 mol%, 5 mol%, and 7 mol% excess   $  PbI_{2}  $ ) is proposed to suppress nonradiative recombination and improve the efficiency of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $ -based inverted PSCs.

In-depth understanding of the effect of various concentrations of   $  PbI_{2}  $   in precursor solution on the morphology and crystallization of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   films, XRD, UV-vis absorption spectra, and SEM measurement were carried out. As shown in Figure S17, Supporting Information, the XRD pattern of the control film shows two characteristic diffraction peaks, which are attributed to the black-phase   $  FAPbI_{3}  $  . With the increase of   $  PbI_{2}  $   concentration, an obvious   $  PbI_{2}  $   diffraction peak (  $  2\theta = 12.6^{\circ}  $  ) appears in   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films, and the diffraction pattern of black-phase   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite is still maintained, which indicate that the crystal structure of perovskite is not affected by excess   $  PbI_{2}  $  . Surprisingly, when we used the Williamson-Hall method to estimate the change of residual lattice strain in the   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   films from XRD patterns for the control and excess 5 mol%   $  PbI_{2}  $   films. It was found that lattice strain showed a change from 0.2715 to 0.2638 for the control and excess 5 mol%   $  PbI_{2}  $   films respectively (Figure S18, Supporting Information), which indicates

that the addition of excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   can reduce the strain of the lattice, minimize defect centers and capture more charge carrier.  $  ^{[39]}  $   According to the UV-vis absorption spectrum (Figure S19, Supporting Information), the results also confirmed the presence of excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   in perovskite films.  $  ^{[59]}  $   Next, we investigated the morphology of perovskite films with different   $  PbI_{2}  $   concentrations by cross-section and top-view SEM (Figure S20 and S21, Supporting Information), it can be seen that excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   only appears at the grain boundaries (GBs) of perovskite. It is well known that most of the defects are mainly distributed at the GBs rather than inside the grain, therefore a large number of carriers are trapped at GBs. According to the study by Jin-song Huang et al., the GB defects are two-dimensional extended defects and deep level defects.  $  ^{[62,63]}  $   As previously reported, the excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   results in a type-I alignment heterojunction to suppress trap induced recombination at GBs.  $  ^{[58,61]}  $

We further investigate the influence of the excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   on the radiative recombination of the   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite layer by the photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) measurement. Refer to the previously reported methods,   $  QFLS_{rad}  $   is calculated to be 1.23 eV.  $  ^{[66]}  $   According to the corresponding calculation method above, the QFLS of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   film deposited on quartz is estimated to be 1.01 eV. # 3. Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated that MeO-2PACz is better than the commonly used HTMs substrates (PEDOT:PSS,   $  NiO_{x}  $  , and PTAA) for stabilizing MACl assisted deposition of black-phase   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   films in inverted-structured PSCs. Whereafter, a facile and effective   $  PbI_{2}  $   in-situ self-passivation strategy is developed to boost   $  V_{OC}  $   and improve the efficiency of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   based inverted device. The excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   can passivate the defects of GBs effectively, contributing to reduced defect density and suppressed non-radiative recombination losses, therefore achieving promising efficiency with high   $  V_{OC}  $  . As a result, the optimized device of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $ : 5 mol%   $  PbI_{2}  $   achieved an impressive PCE of 22.13%, which is the highest efficiency of   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   based inverted devices. Moreover, the best device exhibited free of hysteresis and excellent long-term stability, remaining 92% of its initial PCE after 800 h aging under air condition with RH of   $  50 \pm 5\%  $   at room temperature.
