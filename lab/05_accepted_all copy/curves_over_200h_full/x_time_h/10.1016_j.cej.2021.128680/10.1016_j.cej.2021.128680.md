# 1. Instruction

Organic-Inorganic or all-inorganic halide perovskite display tremendous potential in photoelectrical field owing to their extra performance and low cost. [1,2] The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has increased from 3.8% [3] to 25.5% [4] in the last decade, via optimizing film growth, composition engineering, interface engineering and traps passivation. [5–8] Seok et al. stabilized the α-FAPbI₃ phase by doping with methylenediammonium dichloride (MDACl₂) and achieved a certified PCE of 23.7%. [9] Kim et al. introduced methylammonium chloride (MACl) additive in FAPbI₃-based perovskite which induced intermediate phase stabilization and fabricated PSCs with a certified PCE of 23.5%. [10] It is important to note that all these efficient cells were based n-i-p structure with high-temperature-sintered mesoporous titanium oxide (TiO₂) electron transporting layer (ETL). The high-temperature (up to 500 °C) and long-duration (2–5 h) sintering process limit the compatibility with plastic substrates and hinder commercial applications of PSCs. The long-lifetime photo-generated charge carriers in perovskite can flow through polycrystalline films before recombination without mesoporous ETL. [11] Consequently, developing low-temperature processed planar

$ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  is a promising alternative for  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  electron transporting layer (ETL), owing to the high charge mobility and chemical stability. Defects accumulating at interfaces and grain boundaries are detrimental to the performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, a ligand-biphenyl-3,3',5,5'-tetracarboxylic acid butyl amine (BPTC-BN) with four carbonyls and conjugated electron is used to passivate defects at  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ /perovskite interface. Meanwhile perovskite grown on  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ /BPTC-BN shows increased and uniform grain size. Benefitting from decreased defects at interface and bulk perovskite, a planar PSC with a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 21.23\% $  and an open voltage  $ (V_{OC}) $  of 1.164 V is obtained. In contrast, devices based on single  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  layers deliver a PCE of  $ 19.38\% $  and a  $ V_{OC} $  of 1.114 V. In addition, this interface passivation strategy also improves device operational stability substantially, retaining  $ 84\% $  of its initial efficiency after  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  tracking at maximum power point (MPP). The results prove the prime importance of interface for the photovoltaic performance especially operational stability of PSCs.

ETL, including   $  TiO_{2}  $  ,   $  SnO_{2}  $  , ZnO and CPTA et al. organic molecules, become particularly important. [12–15]

In the planar n-i-p structured,   $  SnO_{2}  $   is a promising alternative ETL to   $  TiO_{2}  $   [16] for its wider bandgap (greater than 3.6 eV), the stability under UV illumination. [17] and the higher electron mobility.   $  SnO_{2}  $   could ensure the effective charge extraction from perovskite even in planar configuration, which mitigates the serious hysteresis behavior in   $  TiO_{2}  $   based planar devices. [18] Fang group firstly reported   $  SnO_{2}  $   as an effective ETL in planar PSCs achieving a PCE of 17.2%. [19] You et al. adapted commercialized   $  SnO_{2}  $   aqueous colloidal dispersion to form compact ETL and fabricated PSCs with certificated PCE nearly 20% almost without hysteresis. [20] Even though, further work was requisite to modify the imperfect energy-band match, surface and bulk defect of   $  SnO_{2}  $   which resulted in large voltage loss, eroded fill factors (FFs) and severe hysteresis behavior in   $  SnO_{2}  $   based planar devices. [21,22] Our group have employed ethylene diamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA) to passivate ZnO ETL in organic solar cells and proved the chelation function of its four carboxyl groups. [23] Liu group utilized this chelation function of EDTA to passivate bulk and improved the Fermi level (  $  E_{F}  $  ) and electron mobility of   $  SnO_{2}  $ . As a result, a certificated efficiency of 21.52% was reached with eliminated hysteresis. [24] Zhang et. al.

Herein, a commonly used ligand-biphenyl-3, 3', 5', 5'-tetracarboxylic acid (BPTC) [37,38] was used as a modifier on   $  SnO_{2}  $   surface to passivate its surface defects and uncoordinated Pb at perovskite bottom interface. Both the four carbonyl groups and the conjugated electrons could simultaneously take effect. We also verified the necessary of the existence of   $  \pi  $   electron through comparing with EDTA which also possesses four carbonyl groups but no conjugated group. In order to improve the solubility and film morphology of BPTC or EDTA film, we reacted them with butylamine and dissolved in trifluoroethanol solvent (named as BPTC-BN and EDTA-BN, respectively) (Figure S1 and Figure S2) for solution fabrication processing as our previous work. [39,40] Subsequently the energy alignment, perovskite growth quality, ETL/perovskite interfacial traps were all optimized (or suppressed). PSCs based on   $  SnO_{2}/BPTC-BN  $   planar ETL showed a champion efficiency of 21.23% (  $  V_{OC}  $  : 1.164 V) with reduced hysteresis. Profiting from the reduced defects at ETL/perovskite interface, improved stability retaining 84% of the original PCE after tracking at maximum power point after 1000 h, was achieved.

# 2. Materials and methods

Fabrication of Perovskite Solar Cells: ITO glass was cleaned by sequentially washing with detergent, deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol. The cleaned substrate was treated with ultraviolet light (UV)/O  $  _{3}  $  for 20 min. Then, SnO  $  _{2}  $  nanoparticle precursor (Alfa Aesar, diluted in H  $  _{2}  $  O by volume ration 1:7) was coated on substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s, and annealed in ambient air at 150 °C for 30 min. For the BPTC-BN and EDTA-BN modified SnO  $  _{2}  $  ETL, the 10 mg BPTC or EDTA (Sigma Aldrich) were dissolved in 2, 2, 2-trifluoroethanol (99% +, Alfa Aesar) with excessive butylamine (30 μl) and were diluted to the required concentration for use. The modifier layer was spin-coated on SnO  $  _{2}  $  substrate at 2000 r.p.m. and annealed at 100 °C in N  $  _{2}  $  to evaporate solvent and strengthen the passivation function. Next, a perovskite film was fabricated through a two-step solution process as shown in our previous work. [40] The active area of each device is 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $ .

Characterization and Measurements: J-V characteristics (Keithley 2400 SourceMeter) were measured under one sun illumination (AM1.5G, 100 mW/cm²) using the solar simulator (Enlitech, SS-F5-3A). The light source is a 45-W xenon lamp calibrated by a standard Si reference solar cell (Enli/SRC2020, SRC-00201). The EQE measurement was conducted by ORIEL IQE 200TM measurement system. SEM (Hitachi, S-4800) was used to characterize the morphology of perovskite film. AFM (Dimension 3100 V, Veeco) were carried out to compare the roughness of different ETLs. XPS and UPS measurements were carried out using Kratos AXIS ULTRA DALD XPS/UPS system. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) was recorded in transmittance mode using IR spectrometer instrument (Thermo, Nicolet 6700). XRD was performed by Bruker AXS D8 Advance equipped with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 0.154 nm). EIS and M–S plot were measured by an electrochemical working station (Solartron Analytical). EIS was measured under dark with a bias voltage of 800 mV at frequencies ranging from 0.1 Hz to 1.0 MHz and the oscillation potential amplitudes are 20 mV. M–S plot were measured under 1 sun illumination (AM 1.5) at 1 k Hz frequency and bias potentials between 0 and 1.2 V.

# 3. Results and discussion

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) image of different ETLs were shown in Fig. 2. Pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   was flat and uniform with a roughness of 1.08 nm, which further reduced to 0.46 nm after modification with BPTC-BN, whereas increased to 10.50 nm after coating with EDTA-BN. This indicated that BPTC-BN exhibited regular molecular arrangement for its   $  \pi-\pi  $   packing action. However, EDTA-BN was less regular arranged on   $  SnO_{2}  $   than BPTC-BN, meanwhile meaning the large roughness and lower coordinated ration of the carbonyl groups on EDTA-BN. To clarify the function group on BPTC-BN at   $  SnO_{2}  $ /perovskite interface, the Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra measurement was employed for   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite layer, respectively. In Figure S4a, stretching vibration peak of C–C in the aromatic (  $  \sigma_{C=C}  $  ) and C = O (  $  \sigma_{C=O}  $  ) appear at 1599 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   and 1680 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   in BPTC molecular. After reacting with perovskite, the peaks of C = C and C = O disappeared, indicating the interaction between   $  \pi  $   electron and C = O with perovskite. In combination with the apparent shifted Pb 4f signal peaks in Fig. 2e, we could conclude that the passivation function at BPTC-BN/perovskite interface origins from the interaction between bare Pb with   $  \pi  $   electron and -C = O group on BPTC-BN molecular. However, only interaction between bare Pb and -C = O group exists when EDTA-BN modifier is used (Figure S4b). In Figure S4c, stretching vibration peak of C–C in the aromatic (  $  \sigma_{C=C}  $  ) and C = O (  $  \sigma_{C=O}  $  ) appear at 1599 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   and 1680 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   in BPTC molecular shifted to 1561 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   and 1615 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   after interacting with   $  SnO_{2}  $ . This shift origins from the reduced electron density on C = O group and benzene ring, and verifies the interaction between Sn atom with   $  \pi  $   electron and -C = O group on BPTC-BN molecular, combining with the apparent shifted Sn 3d signal peaks in Fig. 2d. Similarly, only interaction between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and -C = O group exists when EDTA-BN modifier is used (Figure S4d).

Moreover, the benzene ring on BPTC-BN molecular is an electron-donating group and the carboxylic ion is an electron-withdrawing group. The high charge density of carbonyl and central aromatic rings on BPTC-BN calculated through density functional theory (DFT, Fig. 2f-g) further proved the strengthened passivation function and facilitated electron transport after BPTC-BN modification, which reduced voltage loss and hysteresis effect. To investigate the change of band positions induced by BPTC-BN or EDTA-BN modification, the ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) of   $  SnO_{2}  $   before and after modification was shown in Fig. 3. The Fermi level of   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL was calculated as -4.4 eV, which were -4.08 eV and -4.24 eV after modified by BPTC-BN and EDTA-BN respectively, indicating a matched energy level between   $  SnO_{2}/BPTC-BN  $   and perovskite (Figure S5). Both BPTC-BN and EDTA-BN exhibited quadrupole moment for the tetracarboxylic groups. The charge-quadrupole interaction between interfacial modifier and the tin atom in   $  SnO_{2}  $   could result its energy level shift. It has been reported that the change of energy level is as a function of the molecular orientation, where random orientation reduces the quadrupole moment. Both BPTC-BN and EDTA-BN showed full surface coverage (Figure S6) on   $  SnO_{2}  $   which guaranteed the comparability of the energy shift in terms of quadrupole moments. [41] BPTC-BN molecular exhibits more regular arrange due to the intermolecular   $  \pi-\pi  $

interaction, while EDTA-BN molecular exhibit relative disordered arrange, which is in accordance with the AFM results. As a result, the charge-quadrupole interaction energy for BPTC-BN crystalline films is higher than that of EDTA-BN film. Therefore,  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  with BPTC-BN modified delivered a higher energy shift.

In addition to interfacial property, the perovskite crystal quality was also improved after coating BPTC-BN on   $  SnO_{2}  $   substrate. As shown in Fig. 4, the perovskite grains become more denser and smoother, and grain size increased to more than 1   $  \mu  $  m after BPTC-BN modifying   $  SnO_{2}  $ . The perovskite crystallized on   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}/EDTA-BN  $   also showed   $  \sim  $   1   $  \mu  $  m grain size but the grain regularity and uniformity were inferior to that on   $  SnO_{2}/BPTC-BN  $   substrate. Therefore, in the cross-section SEM image, perovskite grain across the whole section was observed on the device based   $  SnO_{2}/BPTC-BN  $   ETL but unregular grains were in the majority in the other two cases. This enlarged and even perovskite grain was due to the relatively non-wetting surface of BPTC-BN (Figure S7), which increased nucleus spacing by suppressing heterogeneous nucleation and facilitated grain boundary migration in grain growth by imposing less drag force. [42] The X-ray diffraction (XRD) peaks became stronger and sharper for perovskite films on   $  SnO_{2}/BPTC-BN  $ , agreeing with the large grains and better film crystallinity (Figure S8). The peak at 12.8° indicated the existence of a trace of   $  PbI_{2}  $ , which was in accordance with the perovskite film fabricated through two-step method in previous report. [43] No significant change of the diffraction peak ratio was observed, indicating the different substrates do not cause crystal orientation change.

In the TPC measurement, the charge transfer lifetime decreased after modified with BPTC-BN, resulting from reduced interfacial defects and better crystal quality of perovskite, consistent with the TRPL measurement. Meanwhile the TPV spectra showed longer charge-recombination lifetime of BPTC-BN modified device compared with device based on pure   $  SnO_{2}  $  . This indicated a lower defect concentration and superior electronic quality in BPTC-BN modified devices, consistent with the higher   $  V_{OC}  $   and slower charge recombination as concluded from the EIS measurement. Therefore, the lower   $  \tau_{s}  $   was origin from accelerated charge transferring other than increased interfacial recombination.

The long-term stability was the most urgent issue for the commercialization of PSCs. It has been reported that stability loss in PSCs could arise from imperfect interfaces and charge recombination between the selective contact at the illumination side and the perovskite film grown on top. Therefore, to exam the effect of modified  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  on device stability, we tracked performance of device with pristine and BPTC-BN modified  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  under MPP tracking with a continuous one-sun

irradiation in a nitrogen atmosphere in   $  N_{2}  $   (Fig. 7). The reference cells show a rapid loss of PCE (below 60% of the original PCE after working for 450 h), while the   $  SnO_{2}/BPTC-BN  $   cell exhibited a longer lifetime, retaining 84% of its initial PCE after 1000 h. The rapid decline at beginning was attributed to the mobile of dopants and the crystallization of HTL. [51] The nearly unchanged performance at last 950 h may benefit from the suppressed trap induced degradation at ETL/perovskite interface and grain boundary in perovskite bulk. The normalized PCE as a function of the storage time in air environment (40% relative humidity) was also depicted (Figure S14). Therefore, in addition to the perovskite active layers themselves, the interfacial property plays an important role in the long-term stability of PSCs. Ameliorating interfacial transporting materials and their interaction with perovskite layer is an effective pathway to improve device stability.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, defects at   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface were passivated through a chelated ligand modifying. Experimental results indicate the crucial status of both the four carbonyl groups and conjugated electrons. The electron extraction rate has been significantly improved benefitting from the facilitated interfacial energy level and decreased interfacial defect state. Meanwhile, the highly crystalline perovskite film with increased and uniform grain was obtained via suppressing heterogeneous nucleation. With reduced interfacial and grain boundary recombination loss, planar heterojunction PSCs based on   $  SnO_{2}  $  /BPTC-BN ETL showed a champion efficiency of 21.23% with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.164 V and a reduced hysteresis. In addition, the device operational stability was also improved substantially, retaining 84% of the original PCE after tracking at maximum power point after 1000 h, which indicated the vital role of interface in the device stability issue.
