The crucial component, bulky spacers, in two-dimensional Ruddlesden-Popper (2DRP) layered tin (Sn) perovskites are highly limited by halide ammonium salts, leading to the insufficient control of complex crystallization process due to the limited interaction between bulky spacers and 2DRP perovskite frameworks. Here, we report an ionic liquid-bulky spacer, butylammounium acetate (BAAcO), for constructing efficient and stable 2DRP Sn-based perovskite solar cells (PSCs). In contrast to the traditional halide ammonium bulky spacer, butylammounium iodide (BAI), the AcO  $  ^{-}  $  -functional group in BAAcO has a strong interaction with formamidine ions (FA  $  ^{+}  $  ) and Sn  $  ^{2+}  $  . The inter-component interaction allows the formation of controllable intermediates for the favorable growth of smooth, dense, and highly oriented perovskite films. A PSC with power conversion efficiency of 10.36% (7.16% for BAI) is achieved, which is the highest report, along with improved stability with ~90% retained after ~600 h storage in N  $  _{2}  $   atmosphere without any encapsulation.

# 1 Introduction

Environmentally-friendly metal halide tin (Sn) perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted much attention and been considered as an ideal alternative of nocuous traditional lead (Pb) counterparts due to its broader light harvesting range, lower binding energy, and higher carrier mobility   $  [1-10]  $  . However, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of Sn-

based PSCs are well below that of their Pb counterparts (25.5% certified) [11,12], because of high p-type doping and high background carriers caused by low formation energy of Sn vacancies. In addition to lower efficiency, the poor stability on account of easy oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   is another problem in Sn-based PSCs [13,14]. Though the progresses have been made in three-dimensional (3D) Sn-based perovskites (e.g.,   $  FASnI_{3}  $  ) by employing solvents with strong polarity [15,16], applying conjugated organic cations [17,18], reducing air/solution surface energy [19], and balancing energy levels [20,21], further improvement of efficiency with enhanced

Recently, two-dimensional Ruddlesden-Popper (2DRP) Sn-based perovskites, with multiple quantum wells, have been widely reported to be more stable than 3D Sn-based perovskites owing to the introduction of hydrophobic bulky spacers and suppressed ion migration/self-doping effect [22,23]. Taking both lower toxicity and improved stability into consideration, the 2DRP Sn-based PSCs provide a new way for approaching real industrialization of PSCs. 2DRP halide perovskites take the general chemical formula of  $ (\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{NH}_3)_2\mathrm{A}_{n-1}\mathrm{M}_n\mathrm{X}_{3n+1} $ , where  $ \mathrm{R}-\mathrm{NH}_3 $  represents bulkier organic ammonium (spacer), A, M, and X are a small molecule or alkaline metal, a group-IVA metal, and a halide, and  $ n $  is an integer [24,25]. The layered segments are separated and bound together by the bulky ammonium spacers that are alternately inserted as ligands to replace the original A-site cations partly.

In 2DRP Sn-based perovskites, the competition between bulky spacers and ammonium salts (e.g.,  $ \mathrm{FA}^+ $ ) plays an important role in crystal growth of the perovskites, and leads to the fact that the bulky spacers prefer to limit crystal grow parallel to the substrate whereas other ammonium salts tend to expand crystal grow vertical to the substrate [26], which leads to the crystallization process in 2DRP Sn-based perovskites becoming more complex and uncontrollable than that of 3D Sn-based perovskites. Therefore, the bulky spacers play an important role in managing the crystallization process compared to 3D Sn-based perovskites due to the unique degree of freedom in 2DRP Sn-based perovskites.

Substantial efforts have been extensively devoted to explore the bulky spacers. To date, all bulky spacers incorporated in 2DRP Sn-based perovskites, however, are traditional halide ammonium salts, including butylammonium iodide (BAI) [15,22], phenethylammonium iodide (PEAI) [27], phenethylammonium chloride (PEACl) [28], and 5-ammoniumvaleric acid iodide (5-AVAI) [29]. The halide ions in these traditional bulky spacers have less interaction with perovskite precursors, losing enough ability to control such complex crystallization process. This interaction controls the assembly of 2DRP Sn-based perovskite segments and the crystallization process. Exploration of novel bulky spacers with such controllable interaction enables the enhancement of the stability and the optoelectronic conversion efficiency of 2DRP Sn-based PSCs.

Here, we utilize a new ionic liquid-bulky spacer, butylammonium acetate (BAAcO), to replace traditional halide spacers, BAI, in 2DRP Sn-based perovskite to enhance the inter-component interaction and control the crystallization process. The  $ \mathrm{AcO}^- $ -functional group in BAAcO has a strong interaction with formamidine ions  $ (\mathrm{FA}^{+}) $  and  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  in precursor solution, leading to the formation of intermediates and the delayed crystallization rate. The strengthened interaction

endows the high quality 2DRP Sn-based perovskites with narrow phase distribution and highly vertical crystal orientation. Benefiting from this high quality 2DRP Sn-based perovskite fabricated from BAAcO, the carrier extraction and transport are more efficient, favoring the PCE of solar cells up to 10.36% (7.16% BAI), which is the highest report in the state-of-the-art 2DRP Sn-based PSCs. Most importantly, the corresponding devices hold ~90% of initial efficiency after storage in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere for ~600 h without any encapsulation.

# 2 Experimental

We mixed BAAcO (Figure S1) or BAI, PEACl, FAI, and   $  SnI_{2}  $   with suitable stoichiometric ratios and added   $  SnF_{2}  $   and guanidine iodide (GAI) acting as additives to prepare the precursors of 2DRP Sn-based perovskites (  $  BA_{x}PEA_{1-x}  $  )2-  $  FA_{n-1}Sn_{n}I_{3n+1}  $   (x=0.3, <n>=4) and then fabricated films through simple solution method with chlorobenzene (CB) as anti-solvent. Finally, We incorporated these 2DRP Sn-based perovskite films into devices with the structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS (~40 nm)/2DRP Sn-based perovskite (~150 nm)/ICBA (~50 nm)/LiF (1 nm)/Al (100 nm) (Figure 1a). More details about experimental sections can be found in Supporting Information online.

# 3 Results and discussion

The PCE of 2DRP Sn-based PSCs fabricated from using BAAcO (+BAAcO) instead of BAI (+BAI) acting as bulky spacers is improved from 7.16% (  $  V_{OC}  $  : 0.58 V,   $  J_{SC}  $  : 19.27 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and FF: 64.02%) to 10.36% (  $  V_{OC}  $  : 0.65 V,   $  J_{SC}  $  : 23.33 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and FF: 68.56%), as shown in Figure 1b and Table 1 (the optimization of PCE is shown in Table S1). To the best of our knowledge, this is the highest record among 2DRP Sn PSCs (Table S2). The +BAAcO solar cells behave negligible hysteresis effect (Figure 1c, d), and exhibit stable outputs of current density and PCE under 1 sun illumination (AM 1.5G) at a maximum power point voltage (Figure 1e). Furthermore, +BAAcO 2DRP Sn-based PSCs show better repeatability than +BAI devices (Figure 1f).

Many studies [25,30,31] have reported that carboxylate ions have strong interaction with perovskite precursors, like  $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $ , and  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $ , in solution. Similarly, the  $ \mathrm{AcO}^- $ -functional group in  $ +\mathrm{BAAcO} $  still has strong interaction with  $ \mathrm{FA}^+ $  and  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $ , and the enhanced performance of  $ +\mathrm{BAAcO} $  2DRP Sn-based PSCs is attributed to this strong interaction, which can control crystallization process, and also the passivation effect of  $ \mathrm{AcO}^- $  [32].

As shown in Figure 2a (left), the +BAAcO samples do not show any perovskite peak (  $  2\theta\approx13.94^{\circ}  $  corresponding to (111) plane and  $  2\theta\approx28.14^{\circ}  $  corresponding to (202) plane in 2DRP Sn-based perovskite [22]) until anti-solvent CB was dipped at time point of 35 s. On the contrary, the +BAI samples show apparent perovskite peaks at time point of 35 s before CB was dipped (Figure 2b), left). It is evident that the crystallization rate was delayed effectively in the +BAAcO samples compared with that of +BAI samples due to the effectively strengthened interaction with perovskite precursors. Furthermore, an upshift of  $  2\theta  $  from crystallization time point of 35 s (with CB dipped) to crystallization time point of 60 s (Figure 2a, right) was observed in the +BAAcO samples. The upshift of  $  2\theta  $  was recovered quickly after the samples were annealed under  $  60^{\circ}C  $  for 1 min (Figure 2a, right, 60-1) and no further changes during subsequent annealing treatments. By contrast, no diffraction shift was observed during crystallization process of the +BAI samples (Figure 2b, right). Based on the basic equation,  $  2d\sin\theta=n\lambda  $ , of XRD, the upshift of  $  2\theta  $  is caused by the decrease of interplanar spacing d of the crystals (crystal shrinkage) [33].

Therefore, we speculate that the  $ \mathrm{AcO}^{-} $ ions enter the perovskite lattices and then form the intermediates  $ (\mathrm{BA}_{0.3}\mathrm{PEA}_{0.7})_2\mathrm{FA}_3\mathrm{Sn}_4(\mathrm{I}_{1 - x}\mathrm{Ac}_x)_{13} $  ( $ 0 < x < 1 $ ,  $ < n > = 4 $ ) during the crystallization process of the +BAAcO samples, resulting in the crystal shrinkage and then leading to the upshift of  $ 2\theta $ . Moreover, the upshift of  $ 2\theta $  recovers rapidly after annealing treatment probably resulting from the thermodynamic instability of intermediates.

To verify the above speculation, the Castep module in Materials Studio with Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) was used to optimize the related structure of   $  (\mathrm{BA}_{0.3}\mathrm{PEA}_{0.7})_{2}\mathrm{FA}_{3}-\mathrm{Sn}_{4}\mathrm{I}_{13}  $   (<n>=4) and intermediates   $  (\mathrm{BA}_{0.3}\mathrm{PEA}_{0.7})_{2}\mathrm{FA}_{3}\mathrm{Sn}_{4}-(\mathrm{I}_{1-x}\mathrm{Ac}_{x})_{13}  $   (<n>=4), as shown in Figure 2c and Table S3. The calculation results indicate the existence of crystal structures of intermediates with different   $  AcO^{-}  $  substitution. Furthermore, the crystal lattices shrank by   $  \sim0.24\%  $   and   $  \sim0.2\%  $   in c direction when different   $  AcO^{-}  $  enter the lattice, consistent with the results in Figure 2a. Moreover, the intermediates with different   $  AcO^{-}  $  substitution have higher total energy of   $  \sim2,974  $   kcal/mol and   $  \sim3,280  $   kcal/mol than   $  \sim2,552  $   kcal/mol of the structure with all I ions, which proves the thermodynamic instability of the intermediates corresponding to the above speculation [34].

The reduced recombination and the effective carrier extraction at the interface not only conduce the improved current density, but also assist the enhanced voltage and fill factor.

To further gain more insights into effects of BAAcO on bulk traps and surface defects of 2DRP Sn-based perovskites, the dark current density and open circuit voltage/current density depending on light intensity (  $  V_{OC}  $   vs. light intensity and   $  J_{SC}  $   vs. light intensity), with relationships of   $  V_{\mathrm{OC}} \propto (n k T / q) \ln I  $   and   $  J_{SC} \propto I^{\alpha}  $  , respectively, were measured. The lower dark current density of +BAAcO samples, as shown in Figure S10a, indicates the fewer bulk traps and surface defects in +BAAcO 2DRP Sn-based perovskites. Moreover, the lower light ideal factor (n=1.230) of the samples based on +BAAcO (n=1.257 of +BAI samples, Figure S10b) demonstrates the suppressed Shockley-Read-Hall (SRH) trap-assisted recombination [40], confirming again the reduced trap states in +BAAcO 2DRP Sn-based perovskites. The higher   $  \alpha  $   value of 0.970 in +BAAcO samples (0.915 in +BAI sample, Figure S10c) explains not only the reduced non-geminate recombination [41], but also the weaker space charge effect [42], corresponding to the high quality 2DRP Sn-based perovskite films with high crystallinity, few pinholes, and efficient carrier transport/extraction.

The stability of both devices and films was also studied (Figure 5). It is clear that the +BAAcO devices maintain ~90% of origin efficiency for ~600 h when they are kept in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere without any encapsulation, whereas the devices based on +BAI only hold ~55% after ~200 h, as shown in Figure 5a. Furthermore, the degradation of +BAAcO perovskite films is not apparent after being placed in air with average temperature of ~25 °C and humidity of ~40% for ~7 h. However, the +BAI perovskite films show distinct

decomposition, as shown in Figure 5b. The improved stability in +BAAcO 2DRP Sn-based perovskites is ascribed to the restrained oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $  , which has been illustrated by the higher intensity of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   signal in +BAAcO films, as shown in Figure 5c, d. Moreover, pinholes are always the paths for direct contact between oxygen/water and   $  Sn^{2+}  $   inside the perovskites, thereby causing the decomposition of perovskites. Therefore, the high quality films with few pinholes fabricated from BAAcO can also contribute to the inhibited oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $  .

In order to further explore the deep mechanism of the improved stability in +BAAcO 2DRP Sn-based perovskites, we further investigated the O 1s, Sn 3d  $  ^{5}  $  , and N 1s spectra of X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). As can be seen from Figure 5e, the apparent dominant peak at the binding energy of ~531.98 eV in +BAAcO 2DRP Sn-based perovskite films, which can be assigned to COO  $  ^{-}  $   functional groups [43], demonstrates the residual Ac in films. As discussed in Figure 2, the intermediates with AcO  $  ^{-}  $   entering the lattice are not stable. We thus propose that the residual AcO  $  ^{-}  $   probably exist on the film surface and grain boundaries rather than lattices. Moreover, the peaks of O 1s, Sn 3d  $  ^{5}  $  , and N 1s all show the increase in binding energy of ~0.18 eV, illustrating the existence of chemical interaction between residual AcO  $  ^{-}  $   and Sn  $  ^{2+}  $  /NH  $  _{2}^{+}  $   (Figure 5e–g) [32]. The strong chemical interactions protect the film surface from oxygen/water, passivate the grain boundaries [44,45], and suppress the ion migration, therefore resulting in the improved environmental stability of +BAAcO 2DRP Sn-based perovskites (Figure 5h). Furthermore, the +BAAcO perovskite films show no apparent peaks corresponding to Sn  $  ^{0}  $   defects in XPS spectra compared with +BAI sample (Figure 5f), which is still attributed to this strong chemical interaction, protecting the FA from volatilizing out of perovskite crystals (the main reason for generation of Sn  $  ^{0}  $   defects) [46,47]. The fewer Sn  $  ^{0}  $   defects in +BAAcO 2DRP Sn-based perovskites contribute to the improved performance and stability of photovoltaic devices.

# 4 Conclusions

In summary, we report a new ionic liquid-bulky spacer BAAcO instead of the traditional halide ammonium salt spacer, BAI, to effectively regulate the crystallization process of 2DRP Sn-based perovskites through the formation of intermediates and the retarded crystallization rate. The strengthened interaction between  $ \mathrm{AcO}^- $  and  $ \mathrm{FA}^{+}/\mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  was successfully revealed, which is suggested to be difficult in traditional halide spacers. The high quality 2DRP Sn-based perovskite films with narrow phase distribution and highly vertical crystal orientation were achieved, which is beneficial to the efficient carrier transport and extraction.
