# Vertical Orientated Dion-Jacobson Quasi-2D Perovskite Film with Improved Photovoltaic Performance and Stability

Fengzhu Li, Jie Zhang, SaeByeok Jo, Minchao Qin, Zhen Li, Tiantian Liu, Xinhui Lu, Zonglong Zhu,* and Alex K.-Y. Jen*

Dion–Jacobson (DJ) phase 2D layered perovskites with diammonium organic cations demonstrate improved stability over 3D perovskites under thermal/photo/moisture stresses. However, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of DJ phase perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) is often limited by the poor charge transport across the perovskite layers due to the crystal growth direction that tends to be parallel to the substrate. Here, a simple and effective method is demonstrated by employing a   $  NH_{4}SCN  $   additive to facilitate the orientation of perovskite crystal growth to be perpendicular to the substrate. Also, the layer number distribution can be narrowed to around n=3 and n=4 with   $  NH_{4}SCN  $   addition. The device derived from the quasi-2D DJ (BDA)  $  (MA)_{4}Pb_{5}I_{16}  $   perovskite film processed with   $  NH_{4}SCN  $   shows a PCE of 14.53%, which is among the highest values reported for 2D PVSCs prepared at room temperature. Moreover, the device retains 85% of its initial PCE after 900 h storage in ambient conditions with a humidity level of   $  50\pm5\%  $  . These results demonstrate that this attractive approach will enable highly efficient and stable PVSCs to be made for renewable energy applications.

Recently, 2D perovskites have emerged to show some promise of addressing these issues due to their better thermodynamic and environmental stability.  $  ^{[17-30]}  $   Due to different organic spacer groups, two main archetypes of 2D perovskites have been investigated, namely, Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) and Dion–Jacobson (DJ) layered 2D perovskites with general formulas

of   $  A^{\prime}_{2}A_{n-1}Pb_{n}X_{3n+1}  $   and   $  A^{\prime\prime}A_{n-1}Pb_{n}X_{3n+1}  $   (  $  A^{\prime}  $   and   $  A^{\prime\prime}  $   are mono- and diammonium organic cations respectively, A is   $  \mathrm{CH(NH_{2})_{2}}  $   (FA),   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}  $   (MA) or Cs, X is halide, n=1, 2, ...).

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites have attracted tremendous attentions as one of the most promising materials for next-generation photovoltaics because of their superior semiconducting properties, facile solution processability, and low fabrication cost.  $  ^{[1-10]}  $   They possess easily tunable bandgaps, high absorption coefficients, low exciton binding energy, and

The RP layered perovskites are more investigated 2D perovskites, which incorporate two layers of monovalent ammonium cations (butylamine, phenylamine, etc.) between the lead halide inorganic layers per unit cell, thus there is a van der Waals gap between the adjacent unit cells.  $  ^{[24-30]}  $   In contrast, the unit cell of DJ layered perovskites contains only one single layer of diammonium organic cations, such as 1,4-butanediammonium (BDA), inserted into lead halide inorganic layers, leading to a more rigid structure than that of RP phase.  $  ^{[31,34]}  $   It is worth noting that although the RP perovskites possess higher flexibility than the DJ perovskites, the unique structure of DJ perovskites allows the inorganic stacks to be more uniform and closer, rendering better alignment and less displacement of perovskites. Additionally, the shortened interlayer distance might reduce the barrier of charge transfer between inorganic   $  [PbI_{6}]^{4-}  $   slabs, which will benefit the charge transport of derived quasi-2D perovskite devices.  $  ^{[35-39]}  $   These characteristics enable DJ perovskites to be a promising class of masteries for photovoltaic applications. However, it is important for the perovskites layers to grow and be oriented perpendicular to the substrate in order to facilitate the charge transport and collection by electrodes. long carrier diffusion lengths, which enable the derived perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) to demonstrate a very high certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.2% recently.  $  ^{[11,12]}  $   Although the high PCE of PVSCs is on par with those from the inorganic counterparts, there are still some concerns about their long-term stability under the vigorous operational conditions.  $  ^{[13-16]}  $   In this regard, the recent research efforts have been redirected at improving the PVSC stability under thermal/photo/moisture stresses.

Combining the GIWAXS and TA results, there are three possible effects of   $  NH_{4}SCN  $   on the formation of perovskite films in our work. First, the   $  SCN^{-}  $  in precursor solution may replace   $  I^{-}  $  and bonded to   $  [PbI_{6}]^{4-}  $ , because of the lone pair electrons from the S and N atoms in   $  SCN^{-}  $  can offer strong interactions with   $  Pb^{2+}  $ .  $  ^{[43]}  $   The exposed end of   $  SCN^{-}  $  is negatively charged, and thus the perovskite colloids with   $  SCN^{-}  $  should be mutually repulsive in the precursor, resulting in the potential dimensional control of colloids and the change of film structure, leading to more colloids with n = 3 and n = 4 demonstrated by above TA results. Second, considering the negatively charged end of   $  SCN^{-}  $ , the interaction between substrate and precursor/colloids may also impact the vertical orientation of quasi-2D perovskite. We studied the effect of the electrostatic force between substrates and colloids on different film structures (Figure S7, Supporting Information). The XRD results of the films showed that the orientation parallel to the substrate was significantly diminished when the surface charge is changed from positive (on glass) to neutral (on PTAA) or negative (on poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)-poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) and PTAA (doped with F4TCNQ)). It is noteworthy that Kanatzidis and co-workers reported that the crystal orientation preferred to be vertical when the n-number is larger than 2. Although the n-number was concentrated at n = 3 and n = 4 after adding 2   $  SCN^{-}  $ , the parallel and vertical orientation in the film on glass was still comparable. Therefore, we believe that the electrostatic interactions are one of the reasons among the surface energy caused   $  SCN^{-}  $  induced vertical orientation. At last, HSCN and   $  CH_{3}NH_{2}  $   gases formed from the reaction of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}  $  cations and   $  SCN^{-}  $  anions during thermal annealing

might help repair and improve the crystalline quality of perovskite thin films.  $  ^{[22,41,42]}  $

The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra of quasi-2D (BDA)(MA) $ _{4} $ Pb $ _{5} $ I $ _{16} $  perovskite with or without NH $ _{4} $ SCN are presented in Figure 4a. Compared with the control film without NH $ _{4} $ SCN, a redshift among PL peaks was observed in the PL spectra with 1 SCN $ ^{-} $ , 2 SCN $ ^{-} $ , and 3 SCN $ ^{-} $ . However, PL spectrum of the film with  $ x $  SCN $ ^{-} $  shows nearly no peaks for the quantum wells of  $ n = 1, 2, 3, $  and 4 no matter the use of front or back excitation (Figure S8, Supporting Information), unlike that from the quasi-2D RP perovskite film. $ ^{[21-33]} $  This implies that the film structure should be uniform from top to the bottom of the entire perovskite films. In order to further explore the effect of SCN $ ^{-} $  on the defect states of quasi-2D perovskites, we have conducted temperature-dependent PL measurements on the films without or with 2 SCN $ ^{-} $  from 160 to 330 K (Figure 4e,f). The PL of both films showed blue-shifted peaks when the temperature was increased from 160 to 330 K, which might be due to the change of lattice strain induced by temperature. The PL width of 0 SCN $ ^{-} $  film showed a dumbbell-shape in the temperature range between 160 and 330 K. When the temperature was between 160 and 250 K, there are additional high-energy emissions from the 0 SCN $ ^{-} $  film. Because the PL spectra did not show inhomogeneous multipeak emission features (Figure 4b), we speculate that it could be due to the components of quasi-2D perovskite with smaller  $ n $ -numbers. The PL from these components were quenched by the nonradiative recombination that could be suppressed under low-temperatures ( $ < $ 250 K). When the temperature was increased over 300 K, the PL spectra were broadened due to the electron–phonon coupling at room temperature. In contrast, the PL spectra of film with 2 SCN $ ^{-} $  were broadened with temperature increased from 160 to 330 K. Furthermore, the PL spectra of 2 SCN $ ^{-} $  film showed better symmetry and narrower width than that of 0 SCN $ ^{-} $ . These features implied that 1) there are fewer defect states near the band edge of 2 SCN $ ^{-} $  film; 2) the components phase or  $ n $ -number distribution in 2 SCN $ ^{-} $  film is purer than that in 0 SCN $ ^{-} $  film, which is consistent with the TA results.

Time-resolved PL measurements are carried out on the perovskite film onto PTAA-coated ITO glass to study the effect of   $  NH_{4}SCN  $   on the excited-state properties of perovskites (Figure 4d). The average lifetime (  $  \tau_{avg}  $  ) can be calculated with   $  \tau_{avg} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} A_{i} \tau_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} A_{i} \tau_{i}}  $  .   $  \tau_{avg}  $   is 252.70 ns in the case of 0   $  SCN^{-}  $  , and it decreases with the   $  NH_{4}SCN  $   addition, indicating that   $  NH_{4}SCN  $   additive facilitates the transfer of the photogenerated charge carriers from perovskite to ITO/PTAA (Table S2, Supporting Information).

The diode ideal factor  $ (n) $ , which can be calculated with the equation of  $ n = k_{\mathrm{B}}T / q\times V_{\mathrm{OC}} / \ln J_{\mathrm{L}} $ , where  $ k_{\mathrm{B}}, T,q, $  and  $ J_{\mathrm{L}} $  are Boltzmann constant, absolute temperature, elementary charge, and photogenerated current density, respectively, is commonly used to describe the dependence of applied light intensity and voltage.[44,45] The  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  and  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  were plotted as a function of light intensity in Figure 5g,h, respectively. The device based on the control film has a slope of  $ 2.49k_{\mathrm{B}}T / q $  (Figure 5g), suggesting a severe trap-assisted Shockley-Read-Hall (SRH) recombination in the device.[46] In contrast, the slope was reduced to  $ 1.36k_{\mathrm{B}}T / q $  for the device based on the film with 2 SCN-, indicating that the SRH recombination was effectively suppressed due to the better crystal quality demonstrated by XRD results. The plots in Figure 5h show a slope of 0.96 and 0.97 for the devices based on the (BDA)(MA) $ _4 $ Pb $ _5 $ I $ _{16} $  without and with 2 SCN-, respectively. The larger slope of the device with 2 SCN- manifests the weaker bimolecular recombination, which can lead to higher  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  and FF.[47] This should be due to the vertical crystallite orientation and better crystal quality by using NH $ _4 $ SCN additive.

Furthermore, the long-term stability of devices based on the film with 0 SCN  $  ^{-}  $  and 2 SCN  $  ^{-}  $  (Figure 5i) was tested.

In conclusion, we demonstrated highly efficient quasi-2D DJ (BDA)(MA)  $  _{4}  $  Pb  $  _{5}  $  I  $  _{16}  $  PVSCs by tuning the crystal vertical alignment as well as the layer number distribution with NH  $  _{4}  $  SCN additives. The evolution of film crystallite orientation with different amounts of NH  $  _{4}  $  SCN has been revealed by XRD and GIWAXS measurements. By optimizing perovskite films and devices, an inverted PVSC with vertically grown DJ (BDA)(MA)  $  _{4}  $  Pb  $  _{5}  $  I  $  _{16}  $  perovskite showed a high PCE of 14.53%, which is among the highest PCEs reported for 2D PVSCs prepared at room temperature. Moreover, the fabricated devices possessed good stability, which could retain 85% of their initial PCE under a relative humidity of 50 ± 5% for 900 h. We believe that this simple but effective strategy can provide more efficient fabrication processes of quasi-2D perovskites for PVSCs.
