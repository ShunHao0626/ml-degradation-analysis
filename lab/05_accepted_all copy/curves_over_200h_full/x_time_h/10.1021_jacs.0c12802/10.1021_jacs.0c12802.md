# Synergistic Effect of Fluorinated Passivator and Hole Transport Dopant Enables Stable Perovskite Solar Cells with an Efficiency Near 24%

Hongwei Zhu, Yameng Ren,  $  ^{*}  $   Linfeng Pan, Olivier Ouellette, Felix T. Eickemeyer, Yinghui Wu, Xianggao Li,  $  ^{*}  $   Shirong Wang, Hongli Liu, Xiaofei Dong, Shaik M. Zakeeruddin, Yuhang Liu,  $  ^{*}  $   Anders Hagfeldt, and Michael Grätzel  $  ^{*}  $

ABSTRACT: Long-term durability is critically important for the commercialization of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). The ionic character of the perovskite and the hydrophilicity of commonly used additives for the hole-transporting layer (HTL), such as lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) and tert-butylpyridine (tBP), render PSCs prone to moisture attack, compromising their long-term stability. Here we introduce a trifluoromethylation strategy to overcome this drawback and to boost the PSC's solar to electric power conversion efficiency (PCE). We employ 4-(trifluoromethyl)benzylammonium iodide (TFMBAI) as an amphiphilic modifier for interfacial defect mitigation and 4-(trifluoromethyl)pyridine (TFP) as an additive to enhance the HTL's hydrophobicity. Surface treatment of the

triple-cation perovskite with TFMBAI largely suppressed the nonradiative charge carrier recombination, boosting the PCE from 20.9% to 23.9% and suppressing hysteresis, while adding TFP to the HTL enhanced the PCS's resistance to moisture while maintaining its high PCE. Taking advantage of the synergistic effects resulting from the combination of both fluoromethylated modifiers, we realize TFMBAI/TFP-based highly efficient PSCs with excellent operational stability and resistance to moisture, retaining over 96% of their initial efficiency after 500 h maximum power point tracking (MPPT) under simulated 1 sun irradiation and 97% of their initial efficiency after 1100 h of exposure under ambient conditions to a relative humidity of 60–70%.

# INTRODUCTION

During the past decade the development of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has become a game-changer in the field of photovoltaics with relatively rapid progress in the attainment of power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of over 25%.  $  ^{1-4}  $   Along with the fundamental breakthroughs, the commercialization of PSCs has advanced.  $  ^{5,6}  $   Improvement in the stability of highly efficient PSC devices remains the most challenging issue  $  ^{7-9}  $   to this PV technology. The best-performing PSCs are mostly based on an n-i-p device structure, where n refers to an n-type semiconductor, normally   $  TiO_{2}  $    $  ^{10,11}  $   or   $  SnO_{2}  $  .  $  ^{12,13}  $   The organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite used as the light-harvesting layer is sandwiched between the electron-transporting layer (ETL) and the hole-transporting layer (HTL) for selective charge carrier extraction. Since the perovskite is protected by the   $  TiO_{2}  $   or   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL as well the transparent conductive substrate, e.g. a fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) or indium-doped tin oxide (ITO) glass, water and oxygen ingression from the environment destroys the PSCs mainly via the perovskite/HTL interface.  $  ^{14,15}  $   Ambient moisture

Fluorination of the phenyl ring of PEAI has been previously proved to be beneficial for increasing hydrophobicity and improving interfacial charge transport.  $  ^{26,27}  $   Here we investigate the amphiphilic 4-(trifluoromethyl)benzylammonium iodide (TFMBAI) as a molecular modifier to further substantially augment the hydrophobicity of   $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.85}MA_{0.10}Pb-(I_{0.97}Br_{0.03})_{3}  $   triple-cation perovskite films (Figure 1a).

TFMBAI anchors to the perovskite surface by insertion of its ammonium group in A cation surface sites (Figure 1b). Apart from enhancing the hydrophobicity, the trifluoromethylation also inhibits the access of lithium ions to the perovskite. The latter thus can assume a triple function; its polar ammonium head group inserts into A cation vacancies present at the perovskite surface preventing nonradiative charge carrier recombination, while the strongly hydrophobic fluorinated methyl tail protects the perovskite film from being attacked by water molecules and from the ingression of lithium ions used as dopants for the hole conductor. In this fashion the TFMBAI modifier boosts both the efficiency and stability of the PSC.

The perovskite film treated with TFMBAI exhibits a much higher photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) and lower   $  V_{oc}  $   loss against the radiative limit in comparison with the pristine film, revealing the excellent defect passivation effect of TFMBAI. To increase the hydrophobicity of the spiro-OMeTAD HTL, while maintaining the high efficiency of the spiro-OMeTAD/Li-TFSI/tBP system, we added 4-(trifluoromethyl) pyridine (TFP) to the HTL (Figure 1a,b). Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements and external photoluminescence quantum efficiencies (  $  PLQY_{ext}  $  ) show that the introduction of TFP does not alter the extraction of holes and recombination processes at the perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD interface. A champion PSC device based on perovskite/TFMBAI/(spiro-OMTAD/TFP) obtained an outstanding   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.163 V, with a low voltage loss of 119 mV with regard to the radiative limit. Remarkably, TFMBAI/TFP-based PSCs retain over 96% of their initial efficiency after 500 h maximum power point tracking (MPPT) under simulated full-sun irradiation and 97% of their initial efficiency after 1100 h under ambient conditions at a relative humidity of 60–70%.

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

TFMBAI Passivation. Benzylammonium iodide (BAI) and phenylethylammonium iodide (PEAI) have been applied previously to modify the perovskite/HTL interfaces.  $  ^{28-30}  $   Our earlier research showed that chemically tailored passivation materials with different functional substituent groups can improve the photovoltaic performance and stability of PSCs simultaneously.  $  ^{31,32}  $   In this study we introduce a trifluoromethyl group at the para position of the phenyl ring in BAI, yielding TFMBAI as a molecular modifier for PSCs. Although TFMBAI has been reported to find application in PSCs to produce 2D Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) perovskites, highly efficient PSCs based on TFMBAI passivation have rarely been reported.  $  ^{33-35}  $   In comparison with benzylammonium iodide (BAI) passivated perovskite, the contact angle between a water droplet and trifluoromethylated TFMBAI is improved from 79 to 102° (Figure S1), implying superior moisture resistivity for TFMBAI-passivated perovskites.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was applied to characterize the surface morphologies of the perovskites without and with TFMBAI passivation, as shown in Figure 1c,d. The slightly textured structure as shown in Figure 1d indicates the formation of a top layer on the surface of the perovskite film, while the grain size does not change in comparison with the pristine perovskite film. Effect of TFP as HTL Dopant. The chemical structure of TFP is shown in Figure 1a. TFP is derived from tBP, where the tert-butyl group on tBP is substituted by a trifluoromethyl group. The introduction of TFP into the HTL substantially increased the resistance to moisture uptake, as revealed by contact angle measurements (CAMs). As shown in Figure S4, the contact angle between a water droplet and HTL films with varying amounts of TFP (denoted as TFPx, where x = 0.1–0.6 times the relative molar concentration of tBP added to the spiro-OMeTAD/tBP solution) increases from 70.5 to 97.5°, indicating an increased hydrophobicity by the substitution of tBP by TFP. To verify the potential photovoltaic application of TFP in solar cell devices, PSC devices with a n-i-p structure of FTO glass/compact-TiO₂/mesoscopic-TiO₂/perovskite/HTL/gold were fabricated, in which perovskite refers to a composition of Cs₀.₀₅FA₀.₈₅MA₀.₁₀Pb(I₀.₉₇Br₀.₀₃)₃ with a 5% excess of PbI₂, and various amounts of TFP were introduced into the HTL. The J-V curves are shown in Figure S5, with summarized photovoltaic parameters being shown in Table S2. Concentration screening shows that the best photovoltaic performances were obtained at x = 0.3 TFP concentration, at which the film shows good hydrophobicity with a contact angle of up to 97.6°. Although Li-TFSI still exists within the HTL, the hydrophobicity of the HTL estimated from contact angle measurements is substantially increased. Surface morphologies of the HTLs with and without TFP dopant have been characterized by SEM (Figure S6). The negligible difference between the SEM images indicates that the introduction of TFP does not affect the film morphology during the casting of the HTL.

We analyzed the hole extraction and charge recombination of the perovskite/HTLs with TFP by time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) and absolute intensity PL measurements (Figure S7). We observed a similar TRPL decay for HTL-casted perovskite films with and without TFP. # CONCLUSION

A new strategy to improve the photovoltaic performance and operational stability of PSCs has been demonstrated. Through the synergistic effect of a trifluoromethylated passivation material (TFMBAI) and HTL dopant (TFP), we achieve an outstanding PCE approaching 24% for PSCs with excellent long-term stability. XRD, TRPL, and PLQY measurements reveal that the introduction of TFP can maintain the excellent hole extraction properties of the state of the art spiro-OMeTAD/tBP/Li-TFSI system, while increasing the hydrophobicity of the HTL film. Perovskite films with a TFMBAI passivator also exhibit significantly suppressed nonradiative

recombination at the perovskite/HTL interface and create a more moisture resistant interface. The champion cell based on perovskite/TFMBAI/TFP yielded an impressive PCE of 23.9%, which is considered among the best-performing mesoscopic PSCs so far. Furthermore, the improved hydrophobicity of the perovskite surface and HTL layer leads to a superior resistance to moisture uptake. Remarkably, PSCs with perovskite/TFMBAI/TFP retain over 96.5% of their initial PCE after 500 h of MPP tracking under continuous full-sun light irradiation, and more than 97% of their initial efficiency is maintained after 45 days under an RH of approximately 60% ambient air. Our work provides an effective approach to achieve high efficiency and long-term operational stability for the advanced PSC technique.
