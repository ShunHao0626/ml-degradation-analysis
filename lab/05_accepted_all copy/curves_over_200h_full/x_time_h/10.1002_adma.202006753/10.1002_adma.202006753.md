# Reducing Energy Disorder of Hole Transport Layer by Charge Transfer Complex for High Performance p–i–n Perovskite Solar Cells

Guiying Xu, Rongming Xue, Samuel J. Stuard, Harald Ade, Chenjie Zhang, Jianlin Yao, Yaowen Li,* and Yongfang Li

Solution-processed organic semiconductor charge-transport layers (OS-CTLs) with high mobility, low trap density, and energy level alignment have dominated the important progress in p–i–n planar perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs). Unfortunately, their inevitable long chains result in weak molecular stacking, which is likely to generate high energy disorder and deteriorate the charge-transport ability of OS-CTLs. Here, a charge-transfer complex (CTC) strategy to reduce the energy disorder in the OS-CTLs by doping an organic semiconductor, 4,4'-(4,8-bis(5-(trimethylsilyl)thiophen-2-yl)benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']dithiophene-2,6-diyl)bis(N,N-bis(4-methoxyphenyl)aniline) (BDT-Si), in a commercial hole-transport layer (HTL), poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine (PTAA), is proposed. The formation of the CTC makes the PTAA conjugated backbone electron-deficient, resulting in a quinoidal and stiffer character, which is likely to planarize the PTAA backbone and enhance the ordering of the film in nanoscale. The resultant HTL exhibits a reduced energy disorder, which simultaneously promotes hole transport in the HTL, hole extraction at the interface, energy level alignment, and quasi-Fermi level splitting in the device. As a result, the p–i–n planar pero-SCs with optimized HTL exhibit the best power conversion efficiency of 21.87% with good operating stability. This finding demonstrates that the CTC strategy is an effective way to reduce the energy disorder in HTLs and to improve the performance of planar pero-SCs.

Very few OS-CTLs of pero-SCs addressing energy disorder of the CTLs were reported in the literature. Thus far, only the electron transport layer (ETL) has demonstrably reduced energy disorder by enhancing molecular ordering. Huang and co-workers  $  ^{[14]}  $  found that the

energetic disorder in ETLs such as in [6,6]-phenyl- $ \mathrm{C}_{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) induced band tail and additional electronic states, leading to reduced quasi-Fermi level splitting and low efficiency. They proposed that solvent-annealed PCBM ETL with high ordering could mitigate its energy disorder and increase the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of p-i-n planar pero-SCs. Similarly, Miyano and co-workers[15] synthesized a highly-crystalline fullerene derivative  $ (\mathrm{C}_{60} \mathrm{MC}_{12}) $  to replace amorphous PCBM for enhancing the crystallization of ETL. The highly crystalline ETL could efficiently reduce energy disorder. In comparison, HTMs usually have more flexible chains required for increasing their solubility. These long and flexible chains make it difficult to improve molecular ordering through a simple external treatment, resulting in larger energy disorder and more serious surface recombination.[16] However, few attention has been paid to tuning the energy disorder of the HTL, which is likely more critical to the performance of p-i-n pero-SCs.

Charge-transport layers (CTLs), which significantly influence charge extraction and recombination, play an important role in improving photovoltaic performance of perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs).  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   Solution-processed organic semiconductors with versatile molecular structures, fine-tuned energy levels, and low-temperature processing have been intensively explored as both hole-transport materials (HTMs) and electron-transport materials (ETMs) for the pero-SCs.  $  ^{[4-7]}  $   However, their electronic states in thin films are dramatically affected by molecular stacking, and their weak intermolecular interactions usually have a high degree of energy disorder resulting in low carrier mobility and severe interface recombination.  $  ^{[8-11]}  $   Therefore, the performance of p–i–n pero-SCs, which is highly dependent on OS-CTLs, still lags that of n–i–p pero-SCs.  $  ^{[12,13]}  $

Poly[bis(4-phenyl) (2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA) is the most widely used HTL material in p–i–n planar pero-SCs due to its high film-forming quality at low temperature, matched energy levels, and nonwetting surface.  $  ^{[17-20]}  $   However, at the microscopic scale, PTAA HTL is likely to form high energy disorder and poor   $  \pi-\pi  $   interaction due to the long, distorted, and twisting triphenylamine (TPA)-based backbone. This inferior behavior results in unfavorable electronic states in the HTL in terms of hole mobility, surface recombination, and even quasi-Fermi level splitting.  $  ^{[21-23]}  $   Charge-transfer complexes (CTC) formed between the polymer donor and acceptor molecules in the electronic ground state have already been demonstrated to be an efficient way to tune the polymer chain conformation,  $  ^{[24-27]}  $   phase separation in blend films,  $  ^{[28,29]}  $   and photooxidation stability.  $  ^{[30,31]}  $   Paraschuk et al.  $  ^{[32]}  $   revealed that the CTC is likely to form when blending a small amount of the acceptor 2,4,7-trinitrofluorenone with the amorphous donor polymer poly[methoxy-5-(20-ethylhexyloxy)-1,4-phenylene-vinylene] (MEH-PPV). They pointed out that the formed CTC is highly dependent on the molecular skeletons of both the acceptor and donor polymer rather than the acceptor electron affinity and polymer concentration. As a result, the electron-deficient conjugated chain of MEH-PPV acquires a quinoidal character, making the conjugated polymer backbone stiffer and facilitating MEH-PPV backbone planarization and assembly into structurally and electronically ordered film at a nanoscale region.  $  ^{[24]}  $

Here, we synthesized a small molecule 4,4'-(4,8-bis(5-(trimethylsilyl)thiophen-2-yl)benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']dithiophene-2,6-diyl)bis(N,N-bis(4-methoxyphenyl)aniline) (BDT-Si) with a planar fused ring benzodithiophene (BDT) core and alkylsilyl side chains on the BDT unit, which favors electron density delocalization all over the molecule. We found that BDT-Si with homogenous electron distribution is likely to accept electrons from PTAA when doping it into PTAA, forming a CTC with the electron-deficient PTAA film possessing quinoidal character and planar geometry. This is beneficial in enhancing intermolecular stacking, reducing the energy disorder of the PTAA film. The resultant BDT-Si-doped PTAA film is able to overall improve the electronic states of HTL, such as the charge-transport ability, extraction at the interface of PTAA/perovskite, energy level alignment in the device as well as enhancing quasi-Fermi level splitting. Finally, the p–i–n planar pero-SCs with a structure of indium tin oxide (ITO)/PTAA:BDT-Si/Rb $  _{0.05}  $ Cs $  _{0.05}  $ FA $  _{0.8}  $ MA $  _{0.1}  $ PbI $  _{2.85}  $ Br $  _{0.15}  $ /C $  _{60}  $ /2,9-dimethyl-4,7-diphenyl-1,10-phenanthroline(BCP)/Cu exhibited a promising PCE of 21.87% with substantial enhancement in operating stability.

As shown in Figure 1a, the small molecule BDT-Si, consisting of a planar fused ring benzodithiophene (BDT) core  $  ^{[33-35]}  $  and alkylsilyl side chains on the BDT unit, was simply synthesized through lithiation and Suzuki coupling reactions. The details of the synthetic process were described in Experimental Section. The molecular structure of BDT-Si was confirmed by  $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR spectra,  $  {}^{13}  $  C NMR spectra, and MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry, as shown in Figures S1–S3, Supporting Information. Thermal gravimetric analysis measurement reveals that BDT-Si exhibits good thermal stability with a high thermal decomposition temperature of 445.5 °C (5 wt% loss, Figure S4, Supporting Information). The highest occupied molecular orbital energy level

(HOMO) of BDT-Si and PTAA were measured to be -5.34 and -5.26 eV by cyclic voltammetry (Figure S5a, Supporting Information). According to the optical bandgaps ( $ E_{\text{g}}^{\text{opt}} $ ) of PTAA (2.92 eV) and BDT-Si (2.47 eV) estimated from absorption edges ( $ \lambda_{\text{edge}} $ ) (Figure S5b, Supporting Information), the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels were determined to be -2.34 and -2.87 eV respectively. The detailed parameters are summarized in Table S1 (Supporting Information). Density functional theory (DFT) calculation on BDT-Si demonstrates that the electron density is not only delocalized over the BDT core but also homogeneously distributed over the alkylsilyl substituents (Figure 1a), which may be attributed to the bond interactions of the low-lying  $ \sigma^{*} $  orbital of the Si atom with the  $ \pi^{*} $  orbital of the aromatic units. $ ^{[36,37]} $  The charge delocalization over the BDT-Si molecule may facilitate the formation of the CTC.

To demonstrate the existence of CTCs in BDT-Si-doped PTAA films, we first explored the electronic behavior between PTAA and BDT-Si molecules. Polymer solar cells (PSCs) with the structure of ITO/PTAA or PTAA:BDT-Si (1:1)/C $ _{60} $ /BCP/Cu were constructed. As shown in Figure 1b, the PSC based on PTAA film showed a negligible  $ J_{sc} $ , while the PTAA:BDT-Si-based devices exhibited a significantly enhanced photocurrent response. This demonstrates that an efficient charge transfer (CT) occurred between PTAA and BDT-Si, which is similar to the photovoltaic response of the heterojunction PSCs with the binary active layer containing a donor and an acceptor. $ ^{[38]} $  Considering the LUMO energy level matching between PTAA and BDT-Si, we assume that BDT-Si may accept electrons from PTAA (see inset in Figure 1b), providing the possibility to form the donor–acceptor CTC. DFT calculation further confirmed the existence of CTC (Figure 1c). As for PTAA,  $ \pi $ -electrons are delocalized over the units of the PTAA molecule (the left solid rectangle). When the acceptor molecule BDT-Si is bound on the above-mentioned units, the electron density can be transferred from the PTAA chain to the BDT-Si acceptor, and the overlapping electronic orbitals between PTAA and BDT-Si would result in the formation of the CTC (right dash rectangle). $ ^{[39,40]} $  Moreover, theoretical calculation of HOMO and LUMO electron cloud was also demonstrated by DFT calculation (Figure S6, Supporting Information). The cross section of both HOMO and LUMO of the complex show an overlap  $ \pi $  clouds between the donor (upper) and acceptor (underneath). This result strongly proves the overlapping electronic orbitals between PTAA and BDT-Si, and the existence of CTC formation. In addition, the CTC generally shows closed HOMO and LUMO to those of the donor and acceptor, respectively, as illustrated in the inset of Figure 1d. $ ^{[41]} $  Therefore, the bandgap of CTC is usually smaller than those of the donor and acceptor, resulting in red-shifted absorption. As expected, increasing the content of BDT-Si in PTAA film results in a gradual red-shifting of the UV–vis absorption spectra in the region of 390–425 nm (Figure 1d). The new absorption band at  $ \approx $ 500 nm is ascribed to the ground state of the CTC.

To directly observe the formation of CTC, Raman spectroscopy characterization was employed. As shown in Figure 1e, the pristine PTAA film exhibited the strongest Raman band at  $ \approx 1600~\mathrm{cm^{-1}} $  corresponding to the symmetric stretching vibration of the phenyl ring in PTAA; this band downshifted by  $ \approx 5~\mathrm{cm^{-1}} $  after doping with  $ 2\% $  BDT-Si. The improved performance of the device based on PTAA:BDT-Si motivated us to investigate the influence of HTL energy disorder on the charge dynamic behavior of the devices. First, we measured time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectroscopy. Figure 5a shows the TRPL spectra of the pristine perovskite, PTAA/perovskite, and PTAA:BDT-Si/perovskite. The PL lifetimes were fitted with a biexponential decay, yielding a fast decay  $ (\tau_{1}) $  component and a slow decay  $ (\tau_{2}) $  component are shown in Table S3, Supporting Information. The short time scale  $ \tau_{1} $  is attributed to the charge transfer of free charge carriers at the interface, and  $ \tau_{2} $  represents the non-radiative decay of recombination.[50-52] For the pristine perovskite film, the  $ \tau_{2} $  exhibited an absolutely dominated ratio  $ (f_{2} = 99.56\%) $ , which indicates that the photogenerated carrier is mainly affected by the non-radiative decay mechanism. However, such dominating effect could be reduced by incorporating HTL, instead, the ratios of component  $ (f_{1}) $  were increased. For the PTAA/perovskite sample, the  $ f_{1} $  was increased to  $ 6.40\% $  accompanying with a  $ \tau_{1} $  of 3.78 ns. In comparison, PTAA:BDT-Si-based sample showed a continuously increased  $ f_{1} $  to  $ 8.60\% $  with a  $ \tau_{1} $  of 2.19 ns. The result suggests that PTAA:BDT-Si facilitates hole collection from perovskite due to its reduced energy disorder, which may improve the electronic states of PTAA such as aligning energy levels and increasing hole mobility. To further understand the

influence of HTLs with varying energy disorder on the exciton dissociation and charge collection properties in the operating devices, plots of photocurrent (  $  J_{ph}  $  ) versus the effective applied voltage (  $  V_{eff}  $  ) for the devices with or without BDT-Si were measured (Figure 5b).   $  J_{ph}  $   is defined as   $  J_{ph} = J_{L} - J_{D}  $  , where   $  J_{L}  $   is the photocurrent density measured under illumination (AM1.5G, 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) and   $  J_{D}  $   is the current density in the dark.   $  V_{eff}  $   is defined as   $  V_{eff} = V_{0} - V_{app}  $  , where   $  V_{0}  $   and   $  V_{app}  $   are the voltage when   $  J_{ph}  $   equals zero and the applied bias, respectively.  $  ^{[53,54]}  $   The exciton dissociation probability (  $  P_{diss}  $  ) is estimated using the equation   $  P_{diss} = J_{ph}/J_{sat}  $  , where   $  J_{sat}  $   is the saturation photocurrent density. The   $  J_{sat}  $   is mainly related to the absorbed incident photons at high   $  V_{eff}  $  , and it could be defined as the photocurrent, where the photogenerated excitons could be separated into free charge carriers and collected by corresponding electrodes. Therefore, the charge collection efficiency under different   $  V_{eff}  $   could be estimated by the   $  J_{ph}/J_{sat}  $   ratio.  $  ^{[55,56]}  $   The device with PTAA:BDT-Si HTL exhibited a high   $  P_{diss}  $   of 99.80%, while the devices based on PTAA HTL exhibited a lower   $  P_{diss}  $   of 97.22%. It suggests that the PTAA:BDT-Si HTL with high mobility and favorable energy level alignment could more efficiently extract charge carrier at PTAA:BDT-Si/perovskite interface and then transport to the electrode, giving a lower recombination. To investigate the recombination behavior in the whole device, we measured the electroluminescence (EL) spectra of pero-SCs based on PTAA and PTAA:BDT-Si HTL with the same injection current. The device based on PTAA:BDT-Si shows a higher EL intensity, suggesting that PTAA:BDT-Si HTL does not introduce additional or even slightly suppressed interface recombination (Figure S20, Supporting Information).  $  ^{[57,58]}  $   It agrees well with the decreased the ideality factors (  $  n_{1D}  $  ) from   $  1.55 \pm 0.07  $   (PTAA-based device) to   $  1.35 \pm 0.04  $   (PTAA:BDT-Si-based device) that obtained from the slope of the   $  V_{oc}  $   versus ln (  $  P_{light}  $  ) plot (Figure S21, Supporting Information).  $  ^{[59,60]}  $

To understand the influence of the HTLs on the operational stability, we carried out transmission electron microscopy (TEM) measurements to monitor changes in the morphology of the HTL under simulated AM1.5G irradiation  $ (100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2}) $  using a xenon-lamp-based solar simulator (SF300A, Sciencetech) without any ultraviolet filter in ambient air. As shown in Figure 6c, serious aggregation occurred in the pristine PTAA film after aging 4 days, while the PTAA:BDT-Si film maintained its morphology. It suggests that PTAA film with high energy disorder would more obviously accelerate PTAA chains movement under the driving force of irradiation and heating due to lack the bound of CTC, thus resulting in the serious aggregation. To study the influence of aggregation behavior on device performance in situ, the PTAA and PTAA:BDT-Si films aged from 0 to 4 days were used to fabricate pero-SCs. As shown in Figure 6d,e and

Table S4, Supporting Information, as the devices aged, the PCE of the devices with irradiated PTAA HTL dramatically dropped from 19.81% to 11.13% in just 4 days. This serious degradation was caused by the decreased   $  J_{sc}  $   and FF, suggesting that the charge transport and extraction abilities are substantially suppressed due to the aggregation of PTAA film. In contrast, the devices fabricated with irradiated PTAA:BDT-Si HTL maintained their efficiencies as they aged from 0 (PCE = 21.44%) to 4 days (PCE = 18.67%). This was due to the fact that the PTAA:BDT-Si HTL with low energy disorder could contribute to a stable morphology. However, we still observed a slightly decreased PCE for the PTAA:BDT-Si-based devices due to the reduced   $  V_{oc}  $  , which may stem from breaking the bond in the conjugated PTAA film.

In conclusion, we synthesized a small molecule BDT-Si with high homogenous electron distribution which can function as an electron acceptor to construct a CTC when blended with PTAA. The formed CTC reduced the energy disorder of the HTL PTAA film due to enhanced intermolecular stacking and ordering. Moreover, we also confirmed that this low-energy-disorder PTAA:BDT-Si film improved the electronic states of the HTL in the device, such as the increased hole transport ability in HTL, better hole extraction at the interface of PTAA:BDT-Si/perovskite, improved band energy level alignment as well as the increased quasi-Fermi level splitting in the devices. As a result, the p-i-n planar pero-SCs based on PTAA:BDT-Si HTL exhibited significantly improved photovoltaic performance, delivering a PCE of 21.87% and robust operating stability. This CTC strategy to reduce the energy disorder of the HTL opens a new avenue for improving the performance of p-i-n planar pero-SCs by breaking through the traditional CTL optimization and perovskite passivation methods.
