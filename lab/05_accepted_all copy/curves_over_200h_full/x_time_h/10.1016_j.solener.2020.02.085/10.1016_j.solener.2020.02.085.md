# ARTICLEINFO

Owing to the superior optoelectronic properties of perovskite materials, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been increased dramatically within several years, but the poor thermal, humidity, and light stability of these PSC devices hinders the progress to their practical application. We obtained an inspiration from two-dimensional (2D) Ruddlesden–Popper perovskite solar cells with good photovoltaic performance and placed the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite layer inside two fully-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite layers in the cubic   $  \alpha  $   phase. The middle layer has lower stability than the two outer ones, which protect the middle layer by impeding the organic ions of the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite middle layer from diffusing outside and causing damage to neighbor CTLs. Water molecules from air are also obstructed from reaching the hybrid perovskite layer. We used 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium tetrafluoroborate (BMIM-  $  BF_{4}  $  ) ionic liquid and 3-(decyldimethylammonio) propane-1-sulfonate (DDMAPS) and obtained phase-stable fully-inorganic   $  \alpha  $   phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $  . The constructed PSCs have extremely high stabilities and high PCEs. After 1000 h of illumination under AM1.5 illumination in air at 60 °C (Humility: ~60%), PSCs with a sandwich structure of three perovskite layers maintain nearly all the original PCE of 21.32%, while those without that only remain 76.63%.

# 1. Introduction

Fully-organic and organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells has intrigued enormous research interests of researchers (Green et al., 2014; Brenner et al., 2016; Zhang et al., 2016; McGehee, 2014; Park et al., 2016; McMeekin et al., 2016; Zhang et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2018; Huang et al.; Oener et al., 2017). The photovoltaic conversion efficiency (PCE) of these solar cells has been increased dramatically within several years, owing to the superior optoelectronic properties of fully-organic and organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials (NREL; Im et al., 2011; Wehrenfennig et al., 2014; Burschka et al., 2013; Jeon et al., 2014; Liu and Kelly, 2013; Yang et al., 2017; Kim et al., 2012; Tian et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2018). But in these materials organic cations; like methylammonium (MA) and formamidinium (FA) are volatile and tend to diffuse to neighbor carrier transport layers (CTLs) and do damage to them (Conings et al., 2015). In our previous work (Yang and Dettori, 2019), we developed a  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -layer-free PSC with good light stability and an averaged PCE of  $ 18.53\% $ , which is close to that of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -layer-containing devices, and rather high compared to other  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -layer-free PSCs reported, by inserting an layer of bathophenanthroline, which is applied by people as materials for hole-blocking layers or electron-transporting layers (ETLs) (Yuan et al., 2015); between the ITO substrate and the perovskite film.  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  can also be employed as ETL materials for PSCs (Ye et al., 2018; Zhao et al., 2018; Wang and Liao, 2018). The conduction band edge of   $  SnO_{2}  $   is rather lower than perovskite, resulting in a loss of voltage of PSCs (Xiong et al., 2018). Our research shows that bathophenanthroline layers in PSCs block hole transport, enhance the charge transport and promote light stability. But PCE of the prepared bathophenanthroline layer-containing PSCs still have an observable declining trend, only 94.9% remaining after 400 h continuous illumination. As a result, we continued our research to improve the stability of PSCs further.

Alternatively, fully-inorganic perovskites, such as   $  CsPbI_{3}  $  , are heat-stable up to 460 °C (Sutton et al., 2016). Furthermore   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   in the cubic   $  \alpha  $   phase; with a bandgap of 1.73 eV, (Beal et al., 2016) is rather appropriate for application in photovoltaic devices. However, it is well known that the cubic   $  \alpha  $   phase, tetragonal   $  \beta  $   phase and orthorhombic   $  \gamma  $   phase of fully-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite are unstable at room temperatures and tends to degrade rapidly to a non-perovskite   $  \delta  $   phase, which dramatically reduces the absorption of sun light and gives poor photovoltaic performance. Additionally, the PCE of all-inorganic PSCs is relatively low compared to PSCs made of fully-organic and hybrid perovskites.

The total Gibbs free energy, including contributions from both the bulk and surface of a phase of a material, determines its thermodynamic stability. Reduction of the material size would give a rise in the ratio between surface and volume, so a unstable phase can be made stable owing to the increase of surface energy contribution. Some researchers then used solvent to control the growth of full-inorganic perovskite film (Wang et al., 2018). Herein we used our agents to decrease crystal grain dimensions and obtained phase-stable full-inorganic  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  in a cubic  $ \alpha $  phase.

Some researchers (Wang et al., 2019) inserted a chlorinated graphene oxide (CGO) layer between the full-organic perovskite layer and carrier transport layer and found that strong bonds are formed between lead of the perovskite layer and both chlorine and oxygen atoms in the CGO layer. This inserted layer made fabricate solar cells maintained  $ 90\% $  of their initial PCE after operation at  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $ , however apparent PCE drops still existing. They stated that the inserted CGO layer and the formed heterostructure thereafter between CGO layer and the perovskite layer can selectively extract photogenerated charge carriers and impede the loss of decomposed components from soft perovskites, thereby reducing damage to the organic charge-transporting semiconductors. The apparent PCE drops show that though there are strong bonds, the formed heterostructure cannot function well enduringly as people wish and cannot impede the loss of the volatile components for long times.

Researchers tend to design cells with fewer layers if possible as they believe that would avoid carrier and PCE losses due to the resistance of interfaces between neighbor layers. We noticed that two-dimensional (2D) Ruddlesden–Popper perovskite solar cells also show good photovoltaic performance, though neighbor 2D layers of the perovskite film are only loosely connected, unlike the 3D perovskite film where neighbor layers are tightly bonded together. We obtained inspiration from that and designed solar cells with three 3D perovskite films, which form a sandwich structure, so as to garner both high PCEs and extremely high stabilities. We placed the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite layer inside two other fully-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite layers in a cubic   $  \alpha  $   phase, because the middle layer has lower stability than the two outer ones, which are to protect the middle layer by preventing the organic ions of the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite middle layer from diffusing to neighbor ETL and hole transport layers (HTLs). Water molecules from air are impeded from reaching the hybrid perovskite layer as well. Section 2 describes experimental methods. Results and conclusions are presented in Sections 3 and 4 respectively.

# 3. Results and discussion

In organic light-emitting diodes or solar cells, bathophenanthroline is applied to block hole transport, enhance the charge transport and promote light stability. Fig. 2(b) demonstrates the architecture of the PSC without that structure, instead with only one perovskite layer-the hybrid one. Fig. 3(a) and (b) show the structure formula of 1-Butyl-3-methylimidazolium tetrafluoroborate (BMIM-BF $ _4 $ ) ionic liquid (IL) and 3-(decyldimethylammonio) propane-1-sulfonate (DDMAPS), respectively.

Fig. 3(c) and (d) presents the top-view SEMs of the hybrid perovskite film and the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  perovskite film, respectively. It is observed

that for the hybrid perovskite film, the crystal grains, whose averaged dimension is around 150 nm, are arranged rather densely, facilitating charge carrier transport, while for the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite film, the averaged dimension of the crystal grains is rather small, around 20–30 nm, due to the function of BMIM-  $  BF_{4}  $   and DDMAS put in the precursor solution. We know the total Gibbs free energy, including contribution from both the bulk and surface of the material, determines the thermodynamic stability of a phase. Reduction of the size of crystal grains of full-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   in a cubic   $  \alpha  $   phase would give a rise in the ratio between surface and volume ratio (Wang et al., 2018), so a formerly unstable phase is now made stable. The extremely high stabilities of the cells with a sandwich structure proved that the full-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   in a cubic   $  \alpha  $   phase we fabricated is phase-stable, as otherwise it is impossible for the cells to owe such stabilities.

External quantum efficiency (EQE) measurements were performed and the results are presented in Fig. 5. It is observed that the cells with a sandwich structure of three perovskite layers have higher EQEs and therefore current densities than those without that. The hybrid perovskite shows best efficiency in transforming light in the range of 500–750 nm, so enhancement of charge transfer would incur most significant response from light in the range.

A surprisingly magnificent promotion of cell stability was obtained due to the application of the sandwich structure. Using the fabrication process, in a batch, 15 PSCs with a sandwich structure of three perovskite layers and 15 ones without that, instead with only a hybrid perovskite layer, were constructed, respectively. With these cells, stability tests were done. # 4. Conclusions

The PCE of PSCs has been increased dramatically from 3% to 22% within several years, owing to the superior optoelectronic properties of fully-organic and organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials. PSCs have good prospects for commercialisation, but in these materials organic cations, such as MA and FA are volatile and tend to diffuse to neighbor CTLs and do damage to them. The hybrid perovskite layer can interact with water molecules from the air and induce humidity instability of the cells. The poor thermal, humidity, and light stability of these PSC devices hinders the progress to their practical application. We know fully-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskites are stable over 400 °C and as the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskites in a cubic α phase has a bandgap of 1.73 eV, it is rather appropriate for the application in photovoltaic devices. But it is well known that the cubic α phase is unstable at room temperatures and tends to degrade rapidly to a non-perovskite δ phase, leading to reduction of the absorption of sun light and thereafter poor photovoltaic performance. Moreover, the PCE of all-inorganic PSCs is relatively lower than PSCs of fully-organic and hybrid perovskites. We obtained an inspiration from the fact that two-dimensional (2D) Ruddlesden–Popper perovskite solar cells also show good photovoltaic performance, though neighbor 2D layers of the perovskite film are only loosely connected and designed solar cells with multiple 3D perovskite films, which form a sandwich structure, in order to achieve both high stabilities and high PCEs. We placed the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite layer inside two other fully-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite layers in a cubic α phase, because the middle layer has lower stability than the two outer layers, which are to protect the middle layer by preventing the organic ions of the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite middle layer from diffusing to neighbor CTLs. Water molecules from air are also impeded from the hybrid perovskite layer. We also used agents to decrease crystal grain dimensions and obtained phase-stable full-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   in a cubic α phase. The constructed PSCs have extremely high stabilities and high PCEs. After 1000 h of illumination under AM1.5 illumination in air at 60 °C (Humility: ~60%), PSCs with a sandwich structure of three perovskite layers remain nearly all the original PCEs, while those without that only maintain 76.63%. The containment of the hybrid perovskite layer with fully-inorganic perovskite layer avoid the direct contact of that layer with the CTLs, so volatile organic ions cannot flee to pinholes in those layers and water molecules from air cannot reach the hybrid perovskite layer. The hybrid perovskite layer and CTLs are therefore stabilized.
