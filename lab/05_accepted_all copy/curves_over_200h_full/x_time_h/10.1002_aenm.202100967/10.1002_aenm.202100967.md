# Dopant-Free Hole-Transporting Material with Enhanced Intermolecular Interaction for Efficient and Stable n-i-p Perovskite Solar Cells

Jing Wang, Xin Wu, Yizhe Liu, Tian Qin,* Kaicheng Zhang, Ning Li, Juan Zhao,* Ruquan Ye, Zhanxi Fan, Zhenguo Chi, and Zonglong Zhu*

Developing low-cost, efficient, and stable dopant-free hole-transporting materials (HTMs) in perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) is essential to their commercial deployment. Herein, the synthesis of a novel spirofluorene-dithiolane based small molecular HTM, SFDT-TDM, through facile and low-cost synthetic routes is reported. The C–H  $  \cdots\pi  $  interactions in adjacent SFDT-TDM are beneficial for high hole mobility and the methylthio groups in SFDT-TDM can serve as Lewis bases to passivate the defects on the surface of perovskite films, leading to suppressed non-radiative recombination and enhanced charge extraction at the perovskite/HTM interface. As a result,  $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $  based PVSCs with SFDT-TDM as the HTM realize champion power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of 21.7% and 20.3% for small-area (0.04 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) and large-area (1.0 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) devices with negligible photocurrent hysteresis, respectively. Additionally, all-inorganic  $  CsPbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $  based PVSCs with SFDT-TDM demonstrate an impressive PCE of 17.1% along with excellent stability. This work highlights the great potential of the spirofluorene core for exploring low-cost and dopant-free HTMs for PVSCs with high efficiency and stability.

Generally, the synthesized HTMs should have an appropriate energy level, high carrier mobility, superior stability, and potential defects passivation effects on the perovskite films, which have been extensively reported recently.  $  ^{[4c,5]}  $   Currently, dopant-free HTMs can be divided into two categories: small molecule-based HTMs and polymer-based HTMs.  $  ^{[6]}  $   Among them, small molecules have drawn much attention due to their precise molecular weight, facile synthesis process, and less batch-to-batch variation making,  $  ^{[3b]}  $   making them promising for large-scale production in the industry. Until now, various molecule structures have been used to design new dopant-free HTMs, including donor-acceptor structure,  $  ^{[7]}  $   metal-macrocycle framework,  $  ^{[8]}  $   aromatic conjugated,  $  ^{[9]}  $   fused-ring  $  ^{[10]}  $   2D (quasi) planar structure, and so on. Among the small molecule dopant-free HTMs, the 3D Spirofluorene structures have been widely employed due to their merits of isotropic charge transport properties, good processability, and electrical properties close to their perpendicular arrangements by an   $  sp^{3}  $  -hybridized carbon center.  $  ^{[11]}  $   However, due to the rigid structures of Spirofluorene and its highly sterical nature,  $  ^{[12]}  $   the poor intermolecular contact may cause a reduction of charge carrier mobility in a thick-film state. As a result, most of the reported dopant-free HTMs with Spirofluorene structure result in inferior device performance.  $  ^{[11,13]}  $   Therefore, modifying the molecular structure of Spirofluorene-based HTMs to achieve high mobility and appropriate energy level with facile synthetic routes is of great importance in PVSCs.

Herein, we rationally designed and synthesized a novel Spirofluorene-dithiolane structured HTM, named SFDT-TDM, shown in Figure 1a. Compared with the common spirobifluorene core, the Spirofluorene center containing a five-member dithiolane ring exhibits less steric hindrance but still inherits the rigid structural property from the spirobifluorene core, thus facilitating to enhance the intermolecular stacking of SFTD-TDM and booting the hole mobility of SFTD-TDM. Meanwhile,

the sulfur (S) atoms can act as a Lewis base to passivate defects such as uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   in the perovskite layer,  $  ^{[13d,14]}  $   which will help to suppress non-radiative losses in the perovskite/HTM interface and improve the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) and PCE of devices. Moreover, the -SMe group can also increase the solubility of SFTD-TDM, which is significant to the film-formation ability and film morphology of SFTD-TDM layer. SFDT-TDM was applied to n-i-p structured MA-free PVSCs and realized champion PCEs of 21.7% and 20.3% for small-area (0.04 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) and large-area (1.0 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) devices with negligible hysteresis. Meanwhile, devices with all-inorganic perovskite   $  CsPbI_{x}Br_{3-x}  $   also exhibit a remarkable PCE of 17.1%. Besides, SFDT-TDM-based device show improved stability than Spiro-OMeTAD based devices, which maintains 80% of its initial performance after stored in the ambient environment for 480 h. Moreover, the facile synthetic routes of as-prepared SFDT-TDM enable a low cost of 12.03 USD/g, which is much lower than the price of commercialized Spiro-OMeTAD. This work proved that simply altering the molecule structure of spirofluorene-based HTMs can simultaneously reduce the cost, enhance performance, and improve the long-term stability of PVSCs, which makes them promising candidates for commercialization.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The synthetic routes of SFDT-TDM are presented in Figure 1a and details can be found in the Supporting Information. Briefly, SFDT-TDM was synthesized from cheap raw materials and experienced two simple steps with high yields. More details of SFDT-TDM were confirmed by NMR spectroscopy and MALDI-TOF-MS (Figures S1–S3, Supporting Information). As the cost is one of the most important issues for HTM to be commercialized, we calculated the cost of as-prepared HTM. The cost of as-prepared SFDT-TDM was calculated to be roughly 12.03 USD/g (Table S1, Supporting Information), which is lower than the commercial price of Spiro-OMeTAD (119 to 411 USD/g).

Another crucial issue for HTM in PVSCs is the stability. Typically, amorphous to crystalline phase transition happens in Spiro-OMeTAD under thermal conditions, causing adverse effects on device stability. To evaluate the thermal stability of SFDT-TDM, thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) measurements were carried out. As shown in Figures S4 and S5, Supporting Information, SFDT-TDM shows good thermal stability with a thermal decomposition temperature   $  T_{\mathrm{d}}  $   corresponding to 5% weight loss at 468 °C, which is higher than Spiro-OMeTAD   $  T_{\mathrm{d}} = 422 \, ^{\circ}\mathrm{C}  $  ,  $  ^{[15]}  $   indicating SFDT-TDM is potential to be a stable HTM in PVSCs.

Apart from the cost and stability, the device performance is also of great importance. Therefore, we characterized the optical and electrical properties of SFDT-TDM. First, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were carried out to get an insight into molecular geometries and frontier molecular orbitals (FMOs) distribution of SFDT-TDM. As illustrated in Figure 1b, the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of SFDT-TDM is mainly localized on the whole molecule except the dithiolane ring, while the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) is concentrated on the Spirofluorene core. The optical properties of SFDT-TDM in both solution and solid states were characterized by UV-Vis absorption spectra, as shown in Figure S6, Supporting Information. The main absorption peak occurring from 280 to  $ 350\mathrm{nm} $  can be assigned to the  $ \pi -\pi^{*} $  transition, and the absorption peak from 350 to  $ 440~\mathrm{nm} $  is caused by intramolecular charge transfer from diphenylamine blocks to spirofluorene-dithiolane core. Combined with the UV-Vis absorption spectra (Figure S6, Supporting Information) and first oxidation potential from cyclic voltammogram (CV, Figure S7, Supporting Information), the HOMO/LUMO energy levels of SFDT-TDM are determined to be  $ -5.25 / - 2.36\mathrm{eV} $ . As the energy band diagram of materials in Figure 1c demonstrated, the lower-lying HOMO level of SFDT-TDM ensures sufficient hole extraction from the perovskite absorber layer, and the LUMO level

is shallow enough to block electrons.  $  ^{[16]}  $   It is also worth noting that the deeper HOMO of SFDT-TDM than Spiro-OMeTAD results in better energy level alignment between perovskite and HTM, facilitating charge transfer and reducing the Voc loss.  $  ^{[14a]}  $

Besides, the hole mobility of SFDT-TDM and doped Spiro-OMeTAD in film state was characterized by the space-charge limited current (SCLC) method.  $  ^{[14a]}  $   As displayed in Figure 2a, the hole mobility of SFDT-TDM was calculated to be   $  1.97 \times 10^{-4} \, cm^{2}V^{-1}s^{-1}  $  , which is comparable to that of doped Spiro-OMeTAD (  $  3.05 \times 10^{-4} \, cm^{2}V^{-1}s^{-1}  $  ). To explain the origin of high mobility, the relationship between mobility and the molecules arrangement was studied through grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) measurements, which revealed the molecular orientation of SFDT-TDM. As shown in Figure 2b, the film represents an edge-on orientation, as can be discerned from the apparent peaks in the in-plane direction (  $  q_{xy} \approx 1.3 \, \AA^{-1}  $  ) and the out-of-plane direction. While the corresponding distance of molecules packing is about 4.83 Å, it is still beneficial to the vertical charge transport of SFDT-TDM. The molecular orientation in the out-of-plane direction could account for the high mobility of SFDT-TDM film. Moreover, there is almost no change on the GIWAX patterns under 100 °C, confirming the superior thermal stability of SFDT-TDM in a thin-film state (Figure S8, Supporting Information). Single crystal structure analysis was conducted to investigate the molecular pattern of SFDT-TDM. As shown in Figure 2c and Table S2, Supporting Information, the dithiolane ring is almost perpendicular to the fluorene plane, leading to molecules without close intermolecular packing in the crystal. The hydrogen atoms on the lateral diphenylamine of SFDT-TDM can connect with other fluorene parts of adjacent molecules through C–H···π interactions. The range of C–H···π distance in SFDT-TDM is about 3.7 to 4.2 Å, which might be longer than the values of typical π–π stacking distance. However, such C–H···π interactions between adjacent SFDT-TDM molecules are still advantageous to the hopping transport.  $  ^{[17]}  $   Although there are

As the film morphology plays a vital role in determining the device performance of PVSCs,  $  ^{[18]}  $   Atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements were carried out to observe the surface morphology of the SFDT-TDM layer on ITO (Figure S9, Supporting Information) and perovskite layer (Figure 2d,e). The AFM images show SFDT-TDM film with a smooth and homogeneous surface, which is beneficial for interfacial contact and hole transport. The root-mean-square roughness (RMS) of SFDT-TDM on ITO is only 0.43 nm, and the full coverage of SFDT-TDM on the perovskite layer can reduce the RMS of perovskite from 37.0 to 11.6 nm for perovskite/SFDT-TDM. In comparison, AFM images of Sprio-OMeTAD on ITO and perovskite substrates were all provided in Figure S9, Supporting Information, and the roughness are 0.692 and 13.33 nm, which are comparable to SFDT-TDM on the same substrates.

Furthermore, the electrostatic surface potential (ESP) analysis was conducted to investigate the charge distribution on the molecules. As seen in Figure 3a, the significant positive charges are delocalized on the whole molecule. Simultaneously, the sulfur atoms are featured with negative charges, indicating

that the lone pair electrons on the sulfur atoms can passivate the defects on the perovskite surface via Lewis acid–base interaction.  $  ^{[18b]}  $   To further confirm the potential interactions between perovskite and SFDT-TDM, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were implemented to examine the chemical bonding states on the surface of perovskite/SFDT-TDM (Figure 3b,c). Compared with pure perovskite and SFDT-TDM films, it is found that the characterization peaks of Pb 4f for perovskite/SFDT-TDM bilayer shift towards lower binding energy compared with pure perovskite film, while that of S 2p show an opposite shift direction towards higher binding energy. This phenomenon can be explained by the changed electron density around the atoms since S atoms from –SMe groups donate their lone pair electrons to the empty 6p orbital of Pb  $  ^{2+}  $  , indicating that SFDT-TDM can passivate the trap states that exist on the surface of perovskite film.  $  ^{[14f,19]}  $

As shown in Figure 3d, the PL quenching effect of perovskite/SFDT-TDM multilayer is stronger than that of the bare perovskite film and perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD multilayer, revealing SFDT-TDM can efficiently extract holes from the perovskite layer.[20] The PL lifetime was obtained by calculating the bi-exponential fits to TRPL spectra (Figure 3e).[21] The PL lifetime of the pure perovskite film is  $ 94.72\mathrm{ns} $ , which significantly decreases to 58.44 and  $ 30.7\mathrm{ns} $  after depositing SFDT-TDM or Spiro-OMeTAD on the surface, respectively. These results suggest the superior stability of SFDT-TDM based devices.

Furthermore, the   $  V_{oc}  $   loss analysis for Spiro-OMeTAD and SFDT-TDM based devices was investigated based on the detailed balance theory principle.  $  ^{[27]}  $   First,   $  V_{oc, rad}  $   is defined as maximum   $  V_{oc}  $ , assuming that the radiative recombination is the only inevitable recombination mechanism within devices. Combining the real quantum efficiency measured by both sensitive Fourier transform photocurrent spectroscopy (FTPS) and electroluminescence (EL) spectroscopy,   $  V_{oc, rad}  $   can be obtained according to the reciprocal relationship between the photovoltaic quantum efficiency and the EL of PVSCs (Figure 5e,f).  $  ^{[27,28]}  $   Therefore, the   $  V_{oc, rad}  $   for SFDT-TDM and Spiro-OMeTAD based devices are calculated to be 1.153 and 1.149 V, respectively. The real attainable   $  V_{oc}  $   would be further declined due to the existence of unavoidable nonradiative recombination-induced voltage loss   $  \Delta V_{oc, nr}  $   (  $  \Delta Voc, nr = Voc, rad - Voc  $  ) in the working devices.  $  ^{[29]}  $   As summarized in Table 2,   $  \Delta V_{oc, nr}  $   of SFDT-TDM-based solar cell (0.023 V) is much smaller than that of the control devices (0.054 V). The reduced   $  \Delta V_{oc, nr}  $   is in good agreement with the higher EL intensity (Figure S13, Supporting Information), indicating the non-radiative recombination can be significantly suppressed in SFDT-TDM based PVSCs.

Notably, this efficiency is still one of the highest PCE values amongst I–Br type hybrid all-inorganic PVSCs with HTMs under dopant-free conditions.  $  ^{[14a,16,30]}  $

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we have developed a low-cost small molecule based on spirofluorene core, SFDT-TDM, and successfully applied it as a dopant-free HTM in high performance n-i-p PVCSs with different perovskite compositions. The C–H  $  \cdots\pi  $  interactions in adjacent SFDT-TDM molecules enable high hole mobility, and the methylthio group in SFDT-TDM can act as a Lewis base to passivate the surface defects of perovskite, which is beneficial for the suppression of non-radiative recombination and improvement of charge extraction and transport. As-optimized  $  Cs_{x}FA_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $ -based PVSCs with SFDT-TDM as HTM delivered remarkable PCEs of 21.7% (0.04 cm  $  ^{2}  $ ) and 20.3% (1 cm  $  ^{2}  $ ) for small-area and large-area PVSCs. Besides, a superior PCE of 17.1% is achieved when employing SFDT-TDM in all-inorganic  $  CsPbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $  PVSCs. Moreover, compared with doped Spiro-OMeTAD, the dopant-free SFDT-TDM significantly improved the long-term photostability and store stability of devices. The facile synthetic routes of synthesized SFDT-TDM showed a low cost of 12.03 USD/g, which is much lower than commercialized Spiro-OMeTAD (119.68 USD/g). This work proved that the spirofluorene core has an immense potential to be used for the development of low-cost small molecule dopant-free HTMs for the fabrication of efficient and stable PVSCs.
