# 2-Thiopheneformamidinium-Based 2D Ruddlesden-Popper Perovskite Solar Cells with Efficiency of 16.72% and Negligible Hysteresis

Yixin Dong, Di Lu, Zhiyuan Xu, Hongtao Lai, and Yongsheng Liu*

Formamidinium (FA)-based 3D perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been widely studied and they show reduced bandgap, enhanced stability, and improved efficiency compared to MAPbI $ _{3} $ -based devices. Nevertheless, the FA-based spacers have rarely been studied for 2D Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) perovskites, which have drawn wide attention due to their enormous potential for fabricating efficient and stable photovoltaic devices. Here, for the first time, FA-based derivative, 2-thiopheneformamidinium (ThFA), is successfully synthesized and employed as an organic spacer for 2D RP PSCs. A precursor organic salts-assisted crystal growth technique is further developed to prepare high quality 2D (ThFA) $ _{2} $ (MA) $ _{n-1} $ Pb $ _{n} $ I $ _{3n+1} $  (nominal  $ n = 3 $ ) perovskite films, which shows preferential vertical growth orientations, high charge carrier mobilities, and reduced trap density. As a result, the 2D RP PSCs with an inverted planar p-i-n structure exhibit a dramatically improved power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 7.23% to 16.72% with negligible hysteresis, which is among the highest PCE in 2D RP PSCs with low nominal  $ n $ -value of 3. Importantly, the optimized 2D PSCs exhibit a dramatically improved stability with less than 1% degradation after storage in N $ _{2} $  for 3000 h without encapsulation. These findings provide an effective strategy for developing FA-based organic spacers toward highly efficient and stable 2D PSCs.

Formamidinium (FA)-based 3D PSCs have been widely studied and they showed reduced bandgap and enhanced stability compared to MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskite devices.  $  ^{[31-33]}  $  However, 2D RP PSCs using FA-based bulky spacers have rarely been investigated.

In this work, a FA-based organic spacer, 2-thiopheneformamidinium (ThFA) (Figure 1a), was successfully developed and applied to 2D RP PSCs. A precursor organic salts-assisted crystal growth (PACG) method was further developed to induce the crystal growth and orientation, and tune the film quality, resulting in enlarged carrier lifetime, improved charge carrier mobility, and suppressed nonradiative recombination loss. High-resolution transmission electron microscopic (HRTEM), X-ray diffraction (XRD) and grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) results show that the 2D phases and the 3D-like phases coexist and is distributed randomly in the film, and the corner-sharing  $ \left[\mathrm{PbI}_{6}\right]^{4-} $  octahedron layers is preferentially orientated vertical to the substrate. The optimized device based on PACG-processed  $ (\mathrm{ThFA})_{2}\mathrm{MA}_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{1n+1} $  (nominal  $ n = 3 $ ) (Figure 1a) exhibits a champion PCE of  $ 16.72\% $  with negligible hysteresis, which is among the highest efficiency so far for 2D RP PSCs with nominal  $ n $ -value of 3. In contrast, the control device only shows a poor efficiency of  $ 7.23\% $ . Importantly, the 2D devices showed dramatically improved stability in comparison with their 3D counterparts.

The 2D films show multiple exciton peaks at  $  \approx  $  412 and 480 nm due to the strong quantum confinement and it could be assigned to the 2D perovskite phases with low n-values.  $  ^{[28]}  $  The distinct absorption peak around 750 nm could be attributed to the 3D or 3D-like phases. The energy levels (Figure 2c) of the control and the PACG-processed 2D perovskite,   $  (\mathrm{ThFA})_{2}(\mathrm{MA})_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{3n+1}  $   (nominal n = 3), was determined by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (Figure S2, Supporting Information) and optical absorption measurements. Compared to control film, the secondary electron cut-off determined work function of PACG-processed film shifted from -4.64 to -4.43 eV. The valence band maximum and the conduction band maximum (CBM) has a slight shift of 120 meV toward vacuum, and the Fermi level shifts by about 210 meV toward CBM for PACG-processed films compared to control film. The more n-type character is likely attributed to the change in the ratio of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   and organic cations in the PACG-processed film because   $  PbI_{2}  $   termination in the perovskite surface may result in deeper energy levels than organic ammonium iodide termination.  $  ^{[35]}  $   As organic ammonium salts

(such as MAI and ThFAI) have good solubility in IPA, a tiny amount of organic ammonium salts on the surface of control film could be washed away and formed the lead iodide terminated perovskite surface. On the contrary, organic ammonium terminated surface could be formed for PACG-processed film due to the incorporation of organic salts, including ThFAI, MAI, and MACl, in IPA, resulting in a shallower energy level. This more n-type feature suggests that the PACG-processed 2D perovskite have a larger number of electron-filled traps, which could reduce the trap-assisted recombination loss and facilitate the charge transfer between perovskite film and electron transport materials.  $  ^{[36,37]}  $

As shown in Figure S4,c, Supporting Information, the PACG-processed 2D perovskite film shows dramatically enlarged diffraction peaks intensity and narrow FWHM (the full width at half maximum) of (111) peak, indicating its superior crystallinity. Typically, the (202) and (111) direction peaks of 2D RP perovskite represent the crystallographic planes of inorganic [PbI $ _6 $ ] $ ^{4-} $  octahedral layer aligning vertical and tilted to the substrate, respectively.[27] The XRD intensity ratio of (202)/(111) was increased from 0.43 to 0.61 for 2D perovskite film before and after PACG-processing, suggesting the more layered 2D perovskite phases prefer to grow vertical to the substrate for PACG-processed film, and thus facilitating efficient charge transport between the top and the bottom electrodes. The control film using IPA as antisolvent exhibits weak (111) and (202) diffraction peaks as well as (0k0) peaks at small angles (<10°) (Figure 3a), indicating that the 2D perovskite slabs with different  $ n $ -values grow in both vertical and parallel directions with respect to the substrate.[24] When the layered 2D perovskite slabs grow parallel to the substrate, the charge transport between two electrodes would be hindered by the insulating ThFA-based organic spacer layer, leading to poor photovoltaic performance. The formed 2D RP perovskite were further confirmed by  $ ^1 $ H NMR spectra (Figures S5 and S6, Supporting Information), which showed the obviously shifted proton signal of the  $ - $ NH $ _3^+ $  group in 2D perovskite compared to ThFAI, suggesting the N–H–I $ ^- $  hydrogen bonding interaction between  $ - $ NH $ _3^+ $  and iodide anions in the corner-sharing [PbI $ _6 $ ] $ ^{4-} $  octahedron layers.[46]

To better evaluate the orientation for control and PACG-processed 2D perovskite, GIWAXS pattern was performed. As shown in Figure 3b, the control film exhibits obvious diffraction rings and weak diffraction intensity of (111) or (202), suggesting random orientation and weak crystallinity of the polycrystalline films, which is consistent with the XRD results discussed above. Moreover, the evident series diffraction of (0k0) suggest that a large amount of layered perovskite slabs grow along with the parallel direction to the substrate, which would retard efficient charge transport in the devices. In contrast, as shown in Figure 3c, the PACG-processed film exhibits sharp and sporadic Bragg spots corresponding to a highly preferential vertical orientation of 2D perovskite bearing ThFA spacer layers with respect to the substrate.  $  ^{[16]}  $   The growth of   $  [PbI_{6}]^{4-}  $   octahedron layers vertical to the substrate would facilitate efficient charge transport and collection between two electrodes, leading to improved photovoltaic performance of the devices.

The  $ J - V $  curves of the optimized devices from both reverse and forward scans are shown in Figure 4b and the data are summarized in Table 1. The space charge limited current (SCLC) analysis are evaluated to investigate the trap-state densities and the carrier mobilities using a device structure of ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /2D perovskite/PCBM/Ag. As shown in the Figure 5d, the  $ J-V $  curves with double-logarithmic scale under dark condition show three typical fitting straight lines regions: a linear ohmic region at low bias (slope  $ k=1 $ ), a trap-filled region at intermediate bias region (slope  $ k>3 $ ), and a trap-free SCLC region at high bias (slope  $ k=2 $ ). The bias voltage at the kink point between the ohmic region and the trap-filled region is defined as trap-filled limited voltage ( $ V_{\text{TFL}} $ ). Trap-state densities can be quantitatively determined by the follow equation: $ ^{[54]} $   $ N_t = (2\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r V_{\text{TFL}})/(qL^2) $ , where  $ N_t $  is electron or hole trap-state density,  $ \varepsilon_0 $  is the vacuum permittivity ( $ 8.85 \times 10^{-12} $  F m $ ^{-1} $ ),  $ \varepsilon_r $  is the relative dielectric constant ( $ \varepsilon_r = 25 $ ), $ ^{[21]} $   $ L $  is the thickness of 2D perovskite film,  $ V_{\text{TFL}} $  is the onset voltage of the trap-filled limit region, and  $ q $  is elementary charge ( $ 1.6 \times 10^{-19} $  C). The trap-state densities were calculated to be  $ 2.06 \times 10^{16} $  and  $ 8.33 \times 10^{15} $  cm $ ^{-3} $  for control and target devices, respectively. The lower trap density is expected to reduce the charge carrier recombination, leading to improved mobility, which is consistent with the enhanced and balanced mobility of the PACG-processed film as shown in Figure S10b, Supporting Information and Table 1. The decreased trap-state densities and enlarged carrier mobilities indicated that the nonradiative trap-assisted recombination had been significantly suppressed, which are also consistent with the carrier recombination and the transportation kinetics discussed above.

Stability of unencapsulated devices based on PACG-processed 2D perovskite and 3D MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskites stored in nitrogen glovebox were conducted (Figure 5e; Figure S11, Supporting Information). As shown in Figure 5e, the 2D perovskite devices maintain the average efficiency of over 99% of initial PCE after 3000 h. However, the average PCEs decreased to 41% of their initial values for 3D MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  PSCs after 1390 h. We also investigated the illumination stability under one sun (white light-emitting diode (LED), 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $ ), as shown in Figure S12, Supporting Information. The 2D perovskite device maintained 90% of its initial PCE after continuous light soaking for 216 h, while the PCE of corresponding 3D MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  device decayed to nearly zero after 192 h. The improved stability of 2D PSCs indicate that ThFA-based 2D perovskite devices have a better stability than 3D MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  devices. The improved long-term stability of 2D PSCs could be ascribed to the incorporated bulky hydrophobic ThFA spacer, which exhibit better stability than MA and can also prevent ion motion, thus suppressing the degradation of the perovskite film. These results indicate that the FA derivative, ThFA, is an excellent spacer for 2D PSCs.

# Experimental Section

Synthesis of 2-Thiopheneformamidinium lodide: Sodium methylate (742 mg, 13.7 mmol) was dissolved in 150 mL methanol at 0 °C in nitrogen atmosphere, then 2-thiophenecarbonitrile (12.8 mL, 137.4 mmol) was slowly added to the solution. After stirring for three days at room temperature,   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   (7.35g, 137.4 mmol) was added. The unreacted   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   was removed by filtration after being stirred at room temperature for 40 h. After removing the solvents under reduced pressure, NaOH aqueous solution was added to the dichloromethane solution (30 mL) of the white solid residue. After being stirred at 0 °C for 2 h, the reaction mixture was poured into water and extracted with dichloromethane. Then, the solvents were removed under reduced pressure and 2-thiopheneformamidine was obtained as white solid (8.27g, Yields: ≈48%). The HI solution was added dropwise into the solution of 2-thiopheneformamidine (8.27 g, 65.5 mmol) in methanol (100 mL) at 0 °C. After stirring at 0 °C for 4.5 h, the solvent was removed under reduced pressure. Then, the solid was washed three times using diethyl ether and recrystallized with ethanol. The ThFAI was obtained as white crystals (Yields: ≈59%).   $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR (400 MHz, DMSO-  $  d_{6}  $  , δ): 9.34 (s, 2H), 8.89 (s, 2H), 8.14 (dd, J = 4.9, 1.0 Hz, 1H), 8.10–8.07 (m, 1H), 7.33 (dd, J = 4.8, 4.0 Hz, 1H).

Preparation of 2D Perovskite Precursor Solution: The 2D perovskite (ThFA)  $  _{2}  $  (MA)  $  _{2}  $  Pb  $  _{3}  $  I  $  _{10}  $  (the concentration of Pb  $  ^{2+}  $  was 1.1 M in DMSO) precursor solutions were prepared in a stoichiometric ratio of 2:2:3 of ThFAI, MAI, and PbI  $  _{2}  $  in a DMSO solvent. Typically, 507.1 mg of PbI  $  _{2}  $ , 116.6 mg of MAI, and 186.3 mg of ThFAI were added to 1 mL of DMSO. The MACI was added to the precursor solution with a MACI/MAI weight ratio of 0.5:1. The solutions were stirred at 70 °C for 4–5 h and cooled to room temperature before use.

Device Fabrication: The inverted planner PSCs were fabricated on indium tin oxide (ITO) substrates. ITO glasses were cleaned stepwise in acetone, IPA, and deionized water. The ITO substrates were treated with UV-ozone for 20 min before use. A thin layer of PEDOT:PSS was filtered by 0.45   $  \mu  $  m PES filter and spin-coated on the ITO glasses at 4000 rpm for 20 s. After annealing in air at 150 °C for 15 min, the PEDOT:PSS coated substrates were transferred to a nitrogen-filled glovebox. The as-prepared perovskite precursor were then spin-coated onto the top of ITO/PEDOT:PSS substrate at 4000 rpm for 70 s. Before the end of 20 s, 160   $  \mu  $  L of IPA antisolvent was poured onto the perovskite films. For PACG perovskite thin film, first, ThFAI, MAI, and MACI were mixed in the same molar ratio as in the precursor and dissolved in IPA (ThFAI concentration was 0.024 mmol mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ). For the PACG-processed film, the as-prepared perovskite precursor was spin-coated onto the top of ITO/PEDOT:PSS substrate at a low speed of 1000 rpm for 10 s, followed by a high speed of 4000 rpm for 60 s. Before the end of 20 s of the second spin-coating step, 160   $  \mu  $  L IPA containing organic ammonium

salt was poured onto the perovskite films. After annealed at 100 °C for 25 min in air, the samples were transferred to the glovebox, and a PCBM solution (30 μL, 20 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  in CB) was spin-coated at 1200 rpm for 40 s. Then, BCP (0.6 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  in IPA) solution was spin-coated onto PCBM at 1000 rpm for 60 s. Finally, a 100 nm Ag electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation under a vacuum of 1 × 10  $  ^{-4}  $  Pa. The effective area of the device was 0.1 cm  $  ^{2}  $  defined by mask.
