# Controlling films structure by regulating 2D Ruddlesden-Popper perovskite formation enthalpy for efficient and stable tri-cation perovskite solar cells

Chao Liang, $ ^{a} $  K. M. Muhammed Salim, $ ^{b} $  Pengwei Li, $ ^{c} $  Zhuo Wang, $ ^{d} $  Teck Ming Koh, $ ^{b} $  Hao Gu, $ ^{a} $  Bo Wu, $ ^{e} $  Junmin Xia, $ ^{a} $  Zhipeng Zhang, $ ^{a} $  Kaiyang Wang, $ ^{a} $  Tanghao Liu, $ ^{a} $  Qi Wei, $ ^{a} $  Sisi Wang, $ ^{a} $  Yuxin Tang, $ ^{a} $  Guosheng Shao, $ ^{d} $  Yanlin Song, $ ^{c} $  Nripan Mathews $ ^{*b} $  and Guichuan Xing $ ^{*a} $

The incorporation of bulky organic cations to metal-halide perovskites, forming the 2D-3D heterojunctions, has dramatically improved the stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Nevertheless, the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of these PSCs are typically sacrificed because the formed 2D structures possess larger dielectric confinement, wider bandgaps, higher exciton binding energies and lower charge-carrier mobilities than the 3D perovskites. Here, we demonstrate that the environmental stability of the PSCs could be significantly improved without sacrificing the efficiency by introducing hydrophobic polyfluorinated cations  $ (\mathrm{CF}_3\mathrm{CF}_2\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{NH}_3^+,5\mathrm{F - PA}^+) $  to the metal-halide perovskites. Due to the large 2D perovskite formation enthalpy with polyfluorinated cations, the addition of such cations will form a protective layer at the grain boundaries of 3D perovskite rather than form the 2D perovskites. The resultant solar cells based on 5F- $ \mathsf{PA}_{0.05}[\mathsf{Cs}_{0.05}(\mathsf{MA}_{0.17}\mathsf{FA}_{0.83})_{0.95}]_{0.95}\mathsf{Pb}(\mathsf{Br}_{0.17}]_{0.83})_3 $  exhibit a substantially increased PCE of  $ 22.86\% $  compared with that  $ (20.69\%) $  of the control  $ \mathsf{Cs}_{0.05}(\mathsf{MA}_{0.17}\mathsf{FA}_{0.83})_{0.95}\mathsf{Pb}(\mathsf{Br}_{0.17}]_{0.83})_3 $  devices. More importantly, the optimized devices could keep  $ 80\% $  of their original PCEs after  $ >3000\mathrm{h} $  in ambient environment with  $ 65\pm 10\% $  relative humidity, which is attributed to hydrophobic fluorine moieties. This

work provides new understandings of enhancing the PSC stability by incorporating polyfluorinated cations.

In the last decade, power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of three-dimensional (3D) perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been apace improved from 3.8% to 25.2%, reaching those of traditional solar cells.  $  ^{1-3}  $   However, instability under moisture, UV irradiation, and high temperatures, seriously hinders the commercialization of 3D PSCs.  $  ^{4-7}  $   By contrast, low-dimensional Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) perovskites have demonstrated higher environmental stability because of the powerful van der Waals interaction between the hydrophobic bulky cation and the   $  [PbI_{6}]^{4-}  $   units.  $  ^{8-9}  $   In addition, optoelectronic properties of these 2D RP perovskites can be easily adjusted by employing various bulky cation, which makes them promising alternatives to 3D perovskites in solar cells.  $  ^{10}  $

The generic structural formula of 2D RP perovskites is   $  L_{2}A_{n-1}M_{n}X_{3n+1}  $   (n = 1, 2, ...∞), where L is a large alkylammonium cation, A is an ammonium cation, M is a divalent metal, and X is a halide.  $  ^{[11-14]}  $   Now, various bulky organic molecules have been tried in 2D PSCs, such as   $  C_{6}H_{5}C_{2}H_{4}NH_{3}  $   (PEA), n-  $  C_{4}H_{9}NH_{3}  $   (n-BA), iso-  $  C_{4}H_{9}NH_{3}  $   (iso-BA),   $  \mathrm{CH}_{3}(\mathrm{CH}_{2})_{2}\mathrm{NH}_{3}  $   (PA), Propane-1,3-diammonium (PDA), 2-thiophenemethylammonium (ThMA).  $  ^{[10,15-20]}  $   To get decent efficiency and good stability simultaneously, the 2D-3D heterostructured PSCs is developed by Snaith et al.  $  ^{[21]}  $   By incorporating n-BA into the cesium-formamidinium lead halide perovskite, the 3D perovskite grain boundaries (GBs) were protected by layered 2D RP phases. The authors achieved   $  17.5 \pm 1.3\%  $   PCE and 1000 h stability in the air. Liu et al. introduced 2-chloroethylamine (CEA  $  ^{+}  $  ) into   $  Cs_{0.1}FA_{0.9}Pb(I_{0.9}Br_{0.1})_{3}  $   (FA =   $  CH_{3}(NH_{2})_{2}^{+}  $  ) 3D perovskite to form the 2D-3D heterostructure, obtaining 20.08% PCE and 2400 h stability at room temperature.  $  ^{[22]}  $   To further enhance stability of the 2D RP PSCs, some more hydrophobic fluorinated cations were introduced recently.  $  ^{[23-28]}  $   A stability of 576 h and a PCE of 13% were obtained under an ambient environment at 70 °C. Furthermore, the carrier diffusion lengths can be obtained on the basis of the following equation:

$$
\mathrm{L} = \sqrt {\mu \mathrm{k} _ {\mathrm{B}} \mathrm{T} / \mathrm{e} \tau}
$$

(2)

where   $  \mu  $   is the mobility,   $  \tau  $   is the charge carrier lifetime and e is the elementary charge. Notably, the pristine perovskite film yields the electron and hole diffusion lengths of 693 ± 60 nm and 602 ± 51 nm. Whereas the corresponding carrier diffusion lengths of 5% 5F-PAI-incorporated perovskite films are improved to 1372 ± 80 nm and 1331 ± 73 nm (Fig. 3e). The superior carrier mobilities and diffusion lengths in 5% 5F-PAI-incorporated perovskite films should originate from the large perovskite grain size and effective defects passivation.  $  ^{35}  $   To evaluate trap densities of the perovskite films, a photo-injected carrier density dependent PL method was implemented (Fig. 3f).  $  ^{45}  $   According to the fittings, the trap density of the control perovskite film was calculated as   $  3.3 \times 10^{16}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  , which is significantly higher than that (  $  1.6 \times 10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $  ) of the 5% 5F-PAI-incorporated perovskite film. These results further confirmed that the 5F-PAI incorporation can efficaciously reduce the trap density and enhance the carrier mobilities and carrier diffusion lengths.

To further probe the dimensionality distribution and carrier dynamics in these 5F-PAI incorporated perovskites, the ultrafast transient absorption (TA) was conducted. Fig. 4a-f show the representative TA spectra of the  $ 0\% $ ,  $ 1\% $ ,  $ 3\% $ ,  $ 5\% $ ,  $ 7\% $  and  $ 10\% $  5F-PAI-incorporated perovskite films following excitation at 425 nm (1 KHz, 100 fs,  $ \sim 0.5~\mu \mathrm{J} / \mathrm{cm}^2 $ ). The results show TA spectra of

all the films matched closely with that reported for the   $  Cs_{0.05}(MA_{0.17}FA_{0.83})_{0.95}Pb(Br_{0.17}I_{0.83})_{3}  $   3D perovskite  $  ^{46}  $  , which demonstrates these films should be dominated by the 3D perovskite. Fig. 4a-f also show that the intensity of the photobleaching (PB) peaked at 730 nm could be slightly increased by incorporating suitable amount 5F-PAI, which agrees well with the PL results. In the whole visible range, except the typical two PB peaks (located at 470 nm and 730 nm) related to the 3D perovskite could be monitored, no other PB peaks related to 2D counterparts could be identified for all the films  $  ^{24}  $  , even at much higher pump fluences and 5F-PAI incorporation ratios (Fig. S12-S15). The TA kinetic traces were fitted and shown in Fig. 4g. 5% 5F-PAI-incorporated perovskite film showed a longer lifetime (43 ns) than that (21 ns) of the pristine perovskite film. This elongated carrier lifetime should be induced by that the 3D perovskite grains are passivated and protected by the hydrophobic 5F-PAI, which is also consistent with the TRPL observations. On the contrary, there are PB peaks associated with 2D perovskite in non-fluorinated PAI incorporated perovskite films (Fig. 5). These results suggest again that the 5F-PAI incorporation will not induce the formation of RP perovskites. However, at high 5F-PAI incorporation ratios, the excitonic absorption bleaching peak (500 nm) of   $  PbI_{2}  $   could be detected.  $  ^{47}  $

# Conclusions

In summary, the environmental stability of the PSCs could be significantly improved without sacrificing the efficiency by introducing hydrophobic polyfluorinated 5F-PAI into the metal-halide perovskites. Due to the large formation enthalpy, the addition of 5F-PAI will form a protective layer at the 3D perovskite GBs rather than form the 2D perovskites. Therefore, the dielectric confinement, wide bandgaps, high exciton binding energies and low charge-carrier mobilities associated with the 2D perovskites could be avioded in the 5F-PAI incorporated perovskites.
