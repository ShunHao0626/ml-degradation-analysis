# Abstract

Despite the tremendous progress in the efficiency of perovsite solar cells (PSCs), nonradiative recombination losses, mainly associated with the interfacial defects, still remain as a challenge that hinders their commercialization. In this study, we report a facile interface engineering strategy for highly-efficient planar PSCs by employing in a series concentration of 2-methylbenzimidazole (MBIm) between  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  electron transporting layer (ETL) and photoactive perovskite layer. The preliminary results demonstrate that MBIm molecules reduce the band□offset and enlarge the built-in potential ( $ V_{\mathrm{bi}} $ ) between perovskite and  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ , resulting in a lower photovoltage loss. Besides, MBIm provides an efficient passivation by donating the lone pair electrons to the uncoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  ions in perovskite structure through the formation of Lewis adducts, thereby minimizing nonradiative recombination in the ensuing devices. As a result, a remarkable increase in the efficiency from  $ 19.5\% $  (pristine cell) to  $ 21.6\% $  (3mM-MBIm modified cell) was achieved with a dramatic increase in  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  (~90 mV). Meanwhile, an admissible improvement in long-term stability was obtained by retaining  $ \sim 85 $  and  $ 90\% $  of initial performance under high humidity and continuous light soaking conditions, respectively. The prolonged stability is ascribed to the formation of compact and high-quality perovskite layer deposited on the modified surfaces. We believe that this study offers an efficient strategy by minimizing the nonradiative recombination losses through ETL/perovskite interface for high-efficiency and stable perovskite cells.

Keywords: Perovskite solar cells (PSCs),  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  electron transporting layer (ETL), Interface engineering, Nonradiative recombination, 2-methylbenzimidazole

# Introduction

Since the energy consumption is growing as a function of population and economic development, green technologies are improving far faster to meet the world's rising energy demand beyond fossil fuels [1]. Among various clean energy technologies, photovoltaics (PVs), which directly converts the solar energy into electrical energy, can strongly promote the transition from fossil sources to alternative renewable energy sources. Organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are the most emerging area of research among different PV technologies owing to their salient optoelectronic properties and facile processing routes [2-8]. Following the first demonstration by Miyasaka et al. [9], a striking progress has been registered on the cell performance and thanks to this, efficiencies have gone from single digits to a certified value of  $ 25.2\% $  in a short period of time [10]. Notwithstanding, substantial photovoltage losses due to nonradiative recombination in such devices, mainly originated from interfacial defects, prohibit them from reaching the radiative limit defined by the Shockley–Queisser (SQ) theory [11-14]. Thereof, highly-efficient cells with low open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ ) deficit can be fabricated only by minimizing the concentration of defects associated with recombination processes in the active layer and by eliminating the defect-states at the corresponding interfaces [15].

As far as we know, the interfaces between perovskite and charge transporting layers, especially electron transporting layer (ETL) – as revealed by the recent impedance studies   $  [16-19]  $   – play an indispensable role in reducing the nonradiative recombination losses by transferring the photo-generated charges effectively   $  [20]  $  . The utilization of an engineered interface between the ETL and perovskite is one of the most effective ways to eliminate these parasitic charge carrier recombinations. Such an engineered interface can also acquire a high-quality electron conductor with reduced band☐offset. Additionally, it plays a subsidiary role in inducing the formation of compact perovskite layer for improving the efficiency and stability of ensuing perovskite cells   $  [21]  $  . Therefore, the interfaces and the interfacial materials need to be carefully engineered to avoid all the aforementioned issues.

Thus far, tremendous efforts have been devoted to suppress such defects and perform molecular modification at ETL/perovskite interface by introducing organic or inorganic interlayer materials   $  [17, 22, 23]  $  . Compared to inorganic materials, organic materials, especially small molecules, possess the advantages of well☐defined structure, multifunctional anchoring groups, good surface coverage, and perfect batch☐to☐batch reproducibility   $  [24]  $  . In

one of the pioneering works in this field, Liu et al. introduced organic silane layer between   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite [25]. With this method, they successfully obtained an ETL with lower work function as well as compact perovskite film with improved crystallinity and less defects. The resulting devices indicated a noticeable improvement in the efficiency from 9.6 to 12.7%. In another interesting study, a zwitterionic compound, 3-(1-pyridinio)-1-propanesulfonate, was introduced to modify the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL/perovskite interface [26]. This modification led to a shift in the work function of ETL resulting in more effective electron extraction and an enhancement in built-in potential (  $  V_{bi}  $  ). Moreover, an improvement in the stability of devices was reported by the help of positively charged atoms in the zwitterion, which passivated Pb–I antisite defects. In a similar direction, the density of trap states was reduced by introducing a thin layer of 3,3-diphenylpropylamine (DPPA) as a passivation material at the interface between perovskite and ZnO ETL [27]. The oxygen deficiency based defects on the ZnO surface were passivated by the formation of Zn–N bond, indicating improved charge collection and reduced interfacial (parasitic) charge recombination. Also, perovskite film on modified ETL showed an improved crystallinity with compact grains and reduced trap density. As a result, a significant enhancement in the performance associated with the remarkable increase in   $  V_{OC}  $   was reported.

Inspired by these works, we employed small organic molecule 2-methylbenzimidazole (denoted as MBIm) for the first time to modify the mixed cation/halide based perovskite and ETL interface. Not only facile deposition method, but also low cost and commercial availability of MBIm make it as a feasible candidate for large-scale industrial applications. In this study, low-temperature processed   $  SnO_{2}  $   is preferred as ETL owing to its wide optical bandgap (3.6–4.0 eV), high electron mobility over   $  100\ cm^{2}V^{-1}s^{-1}  $  , high conductivity, deep conduction band, and excellent chemical/UV stability. The influence of MBIm molecule on the photovoltaic performance and long-term stability of the PSCs was elucidated by modulating the passivating layer concentration (1 mM to 10 mM). As an imidazole derivative, MBIm is a heterocyclic building block [28]. As reported by Hagfeldt et. al, heterocyclic building blocks efficiently attach to the metal oxide-based ETL surface, and shift the conduction-band edge of ETL toward higher energy levels, and thus, retard the interfacial electron recombination by increasing the electron lifetime [29, 30]. Thanks to the molecular structure of MBIm, it can interact with the Lewis acid defects in perovskite thorough N atom, whereas methyl chain in the structure presents hydrophobicity. These functional groups can provide considerable potential for more effective passivation effect in device applications.

In addition to significantly improved crystal quality and open grain boundary-free surface morphology of perovskite, the obtained results have been intensively shown that interfacial treating reduces the density of trap states along the interface and enables faster electron transport by minimizing the energetic disorder. Consequently, an admissible efficiency of 21.6% was achieved with a notable increase in   $  V_{OC}  $   of ~90 mV compared with those yielded by the MBIm-free cells (19.5%). The possible reasons of the performance improvement were investigated using various techniques such as space-charge limited current (SCLC), Mott-Schottky (M-S), photoluminescence (PL), and electronic impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements.

# Results and Discussion

Here we modified the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  ETL/perovskite interface by MBIm small molecules (Fig. 1a). The experimental procedure for the device fabrication is detailed in the Supplementary Information. Briefly, MBIm precursor solutions prepared in different concentrations ranging from 1 to  $ 10\mathrm{mM} $  were deposited onto the low-temperature processed  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  ETLs. Thus, triple-cation perovskite-based PSCs consisting of FTO/  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ / passivation layer/ perovskite/ spiro-OMeTAD/ Au were fabricated.

Before discussing the PV results, we carry out a range of film characterizations to clearly understand the impact of passivation layer on the interfacial and surface properties. In order to identify the formation of MBIm on the ETL surface, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurement was conducted before and after modification. As shown in Fig. 1b, a splayed N 1s peak located at ca.  $ 400\mathrm{eV} $ , showing the presence of nitrogen in the MBIm molecule, demonstrates the successful formation of MBIm overlay on the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  film. Fig. 1c and S1 indicate that surface treatment has no significant impact neither on the transmittance nor the absorbance of the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ . Moreover, the optical bandgap of the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  films is unaltered after the introduction of MBIm layer. As shown in the inset of Fig. 1c, Tauc plot shows a similar optical bandgap of  $ 3.74\mathrm{eV} $  for pristine and modified films. It can be stated that the formation of perovskite film onto the modified ETLs would not be affected in terms of optical properties.

The interaction of MBIm with perovskite was investigated by the Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy and depicted in Fig. 1d and e. The stretching vibration of the C=N double-bond in the MBIm shifted from 1559 to 1543 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  and the corresponding vibration strength was gradually weakened in the form of PbI  $  _{2}  $  -MBIm compared to only MBIm. Fig. 2 schematizes the function of the MBIm small molecules employed between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite layers. As clearly seen in FTIR results, MBIm can exhibit an efficient passivation role by donating lone pair electron to the uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   atoms which act as electron traps deteriorating charge transport efficiency. By forming a coordinate or dative-covalent bond with the bottom surface of photoactive layer, it delocalizes the valence electron density from the Pb-exposed surface to bulk [31]. This improves the miscibility with the perovskite layer, indicating an improved perovskite crystallization and a better passivation effect. This “anchoring effect” associated with the MBIm modification provides a potential to lower the recombination rate at the interface, contributing an improvement in the   $  V_{OC}  $   [32, 33].

In order to understand the effect of MBIm passivation on the ETL, the surface morphology/topography of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   films before and after modification was examined via scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements. Similar to optic results, surface morphology was not noticeably affected by the MBIm treatment (Fig. 3a-d). Even if MBIm small molecules occupy the pin☐holes and minimize outlines of FTO grains leading shunt-paths between FTO and perovskite layer, this can not be detected from the SEM and AFM results due to very thin coating thickness. However, the surface roughness of the corresponding ETLs decreased considerably with the increase of MBIm concentration, reducing from   $  \sim23  $   nm for the pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   film to as low as   $  \sim18  $   nm for MBIm prepared in 3 mM concentration (Fig. 3e-f). The difference in roughness clearly implies the impact of MBIm layer on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL. The rest of AFM images in other concentrations is displayed in Fig. S2 in the Supplementary Information section.

The smooth surface of modified  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ETLs exhibits a remarkable impact on the growth mechanism of perovskite film. As compared to deep slits (open grain boundaries) containing perovskite film on pristine  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  (Fig. 4a and b), more flat and compact perovskite grains can be obtained on the modified surfaces (Fig. 4c and d). These deep slits extending along the grain boundaries of perovskite grains would promote critical current leakages and/or ion migration due to the lack of inter-connection between grains. As a result of this, a substantial reduction in the PV performance and device stability would be inevitable. In order to understand the enhancement in perovskite morphology, we investigated the wetting-ability of interlayer on  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  film by contact angle measurement. As shown in Fig. S3, the contact angles of perovskite precursor solution on modified  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  films were increased from  $ 26^{\circ} $  to  $ 41^{\circ} $  with the increase in concentration from  $ 1\mathrm{mM} $  to  $ 10~\mathrm{mM} $ , whereas the pristine  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  provided a contact angle of only  $ 22^{\circ} $ . This effect can be ascribed to the presence of hydrophobic methyl group and benzene ring in MBIm structure (Fig. 1a). As is well known, low-wetting ability facilities the growth of compact grains as a result of the grain boundary migration [34, 35]. Furthermore, such a smooth ETL surface provides a potential for better inter-connection with the photoactive layer. This significantly reduces defect-states at the interface, thus, minimizes nonradiative recombination processes [36, 37]. This can be evidently seen in the cross-sectional SEM images of the devices based on pristine  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and  $ 3\mathrm{mM - MBIm} $  modified  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ETLs (Fig. 4e and f).

We next fabricated a series of planar PSCs in regular architecture to assess whether the effect of MBIm on promoting ETL/perovskite interface contributes to the device performance. PV characteristics were measured under the 100 mW/cm $  ^{2}  $  of simulated sunlight at room temperature. Fig. 5a demonstrates the MBIm-concentration-dependent current density–voltage (J–V) characteristics of the best-performing devices. The pristine SnO $  _{2}  $  ETL based planar device show a typical efficiency of 19.5% with a short-circuit current density ( $  J_{\text{SC}}  $ ) of 23.4 mA/cm $  ^{2}  $ , an open-circuit voltage ( $  V_{\text{OC}}  $ ) of 1.11 V and a fill factor (FF) of 0.75. In comparison, devices based on modification layer exhibited a better performance for each concentration and the highest PV performance was achieved with 3 mM-MBIm modified devices (Fig. 5b). The best-performing device employing 3 mM-MBIm achieved an outstanding  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $  of 1.20 V with an impressive efficiency of 21.6%. The statistics of PV parameters validate the reproducibility of our results suggesting the improvement in the performance of PSCs (Fig. S4 and Table 1). As compared to other reports in the literature (Table S2), a considerable increase in  $  V_{\text{OC}}  $  was achieved in the presence of MBIm passivation and reached up to 1.22 V for 10 mM concentration of MBIm.

This admissible improvement in   $  V_{OC}  $   signs the remarkable reduction in nonradiative recombination losses at the interface and could be associated with the modification of interfacial energetic alignment [6, 38]. We thus investigated the junction property between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite by performing Mott-Schottky (M–S) plots using capacitance–voltage

(C-V) analysis (Fig. S5). The obtained curves showed that the built-in potential  $ (V_{\mathrm{bi}}) $  estimated from the x-intercept of the linear regime was improved from  $ 1.01\mathrm{V} $  for device without any passivation layer to  $ 1.11\mathrm{V} $  for  $ 3\mathrm{mM - MBIm} $  treated device, indicating a good energetic alignment (low band-offset) at the interface [39, 40]. The enlarged  $ V_{\mathrm{bi}} $  and extended depleted region is illustrated in Fig. S6 for a clear comparison of bare and modified  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  layers in regards of energy levels. Such an enlarged built-in field of the depletion region not only can suppress the back reaction of electrons from the  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  ETL to the perovskite layer, but also assist in the charge transportation of the photo-generated electrons, indicating the enhanced output voltage of perovskite cells.

Nevertheless, further increase in MBIm concentration up to 10 mM resulted in a significant reduction in   $  J_{SC}  $   to 22.3 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  . Apparently, the presence of higher concentration of MBIm may substantially hinder the transformation of electron into the ETL due to the higher internal resistance. As shown in Fig. 5c, the external quantum efficiency (EQE) curve obtained by the integration of the spectral response demonstrated a high quantum yield in a broad spectral region and integrated photocurrent densities (23.23, 23.30, 23.31, 23.13, and 21.84 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   for 0, 1, 3, 5, and 10 mM-MBIm treated device, respectively) are consistent with the   $  J_{SC}  $   obtained by J-V measurements (Fig. 5a).

By holding the solar cells under maximum power point tracking (MPPT) at a constant bias-voltage, we performed the stabilized power output (SPO) over time (Fig. 5d) and tabulated the obtained SPO values in Table 1. The difference in efficiency between instant and the authentic performance can be ascribed to the possible hysteresis phenomenon. In Fig. 5e and f, we show forward and reverse  $  J - V  $  curves of control and  $  3\mathrm{mM - MBIm}  $  modified devices to determine the hysteresis index (HI). The highest HI of 0.12 was found for the control device and reduced to 0.02 for  $  3\mathrm{mM - MBIm}  $  modified PSC. The rest of  $  J - V  $  curves in different scan directions is shown in Fig. S7 in the Supplementary Information section. Such remarkable difference in HI can be ascribed to open grain boundaries that function as recombination centers and facilitate ion migration (possibly iodide ions), leading to large  $  J - V  $  hysteresis [41]. This finding emphasizes the importance of perovskite crystallinity on the PV performance of cells. To elucidate the effect of modification on the crystal quality of the perovskite film, we carried out X-ray diffraction (XRD) experiments on the perovskite samples with and without MBIm treatment (Fig. 6a and b). The peak positions in the XRD patterns of perovskite films on pristine and MBIm-treated  $  \mathrm{SnO}_2  $  ETLs were unaltered. However, the increase in the intensity of main diffraction peaks suggests better crystallinity, in good agreement with the SEM images (Fig. 4a-d).

To further investigate the mechanism behind the improvement in the PV performance of devices based on modified interface, we performed steady-state photoluminescence (PL) measurement of perovskite films deposited on glass, pristine  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ , and MBIm modified  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  substrates. As shown in Fig. 6c, the pristine perovskite film deposited on glass exhibits a highly intense PL emission around  $ 790~\mathrm{nm} $ . Meanwhile, a strong quenching was observed when the perovskite is interfaced with the ETLs, indicating an effective extraction of the photoinduced electrons through the perovskite/ETL interface [42, 43]. Moreover, a slight blue shifting in the peak position was obtained with the increasing concentration of MBIm, revealing a reduction in the nonradiative recombination losses associated with the passivation of the defect states at the interface [32]. This can be ascribed to the coordination between MBIm and the uncoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  ions in the perovskite matrix.

The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were performed to further analyse the interface charge transport and recombination behaviors in PSCs. Fig. 7b shows the Nyquist plots of the devices with and without MBIm passivation layer at a bias of  $ 0.7\mathrm{V} $  under dark condition. The curves were fitted using the equivalent model (Fig. 7a). The obtained semicircle in the high-frequency region for each type of cells corresponds the carriers' recombination behavior between ETL and perovskite [47]. The fitted parameters extracted from the Nyquist plots is given in Table S1. The value of series resistance  $ (R_{\mathrm{s}}) $ , the starting point value at the real part of the high frequency, is estimated between  $ 12.5 - 12.9\Omega $  for up to 5 mM-MBIm, showing a very small rangeability. However, a sudden increase was obtained for  $ 10~\mathrm{mM} $ -MBIm modified device ( $ 16.9\Omega $ ). The decrease in the  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  and FF parameters can be explained on the basis of higher  $ R_{\mathrm{s}} $  of  $ 10~\mathrm{mM} $ -MBIm modified device. This indicates a weakened ohmic contact of the ETL/perovskite due to the excess amount of MBIm at the interface [40].

On the other hand, the MBIm modified PSCs exhibited a significant difference in charge recombination resistance   $  (R_{\mathrm{rec}})  $   as compared to control device.   $  R_{rec}  $   in devices with MBIm modified   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL is increased from 3.9 to 6.6 kΩ with the increase in MBIm concentration while control device based on pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL shows a   $  R_{rec}  $   of 2.8 kΩ. This increase indicates an efficient suppression of back recombination at the ETL/perovskite interface as coinciding with the   $  V_{OC}  $   in J-V curves of the PSCs. # Conclusions

In the present study, MBIm small molecules-based engineering strategy was successfully employed at the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL/perovskite interface. The obtained results show that the introduction of MBIm interlayer is able to obtain a lower density of interfacial trap states as well as strong binding between ETL and perovskite. Specifically, MBIm interlayer enlarged the built-in potential and tailored the band-offset between   $  SnO_{2}  $   and perovskite. In addition, MBIm molecules presented an effective passivation of the uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   atoms in perovskite by forming coordinate or dative-covalent bond. These findings facilitate an efficient charge separation and charge transport in the devices as confirmed by the spectroscopic and electrochemical measurements, resulting in minimized nonradiative recombination at the corresponding interface. The significant reduction in defects and efficient suppression of nonradiative recombination resulted in enhanced PV performance as compared to pristine device. The champion efficiency of 21.6% was obtained for the 3mM-MBIm employing planar cell with a remarkable improvement in   $  V_{OC}  $ , while the efficiency of the pristine cell is 19.5%. Besides, the passivation of   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL induced the formation of compact perovskite film with improved crystallinity, allowing a reduction in the moisture penetration and ion migration. Thus, relatively more stable perovskite cells were obtained under moisture stress and light soaking. Considering the overall assessment, suppression of the interface-dependent nonradiative recombination by interlayer plays a major role to improve the efficiency and long-term stability of perovskite cells. This invites more concerted and innovative research efforts to develop alternative functional interface materials that will make perovskite PV technology more attractive to the industry.

# Highlights

- A small molecule, 2-methylbenzimidazole (MBIm), is used to modify the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ETL/ perovskite interface.

- MBIm interlayer shifts the work function of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  resulting in more effective electron extraction and an increase in the built-in potential.

- Lone pair electrons in the MBIm passivate uncoordinated ions in perovskite structure through the formation of Lewis adducts, minimizing nonradiative recombination in the ensuing devices.

- The passivation of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ETL induces the formation of compact perovskite film with improved crystallinity, allowing a reduction in the moisture penetration and ion migration.

- With these desirable properties, not only prolonged stability but also breakthrough efficiency of 21.6% is achieved with a dramatic increase in   $  V_{OC}  $  (  $  \sim  $  90 mV).
