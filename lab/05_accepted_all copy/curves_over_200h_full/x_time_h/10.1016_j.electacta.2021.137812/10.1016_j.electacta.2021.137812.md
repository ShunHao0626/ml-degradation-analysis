# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been considered as the next generation photovoltaic devices with the most development potential and application value due to their fascinating photovoltaic performance and low-cost manufacturing process   $  [1-5]  $  . In the past decade, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has sharply increased from 3.8% to 25.2%   $  [6,7]  $  . However, the efficiency, cost, and stability should be balanced for future commercialization of PSCs   $  [8]  $  .

On one hand, the soft ionic solid nature of halide perovskite materials would produce a large number of crystal defects in the obtained perovskite film during low-temperature crystallization process [9]. Such defects might affect band alignment [10], and even result in charge recombination [11–13], limiting the device efficiency and stability. In addition, it is believed that ion migration and/or charge accumulation at the interfaces in PSCs can be contributed to the hysteresis and un-stability phenomena [14–17]. In spite of great advances in low-cost fabrication of air-processed perovskite solar cells (PSCs), there are still many opportunities to further improve device performance. Herein, we show that, by introducing monosodium glutamate (MSG) with carboxyl group, amino group, and  $ \mathrm{Na^{+}} $  cationic, known as gourmet powder, it is likely to simultaneously enhance the quality of perovskite film and electron transport layer, and interfacial charge extraction. As a multifunctional modify agent, the electric dipole effect and strong crosslinking of MSG can effectively reduce defects, accelerate interfacial charge transfer, alleviate hysteresis, and greatly improve performance of air-processed PSCs. Due to these unique characteristics of MSG, the champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 17.71\% $  with negligible hysteresis has been achieved based on the structure of FTO/MSG- $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 / \mathrm{FA}_{0.5}\mathrm{MA}_{0.5}\mathrm{PbI}_3 / \mathrm{Spiro - OMeTAD} / \mathrm{Au} $ . Recently,  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  has been considered as a promising electron transport material owing to its high electron mobility, matching energy level, and good photostability under UV illumination [20-23]. However,  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ETL still suffers some disadvantages, such as reduced built-in potential at the perovskite/ETL interface, numerous surface defects and traps, and inefficient charge extraction [24-27]. Doping and surface modifications are efficient strategies to address the aforementioned problems [28]. Several molecules additives with various functional groups have been employed for modifications of ETL to improve the device performance. The passivation and modifying interfacial effect largely depend on unique molecular structure of functional groups. Furthermore, low-cost and facile manufacturing processes are very important for the commercialization of PSCs. Although the performance improvement [37-44], the all-air-processed PSCs usually show lower PCE and severe hysteresis compared with the devices prepared in glove box. Therefore, further promotion of device performance is required.

In this work, we first report a low-temperature solution-process to functionalize   $  SnO_{2}  $   aqueous colloidal by doping monosodium glutamate (MSG), popularly known as gourmet powder. The MSG was chosen because it contains specific functional groups, including the carboxyl group, amino group, and   $  Na^{+}  $  cationic. As a food additive, MSG has the characteristics of nontoxic, low-cost, and abundant reserves, and importantly, MSG can be dissolved directly in water. It is found that the cooperation with the electric dipole effect and strong crosslinking of MSG, the ETL/perovskite interface could be optimized, accelerating charge transfer and reducing interface defects. As we expected, the champion PCE of 17.71% with negligible hysteresis has been achieved with the structure of FTO/MSG-  $  SnO_{2}/FA_{0.5}MA_{0.5}PbI_{3}/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au  $  . Moreover, the unencapsulated device exhibits remarkable long-term stability, preserving over 91% of its initial PCE after storage in the air condition for 1320 h. Our work demonstrates a green functionalization method for high-performance planar air-processed PSCs.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Reagents and materials

The   $  SnO_{2}  $   aqueous colloidal dispersion (tin (IV) oxide, 15wt% in   $  H_{2}O  $   colloidal dispersion) was obtained from Alfa Aesar. Lead iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  ), Methylammonium iodide (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  ), Formamidinium iodide (  $  \mathrm{CH(NH_{2})_{2}I}  $  ) and 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-4-methoxyphenylamino)-9,9' Spiro-Bifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD), 4-tert-butypyridine (TBP) and lithium Bis (trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), chlorobenzene (CB) and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) were obtained from Youxuan Trade, monosodium glutamate (MSG) was purchased from Macklin. All of the other available materials and reagents were purchased from Aladdin without any further purification.

# 2.2. Fabrication of   $  SnO_{2}  $   and MSG-  $  SnO_{2}  $   films

The   $  SnO_{2}  $   aqueous colloidal dispersion was diluted in deionized water (1:3, v/v), denoted as solution-A. Then 3 mg MSG was dissolved in 1 mL of deionized water denoted as solution-B, solution-B was adding into solution-A in a volume ratio of 1:3 denoted as solution-C. After that, solution-A and solution-C were stirred for 12 h at room temperature and filtered through a 0.45 mm PVDF syringe filter before used, then the solutions were spin-coated on the clean FTO substrate at 3000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 100 °C for 100 min in atmospheric environment without humidity control. Finally, the   $  SnO_{2}  $   and MSG-  $  SnO_{2}  $   films were acquired.

# 2.3. Device fabrication

Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) substrates were ultrasonically cleaned in detergent solution, deionized water, isopropanol, acetone, ethanol for 30 min each and subsequently cleaned by UV-ozone for 15 min. Later, the   $  FA_{0.5}MA_{0.5}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite films were fabricated on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   or MSG-  $  SnO_{2}  $   ETLs by a typical one-step anti-solvent method. In details, 1 M of   $  FA_{0.5}MA_{0.5}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite

precursor solution dissolved in a mixed solvent of anhydrous DMF: DMSO (4:1, v/v), the molar ratios of FAI/MAI, (FAI+MAI)/PbI  $  _{2}  $  were all 1/1. The solution was spin-coated at 500 rpm for 3 s following with 3000 rpm for 50 s. During the second spin-coating step end of 12 s, 350  $  \mu  $  L of ethyl acetate was dripped on the spinning substrate as an anti-solvent, then the film was annealing at 70 °C for 5 min and 120 °C for 10 min.

The hole transport layer (HTL) was deposited on the perovskite film containing 80 mg of spiro-OMeTAD,28.5μL 4-tert-butypyridine, 17.5 μL Li-TFSI (520 mg Li-TFSI in 1 mL acetonitrile) and 1 mL chlorobenzene then by spin-coating at 3000 rpm for 25 s. Finally, about 60 nm thick Au counter electrode was deposited via vacuum thermal evaporation at rate of 1.0 Å s  $  ^{-1}  $  . The seize of the cells are 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  , and the 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $   active area of the PSCs was determined through a shadow mask. All processes were performed in ambient conditions (the average temperature was 30 °C, average relative humidity was 35%) and the devices were stored without any encapsulation.

# 2.4. Characterization and measurement

X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern was collected by a Panalytical Empyrean X-ray diffractometer with a Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda=1.540\ \AA  $  ). X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (XPS) (PerkinElmer, PHI 5400 ESCA system) was recorded for chemical composition and state analysis of SnO  $  _{2}  $   and MSG-SnO  $  _{2}  $  . Field-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (Carl Zeiss, Supra55) was employed to the morphology and cross-section SEM image under an accelerating voltage of 15 kV. Atomic force microscopy (AFM) (Bruker, Dimension Icon) FastScan Scanning Kelvin Probe Microscopy (SKPM) was used to calculate the work function and evaluate the roughness of different films. The absorption spectra were performed by the UV-Visible spectrophotometer (UV-vis) (Beijing Purkinje General Instrument Co. Ltd, TU1901). Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectrum and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra were measured by fluorescence spectrometer (HORIBA, V2.7). Molecular dipole moments (  $  \mu_{mol}  $  ) were simulated through density functional theory (DFT) at B3LYP function with 6–31G(d, p) basis set, which was performed with the Gaussian 09 program package [45].

Photocurrent density-voltage (J-V) curves, open-circuit photovoltage decay (OCVD) and the electron trap-state density tests were measured with an electrochemical workstation (VersaSTAT 3, Ametek, USA) and a xenon lamp class ABB solar simulator (94021A, Newport, USA) as standardized by a standard Si solar cell (1218, Newport, USA). The PSCs devices were illuminated with a sweep rate was   $  0.2 \, V s^{-1}  $   under AM 1.5 G radiation (1 sun conditions,   $  100 \, mW cm^{-2}  $  ). The 50 PSCs devices were prepared and measured to obtain the statistical histograms of PCEs. The Crown-tech solar cell quantum efficiency measurement system (QTest Station 500AD, USA) was used to measure incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) of the devices. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was measured by an electrochemical workstation under illumination at 1.05 V bias voltages in the frequency range of   $  0.1-10^{5} \, Hz  $  .

To further explore the effects of MSG modifying on  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  ETL, the conductivity and electron mobility of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  and MSG- $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $

films were investigated using the electron-only devices. Fig. 1c shows the linear current-voltage  $ (J - V) $  curves under dark. It is indicated that the curve slope of MSG- $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  film is significantly higher than that of  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  film, suggesting a significant increase in electrical conductivity. Furthermore, the space charge-limited current (SCLC) model was used to calculate the electron mobility of the  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  films with or without MSG. The mobility can be evaluated according to the Mott-Gurney law (see equation S1 in the Supporting Information) [50]. As shown in Fig. 1d, the electron mobility significantly increases from  $ 9.48\times 10^{-4}\mathrm{cm}^2\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1} $  (pristine  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  film) to  $ 1.66\times 10^{-3}\mathrm{cm}^2\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1} $  (MSG- $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  film). Such high electron mobility is attributed to the decreased trap-state density [51], which could effectively promote electron transport and reduce interfacial charge accumulation.

The molecular dipole moments (  $  \mu_{mol}  $  ) calculations and work function (WF) measurement were performed to feature the electronic properties of MSG-  $  SnO_{2}  $   film. To obtain significant photogenerated electron injection and transport for improving device performance, a favorable interfacial energy level alignment is required [52]. The mismatching energy levels would result in nonradiative recombination, and the energy barrier at the perovskite/ETL interface caused by a mismatch in WF would also induce un-efficient electron injection, but the band offset can be optimized by using surface modification [53,54]. DFT calculation showed that the   $  \mu_{mol}  $   of MSG is 5.06D (Fig. 2). A high   $  \mu_{mol}  $   value indicates great built-in field, which will reduce interfacial energy barrier and provide an enhanced driving force for efficient photogenerated electron extraction from perovskite layer to ETL [55–57]. Additionally, the interfacial dipole effect of MSG was further investigated by WF measurement using SKPM. As shown in Fig. S5, the MSG-  $  SnO_{2}  $   film gives an average surface potential (SP) of -422.69 mV, which is much lower than the value of pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   of -373.78 mV. To further demonstrate the effect of MSG on the photovoltaic performance of the device, the planar PSCs with the architecture of FTO/SnO₂ or MSG-SnO₂/perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au have been constructed in air. The J-V data have been collected under AM1.5 G sunlight (100 mW cm⁻²) to evaluate photovoltaic parameters. Fig. 5a and b show the reverse and forward scan J-V curves of the different devices based on MSG-SnO₂ or control SnO₂ ETLs, where all photovoltaic parameters can be obtained. The champion device based on control SnO₂ present a PCE of 16.56% with a Voc of 1.019 V, a Jsc of 22.37 mA cm⁻², and a FF of 72.59% under reverse scan, while the champion device with MSG achieves a more superior PCE of 17.71% with a Jsc of 22.55 mA cm⁻², a Voc of 1.056 V, and a FF of 74.38%. It is clear that the Voc and PCE have improved significantly with the addition of MSG, more notably, compared to control SnO₂ device, the hysteresis index (HI=(PCEreverse-PCEforward)/PCEreverse) [66] of MSG-SnO₂-based device is reduced from 10.14% to 2.03% for MSG-SnO₂ device. It was found that interface defects and charge accumulation are important factors causing hysteresis [14,67]. Thus, the lower HI of MSG-SnO₂ device reflects that MSG dopants can effectively promote charge transport and can effectively reduce interface defects, which is confirmed by PL and TRPL. On the other hand, the migration of halogen ions is also an important cause of hysteresis. Herein, the migration of I ions could be effectively suppressed due to the presence of Na⁺ and the interaction between perovskite and MSG-SnO₂ ETL, which also contributes to the reduction of hysteresis. Eventually, the high-performance planar PSCs can be fabricated in air atmosphere via this effective MSG doping.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, for the first time, we introduced multifunctional gourmet powder with special functional groups to PSCs. With a device structure of FTO/MSG-  $  SnO_{2}/FA_{0.5}MA_{0.5}PbI_{3}/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au  $  , the high-efficiency and stable air-processing PSCs have been fabricated. It was found that MSG doping not only modifies the characteristics of   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL, but also effectively promotes the formation of high quality perovskite films, simultaneously, the performance of all aspects of the MSG-  $  SnO_{2}  $   film was improved than that of the pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   film. In addition, cooperating with the electric dipole effect and strong crosslinking of MSG, the ETL/perovskite interface

could be optimized, accelerating charge transfer and reducing interface defects. Consequently, the  $ \mathrm{MSG - SnO_2} $  device exhibits the best PCE of  $ 17.71\% $  with negligible hysteresis. Meanwhile, the unencapsulated device shows excellent long-term stability, maintaining  $ 91.25\% $  of its initial PCE even after storing for  $ 1320\mathrm{h} $  under ambient condition. Our work provides an interesting opportunity using greener and lower cost food additive to realize high-performance and stable PSCs towards commercialization.
