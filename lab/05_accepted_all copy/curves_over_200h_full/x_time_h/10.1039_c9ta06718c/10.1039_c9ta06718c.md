Abstract: Solution-processed perovskite films inevitably have a number of ionic defects, regarded as non-radiative recombination center, which limits the overall efficiency and the stability of perovskite solar cells (Pero-SCs). Passivation of these defects has been proven to be an efficient strategy to suppress the charge recombination and thus improve the performance of the Pero-SCs. Herein, for the first time, a  $ \pi $ -conjugated and alcohol-soluble small molecule with bilateral carboxyl and thiophene groups, namely 2,5-di(thiophen-2-yl)terephthalic acid (DTA) was introduced into the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  ( $ \mathrm{MA} = \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3 $ ) film, to passivate the defects and enhance the performance for the corresponding planar  $ p - i - n $  Pero-SCs. With high electron density, the symmetric carboxyl groups in both sides of DTA can effectively coordinate with unsaturated  $ \mathrm{Pb^{2 + }} $  cations to passivate the defects in the perovskite. This efficient defect passivation can reduce the charge trap density and increase the carrier lifetime, which leads to a significant enhancement of the open-circuit voltage from  $ 1.07\mathrm{V} $  for control Pero-SC to  $ 1.17\mathrm{V} $  for the passivated one, resulting in a power conversion efficiency

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid halide perovskite materials have attracted considerable attention owing to their excellent properties of high absorption coefficient,  $  ^{1}  $   wide absorption ranges,  $  ^{2}  $   tunable direct band-gap,  $  ^{3}  $   and long charge carrier diffusion length  $  ^{4}  $  . Particularly, perovskite materials have been applied to photovoltaic devices with promising results and have shown rapid development.  $  ^{5}  $   In the past few years, the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of perovskite solar cells (Pero-SCs) have been significantly enhanced from the initial 3.8% to the certified 24.2%.  $  ^{6,7}  $   Although the highest efficiency obtained in Pero-SCs is already higher than the commercial CIGS and CdTe thin film solar cells, the questionable long-term stability of the perovskite materials have slowed down their commercial application.  $  ^{8-10}  $   Recent researches have suggested that the degradation of Pero-SCs is mainly caused by humidity, oxygen, UV illumination, heat and electric fields.  $  ^{11,12}  $   Many efforts have been made to address this

problem. For example, interface engineering  $  ^{13-15}  $  , encapsulation or passivation  $  ^{16,10,1029}  $  , and controlling the film quality  $  ^{17}  $   have been proposed to further improve the efficiency and stability of the Pero-SCs. Nevertheless, to obtain Pero-SCs with competitive performance and accelerate their commercial application processes, efficient method is still in urgent need to address key issues concerning their performance and durability.

Solution process and thermal evaporation can be utilized for the deposition of the perovskite thin films.  $  ^{18}  $   Since the solution process is convenient and can be used for large-scale preparation, it currently becomes the most common method to fabricate the Pero-SCs. However, solution-processed perovskite films usually have a large number of ionic defects at the surface and grain boundaries (GBs), which not only could induce charge carrier recombination, but also could serve as a path for water to permeate. In addition, charge carrier recombination via those defect states induces nonradiative recombination. This recombination often generates a path for energy loss, which would undermine the fill factor (FF) and open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ). Thus, to achieve better photovoltaic performance of Pero-SCs, many studies focusing on the perovskite passivation have been explored recently.  $  ^{16, 19-21}  $

Under-coordinated Pb ions are considered to be the main sources of electronic trap states.  $  ^{[22-24]}  $   These under-coordinate Pb ions have a net positive charge (  $  Pb^{2+}  $  ), which facilitates the formation of bonds with electron-rich molecules. Passivation with the electron-rich molecules was considered as an effective strategy to reduce the defects at the crystal surface and GBs caused by   $  Pb^{2+}  $   cations, which can significantly suppress the charge recombination and increase the carrier lifetime in the perovskite films. Thus,

many small molecules such as guanidinium  $  ^{25}  $  , phenethylammonium fodiide  $  ^{26}  $  , trioctylphosphine oxide  $  ^{27}  $  , alkylamine  $  ^{28}  $   and carboxyl derivatives  $  ^{18, 29, 30}  $   have been employed during the preparation of Pero-SCs to passivate the defect traps and decrease the non-radiative recombination in the perovskite active layers. Snaith group demonstrated that, Lewis bases thiophene could bind the under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   cations in   $  MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   and effectively passivate the defect sites in the perovskite films.  $  ^{31}  $   Organic   $  \pi  $  -conjugated small molecules (such as IDIC  $  ^{32}  $   and ITIC  $  ^{33}  $  ) with the carboxyl group were also introduced into the Pero-SC as an interlayer to heal the trap defects. More recently, Yang et al. introduced caffeine with carbonyl group to slow down the perovskite crystal growth via interacting strongly with   $  Pb^{2+}  $   cations, resulting in a high device PCE of 20.25%.  $  ^{34}  $   In particular, Han group demonstrated that the passivation effect intensified with the increase of electron density on the carboxylate group, resulting in a significant PCE enhancement from 18.52% to 20.43%.  $  ^{35}  $   It can be seen from the previous studies that carbonyl or carboxyl with rich electrons can efficiently passivate the trap defects at the crystals surface and GBs in the perovskite films through the Lewis acid-base reaction with the under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   cations. However, the synthesis routes of most organic molecules are relatively complex. Moreover, when these organic molecules introduced into the perovskite, in most of cases, the use of benzenes solvent such as toluene or chlorobenzene, which is harmful to the environment, cannot be avoided. Therefore, there is a high urgent to explore simple and eco-friendly molecules introduced into perovskite to passivate the defects for improving the performance of the corresponding Pero-SCs.

In this work, we adopted a  $ \pi $ -conjugated and alcohol-soluble small molecule with bilateral carboxyl and thiophene groups, namely 2,5-di(thiophen-2-yl)terephthalic acid (DTA) (Fig.1a), for passivating defects in  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  device for the first time. DTA mixed with the organic salts MAI and MACl in isopropanol (IPA) are diffused into  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  via a two-step method, in which the benzenes solvent such as toluene or chlorobenzene was avoided, to let DTA incorporate into the inside of perovskite to fabricate the passivated  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  film. Our results showed that the symmetric carboxyl groups of DTA have high electron density and play a key role in passivating defect by forming Pb-O coordination. Consequently, the introduction of DTA resulted in decreased defect density, reduced charge carrier recombination, and enhanced performance of Pero-SC. The Pero-SC with inverted planar structure passivated with DTA exhibits significantly enhancing the PCE from  $ 19.10\% $  to  $ 21.45\% $ . To the best of our knowledge, this is one of the highest efficiencies in inverted planer Pero-SCs. Moreover, this DTA passivation strategy not only reduces the  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  loss (0.38V) but also improves the reproducibility of photovoltaic performance for the passivated device. In addition, the DTA passivated devices also exhibit a significant improvement in environmental stability and thermal stability. The high-performing devices can sustain over  $ 85\% $  of the initial PCE after 40 days aging in the ambient environment without any encapsulation and  $ 500\mathrm{h} $  of thermal aging at  $ 65^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  in a nitrogen atmosphere.

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, a simple and alcohol-soluble molecule (DTA) with bilateral carboxyl and thiophene groups was introduced into the perovskite layer to passivate the trap

defects for the first time. Theoretical calculation and experimental studies demonstrated that, the carboxyl groups in the DTA molecule have the highest electron cloud distribution and can sufficiently form coordination with the under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   cations, leading to reduced trap defects and suppressed non-radiative recombination in the perovskite active layer. The perovskite films passivated with DTA show higher steady-state PL intensity, longer decay lifetime, more compact morphology without pin-holes on the surface and fewer trap densities. When fabricating the Pero-SCs by doping DTA into the organic salts (MAI and MACl) precursor solutions, the champion PCE of 21.45% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.17 V was achieved, which is one of the highest efficiencies in the planar p-i-n Pero-SCs reported so far. In particular, a significant boost of 120 mV for   $  V_{oc}  $   and a notably small   $  V_{oc}  $   loss of 0.38 V were also obtained from the passivated devices. The environmental stability and the thermal stability of Pero-SCs were also notably improved after passivated by DTA. This study demonstrated the great potentials of DTA molecule as additive to improve the photovoltaic performance of Pero-SCs, which could be extendable to DTA derivatives as well as the application on Pero-SCs with different active layers and structures.
