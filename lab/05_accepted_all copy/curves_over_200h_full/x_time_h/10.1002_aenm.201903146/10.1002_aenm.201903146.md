# Dopant-Free, Donor-Acceptor-Type Polymeric Hole-Transporting Materials for the Perovskite Solar Cells with Power Conversion Efficiencies over 20%

Guofeng You, Qixin Zhuang, Lijun Wang, Xinyu Lin, Ding Zou, Zhenghuan Lin, Hongyu Zhen,* Wenliu Zhuang, and Qidan Ling

The rich molecular design of electron donor (D)-acceptor (A) polymers offers many valuable clues to obtain high-efficiency hole-transporting materials (HTMs) for use in perovskite solar cells (PVSCs). The fused aromatic or heteroaromatic units can increase the conjugation of the polymer backbone to facilitate electron delocalization, which increases the rigidity of adjacent units to prevent rotational disorder and lower the reorganization energy, leading to improved carrier mobility and optimized film morphology. In this work, fused-ring ladder-type indacenodithiophene and indacenodithieno[3,2-b]thiophene are used as D units, benzodithiophene-4,8-dione as the A unit, and thienothiophene as a  $ \pi $ -bridge to form the D-A polymers PBDTT and PBTTT, respectively. Both polymers exhibit favorable properties as HTMs including suitable energy levels, high hole mobility, and excellent film quality. Both dopant-free HTMs endow n-i-p PVSCs with promising performance and stability. A maximum power conversion efficiency of  $ 20.28\% $  is achieved for PBDTT-based devices, which is among the highest values reported to date.

Fused-ring ladder-type units such as indacenodithiophene (IDT)  $  ^{[37-41]}  $   and indacenodithieno[3,2-b]thiophene (IDTT)  $  ^{[38,41-47]}  $   are classic building blocks of D–A polymers. Stability is a primary evaluation index for solar cells. It is well known that dopant-free, compact, and hydrophobic films can act as effective protection layers, which is especially important for n-i-p PVSCs in which the perovskite is covered with HTL. We conducted stability tests of the devices with different HTMs under ambient conditions (humidity around 30%, room temperature). Figure 7 reveals that the devices with PBDTT and PBTTT showed favorable stability, maintaining over 80% of their original PCEs after storage for 720 h. In contrast, the PCE of the spiro-OMeTAD-based device decayed by nearly 40% from the original value in the same time period. The inset photographs in Figure 7 illustrate that after storage for around 1000 h, partial decomposition of the perovskite in the spiro-OMeTAD-based device was clearly observed. # 3. Conclusion

We developed two IDT/IDTT-BDD-based D-A polymers for use as high-efficiency polymeric HTMs of PVSCs. Promising device performance was achieved for n-i-p PVSCs containing the dopant-free polymers. The maximum PCE of  $ 20.28\% $  obtained for a device based on PBDTT is among the highest values achieved to date for PVSCs with dopant-free D-A polymer HTMs. The devices with PBDTT and PBTTT exhibited superior stability to those with doped spiro-OMeTAD because of the dopant-free, compact, and hydrophobic polymeric HTLs. The optimal thicknesses of PBDTT and PBTTT were only around  $ 25\% $  of that of spiro-OMeTAD. These results cast light on the promise of high-efficiency PVSCs using dopant-free polymeric HTMs, which have overwhelming advantages over doped small-molecule HTMs in terms of cost, processability, and stability, enabling the development of solution-processed PVSCs.
