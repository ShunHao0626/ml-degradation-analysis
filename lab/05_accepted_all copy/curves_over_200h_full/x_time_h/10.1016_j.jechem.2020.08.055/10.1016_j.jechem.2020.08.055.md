# Abstract

Dion-Jacobson phase two-dimensional (DJ 2D) perovskites, recently attracting considerable interests, exhibit excellent environmental stability and structural tunability, but their solar cells still offer unsatisfactory power conversion efficiencies (PCEs). Herein, we develop DJ 2D perovskites employing formamidinium (FA $ ^{+} $ ) as a ternary cation in the perovskite cages ((PDA)(FA) $ _{x} $ (MA) $ _{3-x} $ Pb $ _{4} $ I $ _{13} $ ,  $ x $ =0, 0.15, 0.3 and 0.6, PDA=1,3-propanediammonium) for highly efficient and stable perovskite solar cells (PSCs). We found that the DJ 2D perovskite with a 10% FA $ ^{+} $  fraction presents improved crystallinity, preferred vertical orientation, and longer charge carrier lifetime compared to that without FA $ ^{+} $  doping. As a result, the FA-doped DJ 2D PSCs exhibit a champion PCE of 14.74% with superior device stability. The unencapsulated devices sustain over 92% of its initial PCE after storage at a constant relative humidity (RH) of 65% for 6000 h, 90% by heat at 85 °C in air for 800 h, and 94% under 1-sun illumination for 5000 h. These findings demonstrate that the incorporation of FA cation into the DJ 2D perovskite is a promising strategy to develop highly efficient and stable DJ 2D PSCs.

Keywords: Dion-Jacobson; 2D perovskites; Perovskite solar cells; Formamidinium doping; Power conversion efficiency

# 1. Introduction

Stability issue has become a major challenge for the application of organic-inorganic hybrid halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs), although a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of over 25% has already achieved [1–7]. Recently, two dimensional (2D) layered hybrid perovskites have emerged as alternatives to traditional 3D perovskites because of their higher environmental stability as well as structural diversity [8–14]. Among them, Ruddlesden-Popper (RP) phase 2D perovskites containing monoammonium cations have attracted considerable research interests, and efforts are made on optimizing crystal orientation and optoelectronic properties etc. to enhance the efficiency of RP 2D PSCs [11,15,16]. On the other hand, Dion-Jacobson (DJ) phase 2D perovskites, including divalent organic cations between inorganic perovskite layers, are a new class of 2D perovskites with even higher stability than RP analogues [2,17]. DJ 2D PSCs based on 3-(aminomethyl)piperidinium (3-AMP) and its derived cations have been reported with PCEs from 7.34% to 9.2% and good device stability [18,19]. Recently, we have developed ultrastable DJ 2D PSCs based on 1,3-propanediammonium (PDA $  ^{2+}  $ ) with a maximum PCE of 13.3% and proposed that the absence of van der Waals gaps plays a major role in the stability of such 2D perovskite materials [20]. Noted that in those works the inorganic layer number (n value) is smaller than 5, guaranteeing the high stability of the DJ 2D perovskites, which is the original intention of developing 2D perovskite materials. Although some DJ 2D PSCs such as those based on benzene dimethanammonium (BzAD) cation have been reported to provide a maximum PCE of 15.6%, the n value reaches 10, which is not beneficial for the device stability (20% of initial PCE lost after one week exposure to 20%–50% relative humidity (RH) under dark) [21]. Therefore, it is of critical significance to further increase the PCE of PSCs based on DJ 2D perovskites with small n values (such as n≤4) in order to maintain enough material and device stability.

For this purpose, different attempts, such as film deposition techniques, cation/compositional engineering, and solvent engineering, have been made to further improve the PCE of DJ 2D PSCs having low  $  n  $  values. Among them, the compositional engineering approach, i.e. introducing additional cations such as formamidinium ( $  \mathrm{FA^{+}}  $ ), can alter or eliminate unwanted crystal phases and enable control of the crystal quality. For example, Wu et al. and Ke et al. separately reported an FA-doped DJ 2D perovskite (3-AMP)( $  \mathrm{MA_{0.75}FA_{0.25})_3Pb_4I_{13}}  $  fabricated with different methods, delivering higher device efficiency and stability than the one without the introduction of  $  \mathrm{FA^{+}}  $  [6,22]. However, the PCE of such FA-doped DJ 2D PSCs is still lower than RP 2D and 3D counterparts, and their device stability needs to be further enhanced.

In this work, on the basis of highly stable PDA-based DJ 2D perovskites (PDA)(MA) $ _{3} $ Pb $ _{4} $ I $ _{13} $  we previously reported, the FA $ ^{+} $  is incorporated to partially replace the MA $ ^{+} $  ((PDA)(FA) $ _{x} $ (MA) $ _{3-x} $ Pb $ _{4} $ I $ _{13} $ , x=0, 0.15, 0.3 and 0.6) to further improve the efficiency of DJ 2D PSCs. We found that by replacing 10% MA $ ^{+} $  with FA $ ^{+} $  the resultant FA-doped DJ 2D perovskite exhibits improved crystallinity, preferred crystal orientation, and prolonged charge carrier lifetime, boosting the device PCE from 13.28% to 14.74% under standard illumination. Moreover, the FA-doped DJ 2D PSCs are quite stable when subjected to a constant RH of 65% for 6000 h, heat at 85 °C for 800 h, and 1-sun light illumination for 5000 h.

# 2.1 Materials and methods

Formamidinium iodide (FAI, 98.99%, Sigma-Aldrich), lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ , 99%, Sigma-Aldrich), methylammonium iodide (MAI, 99.5%, Lumtec), 1,3-propanediamine (PDA, 98%, Inochem),  $ \gamma $ -butyrolactone (GBL, 99%, Shanghai Bai Shun Biological Technology Co., Ltd.), dimethyl sulfoxide (2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-Spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD, 99%, Borun New Material Technology Co., Ltd.), lithium

The XRD analysis was carried out on Powder X-ray Diffractometer (Rigaku) equipped with copper X-ray source, (Cu Kα: λ = 1.54 Å) at a voltage of 40 kV and 200 mA current. UV-Visible absorption spectra of FA-doped DJ 2D Perovskites thin films were measured at room temperature via Cary 5000 UV–Vis–NIR spectrophotometer, Varian Inc, USA. The surface morphology of the DJ 2D perovskites thin films was investigated on a Quanta 200F microscope (FEI Company) with an accelerating voltage of 0.5–30 kV. The cross-sectional SEM analysis of the corresponding samples were conducted on JSM-7800F JEOL. The GI-XRD patterns were obtained using the Beamline BL14B1 at the Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF) with the incident photon energy of 10 keV (wavelength of 1.2398 angstrom) at an incident angle of 0.4° and an exposure time of 30 s. The FA-doped DJ 2D perovskite samples for the GI-XRD measurement were prepared on FTO/TiO₂ substrates. Time-resolved photoluminescence spectra were recorded on an FLS920 fluorescence spectrometer (Edinburgh Instruments) in air at room temperature. A picosecond pulsed diode laser (406.8 nm) was used as the excitation source.

# 2.2 Preparation of FA-based DJ 2D perovskite precursor solutions

The FA-doped DJ 2D perovskite precursor solutions (0.8 M) were prepared from a stoichiometric ratio of MAI, FAI,  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and  $ \mathrm{PDAI}_2 $  in a mixed solvent of GBL and DMSO (7:3 volume). Specifically, the FA-0-based perovskite precursor solution was prepared by dissolving  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  (0.8 mmol, 368 mg), MAI (0.60 mmol, 95 mg) and  $ \mathrm{PDAI}_2 $  (0.2 mmol, 65 mg) in a mixed solvent of DMSO and GBL (300 μL+700 μL). The solution was stirred overnight at room temperature and filtered through 0.22 μm PTFE filter before use. The FA-5-based

# 2.3 Device fabrication and measurement

All devices were fabricated on cleaned fluorine doped tin oxide (FTO)-coated substrates. The substrates were ultrasonically cleaned with deionized water, ethanol, and isopropanol subsequently. The cleaned substrates were then dipped into a 0.2 M aqueous solution of   $  TiCl_{4}  $   and placed in oven at 70 °C for one hour. The glass/FTO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   substrates were then thoroughly washed with deionized water and finally blown with nitrogen gun. The FA-doped DJ 2D perovskite layers were fabricated by using a recently reported hot-casting method [11]. The substrate glass/FTO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   was first pre-heated at 190 °C for 8 min, then transferred to the spin-coating chuck. Afterwards, the aforementioned FA-doped DJ 2D perovskite precursor solution (80 μL) was dropped on the substrate then spin-coated at 4000 rpm for 40 s. The color of the thin film quickly changed from pale to black as solvent evaporated. After spin coating of the 2D perovskite layer the substrate glass/FTO/  $  TiO_{2}/FA  $  -doped 2D perovskite was cooled down to room temperature. The spiro-OMeTAD solution was spin-coated on the perovskite layer at 4000 rpm for 30 s. The spiro-OMeTAD solution was prepared by dissolving 90 mg of spiro-OMeTAD in one mL chlorobenzene with 22 μL Li-TFSI salt in acetonitrile (520 mg/mL) and 36 μL tBP. Finally, a 100 nm thick counter gold electrode was thermally evaporated under vacuum of   $  4 \times 10^{-4}  $   torr. The current density verses voltage (J-V) curves of the fabricated devices were measured in ambient condition at room temperature by

A black metal mask with an aperture was used to control the light scattering and focus the light on the active device area  $ (0.066\mathrm{cm}^2) $  during measurement. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectrum was obtained using a solar cell spectral response measurement system (Enli technology Co. Ltd).

# 2.4 Environmental stability test

The long-term humidity stability was conducted by storing unencapsulated devices and thin films inside an environmental chamber at a constant humidity (65% RH) at room temperature. The heat test was conducted by placing unencapsulated devices and thin films on a hot plate at a specific temperature in air, where the RH was in the range of 40-70%. The light soaking stability test of devices was conducted inside glove box under 1-sun illumination without encapsulation, using a constant light illumination system (Enli technology Co. Ltd).

# 3. Results and discussion

For the simplicity, the FA-based DJ 2D perovskites, (PDA)(FA)  $  _{x}  $  (MA)  $  _{3-x}  $  Pb  $  _{4}  $  I  $  _{13}  $   (x=0, 0.15, 0.3 and 0.6), containing different FA  $  ^{+}  $  ratios are denominated as FA-0, FA-5, FA-10, and FA-20, respectively, where the number next to FA represents the molar percentage of FA  $  ^{+}  $  with respect to MA  $  ^{+}  $ . Their detailed preparation procedure is described in the Experimental section. First, we studied the UV-Vis absorption property of the FA-doped DJ 2D perovskites (Fig. 1a). The FA-0 film has an absorption onset at 756 nm, which shifts to 767 nm after 5% MA  $  ^{+}  $  replaced with FA  $  ^{+}  $  (FA-5). With the FA  $  ^{+}  $  ratio further increases, the red shift is larger, a similar trend as that found in the FA-doped RP 2D perovskites [23,24]. The red shift corresponds to the reduced band gap (E  $  _{g}  $ ) with the increase of FA  $  ^{+}  $  ratio, due to the bigger ionic radius of the FA  $  ^{+}  $  than the MA  $  ^{+}  $  [25]. Additionally, photoluminescence (PL) measurements were carried out to detect the emission property of the FA-doped DJ 2D

Then, we investigated the crystallinity of FA-doped DJ 2D perovskite films using the X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurement as shown in Fig. 1(c). Two dominant peaks are noted at diffraction angles  $ (2\theta) $  of  $ 14.28^{\circ} $  and  $ 28.40^{\circ} $ , representing the characteristic (111) and (222) crystallographic planes of 2D perovskites, respectively [3,18,26,27]. Interestingly, when  $ 10\% $   $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $  is replaced with  $ \mathrm{FA^{+}} $  (FA-10) the intensity of diffraction peaks significantly increases, indicating a notable improvement in the crystallinity. In addition, it is found that the (111) peak slightly shifts toward a smaller angle after the incorporation of FA cations (Fig. S1). It can be attributed to the bigger size of the  $ \mathrm{FA^{+}} $  compared to the  $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $ , which expands the crystal lattice [23,24] This observation also indicates that the FA cations are included in the crystal lattice. For the FA-20, however, a new set of peaks (0k0) around  $ 10^{\circ} $  is found and the crystallinity is noticeably decreased.

The cross-sectional SEM images of FA-doped DJ 2D perovskite films with different  $ \mathrm{FA^{+}} $  ratios are depicted in Fig. 1(d). The FA-0 film is composed of randomly stacked crystal grains. After  $ 5\% $   $ \mathrm{FA^{+}} $  incorporation (FA-5) the grains stack more orderly, but some small crystal grains can still be observed. When the  $ \mathrm{FA^{+}} $  ratio further increases (FA-10), small crystal grains almost disappear, which decreases the grain boundaries along the vertical direction in favor of more efficient charge transports. For the FA-20, the film surface becomes rough with the appearance of small crystal grains, which will deteriorate the contact between the perovskite layer and the charge transport layer.

The crystal orientation of the 2D perovskites remarkably impacts on the charge transport and thus the photovoltaic performance, and a vertical growth of the 2D perovskite crystal with respect to the substrate is generally preferred. To elucidate the influence of  $ \mathrm{FA^{+}} $  incorporation on the orientation of DJ 2D perovskites, the grazing-incidence wide angle X-ray diffraction (GIXD) assessment of thin films was conducted. The FA-0 and FA-5 show similar sharp and discrete Bragg spots at  $ q_{\mathrm{z}} = 10~\mathrm{nm}^{-1} $  and  $ 20~\mathrm{nm}^{-1} $  (Fig. 2a and b), which are assigned to (111) and (222) planes, respectively [28,29]. The FA-10 sample exhibits the same set of diffraction spots (Fig. 2c), but with even stronger intensity in the  $ q_{\mathrm{z}} $  direction, which can be clearly differentiated by a plot of their integrated intensity (Fig. S2), indicating more highly oriented crystal planes. However, the FA-20 film has diffraction rings instead of spots (Fig. 2d), suggesting its random orientation.

In order to study the effect of   $  FA^{+}  $  doping on the photovoltaic performance, we compare the carrier lifetime and the carrier recombination process of the FA-0 and FA-10-based films and devices. First, time resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra were measured to investigate the carrier lifetime of FA-doped DJ-2D perovskites. Fig. 3(g) shows the TRPL spectra of FA-0 and FA-10 films deposited on glass. The decayed lifetime of FA-10 film is found to be 400 ns, which is about 5 times greater than that of FA-0 film. The prolongation of the carrier lifetime in the FA-10 film can contribute to slower carrier recombinations and better charge transports in the solar cell [31]. The carrier recombination behavior in the FA-0 and FA-10-based PSCs are then investigated by measuring the light intensity (I) dependence of   $  J_{sc}  $   and   $  V_{oc}  $  . The general relation between   $  J_{sc}  $   and I is   $  J \propto I^{\alpha}  $  ; if the   $  \alpha  $   value is close to 1, the device shows high charge mobilities and weak space charge effects [32,33]. Fig. 3(h) represents the plot of   $  J_{sc}  $   vs I for the FA-0 and FA-10-based devices under a double logarithmic scale, where the fitted   $  \alpha  $   value is 0.92 and 0.96, respectively. The larger   $  \alpha  $   value of the FA-10-based device suggests its weaker bimolecular recombination, leading to improved FF and   $  J_{sc}  $   [34]. Furthermore, the light intensity dependence of   $  V_{oc}  $   is given in a semilogarithmic scale in Fig. 3(i). Generally, the trap-assisted Shockley–Read–Hall recombination plays the role when the slope deviates from 1 kT/q, where q is elementary charge, k is Boltzmann constant, and T is temperature [35]. The result shows a slop of 1.48 kT/q for the FA-10-based device, which is smaller than 1.88 kT/q for the FA-0-based device, suggesting the suppressed trap-assisted recombination in the former. These observations are consistent with the enhanced crystal quality as demonstrated by XRD and SEM results.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we have developed FA-doped DJ 2D perovskites, (PDA)(FA)  $  _{x}  $  (MA)  $  _{3-x}  $  Pb  $  _{4}  $  I  $  _{13}  $ , with different ratios of FA  $  ^{+}  $  for the application in DJ 2D PSCs. It is found that the DJ 2D perovskite film exhibits improved crystallinity and preferred vertical orientation as well as prolonged carrier lifetime when replacing 10% MA  $  ^{+}  $  with FA  $  ^{+}  $ . Consequently, the FA-10-based DJ 2D PSC achieves a maximum PCE of 14.74%, higher than that obtained from devices without FA  $  ^{+}  $  doping. More importantly, the FA-doped DJ 2D PSCs and films show excellent environmental stability, when subjected to humidity, heat and light illumination. This work demonstrates that the incorporation of FA cation into DJ 2D perovskites is promising to improve the efficiency of DJ 2D PSCs, while maintaining excellent device stability.
