# ABSTRACT

The organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved rapid progresses in this decade. However, the limited solar spectral utilization has restricted the further improvement of performance for the PSCs. One promised approach on solving this problem is utilizing IR to visible upconversion nanoparticles (UCNPs) in the PSC devices. Despite, confined by the lower quantum yield and smaller absorption cross section of the traditional UCNPs, its application is still a great challenge. In this work, the IR-783 dye sensitized core/shell   $  NaYF_{4}:Yb^{3+}  $  ,   $  Er^{3+}@NaYF_{4}:Yb^{3+}  $  ,   $  Nd^{3+}  $   UCNPs, were synthesized and coupled with plasmonic Au nanorods (AuNRs) film. Thereby, the upconversion luminescence (UCL) intensity was enhanced about 120 folds, while the luminescent quantum yield (QY) was improved from 0.2% to 1.2%. Then, the composite UCNPs were assembled on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer of the PSCs, which resulted in the power conversion efficiency (PCE) increasing from 19.4% to 20.5% under simulated 100 mw/cm² AM 1.5G irradiation. Up to now, it is the highest PCE for the PSCs based on various upconversion devices. Under the irradiation of sun concentrator (1 W/cm²), the PCE of the device can be further improved to 21.1%. The studies in depth indicate that under standard sunlight irradiation, the improvement of PCE is contributed by both the IR to visible UCL and the scattering effect of the UCNPs. Under irradiation of sun concentrator, the UCL contributes dominantly to the further improvement of PCE. This work provides an effective method on increasing the luminescent QY utilized in the PSCs and is of great significance to the future concentrating PSC devices.

# INTRODUCTION

Recently, organic and inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskite materials have attracted extensive interests due to their advantages of low manufacturing cost, low exciton binding energy and long charge diffusion length.  $  ^{1-4}  $   Because of these advantages, PSCs have achieved great developments with rapid increasing PCE from 3.8% to 25.2% within ten years.  $  ^{5}  $   The performance improvement of PSCs was generally based on optimization of structure or passivation of perovskite defects.  $  ^{6,7}  $   In general, the common perovskite layer has an optical band gap of about 1.5 eV, allowing absorption of incident light with a wavelength range from 300 nm to 800 nm.  $  ^{8}  $   That means about 50% of the infrared (IR) light from solar energy will be wasted. Therefore, it is imperative to utilize IR especially near-infrared (NIR) solar energy, which remains a major challenge for PSCs.  $  ^{9}  $

One strategy to broaden the IR response of the PSCs is to introduce UC particles/layers, which can convert IR photons into short visible photons. $ ^{10, 11} $  These visible photons can be absorbed by the perovskite absorption layer to generate free charge carriers, thus improving the photocurrent (Jsc) of the perovskite devices. $ ^{12} $  In some previous literature, up conversion materials were introduced into solar cells. In DSSC, Wu et al. introducing Y $ _{0.78} $ Yb $ _{0.20} $ Er $ _{0.02} $ F $ _3 $  into TiO $ _2 $  resulted in a PCE of 7.90%, which originated from the luminescent center improved the Jsc and light harvesting. $ ^{13} $  Several attempts have been performed subsequently related to exploring the UCNPs in the PSCs devices. $ ^{14, 15} $  There are mainly two traditional methods to introduce the UCL materials. One is to place the upconversion layer outside of the device, and the other is to place the UCNPs inside of the device, such as inserted the UCNPs into the electron modification layer. $ ^{16, 17} $  For the former case, in one of our previous works, the LiYF $ _4 $ :Yb,Er single crystal was placed in the front of the perovskite device, which relatively improved 7-8% of the PCE of the PSC device under the irradiation of concentrated sunlight ( $ \sim $ 1.5 W/cm $ ^2 $ ), however, under the irradiation of standard

Therefore, it is critical to improve the luminescent QY and absorption cross section of the UCNPs while they are used to expand the IR response of the PSCs.  $  ^{19}  $

In recent years, it has been reported that dye sensitized UCNPs can effectively enhance UCL. For instance, Zou et al. developed a strategy to enhance the UCL of   $  NaYF_{4}:Yb,Er  $   nanoparticles, in which IR-806 dye molecules acted as an antenna to absorb NIR photons at a broad wavelength range, and then transferred the energy to   $  Yb^{3+}  $  .  $  ^{19}  $   However, the large mismatch between the emission spectra of IR-806 dye and the absorption spectra of lanthanide ions partly inhibited the energy transfer process. To overcome the energy mismatch, Chen et al. obtained efficient UCL by designing IR-808 dye sensitized   $  NaYbF_{4}:Tm^{3+}@NaYF_{4}:Nd^{3+}  $   core/shell UCNPs, in which the dye had a wide absorption cross section and spectral overlap with   $  Nd^{3+}  $  .  $  ^{20}  $   Besides, the introduction of metal nanostructures with strong local surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) is also an effective method to enhance UCL.  $  ^{21}  $   It is expected that the coupling

# RESULTS and DISCUSSION

Hexagonal core/shell structure   $  NaYF_{4}  $   with low phonon energy was selected as the host material for its efficient lanthanide emission characteristics. The synthesis procedure was described in the previous literature.  $  ^{[23]}  $   In this work, ligand-exchange was required to replace the oleic acid (OA) on the surface in order to connect the IR-783 dye. Specifically, nitrosonium tetra-fluoroborate (  $  NOBF_{4}  $  ) was used to convert the UCNPs through hydrophobic OA-coated into the hydrophilic   $  BF_{4}^{-}  $  coated, so as to facilitate the connection of subsequent IR-783 dye.   $  NOBF_{4}  $   was dispersed in DMF solution to exchange OA ligands, and then mixed with UCNPs dispersed in n-hexane solution to fully oscillate and obtain UCNPs coated by   $  BF_{4}^{-}  $  . As illustrated in Figure 1a, the IR-783 dye was mixed with the former solution and attached on the surface of UCNPs to obtain IR-783 dye sensitized core/shell structure UCNPs. The IR-783 dye

# CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, in order to improve the absorption cross section and the quantum yield of the UCNPs, we synthesized the IR-783 dye sensitized core/shell   $  NaYF_{4}:Yb^{3+}  $  ,   $  Er^{3+}@NaYF_{4}:Yb^{3+}  $  ,   $  Nd^{3+}  $   UCNPs and further coupled them with plamonic AuNRs. Induced by the sensitization of dye molecule and LSPR effect of the AuNRs, the UCL intensity was enhanced ~120 fold, while the luminescent QY was increased from original 0.2 % to 1.2% under the excitation of 8 W/cm² 980 nm laser diode. When the UCNPs/IR-783 dye/AuNRs composite films were introduced into the PSCs, the PCE of the devices was increased from 19.4% to 20.5% under simulated sunlight AM 1.5G irradiation (100 mw/cm²). The systematic studies indicated that the PCE enhancement was contributed by both the IR to visible UCL and enhanced light harvesting induced by scattering of the UCNPs. Under the irradiation of sun concentrator with power density of 1 W/cm², the PCE of the device was further improved from 20.5 % to 21.1%, which originated mainly from the contribution of NIR to visible UCL. When the devices were stored in ambient conditions, the PCE of the UCNPs/IR-783 dye/AuNRs film modified devices maintained over 80% of the initial value after 1500 h. This work indicates that insulating dye-sensitized UCNPs in the PSCs is a helpful approach to enhance the PCE of the PSCs.
