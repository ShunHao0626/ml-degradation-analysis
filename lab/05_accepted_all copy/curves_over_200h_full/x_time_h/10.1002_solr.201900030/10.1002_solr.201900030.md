# High Efficient Hole Extraction and Stable All-Bromide Inorganic Perovskite Solar Cells via Derivative-Phase Gradient Bandgap Architecture

Guoqing Tong, Taotao Chen, Huan Li, Wentao Song, Yajing Chang, Jingjing Liu, Linwei Yu, Jun Xu, Yabing Qi,* and Yang Jiang*

# 1. Introduction

Inorganic perovskite materials have demonstrated outstanding performance in the field of photovoltaic devices due to their superior charge carrier transport properties and excellent thermal stability. In particular, the inorganic perovskite derivative phases show special properties in terms of phase stability and optoelectronic application, especially in the phase transition investigation. However, their commercial applications still face challenges due to the large recombination at the interface, resulting in poor efficiency and metastable phases such as iodide perovskite existing in the film. Herein, an all-bromide inorganic perovskite solar cell has been developed by introducing the derivative phases (CsPb $ _2 $ Br $ _5 $  and Cs $ _4 $ PbBr $ _6 $ ) to construct gradient bandgap architecture. This graded heterojunction device is realized with a controllable sequential vapor deposition procedure. The valance band maximum elevates gradually with the presence of derivative phases and effectively blocks electrons and boosts the hole extraction efficiency at the counter electrode, which promotes charge separation and reduces the interface recombination. Ultimately, an impressive power conversion efficiency of 10.17% is achieved through a CsPbBr $ _3 $ /CsPbBr $ _3 $ -CsPb $ _2 $ Br $ _5 $ /CsPbBr $ _3 $ -Cs $ _4 $ PbBr $ _6 $  architecture strategy with excellent stability above 3000 h (85% of initial performance) in a humid environment (@RH ≈45%) and 700 h (83% of initial efficiency) under thermal conditions (@ 100 °C).

It is noteworthy that the cesium halide inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are developing rapidly in recent years due to

high carrier mobility, broad spectrum of light absorption (up to   $  \approx700  $   nm) and tunable bandgap (from 1.7 to 2.4 eV) by controlling the ratio of halide composition.  $  ^{[15-19]}  $   The impressive PCEs exceeding 10% have been realized for   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   and their mixed phase PSCs. Especially with a hole transport layer (HTL)-free architecture, the all inorganic PSCs have achieved a PCE up to 9%.  $  ^{[20-24]}  $   In parallel, they also showed better stability than the hybrid PSCs in the humid environment owing to the substitution of organic methyl ammonium with inorganic cesium cation, and the replacement of organic HTLs such as spiro-OMeTAD with carbon/gold electrode.  $  ^{[16,25-27]}  $   For example, an all inorganic PSC using   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   as the light absorption layer and carbon as the counter electrode was first proposed and exhibited no degradation in humid air for more than three months.  $  ^{[28]}  $   In this simple architecture, the inorganic perovskite can serve not only as a light capture layer but also as a hole conductor layer for the perovskite, which displays unique ambipolar property.  $  ^{[29,30]}  $   Although this simple architecture

Metal halide perovskites (MAPbX $ _3 $ , X = Cl, Br, I) are emerging as the most promising candidates in photovoltaic materials featuring the broad and tunable absorption cross-section, superior mobility and carrier lifetime, etc. $ ^{[1-5]} $  Among these materials, the most notable compounds are the iodinated hybrid perovskites of MAPbI $ _3 $  and its mixed phases, with a certified highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) of above 23.7%, respectively. $ ^{[6]} $  Whereas, they suffer from poor humidity and thermal stability, because of the volatile nature of the organic component and larger radius of iodide, which is the major obstacle for their future applications. $ ^{[7,8]} $  To alleviate the issues associated with organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites, researchers have been studying inorganic perovskite materials, which have shown promising optoelectronic performance in solar cells, light-emitting diodes (LEDs), photodetectors (PDs) and lasers. $ ^{[9-14]} $

Several studies have reported large recombination of electrons and holes at the interface between perovskite and counter electrode, which is ascribed to the lower hole extraction efficiency and electrons transport to counter electrode resulting in a low photocurrent.  $  ^{[25,29,31]}  $

Previously, several strategies have been used to overcome the recombination in the PSCs to obtain high efficiencies, such as interface modification,  $  ^{[31-33]}  $   heterojunction engineering.  $  ^{[12,25,34]}  $   The multigraded-heterojunction (GHJ) with an optimal energy band was achieved between MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   and CsPbBr  $  _{x}  $  I  $  _{3-x}  $   via precisely tuning the halide proportion and distribution, which demonstrated a significantly improved PCE of 11.33%.  $  ^{[19]}  $   CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br and CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   quantum dot (QD) were also used as component cells to construct graded bandgap CsPbI  $  _{2+x}  $  Br  $  _{1-x}  $   perovskite solar cells showing a remarkable PCE of 13.45%.  $  ^{[12]}  $   The appearance of energy alignment bends the valance/conduction band edge between the perovskite film and electrode, which boosts the electron/hole splitting and extraction.  $  ^{[12,25,35]}  $   Therefore, these efforts can effectively improve carrier transport and boost the efficiency of PSCs. However, the inorganic iodide is not the most stable in halides, whether in the precursor or in the synthesized halide perovskite. The instability is a concern due to the most of above efforts that are on the basis of the iodide-perovskite and its quantum dots, such as CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   and CsPbI  $  _{x}  $  Br  $  _{3-x}  $ . It is found that the black cubic perovskite phase (  $  \alpha  $  -phase) can only exist stably at a high temperature above 300 °C, but will turn to the undesired yellow orthorhombic non-perovskite phase (  $  \delta  $  -phase) rapidly in ambient condition, which leads to instability of iodide perovskite.  $  ^{[8]}  $   In contrast, bromine with a smaller atomic radius was introduced into inorganic halide perovskite to replace iodine, resulting in improved stability.  $  ^{[36-38]}  $   For example, a bromide inorganic PSC (CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $ ) with a PCE of 9.7% by using Br to substitute I was proposed, which achieved an impressive stability without degradation for more than 130 days at 25 °C and 40 days at 90 °C.  $  ^{[39]}  $   According to the composition engineering, the mixed halide perovskites such as CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br and CsPbIBr  $  _{2}  $   were also reversed to the orthorhombic non-perovskite phase after exposure in air for a long time. This phase transformation process renders CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   and its mixed phase unstable at room temperature or in humid environment, which significantly impedes their future application.  $  ^{[40,41]}  $

Based on the above situation, all-bromide perovskite materials with superior stability and available precursors can be reasonably considered as competitive components for all inorganic PSC. Up till now, several novel bromide inorganic perovskite derivative-phases, i.e.,   $  CsPb_{2}Br_{5}  $   and   $  Cs_{4}PbBr_{6}  $ , with different bandgaps have been explored by controlling the proportion of the CsBr/PbBr  $  _{2}  $   precursors.  $  ^{[31]}  $   Their high absorption in the ultraviolet region, ultrastability and high photoluminescence quantum yield have endowed them with excellent performance in LEDs and PDs.  $  ^{[31,42-45]}  $   Previous studies found that the presence of the derivative-phase can not only improve the light capture in the UV region, but also passivate the interface defects.  $  ^{[45-47]}  $   On the contrary, the intermediate phase like   $  Cs_{4}PbI_{6}  $   could also be detected in the iodide perovskite, which turned into   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   quickly with excess CsI at the interface, resulting in poor performance for the iodide PSCs.  $  ^{[7]}  $

In this study, we introduce all-bromide perovskite derivative-phases upon the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   compact layer to construct a novel gradient bandgap architecture of   $  FTO/c-TiO_{2}/CsPbBr_{3}/CsPbBr_{3}-CsPb_{2}Br_{5}/CsPbBr_{3}-Cs_{4}PbBr_{6}/carbon  $  . The   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   and its derivative-phase can be precisely controlled by tuning the thickness of CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   precursor film via a sequence vapor deposition. This energy alignment of valance and conduction bands between the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $ ,   $  CsPbBr_{3}-CsPb_{2}Br_{5}  $   and   $  CsPbBr_{3}-Cs_{4}PbBr_{6}  $   thin film provides an efficient transfer path and energy barriers to the electrodes and holes, respectively. On the basis of this graded adjustment and synergistic effect, a PCE of 10.17% without HTL was obtained. Furthermore, the devices present nearly unchanged under the humid condition with a relative humidity of   $  \approx45\%  $   for 3000 h. This gradient bandgap design represents a great advancement in all inorganic PSCs and opens the window of perovskite derivative-phase.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The formation of the gradient bandgap architecture is schematically illustrated in Figure 1a. Here, a uniform and full coverage film of pure   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   (Figure 1b and Figure S1, Supporting Information) is firstly deposited on the FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate by thermal evaporation (step ①). Following a simple annealing treatment at 350 °C for several minutes, a   $  CsPbBr_{3}-CsPb_{2}Br_{5}  $   derivative-phase was coated up the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film with a ratio of 1:1.1 for   $  CsBr/PbBr_{2}  $   (step ②). The same deposition step was carried out with the   $  PbBr_{2}/CsBr  $   ratio of 1:1.1 to obtain the   $  CsPbBr_{3}-Cs_{4}PbBr_{6}  $   derivative-phase (step ③), as seen in Figure S2, Supporting Information, the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   crystals are fully covered with derivative layer (  $  CsPbBr_{3}-CsPb_{2}Br_{5}/CsPbBr_{3}-Cs_{4}PbBr_{6}  $  ). # 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, we demonstrate all-bromide inorganic PSCs based on perovskite derivative-phase with excellent power conversion efficiency. The HTL-free and gradient bandgap architecture were fabricated by controlling the thickness of CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   thin films, which provided an efficient hole transport path and a higher multigrade energy barrier for electron transport. An outstanding PCE of 10.17% was achieved using the architecture of   $  CsPbBr_{3}/CsPbBr_{3}-CsPb_{2}Br_{5}/CsPbBr_{3}-Cs_{4}PbBr_{6}  $  . More importantly, this all-bromide inorganic PSCs presented an impressive stability (  $  \approx85\%  $   of their initial performance) under humid condition for more than 3000 h and could retain 83% of their initial efficiency after stored under thermal environment (at 100°C) for   $  \approx700  $   h. These results indicate that the gradient bandgap by vapor deposition controlling can offer a novel approach to prepare hybrid inorganic PSCs with high PCE and excellent stability.
