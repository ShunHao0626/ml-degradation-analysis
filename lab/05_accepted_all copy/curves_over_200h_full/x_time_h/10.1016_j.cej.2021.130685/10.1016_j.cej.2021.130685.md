# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have drawn increasing attention because metal halide perovskites possess high absorption coefficients, long carrier diffusion lengths, high carrier mobility, low exciton binding energy, low cost, and ease of fabrication   $  [1-3]  $  , which led to the achievement of a certified record power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.5% up to now   $  [4]  $  . However, there is still large room for efficiency improvement in light of high theoretical Shockley–Queisser limit efficiency over 30% for single-junction PSCs   $  [5,6]  $  . In addition, poor moisture stability hinders the practical application of PSCs. Therefore, it is highly desirable to further increase PCE and moisture stability.

It is well known that poor perovskite film quality resulting from uncontrollable crystallization is one of the important reasons for efficiency losses. To date, various methods have been developed to modulate the perovskite crystallization, such as perovskite composition

The trap-assisted nonradiative recombination at grain surface and grain boundary (GB) of perovskite films impede the further improvement of power conversion efficiency (PCE) and stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). In addition, the poor moisture stability of perovskite films hinders the commercial application of PSCs. Herein, we report a multifunctional GB modification strategy where the wide-bandgap hydrophobic   $  PbSO_{4}  $   modification layer is in situ formed at the GBs of perovskite films through the reaction between methylamine sulfate and   $  PbI_{2}  $  . It is revealed that multiple functions are achieved after the formation of   $  PbSO_{4}  $   modification layer, including crystallization improvement, defect passivation, and hydrophobicity improvement. As a result, the   $  PbSO_{4}  $   modified device exhibits a PCE enhancement from 19.53% to 21.90% as compared to the control device. Moreover, up to 71% of its initial PCE is maintained for the unencapsulated   $  PbSO_{4}  $   modified device after aging under a relative humidity of   $  80 \pm 5\%  $   for 250 h. This work proposes an effective multifunctional approach to modify the GBs of perovskite films for the purpose of simultaneous increasement of PCE and stability.

To achieve this goal, a variety of additives have been developed, such as Lewis base molecules   $  [11,19]  $  , Lewis acid molecules   $  [20]  $  , organic or inorganic salts   $  [21,22]  $  , etc. Lewis base molecule containing N, O and/or P electron donors could be one effective defect passivator since positively charged halide vacancy defects dominate among possible point defects   $  [23]  $  . In view of this, hydrophobic organic fullerene and its derivatives have been attempted   $  [20,25,26]  $  . As we discussed above, the PCE and stability of PSCs are highly dependent on perovskite crystallization, GB defects, and the hydrophobic property of the incorporated additives. Presently reported most additives only could play one or two functions of improving crystallization, passivating GB defects, and ameliorating moisture stability   $  [24]  $  . For example, some additives (e.g., MACl   $  [27,28]  $  , MASCN   $  [29]  $  , NH  $  _{4}  $  SCN   $  [30]  $  , HI   $  [31]  $  , etc.) were found to be effective in controlling perovskite crystal growth but not effective for defect passivation. The above research results encourage us to develop multifunctional additive molecules to control perovskite crystal growth, passivate film defects, and improve moisture resistance.

# 2. Results and discussion

Fig. 1a shows the chemical structure of MS, which contains a sulfate anion (  $  SO_{4}^{2-}  $  ). The   $  SO_{4}^{2-}  $   in MS was expected to be able to react with the excess   $  Pb^{2+}  $   to in situ form ultrathin   $  PbSO_{4}  $   modification layer at the GBs of perovskite films during perovskite crystallization and growth. Since   $  PbSO_{4}  $   is highly hydrophobic, first, the formed   $  PbSO_{4}  $   modification layer was expected to be capable of preventing ambient water and oxygen from attacking perovskite films, as shown in Fig. 1b. Second, the crystallization of perovskite films could be modulated due to the strong chemical interaction between   $  SO_{4}^{2-}  $   and   $  Pb^{2+}  $ . Finally, the formed   $  PbSO_{4}  $   modification layer was expected to be able to passivate the undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   at the GBs and surface of perovskite films, as exhibited in Fig. 1c.

The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) were performed to further confirm the mechanism of action between methylamine sulfate and perovskite. Compared with the control film, strong S 2p and O 1 s signals can be detected in the MS modified films (Fig. S1), indicating the existence of  $ \mathrm{SO}_4^{2-} $  in the final perovskite films. Notably, the binding energy of the S 2p peak in the MS modified perovskite film shifted to a lower binding energy position to that of in pure MS powders (Fig. 1d), indicating the coordination reaction between the  $ \mathrm{SO}_4^{2-} $  in MS and the  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  in the perovskite [36]. Also, as is revealed in Fig. 1e, the Pb  $ 4\mathrm{f}_{7/2} $  and Pb  $ 4\mathrm{f}_{5/2} $  peaks of the control perovskite film were detected at  $ 138.07\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ 142.92\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively, both of which moved to lower binding energy by  $ \sim 0.13\mathrm{eV} $  after MS modification. We fabricated electron-only devices to calculate the trap density of the perovskite films without and with MS by using the space charge limited current (SCLC) method [41]. As illustrated in Fig. 3f, the dark current increases linearly with an increasing applied voltage at the low bias region and passes the kink-point to the trap-filled region. The moisture stability poses a serious challenge for the commercial application of PSCs. The moisture stability of the perovskite films without and with MS under a high relative humidity of   $  85 \pm 5\%  $   was evaluated. As illustrated in Fig. 5a, it was found that the target perovskite film was still black while the control film was yellowish-brown, suggesting almost complete degradation. A significantly reduced absorption intensity was found for the control perovskite film while the absorption intensity was almost maintained (Fig. 5b). In addition, much more serious degradation can be observed for the control perovskite film in comparison with the target film, as evidenced by XRD results (Fig. 5c). We then performed SEM characterization on the aged perovskite films. As shown in Fig. S10, the grains of the control perovskite film seriously decomposed with obviously exposed FTO substrate. On the contrary, the MS modified perovskite films still exhibit a complete and flat morphology. Therefore, MS modification can significantly improve the environmental stability of the films. Subsequently, we further investigated the moisture stability of the devices without and with MS. Fig. 5e shows the ambient stability of the control and target devices under  $ 25 \pm 5\% $  RH in the dark at room temperature, and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters are seen in Table S2. The control device degraded thoroughly after  $ 1300\mathrm{h} $  while the target device maintained  $ 91\% $  of its initial PCE value after  $ 2000\mathrm{h} $ . The target device degraded by only  $ 29\% $  after aging for  $ 250\mathrm{h} $ , while the control device degraded by up to  $ 91\% $  after aging for only  $ 109\mathrm{h} $ . The above results implied that the moisture stability of perovskite films and devices was significantly improved after the incorporation of the GB passivation layer. On the one hand, the hydrophobic  $ \mathrm{PbSO_4} $  GB modification layer should be responsible for the increased moisture stability. On the other hand, the increased grain size and reduced defect density also should contribute to the enhanced moisture stability. It has been widely demonstrated that increased gain size and reduced defect density can increase device stability [10,11,44]. We then address the light stability performance of corresponding PSCs. Both of the two PSCs are stored in the ambient atmosphere at room temperature without encapsulation, and the corresponding PCEs versus time are plotted inside of Fig. S11. Light soaking tests show that the control PSCs could exhibit a rapid  $ 30\% $  loss in PCE after  $ 120\mathrm{h} $  of continuous illumination, while the MS modified PSC could maintain  $ 86\% $  of its initial efficiency after  $ 120\mathrm{h} $ . Further improvement of the light stability and air stability of PSCs could be attributed to improved crystallinity and effective inhibition of ion migration by  $ \mathrm{PbSO_4} $  passivation layer to boost the stability of the perovskite films. Further, the in-situ produced  $ \mathrm{PbSO_4} $  can effectively inhibit the migration of halogen ions between grains, which is the main reason for the decreased humidity stability and light stability. Above, the significantly increased stability is related to the hydrophobic surface, the reduced defect density, and suppressed ion migration by the in-situ produced  $ \mathrm{PbSO_4} $  passivation layer.

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated a multifunctional GB modification strategy where the wide-bandgap hydrophobic   $  PbSO_{4}  $   modification layer is in situ formed at the GBs of perovskite films through the reaction between methylamine sulfate and   $  PbI_{2}  $  . After the formation of   $  PbSO_{4}  $   GB modification layer, the crystallinity is improved, the gain size is enlarged, the GB defects are effectively passivated, and the perovskite grains are protected from the attack by water and oxygen. The above favorable properties contribute to simultaneously enhanced PCE and stability. As a result, the   $  PbSO_{4}  $   modified device delivers a promising PCE of 21.90% and sustains 71% of its initial PCE after aging under a relative humidity of   $  80 \pm 5\%  $   for 250 h. We promote an effective in situ defect passivation strategy to achieve improved photovoltaic performance and water resistance stability in PSCs.
