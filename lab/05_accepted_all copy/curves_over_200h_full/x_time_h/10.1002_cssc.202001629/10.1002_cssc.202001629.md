# A Dendrite-Structured RbX (X=Br, I) Interlayer for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   Perovskite Solar Cells with Over 15% Stabilized Efficiency

Supporting information for this article is given via a link at the end of the document.

Abstract: Interface engineering has shown great potential to improve the photovoltaic performance and long-term stability of perovskite solar cells. Herein, RbX (X=Br, I) materials were developed as the interfacial modifiers for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cells that realized power conversion efficiencies (PCE) over 15% with a high open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.27 V. The RbX interlayer that presented a dendrite-shaped structure could optimize the surface wetting behavior of   $  TiO_{2}  $   films and thus enabled the high-quality of perovskite films. More importantly, the RbX could better align the devices' energy levels and passivate surface defects because of its large bandgap. The PCE of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   device with RbX interlayer remained 87% of its initial efficiency for 800 h under the ambient atmosphere.

# Introduction

Inorganic caesium lead halide perovskites (CsPbX $ _3 $ , X = I, Br, or mixture) have gained broadly attention due to their notable advantages of thermal stability and competitive optoelectronic properties. $ ^{[1]} $  Compared with phase-instable CsPbI $ _3 $  perovskite, CsPbI $ _2 $ Br has a relatively large band gap of 1.91 eV, but with more excellent phase stability. $ ^{[2]} $  However, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is still far away from its theoretical efficiency, mainly attributed to the intense charge recombination loss and thus the large open-circuit voltage (V $ _{\text{OC}} $ ) deficit. $ ^{[3]} $

Previous studies have identified that the charge recombination mainly occurs at interfaces or grain boundaries of perovskites with several possible reasons.  $  ^{[4]}  $   One is the mismatched band alignment between perovskite and transport layers, resulting in the accumulation of carriers at the interface.  $  ^{[4b]}  $   On the other hand, surface imperfections at grain boundaries have been manifested as charge trapping sites, providing channels for Shockley-Read-Hall recombination.  $  ^{[5]}  $   Toward these issues, interfacial engineering strategy has been demonstrated that can align the matched energy levels, reduce non-radiative recombination and enhance the interface linking between each layer.  $  ^{[6]}  $   Recently, several kinds of interfacial layers, such as tert-butyl cyanoacetate (t-BCA), mino-functionalized polymer (PN4N), aminothiazolium iodide (ATI) and 2-thiopheneme-thylammonium iodide (Th-NI) have been developed for the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cells. However, they usually need expensive materials or time-consuming procedures, which emphasize the importance of developing low-cost, easy fabrication and efficient interlayers.  $  ^{[7]}  $   As an alkali cation, Rb content has shown a positive role in promoting the performance of   $  MAPbX_{3}  $

and   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells in previous reports.  $  ^{[8]}  $   Moreover, the application nanostructures, such as branched   $  TiO_{2}  $ , have also advanced the performance of perovskite opto-electrical devices.  $  ^{[9]}  $   In this work, we demonstrate a dendrite-structured RbX (X=Br, I) interlayer for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cells that can assist the formation of high-quality perovskite films. In addition, the RbX interlayer downshifts the   $  TiO_{2}  $   conduction band for optimized band alignment and elongates the carrier lifetime. Devices based on such interlayer delivered over 15% PCE and good ambient stability.

The wetting behavior of the substrate is of significance for the deposition of the perovskite films. We measured the DMSO contact angles of the as-prepared  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  substrates. After the introduction of RbBr and Rbl interlayers, the contact angle of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  film decreases from  $ 40^{\circ} $  to  $ 30^{\circ} $  and  $ 20^{\circ} $ , respectively (Figure 2a). The optimized wetting behavior would contribute to the speeding of the precursor solution, and thus enable full surface coverage of the resulted perovskite films. We next prepared the perovskite layer as schemed in Figure 2b, where water/isopropanol solution with or without RbX was firstly deposited on the surface of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ -coated substrate. Figure 2c, d and Figure 2e show the surface characteristics of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite films with and without RbX layers. As expected, perovskite films with RbX interlayer allow the full coverage of the substrates, where as many hundred nanometer sized holes can be observed on the pristine film.

Moreover, the average root-mean-square roughness (  $  R_{a}  $  ) of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films without and with RbBr or Rbl interlayers are 10.61 nm, 6.55 nm and 5.35 nm, respectively (Figure 2f-h). It is indicated that the surfaces of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films with RbX interlayer are smoother. The nonuniformity of the pristine perovskite film would further incur the weak light harvesting capability, leading to a low current density in the solar cells. XPS spectra of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film with and without RbX interlayers were implemented to check the existence of Rb in perovskite layers. There was no Rb 3d peak and no obvious peak shift for the Cs 3d, Pb 4f, I 3d, and Br 3d in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film with RbX interlayer, suggesting that there is almost no RbX is incorporated into the perovskite (Figure S4 in the Supporting Information). Ultra-violet (UV-vis) absorption spectra of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films presents apparently improved absorbance for the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films with RbX layer due to the full coverage of the substrates (Figure 3a).  $  ^{[6]}  $   XRD patterns in Figure 3b reveal the   $  \alpha  $  -phase cubic structure of all   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films that the main diffraction peaks at 14.46° and 29.45° can be well indexed to that of (100) and (200) planes.  $  ^{[7c]}  $   As the interlayers are thin, no signals of RbBr or Rbl are detected.

Solar cells were fabricated with the architecture of FTO/TiO $ _2 $ /CsPbI $ _2 $ Br/P3HT/Ag (Figure 4a and Figure S7 in the Supporting Information). All devices were measured under simulated AM 1.5G irradiation (100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $ ), with a reverse scan rate of 0.15 V s $ ^{-1} $ . J-V characteristics of the devices with and without RbX interlayers are shown in Figure 4b and the photovoltaic parameters are provided in Table S2 (Supporting Information). The pristine device showed a short-circuit density (J $ _{SC} $ ) of 15.18 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , a V $ _{OC} $  of 1.16 V, a fill factor (FF) of 0.738, generating a PCE of 12.96%. By inserting the RbBr interlayer, the J $ _{SC} $ , V $ _{OC} $ , FF and PCE were dramatically improved to 15.39 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , 1.27 V, 0.779 and 15.22%, respectively. Device with Rbl interlayer attained a maximum PCE of 15.42%, with high photovoltaic parameters of J $ _{SC} $ , V $ _{OC} $  and FF of 15.54 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , 1.27 V and 0.784, respectively. It can be noted that the hysteresis of CsPbI $ _2 $ Br solar cells with RbX interlayer was reduced under opposite scan directions. The integrated current density (J $ _{SC} $ ) from external quantum efficiency (EQE) for the prinstin, Rbl and RbBr PSCs were calculated to be 14.46, 15.07 and 14.96 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , respectively, in consistent with the J-V measurements (Figure S8 in the Supporting Information). The statistical PCE values further verify the reproducibility of this strategy: the mean PCEs of pristine, RbBr and Rbl devices were 12.74 ± 0.57%, 14.27 ±

0.53% and 14.17 ± 0.53%, respectively (Figure 4c). Device with RbI interlayer exhibited the stabilized photocurrent density of 15.52 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  and PCE of 15.37% within 200 s held at the MPP of 0.99 V in ambient air (Figure 4d). Furthermore, the device with RbBr interlayer also maintained a steady PCE of 15.18% with stabilized current density of 15.32 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  under the same conditions, again confirming the credibility of J-V measurements (Figure S9 in the Supporting Information).

Carrier dynamics of PSCs with and without RbX interlayers were measured by electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS). The fitted Nyquist plots with the equivalent circuit diagram at the bias voltage of 1.0 V are shown in Figure 4e. The recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film with RbX interlayer had a significant increase compared with that of the pristine one. Mott-Schottky curves of PSCs displayed that the built-in potential (  $  V_{Bi}  $  ) of pristine, RbBr and Rbl were 0.90, 0.98 and 1.03 V, respectively, in accordance with the   $  V_{OC}  $   values from J-V measurements (Figure S10 in the Supporting Information).  $  ^{[6b]}  $   Therefore, the optimized band alignment and passivation effect of RbX (X=Br, I) interlayers can enhance the interfacial charge extraction, as well as reduce carrier trapping that uplift the Fermi level splitting of solar cell devices.  $  ^{[6b,17]}  $   In addition, we measured the space charge limited current (SCLC) characteristics of electron-only devices with and without RbX interlayers, and the results are shown in Figure S11 (Supporting Information). It shows the reduced trap-fill limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ) and trap density (  $  N_{I}  $  ) in the perovskite films with RbX layer.  $  ^{[18]}  $   The unencapsulated solar cells were then subjected to air for long-term stability tests. The devices with Rbl interlayer retained about 88% of its initial efficiency after storage for over 800 h (Figure 4f), while the pristine one lost 50% of its initial efficiency. We further measured the long-term stability of PSCs with and without RbX layers under dark storage in the   $  N_{2}  $   glovebox. As shown in Figure S12 (Supporting Information), device with RbX layer exhibited 89% PCE comparison to its initial PCE during 1500 h. In contrast, the PCE of pristine sample dropped nearly 17% under the same conditions. The improvement of long-term stability with RbX interlayer may be due to the high-quality films.  $  ^{[18b]}  $

# Conclusion

In summary, we have shown a dendrite-structured RbX (X=Br, I) interlayer for the interface engineering of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cells via a simple solution process. Such interlayer can align the energy level, optimize the surface wettability and reduce charge recombination at the   $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface. A champion PCE of 15.42 % was achieved in the planar   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with RbI interlayer. Importantly, the long-term stability of device with RbX interlayer exhibited obvious improvement in comparison to the pristine one. This work provides a simple and efficient approach to improve the performance and stability of PSCs.

Preparation of perovskite solar cells: FTO substrates were ultrasonically cleaned by detergent solution, distilled   $  H_{2}O  $  , alcohol, and acetone for 20 min respectively. After being dried by nitrogen flow, the substrate was then immersed in a 25 mM   $  TiCl_{4}  $   aqueous solution for 60 min at 70 °C and washed with distilled water and ethanol, followed by annealing at 500 °C for 60 min in muffle to form a compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   blocking layer. To prepare the RbX interlayer, 2.5 mg/ml RbBr or 2.0 mg/ml RbI of water/isopropanol (volume ration 1:2) solution was spin-coated onto the   $  TiO_{2}/FTO  $   substrate at 3000 rpm for 20 s, followed by annealing at 200 °C for 8 min in air. The preparation of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films was based on a modified method according to the literature.  $  ^{[2]}  $   P3HT (15 mg/mL in chlorobenzene, with 45 μL Li-TFSI solution (2 mg in 1 mL acetonitrile) and 10.2 μL t-BP) was spin-coated onto the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film at 2500 rpm for 25 s as a hole transporting layer. Finally, a 100-nm-thick Ag layer was evaporated onto the HTL-coated perovskite film to finish the device fabrication.

Characterization: Field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM, HITACHI S4800) and atomic force microscopy (AFM, Veeco/DI) were used to characterize the morphology and surface roughness of the films. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were recorded with an X-ray diffractometer Bruker D8 Advance operated Cu Kα radiation. The surface elemental composition of TiO₂ films was analyzed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, PHI5300, Mg anode, 250 W, 14 kV), and the binding energy of the C 1s peak at 284.8 eV was taken as an internal reference. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) spectra was recorded with He source of incident energy of 21.22 eV (He I line) in Ningbo Institute of Industrial Technology, CAS, Ningbo. Ultra-violet (UV-vis) absorption spectrometry were collected using a Cary 500 UC-Vijs-NIR spectrophotometer. Photoluminescence (PL) spectra was acquired at room temperature by exciting the sample deposited onto the TiO₂ or TiO₂ with RbX (TiO₂-RbX) FTO-substrate with the Fluorolog-3-p spectrophotometer under the excitation wavelength of 380 nm. The solar cells were illuminated by a solar light simulator (Solar IV-150A, Zolix) and light intensity was calibrated by a standard Newport calibrated KG5-filtered Si reference cell. Photocurrent density-voltage (J-V) curves of solar cells were measured by Keithley 2400 digital sourcemeter with a scan rate of 0.15 V s⁻¹ in dry air (relative humidity ~ 15 ± 3%) and solar cell devices were masked with a metal aperture to define the active area of 0.0625 cm². The steady state power output of the best-performing device was measured by biasing the device at maxing power point (MPP) by a Keithley 2400 digital sourcemeter. Impedance spectra of solar cells were acquired by using electrochemical workstation (Parstat 2273, Princeton) under certain working mode in dark conditions.
