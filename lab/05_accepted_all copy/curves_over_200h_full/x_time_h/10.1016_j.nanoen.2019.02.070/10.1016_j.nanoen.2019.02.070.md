# ARTICLEINFO

The quality of perovskite absorber is one of the most important factors to influence the efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, it is still challenging to obtain perovskite layers with required properties including large grain sizes, better crystallinity, less grain boundaries, and uniform morphology by the current preparation techniques. Here we develop a novel method, where the  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  nanoparticles (NPs) are introduced into the chlorobenzene anti-solvent to improve the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film quality in terms of film structure, morphology and crystallinity, leading to reduced charge recombination and improved charge transfer.  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  NPs play a role as nucleation centers in the growth process of perovskite films, and  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  NPs also induce a passivation layer  $ \mathrm{Cs}_{1 - x}\mathrm{MA}_x\mathrm{PbI}_{9 - x}\mathrm{Br}_x $  on the top of perovskite layer. The charge transport and power conversion efficiency (PCE) are improved due to the introduction of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  NPs. A champion PCE of  $ 20.46\% $  is obtained for the PSCs based on high quality perovskite film prepared with  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  NPs. In addition, the PSCs with  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  NPs also exhibit improved stability. This work not only demonstrates a novel strategy to prepare high quality perovskite films for PSCs with high efficiency and stability, but also provides important insight in the growth mechanisms of perovskite films toward high crystallinity and less defects.

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted much attention due to their high PCE, simple solution based fabrication methods and excellent optoelectronic properties   $  [1-4]  $  . Power conversion efficiency (PCE) record of PSCs has reached   $  \sim23.3\%  $     $  [5]  $  , which is close to the PCE of the commercial silicon solar cells. However, the reported PCE record of PSCs is still rather far away from their theoretical limit PCE (31%)   $  [6]  $  . Therefore, how to further improve the PCE of PSCs is still challenging. Another very urgent issue needs to be addressed is the poor environmental stability of PSCs. It is reported that interfacial charge transfer and the crystallization properties of the perovskite layers influence the PCE and environmental stability of PSCs significantly   $  [7-12]  $  . Recently, PbS has been incorporated into PSCs as a hole-transporting layer, which facilitates the hole transport, leading to improved PCE   $  [13]  $  . High quality perovskite films are required to obtain high efficiency of PSCs. Typically, high quality perovskite films lead to decreased carrier recombination and improved the carrier mobility & lifetime. Several methods have been reported to enhance the crystallinity of perovskite layers by controlling the crystallization rate. Therefore, it is necessary to develop compatible methods to fabricate PSCs with high efficiency and stability.

Recently, perovskite nanoparticles (NPs) have attracted intensive attention due to high absorption coefficient, tunable band gap, and high PL quantum yields (PLQY) [16-19]. Most importantly, perovskite NPs have the similar properties with perovskite absorption layer in terms of stoichiometry and crystal structure. Cha et al. introduced  $ \mathrm{MAPbBr_3 - x_x} $  nanocrystals to the perovskite layer and hole transport layer (HTL) to control the perovskite band structure and improve hole extraction [20]. However, the poor chemical stability of  $ \mathrm{MAPbBr_3 - x_x} $  limits its application in PSCs. # 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

2.1.1. Synthesis of  $ CsPbBr_3 $  NPs

The Cs-oleate solution was synthesized by   $  Cs_{2}CO_{3}  $   (0.814 g, Sigma-Aldrich), 30 mL octadecene (ODE, 90%, Alfa Aesar) and 2.5 mL oleic acid (OA, 90%, Alfa Aesar) in a 100 mL flask. The solution was kept at 120 °C for 1 h under the protection of   $  N_{2}  $  . Then, the solution was further heated to 150 °C and maintained for 3 h to completely dissolve   $  CsCO_{3}  $  . For the synthesis of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs,   $  PbBr_{2}  $   (0.138 g, Sigma-Aldrich) and ODE (10 mL, Alfa Aesar) were transferred into a 50 mL 3-necked flask. Then the flask was kept at 120°C for 1 h. After that, OA and oleylamine (OLA, Aladdin) with volume ratio of 1:1 were added. When the   $  PbBr_{2}  $   was dissolved completely, the solution was heated to 160°C. Then, 1 mL Cs-oleate solution was injected instantly. Five seconds later, the colloidal solution was cooled in an ice-water bath. Finally,   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs were dispersed in chlorobenzene.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

FTO glasses were firstly etched with zinc powder and 2 molL  $  ^{-1}  $  HCl (57%, Aladdin). The etched FTO substrates were washed by water, ethanol, and isopropanol. After that, the FTO substrates were treated by O  $  _{3}  $  plasma for 20 min. The compact TiO  $  _{2}  $  (c-TiO  $  _{2}  $ ) films were fabricated on the washed FTO by spin coating a solution of titanium diisopropoxide bis (acetylacetonate) (97%, Sigma-Aldrich) diluted in 1-butanol (MACKLIN (Shanghai, China)) with the speed of 2000 rpm for 30 s. To fabricate a mesoporous TiO  $  _{2}  $  (m-TiO  $  _{2}  $ ), TiO  $  _{2}  $  paste (Dyesol 30NR-T) was diluted with ethanol (weight ratio of 1:5). The diluted TiO  $  _{2}  $  solution was coated on the top of c-TiO  $  _{2}  $  layer with 4000 rpm for 30 s. The FTO/c-TiO  $  _{2}  $ /m-TiO  $  _{2}  $  substrates were annealed at 135 °C for 5 min and heated at 500 °C for 40 min in ambient air. The perovskite solution was prepared by the solutions of PbI  $  _{2}  $  (1 M, Advanced Election Technology Co., Ltd), and MAI (1 M, Advanced Election Technology Co., Ltd) in anhydrous DMF:DMSO (MACKLIN (Shanghai, China)) mixed solvent (v:v = 7:3). Then the perovskite solution was prepared on the top of m-TiO  $  _{2}  $  film by spin coating with 1000 rpm for 10 s and 5000 rpm for 20 s. 400 μL chlorobenzene (MACKLIN (Shanghai, China)) containing CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $  NPs with different concentrations was dropped on the substrate. Then, the films were annealed at 100 °C for 15 min in glovebox. Then, an HTL solution of 144.6 mg of spiroOMeTAD, 57.6 μL of 4-tert-butylpyridine and 35 μL of Li-TFSI (predissolved in acetonitrile) in chlorobenzene was spin-coated on the top of perovskite film with 3000 rpm for 30 s. In the end, Au electrode (100 nm) was fabricated by thermal evaporation.

# 2.3. Device characterization

The transmission electron microscope (TEM) images of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs were obtained by Hitachi H-8100IV with the acceleration voltage of 200 kV. The scanning electron microscope (SEM) images was collected by the Hitachi S-4800 equipped with an EDS system (EDAX Genesis 2000). Crystal phase information of the films was studied by X-ray power diffraction (XRD) by a Cu target radiation source (  $  \lambda = 1.54 \AA  $  ). The curves of photocurrent density versus on the voltage (J-V) were measured under AM 1.5 G simulated illumination (100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $  ,

Model 91160, Newport). Photospectral response was measured by an external quantum efficiency (EQE) measurement system (QEX10, PV Measurement), which was equipped with monochromator, a lock-in amplifier, Xe lamp, and current-voltage amplifier. The optical properties of the samples were measured by an UV-vis spectrophotometer (UV-2550). The absolute PLQY of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  NPs was obtained with a fluorescence spectrometer (FLS920P, Edinburgh Instruments) equipped with an integrating sphere. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) data were obtained by an ESCALab220i-XL electron spectrometer from VG Scientific. The photoluminescence (PL) spectra were collected by the Omni- $ \lambda $ 300 Monochromator/Spectrometer of Zolix. The time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra were collected with the PL spectrometer (Edinburgh Instruments, FLS 920). The roughness of film was characterized by atomic force microscopy (AFM; 5500, Agilent, Santa Clara, CA).

# 3. Results and discussion

The   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs were synthesized by hot injection method (more details are in the experimental section). The TEM image and size distribution of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs are shown Fig. 1a. The average size is about 10 nm with excellent monodispersity. The XRD patterns of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs displayed in Figure S1 indicate that   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs are indexed into cubic phase. To further investigate optical properties of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs, the absorption and PL emission spectra of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs were measured (Fig. 1b). The absorption peak is at around 510 nm, indicating that the band gap is 2.43 eV for   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs. Sharp PL emission peak at 526 nm is observed with the full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) of 24 nm. In addition to the emission wavelength, PLQY of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs is another critical factor for the application in solar cells [23]. PLQY of 80% is obtained for our   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs. The photographs of the chlorobenzene without and with   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs are shown in Fig. 1c. Good UV light stability of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs is also required for solar cell applications. Figure S2 shows that the PL of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs is very stable even after exposure to UV light for 30 min, which makes   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs very promising for solar cell applications [24].

Fig. 1d schematically presents the detailed preparation process of fabricating perovskite films using chlorobenzene and  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  NPs as the anti-solvent. The anti-solvent was slowly dropped on the prepared perovskite film.  $ \mathrm{Cs}^- $  and  $ \mathrm{Br}^+ $  are produced as  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  are dissolved in perovskite precursor solution, leading to the formation of  $ \mathrm{Cs}_{1 - y}\mathrm{MA}_y\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Br}_x $  on the surface of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film. Then the substrates were heated at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 15\mathrm{min} $  to evaporate the residual solvent. The typical device architecture used in this study is the structure of FTO/the  $ c\text{-TiO}_2/ $   $ \mathfrak{m}\text{-TiO}_2/ $  perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD/Au as presented in Fig. 1f. The thicknesses of the FTO,  $ c\text{-TiO}_2 $ ,  $ m\text{-TiO}_2 $ , perovskite, spiro-OMeTAD and Au layers are around  $ 570~\mathrm{nm} $ ,  $ 35~\mathrm{nm} $ ,  $ 200~\mathrm{nm} $ ,  $ 350~\mathrm{nm} $ ,  $ 200~\mathrm{nm} $  and  $ 100~\mathrm{nm} $ , respectively, according to the cross-sectional SEM image (Fig. 1e), which also displays a well-defined layer by layer structure with apparent interfaces.

To study the influence of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs on the crystal quality and morphology of the perovskite films, top view SEM images of perovskite films with different   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   concentrations are displayed in Fig. 2(a-d). All perovskites films show very uniform surface, which is consistent with previous report [21]. Although the morphology of the pristine perovskite film is uniform with grain size distribution of 100–300 nm, there are still some voids and pinholes. The voids are detrimental to charge transport and collection. The introduction of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs can effectively increase grain sizes to 1–2   $  \mu  $  m as the concentration of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs increases from 1% to 2%. As shown in Fig. 2c, the grain size distribution of the perovskite film with 2%   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs is relatively uniform.   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs provide additional seed-like nucleation sites, promoting the formation of perovskite lattice structure. The grain size is clearly reduced as the concentration of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs increases to 3% as shown in Fig. 2d. It is possible that too many seeds disturb the growth of perovskite films. Therefore, the introduction of a proper amount of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs is an effective method to fabricate perovskite films with less grain boundaries, which leads to reduced charge recombination and enhanced charge extraction and collection [25]. To further study the influence of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs on perovskite morphology, corresponding AFM measurements were carried out, and the AFM images are shown in Figure S3. The perovskite film with 2%   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs exhibits the decreased surface roughness (4.71 ± 0.16 nm) compared with the pristine film (5.29 ± 0.18 nm). In addition, the increased grain size of the perovskite film with 2%   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs can be observed in AFM images, which is consistent with the results of SEM images. The smaller root-mean-square roughness (RMS) also implies smoother surface, less grain boundaries and voids, leading to improved carrier extraction. The improved quality of perovskite films

In order to further study the mechanisms of the improved cell performance by   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs, some optical and electrical measurements were carried out. The PL spectra of perovskite layers without and with 2%   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs on FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}  $   substrates are shown in Fig. 5a. The PL peak of the pristine perovskite film locates at 778 nm. The PL intensity of perovskite film with   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs decreases dramatically compared with the pristine perovskite film. This can be attributed to more efficient charge transfer from perovskite films to   $  TiO_{2}  $   due to the improved crystal quality of perovskite films by   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs, resulting in lower PL intensity [33]. Furthermore, there is slight blue shift (7 nm) for perovskite with 2%   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs compared with the pristine perovskite film, which is consistent with the variation of band gap. The substitution of   $  I^{-}  $  by   $  Br^{-}  $  occurs due to the introduction of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs. According to the former report, this could also lead to increased band gap and blue shift of PL [34]. This also confirms that the additional perovskite layer (  $  Cs_{1-y}MA_{y}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $  ) with the enriched Br composition is formed on the perovskite film surface by introducing   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs. The quenching is also confirmed by TRPL decay curves as shown in Fig. 5b. The biexponential decay function is used to fit the data, and the fitting results are shown in Table S2. A fast decay constant (  $  \tau_{1}  $  ) expresses charge extraction, and a slow decay constant (  $  \tau_{2}  $  ) is associated with radiative recombination. Compared with the pristine perovskite film (104.77 ns), the lifetime of the perovskite film with 2%   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs is 88.04 ns. This indicates that introduction of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs increases the radiative recombination but inhibits non-radiative recombination, which leads to the improvement of Voc and FF [35]. It is possible that the charge recombination is inhibited due to surface passivation effect of the   $  Cs_{1-y}MA_{y}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   layer on the top of perovskite films. The passivation layer can facilitate

electron transfer, and reduce electron recombination with the holes in the back contact. To obtain more information on carrier transportation and recombination, EIS measurement is carried out. The addition of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs to the perovskite films may also influence work function of perovskite films and the interface band alignment in the devices, which is further studied by UPS in Fig. 5j. The work function and the valence band maximum (VBM) are calculated from the binding energy cutoff (Ecutoff) in the range of 10–20 eV. The VBM values are -5.78 eV and -5.41 eV for perovskite without and with   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs, respectively. The conduction band minimum (CBM) is obtained from the VBM and the band gap (  $  E_{g}  $  ). According to the UV–vis absorption spectra shown Fig. 2g,   $  E_{g}  $   values are 1.59 and 1.60 eV for pristine perovskite and perovskite with 2%   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs, respectively. The CBM values are determined to be -4.19 eV and -3.81 eV for perovskite without and with   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs, respectively. The schematic energy band diagram is shown in Fig. 5k. The data for   $  TiO_{2}  $ , spiro-MeOTAD, FTO, and Au are obtained from the literature [42]. By introducing   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NPs, an additional perovskite layer (  $  Cs_{y}MA_{1-y}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $  ) with the enriched Br composition is formed on the top of perovskite film as depicted in Fig. 1b. The VBM of the perovskite film is slightly upshifted, which matches better with the VBM of spiro-MeOTAD, leading to more efficient hole transport, compared with the pristine perovskite film. The upshifted VBM could prevent backflow of electrons from the conduction band of the perovskite to spiro-MeOTAD, improving the charge collection and reducing the carrier recombination process. Furthermore, the upshifted CBM also benefits electron injection into   $  TiO_{2}  $   due to increased driving force. As a result, Voc, FF, and PCE are enhanced.

# 4. Conclusion

In summary, perovskite films with highly crystallinity and less defects are successfully prepared by   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NP additive in chlorobenzene solution. The   $  Cs_{1-y}MA_{y}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   passivation layer is formed on the top of the primary   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ , which can facilitate the charge transport at the interfaces. Moreover, this method is highly reproducible with average PCE of 20.10% for 40 cells. The optimized PCE of 20.46% can be achieved under AM 1.5 illumination. This work demonstrates the novel functions of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   NP additive, which can improve the crystallization quality of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   films and form one additional passivation layer, leading to improved charge transfer and PCE. This work will also induce the significant investigations of the influence of perovskite NP additives on the performance of PSCs.
