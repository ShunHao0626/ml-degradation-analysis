# p-Type Carbon Dots for Effective Surface Optimization for Near-Record-Efficiency   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   Solar Cells

Xi Guo, Biao Zhao, Kunxiang Xu, Shaomin Yang, Zhike Liu,  $  ^{*}  $   Yu Han, Jie Xu, Dongfang Xu, Zhanao Tan,  $  ^{*}  $   and Shengzhong (Frank) Liu  $  ^{*}  $

Interface modification to minimize charge recombination and trapping for efficient charge transport is crucial for the performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, functionalized p-type blue carbon dots (B-CDs) are ventured as an interface passivation layer to enhance the efficiency and long-term stability of all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. It is found that first the blue carbon dots with abundant N–H, C–N, C–O, and C=O functional groups effectively passivate defects by reacting with   $  I^{-}  $  and   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions in the perovskite through hydrogen and coordinative bonds. Second, the p-type B-CDs modifiers form a P–N junction with the n-type perovskite to provide efficient pathways for hole transfer and electron blocking. Third, the B-CDs increase the hydrophobicity of the perovskite film to improve the stability of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. With the above advantages, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC with B-CDs modification shows an efficiency as high as 16.76%, one of the highest for its type. In addition, the modification renders significant improvement of air and light stability, with 95.33% of the initial PCE retained after storage in the ambient environment for 1000 h. This work demonstrates the great potential of B-CDs application in perovskite-based optoelectronic devices.

Recently, various carbon nanomaterials (fullerene and its derivatives, graphene oxide (GO), carbon nanotubes, carbon quantum dots (GQDs), graphdiyne, and so forth) have been developed to modify the charge transport layer and perovskite layer.  $  ^{[11-14]}  $   Compared with polymer-based interface modifiers, the carbon nanomaterials are readily synthesized, low-cost and stable, which means that carbon-based modifiers offer practical advantages in terms of both cost and technical aspects.  $  ^{[15]}  $   Carbon modifiers as additives into PSCs have garnered significant attention because they successfully enhance charge transport properties and device stability.  $  ^{[16]}  $   However, the poor dispersion and easy aggregation of carbon nanomaterials in common solvents unfortunately limit their application in the perovskite solution.  $  ^{[17]}  $   Therefore, it is more reliable to modify the surface of perovskite film with carbon nanomaterials, which will not affect the crystallization of the perovskite film and the quality of the perovskite solution.

# 2. Results and Discussion

As shown in Figure 1a, the blue carbon dots (B-CDs) were prepared from o-phenylenediamine (o-PDA) and phloroglucinol by a solvothermal method.  $  ^{[28,29]}  $   The synthesis details are described in the Experimental Section. The prepared B-CDs in ethanol exhibited strong blue emission when under UV light (  $  \lambda = 375  $   nm) illumination. The morphology of the B-CDs was observed by transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and, as shown in Figure 1b, the B-CDs with an average size of   $  \approx 10  $   nm are well dispersed. Figure 1c presents the UV–vis absorption and PL spectrum of the B-CDs in ethanol. The absorption edge of the B-CDs is   $  \approx 340  $   nm, and from the Tauc equation   $  (\alpha h\nu)^{1/2} = B(h\nu - E_{\mathrm{g}})  $  ,  $  ^{[6]}  $   where   $  \alpha  $   is the absorption coefficient, hν is the photon energy, and B is a constant, together with the Tauc curve of the absorption spectrum in Figure 1d, the band edge   $  (E_{\mathrm{g}})  $   of B-CDs is calculated to be   $  \approx 3.25  $   eV, which is in accordance with the literature.  $  ^{[30]}  $   The PL emission shows a strong and broad peak in the 325–550 nm wavelength range, and the B-CDs exhibited strong blue emission when excited by UV light (  $  \lambda = 375  $   nm).  $  ^{[6]}  $

In order to explore the interaction between the B-CDs and   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  , Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy was conducted to investigate the surface chemical structure of the B-CDs and B-CDs/  $  PbI_{2}  $  . As shown in Figure 3a, three bonds (C=O, N-H, and C-N) can be observed in the FTIR spectra of the pure B-CDs, and they all shifted to lower wavenumber after contact with   $  PbI_{2}  $  . The shift of the C=O/C-N peaks is ascribed to the coordination interactions between   $  Pb^{2+}  $   and C=O/C-N.  $  ^{[34]}  $   The shift of the N-H peak is due to the formation of a hydrogen bond (N-H···I) between the N-H and I in the   $  PbI_{2}  $  . Therefore, B-CDs can passivate not only cations but also anions in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite. XPS was applied to explore the chemical state of elements in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -B-CDs film. As shown in Figure 3b, the N 1s signal can be assigned to pyridinic N, N-H bonds and C-N bonds for the three peaks centered at 398.2, 400.1, and 401.0 eV, respectively.  $  ^{[35,36]}  $   Compared with pure B-CDs, the peak positions of the N-H and C-N bonds in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -B-CDs film show an obvious shift to higher binding energy, confirming an interaction between N in the B-CDs and perovskite, which is consistent with the FTIR results shown in Figure 3a. As shown

in Figure 3c, the O 1s signal can be assigned to absorbed O, C–O bonds, and C=O bonds for the three peaks centered at 530.9, 532.3, and 533.2 eV, respectively.  $  ^{[37,38]}  $   The peak positions of the C–O and C=O bonds in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br-B-CDs  $   film show an obvious shift to higher binding energy with respect to pure B-CDs, confirming an interaction between O in B-CDs and Pb in the perovskite, which is also consistent with the FTIR results shown in Figure 3a. To further confirm the interaction between B-CDs and   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , the XPS spectra for different elements (Cs 3d, Pb 4f, I 3d, Br 3d) in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   are shown in Figure 4a–d, respectively. The peak positions of Pb 4f and I 3d shift to lower binding energy while exhibiting a substantial decrease in peak intensity with B-CDs passivation due to the interaction of Pb–O and N–I, which confirms the strong interactions between B-CDs and Pb/I in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and is consistent with the FTIR and XPS results exhibited in Figure 3.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, p-type B-CDs (  $  \approx  $  10 nm) were synthesized and applied as surface modification materials in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs, and they not only induce a strong interaction with the perovskite to passivate interface defects but also provide efficient pathways for hole transfer and electron blocking. Simultaneously, the B-CDs provide an effective barrier against water molecules due to their hydrophobic property, reducing the degradation of the perovskite film. Based on the B-CDs modification,

the optimized device achieves a champion efficiency as high as 16.76%, one of the highest PCEs for   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs. The unencapsulated   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSC retains 95.33% of its initial efficiency after aging in air with 25% relative humidity at 30 °C for 1000 h. This work establishes the great potential of B-CDs as an effective surface modification to realize high-efficiency and stable inorganic PSCs.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: Cesium iodide (CsI) (≥99.5%), lead bromide (PbBr₂) (≥99.5%), lead iodide (PbI₂) (≥99.5%), and lead acetate (PbAc₂) (≥99.5%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. PbI₂(DMSO)₂ and PbBr₂(DMSO) adducts were synthesized in accordance with the previously reported method.[9,10] N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) (≥99.9%) and dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) (≥99.9%) were purchased from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co. Ltd. 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP) and bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide lithium salt (Li-TFSI) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.

Preparation of B-CDs: B-CDs were prepared from o-phenylenediamine (o-PDA) and phloroglucinol by the solvothermal method. First, 1.92 g o-PDA and 1.50 g phloroglucinol were dispersed in 150 mL ultrapure water. Then, 10 mL 98%   $  H_{2}SO_{4}  $   was carefully dropped into the solution. Subsequently, the solution was transferred into a 300 mL poly(tetrafluoroethylene) (Teflon)-lined autoclave for the solvothermal reaction and heated at 180 °C for 8 h. When the reaction finished, the reactor was cooled to room temperature to obtain a tawny mixed solution. Next, NaOH and   $  NaHCO_{3}  $   were used to adjust the pH of the final solution followed by removing the inorganic salts. The crude product was purified by silica gel column chromatography with 1:25 (v/v) methanol/DCM ether as the eluent to give 300 mg light yellow powder. 0.5–5 mg B-CDs powder was dissolved in 1 mL IPA to obtain the B-CDs solution.

Device Fabrication: FTO-coated glass substrates were sequentially cleaned with acetone, IPA, and distilled water in an ultrasonic bath for 30 min and then dried with   $  N_{2}  $  . The clean FTO glass substrates were then treated with UV-Ozone for 10 min prior to the deposition of a   $  TiO_{2}  $   electron-transport layer (ETL). The   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was deposited by immersing FTO glass substrates in 200 mL aqueous solution with 4.5 mL titanium tetrachloride for 60 min at 70 °C then rinsed with distilled water and annealed at 200 °C for 30 min. The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution was prepared by dissolving 0.2886 g   $  PbBr_{2}(DMSO)  $  , 0.3558 g   $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)  $  , 0.2598 g CsI, and 0.0082 g   $  PbAc_{2}  $   (molar ratio = 1:1:2:0.015) in DMF and DMSO (4:1 v/v). The resulting perovskite precursor solution was spun at 1000 rpm for 10 s, followed by 4000 rpm for 40 s. Afterward, the film was annealed at 35 °C for 6 min, 120 °C for 10 min, and 180 °C for 4 min to obtain the perovskite layer. Similarly, the as-prepared B-CDs solutions with different concentrations were deposited onto the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film at 3000 rpm for 30 s followed by thermal annealing at 85 °C for 10 min to form the   $  TiO_{2}/perovskite/B-CDs  $   structure. The hole-transport layer was prepared by spin-coating Spiro-OMeTAD solution (90 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) doped with 36 μL t-BP and 22 μL Li-TFSI (520 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) solution in acetonitrile at 5000 rpm for 30 s onto   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films to produce a   $  TiO_{2}/perovskite/B-CDs/Spiro-OMeTAD  $   architecture. Finally, an 80 nm Au electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation through a shadow mask to form a device active area of 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  .

Characterization: The transmission electron microscope images of B-CDs were taken on a JEM-2100F at an accelerating voltage of 200 kV; the samples were prepared by drop-casting diluted B-CDs solutions onto 200-mesh carbon-coated copper grids. XRD patterns of perovskite films were acquired on a XRD-2700BH diffractometer. The absorption spectra of perovskite films were measured using a UV-vis NIR spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer, Lambda 950). PL (excitation at 510 nm) spectra were measured using a FLS980 spectrometer (Edinburgh Instruments Ltd). SEM images of perovskite films were obtained by field-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM, HITACHI, SU-8020). Contact-angle pictures of perovskite films were obtained on an Optical Contact Angle Measuring Device (OCAME, DataPhysics, OCA 20). XPS and UPS of the perovskite films were carried out using a photoelectron spectrometer (ESCALAB 250Xi, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The J-V curves of the inorganic PSCs were measured using a Keithley 2400 source under AM 1.5G illumination at 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  . The devices were tested from 2 to -0.1 V at a rate of 10 mV s  $  ^{-1}  $  . The external quantum efficiencies (EQEs) of the PSCs were recorded using a QTest Station 2000ADI system (Crowntech, Inc.). EIS analysis was performed on a Zahner Electrochemical Workstation.
