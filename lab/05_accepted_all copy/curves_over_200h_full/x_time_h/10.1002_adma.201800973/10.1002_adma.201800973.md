# Divalent Anionic Doping in Perovskite Solar Cells for Enhanced Chemical Stability

Jue Gong, Mengjin Yang, Dominic Rebollar, Jordan Rucinski, Zachary Liveris, Kai Zhu,  $  ^{*}  $   and Tao Xu  $  ^{*}  $

The chemical stabilities of hybrid perovskite materials demand further improvement toward long-term and large-scale photovoltaic applications. Herein, the enhanced chemical stability of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   is reported by doping the divalent anion   $  Se^{2-}  $   in the form of PbSe in precursor solutions to enhance the hydrogen-bonding-like interactions between the organic cations and the inorganic framework. As a result, in 100% humidity at 40 °C, the 10% w/w PbSe-doped   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films exhibited >140-fold stability improvement over pristine   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films. As the PbSe-doped   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   films maintained the perovskite structure, a top efficiency of 10.4% with 70% retention after 700 h aging in ambient air is achieved with an unencapsulated 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -based cell. As a bonus, the incorporated   $  Se^{2-}  $   also effectively suppresses iodine diffusion, leading to enhanced chemical stability of the silver electrodes.

Organic–inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskites (ABX $ _3 $ , A = CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3^+ $ , (NH $ _2 $ ) $ _2 $ CH $ ^+ $ , B = Pb $ ^{2+} $ , Sn $ ^{2+} $ , and X = Cl $ ^- $ , Br $ ^- $ , I $ ^- $ ) emerges as the most focused photovoltaic (PV) material, $ ^{[1,2]} $  in which methylammonium lead iodide, (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  or MAPbI $ _3 $ ) represents the best performer in terms of efficiency, $ ^{[3,4]} $  light absorber (1.5 × 10 $ ^4 $  cm $ ^{-1} $  at 550 nm), $ ^{[5,6]} $  charge generation, $ ^{[7]} $  carrier diffusion lengths, $ ^{[8,9]} $  and lifetimes. $ ^{[8,10]} $  Despite such prominences, it is still battling against several inherent problems, including lead toxicity, low chemical/device stabilities in moisture, current–voltage hysteresis, and migration of iodine that plagues device electrodes. $ ^{[11-16]} $

According to previous studies, the first step of structural degradation due to moisture involves the formation of   $  MA_{4}PbI_{6}\cdot2H_{2}O  $   intermediate.  $  ^{[16]}  $   To tackle this problem, modifications of chemical composition in the active perovskite layer has been practiced through additives of new chemical

higher covalency, thus favoring charge generation and hole transport.  $  ^{[22]}  $   On the other hand, weakly electronegative anions lead to weak attractions, deteriorating chemical stability. This is evident through Br  $  ^{-}  $   (i.e., MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Br  $  _{x}  $  ), which aids the stability of the perovskite structure, but high doping levels of Br  $  ^{-}  $   enlarges the band gap. MAPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br has an optical bandgap of 1.8 eV, while MAPbIBr  $  _{2}  $   has an optical bandgap of   $  \approx  $  2.0 eV.  $  ^{[23-25]}  $   Because the organic cations are pinned in the inorganic framework by electrostatic interaction,  $  ^{[26]}  $   introducing a higher valence anionic charge should greatly increase such electrostatic interaction so as to stabilize CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}^{+}  $   from hydration. This inspires us to focus on multivalent ion doping such as S  $  ^{2-}  $   and Se  $  ^{2-}  $ , both of which form chemical bonds with Pb  $  ^{2+}  $   with high covalency. Plus, band gaps of PbS (0.37 eV) and PbSe (0.28 eV) are even smaller than MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ .  $  ^{[27]}  $   As the difference in electronegativity (  $  \Delta\chi  $  ) between Pb and Se is even slightly smaller than that for Pb and I (  $  \Delta\chi  $   for Pb–Se = 0.68, and   $  \Delta\chi  $   for Pb–I = 0.79, on the Pauling Scale), doping PbSe is expected to have a minimized negative impact on the desired covalent nature of the inorganic framework in hybrid perovskite.

Figure 1 shows a hypothesized lattice structure of PbSe-doped  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and illustrates the enhanced electrostatic interaction between  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3^+ $  and the inorganic framework contributed by  $ \mathrm{Se}^{2-} $ . Moreover, we found that the addition of  $ \mathrm{Se}^{2-} $  in the perovskite lattice mitigates the migration of iodine, thus suppressing the deterioration of silver electrode due to formation of AgI.

The detailed methods of thin film synthesis are given in Supporting Information (SI). To rule out the physical effects of thin film surface morphology on their chemical stabilities, scanning electron microscopy was first performed on both MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  and 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  thin films (Figure S1a,b, Supporting Information), where high surface coverages of perovskite grains were observed on both thin films. It can therefore attribute any difference in thin film properties to the intrinsic chemical natures of the samples. Also, energy-dispersive X-ray analysis (Figure S1c, Supporting Information) confirm the nearly stoichiometric existence of Se, Pb, and I in 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  film sample. Table S1 in the Supporting Information summarizes the statistics of elemental analysis.

The detailed method is in SI, with depiction of moisture aging chamber in Figure S2. The doped film exhibited strengthened chemical stability over 140-folds with respect to pristine MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  film (defined as the aging time for a detectable degradation of doped MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  thin film/time for complete degradation of pristine MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  thin film, thereby 72 h/0.5 h = 144). 5% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  was also tested to verify this trend in stability and to observe if there was a concentration dependence. As shown in Figure S3b in the Supporting Information, 5% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  thin film still exhibits much better stability than the pristine MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  thin film, although it is slightly overperformed by 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ .

Due to greater electronegativity, divalent   $  S^{2-}  $   in the form of PbS, was also considered to compare with PbSe. Figure S3c in the Supporting Information displays the evolution of reflectance spectra of 10% w/w PbS:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   thin film after aging in 100% RH for various time. Within expectation, time-dependent reflectance spectra are highly overlapped across 196 h of accelerated aging, and onset area between 800 and 850 nm is still preserved after 196 h of aging, thereby indicating 10% w/w PbS:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   thin film also had enhanced chemical stability.

Tauc plots (Figure S4a,b, Supporting Information) display the evolutions of bandgaps of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  thin film and 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  thin film at various aging time. Over a longer aging period, (0–120 h) as compared to MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  film (0–30 min), 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  film demonstrates a small widening of bandgap from 1.54 to 1.56 eV, while bandgap in pristine MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  film was widened from 1.54 to 2.31 eV. Both 5% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  and 10% w/w PbS:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  thin films also exhibited negligible bandgap change after long-time aging (Figure S4c,d, Supporting Information).

X-ray diffraction (XRD) studies on PbSe, MAPbI $ _3 $ , 5% w/w PbSe:MAPbI $ _3 $  and 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI $ _3 $  samples were collected in Figure 2c. PbSe (red) exhibited three strong diffraction peaks found at 25°, 30°, and 42° of 2θ $ ^{[33]} $ ; these peaks are absent in 5% w/w PbSe:MAPbI $ _3 $  (blue) as well as 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI $ _3 $  (purple), proving that single PbSe phases did not exist in either PbSe:MAPbI $ _3 $  samples. By closely examining MAPbI $ _3 $  diffraction pattern (black), it has distinctive (110), (220), (310), (224), and (314) structures at 14.9°, 29.2°, 32.6°, 41.3°, and 43.9° of 2θ angles, agreeing well with the patterns reported elsewhere, $ ^{[3,34,35]} $  while both 5 and 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI $ _3 $  samples exhibit similar patterns to those of MAPbI $ _3 $ , with change in the relative intensities of diffraction peaks. This fact demonstrates that the perovskite natures of PbSe:MAPbI $ _3 $  materials were not structurally altered due to the PbSe dopant, but detailed preferred orientation of crystallographic planes was

indeed influenced by dopant. In fact, as PbSe dopant increased in concentration, full widths at half maxima of (110) and (112) planes (with large lattice spacings) monotonically decreased, indicating improved crystallinities at long-range order. However, at small lattice spacings, high concentration 10% w/w PbSe dopant results in reduced crystallinities of (220) and (310) planes, thereby signifying disordered short-range structures (Figure S5, Supporting Information). Visually guided by dashed lines in Figure 2c, diffraction peaks of 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  shift towards smaller 2θ angles for (110), (112), (220), and (310) planes in comparison with MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ , and therefore indicates enlarged lattice constants as caused by intercalation of dopant atoms into the perovskite lattice. Such observation is in accordance with previous reports,  $  ^{[24,36]}  $  where increased content of I  $  ^{-}  $  in MAPbI  $  _{3-x}  $  Br  $  _{x}  $  and insertion of water molecules in MAPbBr  $  _{3}  $  systems result in diffraction peaks shifted to smaller 2θ angles. Although Se  $  ^{2-}  $  (r = 198 pm) has an ionic radius slightly smaller than I  $  ^{-}  $  (r = 220 pm),  $  ^{[37-40]}  $  it is still perfectly reasonable to result in an expanded perovskite lattice due to interstitial intercalation (instead of X-site lattice points), and interstitial intercalation should lead to inefficient atomic packing, as in the cases of small alkali metal ion (Rb  $  ^{+}  $ , Li  $  ^{+}  $ , etc.) doped perovskite structures.  $  ^{[41,42]}  $  These facts justify the illustration of Se  $  ^{2-}  $  intercalataion scenario shown in Figure 1. It can herein be inferred that the origin of enhanced chemical stability of 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  should lie in lattice-incorporated Se  $  ^{2-}  $ . Supportively, such shifts toward smaller 2θ also occur in 10% w/w PbS:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ , as shown in Figure S6 in the Supporting Information. Electrically, the incorporated Se  $  ^{2-}  $  and S  $  ^{2-}  $  ions lead to notably enhanced conductivities of thin films in comparison with pristine MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  (Figure S7, Supporting Information), which is in good agreement with previous observations of increased conductivities due to ion intercalations.  $  ^{[41,43,44]}  $

To fundamentally probe electrostatic interactions between   $  Se^{2-}  $   and cationic   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}  $  ,   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  , and 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  , thin films were characterized by Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR). Figure 2d illustrates normalized FTIR spectra of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (black) and 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (red) thin films, with peak around 3201 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   corresponding to N–H stretch.  $  ^{[45-47]}  $   N–H stretching modes at 3201 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   redshifts to 3114 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   in 10% w/w PbSe:CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $   films due to the electrostatic attraction between Se  $  ^{2-}  $   and cationic CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}^{+}  $  . Our IR study follows the observation that Se interacts with H in N–H and retard stretching modes of N–H.  $  ^{[48]}  $   Also, significant band broadening is witnessed on N–H stretch in 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  , relative to the peak of N–H stretch in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  , matching the pattern of strong electrostatic interaction.  $  ^{[49]}  $   Apart from the redshift of N–H stretching frequency, no obvious change of N–H bending frequency was observed, likely due to the poor alignment of 2D N–H symmetric bending (scissor mode)  $  ^{[45,46]}  $   with the vector of H–Se  $  ^{2-}  $   interaction.

This poor PV performance suggests that  $ S^{2-} $ -profoundly hinders charge transport in doped perovskite materials, likely due to its greater electronegativity. Indeed,

photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence studies (Figure S10, Supporting Information) show that 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  thin film has a surprisingly long carrier lifetime (544.5 ns), in contrast to 10% w/w PbS:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  thin film that has much shorter carrier lifetime (60.2 ns) as compared to the carrier lifetime of pristine MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  thin film (111 ns), and thus signifying more recombination centers as introduced by PbS dopant. The order in carrier lifetime magnitude closely correlates with the electronegativity order (S>I>Se). These results clearly indicate much enhanced chemical stability of 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ . Atomically, Se $ ^{2-} $  poses strong electrostatic interaction with MA $ ^+ $  in perovskite materials, suppressing librational motions of MA $ ^+ $ , reducing iodine migration. $ ^{[11,54]} $  As such, PbSe dopant effectively aid in suppressing iodine diffusion and contributes to stable solar cell performance. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) studies show the absence of iodine on the Ag electrode for 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI $ _3 $ -based sample in contrast to the presence of I on the Ag electrode for MAPbI $ _3 $ -based sample (Figure 3g, h). By examining the areas other than Ag electrode, XPS results further reveal a decrease in binding energies of Pb 4f electrons under higher doping concentrations of PbSe or at lower concentrations of PbS in MAPbI $ _3 $  (Figure S11a and Table S4, Supporting Information), with binding energies of I 3d electrons displaying a similar trend (Figure S11b and Table S4, Supporting Information). These results clearly demonstrate a difference in electronegativities of structurally incorporated Se $ ^{2-} $  and S $ ^{2-} $  dopant ions.

In summary, we have demonstrated that divalent chalcogenide   $  Se^{2-}  $  , in the form of PbSe doping, can mitigate low moisture stability of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   thin films. PV performance with long-term stability was achieved on 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -based solar cell device. The Ag cathode of 10% w/w PbSe:MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   solar cell was chemically preserved due to suppressed I diffusion in device after aging over a long period of time. Our work has opened a workable route to improve the chemical stability of the components in perovskite solar cells, while simultaneously achieving considerable PV performance. We will continuously seek to realize the divalent anion doping methods in much more efficient devices through optimizing the processing conditions, doping concentrations, and material compositions of perovskite absorbers in future studies.
