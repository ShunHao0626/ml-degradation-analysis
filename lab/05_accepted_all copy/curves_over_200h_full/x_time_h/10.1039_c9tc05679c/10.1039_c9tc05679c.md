# $ \mathrm{CoBr}_2 $ -doping-induced efficiency improvement of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  planar perovskite solar cells

Deng Wang, Wenjing Li, Zhenbo Du, Guodong Li, Weihai Sun, Jihuai Wu, Zhang Lan*

Although perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved the highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.2 % in a very short period of development, they suffer from poor stability because the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials are easily decomposed under the attacks of oxygen, moisture, heat and ultraviolet light. It is demonstrated that an all inorganic perovskite material of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   has excellent stability against moisture, oxygen and heat, but it has a little wide bandgap of 2.3 eV, which seriously hinders light absorption and leads to poor PCE. Metal ions doping is a good approach to overcome the drawback because it can effectively tune the bandgap, enhance light absorption, suppress trap states and adjust the energy level. Here, we use the   $  CoBr_{2}  $   doping route to fabricate high quality and crystallinity of   $  CoBr_{2}  $   doped   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film. As a result, a champion device with   $  CsPb_{0.998}Co_{0.002}Br_{3}  $   film yields a PCE as high as 8.57 %, much higher than that of the pristine   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   one (6.81 %). It is found that the lower trap defects and higher carrier density of the   $  CsPb_{0.998}Co_{0.002}Br_{3}  $   film are key reasons for the enhancement because they bring about better charge carrier dynamics and suppressed dark recombination.

# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted tremendous interest due to the rocketed power conversion efficiency (PCE) to the highest certified value of 25.2 % in a very short period of development.  $  ^{1,2}  $   However, the PSCs suffer from poor stability because the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials are easy decomposed under the attacks of oxygen, moisture, heat and ultraviolet light, so improving long-term stability of PSCs is the prerequisite for their practical application.  $  ^{3,4}  $   Substituting organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite materials with all inorganic ones such as   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   and   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   is an effective way to enhance stability of the PSCs.  $  ^{5,6}  $   Although   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   has a suitable bandgap of 1.73 eV, it easily degrades from perovskite phase of   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   to nonperovskite phase of   $  \delta  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   at room temperature, leading to quick attenuation of photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{7}  $   Contrarily,   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   shows excellent stability against moisture, oxygen and heat, but it has a little wide bandgap of 2.3 eV, which seriously hinders light absorption and leads to poor PCE.

Metal ions doping is a good approach to obviously improve photovoltaic performance of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs because it can effectively tune the bandgap, enhance light absorption, suppress trap states and adjust the energy level.  $  ^{8,9}  $   Tang's group reported the lattice doping of lanthanide ions to enlarge

grain size of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films and prolong carrier lifetimes, yielding a high PCE of 10.14 % and an ultrahigh open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.594 V of the   $  CsPb_{0.97}Sm_{0.03}Br_{3}  $   PSCs;  $  ^{10}  $   they also used perovskite divalent hard Lewis acids (  $  Mg^{2+}  $  ,   $  Ca^{2+}  $  ,   $  Sr^{2+}  $   and   $  Ba^{2+}  $  ) to dope   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films and achieved a champion   $  CsPb_{0.97}Sr_{0.03}Br_{3}  $   device with a PCE as high as 9.63%,  $  ^{11}  $   recently, they used M-substituted (  $  M=Ba^{2+}  $  ,   $  Ca^{2+}  $  , or   $  Ni^{2+}  $  )   $  Cu(Cr,M)O_{2}  $   nanocrystals as efficient HTMs in all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs and achieved a champion PCE as high as 10.18 %,  $  ^{12}  $   they also found precise stress control of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films could give ultrahigh voltage of 1.622 V and high efficiency of 10.71 %.  $  ^{13}  $   Guo et al. found that doping   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   with   $  SnBr_{2}  $   brought about more continuous and nonporous films, modified band structure and accelerated chare transport of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   layers, resulting in an efficiency improvement from 6.85% to 8.63% of the corresponding devices.  $  ^{14}  $

In this work, we use   $  CoBr_{2}  $   dopant to improve the quality and crystallinity of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film. As a result, obviously enhanced photovoltaic performance of the   $  CoBr_{2}  $   doped   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   planar PSCs is achieved and a champion device with   $  CsPb_{0.998}Co_{0.002}Br_{3}  $   film yields a PCE as high as 8.57 %, having 25.84 % enhancement compared with that of the pristine   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   one (6.81 %). The obviously enhancement of photovoltaic performance is mainly attributed to the lower trap defects and higher carrier density of the   $  CsPb_{0.998}Co_{0.002}Br_{3}  $   film, which further result in better charge carrier dynamics and suppressed dark recombination in the corresponding device.

# Fabrication of solar cells

ITOs were consecutively cleaned by detergent, ethanol, and UV-ozone treatment.   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs were prepared on the laser-patterned and cleaned ITOs with size of   $  1.5\times1.5~cm^{2}  $  . Firstly, the   $  TiO_{2}  $   NCs ethanol solution (6 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) was spin-coated on ITOs at 5000 rpm for 30 s and heated at 150 °C for 2 min, soon afterward spin-coated once again and heated at 150 °C for 30 min. Secondly,   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   layers were deposited on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs by a modified multistep spin-coating technique according to the reference and Scheme 1.  $  ^{16}  $   In details, 1.0 M   $  PbBr_{2}  $   DMF solution was heated at 75 °C until the solution became clear and transparent, specially,   $  CoBr_{2}  $   was dissolved in   $  PbBr_{2}  $   solution with mole ratio of 0.001/0.999, 0.002/0.998 and 0.003/0.997 versus   $  PbBr_{2}  $   for preparing   $  CsPb_{0.999}Co_{0.001}Br_{3}  $  ,   $  CsPb_{0.998}Co_{0.002}Br_{3}  $   and   $  CsPb_{0.997}Co_{0.003}Br_{3}  $   films, respectively, and the substrates with   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETLs were preheated at 75 °C in advance. # Characterization

A scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Hitachi S-4800) and an atomic force microscopy (AFM, Bruker Multimode 8) were used to observe the morphologies of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  films. An X-ray diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation ( $ \lambda = 1.5418\AA $ ) (Bruker D8 Advance) was used to detect the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns. A photoelectron spectrometer (ESCALAB 250 Xi, Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used to measure the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) spectra of the samples. An energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS, Oxford Inca) was used to characterize the elements in the samples. A UV-Vis-NIR spectrometer (Lambda 950) was used to measure UV-Vis absorption spectra. A fluorescence spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific Lumina) was used to acquire the steady-state photoluminescence (PL). # Conclusions

In conclusion, we have demonstrated that the   $  CoBr_{2}  $   doping is an effect way to obviously enhance photovoltaic performance of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   planar PSC. The best-performance planar PSC with   $  CsPb_{0.998}Co_{0.002}Br_{3}  $   film achieves a high PCE of 8.57 %, much higher than that of the pristine   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   device (6.79 %). The   $  CoBr_{2}  $   doped   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films show compact morphologies nearly without any pinholes as seen in the SEM images; moreover, the crystallinity of these films is also enhanced, especially the   $  CsPb_{0.998}Co_{0.002}Br_{3}  $   film, as found from XRD patterns. The improved quality and crystallinity bring about lower trap defects and higher carrier density of the   $  CsPb_{0.998}Co_{0.002}Br_{3}  $   film than those of the pristine   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film as revealed by the SCLC and Mott-Schottky data, which further result in better charge carrier dynamics and suppressed dark recombination as confirmed by the PL, TRPL, J-V characteristic depended on light intensity, and KPFM data. Therefore,   $  CoBr_{2}  $   doping is a feasible way for preparing efficient   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   planar PSCs.
