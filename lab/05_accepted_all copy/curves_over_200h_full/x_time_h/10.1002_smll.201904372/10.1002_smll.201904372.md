# Goethite Quantum Dots as Multifunctional Additives for Highly Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Hui Chen, Qiang Luo, Tao Liu, Jing Ren, Shuang Li, Meiqian Tai, Hong Lin, Hongcai He, Jinshu Wang, and Ning Wang*

Minimization of defects and ion migration in organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite films is desirable for obtaining photovoltaic devices with high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term stability. However, achieving this target is still a challenge due to the lack of efficient multifunctional passivators. Herein, to address this issue, n-type goethite (FeOOH) quantum dots (QDs) are introduced into the perovskite light-absorption layer for achieving efficient and stable perovskite solar cells (PSCs). It is found that the iron, oxygen, and hydroxyl of FeOOH QDs can interact with iodine, lead, and methylamine, respectively. As a result, the crystallization kinetics process can be retarded, thereby resulting in high quality perovskite films with large grain size. Meanwhile, the trap states of perovskite can be effectively passivated via interaction with the under-coordinated metal (Pb) cations, halide (I) anions on the perovskite crystal surface. Consequently, the PSCs with FeOOH QDs achieve a high efficiency close to 20% with negligible hysteresis. Most strikingly, the long-term stability of PSCs is significantly enhanced. Furthermore, compared with the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  -based device, a higher PCE of 21.0% is achieved for the device assembled with a   $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.81}MA_{0.14}PbBr_{0.45}I_{2.55}  $   perovskite layer.

Herein, taking inspiration from goethite (FeOOH) that can work as adsorbent for various toxic cations and anions due to its beneficial roles either as a Lewis acid or Lewis base, we introduce, for the first time, FeOOH quantum dots (QDs) with n-type semiconductor property into   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   active layer as a highly efficient trap state passivator for PSCs in this work. FeOOH QDs within the perovskite layer play two key functions in improving the film quality and optoelectronic properties. First, a certain content of FeOOH QDs can reduce the intrinsic defect density of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   by increasing the grain size and simultaneously suppress the ionic migration by interacting with the undercoordinated lead cations, organic cations, and halide anions. Second, FeOOH QDs can improve the electron mobility of perovskite and thus facilitate electron extraction and transportation. As a result, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs with the reduced defect density is increased from 17.2% to as high as 19.7% with a negligible hysteresis. Remarkably, the efficiency degradation of the unsealed device is less than 3% at   $  10 \pm 5\%  $   relative humidity (RH) condition for 60 d storage. In addition, more than 92% of the initial efficiency is retained under continuous full-sun illumination for 480 h, indicating the improved operational stability. Especially when PSCs with FeOOH QDs were kept at 85 °C for 360 h, 94% of initial efficiency was maintained, which was significantly superior to the control device. Furthermore, the same strategy was also applied to the device based on   $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.81}MA_{0.14}PbBr_{0.45}I_{2.55}  $   perovskite active layer. The results showed that the efficiency was increased from 19.1% to 21.0% and the operational stability was also enhanced.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The FeOOH QDs were obtained via a simple and low-cost method, in which the hydrolysis and dehydration of   $  FeCl_{3}\cdot6H_{2}O  $   in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) occurred in the presence of ammonium bicarbonate (  $  NH_{4}HCO_{3}  $  ) at room temperature. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images, as shown in Figure 1a, reveal that FeOOH QDs have uniform particle size distribution (  $  \approx3  $   nm). The high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) image (inset in Figure 1a) clearly shows the fringe spacing of about 0.16 nm, which corresponds to the (002) plane of goethite phase. The selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern presents only two broad and diffused rings, which can be indexed to the diffraction of (021) and (002) planes of goethite, indicating the polycrystalline structure and low

crystallinity of FeOOH QDs. The crystal structure of FeOOH QDs is further confirmed by X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern (Figure 1b), which shows only two wide and low-intensity diffraction peaks at 34.7° and 63.4°, which is indexed to the (021) and (002) planes of goethite (JCPDS 29-0713), respectively. As shown in Figure 1c, the full scan X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) of FeOOH QDs identifies the coexistence of Fe and O elements in the sample.

# 2.1. Photovoltaic Performance

FeOOH QDs was subsequently introduced into perovskite precursor solution for the growth control of perovskite crystal and defect passivation of the perovskite film. The device has a typical mesoporous architecture consisting of FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $ /mp-TiO $ _2 $ /perovskite+FeOOH QDs/spiro-OMeTAD/Au (Figure 1d). The optimized photovoltaic performances for the CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ -based devices with various FeOOH QDs concentrations are shown in Figure 2a and Table S1 (Supporting Information). PCEs of PSCs were increased first, and then dropped with the increase of the FeOOH QDs concentrations. The best cell performance could be achieved at a concentration of 0.1 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ , which was selected for in-depth studies in the following section. Figure 2b displays the photocurrent current density-voltage ( $ J-V $ ) curves of the best performing devices with and without FeOOH QDs under AM 1.5G illumination with forward and reverse scans. The corresponding detailed photovoltaic performance parameters are summarized in Table 1. FeOOH QD additives not only improved the short-circuit current density ( $ J_{sc} $ ) and open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{oc} $ ) of the PSCs, but also helped to eliminate the photocurrent hysteresis. As a result, the champion PSC with FeOOH QDs achieved a PCE of 19.7% with  $ J_{sc} $  of 23.95 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ ,  $ V_{oc} $  of 1.06 V, fill factor (FF) of 77.5% under reverse scan, and forward scan PCE of 19.3% with  $ V_{oc} $  of 1.05 V,  $ J_{sc} $  of 23.87 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and FF of 77%, which are superior to the photovoltaic performance of the reference device without FeOOH QDs.

To further verify the reproducibility of the performance, 50 PSCs were fabricated and tested under AM 1.5G illumination; the statistical distribution of the device parameters is displayed in Figure 2c and Figure S1 (Supporting Information). It is clear that the devices based on the FeOOH QDs show good reproducibility and high performance compared with the control devices. The devices based on the FeOOH QDs obtained an average  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  of 1.06 V, a  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  of 23.75 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and an FF of 77.1% (Table S1, Supporting Information), resulting in an average PCE of 19.1%. For control devices, the average PCE is 16.3%, with  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  of 1.03 V,  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  of 22.54 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and FF of 71%. Meanwhile, to ensure the reliability of the  $  J-V  $  measurement, the steady-state photocurrent and efficiency of the PSCs were recorded at the maximum power point under AM 1.5G illumination, as shown in Figure 2d. The PSCs with FeOOH QDs exhibited a PCE of 19.4% with a steady-state  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  of 21.58 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , whereas the reference device achieved a steady-state  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  of 19.36 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  and a steady-state efficiency of 16.6%. Figure 3a demonstrates the critical roles of FeOOH QDs in regulating the grain size and defect density of perovskite. The quick interactions between the introduced FeOOH QDs and PbI $ _2 $  (or MAI) effectively prevent the chemical reaction between PbI $ _2 $  and MAI, and thus the crystallization kinetics is retarded, thereby resulting in high-quality perovskite films with large grain size. Moreover, the incorporated FeOOH QDs can effectively passivate the trap states of perovskite via interacting with the undercoordinated metal (Pb) cations, organic (MA) cation, and halide (I) anions on the perovskite crystal surface. To evaluate the interaction between FeOOH and perovskite precursor (PbI $ _2 $  and MAI), the interaction energies of PbI $ _2 $ -FeOOH and MAI-FeOOH have been calculated by density functional theory, as shown in Figure 3b–d. The interaction energy of Pb-O, Fe-I, and MAI-FeOOH was calculated to be  $ -40.53 $  kcal mol $ ^{-1} $  ( $ -1.76 $  eV),  $ -61.89 $  kcal mol $ ^{-1} $  ( $ -2.68 $  eV), and  $ -32.22 $  kcal mol $ ^{-1} $  ( $ -1.40 $  eV), respectively, indicating that FeOOH QDs own strong interactions with the organic cations and halogen anion with low activation energy (e.g.,  $ 0.5 $  eV for MA $ ^+ $  ions,  $ 0.1 $  eV for I $ ^- $  ions). $ ^{[41]} $  XPS measurement was carried out to investigate the iron and oxygen core levels binging energy (Figure S5, Supporting Information). # 3. Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated a facile and efficient strategy to passivate perovskite defects and enhance its crystallization by incorporating FeOOH QDs into the perovskite layer. Supported by a series of characterize techniques, such as XRD, SEM, XPS, FTIR, SCLC, and TOF-SIMS, we found that FeOOH QDs could trigger heterogeneous nucleation, passivate the trap states, and inhibit ions migration. We demonstrate that FeOOH QDs have multiple functions in achieving

high-efficiency PSCs. The oxygen in FeOOH QDs with a lone pair electron as a Lewis base could coordinate with Lewis acid of   $  Pb^{2+}  $  . Meanwhile, the -OH of FeOOH QDs could form hydrogen bonds with hydrogen atoms in methylamine (MA) ions. The two above factors effectively prevent the chemical reaction between   $  PbI_{2}  $   and MAI, and thus retard the crystallization kinetics and result in high-quality perovskite films with large grain size. Second, the iron (or oxygen) in FeOOH QDs as a Lewis acid (or base) could interact with Lewis base (or acid)   $  I^{-}  $   (or   $  Pb^{2+}  $  ), further inducing defects passivation via interacting with the undercoordinated metal (Pb) cations, halide (I) anions at the perovskite crystal surface. More importantly, the Fe and -OH of FeOOH QDs interact with iodine and methylamine, respectively, which is beneficial to the inhibition of the methylamine and iodine ions migration. Consequently, the PSCs with FeOOH QDs have achieved a significant efficiency enhancement from 17.2% to 19.7%, with obviously decreased device hysteresis. In addition, the PSCs show excellent long-term stability, by retaining about 97% of the initial efficiency after 60 d storage in dry air, and preserving up to 92% of their initial efficiency under continuous full-sun illumination for 480 h. Most strikingly, the thermal stability of PSCs with FeOOH QDs has been significantly enhanced with 94% of initial efficiency maintained after 360 h at 85 °C. As for the device assembled with   $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.81}MA_{0.14}PbBr_{0.45}I_{2.55}  $   perovskite layer, this strategy could also improve the efficiency from 19.1% to 21.0% and enhance the operational stability with less than 3% loss of PCE under continuous light illumination for 360 h.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: All reagents were used as received without further purification. Lead(II) iodide (PbI $ _2 $ , 99.999%, Sigma-Aldrich),  $ N,N $ -dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.99%, Sigma-Aldrich), methylamine water solution (MA, 40%, Sinopharm), 2, 2',7,7'-tetrakis-( $ N,N $ -di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD, 99.99%, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.), and 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP, 99.9%, Sigma-Aldrich).

Methyl Ammonium Iodide Synthesis: Methyl ammonium iodide (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I) was synthesized through the reaction of 24 mL methylamine and 10 mL hydroiodic acid in a 100 mL round bottomed flask at 0 °C. Then the solvent was removed by a rotary evaporator at 60 °C. This crude product was redissolved into 10 mL ethanol, then 100 mL diethyl ether was slowly dropped along the bottle wall, and snow-white product was deposited. This recrystallization was repeated for three times, and the obtained precipitation was dried in a vacuum oven g at 50 °C for 24 h.

Synthesis of FeOOH QDs: FeOOH QDs were synthesized via a facile chemical reaction between   $  FeCl_{3}\cdot6H_{2}O  $   and   $  NH_{4}HCO_{3}  $   in a solution system. For the typical synthesis, 1 mmol of   $  FeCl_{3}\cdot6H_{2}O  $   and 3 mmol of   $  NH_{4}HCO_{3}  $   were dissolved in 50 mL of DMSO and mixed in the solution with magnetic stirring for 12 h. Subsequently, the products were collected by centrifugation and washed several times with distilled water, and then freeze-dried.

Device Fabrication: The solar cells were fabricated on FTO-coated glass substrates (sheet resistance   $  \approx7\ \Omega\ sq^{-1}  $  , Nippon, Tokyo, Japan). The FTO substrates were etched with zinc powders and hydrochloric acid (2 M) to obtain the required electrode patterns. The etched FTO substrates were then cleaned sequentially with detergent (2% Hellmanex in water), distilled water, ethanol, acetone, and isopropanol by ultrasonication for 20 min, respectively. They were further cleaned under UV–ozone treatment for 15 min before the film deposition. Compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was deposited on the FTO substrate by spin-coating a precursor solution of titanium isopropoxide at 2000 rpm for 30 s, followed by

The perovskite solution was spin coated in two steps, first at 1000 rpm for 10 s and then at 4000 rpm for 30 s. After the films were cooled down to room temperature, HTM was subsequently deposited on the top of the perovskite layer by spin-coating from the spiro-OMeTAD solution at 4000 rpm for 40 s. Then the spiro-OMeTAD-coated films were stored in a drying oven for 12 h. Finally, 100 nm of Au was thermally evaporated on the substrates.

The morphology of samples was characterized with field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) (Zeiss LEO-1530, Germany). The Fourier FTIR spectra were recorded in KBr by using EQUINOX 70 (Bruker, Germany) spectrometer. The Raman spectra were recorded on a LabRam HR800 (Horiba Jobin Yvon), using charge-coupled device as detector with 532 nm laser excitation. XRD patterns were achieved by X-ray diffractometer (D8 Advance, Bruker, Germany). UPS (Axis Ultra DLD) was also carried out, with the Hel (21.21 eV) emission line employed for excitation. The steady-state PL emission spectra were collected by an Edinburgh FLS 920 instrument (Livingston, WL, UK). A picosecond pulsed diode laser with excitation wavelength of 405 nm was available to record the emission decay curves for lifetimes in the range of 50–1000 ns. Ultraviolet-visible absorption spectra were recorded on a Lambda 950 spectrophotometer in the 300–800 nm wavelength range at room temperature (PerkinElmer, USA). The density-voltage (J-V) characteristics of perovskite solar cells were evaluated with a digital source meter (2400, Keithley Instruments, USA) under AM 1.5G illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ). The simulated light source was provided by a solar simulator (91192, Oriel, USA), which was calibrated by a standard silicon solar cell before measurements. A black mask with an aperture area of 0.06 cm  $  ^{2}  $   was put on the surface of devices during measurements. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy was characterized using the Zahner system (Zennium Zahner, Germany) in dark condition with a bias of 0.8 V, with the frequency ranging from 1 Hz to 1 MHz and a modulation amplitude of 10 mV. The IPCE spectra were characterized by using a QEX10 solar cell quantum efficiency measurement system (QEX10, PV Measurements, USA). Prior to the measurement, a standard silicon solar cell was used as the reference (012–2013, Pharos Technology). The TOF-SIMS measurements (Model TOF-SIMS 5, ION-TOF GmbH) were performed with the pulsed primary ions form a Cs  $  ^{+}  $   (2 keV) liquid-metal ion gun for the sputtering and a Bi  $  ^{+}  $   pulsed primary ion beam for the analysis (30 keV).
