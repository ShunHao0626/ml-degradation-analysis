# Doped Copper Phthalocyanine via an Aqueous Solution Process for Normal and Inverted Perovskite Solar Cells

Jin-Miao Wang, Zhao-Kui Wang,* Meng Li, Cong-Cong Zhang, Lu-Lu Jiang, Ke-Hao Hu, Qing-Qing Ye, and Liang-Sheng Liao*

Great efforts toward developing novel and efficient hole-transporting materials are needed to further improve the device efficiency and enhance the cell stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). The poor film conductivity and the low carrier mobility of organic small-molecule-based hole-transporting materials restrict their application in PSCs. This study develops an efficient and stable hole-transporting material, tetrafluorotetracyanoquinodimethane (  $  F_{4}  $  -TCNQ)-doped copper phthalocyanine-3,4',4'',4'''-tetra-sulfonated acid tetra sodium salt (TS-CuPc) via a solution process, in planar structure PSCs. The p-type-doped TS-CuPc film demonstrates improved film conductivity and hole mobility owing to the strong electron affinity of   $  F_{4}  $  -TCNQ. By the   $  F_{4}  $  -TCNQ tailoring, the composite film gives the highest occupied molecular orbital level as high as 5.3 eV, which is beneficial for hole extraction. In addition, the aqueous solution processed TS-CuPc:  $  F_{4}  $  -TCNQ precursor is almost neutral with good stability for avoiding the electrode erosion. As a result, the fabricated PSCs employing TS-CuPc:  $  F_{4}  $  -TCNQ as the hole-transporting material exhibit a power conversion efficiency of 16.14% in a p–i–n structure and 20.16% in an n–i–p structure, respectively. The developed organic small molecule of TS-CuPc provides the diversification of hole-transporting materials in planar PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organometal halide-based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has increased dramatically from the initial 3.9% in 2009 to current 22.1% in a short time.  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   In addition to the merits of broad and strong light absorption,  $  ^{[4,5]}  $   long charge carrier diffusion length,  $  ^{[6]}  $   bipolar transport properties,  $  ^{[7]}  $   and longer carrier lifetime  $  ^{[8]}  $   of the organometal halide perovskites, hole- and electron-transporting layers  $  ^{[9,10]}  $   also play essential role in improving the device performance of PSCs.

For example, the acidity and highly hygroscopic nature of PEDOT:PSS restrict the stability of PSCs,  $  ^{[33-35]}  $   and Spiro-OMeTAD suffers from the multistep synthetic routes, complicated purification procedure, and even in-and-out repeatability.  $  ^{[36,37]}  $   Therefore, there is an urgent motivation to develop cost-effective and highly stable HTMs for achieving high-performance PSCs.

Copper phthalocyanine (CuPc) is an excellent p-type semiconductor employed in organic light-emitting diodes by its high hole mobility, excellent thermal and chemical stability.  $  ^{[38]}  $   With considerable attention, CuPc and their derivatives have recently been utilized as a hole-transporting layer (HTL) in PSCs with long-term durability.  $  ^{[39,40]}  $   Although soluble copper phthalocyanine has already been studied and published previously, the poor solubility of CuPc restricts its application in solution-processable PSCs.  $  ^{[41,42]}  $   Copper phthalocyanine-3,4',4'',4'''-tetra-sulfonated acid tetra sodium salt (TS-CuPc) possesses good solubility in polar solvents,  $  ^{[43,44]}  $   which brings to the potential application in low-cost PSCs as an HTL. Herein, we developed a solution-processed HTM with good conductivity and stability in planar structure PSCs. According to the layer stacking sequence, planar heterojunction PSCs are divided into p–i–n (inverted) and n–i–p (normal) structures.  $  ^{[11-13]}  $   Whatever the structure may be inverted PSCs or a normal PSCs, hole-transporting materials (HTMs) undertake the responsibility of extracting and collecting the photon-generated holes from the perovskite active layer.  $  ^{[14-16]}  $   Poly(3,4-ethylene dioxythiophene):poly(4-styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS),  $  ^{[17,18]}  $   CuI,  $  ^{[19]}  $   NiO,  $  ^{[20,21]}  $   CuO  $  _{x}  $    $  ^{[22,23]}  $   and copper thiocyanate (CuSCN),  $  ^{[24]}  $   germanium dioxide (GeO  $  _{2}  $  ),  $  ^{[25]}  $   graphene oxide (GO),  $  ^{[26]}  $   and poly(triarylamine) (PTAA),  $  ^{[27]}  $   2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (SpiroOMeTAD),  $  ^{[28,29]}  $   and poly-3-hexylthiophene (P3HT),  $  ^{[30]}  $   have been employed as HTMs in p–i–n and n–i–p PSCs, respectively. In the   $  MoO_{3}  $   heavily doped systems, it was confirmed, by the transmission electron microscopy (TEM) (Figure S1, Supporting Information) and polarized optical microscopic images (Figure S2, Supporting Information), that the   $  MoO_{3}  $   molecules were inclined to form clusters by aggregation. The conductivities of doped and nondoped TS-CuPc films were measured by a four-probe method. The dopant-ratio dependent conductivities of TS-CuPc films are also plotted in Figure 2c,d. The conductivity of pristine TS-CuPc is evaluated to be   $  4.3 \times 10^{-5}  $   S cm  $  ^{-1}  $  . Once   $  F_{4}  $  -TCNQ (2.5 wt%) was doped, the conductivity increases to   $  6.2 \times 10^{-3}  $   S cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , which is higher by one order of magnitude than   $  5.6 \times 10^{-4}  $   S cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for 15 wt%   $  MoO_{3}  $   doped one. The improved hole mobility and film conductivity suggest that the hole extraction and transport would be more efficient when using these doped TS-CuPc as the HTMs in PSCs.

The electronic doping also plays a critical role in adjusting the energy levels of the organic semiconductors. For a p-type doping, efficient charge transfer occurs from the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level of a host material to the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level of the dopant, which generally induces a band bending of the host materials by the dopant tailoring. The energy bending of the doped interfacial layer is favorable for charge extraction in practical solar cells. Ultraviolet photoemission spectroscopy (UPS) spectra of the pristine TS-CuPc, TS-CuPc:MoO $ _3 $  (15 wt%), and TS-CuPc:F $ _4 $ -TCNQ (2.5 wt%) were performed as shown in Figure 2e. Upon doping MoO $ _3 $  and F $ _4 $ -TCNQ, the secondary cutoffs of the UPS spectra of TS-CuPc shifted to a lower binding energy gradually. As a consequence, the HOMO level with respect to the vacuum level of the pristine TS-CuPc, TS-CuPc:MoO $ _3 $  (15 wt%), and TS-CuPc:F $ _4 $ -TCNQ (2.5 wt%) is 5.0, 5.1, and 5.3 eV, respectively. The adjusted HOMO level of the TS-CuPc:F $ _4 $ -TCNQ film will be beneficial for hole extraction in PSCs. Figure 2f shows the Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra of the pristine TS-CuPc, TS-CuPc:MoO $ _3 $  (15 wt%), and TS-CuPc:F $ _4 $ -TCNQ (2.5 wt%) films on KBr substrates. Compared to the FTIR spectra of pristine MoO $ _3 $  and pristine F $ _4 $ -TCNQ (Figure S3, Supporting Information), two additional peaks located at 2400 and 2000 cm $ ^{-1} $  which are assigned to the stretching vibration, were observed in TS-CuPc:F $ _4 $ -TCNQ (2.5 wt%) films. This suggests that the charge transfer from TS-CuPc to F $ _4 $ -TCNQ did happen, resulting in a change of the electronic distribution and vibrational structure of the C≡N bond in TS-CuPc:F $ _4 $ -TCNQ composite films.

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, we have developed an efficient and stable hole-transporting material,   $  F_{4}  $  -TCNQ-doped TS-CuPc, to fabricate high-performance PSCs. A p-type doping was realized between TS-CuPc and   $  F_{4}  $  -TCNQ with obviously improved film conductivity and hole mobility. The doping also resulted in a band bending of TS-CuPc with a high HOMO level of 5.3 eV, which is beneficial for hole extraction in corresponding PSCs. The TS-CuPc:  $  F_{4}  $  -TCNQ composite film presented good stability by its almost neutral precursor solution, which can avoid the electrode erosion. More importantly,   $  F_{4}  $  -TCNQ-doped TS-CuPc could be employed as an HTM not only in the p-i-n structure but also in the n-i-p structure PSCs. Particularly, a champion n-i-p structure device with a PCE as high as 20.16% was obtained. The

developed organic small molecule semiconductor of TS-CuPc provides the diversification of HTMs in planar PSCs. It will stimulate the researchers to develop other organic-based HTMs for fabricating high-performance PSCs.

Then, CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _{x} $  layer was deposited at 4000 rpm for 40 s and annealed from 65 to 95 °C with a gradient of 10 °C min $ ^{-1} $  in the glovebox. The fabricated device had a structure of ITO/HTMs/CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _{x} $ /PC $ _{61} $ BM/Bphen/Ag (100 nm). Then, the samples were transferred into the glovebox to deposit the CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3} $  layer at 4000 rpm for 40 s. During the spin-coating process, the chlorobenzene was dropped acting as the antisolvent. The samples were then baked at 100 °C for 10 min. Spiro-OMeTAD was then spin coated on the CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3} $  layer with 5000 rpm 40 s $ ^{-1} $  without annealing, followed by the spin coating of TS-CuPc:F $ _{4} $ -TCNQ and/or TS-CuPc:MoO $ _{3} $  at 5000 rpm for 40 s. XRD patterns of the perovskite films were performed by a PANalytical 80 equipment (Empyrean, Cu Ka radiation). The absorption spectra of perovskite films were conducted by an UV–vis spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer Lambda 750). The work functions of different HTLs were measured by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS). The steady state PL spectra of the perovskite films on different HTLs were collected using Horiba Jobin-Yvon LabRAM HR800 with a 520 nm excitation wavelength. The water contact angles of the grid lines were measured by a PSS OCA20 optical contact-measuring system. The ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) was used to evaluate the energy levels of the doped TS-CuPc films. The transmission IR spectra were recorded from a Bruker Vertex 80V FTIR spectrometer in air.
