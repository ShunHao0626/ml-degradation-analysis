# Abstract

Two-step sequential deposition has been shown as an effective method to improve the quality of perovskite films and performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). In this work, we have developed a two-step sequential deposition method, incorporating an alkali cation (e.g.,   $  Cs^{+}  $  ) and a benzylammonium (  $  BA^{+}  $  ) cation in formamidinium (  $  FA^{+}  $  )-based perovskite thin films for highly efficient and stable PSCs. By combining experimental characterizations and theoretical calculations, we demonstrate that not only the co-incorporation of alkali cations and   $  BA^{+}  $  significantly improves crystallinity and orientational growth of perovskite films, but also   $  Cs^{+}  $  enhances passivation effect of   $  BA^{+}  $  ions on perovskite surface via strengthening bonding between   $  BA^{+}  $  and perovskites. In particular, inclusion of   $  Cs^{+}  $  in   $  BA^{+}  $  -passivated perovskites promotes the preferred orientation of defect-free (100) facets. As a result, we observed remarkable improvements in both open-circuit voltage and fill factor of formamidinium-benzylammonium-

Manipulating perovskite composition by combining small alkali and large organic cations in two-step sequential deposition provides an efficient and facile approach to realize highly efficient and stable perovskite devices.

# 1. Introduction

Organolead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted tremendous attention in both academia and industry due to their rapid rise in efficiency from 3.8%  $  ^{1}  $  to 25.2%  $  ^{2}  $  as well as their relatively simple solution-based processing. Organolead halide perovskite is a class of materials with a general chemical formula of ABX  $  _{3}  $ , where A is an organic cation (CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $   $  ^{+}  $ , MA  $  ^{+}  $  or HC(NH  $  _{2}  $ )  $  _{2}  $   $  ^{+}  $ , FA  $  ^{+}  $ ), an inorganic cation (Cs  $  ^{+}  $ ) or a mixture of both; B is a metal cation (e.g., Pb  $  ^{2+}  $ , Sn  $  ^{2+}  $  or a mixture of both); X is a halide anion (I  $  ^{-}  $ , Br  $  ^{-}  $ , Cl  $  ^{-}  $  or their mixture). Among the broad range of perovskite materials, FA-based compositional perovskites (i.e., FA  $  ^{+}  $  as the major A-site cation) have attracted a great deal of research interest because of good thermal stability and nearly ideal bandgap for photovoltaics.  $  ^{3-9}  $  To date, the anti-solvent method has mostly been used to produce high-quality FA-based compositional perovskites.  $  ^{10-16}  $  However, the reproducibility and scalability of the anti-solvent method is yet to be validated.  $  ^{17}  $  By contrast, two-step sequential deposition method  $  ^{18-21}  $  involving a first step of depositing PbI  $  _{2}  $  on the substrate and a second step of converting PbI  $  _{2}  $  to perovskite by treating PbI  $  _{2}  $  films in organic salt solution showed promise to achieve scalable and efficient FA-based PSCs.  $  ^{22-27}  $

In addition to the rise in efficiency, stability of PSCs has improved substantially in the past few years; nonetheless, further enhancement in long-term stability is still needed before

In sequential deposition process, to improve stability of FA-based PSCs,   $  CsCl^{24}  $  ,   $  CsI^{25,28}  $   and   $  CsF^{29}  $  , were used as additives in the first deposition step of   $  PbI_{2}  $   to enhance perovskite crystallization and stabilize the photoactive perovskite phase. Since the decomposition of a perovskite film usually starts from film surface and grain boundaries,  $  ^{30}  $   introducing molecular additives is another effective strategy to passivate surface defects.  $  ^{31-34}  $   For example, phenylethylammonium iodide (PEAI) was used by Jiang et al.  $  ^{35}  $   and Li et al.  $  ^{36}  $   as an additive in the FAI solution through sequential deposition. The large   $  PEA^{+}  $  cations were found on both surface and grain boundaries of the perovskite films, passivating surface defects and thus improving both device efficiency and stability.

Inspired by the aforementioned strategies, in this work we have explored the possibility of combining alkali cation and molecular surface passivation in a sequential deposition process to achieve highly efficient and stable PSCs. We investigated the effect of alkali cation and benzylammonium (BA $  ^{+}  $ ) cation on quality of perovskite films and corresponding solar cell devices. Our results highlighted the synergistic role of alkali cation and BA $  ^{+}  $  in fabricating high-quality perovskite films. The alkali cation enlarged crystalline grain size and enhanced BA $  ^{+}  $ -passivation effect with increased surface binding strength between FA-based 3D perovskite and BA $  ^{+}  $ . In particular, the co-incorporation of alkali cation and BA $  ^{+}  $  enhanced orientated growth of (1 0 0) facet, and weakened growth along (1 1 1) facet which could induce deep trap states in the PbI $  _{2}  $ -terminated surface according to the DFT calculations. The resulted perovskite showed remarkably improved optoelectronic properties, and the best performance of formamidinium-benzylammonium-cesium (FABACs)-based solar cells reached a champion efficiency of 22.5%. To understand the significantly enlarged crystal grains of FABACs perovskite compared with those of FABA perovskite, we further investigated the role of   $  Cs^{+}  $  or   $  Rb^{+}  $  in the sequential deposition process. The XRD patterns of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   films with and without inclusion of CsI or RbI during deposition were examined. As shown in Fig. S2, the   $  PbI_{2}  $   peak (12.7°) intensity is reduced as the CsI or RbI molar ratio in the   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution increases. This is a result of reactions between   $  PbI_{2}  $   and CsI/RbI. Indeed, the additional peaks   $  \sim10^{\circ}  $   emerged and can be attributed to the yellow-phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   or   $  RbPbI_{3}  $  .  $  ^{38,39}  $   We speculate that the yellow phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   or   $  RbPbI_{3}  $   generated in the first deposition step may act as a growth template or an intermedia phase to modulate the diffusion and intercalation of organic cations and halide anions into the layered   $  PbI_{2}  $ , which might facilitate the crystallization process and guide the orientational growth of the perovskite films due to the variations in interfacial energies.  $  ^{25}  $   We also examined how incorporation of   $  Cs^{+}  $  and   $  BA^{+}  $  may affect the perovskite lattice. In FABA, we observe a negligible shift of the main diffraction peak (Fig. S3), indicating little   $  BA^{+}  $  incorporation in perovskite lattice, i.e., the   $  BA^{+}  $  ions mainly bind to the perovskite lattice as surface passivation molecules. Upon addition of a small amount (5%) of   $  Cs^{+}  $ , a small shift of the diffraction peak to a higher degree can be seen, which is associated with lattice contraction due to incorporation of the small-size   $  Cs^{+}  $  ions. When the   $  Cs^{+}  $  ratio is increased to 20%, a   $  BA_{2}PbI_{4}  $   phase forms (Fig. S4a), and the peak shift might be induced by the formation of the quasi-2D perovskite phase   $  {}^{40}  $ .

To understand the consequences of the change in the preferred crystallographic facets, we calculated density of states (DOSs) of the three relevant crystallographic facets, using DFT calculations. Structural models and DOSs are shown in Fig. S8 and Fig. S9, respectively. We observe deep trap states in (1 1 1) surface (with  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ -termination), while no trap states are found in the (1 0 0) and (2 1 0) surfaces. The trap states found at (1 1 1) surface are caused by under-coordinated Pb-I bonds. Fig. S10 highlights such bonds, where one I only forms one Pb-I bond, whereas two Pb-I bonds are typically formed in bulk and other surfaces of perovskites. To show this more clearly, we plotted the PDOS of these Pb and I atoms to highlight the origin of the defect states (see Fig. S11). This indicates the (1 0 0) orientation, promoted by the incorporation of  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $  in FABA perovskite, is responsible for the improved film quality, via mitigating the undesired trap states of the (1 1 1) surfaces in the FA or FABA perovskites.

Since the   $  V_{oc}  $   of a solar cell is related to the carrier lifetime,  $  ^{50}  $   we further performed TPV characterization of FA, FABA and FABACs-based devices to probe the charge recombination dynamics.  $  ^{50-52}  $   Fig. 4a plots the light intensity dependence of   $  V_{oc}  $ , where the FABA- and FABACs-based devices both show a higher   $  V_{oc}  $   than that of the FA-based device under each light intensity, in agreement with the increased   $  V_{oc}  $   values presented in Table 1. The ideality factor (  $  n_{id}  $  ) of the perovskite solar cells, which is associated with the charge recombination mechanisms (i.e., free carrier recombination vs trap-assisted recombination (Shockley–Read–Hall (SRH) recombination)),  $  ^{53}  $   was also estimated from the slopes of fitting curves in Fig. 4a. Generally, an   $  n_{id}  $   equal to 1 represents direct band-to-band free carrier recombination (ideal case), and a larger   $  n_{id}  $   (normally in the range of 1~2) implies presence of SRH recombination via mid-gap or deep traps.  $  ^{54}  $   The neat FA-based device shows   $  n_{id}  $   of 1.7, suggesting a predominant SRH recombination process. On the other hand, a lower   $  n_{id}  $   value of 1.5 is achieved after   $  Cs^{+}  $  and   $  BA^{+}  $  co-incorporation, implying reduction of SRH recombination in the modified films. To conclude, we have demonstrated a two-step sequential deposition procedure to incorporate alkali and large cations in FA-based perovskite thin films. The co-incorporation of the alkali (Cs $  ^{+}  $  or Rb $  ^{+}  $ ) and BA $  ^{+}  $  cations results in high-quality perovskite films with large and highly oriented grains. In particular, our combined experimental and DFT studies reveal that inclusion of Cs $  ^{+}  $  in FABA perovskite promotes the formation of desired defect-free (1 0 0) facet and suppresses the growth of (1 1 1) facet with deep trap states. In addition, the presence of Cs $  ^{+}  $  ion also enhances the passivation effect of BA $  ^{+}  $  on the FA-based perovskite surfaces via strengthening bonding between BA $  ^{+}  $  and perovskites. Thanks to the synergistic effect of the Cs $  ^{+}  $  and BA $  ^{+}  $  cations, the FABACs perovskites exhibit excellent optoelectronic properties with remarkably reduced charge recombination and enhanced charge transport. By carefully optimizing the concentration of BA $  ^{+}  $  and Cs $  ^{+}  $ , we obtain champion device with PCE reaching 22.5%, which is among the top efficiency for this type solar cells. Importantly, the FABACs perovskite films show superior stability against photo-thermal stress and moisture. The resulting devices remained more than 95% of the initial efficiency after exposure to air (humidity of 60-65%) for 2 months without encapsulation. This study provides a well efficient method to fabricate highly efficient and stable perovskite devices by a two-step sequential deposition.

Interface Formation Energy The alkali cation partially substitutes A-site  $ \mathrm{FA^{+}} $  inside the 3D framework and  $ \mathrm{BA^{+}} $  replaces A-site  $ \mathrm{FA^{+}} $  on top surface of the 3D perovskite lattice. The following formula was used to calculate interface formation energy:

$$
\Delta E = \frac {E _ {B A F A} - E _ {B A} - E _ {F A}}{S}
$$

where S is the area of the interface, and   $  E_{BAFA}  $  ,   $  E_{BA}  $   and   $  E_{FA}  $   are the total energies of the BA  $  ^{+}  $  -passivated perovskites, the BA  $  ^{+}  $   layer and the component of perovskites without BA  $  ^{+}  $   passivation, respectively. The more negative the   $  \Delta E  $  , the stronger the bonding strength of the BA  $  ^{+}  $   layer and components in the BA  $  ^{+}  $  -passivated perovskite.
