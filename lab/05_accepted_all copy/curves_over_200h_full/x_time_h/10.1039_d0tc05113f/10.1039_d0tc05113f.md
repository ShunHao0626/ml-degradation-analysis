# Abstract

Researching planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells has become increasingly popular in view of pursuing both a better photovoltaic performance and enhanced environmental stability. Recently, the use of integrated hole transport layers (HTLs) has attracted much attention because of the improvements in power conversion efficiencies (PCEs). However, previous researches usually focused on the HTLs in regular-type devices, which do not affect the formation of perovskite crystal. Here, we developed the HTL for inverted solar cells, which integrated organic poly[4,8-bis[(2-ethylhexyl)oxy]benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']dithiophene-2,6-diyl][3-fluoro-2-[(2-ethylhexyl)carbonyl]thieno[3,4-b]thiophenediyl] (PTB7) with poly (3, 4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly (styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS). PTB7 is used to tune the energy alignment and hydrophobicity of PEDOT:PSS to improve the hole transportation and perovskite formation. The integrated HTL (PEDOT:PSS/PTB7) demonstrated an enhanced hole extraction performance in perovskite solar cells due to better matched energy alignment. Furthermore, the hydrophobic nature of PTB7 provides a non-wetting surface that is beneficial to the crystal formation of perovskite, resulting in higher quality perovskite crystals. Using this simple process, the developed group exhibited a PCE of  $ 18.43\% $ , which was  $ 16.5\% $  higher than that of the reference devices  $ (15.82\%) $ . In addition, due to the moisture-blocking characteristics of PTB7, the long-term stability of the devices was also obviously enhanced under ambient air condition  $ (25^{\circ}\mathrm{C}) $  without encapsulation.

# 1. Introduction

In recent years, there has been an ever-increasing interest in organic–inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) due to their significant advantages, which include low production cost and simple solution-processability, as well as their light weight and high power-conversion efficiency (PCE).  $  ^{1-5}  $   Within just a few years of development, the PCE of perovskite-based devices has exceeded a certified 25.2%, reaching a level similar to that of silicon-based solar cells.  $  ^{6-12}  $   In addition, their long-term stability and mass production efficiency have been obviously improved, which has increased the possibility of future commercialization.  $  ^{13-15}  $

Generally, planar-configured PSCs are divided into regular and inverted structures based on the stacking sequence of each functional layer.  $  ^{16, 17}  $   The inverted-type devices exhibit slightly lower PCEs than the regular ones despite lower hysteresis in current-voltage characteristics.  $  ^{18, 19}  $   Thus, it is important to improve the PCEs of inverted devices to the levels similar to or higher than those of the regular devices.

The most widely used hole transport layer (HTL) in the inverted device is poly (3, 4-ethylenedioxythiophene): poly (styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS), which is one of the important causes for the PCEs lower than those of regular devices; its use results in a comparatively low open circuit voltage, which may be due to the undesirable energy position of the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO).  $  ^{20}  $   Moreover, PEDOT:PSS is liable to absorb moisture in ambient temperatures due to its hydrophilicity, which facilitates the degradation of perovskite-based devices.  $  ^{21}  $   Many efforts have been paid to replace PEDOT:PSS with other materials; Bi et al. compared different types of HTL materials such as polyvinyl alcohol, PEDOT:PSS, poly(bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine) (PTAA), N4,N4'-bis(4-(6-((3-ethyloxetan-3-yl)methoxy)hexyl)phenyl)-N4,N4'-diphenylbiphenyl-4,4'-diamine, and N-9'-heptadecanyl-2,7-carbazole-alt-5,5-(4',7'-di-2-thienyl-2',1',3'-benzothiadiazole) and obtained the highest PCE of 18.3% using the PTAA as the HTL.  $  ^{22}  $   However, organic semiconductors such as PTAA are usually expensive and require additional impurity doping when used as a high conductivity HTL.  $  ^{22}  $   In

On the other hand, integrating a small amount of new material into a conventional HTL can effectively improve the HTL in a cost-effective manner, without changing conventional fabrication processes. There have been many studies on integrated HTLs in regular PSCs; Kegelmann and coworkers mixed the 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) and PEDOT:PSS to fabricate a double HTL, obtaining the champion PCE of 18.7% with the environmental stability enhanced.  $  ^{16}  $   Xu and coworkers obtained an improvement in PCE from 16.8% to 22.0% using an inexpensive inorganic-organic double HTL; they inserted an ultrathin dithiophene-benzene between CuSCN and Au and argued that this method could also obviously enhance the environmental stability.  $  ^{23}  $   In contrast, integrated HTLs in inverted devices have been seldom studied. Because the HTLs in inverted devices may affect the formation of perovskite crystal, which is closely related to PCE, unlike regular devices, the study of the HTLs for inverted devices is significantly different from that for regular devices.

In this work, we introduce an organic p-type polymer of poly[4,8-bis[(2-ethylhexyl)oxy]benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']dithiophene-2,6-diyl][3-fluoro-2-[(2-ethylhexyl)carbonyl]thieno[3,4-b]thiophenediyl] (PTB7) as an intermediary to modify the PEDOT:PSS layer in inverted PSCs with the aim of simultaneously improving the hole extraction property of the HTL and the crystal quality of perovskite. Inserting PTB7 between the PEDOT:PSS and perovskite layers can improve energy alignment, which is beneficial for the hole-extraction in PSCs. The concentration of PTB7 precursor was carefully controlled to form a partially covered PTB7 intermediary, which can not only ensure the suitable hole extraction characteristics but also create the surface with suitable hydrophobicity on which a perovskite film is deposited. As a result, the developed samples exhibited a quality-improved perovskite crystal, as compared with the control groups. Only a small amount of PTB7 is required to improve the device performance, which is more cost-effective than a full replacement of PEDOT:PSS with an organic semiconductor. After using the PEDOT:PSS/PTB7 as the integrated HTL, the PCE of the PSCs was improved from  $ 15.82\% $  to  $ 18.43\% $ .

# 2. Results and discussion

The device structure of the PSCs is exhibited in Fig. 1a. Experimental details are summarized in the Supporting Information. As shown in Fig. 1b, PTB7 is a widely used organic semiconductor  $  ^{24}  $  with long carbon chains, which can be used to modify the interfacial properties of pristine-PEDOT:PSS. We fabricated the PSCs with an inverted p-i-n configuration of indium tine oxide (ITO)/HTL/perovskite/[6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM)/2,9-dimethyl-4,7-diphenyl-1,10-phenanthroline (BCP)/Ag, in which the HTL possessed an integrated structure of PEDOT:PSS/PTB7 (see the Experimental Section in Supporting Information). The ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) spectra presented in Fig. S1 indicated that the PTB7 possessed a HOMO of -5.15 eV. As shown in Fig. 1c, the PTB7 had a deeper HOMO than the PEDOT:PSS, with a gradient band alignment formed after inserting the PTB7 between the PEDOT:PSS HTL and the perovskite film. At the same time, adding the PTB7 intermediary can effectively suppress the charge recombination since the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of PTB7 is higher than that of PEDOT:PSS, which means it can effectively block the transportation of electrons from the perovskite layer.  $  ^{25}  $

We also carried out EIS measurement under dark condition to gain further insight into the charge extraction and transportation process of perovskite-based devices. As shown in Fig. 5e, the semicircles could be fitted into an equivalent circuit including series resistance (  $  R_{s}  $  ), charge-transfer resistance (  $  R_{ct}  $  ), and capacitor C. Here,   $  R_{s}  $   stands for the internal series resistance, which is a sum of all the serial resistances in the PSCs, and   $  R_{ct}  $   indicates the charge-transfer resistance, which presents the resistance against the process of charge transportation.  $  ^{40}  $   As demonstrated by the Nyquist plots, the sample with the PTB7 intermediary returned a   $  R_{s}  $   of 29.3   $  \Omega  $  , which was 61.7% of that of the sample without PTB7 (47.5   $  \Omega  $  ), suggesting a decreased series resistance after incorporating the PTB7. This indicates a better carrier transport performance of the PTB7-based PSCs, which will ultimately contribute to an enhanced photovoltaic performance of the devices. The long-term stabilities of the PSCs were also investigated in ambient air (humidity of ca. 30% and temperature of ca. 25°C) under 1 sun illumination without encapsulation. As shown in Fig. 5f, the degradation rate of the experimental group was retarded after incorporating the PTB7 organic semiconductor, retaining around 81% of initial the PCE after 300 h of storage. However, the control group without PTB7 only retained 52% of the initial PCE during the same period. More detailed degradation data, including   $  V_{oc}  $  ,   $  J_{sc}  $  , and FF, are summarized in Fig. S9. In view of this, the difference in the degradation rate of perovskite devices can be summarized in terms of several factors. First, the hydrophobic nature of organic PTB7 may favor the blocking of the moisture in the atmosphere, which is considered to be the critical factor in the degradation of perovskite. Second, inserting PTB7 between the PEDOT:PSS and the perovskite film helps to prevent the erosion due to the underlying acidity of PEDOT:PSS, providing a moderate substrate for light adsorption. # 3. Conclusions

In summary, we applied an organic semiconductor (PTB7) to the top of PEDOT:PSS to form an integrated HTL, which improved both the photovoltaic performance and the environmental stability. The inserted PTB7 intermediary can tune the energy alignment and is beneficial to the crystal quality of perovskite film. As a result, the PSCs with the PTB7 intermediary demonstrated an improved photovoltaic performance, returning a PCE of 18.43%, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 22.1 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.09 V, and a FF of 0.76, compared with the lower photovoltaic performance (PCE = 15.82%,   $  J_{sc}  $   = 21.7 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $   = 1.04 V, and FF = 0.69) of the perovskite devices without PTB7. Furthermore, the long-term stability under air ambient condition was clearly enhanced after incorporating the PTB7 to form an integrated HTL. As a result, the experimental group retained 81% of the pristine PCE after 300 h of storage, which was higher than that of the control group (52%). Overall, our research provides a simple and efficient way to improve the performance and environmental stability of inverted PSCs.
