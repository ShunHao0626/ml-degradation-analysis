# Abstract

Recently, mixed-cation perovskite has been extensively used for high-performance solar cells. Nevertheless, the mixed-cation perovskite based on formamidinium methylammonium lead tri-iodide (FA $ _x $ MA $ _{1-x} $ PbI $ _3 $ ) fabricated through the existing methods often suffers phase stability and much trap density. Herein, we demonstrate a facile intermediate engineering approach to improve the quality of mixed-cation perovskite based on FA $ _x $ MA $ _{1-x} $ PbI $ _3 $ . Varying concentrations of methylammonium chloride (MACl) is used to treat FA-MA-PbI $ _3 $ -solvent intermediate. It is noted that MACl has a strong impact on the crystallization kinetics, charge carrier dynamics as well as defect density of obtained perovskite. The mixed-cation perovskite treated with 20 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  MACl yields large grain size, highly uniform morphology, better crystalline stability. Subsequently, the device with acquired high-quality mixed-cation perovskite shows a high efficiency of 20.40%, which is obviously higher than that obtained from the traditional non-treated method. Moreover, the device prepared through the developed method could retain over 85% of the initial efficiency after 860 h at room temperature.

# Introduction

Within a decade, organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have advanced at an exceptional rate, which named as next-generation photovoltaic contender.  $  ^{1-4}  $   The high reported power conversion efficiency (PCE: 25.2%) is largely credited to its number of exceptional optoelectronic properties of perovskite, such as optimum absorption profile, lengthy charge diffusion, high carrier mobility, supple bandgap and flexibility to embed alien ions into its lattice structure.  $  ^{5-10}  $   All of these outstanding properties are tunable via simple compositional engineering or modification in process parameters. Generally, the organic cations, such as formamidinium (FA) and methylamine (MA) largely influence optoelectronic properties and stability of perovskite film.  $  ^{7,11}  $   Mono-cation MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   suffers limitations in terms of further PCE enhancement, lacks extended optical band-edge, and phase instability (tetragonal can turn into cubic phase under a low-phase transition temperature).  $  ^{11-12}  $   Though another mono-cation black   $  \alpha  $  -phase FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   owns an optimum energy bandgap, which is readily transformed to non-perovskite yellow   $  \delta  $  -phase in certain conditions due to large FA cation.  $  ^{11-13}  $

Till now, mixed-cation perovskite has been proved to lift PCE and boost stability than mono-cation perovskite due to the balanced stoichiometric proportions.  $  ^{14-16}  $   Conventionally, mixed-cation perovskites are synthesized by direct mixing of perovskite precursors, however, this orthodox complex composition engineering leads to phase transitional strain and a lower order crystallinity, associated with the uncontrolled cations phase nucleation altering the device function and phase instability.  $  ^{12, 17}  $   The derived defects of the phase impurity from reported orthodox approaches further encourage non-radiative recombination and low carrier mobility, which inhibits cation embedding in the lattice network and cast uncertainties on the scaling-up.  $  ^{17-20}  $

Recently, the blending of several types of additives for the perovskite solution including organic molecules, ionic liquids, polymer derivatives, halides salts, inorganic acids has been widely explored to produce high-quality   $  FA_{x}MA_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite films.  $  ^{[21]}  $   Just a few percents of such additives in perovskite precursor solution could suppress phase

Here, we explored a novel and effective intermediate engineering technique to obtain phase resilient   $  FA_{x}MA_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $  . It is noted that immature MAI-FAI-  $  PbI_{2}  $   intermediate treated with optimized MACl solution results in fine-quality mixed-cation perovskite with large grain size and low trap density. Consequently, the produced high-quality mixed-cation perovskite film shows obviously higher PCE and resilient stability than the mixed-cation perovskite synthesized from the traditionally non-treated method.

# Results and Discussion

This work evidently distinguished the methodological difference between orthodox and our introduced fabrication approach for FAMAPbI $ _3 $  based mixed-cation perovskite. The orthodox approach of preparing the FAMAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite generally relies on the depositing of the precursor solution, formation of CB induced intermediate phase, and then finally evolving the film through thermal annealing. The traditional sequential approach still lacks the state of the art film quality and the lattice stability of the FAMAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite. $ ^{33} $  The approach introduced in this manuscript is a novel and effective technique for fabricating FAMAPbI $ _3 $  film via MACl induced intermediate engineering. The deposited FAI-MAI-PbI $ _2 $ -solvent interim phase is treated with an optimized MACl concentration in IPA. The final resultant film proved itself as a high-quality film in terms of phase purity, stable lattice structure, and enhanced opto-electronic properties. Systematically, both approaches are different with respect to individual steps involved in fabrication and the final performance outcomes of each.

To further ponder into the trap density of the prepared films photo-physical measurements were conducted of the non-treated and treated films. Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) were used to further confirm the notions regarding the trap states in fabricated films (Figure 4a-d). The perovskite films on the bare glass were prepared to escape the chances of charge injection at the interface of the FTO and active layer. The PL intensity affirms a much stronger and sharper PL intensity of the MACl-20 film in comparison to control film (Figure 4a), with more or less uniform distribution curve over the examined spectrum of emission, suggestive of a reduction in defects on the film surface, steady with the PL intensity mapping in Figure 4b-c.  $  ^{31}  $   The corresponding film photoluminescence (PL) intensity mapping was done and presented in Figure 4b-c to understand the charge carrier dynamics. PL measurements provide direct insight into the non-radiative excitons decay performance. The control film shows non-uniform peak intensity triggered by larger trap flux which contributes to poor carrier mobility. While, MACl-20 film exhibits the most uniform and highest peak intensity, suggesting a minimized non-radiative excitons decay, which is almost an order of magnitude greater than that of the control film.  $  ^{36, 38,35}  $   TRPL spectra of control and MACl-20 treated films synthesized on the glass substrates were compared and exhibited in Figure 4d. The decay constants are calculated by a bi-exponential equation, and the respective fitting results are summarized in Table S1. The decay curves are categorized into two decay parts, comprising a fast decay (  $  \tau_{1}  $  ) and slow decay (  $  \tau_{2}  $  ). The   $  \tau_{1}  $   is ascribed to the non-radiative recombination at the interface, and the   $  \tau_{2}  $   is ascribed to the trap recombination in the perovskite bulk.  $  ^{34, 41}  $   In general, the perovskite active layers with large grains were proved to experience longer carrier lifetimes and lower trap density, definitely as a consequence of reduced non-radiative recombination and charge accumulation caused by reduced grain boundaries.  $  ^{28-29}  $   As compared to the   $  \tau_{2}  $   of 21.30 ns of the without control film, the MACl-20 film had lengthy carrier lifetime   $  \tau_{2}  $   of 75.54 ns.
