# 1. Introduction

Hybrid organometal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have drawn significant attention owing to their excellent physical properties: large absorption coefficients, high carrier mobility, tunable bandgaps, and specifically long charge carrier diffusion lengths.  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   Within a very short period of time, power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has markedly increased from 3.8 to 22.7%, outcompeting the present market leader, polycrystalline silicon.  $  ^{[4-12]}  $   In recent years, it has been recognized that the

PCBM as the most commonly used ETL in p-i-n planar PSCs is not perfect. On the one hand, its intrinsic organic properties lead to poor lifetime. On the other hand, PCBM is undesirable for protecting the perovskite from the invasion of the ambient oxygen and moisture.  $  ^{[14,19]}  $   To address these issues, inorganic ETLs as alternatives have been developed. You and co-workers  $  ^{[15]}  $   adopt ZnO in p-i-n planar PSCs as ETL, achieving a PCE of 16.1% and considerable stability, remaining 90% of the initial PCE after 60 days storage in the air of 30–50% relative humidity. However, it should be mentioned that the dispersion and viscosity of inorganic solutions are quite limited, leading to defective film morphology and low film coverage on the perovskite surface, limiting the reproducibility of device performance. Fang et al.  $  ^{[20]}  $   proposed a method that combines organic PCBM and inorganic   $  CeO_{x}  $   as a bilayer ETL, resulting in a PCE of 18.7% and good stability with   $  \approx90\%  $   of the initial PCE retained after 200 h storage in the air of 30% relative humidity. By comparison with the above two techniques, straightly imbedding inorganic dopants into the PCBM layer to form a hybrid ETL displays simpler and more effective, being advantageous to further improve the performance and stability

In this manuscript, we have developed a hybrid of   $  Zn_{0.8}Cd_{0.2}S  $   (ZCS) nanoparticles and PCBM as an efficient hybrid ZCS@PCBM ETL in p-i-n planar PSCs. The ZCS nanoparticles are well known as active photocatalysts for the water-splitting reaction,  $  ^{[23,24]}  $   which is supposed to have a promising application in PSCs, owing to its good electronic conductivity and excellent stability. Experimental results indicate that imbedding of ZCS into the PCBM layer enhances electron extraction, improves electron transportation and inhibits charge recombination, resulting in 19.2% increment of PCE due to the enhanced short-circuit photocurrent (  $  J_{sc}  $  ), improved fill factor (FF), and similar open-circuit photovoltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ). Furthermore, the ZCS@PCBM hybrid ETL can block the erosion of moisture to the perovskite layer, and the corresponding PSC devices demonstrate good long-term stability in the air with high humidity.

To investigate the recombination loss mechanism, we measured the J-V characteristics at various incident light intensities from 10 to   $  100 \, mW cm^{-2}  $   (Figure S6, Supporting Information). It is known that interfacial bimolecular recombination as a loss mechanism corresponds to a linear dependence with a slope of kT/q in the semi-logarithmic plot of   $  V_{oc}  $   versus light intensity, where k, T, and q are the Boltzmann constant, temperature in Kelvin, and the elementary charge, respectively.  $  ^{[33,34]}  $   When the charge recombination becomes more serious, a stronger dependence of   $  V_{oc}  $   on light intensity with a slope greater than kT/q is observed.  $  ^{[34]}  $   As shown in Figure 6c, the slope of the device with ZCS@PCBM is   $  1.07 \, kT/q  $  , whereas that of device with pure PCBM is   $  1.42 \, kT/q  $  . This result implies that incorporating ZCS nanoparticles in PCBM can reduce the density of interfacial defects, and hence charge recombination is significantly suppressed, which at least partly accounts for the enhanced charge collection efficiency and increased photocurrent generation.

Finally, to realize practical applications, PSCs should be featured with non-hysteresis, test-condition-independence, constant maximum power output, and excellent air stability. Figure 7a shows the J-V curves of ZCS@PCBM-based PSC with both reverse-scan and forward-scan modes under standard AM1.5G illumination. It is evident that no appreciable hysteresis appears. Moreover, the J-V curves of the device at different scanning rates from 20 to 120 mV s  $  ^{-1}  $  were also recorded. The current-voltage curves almost overlap with each other (Figure 7b) at various scan rates, suggesting that the photovoltaic performance is independent of scan rate at the tested range. In addition, the steady-state photocurrent that represents the actual power output was also characterized. As shown in Figure 7c, the photocurrent rises quickly to maximum and remains constant thereafter.

At last, long-term stability of devices was monitored and normalized in Figure S8, Supporting Information. All devices were stored and measured in the air (humidity 30–60%) without any encapsulation. As shown in Figure 7d, the PCE of devices based on ZCS@PCBM remains 82% of the initial PCE after storage of 500 h, which is much better than the one with pure PCBM (remaining 13%). This indicates that hybrid of ZCS nanoparticles and PCBM is an effective way to shield the device from ambient moisture, which thus improves the long-term stability. However, the storage stability is not good enough for practical applications. The long-term stability under light soaking was also tested, and the stability is shown in Figure S9, Supporting Information. The device with the   $  Zn_{0.8}Cd_{0.2}S@PCBM  $   ETL maintained 82% of its initial PCE after light soaking for 213 h, while that with PCBM ETL only maintained 46% of its initial PCE. However, after the devices were soaked under one-sun light for 341 h, the normalized PCE of the   $  Zn_{0.8}Cd_{0.2}S@PCBM  $   device decreased significantly to 46% while the PCBM device no longer turned light into electricity. As compared to the storage stability, the stability under light soaking declines, but the   $  Zn_{0.8}Cd_{0.2}S@PCBM  $   device still has better long-term stability than the PCBM device under light soaking.

It is reported that the hybrid ZnO/PCBM ETL shows high electron mobility and cascade bandgap alignment.  $  ^{[37]}  $   As a consequence, the ZnO/PCBM hybrid improves   $  J_{sc}  $   significantly, resulting in PCE enhancement from 12.72 to 14.17%.  $  ^{[37]}  $   Compared to the ZnO/PCBM hybrid, the   $  Zn_{0.8}Cd_{0.2}S  $  /PCBM hybrid can not only increase electron mobility, but also suppress interfacial charge recombination. Moreover, the   $  Zn_{0.8}Cd_{0.2}S  $   present in the hybrid can protect the perovskite layer from the erosion of ambient moisture, thus increasing the long-term stability. Therefore, the   $  Zn_{0.8}Cd_{0.2}S  $  /PCBM is superior to the ZnO/PCBM hybrid ETL.

The ZCS nanoparticles were synthesized according to the reported method.  $  ^{[23]}  $   Zn-(Ac)  $  _{2}  $    $  \cdot  $  2H  $  _{2}  $  O (0.133 g) and Cd(Ac)  $  _{2}  $    $  \cdot  $  2H  $  _{2}  $  O (0.439 g) were dispersed in distilled water (30 mL), into which Na  $  _{2}  $  S aqueous solution (0.3 M, 10 mL) was added dropwise followed by stirring for 2 h at room temperature.
