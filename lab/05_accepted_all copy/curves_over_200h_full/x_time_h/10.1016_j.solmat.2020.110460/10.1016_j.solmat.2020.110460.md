# 1. Introduction

The climate and environmental problems brought about by the continued consumption of fossil energy have made people realize that it is imperative to replace fossil energy with green renewable energy with the development of the social economy and the increase of the world population. Photovoltaic power generation converts solar energy into zero-emission electricity directly, providing a clean alternative to address energy and environmental issues   $  [1,2]  $  . In recent years, a new type of solar cells-perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has received great attention because of its many advantages such as strong absorption coefficient, wide light absorption wavelength range, longer carrier lifetime and diffusion length   $  [3]  $  . PSCs are developing rapidly with a sharp increase in power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 3.8% reported in 2009 to 25.2% certified today in the decade   $  [4–9]  $  , which has become one of the most competitors of silicon solar cells. # ABSTRACT

Carbon-based all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been a promising candidate in the field of photovoltaics due to its simple preparation process and excellent stability in air with high humidity. However, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of such cells is still unsatisfactory to this day because of the large energy offset and poor hole extraction at the interface of   $  CsPbBr_{3}/carbon  $  . Here, a   $  MoO_{2}/N  $  -doped carbon nanospheres (NC) composite with high hole mobility and matched energy level is prepared by a facile one-step pyrolysis process and introduced into carbon-based all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSC as a hole-transporting material (HTM) to enhance energy level alignment, interface contact and charge extraction as well as to passivate   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   surface defects, realizing the reduction of energy loss and charge recombination. The optimal PSC based on   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composite without encapsulation achieves a maximum PCE as high as 9.40% in comparison with 6.68% efficiency for the reference device and exhibits an outstanding long-term stability over 800 h in air atmosphere with 80% relative humidity. This work provides an effective approach to build high-performance and stable all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs by introducing simple synthetic and efficient   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composite inorganic HTM.

Many studies have mentioned that the formation of all-inorganic cesium lead halides (CsPbX $ _3 $ , X = I $ ^- $  or Br $ ^- $ ) by using the inorganic Cs $ ^+ $  ions instead of organic MA $ ^+ $  or FA $ ^+ $  ions is an effective rescue method to overcome the instability of PSCs toward heat [11–14]. The fully brominated CsPbBr $ _3 $  perovskite with high carrier mobility among various all-inorganic cesium lead halides can be synthesized in air atmosphere free of any humidity control and thereby considered as the most potential and stable light-absorbing material for PSCs.

It is generally known that the hole-selectivity of carbon is inefficient and the energy offset between valence band (-5.6 eV) of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   and work function (-5.0 eV) of carbon is as large as 0.6 eV, which affects the efficiency of hole extraction and the alignment of energy level, leading to a serious charge recombination at   $  CsPbBr_{3}/carbon  $   interface as well as a marked loss of open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) and therefore a much lower PCE compared with the efficiency of HTM-based devices [15,17,18]. To address this problem, quantum dots (QDs) including carbon [19], black phosphorus [20] and   $  CuInS_{2}/ZnS  $   QDs [21] etc. have been modified between   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   and carbon as an intermediate energy level to promote hole extraction and to reduce the energy level difference, resulting in an increased PCE over 8%. The poor coverage of QDs on perovskite surface makes it impossible to optimize the hole extraction ability, therefore, the addition of HTMs is more efficient and viable in charge transport kinetics and device architecture. A few organic HTMs such as poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT) [22], Cu-phthalocyanine [23], polyaniline and polypyrrole [24] have been deliberately incorporated into carbon-based   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs to improve hole extraction and to suppress charge recombination for a remarkable enhanced PCE. However, the organic HTMs are expensive, unstable, hygroscopic and doped with inorganic salt or acid ions [15], often causing high cost and negative impact on PSCs stability.

Inorganic HTMs such as   $  V_{2}O_{5}  $   [25],   $  MoO_{2}  $   [26],   $  NiO_{x}  $   [27] and   $  CuO_{x}  $   [28] have been considered as an promising alternative to organic HTMs due to their high hole mobility, low cost and non-hygroscopicity [29]. Among all the inorganic HTMs,   $  MoO_{2}  $   has attracted much attention owing to its distinctive metal electrical conductivity, which is related to its mixed interatomic bonds and relatively high density states at Fermi level [26]. However, the conductivity of   $  MoO_{2}  $   is about 2.9 times smaller than that of spiro-OMeTAD [30], making its device an unsatisfactory photovoltaic performance. High conductivity carbon materials like carbon nanotubes, carbon spheres, graphene oxide and graphdiyne have always been incorporated with inorganic HTMs or polymers as conductive fillers to improve the conductivity of the corresponding composites [29]. For instance, the introduction of organic functionalized carbon nanostructures into P3HT as a HTM has remarkably increased the device PCE in comparison with the PSCs tailored with pure P3HT owing to the enhanced conductivity and hole mobility. Moreover, the enhancement of local contacts at perovskite/HTM and HTM/back-electrode interfaces causes an efficient photo-generated charge extraction for the HTMs incorporated with carbon materials, which also contributes to the improvement of device photovoltaic performance [31].

Herein, the  $ \mathrm{MoO_2 / N} $ -doped carbon nanospheres (NC) composites are prepared by a facile one-step pyrolysis process which employs inorganic-organic molybdate/polyaniline-pyrrole hybrids as a precursor and used as a HTM with improved electrical conductivity and hole mobility for all-inorganic  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  PSCs. The inorganic-organic molybdate/polyaniline-pyrrole hybrids precursor prepared by liquid phase method ensures the uniform dispersion of  $ \mathrm{MoO_2} $  in  $ \mathrm{MoO_2 / NC} $  composites and the good combination between  $ \mathrm{MoO_2} $  and NC. In addition, N-doping not only further improves the conductivity of carbon nanospheres as well as the composites but also enhances the contact between the composite HTM and the perovskite surface because of the lone pair electrons of N atom which can bond to the under-coordinated lead ions on the perovskite surface and simultaneously passivate the defect states [32]. Detailed characterizations demonstrate that the introduction of  $ \mathrm{MoO_2 / NC} $  composites as HTMs observably reduces the energy offset at  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $ /carbon interface and perovskite surface defects as well as promotes hole extraction, resulting in a decreased energy loss and charge recombination within PSCs. By systematic optimizing the mass ratio of molybdate to polymer in precursors, the all-inorganic  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  PSC based on  $ \mathrm{MoO_2 / NC} $  composites HTM achieves a champion PCE up

to  $ 9.40\% $  , which is much higher than  $ 6.68\% $  efficiency for the reference HTM-free device. Furthermore, the optimal encapsulation-free PSC displays an excellent long-term stability with  $ 90\% $  maintaining of its initial PCE after exposure to air atmosphere with  $ 80\% $  relative humidity at  $ 25^{\circ}C $  over  $ 800\mathrm{h} $  . The results of this work suggest that the  $ \mathrm{MoO_2 / NC} $  composite is a promising substitute for other high cost and unstable HTMs for enhancing photovoltaic performance of PSCs.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1.Materials

All the analytical grade reagents were commercially obtained and used without further purification.

# 2.2. Synthesis of molybdate/polyaniline-pyrrolehybrids precursors

To prepare molybdate/polyaniline-pyrrolehybrids precursors, various mass of   $  (\mathrm{NH}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{Mo}_{7}\mathrm{O}_{24}\cdot4\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}  $   and 0.10 g of Triton X-100 were first dissolved in 60 mL of deionized water. Then, 0.38 mL of aniline and 0.29 mL of pyrrole were slowly dropped into the above mixed solution under magnetic stirring for 0.5 h and then ultrasonication for 0.5 h to form a homogeneous solution. After that, the mixed solution was kept at 0 °C for half an hour before the oxidative polymerization. Then   $  (\mathrm{NH}_{4})_{2}\mathrm{S}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{8}  $   aqueous solution previously cooled at 0 °C for 0.5 h was added to the above mixture. The solution was stirred to obtain complete mixing, and then polymerized at 0 °C for 12 h without agitation. Next, the obtained dark green product was repeatedly washed with deionized water and absolute ethanol several times until the filtrate was transparent and colorless, and the final product, molybdate/polyaniline-pyrrolehybrids precursor, was vacuum dried at 60 °C for 24 h.

The coexistence of aniline and pyrrole in this molybdic acid polymer was critical for the formation of molybdate/polymer nanostructures. The formation mechanism of the spherical molybdate/polymer nanostructure can be explained as follows [33]: First, pyrrole, aniline and   $  (\mathrm{NH}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{Mo}_{7}\mathrm{O}_{24}\cdot4\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}  $   were combined into different positions of Triton X-100 micelles due to their different hydrophobicity and hydrophilicity. After the addition of ammonium persulfate aqueous solution, the micelle-bound aniline and pyrrole polymerized at the micelle water interface, while at the same time the   $  (\mathrm{NH}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{Mo}_{7}\mathrm{O}_{24}\cdot4\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}  $   diffusion solution at each position has no significant effect on the copolymerization reaction, thus resulting in the formation of molybdate/polymer nanostructure hybrids precursor.

# 2.3. Synthesis of  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2 / \mathrm{NC} $  composites

The carbothermal reduction process was carried out in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere by calcining the as-obtained molybdate/polyaniline-pyrrolehybrids precursors at 600 °C for 3 h. The heating rate from room temperature to 600 °C is 5 °C per minute. The obtained pyrolysis carbonization products were   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composites. The pure   $  MoO_{2}  $   and NC as contrast samples were synthesized under the same conditions using   $  (\mathrm{NH}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{Mo}_{7}\mathrm{O}_{24}\cdot4\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}  $   and polyaniline-pyrrole as precursor, respectively. The   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composites with 0.5 g, 1 g, 2 g, and 4 g   $  (\mathrm{NH}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{Mo}_{7}\mathrm{O}_{24}\cdot4\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}  $   in the hybrids precursors are determined as   $  MoO_{2}/NC-0.5  $  ,   $  MoO_{2}/NC-1  $  ,   $  MoO_{2}/NC-2  $   and   $  MoO_{2}/NC-4  $  , respectively.

# 3. Results and discussion

The synthesized samples are first characterized by XRD to determine the crystal phase structure, as shown in Fig. 1a. All the samples appear the characteristic diffraction peaks of  $ (-111) $ ,  $ (-211) $ ,  $ (210) $ ,  $ (-301) $ ,  $ (-312) $ ,  $ (031) $ ,  $ (-201) $  and  $ (231) $  facets of  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2 $  (JCPDS No. 32-0671) at  $ 2\theta = 26.0^{\circ} $ ,  $ 37.0^{\circ} $ ,  $ 41.4^{\circ} $ ,  $ 49.5^{\circ} $ ,  $ 53.5^{\circ} $ ,  $ 60.2^{\circ} $ ,  $ 66.7^{\circ} $ , and  $ 78.5^{\circ} $ , respectively, and no other peaks of molybdenum compounds are detected. The results demonstrate that the pure phase  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2 $  is successfully synthesized by the pyrolysis of various molybdate/polyaniline-pyrrole hybrid precursors. XPS are recorded to further analyze the surface electronic valence and detailed chemical compositions of  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2/ $  NC-x ( $ x = 0.5 $ , 1, 2 and 4) composites, as shown in Fig. S1 and Fig. 1b-d. All the XPS wide-scan spectrums of  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2/ $  NC-x shown in Fig. S1 present the same distinct characteristic peaks of C, Mo, N and O, indicating the successful doping of N element. It can also be found that the peaks of Mo and O in the composites enhance with the increase of molybdate content in the precursors. The valence and chemical bonding states of each element are analyzed with  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2/ $  NC-1 as a representative. As shown in Fig. 1b, the high-resolution Mo3d spectrum can be deconvoluted into four peaks, the peaks at  $ 229.2\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ 232.8\mathrm{eV} $  correspond to characteristic double peaks of  $ \mathrm{Mo}^{4+} $ , and the weak peaks at  $ 232.4\mathrm{eV} $  and  $ 235.7\mathrm{eV} $  belong to the characteristic doublet of  $ \mathrm{Mo}^{6+} $  [34,35]. The appearance of  $ \mathrm{Mo}^{6+} $  may be attributed to the natural oxidation of trace Mo on the surface during storage in air. The high resolution spectrum of C1s in Fig. 1c presents three peaks at  $ 284.4 $ ,  $ 284.9 $  and  $ 285.9\mathrm{eV} $ , corresponding to the carbide, C-C and C-N species, respectively [36]. In the high resolution spectrum of N1s shown in Fig. 1d, the peaks at  $ 394.8 $ ,  $ 396.2 $ ,  $ 398.2 $  and  $ 400.5\mathrm{eV} $  can be assigned to Mo3p, N-Mo, pyridinic-N and pyrrolic-N, respectively [37-39]. The results of XPS analysis confirm the synthesis of  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2 $  and the doping of N element in carbon matrix and  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2 $ . It is worth mentioning that the N dopant with large electronegativity can effectively change the electronic structure of  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2/ $  NC composites, leading to an increase in conductivity and thereby an enhancement of the charge transfer rate [40]. Furthermore, the lone pair electrons of N atom are trend to bond to the positively charged under-coordinated lead ions on the perovskite surface, which could be confirmed by the high-resolution XPS spectra of Pb4f of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  films with and without  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2/ $  NC-1 shown in Fig. S2. It is observed that the peak position of Pb4f of the perovskite film with  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2/ $  NC shifts  $ 0.20\mathrm{eV} $  to lower energy compared to the  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  without HTM, indicating the reduced electron affinity and the increased electron cloud density of Pb atoms, which evidences that Pb atoms accept more electrons through bonding with the lone pair electron-riched N atoms. The interactions between N atom and under-coordinated lead ions passivate the perovskite surface defects and improve the contact between perovskite and composite HTM, which is in favor of the reduction of interface resistance and charge recombination [32].

The morphology of the  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2 / \mathrm{NC - x} $  composites is investigated by SEM, as shown in Fig. 1e and Fig. S3.  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2 / \mathrm{NC - 1} $  and other composites as well as the pure NC all show a distinct morphology of spherical

nanoparticles with a diameter about 70 nm, indicating that   $  MoO_{2}  $   is closely bonded to NC without phase separation and polyaniline-pyrrole precursor is the basis for the formation of nanospheres. It has also been reported that nanoparticles are beneficial to the formation of smooth, compact and non-porous thin films [41]. Fig. 1f shows the EDS mapping images of   $  MoO_{2}/NC-1  $   composite, it is apparent that the elements of C, Mo, N and O are uniformly distributed within the nanospheres, demonstrating the homogeneous distribution of   $  MoO_{2}  $   as well as the uniform doping of N in the composite. The above results confirm the formation of well-bonded   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composites, which will make the   $  MoO_{2}  $   have high conductivity and hole mobility [26].

The all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs here are assembled by a classical solution-process method in air atmosphere without any humidity control. Fig. 2a depicts the schematic diagram of a whole PSC with the multilayer structure of glass/FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /m-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  /(  $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composites)/carbon, corresponding to the typical cross-section SEM image of an actually prepared device (Fig. 2b). The   $  TiO_{2}  $   and   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   layer are approximately 250 and 450 nm thickness, respectively. The vertical orientation of the single   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   layer with large grains and high crystallinity (see the top view SEM and XRD patterns in Fig. S4) is beneficial for accelerating charge transfer, suppressing charge recombination and reducing grain boundary energy loss. It is noteworthy to mention that the   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composites nanospheres adhere closely to the surface of the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   grains, resulting in an almost invisible layer at perovskite/carbon electrode interface. The close contact between layers is favourable to decrease the internal resistance of device and promote charge separation and extraction. Fig. S5 gives the UPS spectra of   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composites with different dosage of molybdate in the hybrid precursors. By drawing, the work function (WF) of   $  MoO_{2}/NC-0.5  $ ,   $  MoO_{2}/NC-1  $ ,   $  MoO_{2}/NC-2  $   and   $  MoO_{2}/NC-4  $   is 5.10, 5.23, 5.28 and 5.39 eV, respectively, showing an enhanced trend with the amount of   $  MoO_{2}  $   increase. As shown in Fig. 2c, under the solar illumination, the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   crystal absorbs photons and the photo-generated electrons transition from the valence band (VB) of the perovskite to the conduction band (CB), then flow into the CB of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   electron-collecting layer, leaving holes to migrate to the HTMs and carbon electrode [16]. It can be seen that all the WFs of   $  MoO_{2}/NC-x  $   composites are between the VB of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   and the WF of carbon electrode, indicating that the introduction of   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composites HTMs can reduce the interface energy offset and therefore energy loss. In addition, the J-V curves of a hole-only device with a structure of FTO/NiO/  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $  /(  $  MoO_{2}/NC  $  )/carbon are measured in dark to explore the hole mobility (  $  \mu_{h}  $  ) of device with or without   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composites HTMs and the influence of HTMs depositing on the trap state density (  $  n_{trap}  $  ) of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   layer, as shown in Fig. 2d. The   $  \mu_{h}  $   can be calculated by the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method according to the Mott-Gurney law [22]:   $  J_{D} = 9\mu_{h}\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}V^{2}/8L^{3}  $ , where   $  J_{D}  $   is the current in dark,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative dielectric constant of   $  CsPbBr_{3} (\approx22)  $   [42],   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity, V is the applied voltage and L is the thickness of perovskite film. The corresponding   $  \mu_{h}  $   values are summarized in Table S1. It is as expected that the   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composites HTMs based devices display a remarkably increased hole mobility in comparison with the carbon-based HTM-free and pure   $  MoO_{2}  $   HTM based PSCs arising from their well-matched energy levels and improved electrical conductivity (as shown in Fig. S6), which is in favor of promoting hole extraction as well as charge separation and therefor leading to a suppressed charge recombination and an enhanced power output. The current density increases linearly and nonlinearly with voltages in Fig. 2d, and the voltage at the kink point is the trap-filled limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $ ), which determines the   $  n_{trap}  $   on the basis of the following equation:   $  n_{trap} = 2\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}V_{TFL}/eL^{2}  $   [22]. The   $  n_{trap}  $   of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film decreases from   $  7.877 \times 10^{14} cm^{-3}  $   for HTM-free device to   $  6.343 \times 10^{14} cm^{-3}  $   for   $  MoO_{2}/NC-1  $   tailored one (as summarized in Table S1), indicating that the deposition of   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   HTMs effectively heals the defect states of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $ , which may be ascribed to the passivation of positively charged lead ions on the perovskite surface by N element. To further investigate the charge recombination mechanism within the all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs, the relationships of   $  J_{sc}  $   and   $  V_{oc}  $   outputs as a function of the incident light intensity (I) are plotted, as shown in Fig. 3d and e. According to the formula [45]:   $  J_{sc} \propto I^{\alpha} (\alpha \leq 1)  $  , where   $  \alpha  $   is the factor associated with the bimolecular recombination, a large   $  \alpha  $   approximately equal to 1 indicates that there is little bimolecular recombination in device. The   $  \alpha  $   value increases from 0.954 for the reference HTM-free PSC to 0.999 for   $  MoO_{2}/NC-1  $   based device, indicating a low bimolecular recombination and therefore an enhanced charge separation by setting HTMs, which is attributed to the high hole extraction ability and well-matched energy level of   $  MoO_{2}/NC-1  $   composite. Moreover, the dependence of   $  V_{oc}  $   on the light intensity are linearly fitted according to the following equation [45]:   $  V_{\mathrm{oc}} = nK\mathrm{Tln}(I)/q + \text{constant}  $  , where k is the Boltzmann constant, T is the Kelvin temperature, q is the elementary charge and the n value represents a trap-mediated nonradiative recombination factor. Theoretically, a n value equal to 1 indicates that there is no additional trap-mediated monomolecular recombination. The smaller n value of 1.55 for   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSC tailored with   $  MoO_{2}/NC-1  $   than 1.98 for the reference HTM-free device demonstrates the suppression of trap-mediated monomolecular recombination due to the passivation of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   surface defects by depositing   $  MoO_{2}/NC-1  $   composite, which cross-checks the reduction of the trap state density obtained from   $  V_{TFL}  $   analysis.

The capacitance-voltage  $ (C - V) $  curves of all-inorganic  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  PSCs are also measured to understand the charge recombination. As previously reported, the built-in potential  $ (V_{\mathrm{bi}}) $  of the PSCs can be extracted from the linear partial intercept of the Mott-Schottky curves (Fig. 3f) to the voltage axis. It is evidently that the device tailored with HTMs presents a larger  $ V_{\mathrm{bi}} $  than the reference HTM-free PSC, which is attributed to the decreased trap state density as well as the reduced interfacial charge accumulation caused by the high hole extraction of HTMs. A larger  $ V_{\mathrm{bi}} $  can offer a higher driving force for the separation, transport and extraction of photo-generated carriers, resulting in a lower charge recombination and a higher power output, which agrees well with the  $ J - V $  characterization. Besides, the larger slop of Mott-Schottky curves for the HTMs-based device than that of the HTM-free PSC also indicates the lower interfacial charge density according to the fact that the interfacial charge density is inversely proportional to the linear slope of the Mott-Schottky curve. That is to say, the interfacial charge accumulation of HTMs-based device is lower, which is beneficial to reduce charge recombination. Based on the above discussion, it can be concluded that the introduction of  $ \mathrm{MoO}_2 / \mathrm{NC} $  composite with high hole mobility and well-matched WF can promote charge transfer and extraction, passivate surface defects for the suppression of charge recombination and therefore the enhancement of device photovoltaic performance.

The long-term stability and high PCE are equally essential in promoting the practical application of PSCs. The photovoltaic parameters stability of HTM-free and   $  MoO_{2}/NC-1  $   based all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs without encapsulation at 80% relative humidity and 25 °C in air atmosphere is performed to inspect the moisture tolerance of the devices, as shown in Fig. 4 and Fig. S10. It can be found that all photovoltaic parameters of   $  MoO_{2}/NC-1  $   based   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSC display a higher stability than that of the HTM-free device. The PCE of   $  MoO_{2}/NC-1  $   based all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSC maintains 98% of its initial efficiency after aging in air atmosphere with 80% relative humidity at 25 °C for 800 h, while the efficiency retention rate is only 93% for the HTM-free device.

The chemical stability of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   crystals and the protection of hydrophobic carbon surfaces under humidity attack are major factors for the superior moisture tolerance. Besides, the moisture resistance of   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composites further improves the long-term stability of all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSC (as the contact angle shown in Fig. S11). Furthermore, the unencapsulated   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSC with   $  MoO_{2}/NC-1  $   exhibits a higher thermal stability than the HTM-free device under 0% relative humidity at 85 °C in air for more than 240 h, as the normalized photovoltaic parameters presented in Fig. S12, which may be due to the increased crystal stability of perovskite after passivation of defects by   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $  . The high PCE along with the excellent long-term moisture and thermal tolerance indicates that the   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composite is an effective HTM for enhancing the photovoltaic performance of carbon-based all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, we have successfully synthesized   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composite by a facile one-step pyrolysis process and employed the composite as an efficient HTM for carbon-based all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs. The PSCs tailored with the composite HTM have an enhanced charge extraction and a reduced charge recombination due to the energy level matching, the surface defects passivation and the high hole mobility, achieving a maximum PCE of 9.40%, which is much higher than 6.68% efficiency of the HTM-free device. In addition, the optimum device without encapsulation exhibits a remarkable long-term stability after continuously eroding in air with 80% relative humidity at 25 °C for 800 h. These results indicate that using   $  MoO_{2}/NC  $   composite inorganic nanomaterials as HTMs may offer a potential pathway to enhance hole extraction and reduce energy offset as well as surface defects for high-performance PSCs.
