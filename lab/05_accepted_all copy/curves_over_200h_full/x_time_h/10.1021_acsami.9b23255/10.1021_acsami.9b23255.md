Abstract: Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs), as the most rapidly developing next-generation thin-film photovoltaic technology, have attracted extensive research interests, yet their efficiency, scalability, and durability remain challenging.  $ \alpha $ -Fe $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  could be served as electron transporting layer (ETL) of planar PSCs, which exhibited a much higher humidity and UV light-stability compared to TiO $ _2 $ -based planar PSCs. However, the photovoltaic conversion efficiency (PCE) of Fe $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ -based device was still below 15% because of poor interface contact between  $ \alpha $ -Fe $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  and perovskite, and poor crystal quality of perovskite. In this work, we have engineered the interfaces throughout the entire solar cell via incorporating N, S co-doped graphene quantum dots (NSGQDs). The NSGQDs played remarkably multifunctional roles: i) facilitated the perovskite crystal growth; ii) eased charge extraction at both anode and cathode interfaces; iii) induced the defect passivation and suppressed the charge recombination. When assembled with a  $ \alpha $ -Fe $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  ETL, the planar PSCs exhibited a significantly increased efficiency from 14% to 19.2%, with concomitant reductions in hysteresis, which created a new record of PCE for Fe $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ -based PSCs to date. In addition, PSCs with the entire device interfacial engineering showed an obviously improved durability, including prominent humidity, UV light and thermal-stabilities. Our interfacial engineering methodology via graphene quantum dots represents a versatile and effective way for building high efficiency as well as durability PSCs.

Keywords: Perovskite solar cells; interface engineering;  $ \mathrm{Fe_2O_3} $ ; graphene quantum dots; PCE; stability.

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic lead halide perovskites (OILHP) have drawn great concern from the materials research community and motivated the development of low-cost and clean energy solutions towards sustainable energy.  $  ^{1-6}  $   During the past several years, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of small-area perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has rocketed from an initial 3.8% to a certified 25.2%, close to those of industrially engineered silicon and CdTe solar cells.  $  ^{7-13}  $   Such ever-increasing PCE value heavily relies on the compositional and interfacial engineering.  $  ^{14-20}  $   Although PCEs are impressively approaching the performance of the established crystalline silicon and inorganic thin film solar cells,  $  ^{21}  $   non-radiative recombination losses, and environmental and photo stability remain challenging and require rapid solutions.  $  ^{22-24}  $

Compared with other established photovoltaic technologies, one significant advantage of OILHP for photovoltaic applications is their solution processing capability.  $  ^{25-27}  $   However, due to low formation energy, the crystallization of the OILHP takes place even at low temperatures, thereby presenting small crystal size and abundant grain boundaries (GBs) within the polycrystalline film. Defects including dislocations, interstitials, and anion vacancies and coordinatively unsaturated lead cations, as well as impurities tend to accumulate at GBs and surfaces of OILHP.  $  ^{28, 29}  $   A lot of research have shown that the defects at GBs resulted in an increased trap-assisted recombination  $  ^{30-33}  $   and reduced performance of light harvesting OILHPs as well as photoluminescence (PL) efficiency.  $  ^{34,35}  $   The alternative processing routes, such as spin-coating, dip-coating, ink-jet printing, spray-coating and vacuum flash-assisted solution processing

Apart from OILHP aspects, selective charge contact interfaces at anode and cathode play a significant part in affecting both the PCE and long-term stability of PSCs.  $  ^{19, 42}  $   In a typical PSCs architecture, the performance depends essentially on the non-radiative recombination at interfaces from electron transport layer (ETL)/perovskite and perovskite/ hole transport layer (HTL). Hence charge transporting layers play an essential part in enhancing both PCE and long-term stability of PSCs. For instance, both electron transport conductors (such as   $  C_{60}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}  $  ) and hole transport materials (such as foreign dopants, PEDOT: PSS and TaTm) are a source of surface recombination in regular and inverted planar structured PSCs.  $  ^{43-46}  $   The highest efficiency has been achieved using   $  TiO_{2}  $   as an ETL of PSCs, whereas show a poor stability under long-term light irradiation (including ultraviolet light) due to the desorption of absorbed oxygen on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   surface.  $  ^{47}  $   Previously, we revealed that using   $  \alpha-Fe_{2}O_{3}  $   as an ETL material effectively hindered the performance degradation after long-term light illumination, even under intensive UV light irradiation.  $  ^{48-50}  $   Nevertheless, the device efficiency of   $  \alpha-Fe_{2}O_{3}  $  -based planar PSCs is still lower than 15%, which can be attributed to poor interface contact between charge transport layers and perovskite, as well as poor crystal quality of perovskite. Furthermore, PSCs suffer from performance degradation when operating at elevated temperatures, owing to the interface deterioration by chemical reaction between the migrated iodide ions and metal electrode.  $  ^{51, 52}  $   Therefore, tailoring multiple interfaces is of great importance to obtain high-performing PSCs. In this work, multiple interfaces throughout the entire PSCs were simultaneously manipulated with an efficient multifunctional strategy via simultaneously incorporating NSGQDs into  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  (MA = methylammonium) layer, and the perovskite/ selective charge (electron and hole) interfaces, to realize high efficiency and long-life PSCs. NSGQDs in the perovskite film help to slow down the crystallization process, thereby increasing perovskite grain size as well as passivating the trap states. Furthermore, NSGQDs also suppressed charge recombination and facilitated charge extraction from the perovskite to its neighboring charge contact layers. The NSGQDs produced multifunctional interfacial engineering effects, resulting in the highest PCE of  $ 19.2\% $  and negligible device hysteresis. Our unencapsulated devices also exhibited high thermal stability and UV light stabilities, with less than  $ 4\% $  efficiency degradation after more than 1200 hours of storage in a humid environment. These findings further highlight the importance of multiple interfacial engineering for photovoltaic applications.

2.2 Formation mechanism and crystal quality of the perovskite film. To further elucidate the role of NSGQDs in growth mechanism of the perovskites (Fig. 2a), liquid-state  $ ^{1} $ H magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra of the perovskite precursor was carried out (Fig. 2b). The marked peaks are derived from solvent. To isolate the effects of N and S, pristine GQDs were used as a reference due to its similarity to the NSGQDs but without the presence of N and S (Fig. S9), evidently indicating no difference with and without GQDs. In contrast, the peak at 7.87 ppm, which is attributed to the three H atoms of MA cation, exhibits a down field shift to 7.84 ppm after adding NSGQDs to the perovskite precursor solution (Fig. 2b). This shift suggests the interaction between the MA cation and NSGQDs by hydrogen bonds. The formed hydrogen bond mitigated the perovskite growth, thereby achieving the large crystal grains. # 3. Conclusions

By simultaneously incorporating N, S co-doped graphene quantum dots into the perovskite layer and its corresponding charge contact interfaces, we have successfully realized multiple interface engineering throughout the solar cell device and effectively “killed three birds with one stone”. At first, the perovskite grain size can be increased by the added NSGQDs in the perovskite film, which not only slow down the crystallization process but also passivate the defects by coordinating with the under-coordinated lead-cation. Furthermore, the NSGQDs used as the electron interfacial layer promote the charge separation as well as thus inhibit the charge recombination. Furthermore, when NSGQDs are used as the hole interfacial modification layer, the band alignment between the perovskite layer and HTL is optimized, and the stability is greatly improved. Thanks to the multiple interface engineering, PSCs assembled with   $  \alpha-Fe_{2}O_{3}  $   ETL layer display a significant efficiency enhancement from 14% to 19.2% (an increase of 37%), a new record PCE reported for   $  Fe_{2}O_{3}  $  -based PSCs to date. A substantial decrease is observed in the device hysteresis. The humidity stability is enhanced by 78% after 400 hours (82% of the primary PCE after 1200 h), and the thermal stability is increased by 84% after 300 hours. Meanwhile, PCE of   $  Fe_{2}O_{3}  $  -based devices was three times larger than that of   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based devices under the intensive UV irradiation for 120 mins. In addition to the   $  Fe_{2}O_{3}  $ , our methodology presents to be versatile and beneficial for improving the performance of PSCs fabricated with   $  TiO_{2}  $   and   $  SnO_{2}  $   electron
