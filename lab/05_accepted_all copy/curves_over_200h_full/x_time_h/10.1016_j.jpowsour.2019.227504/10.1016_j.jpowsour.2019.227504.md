# ABSTRACT

The ETL/perovskite interface is crucial for the photovoltaic performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) because of its key role in electrons transport and charge recombination. Herein, an ultrathin  $ \mathrm{CaTiO_3} $  layer has facilely been fabricated and incorporated between the  $ \mathrm{mp - TiO_2} $  and perovskite layers. Due to the trap passivation effect and the optimized energy level alignment induced by modification, the electron transport is facilitated while the charge recombination is suppressed effectively. Therefore, the champion device gains a maximum PCE of  $ 19.12\% $  with the enhanced photovoltaic performance. In addition, the stability of PSCs has also been ameliorated by modification, and about  $ 85\% $  of the initial efficiency can be maintained even after exposure in ambient for 1000h.

To overcome these issues, various organic interlayers have been introduced between   $  TiO_{2}  $   and perovskite, for example, the   $  TiO_{2}  $   film had been anchored with perovskite layer by inserting   $  HOCO-R-NH_{3}^{2}I^{-}  $   interlayers and the PCE have been increased [25]. Shih et al. modified the ETL/perovskite interface utilizing an amino acid, glycine, and enhanced the short-circuit current density [26]. Although these organic interlayers can anchor the perovskite and   $  TiO_{2}  $   via functional groups, appeasing the interface trap states and inhibiting the charge recombination, the stability and cost of these organic compounds are additional concerns for practical application. Due to the low cost and superior chemical properties, inorganic interlayers especially metal oxides, have been extensively studied to modify the interface to passivate the surface traps, promote energy level matching and mitigate the instability of   $  TiO_{2}  $   under ultraviolet illumination [27–32]. Perovskite oxides (  $  ABO_{3}  $  ), which have the same perovskite structure as the hybrid perovskite absorber in PSCs, are more attractive than simple oxides due to their high durability, good chemical affinity and proper band alignment [33]. Recently, we used a thin layer of   $  SrTiO_{3}  $   to modify the interface between the mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   and perovskite and a remarkable improvement has been achieved in the device performance of PSCs [34]. Comparatively,   $  CaTiO_{3}  $   has a higher conduction band bottom than that of   $  SrTiO_{3}  $   and thus presents a more suitable band alignment with   $  TiO_{2}  $   and perovskite. In addition, the length of Ti-O bonds in   $  CaTiO_{3}  $   and   $  TiO_{2}  $   are 1.985 Å and 1.976 Å, respectively. The similar lengths ensure good lattice matching and

interface contact, which facilitates the transport of electrons [35]. Moreover, the relatively high permittivity of  $ \mathrm{CaTiO_3} $  can further inhibit carrier recombination at the interface. Therefore, it is supposed that  $ \mathrm{CaTiO_3} $  is favorable as the modification layer to  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  in perovskite solar cells.

Herein, an ultrathin  $ \mathrm{CaTiO_3} $  layer has been fabricated on  $ \mathrm{mp - TiO_2} $  to modify the perovskite/mp-TiO2 interface in PSCs. Due to the cascade conduction band level and the surface traps passivation after modification, the hysteresis of PSCs has been eliminated by the  $ \mathrm{CaTiO_3} $  modification layer successfully, the charge transfer between perovskite and electron transport layer has also been improved effectively. Therefore, a remarkably improvement of photovoltaic performance of PSC device has been obtained, achieving an average PCE of  $ 18.55\% $  in a properly modified  $ \mathrm{mp - TiO_2} $ , which approximately increases by  $ 11\% $  in comparison with that of the non-modified device.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

The etched FTO were sequentially cleaned with deionized water (DI), acetone and isopropanol, and then dried under nitrogen flow. To prepare the N type   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer, an ethanol solution (4.5 ml) containing titanium isopropoxide (0.3 ml) and HCl (0.045 ml) was spin-coated (3500 rpm 30s) on the UV-ozone treated FTO. The samples were sintered at 500 °C for 30 min and formed a compact layer of   $  TiO_{2}  $  . A mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer was spin-coated with a diluted   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste (18NR-T Dyesol) with ethanol solution (5000 rpm, 30s) and then followed by 500 °C annealing for 30 min. To obtain the modified film, a solution of different concentrations of calcium nitrate in DI was spin coated on mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   film (3500 rpm 25 s), annealing at 500 °C for 30 min, as shown in Fig. S7. The perovskite precursor solution was acquired by dissolving FAI (1 M),   $  PbI_{2}(1.1\ M)  $  , MABr (0.2 M),   $  PbBr_{2}  $   (0.22 M) in a mixture of anhydrous DMF/DMSO (4/1 in volume) under constant stirring at 333 K. The CsI solution (1.5 M in DMSO) was added into above solution to make   $  CsI_{0.05}[(FAPbI_{3})_{0.85}(MAPbBr_{3})_{0.15}]_{0.95}  $  . The obtained precursor solution was spin-coated on the samples at 4000 rpm 25s, during which 100 μL of chlorobenzene (CB) was dropped onto the spinning substrate at the first 10s and then heated at 100 °C for 60min. The spiro-MeOTAD solution was deposited onto the obtained perovskite film at 3500 rpm for 30s and kept in desiccator overnight. A gold thin film of ≈80 nm was deposited by thermal evaporation last.

# 2.3. Characterization

XRD patterns were gained by X-ray diffractometer (Philip X'Pert PRO). Ultraviolet photo-electron spectroscopy (UPS) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra were characterized by AXIS-ULTRA DLD-600W, Kratos. Ultraviolet visible spectrophotometer (UV-vis, Lambda 950, PerkinElmer) was employed to gauge the transmittance and light absorption of the films. Field-emission scanning electron microscopy (GeminiSEM300, Carl Zeiss) investigated the surface

morphology. Steady-state photoluminescence (LabRAM HR800) spectra was obtained with laser beam excited at 325 nm. Applying an Edinburgh Instruments Ltd. FLS 980 spectrometer to gain the Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra. Current density-voltage (J-V) curves were censored by employing a digital source meter (Keithley 2400) under 1 sun (AM 1.5G, 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) illumination. A shadow mask was applied to set the active area of the cells at 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $  . The Newport-74125 system was used to examine incident photon-to-current efficiency (IPCE). Employing a potentiostat (IVIUM, IviumStat 10800) to observe the Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) with a frequency ranging from 0.1 Hz to 100 MHz in a dark condition.

# 3. Results and discussion

The XRD and XPS analyses, were carried out to characterize the modified mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $  . As shown in Fig. 1a, no extra peak but the peaks of anatase   $  TiO_{2}  $   (PDF#21–1272) are detected in the XRD pattern of the modified mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   because the modification layer is too thin to be distinguished by XRD. XPS spectra (Fig. 1b–d) further verify the chemical composition of the pristine and modified mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   films. The Ti 2p peaks at approximately 458.9 and 464.7eV in both films, accord well with the Ti 2p  $  _{3/2}  $   and Ti 2p  $  _{1/2}  $  , respectively; while the peaks at 347.5 and 350.9 eV for the modified   $  TiO_{2}  $   film can be well indexed as the Ca 2p  $  _{3/2}  $   and Ca 2p  $  _{1/2}  $   respectively, which is in accordance with the binding energy of Ca in   $  CaTiO_{3}  $   [36]. To further confirm the nature of modification layer, we spin coated the calcium nitrate solution on mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   for several times to obtain a thicker modification layer for XRD analysis. As revealed by Fig. S1, besides the peaks of anatase mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $  , two extra peaks at 33° and 58.9°, corresponding to the (110) and (042) plane of orthorhombic   $  CaTiO_{3}  $   (PDF#01-078-1013), are also detected. The above XRD and XPS analyses demonstrated that a modification layer of   $  CaTiO_{3}  $   has been fabricated on the mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   surface successfully.

Different  $ \mathrm{CaTiO_3} $  modification layers were obtained by treating the mp- $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  with various concentrations of  $ \mathrm{Ca(NO_3)_2} $  solution (0, 1, 2, 3, 4,  $ 8\mathrm{mM} $ ), and the corresponding SEM images were presented in Fig. 2a-b and Fig. S2. # 4. Conclusions

In summary, this work presents a facile strategy which can effectively improve the performance of perovskite solar cells by modifying the mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   with   $  CaTiO_{3}  $  . The   $  CaTiO_{3}  $   modification layer can constrain the recombination and promote the electrons extraction and transport by introducing a more favorable matched energy level at ETL/perovskite interface. Consequently, the average PCE of PSCs based on   $  CaTiO_{3}  $   modified   $  TiO_{2}  $   has been increased by 11% compared with   $  TiO_{2}  $  . Notably, the champion device obtained a PCE of 19.12% as the results of enhanced   $  J_{sc}  $  ,   $  V_{oc}  $  , and FF simultaneously and the J-V hysteresis is negligible.
