# A single crystal derived precursor for improving the performance of   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells  $  ^{\dagger}  $

Qiang Sun,‡ Anjie Gu,‡ Haixuan Yu, Yan Shen  $  ^{ID}  $  and Mingkui Wang  $  ^{ID}  $ *

Reducing the toxicity of halide perovskites is critical in the pathway toward perovskite photovoltaic commercialization. Low toxicity   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   is emerging as a possible alternative in this regard. However, the preparation of high-quality   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   thin films remains a great challenge due to the uncontrollable crystallization of tin-based perovskites. Herein, for the first time we prepared high purity   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   single crystals by the wet chemical method. Utilizing these pre-synthesized   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   single crystals for preparing precursor solutions, larger grain and pinhole-free   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   thin films with low defect density can be obtained. Hence, the   $  CsSnI_{3}  $  -based perovskite solar cells with a device configuration of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/  $  CsSnI_{3}/PC_{61}BM/BCP/Ag  $   achieved an excellent power conversion efficiency of 6.53%, which is the highest value for   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   photovoltaics without additives. Moreover, the optimized device exhibits excellent stability by retaining 91% of its initial efficiency after 1000 hours of storage in a nitrogen atmosphere. This work indicates a promising protocol for perovskite single crystal derived precursors for processing efficient tin perovskite solar cells.

# Introduction

Lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved a remarkable milestone in solar-to-electricity power conversion efficiency (PCE) of over 25%.  $  ^{1}  $   Despite the significant efficiency achievements in hybrid PSCs, however, the toxicity of the heavy metal Pb and the instability of the fragile organic components remain major issues in the pathway toward hybrid PSCs' commercialization.  $  ^{2-5}  $   In this regard, the low toxicity and fully inorganic cesium tin triiodide (CsSnI  $  _{3}  $  ) perovskite is emerging as a promising alternative.  $  ^{6,7}  $   The CsSnI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite with a high light absorption coefficient (approximately   $  10^{4}  $   cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ), low exciton binding energy (about 18 meV), high carrier mobility (  $  \sim500  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   V  $  ^{-1}  $   s  $  ^{-1}  $  ), and a perfect bandgap (1.3 eV),  $  ^{6-8}  $   shows potential as a photoactive material for efficient solar cells.

Unfortunately, the photovoltaic performance of   $  CsSnI_{3}  $  -based solar cells (with a maximum reported PCE of about 11%) lags largely behind that of their lead counterparts.  $  ^{9-10}  $   The greatest challenge currently for processing high-performance   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   solar cells is the uncontrollable crystallization of   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   perovskite  $  ^{11,12}  $   as well as the easy oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   due to the weak inert pair effect of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   to   $  Pb^{2+}  $   which results in high activity of the 5s electrons of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   ions.  $  ^{13-15}  $   Considerable strategies have been developed to improve the quality of   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   films.

by regulating the crystallization processes, including additive engineering,  $  ^{16}  $   dimensional engineering,  $  ^{17}  $   solvent engineering, and so on.  $  ^{12}  $   However, the addition of additives also brought new issues, such as high toxicity, phase separation and reaction with the adjacent charge transport layers.  $  ^{18}  $   Therefore, it is of great significance to look for a simple, non-destructive and reasonable solution for preparation of high-quality   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   perovskite films.

Recently, a strategy of perovskite single crystal redissolution as the precursor is proposed and successfully developed to process efficient and stable lead halide PSCs.  $  ^{19-22}  $   Distinct from the typical synthesis route (chemical reaction of lead halide and iodide salt), the strategy for preparing perovskite thin films through a single crystal redissolution derived precursor can maintain the inherent excellent properties of single crystals, such as accurate stoichiometric ratios, low trap density, highly crystallinity, and well-controlled structural alignment. More importantly, it has been proven that the redissolution of perovskite single crystals in solvents can preserve the original perovskite clusters.  $  ^{23,24}  $   The presence of highly ordered perovskite phases and/or perovskite-solvent intermediate clusters in the precursors based on crystal redissolution facilitates the formation of perovskite nuclei, therefore allowing for orderly and controlled growth of the perovskite thin films. However, a detailed investigation of   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   thin films prepared by the single crystal redissolution strategy is currently lacking, probably due to the difficulty of high quality   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   single crystal preparation.

# Results and discussion

# CsSnI  $  _{3}  $   single crystal preparation and characterization

This study demonstrates high-quality B-γ (a desirable photoactive phase)   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   thin films prepared through single crystal growth and redissolution engineering. Fig. 1a presents the schematic diagram for synthesis of B-γ   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   single crystals by the heat-activated solvent volatilization method. It is worth noting that a solution temperature above 100 °C is crucial for obtaining B-γ   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   single crystals, which can be observed from the optical images and X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   crystals prepared at different temperatures (Fig. S1†). Based on this, we conclude that a certain temperature (>100 °C) is needed to intensify the thermal motion of Cs ions and provide sufficiently activation energy to drive Cs ions into the   $  [SnI_{6/2}]  $  -octahedral gap position to form the perovskite structure (Fig. S2†). On the other hand, considering that the boiling point of N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) solvent is 153 °C, too high a temperature can lead to rapid solvent evaporation which is not conducive to the preparation of high-quality single crystals, and therefore, we select 130 °C as the temperature for   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   single crystal growth. # B-  $ \gamma $  CsSnI $ _3 $  single crystals as a raw material for precursor preparation

Previous reports have proven that perovskite precursor solutions are colloidal dispersions rather than a real solution. The colloidal nanoparticles (NPs) are superstructure networks mainly consisting of Sn and I coordination complexes, as essential building blocks in the precursor dispersion.  $  ^{25-27}  $   These colloidal NPs are the starting point for perovskite nucleation and crystal growth. Therefore, it is crucial to modulate the chemical nature of these perovskite precursor solutions to control the quality of the resulting perovskite films. In the case of tin based perovskites, the crystal growth rate was too rapid and the nucleation was not yet completed, which leads to low-quality thin films with abundant pinholes. Therefore, this hinders the device performance.  $  ^{28}  $   In order to balance the crystal nucleation and growth dynamics of   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   perovskite, in this study, we attempt to enlarge the stable colloidal NPs in the   $  CsSnI_{3}  $   perovskite precursor dispersion to promote the nucleation rate of perovskite. Inspired by previous studies of crystal redissolution engineering that can maintain certain structures of the perovskite in the precursor,  $  ^{19-22}  $   we believe that it is an effective method to enlarge the colloid size in the precursor dispersion for   $  CsSnI_{3}  $  .

Herein, the pre-synthesized B-γ CsSnI₃ single crystals were utilized as a raw material for precursor preparation (denoted as crystal-derived precursor solution). For comparison, CsSnI₃ precursor solution prepared by dissolving CsI and SnI₂ salts (denoted as salt-derived precursor solution) was used as a reference. We then investigated precursor chemistry of the two different solutions through UV-vis absorption and dynamic light scattering (DLS) measurements to verify Sn-I coordination species and size distribution of colloidal NPs in the two different precursor solutions, as shown in Fig. 2a and b. Obviously, the crystal-derived precursor solution (red line) exhibits increased absorption intensity at 370–450 nm and decreased absorption intensity at 260–370 nm compared to the salt-derived precursor solution. The former can be attributed to the absorption transition of [SnI₄]²⁻, and the latter can be attributed to the absorption transition of [SnI]⁺, [SnI₂], and [SnI₃]⁻ species.²⁹⁻³¹ This result confirms that the crystal-derived precursor solution contains more high-coordination Sn-I species, closer to the original perovskite structure. This phenomenon can be attributed to the difference in dissolution processes. According to coordination chemistry theory, the dissolution of CsI and SnI₂ salts requires a complex colloidal self-assembly process, leading to equilibrium with several iodate species, such as: [SnI₂] + I⁻ ⇌ [SnI₃]⁻; [SnI₃]⁻ + I⁻ ⇌ [SnI₄]²⁻. In contrast, the redissolution of B-γ CsSnI₃ crystals is a reverse self-assembly process, including but not limited to: [SnI₆]⁴⁻ ⇌ [SnI₅]³⁻ + I⁻; [SnI₅]³⁻ ⇌ [SnI₄]²⁻ + I⁻. Accordingly, the DLS characterization indicates that the crystal-derived precursor solution contains larger average colloidal particle sizes. Comparing with the salt-derived precursor solution which exhibits a colloidal particle size distribution centered at ~20 nm, the colloidal particle size distribution of the crystal-derived precursor solution increases dramatically to ~60 nm. These results confirm more high coordination Sn-I species and enlarged colloidal particles in the crystal-derived precursor solution, therefore promoting the conversion of Sn-I clusters to the perovskite phase to form perovskite nuclei for CsSnI₃ thin film growth as illustrated in Fig. S5.†

Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) decay measurements were further performed to investigate the nonradiative carrier recombination behavior of the S-CsSnI $ _3 $  and C-CsSnI $ _3 $  thin films. The charge carrier recombination kinetics of the perovskite films were evaluated with TRPL decay measurements as illustrated in Fig. 4b. The C-CsSnI $ _3 $  based PSCs maintained 84%, 73% and 56% of their original PCE after 72 h at 30%, 50% and 70% relative humidity, respectively. In contrast, the S-CsSnI $ _3 $  based PSCs maintained only 23% of their original PCE after 72 h at 30% relative humidity, and almost lost photoactivity within 48 h at a relative humidity >50%. This considerably enhanced stability of the C-CsSnI $ _3 $  based PSCs can be mainly attributed to the tightly packed perovskite grains of the C-CsSnI $ _3 $  thin films which retard the corrosion of water and oxygen through the grain boundaries, as well as the reduced defect density of the C-CsSnI $ _3 $  thin film that mitigated the defect accelerated perovskite film destruction. These strong pieces of evidence demonstrate the significance of the crystal redissolution derived precursor strategy as an effective pathway for achieving efficient and stable CsSnI $ _3 $  PSCs.

# Conclusions

In summary, we successfully prepared B-  $ \gamma $  CsSnI $ _3 $  single crystals using the heat-activated solvent volatilization method. And these pre-synthesized B-  $ \gamma $  CsSnI $ _3 $  single crystals were utilized as a raw material for CsSnI $ _3 $  thin film preparation. These single crystal derived perovskite precursors with large colloidal NPs can promote the nucleation of CsSnI $ _3 $  perovskite, and therefore balance the nucleation and growth rate for preparing high-quality CsSnI $ _3 $  thin films with pinhole-free surface morphology and large grains. As a result, the CsSnI $ _3 $  solar cells with a device configuration of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/CsSnI $ _3 $ /PC $ _{61} $ BM/BCP/Ag achieved an excellent PCE of  $ 6.53\% $  with enhanced stability.
