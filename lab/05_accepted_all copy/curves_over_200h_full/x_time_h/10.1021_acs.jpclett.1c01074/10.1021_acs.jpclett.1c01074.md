# Lattice Strain Relaxation and Grain Homogenization for Efficient Inverted MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  Perovskite Solar Cells

Chengbo Li, Jie Hu, Shurong Wang, Jing Ren, Bin Chen, Taisong Pan, Xiaobin Niu, and Feng Hao*

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: The lattice strain of a perovskite film is vital to the controllable growth and charge transport in perovskite solar cells (PSCs). In this work, a lead chloride   $  \left(\mathrm{PbCl}_{2}\right)  $   assisted crystallization (LCAC) protocol is introduced for releasing the strain across the interface of a   $  NiO_{x}  $  /perovskite, which induces a preferred   $  (h00)  $   crystal plane growth and grain homogenization. PSCs with LCAC show a facilitated charge extraction and suppressed nonradiative recombination. Thanks to the controlled film growth and strain-released interface, the inverted   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   (MA = methylammonium) PSC devices with LCAC deliver a power conversion efficiency (PCE) over 20% with a short-circuit current density   $  \left(J_{sc}\right)  $   of 23.60 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , which is obviously higher than that of the control device with a PCE of 18.36% and a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 21.74 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  . Meanwhile, the LCAC devices maintain 80% of their initial efficiency after being exposed to an ambient atmosphere with a relative humidity of 40% over 1000 h in the dark.

Recently, hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted extraordinary attention due to their rapid growth in power conversion efficiency (PCE).  $  ^{1}  $   Although many methods have been taken to improve the PCE, the highest PCE is still far from reaching its theoretical limit due to the slow charge extraction and nonradiative recombination in the perovskite bulk and interface.  $  ^{2,3}  $   Hence, the controlled growth of a perovskite film and strain-released interface are highly desired for efficient carrier transport and collection in PSCs.  $  ^{4,5}  $   On the one hand, it has been proven that a lower tensile strain in a perovskite film near the interface region increases the mobility of charge carriers, thereby improving the photovoltaic performance of the device.  $  ^{6,7}  $   On the other hand, current works have been devoted to improving the morphology and crystallinity of perovskite films by post-processing,  $  ^{8}  $   solvent engineering,  $  ^{9,10}  $   and chemical additives.  $  ^{11,12}  $

The interface between the inorganic transporting underlayer, such as   $  TiO_{2}  $  ,   $  {}^{13}SnO_{2}  $  ,   $  {}^{14,15}  $   and ZnO,  $  ^{16}  $   and the perovskite layers will cause a lattice distortion and the inconsistent thermal expansion of lattice units. This will further lead to strain and crystallographic inhomogeneity in the perovskite films and, thus, increase the structural instability and lower the carrier mobility.  $  ^{6,17,18}  $   Moreover, the strain accumulation effect leads to a poor phase stability of the perovskite lattice and accelerates the degradation of the material.  $  ^{19}  $   Recently, Sargent et al. reduced the tensile strain in a perovskite film by the hole transport layer with a high thermal expansion coefficient.  $  ^{7}  $   They found that the compressive strain improved the ion migration activation energy as well as the stability of devices. In the present work, a   $  PbCl_{2}  $   assisted crystallization (LCAC) protocol is proposed by combining a seed-induced nucleation site to modulate the perovskite film growth. The LCAC induces the preferred orientation on the   $  (h00)  $   crystal plane. The grain size homogenization and preferred   $  (h00)  $   crystal plane growth leads to a facilitated charge extraction and suppressed recombination, as proved by the transient photocurrent/photovoltage decay (TPC/TPV), dark current–voltage curves and capacitance measurements analysis. Consequently, a PCE over 20% is observed in the corresponding inverted   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   PSC devices with LCAC. Our results demonstrate an effective strategy to manipulate the nucleation and lattice strain release of perovskite thin films, which will be important for the realization of high-performance PSCs.

When the perovskite film is deposited on   $  NiO_{x}  $   by the hot-casting method, the contact formed between the two layers restrains the shrink of the perovskite lattice while being cooled to room temperature, which introduces tensile strain along the in-plane direction (Figure 1a).  $  ^{7}  $   We propose a strain-gradient method to reduce the residual tensile strain in the perovskite film. Different   $  PbX_{2}  $   (X = Cl, Br, I) materials were tested for this purpose. A   $  PbX_{2}  $   (X = Cl, Br, I) layer is first casted on the   $  NiO_{x}  $   substrates to form nucleation sites, followed by the deposition of an   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   layer as described in our previous work.  $  ^{8}  $   The residual stress in the perovskite film is calculated using the Williamson-Hall plot as displayed in Figure S1, and the derived values are presented in Figure 1b. The perovskite film treatment by   $  PbCl_{2}  $   releases the most tensile strain. We speculate that the reason is that   $  PbCl_{2}  $   reduces the strain gradient across the interface of   $  NiO_{x}  $ /perovskite and lower the interfacial energy of perovskite after growth. The cross-section image of the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   film was then examined with a scanning electron microscope (SEM, Figure 1c,d). Confocal laser scanning fluorescence microscopy (CLSM) was also performed to investigate the effect of LCAC on the morphology of perovskite films. A large degree of spatial heterogeneity was observed in the control perovskite film, and the LCAC film showed a more even and smoother morphology with a uniform grain size (Figure S4a,b). The LCAC film had a flatter surface, which can be derived from the smaller mean values (77.641 nm for LCAC and 163.439 nm for control, Figure S4c).  $  ^{26}  $   This indicates that LCAC has a positive effect in coordinating the crystallization dynamics, which also can be reconfirmed from the X-ray diffraction (XRD) spectra of Figure 1h. The strong peaks at   $  14.09^{\circ}  $  ,   $  20.20^{\circ}  $  , and   $  40.63^{\circ}  $   could be indexed to the (110), (200), and (224) planes of the orthorhombic MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite, respectively. Notably, the intensity of the (200) plane increased, and the intensity of (110) decreased substantially in the LCAC film, which indicated a preferred orientation in the (200) crystal plane. (XRD spectra of all variables can be seen in Figure S5.) As shown in Figure 1i, the diffraction intensity ratio of (200)/(110) in LCAC film is 5 times higher than that in the control MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film, further confirming the (h00) preferred orientation in the LCAC film. Theoretical calculations have clarified that the (h00) crystalline plane is more favored for electron transport in the perovskite crystal structure.  $  ^{5,27,28}  $   It has been also reported that the (100) and (200) crystal planes of perovskite are preferentially arranged in the vertical direction of the film.  $  ^{4}  $   That is, the corner of the inorganic framework will be connected to the substrate, which is beneficial to the transport and collection of carriers.  $  ^{28,29}  $   Also, it was indicated that the crystallinity increased from 85.22% for the pristine perovskite film to 97.62% with LCAC (Figure 1i). The theory behind the increased grain size and preferred orientation is mainly due to the uniform growth of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   crystals on PbCl  $  _{2}  $   owing to the lattice strain release and the reduction of surface defects. These results indicated that LCAC can synchronously induce improved crystallinity, preferred orientation, and crystal grains homogenization of the deposited MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film. The improved crystallinity of the perovskite film could also be reflected by the ultraviolet and visible (UV–vis) absorption spectra in Figure 1j. The light absorbance of the LCAC film is improved in the range of 350–750 nm, mostly associated with the enlarged and homogenized grain size and preferred orientation.

on the (100) and (110) interface of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ . The results showed that LCAC is induced through the interaction between a Pb cation with an I anion on the interface (Figure 2).

Moreover, the relative interfacial energy (  $  \Delta\gamma  $  ), which is defined as   $  \gamma_{100}-\gamma_{110}  $  , is below zero with different variable selections (Figure S6, negative   $  \Delta\gamma  $   means that (100) orientation is stable relatively). The strong interaction between PbCl  $  _{2}  $   and MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   interface ions can adjust the   $  \gamma  $   of different crystal planes and stabilize the uncoordinated I anion on the (100) interface in MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  . Therefore, the DFT calculations corroborated the hypothesis that the interface between the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (100) crystal plane and PbCl  $  _{2}  $   is more stable than that of (110) because of the lower   $  \gamma  $   of (100) crystal plane, which endows a preferred orientation on the (100) crystal plane. The LCAC protocol can help lattice expansion or shrinkage to release the tensile strain on the specific crystal plane and therefore obtain the lower   $  \gamma  $  .

An exponential behavior depends on an exponential diode component with an ideal factor of n.  $  ^{33}  $   By fitting the dark J-V curve, n is extrapolated to be 4.87 and 3.79 for the control and LCAC devices, respectively. The decrease of n indicates that LCAC enables a better diode junction quality for the PSC devices. In summary, we have demonstrated an LCAC protocol to modulate the nucleation and crystalline growth of perovskite films. On one hand,   $  PbCl_{2}  $   can serve on a matching nucleation site for perovskite growth, mitigating lattice distortion and interface strain. On the other hand,   $  PbCl_{2}  $   can form strong chemistry bonds with uncoordinated I anions on the (100) interface in   $  MAPbI_{3}  $ , resulting in a significantly reduced lattice distortion. The calculations of surface and interfacial energies using DFT proved that the (100) crystal plane is more preferred with the LCAC protocol. The homogenization of the perovskite crystals induces a facilitated charge extraction and suppressed nonradiative recombination as confirmed from the transient photocurrent and photovoltage decay, dark current–voltage curves, and capacitance analysis. Consequently, an inverted planar PSC device with over 20% efficiency and good operational stability is obtained. Our results provide a simple and versatile strategy for the homogenized and orientated growth of the perovskite films. We believe that the preferred orientation of the perovskite film paves a novel path to high-efficiency PSCs. Also, we believe this promising method will be easily applicable to other interfaces in PSCs and conducive to the practical application of perovskite-related photovoltaic technology and beyond.
