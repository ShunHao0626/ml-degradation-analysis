# Realizing Reduced Imperfections via Quantum Dots Interdiffusion in High Efficiency Perovskite Solar Cells

Lin Xie, Parth Vashishtha, Teck Ming Koh,* Padinhare Cholakkal Harikesh, Nur Fadilah Jamaludin, Annalisa Bruno, Thomas J. N. Hooper, Jia Li, Yan Fong Ng, Subodh G Mhaisalkar,* and Nripan Mathews*

Realization of reduced ionic (cationic and anionic) defects at the surface and grain boundaries (GBs) of perovskite films is vital to boost the power conversion efficiency of organic-inorganic halide perovskite (OIHP) solar cells. Although numerous strategies have been developed, effective passivation still remains a great challenge due to the complexity and diversity of these defects. Herein, a solid-state interdiffusion process using multi-cation hybrid halide perovskite quantum dots (QDs) is introduced as a strategy to heal the ionic defects at the surface and GBs. It is found that the solid-state interdiffusion process leads to a reduction in OIHP shallow defects. In addition,  $ \mathrm{Cs^{+}} $  distribution in QDs greatly influences the effectiveness of ionic defect passivation with significant enhancement to all photovoltaic performance characteristics observed on treating the solar cells with  $ \mathrm{Cs_{0.05}(MA_{0.17}FA_{0.83})_{0.95}PbBr_3} $  (abbreviated as QDs-Cs5). This enables power conversion efficiency (PCE) exceeding  $ 21\% $  to be achieved with more than  $ 90\% $  of its initial PCE retained on exposure to continuous illumination of more than  $ 550~h $ .

In this work, we report an ionic defect passivation strategy employing multi-cation hybrid halide perovskite QDs for highly efficient OIHP solar cells. Through prudent control over the QDs composition, ionic defects at the surface and GBs of OIHP films can be effectively managed via a process termed as ionic solid-state interdiffusion. This systematic study demonstrates the influence of QD's composition on passivation and highlights the importance of ionic solid-state interdiffusion at the surface and GBs in reducing these charged defects. Our strategy yields significant enhancement in open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) resulting in OIHP solar cells with PCE exceeding 21%, retaining more than 90% of its initial PCE despite exposure to continuous illumination for more than 550 h. Our findings not only offer a feasible and straightforward passivation route to enhance device performance and stability, but also rationalizes the working-principle behind such QD-based passivation techniques.

We synthesized multi-cation hybrid halide perovskite QDs, namely   $  MA_{0.17}FA_{0.83}PbBr_{3}  $   (abbreviated as QDs-Cs0),   $  Cs_{0.05}(MA_{0.17}FA_{0.83})_{0.95}PbBr_{3}  $   (abbreviated as QDs-Cs5),   $  Cs_{0.10}(MA_{0.17}FA_{0.83})_{0.90}PbBr_{3}  $   (abbreviated as QDs-Cs10), and   $  Cs_{0.15}(MA_{0.17}FA_{0.83})_{0.85}PbBr_{3}  $   (abbreviated as QDs-Cs15), to passivate the OIHP films. The rationale for selection of these QDs is as follows: 1) multi-cation (  $  FA^{+}  $   and   $  MA^{+}  $  ) hybrid halide perovskite QDs offer excellent optoelectronic properties;  $  ^{[47]}  $   2) reduced defect density and longer carrier lifetime coupled with high synthesis reproducibility and perovskite phase purity of Cs-incorporated multi-cation hybrid halide perovskite QDs;  $  ^{[48]}  $   and the 3) possibility of ionic diffusion with these QDs which enables passivation of vacancies present in OIHP films. In order to understand the underlying reason behind the enhancement to photovoltaic performance of QDs-Cs-modified solar cell devices, various characterization were carried out on the control and QDs-Cs-modified films. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurements were done to determine the energy level of the neat QDs films, control film and QDs-Cs-modified films (Figure S6 and Table S5, Supporting Information). The average valence band   $  E_{\mathrm{V}}  $   of the perovskite film, QDs-Cs0-modified and QDs-Cs5-modified OIHP films are estimated to be   $  5.83 \pm 0.03  $   eV,   $  5.89 \pm 0.05  $   eV, and   $  5.96 \pm 0.09  $   eV, respectively. Upon treating the perovskite films with QDs-Cs0 and QDs-Cs5, the QDs-modified perovskite films exhibit a smaller movement in the opposite direction compared to the average   $  E_{v}  $   of neat QDs-Cs0 film (  $  5.77 \pm 0.04  $   eV) and QDs-Cs5 films (  $  5.66 \pm 0.05  $   eV), which indicate the possible diffusion of QDs.  $  ^{[52]}  $

In general, both the control and QDs-Cs5 modified OIHP films consist of large crystals with grain sizes of over 1   $  \mu  $  m as shown in the scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images (Figure 3a–c). As compared to the control, it is obvious that the deposited QDs-Cs5 accumulate irregularly on the OIHP film surface, forming nanoclusters (  $  \approx  $  100 nm) on the OIHP grains as well as at the GBs, instead of a continuous thin layer over the OIHP film (Figure 3b). However, as depicted in the cross-sectional SEM image (Figure S7a,b, Supporting Information), no obvious change in film thickness (  $  \approx  $  850 nm for the control and QDs-Cs5 modified films) is noted on treating the OIHP film with QDs-Cs5. Noticeable morphological change is observed by annealing the QDs-Cs5-modified film at 70 °C for 10 min (Figure 3c; Figure S7 c–f, Supporting Information). This indicates that solid-state interdiffusion occurred in the OIHP film upon annealing, during which QDs are conveyed to the defect sites, resulting in the boosting of PCE from 20.52% to 21.10%

with significant enhancement in   $  V_{oc}  $   noted (Figure S8 and Table S6, Supporting Information).

To gain insight into the influence of QDs on perovskite crystal lattice, we performed grazing incidence X-ray diffraction (GIXRD) (Figure 3d). Diffraction peaks of the control OIHP film at 14.05°, 28.25°, and 31.67° are assigned to (001), (002) and (012) perovskite planes.  $  ^{[53]}  $   The peak at 12.6° is attributed to the presence of excess lead iodide (PbI  $  _{2}  $  ), which is crucial to the fabrication of high efficiency OIHP solar cells.  $  ^{[54]}  $   On modifying the OIHP films with QDs-Cs0 and QDs-Cs5, the (001) diffraction peak undergoes a shift from 14.05° to 14.00° and 13.97° respectively, indicative of lattice expansion. An additional peak at 7.7° (Figure 3e) evident in the XRD patterns of QDs-Cs modified OIHP films, is likely due to the formation of lower dimensional perovskite phase. This is supported by XRD analysis of OLA-modified OIHP film (Figure S9, Supporting Information) where the exact peak was also noted. Presence of low-dimensional perovskite is known to be beneficial for the reduction of ionic migration and improvement of OIHP solar cells' ambient stability.  $  ^{[55-57]}  $   The formation of low dimensional phase further reiterates the occurrence of solid-state interdiffusion in the QD-modified OIHP films. A similar shift in (001) peak position is observed on modifying MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   with QDs-Cs5 (Figure S4b, Supporting Information), consistent with increased lattice constant a result of elemental substitution. The UV–vis absorption spectra of the control and QD-modified OIHP films are shown in Figure 3f. Although solid-state interdiffusion has occurred in the OIHP film, negligible change in light harvesting capability is observed. The bandgaps decrease slightly from 1.55 eV (control) to 1.54 eV in QDs-Cs5-modified perovskite films.

Cs incorporation is known to contribute toward the enhancement of OIHP rigidity and thermal stability, resulting in highly efficient solar cells.  $  ^{[48,55]}  $   Therefore, it is important to understand the distribution of Cs ions in our multi-cationic QDs, and its role in promoting solid-state interdiffusion on the OIHP film surface. Here, we performed   $  {}^{133}  $  Cs NMR (NMR) in order to identify the elemental distribution in the QDs-Cs with varying Cs content. The   $  {}^{133}  $  Cs NMR spectra of QD-Cs5, QD-Cs10, and QD-Cs15 (Figure S10, Supporting Information) present with resonances at   $  \delta  $   values between 62 and 49 ppm, representing a shift to lower frequencies compared to the   $  {}^{133}  $  Cs resonance for pure   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   (  $  \delta = 117  $   ppm), confirming that the QDs are a mixed-cation phase.  $  ^{[58-60]}  $   A detailed structural analysis of QDs using solid-state NMR measurements is illustrated in our previous report.  $  ^{[49]}  $   The spectra for QD-Cs5 and QD-Cs10 present with two resonances, where the less intense “shoulder” resonance is shifted slightly to lower frequencies (Table S7, Supporting Information). This is indicative of two similar Cs environments: Cs in the bulk perovskite structure, and Cs in the distorted perovskite layers closer to the surface of the nanoparticles. Similar behavior has been previously observed in the solid state NMR of other nanoparticle systems.  $  ^{[61,62]}  $   In contrast, QD-Cs15 presents with a singular resonance suggesting that a negligible proportion of the Cs atoms exists in the near-surface layers. The slight variation in particle size is not sufficient to explain the difference in the ratio of surface to total Cs atoms observed across the QD series if the Cs atoms are assumed to be uniformly distributed throughout the particles (Table S8, Supporting Information). The water contact angle (WCA) measurement was conducted to assess the hydrophobicity of QD-Cs modified OIHP films (Figure S13a,b, Supporting Information). The WCA increased from 82.4° to 97.6° owing to the hydrophobic nature of QDs-Cs modified OIHP film surface. As illustrated in Figure S13c–f in the Supporting Information, QDs-Cs5-modified OIHP film retains the perovskite phase (dark brown color) despite exposure to ambient conditions for 8 days, further exemplifying its moisture tolerance. Thermal stability of the control and QDs-Cs5-modified solar cells carried out at 85 °C in an Ar filled glove box is shown in Figure 5b. About 80% of QDs-Cs5 modified device initial PCE is retained after 168 h, whereas the control device shows rapid degradation to 58.7% of its initial PCE. Maximum power point tracking for the QDs-Cs5-modified OIHP device shows 95% of its initial PCE is retained under continuous illumination in N₂ atmosphere for 560 h (Figure 5c). The remarkable photostability displayed by QD-Cs modified OIHP films is assigned to the reduction in defects at both the interfaces and GBs.[3,6] In short, the reduced defect density and formation of low dimensional perovskite phase in QDs-Cs5 modified OIHP device are the keys to the enhanced thermal and moisture stability observed. Figure 5d depicts the proposed mechanism of ionic defect passivation offered by QDs-Cs5. Cations and anions from QDs-Cs5 occupy the ionic vacancies via solid-state interdiffusion at the OIHP surface and GBs, thus reducing ionic defects and suppressing interfacial non-radiative recombination. In addition, the hydrophobic long chain ligands from QDs-Cs5 form low dimensional perovskite phase on the surface, imparting improved moisture tolerance and ambient stability to the OIHP film.

In summary, we have demonstrated an effective defect passivation strategy, where the introduction of multi-cation hybrid halide perovskite QDs into OIHP films allows the ionic defects to be addressed via a solid-state interdiffusion process. As a result, the QDs-Cs5 modified OIHP device exhibits a PCE of  $ 21.1\% $ , attributed to the reduced non-radiative recombination and extended charge carrier lifetimes. Furthermore, the enhanced moisture and photostability observed upon introduction of the multi-cation hybrid halide perovskite QDs arise due to the formation of low dimensional perovskite which increases surface hydrophobicity and device stability. We have also rationalized the importance of multi-cation hybrid halide perovskite QDs surface composition in determining the extent of passivation. This work provides important insights into ionic defect engineering in perovskite solar cells to achieve both high efficiency and long-term stability.
