# Using 4-Chlorobenzoic Acid Layer Toward Stable and Low-Cost CsPbI $ _2 $ Br Perovskite Solar Cells

Zhuowei Li, Wei Wu, Guanhua Ren, Wenbin Han, Na Li, Chunyu Liu,* and Wenbin Guo*

Inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted a lot of attention due to their thermal stability. Among them,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   are more widely studied due to their reasonable bandgap and phase stability at room temperature. However, poor moisture resistance is an urgent issue to conquer. Herein, solution-processed 4-chlorobenzoic acid (CBA) is used to increase the work function of a Ag electrode, enabling effective hole extraction. More importantly, CBA can effectively avoid the phase conversion of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite from the cubic phase to the undesired nonperovskite δ-phase due to the hygroscopicity of lithium bis (trifluoromethanesulphony)imide (Li-TFSI) in 2,2,7,7-tetrakis(N,N-di-pmethoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD), enhancing the water stability of PSCs. It can also reduce the fabrication cost by replacing the expensive Au electrode and saving the evaporation energy consumption of the commonly used   $  MoO_{3}  $   anode buffer layer. Consequently, the champion device achieves a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 14.3%, with a high open-circuit voltage of 1.21 V. After storing in air at 20% relative humidity (RH) for 500 h, the unencapsulated device maintains more than 95% of the initial PCE.

# 1. Introduction

The power conversion efficiencies (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have exceeded 25% due to the excellent photoelectric properties of perovskite photoactive layers,  $  ^{[1-9]}  $  although the chemical instability of organic–inorganic hybrid perovskites hampers their further commercialization process.  $  ^{[10-13]}  $  Recently, inorganic cesium lead halide PSCs have attracted significant interest due to their outstanding thermal stability, and

The tolerance factor of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   is smaller than 0.9, which leads to an easier conversion from   $  \alpha  $  -phase (black phase) to   $  \delta  $  -phase (yellow phase), especially in an environment with over 30% relative humidity (RH).  $  ^{[28,29]}  $   Generally, 2,2,7,7-tetrakis(N,N-di-pmethoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) is used as the hole transport layer (HTL) in n-i-p-structured PSCs,  $  ^{[30,31]}  $   but it needs to use lithium bis (trifluoromethanesulphony)imide (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butyl pyridine (TBP) as additives. Li-TFSI has strong hygroscopicity, which will further intensify the phase conversion of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , shortening the lifetime of devices.  $  ^{[32]}  $   Therefore, it is urgent to find new strategies to enhance the water stability of inorganic PSCs. Liu and co-workers added   $  MnCl_{2}  $   to the precursor solution, and the water stability of the device was significantly improved due to the hydrophobic feature of   $  Cl^{-}  $ . The device still maintained 97% of its initial efficiency.  $  ^{[33]}  $   Zhuang et al. used hydrophobic 4-bromobenzylamine (BrBeAl) to postprocess a   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, which significantly enhanced the moisture resistance. After being stored in air at 60% RH for 20 min, BrBeAl-CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br films still maintain the pure phase.  $  ^{[34]}  $

Compared with organic–inorganic hybrid PSCs, the price of CsI and CsBr is lower than that of formamidinium iodide (FAI) and methylammonium iodide (MAI) due to the simple purification process, which makes inorganic PSCs have the potential to reduce costs. A Au electrode with a high work function   $  \left(\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{f}}\right)  $   can effectively collect holes, but Au is expensive. To make up for the deficiency of Ag in   $  W_{f}  $  , the common method is introducing   $  MoO_{3}  $   to form a composite anode with Ag to improve the hole extraction efficiency.  $  ^{[35-37]}  $   Nevertheless,   $  MoO_{3}  $   is prepared by vacuum evaporation, inevitably increases the manufacturing cost, which is contrary to the principle of low cost in commercial manufacturing. In this work, we introduced 4-chlorobenzoic acid (CBA) as anode modification layers in  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs. CBA remarkably increases the  $ W_{\mathrm{f}} $  of an Ag electrode that effectively extract holes and suppress charge carrier recombination. Also, the hydrophobic CBA as a barrier layer improves the water instability of the devices caused by the hygroscopic Li-TFSI in Spiro-OMeTAD, effectively prolonging the lifetime of the device. This new composite electrode reduces the cost of devices by substituting expensive Au electrodes and high-temperature evaporation of  $ \mathrm{MoO}_3 $  while achieving high performance and stability.

# 2. Result and Discussion

The perovskite photoactive layer plays an important role in highly efficient PSCs; thus, some properties of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   layer are characterized. First, the X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film is shown in Figure 1a, and strong diffraction peaks appear at   $  14.9^{\circ}  $   and   $  29.8^{\circ}  $ , representing cubic phases of (100) and (200). Figure 1b shows the absorbance and PL spectra of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   electron transport layer (ETL). The  $ W_f $  of the modified Ag electrode was measured by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS), as shown in Figure 2c. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, CBA is used as an anode modification layer in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs, which effectively increases the   $  W_{f}  $   of the Ag electrode, thereby improving the hole extraction efficiency and reducing the charge carrier recombination. The device with the CBA/Ag electrode achieves a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.21 V, an FF of 78%, and a PCE of 14.32%. With the improvement of device efficiency, the fabrication cost is reduced due to the solution-processed method of the CBA layer. Moreover, the hydrophobic CBA can protect the devices from the penetration of water vapor and I ions and effectively protect the perovskite layers and the electrodes, contributing to improved devices stability. Our work provides a low-cost anode buffer layer for inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs toward high efficiency and stability for further wide applications.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: The CsI (  $  \geq  $  99.99%), PbI  $  _{2}  $   (>99.99%), PbBr  $  _{2}  $   (>99.99%), SpiroOMeTAD (>99.8%), Li-TFSI (>99%), and TBP were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. The CBA was purchased from Tokyo Chemical Industry. The SnO  $  _{2}  $   colloid dispersion (tin (IV) oxide, 15% in H  $  _{2}  $  O) was purchased from Alfa Aesar. The N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) and dimethyl sulfoxide(DMSO) were purchased from J & K Scientific.

Device Fabrication: The inorganic PSCs were fabricated with the structure of ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /CsPbI $ _2 $ Br/Spiro-OMeTAD/CBA/Ag. The ITO glass substrate was ultrasonically treated in deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol for 10 min in sequence. Then precleaned substrates were treated with UV-ozone for 20 min. SnO $ _2 $  colloid precursor (tin (IV) oxide, 15% in H $ _2 $ O colloidal dispersion), and ultrapure water were mixed in a volume ratio of 1:4 and stirred

to obtain a   $  SnO_{2}  $   solution with a concentration of 3 wt%. The   $  SnO_{2}  $   solution was spin coated on the substrate at 3000 rpm for 40 s and then annealed at 150 °C on a hot stage for 30 min to obtain the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL. The   $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}(DMSO)  $   were prepared according to previous work.  $  ^{[31]}  $     $  PbI_{2}(DMSO):PbBr_{2}(DMSO):CsI(0.5:0.5:1\;\mathrm{m})  $   was dissolved in a mixed solvent of DMF:DMSO (0.8 mL:0.2 mL) and stirred overnight to obtain a   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution. To fabricate   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   layers, the precursor solution was spin coated on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film at 3000 rpm for 45 s, and 150 μL of chlorobenzene was quickly added onto the rotating substrate after the spin-coated 30 s. The substrate was placed in glove box for 10 min and then transferred to a hot stage and annealed at 180 °C for 10 min. To obtain the Spiro-OMeTAD solution, 50 mg of Spiro-OMeTAD and 22.5 μL of Li-TFSI solution (170 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in acetonitrile) and TBP were dissolved in 1 mL of chlorobenzene. The Spiro-OMeTAD solution was spin coated on the perovskite film at 1500 rpm for 30 s to obtain the HTL. CBA was dissolved in isopropanol with different concentrations (1, 2, 4, 6, 8 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) and spin coated on the HTL at 4000 rpm for 30 s to fabricate CBA anode modified layers. The reference device was fabricated by evaporating 6 nm   $  MoO_{3}  $   on the Spiro-OMeTAD HTL. Finally, 80 nm Ag or Au was deposited under vacuum conditions (<10  $  ^{-4}  $   pa) at an evaporation rate of 1 Å S  $  ^{-1}  $  . The effective area of the device was 4.4 mm  $  ^{2}  $  .

Film and Device Characterization: The cross-sectional SEM image of the device was characterized by a JEOL JSM-7500 field-emission SEM. The XRD patterns of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films were texted by a Shimadzu XRD-6000 diffractometer. The spectra of light absorption and PL were measured by UV1700, Shimadzu, and Shimadzu RF 5301. UPS analyses were conducted on a Thermo Scientific ESCALANTM 250Xi system. The J-V curves were conducted using a Keithley 2400 with a solar simulator intensity of   $  \approx100\ mW\ cm^{-2}  $  . Measurements of EQE were characterized by a Crowntech QTest Station 1000 AD. The Nyquist plots were characterized by a VSP multichannel galvanostatic-potentiostatic system (Bio-Logic SAS). The XPS spectra were characterized by a Thermo Scientific Nexsa. The contact angles were analyzed by a Kruss IL4200.
