# Nonfullerene Electron Transporting Material Based on Naphthalene Diimide Small Molecule for Highly Stable Perovskite Solar Cells with Efficiency Exceeding 20%

Su-Kyo Jung, Jin Hyuck Heo, Dae Woon Lee, Seung-Chul Lee, Seung-Heon Lee, Woojin Yoon, Hoseop Yun, Sang Hyuk Im,* Jong H. Kim,* and O-Pil Kwon*

This study reports a new nonfullerene electron transporting material (ETM) based on naphthalene diimide (NDI) small molecules for use in high-performance perovskite solar cells (PSCs). These solar cells simultaneously achieve high power conversion efficiency (PCE) of over 20% and long-term stability. New NDI-ID (N,N'-Bis(1-indanyl)naphthalene-1,4,5,8-tetracarboxylic diimide) consisting of an N-substituted indane group having simultaneous alicyclic and aromatic characteristics is synthesized by a low-cost, one-step reaction, and facile purification method. The partially flexible characteristics of an alicyclic cyclopentene group on indane groups open the possibility of low-temperature solution processing. The conformational rigidity and aromaticity of phenyl and alicyclic groups contribute to high temporal stability by strong secondary bonds. NDI-ID has herringbone packed semiconducting NDI cores that exhibit up to   $  0.2 \, cm^{2} \, V^{-1} \, s^{-1}  $   electron mobility in field effect transistors. The inverted PSCs based on   $  \mathrm{CH(NH_{2})_{2}PbI_{3-x}Br_{x}}  $   with NDI-ID ETM exhibit very high PCEs of up to 20.2%, which is better than that of widely used PCBM (phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester) ETM-based PSCs. Moreover, NDI-ID-based PSCs exhibit very high long-term temporal stability, retaining 90% of the initial PCE after 500 h at   $  100 \, ^{\circ}C  $   with 1 sun illumination without encapsulation. Therefore, NDI-ID is a promising ETM for highly efficient, stable PSCs.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Design with Introducing Simultaneous Alicyclic and Aromatic Groups

Various material parameters should be considered during the design of nonfullerene organic ETMs for low-temperature solution-processable PSCs with high PCE and long-term temporal stability. ETM materials should have high electron mobility similar to PCBM and its derivatives, as well as suitable highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) levels for high PCE. They should also have moderate solubility in typical target solvents. Neighboring layers in PSCs should exhibit an orthogonal solvent behavior, thus enabling easy solution processability. Finally, the ETM materials should have high thermal and environmental stabilities (i.e., no phase transitions below 250 °C), thus leading to long-term temporal stability in PSCs. Note that PCBM exhibits a phase transition at 250 °C before the melting temperature of 285 °C;  $  ^{[35]}  $  this is called the “lowest phase transition temperature” here.

In this work, NDI is chosen as the n-type semiconducting ETM core because of its efficient electron transporting characteristics and high crystalline characteristics in small molecules.  $  ^{[36-39]}  $   In organic photovoltaics, NDI-based polymers show high thermal and temporal stabilities with high PCE.  $  ^{[40-42]}  $   Multifunctional N-substituted indane groups are introduced with

In previously reported NDI derivatives, introducing  $ N $ -substituted flexible alkyl groups resulted in high solubility in common organic solvents. However, the derivatives showed various mesophasic phase transitions at relatively low temperatures (in some cases, about  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) due to high conformational freedom and flexibility of alkyl groups.[43-47] Electronic devices, including PSCs, may exhibit decreased long-term temporal stability due to low-temperature mesophasic phase transitions in the ETM. However, introducing  $ N $ -substituted wholly aromatic groups into NDI derivatives often results in low solubility in common organic solvents. This limits solution processability and, consequently, a vacuum deposition process is often used for the fabrication of electronic devices.[47-49] In addition, introducing  $ N $ -substituted bulky groups may decrease the density of semiconducting NDI cores in ETLs (i.e., decreasing electron mobility).[38] Consequently, space-filling characteristics of  $ N $ -substituted groups might also be considered in the design of NDI-based ETMs. Solution processability and long-term temporal stability are related to flexibility and rigidity of the chemical groups, respectively.  $ N $ -substituted indane groups having simultaneous alicyclic and aromatic characteristics (cyclopentene and phenyl rings, respectively) are introduced in the NDI-ID ETM (see Figure 1a) in order to yield solution processability and long-term temporal stability of NDI-based ETMs. In indane groups, the partially flexible characteristics of the alicyclic cyclopentene group may result in sufficient solution processability, while the conformational rigidity and aromaticity of aromatic phenyl and alicyclic cyclopentene groups contribute to high temporal stability by strong secondary bonds (e.g., hydrogen bonds and  $ \pi-\pi $  interactions).

As shown in Figure 1a, the new NDI-ID ETM is synthesized by a low-cost, one-step reaction with 1,4,5,8-naphthalenetetracarboxylic dianhydride and 1-aminoindan in the absence of catalysts (see details in the Supporting Information). Over 50% yield was obtained by a conventional recrystallization method. Note that the conventionally used catalyst (e.g., organic amine analogs and inorganic zinc acetate) in the synthesis of NDI-based derivatives is not used for NDI-ID in this work. NDI-based derivatives have strong tendency to form co-crystals with such polar species and residual polar species are often difficult to eliminate by a simple recrystallization method. Compared to other organic fullerene and nonfullerene ETMs, this simple synthesis of NDI-ID ETM without complicated purification processes is very beneficial for low-cost and high-volume ETL synthesis.

# 2.2. Physical Properties

Figure 1b shows differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) thermodiagrams for NDI-ID crystalline powders. NDI-ID exhibits high thermal stability with initial weight low temperature of over 330 °C. In particular, NDI-ID does not show any phase transition below the lowest phase transition temperature at 294 °C (see the solid arrow in Figure 1b). The lowest phase transition temperature of NDI-ID (294 °C) is remarkably higher than that of PCBM (250 °C,

represented by the vertical dotted line in Figure 1b)  $  ^{[35]}  $  and is much higher than the transition temperatures of NDI derivatives with alkyl groups (e.g., there are three phase transitions at 109, 144, and 162 °C for an NDI derivative with tridecanyl alkyl groups).  $  ^{[43]}  $  Therefore, NDI-ID exhibiting high thermal stability with high phase transition temperature might be beneficial to the long-term temporal stability of PSCs.

The solubility of ETMs is an important parameter for the fabrication of solution-processable PSCs. Although NDI-ID exhibits high thermal stability, we observe moderate solubility of NDI-ID in various organic solvents; 5.8, 2.3, 1.5, and  $ 0.8\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  for methylene chloride, dichloroethane, 1,2-dichlorobenzene (1,2-DCB), and  $ N,N $ -dimethylformamide (DMF) at room temperature. The solubility of NDI-ID is lower than that of PCBM, but high enough for the fabrication of solution-processable PSCs and thin film transistors (see the Supporting Information). Note that for the fabrication of inverted p-i-n PSCs in this work, 1,2-DCB is used for a solvent of NDI-ID ETMs. A suitable solubility of NDI-ID for solution-processable PSCs is attributed to introducing partially flexible characteristics of an alicyclic cyclopentene group in indane groups.

Figure 1c shows absorption spectra of NDI-ID in solution and the crystalline state. The wavelengths of absorption maximum of NDI-ID in solution are 362 and 382 nm, which are practically identical to the values for NDI derivatives with N-substituted alkyl groups. Therefore, introducing indane groups with conformational rigidity does not affect the electronic states of the NDI cores. In the crystalline state, the wavelengths of absorption maximum of NDI-ID are slightly redshifted to 366 and 386 nm. This may be related to intermolecular interactions with the J-aggregation of NDI cores in the crystalline state, which is discussed as follows.

The electronic characteristics, including electron mobility, and other physical properties of NDI-ID are strongly affected by molecular ordering of NDI semiconducting cores and intermolecular interactions in the crystalline state. Figure 2a shows a molecular packing diagram of NDI-ID crystals projected along the crystallographic b-axis. The details of single crystal structure analysis of NDI-ID are described in the Supporting Information. In NDI-ID crystals, NDI cores are aligned with a herring-bone packing motif (see Figure 2b). NDI cores form J-aggregated face-to-face   $  \pi-\pi  $   interactions with an interplanar distance of 3.3 Å as shown in Figure 2c. Edge-to-face   $  \pi-\pi  $   interactions with a distance of 2.6 Å are shown in Figure 2d. In addition, NDI cores also form strong hydrogen bonds (C=O  $  \cdots  $  H) with the protons on the aromatic ring and cyclopentene ring with a distance of about 2.6 Å (Figure 2a). High electron mobility in NDI-ID can be expected due to the relatively short distances of face-to-face and edge-to-face   $  \pi-\pi  $   interactions between NDI cores (see Hirshfeld surface analysis  $  ^{[50-53]}  $   in Figure 2c,d, respectively).

In NDI-ID, NDI cores form strong hydrogen bonds (C=O  $  \cdots  $  H) with a distance of about 2.6 Å (Figure 2a and Figure S4 in the Supporting Information) with face-to-face and edge-to-face   $  \pi-\pi  $   interactions (Figure 2c,d). NDI-ID exhibits larger contribution from strong hydrogen bonds (C=O  $  \cdots  $  H) with 17.7% O  $  \cdots  $  H atoms contact compared to only 2.6% in PCBM.  $  ^{[26]}  $

The void morphology  $  ^{[63]}  $  of NDI-ID and PCBM in the solid state is remarkably different. In Figure 4d, the red and blue dotted areas indicate the nonvoid areas of NDI-ID in the solid state, which correspond to face-to-face  $  \pi-\pi  $  stacking areas of NDI cores and the occupied volume by indane groups, respectively. NDI-ID shows a 2D void morphology, while PCBM shows a 3D void morphology.  $  ^{[26]}  $  Therefore, PCBM molecules in the solid state show isotropic migration tendency in all directions. In contrast, the migration of NDI-ID molecules is limited to the nonvoid areas (i.e., the red and blue dotted areas in Figure 4d). The migration tendency of NDI-ID molecules is strictly hindered by the strong supramolecular interactions discussed above.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we have developed a new nonfullerene electron transporting material based on NDI-ID for high-performance, low-temperature solution-processable PSCs showing simultaneously high PCE up to 20.2% (average PCE of   $  18.02\% \pm 1.25\%  $  ) and long-term temporal stability. The NDI-ID-based PSCs showed 90% of their initial PCE after 500 h under 100 °C without encapsulation. Therefore, NDI-ID is a potential ETM for highly efficient and highly stable PSCs, as well as other electronic devices. In addition, introducing an indane group having simultaneous alicyclic and aromatic characteristics into organic semiconducting materials is a good design strategy for achieving both low-temperature solution processability and high temporal stability of various electronic devices. For applying to various electronic applications with NDI-based ETMs, enhanced solubility in various-type solvents (e.g., halogenated and nonhalogenated solvents and polar and nonpolar

solvents) and enhanced thermal stability is more beneficial. Therefore, our future work will develop new NDI-based ETMs by considering the substituent's characteristics including space filling characteristics, conformational rigidity, configurational symmetry, and secondary-bond formation ability with solvent molecules.
