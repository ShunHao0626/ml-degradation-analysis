# 1. Introduction

Metal halide perovskite has attracted extensive attention in both academic and industry communities as a novel photovoltaic material, ascribed to its outstanding optoelectronic properties, including benign defects, high absorption coefficient, tunable direct bandgaps, low exciton binding energy and long charge carrier diffusion length, which contribute an unparalleled power conversion efficiency (PCE) increase for the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) from 3.8% to 25.5% since 2009.  $  ^{[1-4]}  $   Despite the outstanding performance, the sensitivity of perovskite materials to atmospheric moisture still remains a major barrier for the commercialization of PSCs. Humid air would induce rapid degradation of perovskite due to the strong adhesion between moisture and organic cations in the structure, leading to the hydration of perovskite and the consequent formation of degraded product PbI  $  _{2}  $  .  $  ^{[5,6]}  $   Besides, the presence of moisture would also accelerate the degradation process of fully inorganic

perovskite   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   (X = I, Br) under prolonged exposure to high humidity environment.  $  ^{[7]}  $   Therefore, to diminish the effect of moisture on the PCE and operational lifetime of PSCs, the overwhelming majority of hybrid perovskite materials currently in research laboratories are deposited in an inert atmosphere with relative humidity (RH) level less than 1 part-per-million (ppm) or ambient conditions controlled at a low humidity level (RH <30%).  $  ^{[8-12]}  $

However, extensive studies have validated that humidity condition is not always detrimental to the performance of PSCs. It was reported that a proper amount of water molecules can facilitate the nucleation and crystallization of the perovskite material, resulting in the optimization of perovskite film quality with improved crystallinity and photovoltaic performances.  $  ^{[13-17]}  $   Furthermore, it was indicated that a proper humidity level can result in perovskite films with larger and more uniform crystal grains in comparison to the films prepared under inert and dry atmosphere, which lead to much reduced defect density and prolonged operational lifetime of PSCs.  $  ^{[13,18]}  $   To gain moisture resistance during processing under ambient conditions, strategies including adding hydrophobic additives into perovskite precursor, preheating substrates and inert gas flowing have been developed, which demonstrate an appealing step forward towards large-scale industrial deployment.  $  ^{[19]}  $   More detailed analysis illustrates that the formation of perovskite intermediate phases during spin coating and nucleation processes is very sensitive to water molecules in a humid atmosphere, which would influence the crystal growth and result in a nonuniform morphology. To produce highly crystallized and pinhole-free perovskite films in a high humidity environment, it is essential to reduce the exposure time to the high humidity during the nucleation process, and speed up the formation of the intermediate phase.  $  ^{[19,20]}  $

Recently, the emergence of 2D layered perovskite materials has brought more desired moisture stability compared to 3D counterparts, which can be attributed to the hydrophobic nature of the bulky molecule ligands and ameliorated ion migration in the sandwich

structure.  $  ^{[21]}  $   The layered perovskite features a generic chemical formula of   $  A_{2}^{\prime}A_{n-1}M_{n}X_{3n+1}  $   or   $  A^{\prime\prime}A_{n-1}M_{n}X_{3n+1}  $  , named as Ruddlesden Popper (RP) or Dion-Jacobson (DJ) phase, where   $  A^{\prime}  $   and   $  A^{\prime\prime}  $   are monoammonium and diammonium organic cation spacers, respectively.  $  ^{[22]}  $   Extensive attention has been paid to study the crystallization and degradation behaviors of 3D perovskite materials under different humidity conditions. However, the exact role of humidity condition on the crystallization, phase transformation, charge dynamics and the final photovoltaic performances of layered 2D perovskite has not been investigated yet despite its outstanding moisture stability.

Herein, we systematically investigate the effect of moisture on the film quality of the BDAFA $ _{n-1} $ Pb $ _n $ I $ _{3n+1} $  DJ perovskites and the photovoltaic devices. The crystallization process under a high humidity level condition suggests the water molecule can facilitate the crystallization process and suppress the formation of impurities in the film composition. Transient absorption (TA) spectra together with photoluminescence (PL) results demonstrate that the BDAFA $ _{n-1} $ Pb $ _n $ I $ _{3n+1} $  DJ thin film annealed under high humidity condition possesses remarkably reduced trap concentration, leading to a fast charge transfer between different- $ n $  DJ phases and enhanced photoelectronic properties. The DJ structure with the assistance of moisture finally achieves a PCE value as high as  $ 17.17\% $  and demonstrates surprisingly high humidity-, thermal-, and photo-stability properties. Further first-principle calculation demonstrates that water molecules can occupy the iodide vacancies through a strong bonding with Pb ions, leading to enhanced interaction between adjacent inorganic layers, improved photoelectronic and stability properties. Therefore, the work shows that an appropriate moisture exposure of the DJ layered perovskite during crystallization process greatly influences the photovoltaic performances, which can be further explored to other reduced-layered perovskite analogues.

# 2. Results and Discussions

# 2.1. Enhanced orientation in high humidity condition

Through a delicate optimization of the perovskite film fabrication process, a stepwise annealing procedure with appropriate processing time in nitrogen and high humidity air is developed for the DJ thin films. The schematic fabrication procedure of perovskite films with the assistance of moisture is illustrated in Figure 1A. The high humidity environment would lead to the formation of fibrillary intermediates during the nucleation process and reduce the film uniformity due to the strong bonding between water and the solvent molecule.[23] Therefore, to reduce the influence of water molecule on the perovskite precursor and intermediate phases, the spin-coating process is conducted under  $ \mathrm{N}_2 $  and followed by a preheating process, which can help to prevent excessive adhesion of water molecules on the film surface. The perovskite films are transferred to the ambient environment with a high humidity condition ( $ \mathrm{RH} = 65\pm 5\% $ ) for a short-time further annealing of  $ 3\mathrm{min} $  to control the water molecule concentration, prevent the degradation and phase transition process under humidity. The films are covered with a petri dish and further annealed to complete the crystallization process. For the thin film without the preheating step, the film has a poor coverage and high impurity concentration including  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and  $ \delta $ -FAPbI₃, and small- $ n $  DJ phases as shown in Figure S1, which is attributed to adhesion of the excessive water molecules induced decomposition.

The influence of moisture exposure on the crystallization behavior and phase composition of BDAFA $ _{n-1} $ Pb $ _n $ I $ _{3n+1} $  DJ perovskite films with  $ n = 4 $  and 6 is shown in Figure 1B, C. For the pristine BDAFA $ _3 $ Pb $ _4 $ I $ _{13} $  film annealed under N $ _2 $  atmosphere, the typical characteristic peaks of 2D perovskite phases are absent from the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns and there are two main diffraction peaks located at 11.7° and 13.9°, representing the yellow FAPbI $ _3 $  phase and (111) crystallographic planes of large- $ n $  ( $ n = \infty $ ) DJ perovskite phase, respectively. # 2.2. Amelioration of the optoelectronic properties

UV-vis absorption and steady-state PL characterizations were conducted to investigate the optical properties of the DJ perovskite films prepared under   $  N_{2}  $   and high humidity condition, respectively. As shown in Figure 2A, there are several excitonic absorption peaks located at 587, 642 and 685 nm, corresponding to n = 2, 3 and 4 BDAFA  $  _{n-1}  $  Pb  $  _{n}  $  I  $  _{3n+1}  $   phases, respectively.  $  ^{[25]}  $   Besides, the absorption edge is extended to long wavelength of around 800 nm, which indicates the formation of large-n (  $  n = \infty  $  ) DJ phase in both the films annealed under   $  N_{2}  $   and humidity condition. This observation suggests the coexistence of small-n and large-n (  $  n = \infty  $  ) DJ phases in the BDAFA  $  _{n-1}  $  Pb  $  _{n}  $  I  $  _{3n+1}  $   perovskite films. From the PL spectra in Figure 2B, the n = 4 and 6 DJ thin films annealed under   $  N_{2}  $   show a PL peak at 791 nm with a FWHM value over 65 nm. The large FWHM and asymmetric peak are attributed to a collective radiative recombination of DJ phases with different n values in the film. On the contrary, there is only one dominant PL peak centered at 799 nm for the HAAP samples, of which the intensity is almost doubled compared to the pristine one. From the absorption curves, the phase composition and absorption intensity of the thin films processed under different conditions do not show much difference as shown in Figure 2A, so the higher PL intensity and much smaller FWHM of the HAAP DJ films can be attributed to the optimized carrier dynamics and reduced nonradiative recombination centers.  $  ^{[26]}  $   Furthermore, the dark current density voltage (J-V) curves of electron only devices with structure of FTO/SnO  $  _{2}  $  /perovskite/PCBM/Au as displayed in Figure 2C, D, were employed to estimate the trap density of the n = 6 DJ thin films. The HAAP film demonstrates a much lower bulk trap states based on the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) theory. The trap density can be calculated from the trap-filled limit voltage   $  V_{TFL} = en_{t}L^{2} / 2\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}  $  , where e is elementary charge, L is the thickness of the perovskite film (~500 nm),   $  n_{t}  $   is the trap density,   $  \varepsilon  $   and   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   are the vacuum permittivity and relative dielectric constant of perovskite material.  $  ^{[27]}  $   In the trap-

# 2.3. Carrier transfer dynamics

Furthermore, to investigate the phase composition and analyze the relation between optoelectronic properties and carrier transfer dynamics in the DJ thin films, transient absorption (TA) spectroscopy is conducted under a  $ 400\mathrm{nm} $  laser pump excitation. TA spectra of the pristine  $ n = 6 $  DJ thin film as shown in Figure 3A demonstrate that there are several negative bleaching and positive excited absorption peaks. The negative peaks at  $ 583~\mathrm{nm} $ , 638 nm and  $ 679~\mathrm{nm} $  correspond to the transient luminescence of  $ n = 2 $ , 3 and 4 phases, respectively, which are close to the excitonic absorption peak positions for the DJ phases in the steady-state absorption curve shown in Figure 2A. A fast build-up of the bleaching peaks at  $ 583~\mathrm{nm} $  (0.46 ps) and  $ 638~\mathrm{nm} $  (0.96 ps) indicates the excitons are firstly generated in the  $ n = 2 $  and 3 phases after excitation. TA spectra at different time scales demonstrate that the carriers are gradually transferred from small-  $ n $  phases to large-  $ n $  ones. The fitted carrier kinetics at different wavelengths in Figure 3B shows ultrafast dominant decay times of 0.46 ps and 0.96 ps for the  $ n = 2 $  and 3 phases, respectively. However, the photobleaching (PB) at  $ 679~\mathrm{nm} $  ( $ n = 4 $ ) shows a much slower decay time of 9.21 ps, which is much longer than the rising time (0.54 ps) of the PB peak at  $ 770~\mathrm{nm} $ . # 2.5 Theoretical calculation

To gain a molecular insight about the interaction between water molecule and  $ \mathrm{BDAFA}_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_n\mathrm{I}_{3n+1} $  DJ perovskite structure, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were employed to investigate the structure stability of the perovskite crystal before and after water molecule incorporation. The dissociation energy of the  $ \mathrm{BDA}^{2+} $  molecule in the DJ perovskite supercell can be an important indicator for the crystal structure stability, which can be calculated from  $ E_{\text{dissociation}} = E_{\text{remain}} + E_{\text{molecule}} - E_{\text{supercell}} $ , where  $ E_{\text{supercell}}, E_{\text{remain}} $  and  $ E_{\text{molecule}} $  present the energy of the pristine perovskite supercell, the remained perovskite structure after dissociation and dissociated molecule ( $ \mathrm{BDA}^{2+} $ ), respectively. The DJ perovskite structure with iodide vacancy is schematically shown in Figure 6A1 and the calculated  $ E_{\text{dissociation}} $  of BDA molecule from the supercell is  $ 3.7\mathrm{eV} $ . Figure 6A2-A4 and Figure S12 display the perovskite supercell with different amount of water molecule incorporation, which can be used to present the exposure time to humidity condition during annealing process. The water molecule tends to occupy and passivate the iodide vacancy in the first place, and the excess molecules would bond with  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  or  $ \mathrm{FA}^+ $  cations, or form hydrogen bonding with iodide ion during the structure relaxation as

It reaches to the maximum with four H $ _2 $ O incorporation into the DJ perovskite supercell and then starts to decrease again with further increase of H $ _2 $ O concentration, which suggests a more stable DJ perovskite structure with a certain amount of H $ _2 $ O adhesion. Therefore, the DFT calculation result reaches to the same conclusion with the experimental observation, which demonstrates an appropriate amount of water molecule adhesion during annealing process would remarkably reduce the defect concentration and suppress the BDAFA $ _{n-1} $ Pb $ _n $ I $ _{3n+1} $  perovskite segregated into different phases. To further study phase stability property of the HAAP DJ thin film, the ion migration behavior in the perovskite structure is evaluated through characterizing the temperature-dependent conductivity of BDAFA $ _5 $ Pb $ _6 $ I $ _{19} $  perovskite films with a lateral conduction configuration and temperature ranging from 183 K to 373 K as summarized in Figure 6C. The  $ E_a $  value of HAAP BDAFA $ _5 $ Pb $ _6 $ I $ _{19} $  perovskite film is estimated to be 0.22 eV, which is much larger than that of the control sample (0.11 eV). # 3. Conclusion

The impact of moisture exposure during annealing process on the crystallization, charge dynamics, phase transformation, photoelectronic and stability properties of the 2D/3D multidimensional DJ perovskites is systematically investigated. XRD, GIWAXS results together with TA characterization suggest that humidity exposure during annealing process leads to suppressed impurity phases, enhanced crystallinity of the 3D/large-n (n=∞) phase in the film, more efficient carrier transfer between different-n phases upon photoexcitation, which would ultimately optimize the photoelectric property of the DJ perovskite film. The DJ PSCs with humidity-assisted annealing demonstrate highly reproducible photovoltaic performances and can be further applied to optimize the industrialization process of PSCs. The champion device based on HAAP BDAFA $ _{5} $ Pb $ _{6} $ I $ _{19} $  achieves the highest PCE value of 17.17%, while the one of control device is only 9.92%. Besides, the humidity-, thermal- and photo-stability properties of both the films and photovoltaic devices have been significantly boosted. Experimental observation and theoretical modelling results suggest that the water molecules tend to occupy the iodide vacancies through strong bonding with Pb $ ^{2+} $  ions, which suppress ion migration, reduce trap centers and increase the decomposition energy of the DJ phases, accounting for the excellent optoelectronic properties and remarkably stable phase structure. The appropriate exposure to moisture during annealing process has great potential in optimizing photovoltaic performances and elongating operation lifetime of layered PSCs.

Materials: Lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ ), 1,4-butanediammonium iodide (BDAI $ _2 $ ) (99.5%), and spiro-OMeTAD were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Formamidinium iodide (FAI) and Methylammonium chloride (MACl) were bought from Greatcell Solar Materials Pty Ltd. N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, Sigma-Aldrich, anhydrous, 99.8%), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, Sigma-Aldrich, 99%), 4-tert-butylpyridine (4-tBP, Sigma-Aldrich, 96%), chlorobenzene (CB, Sigma-Aldrich, 99.5%), acetonitrile (ACN, Sigma-Aldrich, anhydrous, 99.8%), Li-TFSI (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.95%), and all other chemicals were used as received without further purification.

Thin-Film and Solar Cell Fabrication: Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)-coated glass was sequentially cleaned with deionized water, acetone, isopropanol, and ethanol for 30 min, respectively. Before film deposition, all FTO substrates were dried with a nitrogen gun and cleaned by UV ozone for 15 min. For the deposition of the electron-transporting layer,   $  SnO_{2}  $   film was prepared by spin-coating precursor solution of   $  SnO_{2}  $   quantum dots (0.15 M) on FTO substrate at 500 rpm for 3 s and 3000 rpm for 30 s. The thin films were annealed at 190 °C for 1 h in air, followed by the treatment of UV ozone for 15 min.

The DJ perovskite precursor with a concentration of 0.9 M was prepared by dissolving BDADI, FAI and PbI $ _2 $  by the molar ratio of 1: x-1: x in the mixed solvent with volume ratio of DMF: DMSO=4:1. Additional 5 mol% MACl was added in perovskite precursor. The precursor was spin-coated on the SnO $ _2 $  layer at 6000 rpm for 30 s. During the spin coating process, 120 μL chlorobenzene was dropped at 10 s before the spin process ended. A stepwise annealing process is applied to prepare perovskite films. The control samples were first annealed at 100 °C for 5 min (preheating process), followed by 160 °C for 50 min in N $ _2 $  glovebox. For the humidity-assisted samples, the perovskite films were transferred from the glovebox to the ambient air after the preheating process, annealed on 160 °C hot plate in air
