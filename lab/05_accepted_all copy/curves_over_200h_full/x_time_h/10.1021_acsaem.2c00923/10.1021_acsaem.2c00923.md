# Multifunctional Polymer Capping Frameworks Enable High-Efficiency and Stable All-Inorganic Perovskite Solar Cells

Fangxuan Yi, Qiyao Guo,  $  ^{*}  $   Dengduan Zheng, Rong Zhuang, Junshuai Zhang, Qunwei Tang, and Jialong Duan  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: All-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have shown great potential to balance the efficiency and stability of traditional organic–inorganic hybrid species. However, arising from the rapid crystal nucleation rate and soft lattice of perovskite crystals, solution-processed film fabrication technology generally leads to morphologic flaws, including defective grain boundaries (GBs) and surfaces, which in turn accelerate perovskite phase decomposition upon persistent stimuli and then reduce the photovoltaic performance of PSCs. Herein, polymeric poly(vinylpyrrolidone) (PVP) with enormous functional carbon–oxygen groups is introduced into the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   precursor as a multifunctional polymer capping framework to effectively improve

the film quality and heal the lattice defects (uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $  ). By carefully maximizing the film crystallization and grain coverage, a significantly enhanced PCE of 10.54% coupled with the remarkably enhanced stability under air (  $  \leq20\%  $   relative humidity, >99% retention for 100 days) for a carbon-electrode-based device is achieved. This work paves a comprehensive promotion strategy for the development of perovskite-based optoelectronics.

KEYWORDS:   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite solar cells, polymer capping framework, defect passivation, grain growth template, long-term stability

# 1. INTRODUCTION

As an emerging photovoltaic technology, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted enormous attention owing to their excellent optoelectronic properties such as high absorption coefficient, tunable band gap, long carrier diffusion length, and simple processability.  $  ^{1-4}  $   In the last decade, the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of single-junction PSCs to date has been up to 25.7%,  $  ^{5-10}  $   which is comparable to that of commercial silicon-based solar cells. Despite substantial progress achieved, organic-inorganic hybrid PSCs always suffer from moisture and thermal instability owing to the hygroscopicity and volatilization of organic components (e.g.,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}^{+}  $  or   $  MA^{+}  $  ;   $  \mathrm{CH(NH_{2})_{2}^{+}}  $  or   $  FA^{+}  $  ), which is regarded as the main obstacle for further development and large-scale production.  $  ^{11-14}  $   By substituting the organic cations with cesium ion (  $  Cs^{+}  $  ), an all-inorganic halide perovskite is proven to be an optimal alternative upon considering its native inorganic structure stability, comparable PCE, and preference for assembly of tandem solar cells because of the suitable wide band gap.  $  ^{15-18}  $

Among the various components, cubic-phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   with a favorable band gap of 1.73 eV is regarded hopefully as all-inorganic perovskite material with a considerable PCE up to 21%.  $  ^{19-22}  $   However, similar to organic species, there is also a rapid phase conversion from a photoactive cubic phase to a

# 2. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

2.1. Mechanistic Study of the PCF-Induced Grain Growth and Defect Passivation. As shown in Figure 1a, the electrostatic potential distribution of the monomer (N-vinyl-2-pyrrolidone, NPV) of PVP is calculated, in which the strong polar -C=O groups will withdraw intramolecular electrons closer to them.  $  ^{24,63}  $   In this fashion, the electron cloudy density around -C=O groups increases. When contacting the undercoordinated Pb  $  ^{2+}  $  , the electrons around -C=O will

transfer to undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $  , benefiting PVP as a competitive additive to interact with the perovskite precursor components and thereby stabilize the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   soft lattice structure based on the Lewis acid–base interaction.  $  ^{64}  $   In detail, the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   precursor initiates to assemble and bond to form a distorted metastable state with numerous coordination centers in PVP chains (Figure 1b). In the following annealing treatment, more nuclei of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   are promptly launched with the precursor ion diffusion and spaced uniformly under the guidance of PVP chains (Figure 1c). For the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   nanocrystal growth and phase transition, long-chain PVP molecules with negatively charged -C=O sites strongly anchor at the positively charged uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   defects for cubic   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   lattice consolidation (Figure 1d). As evidenced by Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), the main characteristic peaks at around 1288, 1421, and 1663 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   detected for pure PVP are attributed to the typical functional groups of C–N, C–H, and C=O stretching vibration, respectively (Figure 1e).  $  ^{58,65}  $   Upon mixing the perovskite with PVP, there is an evident red shift to 1278 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for the C–N stretching and 1650 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for the -C=O stretching, indicating the formation of a coordinate bond via the electron donor (-C=O)-acceptor (  $  Pb^{2+}  $  ) pair (i.e., Lewis acid–base adduct) between PVP and the perovskite.  $  ^{24,58}  $   A similar binding energy variation of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   surface elements can be also found in the XPS measurement shown in Figures 1f and S1. The detection of the N 1s signal in the PCF-tailored perovskite film confirms the existence of PVP. In fact, the same phenomenon has been also reported by doping PVP into the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite film, which was annealed at a much higher temperature of 300 °C.  $  ^{24}  $

CsPbIBr $ _2 $  films tailored with different PVP concentrations are further fabricated on a compact-TiO $ _2 $  (c-TiO $ _2 $ ) layer via a two-step thermal treatment process (see Section 4), and the corresponding morphologies are recorded by field-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and shown in Figures 1g and S2. The PCF-modified perovskite layers become flat and homogeneous at low PCF concentrations (0–0.7 wt % PVP is denoted as control, PCF-1, PCF-3, PCF-5, and PCF-7), which is attributed to the template effect of PCF during CsPbIBr $ _2 $  crystallization. By further increasing the PCF concentration to PCF-5 and PCF-7, the viscosity of the perovskite precursor is significantly increased. As a result, the perovskite film suffers from large voids with inferior coverage, which is mainly attributed to the solvent edge effect $ ^{66,67} $  and the long-chain-induced steric hindrance effect. $ ^{68,69} $  When assembling into solar cells, these voids inevitably lead to the increased leakage current, thereby degrading the charge extraction. From the characteristic X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns presented in Figure S3a, all samples exhibit the diffraction peaks at 15.0 and 30.2° corresponding to the (100) and (200) crystal planes of the cubic CsPbIBr $ _2 $  perovskite structure. The diffraction intensity of the PCF-tailored perovskite film increases gradually as compared with the perovskite film without PCF, followed by a distinct decrease in intensity at PCF-5. To further confirm the crystallization evolution, the full width at half-maximum (FWHM) values of characteristic peaks of (100) and (200) planes are extracted from XRD patterns. As shown in Figure S3b, the significantly reduced value after PVP incorporation at low PVP concentrations and increased value at high PVP concentrations undoubtedly demonstrate the above conclusion, $ ^{70} $  which is consistent with the results from SEM images.

2.2. Device Photovoltaic Performance. We subsequently fabricated carbon-electrode-based PSCs to study the photovoltaic performance evolution by incorporating PVP into perovskite films, and the schematic device structure is shown in Figure 2a. From the cross-sectional SEM images of the prepared devices shown in Figure 2b, the control film is obviously untidy and defective, which will in turn accelerate the formation of decomposed species. On the contrary, the PCF-tailored film presents a dense feature without intuitionally aggregated polymer domains, suggesting a possibility of the polymer being arrayed into the intergranular boundaries during the nucleation and growth of  $ \mathrm{CsPbIBr_2} $  grains as an ultrathin

coating layer on the grain surface to hinder the diffusion of ions and moisture. In this fashion, the defects located at the perovskite GBs are primarily passivated to enhance the photogenerated carrier collection. It should be noted that the thickness of the carbon electrode is around 10   $  \mu  $  m to ensure excellent electronic conductivity (Figure S4). Next, the photovoltaic performance of PSC based on PCF is characterized, and the statistical photovoltaic parameters from 13 individual devices are summarized in Figure S5 and Table 1. As the PCF concentration gradually increases from 0 to 0.3 wt %, all of the photovoltaic parameters simultaneously increase, showing a 22.89% enhancement in the overall average PCE (10.47 vs 8.52%) compared to the control devices, which is mainly attributed to the 7.32% enhancement in the average open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) (1.319 vs 1.229 V), 4.81% enhancement in the average circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) (10.89 vs 10.39 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ), and 9.39% enhancement in the average fill factor (FF) (72.93 vs 66.67%).

Figure 2c displays the photocurrent density–voltage (J-V) curves of the control and PCF-tailored PSCs recorded under one standard sun irradiation, demonstrating that the optimal PVP concentration is 0.3 wt % with a significantly improved PCE of 10.54%, which is one cutting-edge value for the carbon-electrode-based   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSC reported previously (Table S1). As shown in Figure 2d, the increased   $  J_{SC}  $   of 10.94 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   is confirmed by the improved incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) in the longer-wavelength region of 450–550 nm. Given the nearly identical optical absorption edges between the control and the PCF-tailored perovskites (Figures 2e and S6), the improved IPCE demonstrates the improvement of the perovskite quality with reduced defects and suppressed nonradiative recombination.  $  ^{71,72}  $   Overall, the target device exhibits a higher integrated   $  J_{SC}  $   value than that of the control one, which is largely consistent with the results from the J-V curves. Moreover, dramatically increased   $  V_{OC}  $   (1.320 V) and FF (73.04%) are important ingredients for PCE increment, which are generally ascribed to the suppressed nonradiative recombination revealed by the steady-state photoluminescence (Figures 2e and S7). The unchanged PL peak position highly agrees with the absorption spectra, indicating that the PCF passivation via the Lewis acid–base interaction will not alter the optical band gap of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskites (Figure S8). In general, there is always a hysteresis for PSC because of the presence of defects. Although the incorporation of PVP into the perovskite film can reduce the hysteresis effect of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSC (Figure S9), a PCE deviation still blurs the performance validity. 2.3. Carrier Dynamics. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was conducted to quantify the parameters of charge transport and recombination. Figures 3a and S11 show the equivalent circuit and Nyquist plots conducted at a bias of 1.0 V close to the   $  V_{OC}  $   under dark conditions, consisting of the series resistance (  $  R_{s}  $  ) and recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ), and the fitted parameters are listed in Table 1. Obviously, the   $  R_{rec}  $   first increases and reaches the maximum value for PCF-3-incorporated PSC and then decreases by further increasing the PVP dosage, indicating the suppressed nonradiative recombination owing to the reduced defect. This can be also cross-checked by time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements, as shown in Figures 3b and S12. The spectra were fitted by a biexponential decay model:   $  I = A_{1} e^{-(t-t_{0})/\tau_{1}} + A_{2} e^{-(t-t_{0})/\tau_{2}}  $  , where   $  \tau_{1}  $   refers to the faster component of trap-mediated nonradiative recombination and   $  \tau_{2}  $   is the slower component assigned to bimolecular radiative recombination in the bulk crystals.  $  ^{73,74}  $   As summarized in Table S2, the average

PL lifetime (  $  \tau_{ave}  $  ) increases from 78.77 ns for the control film to 180.77 ns for the PCF-3-tailored film, validating a reduced defect density enabled by the effective PCF passivation at perovskite GBs. The charge carrier lifetime can be further reflected via the open-circuit voltage decay (OCVD) measurement. As shown in Figure S13, a slower   $  V_{OC}  $   decay behavior for the PCF-3-tailored device further confirms a longer carrier lifetime (Figure 3c) and a substantially retarded carrier recombination process, agreeing well with the results of PL and TRPL characterizations. Furthermore, the built-in potential (  $  V_{bi}  $  ) was extracted by the classical Mott–Schottky (M–S) relation under dark conditions, as shown in Figure 3d. The PCF-3 device delivers a larger   $  V_{bi}  $   of 1.436 V than that of the control device (1.292 V), accompanied by a larger slope (dashed lines), evidently demonstrating the increased driving force to separate photogenerated carriers and extended depletion region owing to the inversely proportional relationship of the interfacial charge density to the slope of the M–S plot,  $  ^{75}  $   which in turn delivers an improved   $  V_{OC}  $   due to the accelerated charge transfer from the perovskite layer to the electrode of the PCF-tailored device.

To further understand the charge extraction/recombination kinetics, the dependence of   $  V_{OC}  $   and   $  J_{SC}  $   on the light intensity (I) for the target and control devices is illustrated in Figures 3e and S14. The PCF-tailored device exhibits a smaller slope of 1.16 kT/q than that of the pristine device (1.64 kT/q), undoubtedly indicating the suppressed nonradiative recombination. In

To quantify the trap density (  $  n_{trap}  $  ) and photogenerated electron mobility (  $  \mu  $  ) of the perovskite films, as shown in Figure 3f, the dark J-V curves of electron-only devices (FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}/CsPbIBr_{2}/PC_{61}BM/Ag  $  ) were recorded under dark condition based on space-charge-limited current (SCLC) model. The voltage between the Ohmic contact and trap-filled region is known as the trap-filled limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ), which is determined by the following equation:  $  ^{16,79}  $     $  V_{TFL} = qn_{trap}L^{2}/2\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}  $  , where L is the film thickness,   $  \varepsilon  $   is the relative dielectric constant, and   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity. The   $  V_{TFL}  $   values for the PCF and control devices are 0.538 and 0.801 V respectively, delivering a 23.58% reduction in the corresponding   $  n_{trap}  $   for the PCF device (  $  8.33 \times 10^{15} cm^{-3}  $  ) than that of the pristine device (  $  1.09 \times 10^{16} cm^{-3}  $  ), which provides strong evidence to confirm that the trap defects have been significantly healed by the PCF. Moreover, at high voltages, the current increases quadratically along with voltage

increment, where the  $ \mu $  can be calculated through the equation:  $ J = 9\varepsilon \varepsilon_0\mu V^2 /8L^3 $ . The  $ \mu $  is improved two times from  $ 1.30\times 10^{-7}\mathrm{cm}^2\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1} $  for the control device to  $ 3.75\times $   $ 10^{-7}\mathrm{cm}^2\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1} $  for the PCF-tailored PSC, which highly agrees well with the improvement of FF and  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ . This conclusion is also demonstrated by dark  $ J - V $  curves of PSCs correlated to the leakage current from carrier recombination in the devices, as shown in Figure S15. According to the diode law that is expressed as  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} = nkT\ln (J_{\mathrm{SC}} / J_0) / q $ , where  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  and  $ J_0 $  are the photogenerated and dark saturation current densities, respectively, the value of  $ J_{0} $  for the PCF-tailored device is estimated to be  $ 1.44\times 10^{-7}\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , which is nearly 2 orders of magnitude lower than that of the pristine device ( $ 6.36\times $   $ 10^{-5}\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ ), demonstrating the reduced leakage current. Meanwhile, both devices exhibit approximate output current in the forward direction, implying a higher rectification ratio and a greatly reduced charge recombination induced by the higher quality of the perovskite along with the trap-state healing closely influenced by the PCF interface.

# 3. CONCLUSIONS

In summary, in this work, PVP has been introduced into a   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   precursor solution as a multifunctional polymer capping framework to form perovskite films via a facile solution process. After systematical investigation, the incorporation of PVP can significantly improve the film quality and chemically consolidate   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   soft lattice structure owing to the interaction between -C=O groups and undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $  . As a result, PVP-tailored carbon-based   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSC delivers an enhanced PCE of 10.54%, which is much higher than the control device with 8.59% efficiency. More importantly, the long-term stability of the optimized PSC is improved with up to 99% PCE retention after 100 days of storage under dark conditions (  $  \leq20\%  $   RH), which provides an alternative and promising strategy to address the main obstacle of moisture instability of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $  -based perovskite devices.

# 4. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

4.1. Materials. Anhydrous isopropyl alcohol (IPA, 99.5%), anhydrous dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.7%), anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.7%), anhydrous chlorobenzene (CB, 99%), diethanol amine (DEA, 99%), and lead bromide (PbBr₂, 99.99%) were obtained from Macklin. Titanium(IV) isopropoxide (99.9%), cesium iodide (CsI, 99.99%), and poly(vinylpyrrolidone) (PVP, M_w = 10,000, K13-18) were purchased from Aladdin. Commercial carbon paste was obtained from Shanghai MaterWin New Materials (MTW-CE-C-003). Deionized water (resistivity > 18 MΩ) was obtained through a Millipore water purification system. Fluorine-doped tin oxide-coated (FTO) substrates with a sheet

resistance of   $  14 \Omega sq^{-1}  $   were purchased from Pilkington. Unless stated otherwise, all materials and reagents were used as received without further purification.

4.2. Fabrication of PSCs. The FTO-coated glass was first etched with the desired pattern via zinc powder and hydrochloric acid; then consecutively washed with detergent, acetone, isopropanol, and deionized water; and finally treated with UV–ozone for 180 s. A compact-  $  TiO_{2}  $  layer (c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) with thickness was prepared by spin-coating an ethanol solution of titanium isopropoxide (0.5 M) and DEA (0.5 M) onto the substrate at 7000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at 500 °C for 30 min. Subsequently, FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  substrates were treated with UV–ozone for another 180 s. Then, 65  $  \mu  $  L of 1.0 M  $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $  precursor containing 367 mg of  $  PbBr_{2}  $ , 260 mg of CsI, 4.9 mg of  $  PbI_{2}  $ , and 0–0.7 wt % PVP (denoted control, PCF-1, PCF-3, PCF-5, and PCF-7) dissolved in 1 mL of DMSO was spin-coated onto the FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  substrate at 1000 rpm for 10 s and then 3000 rpm for 50 s. The film was first placed on a hotplate at 30 °C for 1 min, further annealed at 260 °C for 10 min, and then cooled down to room temperature naturally. Finally, the commercial carbon paste was bladed onto the as-prepared substrate with an active area of 0.09 cm $  ^{2}  $  followed by curing at 100 °C.

4.3. Film Characterizations. The crystal structure of the as-prepared perovskite film was determined by an X-ray diffractometer (XRD, Bruker D8 ADVANCE) using graphite monochromatic copper radiation (  $  \lambda = 1.5406 \AA  $  ). The film morphology was characterized by field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM, SU4800, Hitachi). Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra were recorded by a Thermo Nicolet 6700 spectrophotometer. Surface electronic states were determined using an XPS system (Thermo Scientific, ESCLAB 250XI). All binding energies were referenced to the C 1s peak (284.8 eV). The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra were acquired using a fluorescence spectrophotometer (Lumina, ThermoFisher) equipped with a Xenon lamp at an excitation wavelength of 470 nm. The time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) decay curves were recorded with a Horiba spectrometer excited by a 470 nm laser. The contact angle was obtained by a KRÜSS drop shape analyzer (DSA25E).

4.4. Device Characterizations. The photocurrent density–voltage (J-V) curves of the solar cells were recorded on a Keithley 2420 source-measure unit under irradiation of simulated solar light at an optical intensity of 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  (Newport, Oriel Class A, 91195A, AM 1.5G, calibrated by a standard silicon solar cell). No light soaking or voltage bias was applied before the measurement, and the active area of the cells was defined by a black metal mask with a square aperture of the area of 0.08 cm  $  ^{2}  $ . The incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra were obtained using an IPCE kit from Enli Technology Co., Ltd. equipped with a standard Si crystalline solar cell as a reference. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) spectra, the open-circuit voltage decay (OCVD) curves, and the Mott–Schottky plots were recorded on the CHI660E electrochemical workstation. The moisture and thermal durability under working conditions as well as the long-term stability was measured on the devices without encapsulation.
