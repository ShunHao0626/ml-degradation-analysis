# Tri-Brominated Perovskite Film Management and Multiple-Ionic Defect Passivation for Highly Efficient and Stable Solar Cells

Zekun Gong, Benlin He,* Jingwei Zhu, Xinpeng Yao, Sudong Wang, Haiyan Chen, Yanyan Duan, and Qunwei Tang*

# 1. Introduction

High-quality perovskite films with low imperfections, high hole mobility, and matching energy levels play a crucial role in enhancing performance of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) without hole-transporting materials (HTMs). Herein, it is demonstrated that the incorporation of a stable tetra-bisphenol A (TBBPA) with diphenyl ring, polybromides, and hydroxyl groups additive into a perovskite film can simultaneously manipulate the crystal growth and passivate the defects through coordination interaction between the functional group (—OH, —Br) and the unsaturated halogen and metal ions (Br  $  ^{-}  $  , Cs  $  ^{+}  $  , and Pb  $  ^{2+}  $  ), resulting in a reduced grain boundary as well as imperfection and increased hole mobility of the CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $   perovskite film. In addition, the valence band of a perovskite film with TBBPA additive is shifted upward to approach the work function of the carbon electrode, thereby improving the energy level alignment. Consequently, a significantly boosted charge extraction and reduced charge recombination of the carbon-based HTM-free CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $   PSCs is obtained after incorporating the TBBPA additive, yielding a maximum power conversion efficiency of up to 9.82% of the optimized device. Furthermore, the champion PSC without encapsulation displays a remarkable thermal and moisture stability after being kept in ambient air for 720 h at 85 °C and 85% relative humidity, respectively.

In general, the main reason behind the sluggish efficiency is attributed to the inherent narrow-spectra response ranges of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite and the substantial carrier recombination resulting from multitudinous inevitable imperfections and mismatching energy band alignment within PSCs.  $  ^{[11-13]}  $   Therefore, considerable efforts have been accordingly made to solve the aforementioned issues to attain high efficiency and stability of carbon-based all-inorganic   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   PSCs.

According to previous research, the imperfections in perovskite materials such as undercoordinated halide ions (Br $ ^{-} $ , I $ ^{-} $ ) and metal ions (Pb $ ^{2+} $ ), together with 2D extended defects such as defective surface and grain boundaries, serving as the hole-electron recombination center which severely impede carrier dynamics and remarkably reduce carrier lifetime, thus resulting in inferior photovoltaic performance. $ ^{[14,15]} $  Currently, various effective strategies such as additive engineering, component engineering, and interface engineering have been proposed and proved being capable of attenuating the defects density and carrier recombination rate by precise modulation of the perovskite crystallization kinetics as well as coordinate bonding with uncoordinated ions. $ ^{[16-18]} $  Among the tactics, additive engineering may contain more potential to realize minimized carrier recombination due to its simultaneous function on enlarging perovskite grain size and curing trap states. Actually, during the past years, substantial additives including polymers, $ ^{[19]} $  inorganic salt, $ ^{[20]} $  ionic liquids, $ ^{[21]} $  fullerene derivatives, $ ^{[22]} $  and multifunctional-group organic molecule $ ^{[23]} $  have been applied in effectively healing defects and inhibiting carrier recombination within the perovskite film. For instance, Zhang and co-workers recently introduced a series passivation molecules (PPE, TPN, ABN) with amine and cyano groups into Cs $ _{0.06} $ FA $ _{0.79} $ MA $ _{0.15} $ PbI $ _{2.55} $ Br $ _{0.45} $  precursor solution to enable a remarkably enlarged grain size and highly effective defect passivation, thus inducing an outstanding photovoltaic performance. $ ^{[24]} $  Similarly, Liu et al. used multifunctional passivation molecules (4-fluorobenzamide and 4-chlorobenzamide) to simultaneously facilitate grain growth as well as passivate defects through coordinating metallic Pb $ ^{2+} $  with electron-rich carbonyl and halogen group, significantly propelling the PCE from 19.16% to 21.35%. $ ^{[25]} $  Jin et al. demonstrated that by forming hydrogen bonds with hydroxyl group of butylated hydroxyltoluene, the undercoordinated iodine ions can be anchored, successfully reinforcing the perovskite film quality and thus contributing to a noticeable enhancement in efficiency and stability of MAPbI $ _3 $  PSCs. $ ^{[26]} $  From aforementioned studies, it is easy to find that the introduction of substances with electron-donor groups such as halogen and carbonyl as well as strong polar bonds containing hydrogen such as hydroxyl and amino can effectively ameliorate the crystalline quality of perovskite film and annihilate the bulk defects to inhibit carrier recombination for enhanced photovoltaic performance of PSCs.

Hence, a stable organic molecule with multiple bromine and hydroxyl groups called tetra-bromo-bisphenol A (TBBPA) is utilized to modulate the perovskite crystal and passivate trap states due to the following reasons: 1) the multiple bromine and hydroxyl groups can effectively bond with the uncoordinated ions defect to lessen the trap states density. 2) The material is found that being able to suppress the formation of 2D extended defects, leading to a markedly restrained carrier recombination.

Furthermore, the addition of the multiple functional-groups material simultaneously increases the hole mobility of perovskite film and tunes the electronic property of perovskite to cause an appropriate interfacial energy-band alignment between the perovskite layer and carbon electrode to further boost the carrier separation and transportation. As a result, a champion device with PCE of 9.82%, open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.595 V, short-circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 7.52 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and fill factor (FF) of 81.41% is obtained after TBBPA introduction; its performance is significantly improved compared with that of the original PSCs (PCE = 6.39%,   $  V_{OC}  $   = 1.441 V,   $  J_{SC}  $   = 6.20 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and FF = 71.52%). Impressively, the optimized device displays excellent operational stability, retaining nearly 90% of the initial efficiency after exposing for 720 h in the ambient condition with 85% relative humidity (RH) or at 85 °C. According to these findings, it is revealed that the integration of multiple functional-group compound (TBBPA) with perovskite offers an efficient strategy in imperfections passivation and grain-size enlargement as well as modulating interfacial energy-level alignment to repress the carrier recombination and prolong the carrier lifetime for promoted charge separation and exaction, consequently advancing the photovoltaic performance of the PSCs.

# 2. Results and Discussion

Such high-quality   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite film with substantially enlarged grain and reduced grain boundaries is in favor of suppressing carrier recombination, and thus optimizing the optoelectrical properties of the derived PSCs. Ultraviolet–visible (UV–vis) absorption spectra measurement of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films with and without TBBPA are performed and shown in Figure 3e to explore the potential impact of TBBPA addition on the photon management of   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films. It is noteworthy that all T-  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films show a blue-shift absorption peak and boosted light-absorption intensity compared with the pristine one. The most reasonable explanation behind this result is the enhanced crystallinity of T-  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films as well as the change in dielectric constant on the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   surface resulting from the passivation of the TBBPA additive.  $  ^{[32]}  $   The higher UV–vis absorption of the T-  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films could contribute to boosted photon absorption and photocurrent output and accordingly lead to a considerable improvement in photovoltaic performance for PSCs. Correspondingly, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra of various   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite films deposited directly on bare FTO glass matrix are investigated. In general, perovskite film with a lower defect-state density can enable more photogenerated electrons and holes combining to emit fluorescence and thus leading to a higher PL intensity.  $  ^{[33]}  $   As shown in Figure 3f, the evidently increased PL intensity of T-  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film in contrast to that of the pristine one indicates the fewer trap-state density within T-  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   film due to the enlargement of grain size and the passivation of defects after TBBPA addition. In addition, a blue-shift can be observed for the peak of T-  $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   films, which is in accordance with the UV–vis absorption spectra, also corroborating the passivation of TBBPA toward the perovskite film imperfections.

The PSCs with a multilayer heterojunction architecture of  $ \mathrm{FTO / c - TiO_2 / m - TiO_2 / T - CsPbBr_3 / Carbon} $  have been fabricated and the cross-sectional SEM image for a complete device is measured, as shown in Figure 4a. Such image manifests a  $ 320\mathrm{nm} $  T-CsPbBr₃ perovskite layer closely pinched between c-  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / m - TiO_2} $  and carbon electrode layer. In addition, all structural layers are perpendicularly packed without any clear cracks and pinholes, which is facilitative for the vertical photogenerated

carrier migration, thus substantially depressing carrier recombination and enhancing the holistic photovoltaic performance. This result is also decisive for the realization of an excellent electron-blocking property with superior hole extraction ability and accordingly achieving simultaneous improvement in PCE and  $ V_{\text{OC}} $  output.[36-39] Under an illumination of the standard solar simulator (100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $ , AM 1.5G), the characteristic photocurrent density–voltage ( $ J-V $ ) curves of all the as-prepared PSCs are measured and stacked in Figure 4c and the corresponding photovoltaic performance parameters such as  $ V_{\text{OC}} $ ,  $ J_{\text{SC}} $ , FF, and PCE are also summarized in Table 1. The reasonable explanation for the performance advancement can be attributed to the optimized monocrystalline perovskite film and well-matching interfacial energy band alignment between perovskite absorber layer and carbon electrode, which contributes to the ameliorated carrier extraction and transportation. In addition, this dramatically heightened photovoltaic performance is also associated with the mitigated carrier recombination arising from the high-quality T-CsPbBr $ _3 $  film with less grain boundaries and trap states. Furthermore, to affirm the validity of the visibly enhanced device performance, a series of detailed statistical distribution of the photovoltaic parameters for 30 identical PSCs are summarized in Figure S6, Supporting Information. According to the superior photovoltaic parameters along with a narrower statistical distribution, it is easy to identify the universal validity of TBBPA additive for improving devices photovoltaic performance and reliable repeatability. Clearly, the long-term maximum power-point tracking of the modified devices shows a prominent development of photostability compared with the reference one. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, a compound containing diphenyl ring, polybromides and hydroxyl groups, TBBPA, is used as an additive to ameliorate perovskite film quality and energy-level matching at perovskite/carbon interface for HTM-free PSCs. The crystal lattice shrinkage of perovskite and the passivation of —Br as well as —OH groups toward positively charged metal ions and negatively charged bromine ions, respectively, after TBBPA addition make the T-CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $  perovskite film show a stable crystal structure with larger grain size and fewer trap states, which causes a high hole mobility of perovskite and effectively restrains charge recombination. The upshift of VB for T-CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $  perovskite optimizes the energy-level alignment between perovskite photosensitive layer and carbon cathode, resulting in an expeditious charge extraction and separation. Finally, the carbon-based HTM-free PSCs tailored with T-CsPbBr  $  _{3}  $  film acquire a champion PCE as high as 9.82% with remarkable long-term stability after 720 h sustained storage in ambient air with high humidity or high temperature. This work provides an effective choice to construct high-quality perovskite films for high-efficiency and stable PSCs.

The curves of normalized photovoltaic parameters for solar cells stored under different conditions were obtained by measuring the device photovoltaic performance every 120 h. The relative testing details were as follows: the device was initially tested under continuous illumination at  $ V_{MPP} $  for 120 s to obtain the steady-state photocurrent density ( $ J_{MPP} $ ) curve; subsequently, the PCE steady-state output was derived according to the equation:  $ PCE_{MPP} = (V_{MPP} \times J_{MPP}/100) \times 100\% $ . EIS plots were measured in a frequency range of 1 MHz to 0.1 Hz at  $ V_{OC} $  with an amplitude of 10 mV in the dark. The dark J–V curve of the device was characterized in a voltage range of −1.5 to 1.5 V at a scan rate of 10 mV s $ ^{-1} $ . In light of Mott-Gurney law, the trap-state density and hole mobility of perovskite films were performed by fabricating the hole-only device with a configuration of FTO/NiO $ _{x} $ /perovskite with and without TBBPA additive/carbon based on SCLC model from 0 to 7 V in dark. The  $ V_{OC} $  decay curves were measured by illuminating the device under the irradiation of simulated solar-light intensity controlled at AM 1.5G (100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $ ) at open-circuit for a period (2 or 3 s) to achieve the photo stationary state, and then instantaneously switching off the light and covering the device with a shading box. C–V characterizations were performed at a frequency of 5 kHz with an amplitude of 30 mV under dark conditions against different bias voltages.
