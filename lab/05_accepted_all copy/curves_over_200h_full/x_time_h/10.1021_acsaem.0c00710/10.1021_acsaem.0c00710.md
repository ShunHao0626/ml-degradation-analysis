# 1. Introduction

Solar energy is one of the most important renewable energy resources, thus research is focused on photovoltaic devices that can directly convert solar energy into electricity in an effective, eco-friendly and economical way. Among the   $  3^{rd}  $   generation photovoltaic (PV) technologies, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have revolutionized the field since they exceeded 25% power conversion efficiency (PCE) within only a few years of development.  $  ^{1-5}  $   Perovskite absorbers are characterized by near-ideal optoelectronic properties,  $  ^{6}  $   including tunable band gap, high carrier mobility  $  ^{7}  $   and long carrier lifetime,  $  ^{8}  $   enabling solution-processed and low-cost fabrication methods.  $  ^{9}  $

The state-of-the-art PSCs adopt the mesoporous  $  ^{10}  $  scaffold configuration, though the planar  $  ^{11,12}  $  structure is also of interest due to fabrication simplicity and low manufacturing cost. This structure, denoted as n-i-p, consists of a perovskite absorber sandwiched between a compact n-type electron transport layer (ETL) and a p-type hole transporting layer (HTL). The presence of charge selective contacts is crucial since they ensure the transport of the photogenerated electrons and holes in the perovskite absorber towards the anode and cathode electrodes. In general, their role concerns the optimization of the energy barriers necessary for the selective extraction of the corresponding type of charge carriers and the blocking of the opposite as well as the beneficial alteration of physical contact (i.e. reduced hydrophilicity and lower surface roughness) between the electrodes and the corresponding photoactive layer.  $  ^{13}  $  Besides PCE increase, they also affect the overall stability of PSCs, since these devices can also suffer from intrinsic instability and/or degradation of the perovskite layer under external strains such as UV light, thermal stress or electric bias.  $  ^{14}  $

Therefore, the functionality of ETL and HTL is crucial for the device's overall performance. Concerning the ETL, compact titanium dioxide (TiO $ _2 $ ) is among the best performance materials used in planar PSCs, disposing excellent transmittance and energy bands suitable for a large number of perovskite materials, while it can be easily synthesized and deposited by solution-processed methods. $ ^{15} $  Nevertheless, TiO $ _2 $  is also characterized by low conductivity and poor electron mobility, along with many defects (oxygen vacancies and Ti $ ^{+3} $  ions) that are responsible for high carrier recombination rates at surface trap states. $ ^{16-18} $  To confront these issues, various modifications of the TiO $ _2 $  layer have been proposed including element doping $ ^{19-20} $  or bifacial engineering with other electron transport materials, such as PCBM or SnO $ _2 $ . $ ^{21-22} $

In this context, a promising interface engineering approach  $  ^{23-24}  $  is the incorporation of a dye molecule at the ETL/perovskite interface, which can act as electron transport mediator and facilitate the electron extraction at the anode. Following this concept, the modification of the  $  TiO_{2}  $  ETL by chemisorbing the hydrophobic D35 organic dye  $  ^{25}  $  resulted in an overall improvement in PSC device performance and stability attributed to the improved nanomorphology of the photoactive layer and enhanced electron injection rate towards  $  TiO_{2}  $ . Up to date porphyrins have been widely utilized in various energy related applications (i.e. organic solar cells, molecular hydrogen production), due to their unique and easily tuned electrochemical and optical properties.  $  ^{26-30}  $  In addition, the preparation of symmetrical tetra-substituted porphyrin macrocycles is a well-established and low-cost approach, thus enabling the large-scale application of such derivatives.  $  ^{31-34}  $  Zinc (Zn) metallated porphyrins represent the most studied class of porphyrin materials that have been widely applied as hole transporter interlayers in organic and perovskite hybrid solar cells, respectively.  $  ^{35-39}  $  As electron transport mediators, they have only recently introduced in the literature with intriguing results.  $  ^{40,41}  $  Thus, they comprise robust candidate materials for interfacial engineering.  $  ^{42,43}  $  We note, however, that up to now only zinc-metallated porphyrins have been applied as effective interfacial modifiers despite a vast variety of other metallated porphyrins have been proven beneficial in several classes of energy related applications.  $  ^{44,45}  $  A notable exception is the recent application of a europium (Eu) porphyrin as a perovskite grain boundaries passivation agent resulting to the fabrication of PSCs with improved performance and stability.  $  ^{46}  $  In this work the authors demonstrated that europium cations may interfere with perovskite crystal formation hence improving the quality of the resultant perovskite films and reducing the grain boundary related defect states. Herein, we expand the investigation on porphyrin compounds as interfacial materials by incorporating a magnanese porphyrin in the ETL/perovskite interface of planar PSCs resulting in significant increase in stabilized photovoltaic efficiency. We based our selection on the established role of manganese bearing compounds in controlling grain size and passivating defects in several type of perovskite materials.  $  ^{48}  $   We found that the role of the porphyrin interlayer above the titania substrate is very crucial for the device operation. Indeed, it creates a more hydrophobic environment that favors the growth of more homogenous and stable perovskite films with larger and better crystallized perovskite grains. This leads to a net increase in the photocurrent density, a fact clearly reflected in the total PCE improvement. Moreover, the porphyrin interlayer improves electron transfer to the anode through a significant decrease in the work function (  $  W_{F}  $  ) of the ETL combined with suppression in charge recombination. The latter could be related with the passivation effect of manganese (Mn) in the perovskite absorber in agreement with recent reports which demonstrated that   $  Mn^{2+}  $   could be easily inserted into the interstices of octahedral   $  [PbI_{6}]^{4-}  $   to restrain the generation of vacancy defects accompanying perovskite crystallization.  $  ^{47-48}  $   As a result, we obtained porphyrin-based PSCs enhanced by 22% in terms of stabilized power conversion efficiency (PCE) compared to the reference ones, reaching a PCE of 18.70% for the best device. Moreover, the efficiency enhancement was accompanied by a clear stabilization of the PSC which retained 75% of the initial PCE after 1000h of storage in dark and relatively dry conditions (RH~20%). # 2. Experimental Section

Synthesis of (TMePyP)I $ _4 $ Mn(AcO). 500 mg of the free base porphyrin were dissolved in 250 ml of DMF. The solution was heated and sluggishly stirred until complete solubilization of the porphyrin. Then 10 equivalents of Mn $ ^{II} $ (AcO) $ _2 $  were added and the solution was left until boiling. After the reaction, the mixture was cooled down to room temperature and transferred to an ice-cold, saturated solution of NaCl. The green precipitate formed, was rinsed with deionized water and dried with air flow. Finally, it was transferred to a dry Al $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  column whereby it was eluted with CH $ _2 $ Cl $ _2 $  and afterward with a mixture of CH $ _2 $ Cl $ _2 $ /MeOH. Recrystallization from CH $ _2 $ Cl $ _2 $  at 4 °C, yield 80-90%.  $ ^{49-50} $

Fabrication of the PSCs. Fluorine-doped Tin Oxide (FTO) conductive substrates (20mm x 15 mm, Dyesol 7 Ohm sq $ ^{-1} $ ) were cleaned thoroughly by sonication into sequential baths with Hellmanex, 2-propanol and acetone for 15 min each. The FTOs were also treated by UV ozone for 15 min before compact layer's deposition. For the ETL, a solution of titanium (IV) isopropoxide (Aldrich, 97%) in ethanol was spin-coated at 2000 r.p.m. for 60s. The TiO $ _{2} $  films were annealed at 500°C for 45 min with temperature ramp rate of 5°C min $ ^{-1} $ . For the porphyrin modified films, a 0.5 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  methanolic solution was spin-coated at 2000 r.p.m. for 40 s on top of the TiO $ _{2} $  films. The samples were transferred into an Argon filled glove box, where the CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ PbI $ _{3} $  layer was deposited. For the perovskite solution 40wt% of Methylammonium iodide (Dyesol) and Lead acetate trihydrate (PbAc $ _{2} $ .3H $ _{2} $ O, 99.999% trace metals basis, Aldrich)

# 4. Conclusions

In this work, we presented the beneficial effect of a Mn-metallated porphyrin when inserted in planar PSCs as an anode buffer interlayer. The porphyrin molecules are anchored via carbonyl groups upon the titania substrate, altering its electronic and physical properties, thus creating a waterproof environment. Thus, the perovskite grown atop is characterized by higher crystallinity and larger, more homogenous crystals. Moreover, the presence of the (TMePyP)I $ _4 $ Mn(AcO) is

This fact leads to the formation of an optimized interface between the perovskite and the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , thus facilitating electron transport to the anode electrode. The latter is favored by the high spin paramagnetic configuration of the porphyrin, enabling high electron coupling with the titania underlayer. Moreover, the hydrophobic character of the porphyrin shields the cells against humidity and improves substantially their shelf-shield stability. Excellent behavior was observed also after prolonged thermal stress in high temperatures a fact highlighting the multidimensional role of the (TMePyP) $ \mathrm{I}_4\mathrm{Mn(AcO)} $  in the overall performance of the devices. The present work highlights the advantages of interface engineering via Mn porphyrin light sensitive molecular chromophores as a totally aspiring and innovative approach with multiple consequences in the field of PSCs. The strategy can be expanded to a large number of hydrophobic metaloporphyrins possessing appropriate optoelectronic and structural characteristics. Mn molecular complexes dispose exceptional properties which can be further tuned and optimized via additional magnetic stimulus. This might result in specific interactions at the corresponding interfaces between the compact layer, the magnetic chromophore and the perovskite absorber, enabling the development of highly efficient and more robust energy devices (solar cells, LEDs, FETs) against environmental stresses.
