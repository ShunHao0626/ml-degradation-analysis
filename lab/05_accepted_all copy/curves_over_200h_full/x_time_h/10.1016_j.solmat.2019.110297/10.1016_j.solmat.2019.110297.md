# 1. Introduction

Lead halide PSCs have attracted tremendous interest owing to their promising advantages of low cost synthesis process and high PCE   $  [1-4]  $  . The PCE of PSCs has rapidly increased from 3.8% to 25.2% within ten years, which has become the forefront of photovoltaic research   $  [5]  $  . In addition to the high PCE, the PSCs have a good application prospect in the field of semi-transparent and flexible photovoltaic devices owing to thin perovskite absorber layer and the low-temperature preparation processes   $  [6-8]  $  . Besides, the photovoltaic industry is moving towards ultra-thin and low-cost solar cells, which are suitable for consumer electronics with high mechanical flexibility   $  [9,10]  $  . # ABSTRACT

Decreasing the thickness of perovskite absorption layer is significant for obtaining semi-transparent, tandem and flexible photovoltaic devices with high performance. However, the light harvesting would be influenced due to the limited absorption. Introducing plasmonic metal nanoparticles into perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is regarded as a promising approach on the development of ultra-thin PSCs. In this work, gold nanostars (Au NSs) with strong local electromagnetic field and   $  SnO_{2}  $   surface modification layer were successfully incorporated into ultra-thin PSCs. This design increased light harvesting and carrier transporting. Furthermore, we systematically investigated the plasmon effect of Au NSs on the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the PSCs with different thicknesses of the perovskite layer and observed that the deceased thickness of perovskite layer led to more intense PCE improvement as the same Au NSs were introduced. The ultra-thin PSCs with 250-nm perovskite layer show a PCE improvement from 15.34 to 18.50% after Au NSs/  $  SnO_{2}  $   modification, accounting for a maximum enhancement factor of 20.60%. Simultaneously, the modified PSCs with 400-nm perovskite layer exhibit a best reported PCE of 20.06% for the plasmonic PSCs, with an enhanced factor of 13.70% and enhanced light stability. More importantly, flexible, large area and semi-transparent PSCs were also achieved. The flexible devices show more than 90% of their initial PCE values after 100 bending cycles. Our result promises a facile way for developing efficient, flexible and semi-transparent PSCs with ultra-thin absorption layer.

further increase the performance of semi-transparent and flexible photovoltaic devices, it is necessary to decrease the thickness of the absorber layer, which can improve transparency of the semi-transparent devices and increase mechanical stability of the flexible PSCs   $  [11,12]  $  . However, reducing the thickness of the absorber layer leads to decrease in the light-harvesting efficiency, mainly in the longer visible-near infrared region, thereby reducing the PCE of PSCs   $  [8,13,14]  $  . Therefore, it is pivotal to improve the light-harvesting efficiency of PSCs with thinner absorber layer for the development of semi-transparent and flexible photovoltaic devices.

Reducing the thickness of perovskite absorber layer while improving light-harvesting efficiency can not only increase the PCE of PSCs, but also improves the transparency of the semi-transparent devices and increases the mechanical stability of the flexible PSCs [13,15]. introduce plasmonic metal nanoparticles (NPs) into the devices [16–18]. It is well known that the local surface plasmonic resonance (LSPR) of metal NPs can increase light scattering and local electric field [18,19]. Metal NPs have been intensively applied in different solar cells, including dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs), organic solar cells (OSCs) and PSCs [20–22]. In various PSCs devices, the metal nanostructure-induced PCE enhancement is generally around 10%, and the optimum for the previous literatures is reported by Fu et al., leading the PCE to increase from 15.16% to 17.21% with relative enhancement of 13% by incorporating the Au@Ag core-shell cuboids into   $  TiO_{2}  $   electron transport layer (ETL) [23]. Snaith et al. introduced Au@SiO  $  _{2}  $   into   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer, leading the PCE to increase from 14.5% to 16.3% [24]. The mechanisms for the PCE enhancement can be generally divided into two aspects, light scattering to incident light, and the metal-induced enhancement of the absorption cross section for the perovskite materials [25,26]. Actually, the LSPR properties of metal NPs depend strongly on size, morphology and structure of metal NPs, which impact strongly on the performance of PSCs as they are incorporated into the devices [27]. The theory calculation indicates that Au NSs with anisotropic morphologies have wide, tunable and super-strong LSPR and local electromagnetic field at tips [28–30]. It is expected that they could be used for greatly improving the light absorption of the perovskite absorber layer. It should be also highlighted that the interaction distance for the local electromagnetic field of metal NPs is ranging of several 10 nm, thus its impact on perovskite active layer should depend on the thickness of the layer, however, this important factor has not been revealed in the previous works. Therefore, it is very critical to systemically study the surface plasmonic influence of Au NSs with PSCs with different thicknesses of the perovskite layer. In this work, the Au NSs were prepared by the wet chemistry way and successfully loaded into the   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer of PSCs. In order to prevent the direct attachment of Au NSs with perovskite layer and accelerate the charge transfer, we also incorporated   $  SnO_{2}  $   surface modified layer on the Au NSs by low-temperature electron beam method. We investigated systematically the plasmonic effect of Au NSs on PCE of the PSCs with different thicknesses of the perovskite layer and observed that the deceased thickness of perovskite layer led to more intense PCE improvement as the same Au NSs were introduced. The ultra-thin PSCs with 250 nm absorption layer show a PCE of 18.50% after Au NSs/  $  SnO_{2}  $   modification, with a maximum enhanced factor of 20.6%, which is the highest improvement reported to date. Simultaneously, the modified PSCs with 400-nm perovskite layer exhibit a best PCE over 20% for the plasmonic PSCs, with relative enhancement of 13.7% and highly enhanced light stability. More importantly, the ultra-thin devices in the flexible polyethylene terephthalate (PET) substrate demonstrate very excellent flexibility.

# 2. Experiments

The preparation of Au NSs can be divided into two steps. The detailed procedures in the fabrication of Au NSs refer to previous literature   $  [31]  $  . First, the Au seed was fabricated by citrate reduction way. 1 mM HAuCl  $  _{4}  $   was fabricated by adding 1 g HAuCl  $  _{4}  $   into 2.5 L of water. The solution was fabricated by adding 8 mL HAuCl  $  _{4}  $   solution into 30 mL water under magnetic stirring. Then the temperature was increased to 110 °C. After boiling, 900 μL of sodium citrate solution was put into the solution. In this process, color of the solution changed from transparent to red. In the second step, the 30 mM hydroquinone was firstly fabricated by adding 33 mg of hydroquinone in 10 mL of water. Next, the 2.5 mL of HAuCl  $  _{4}  $   (1 mM) was put into 7 mL water under stirring at 25 °C. Then 22 μL of sodium citrate, 50 μL of gold seeds, and 1000 μL hydroquinone (30 mM) were subsequently mixed into reaction solution. The mixture solution was stirred at 25 °C for 30 min to get Au NSs. The size of the Au NSs was adjusted by changing the content of gold seeds.

The detailed method in the device fabrication referred to our previous work. The FTO substrates were bought from Ying kou you xuan

Trade co., Ltd and cleaned by sonication with water, acetone and methanol in succession for 20 min. The as-cleaned FTO substrates were dried with high nitrogen flow. To fabricate a compact TiO $ _2 $  (c-TiO $ _2 $ ) layer, the solution of 0.15 M titanium isopropoxide bis-(acetylacetonate) in butanol solution was deposited the cleaned FTO glasses by the spin coating, and dried in air at 135 °C for 5 min. A diluted TiO $ _2 $  paste (30 nm sized TiO $ _2 $  NPs) with ethanol (1:7.3, mass ratio) was coated on the c-TiO $ _2 $  layer by spin-coating at a speed of 4000 rpm, which was dried at 135 °C for 5 min and then annealed at 500 °C for 30 min. After cooling down to room temperature, the mesoporous TiO $ _2 $  (m-TiO $ _2 $ ) film was further treated with UV ozone for 20 min. For the deposition of the Au NSs, the Au NSs in ethanol with different concentrations were deposited onto the m-TiO $ _2 $  at 2000 rpm, and annealed at 100 °C for 10 min. The SnO $ _2 $  layers were fabricated by electron beam evaporation method, with a deposition rate of 1 Å/s. The perovskite precursor solution was prepared by mixing FAI (172 mg), MABr (22.4 mg), PbBr $ _2 $  (73.4 mg) and PbI $ _2 $  (507.1 mg) in a mixed solvent of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF). Then 40 μL CsI (1.5 mol/L $ ^{-1} $  in DMSO) was put into the perovskite solution with ratio of 5:95 (v:v) to obtain the Cs $ _{0.05} $ (FA $ _{0.83} $ MA $ _{0.17} $ ) $ _{0.95} $ Pb(I $ _{0.83} $ Br $ _{0.17} $ ) $ _3 $  (CsFAMA) perovskite solution. The perovskite film was synthesized with the protection of N $ _2 $  atmosphere by two-consecutive step program at 1000 and 400 rpm for 10 and 40 s, respectively. In the second step, 100 μL of chlorobenzene was dipped into the center of substrate 20 s before the end of spinning program. Then the perovskite layers were annealed at 150 °C for 15 min in a nitrogen atmosphere. The spiro-OMeTAD was spin coated on the perovskite film at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, the Au electron with 100 nm thickness was prepared on the top of the spiro-OMeTAD film. The active area of our device was measured to be 0.1 cm $ ^{2} $ . The photograph of device is shown in Fig. S1.

The transmission electronic microscopy (TEM) image was taken by JEM-2100 electron microscope. The optical performance was measured by the UV–vis spectrophotometer (UV-1800). The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) (ESCALAB MARK II, VG Inc.) was utilized to determine the compositions and chemical state of elements in the perovskite films with X-ray source of Al Kα. The J-V curves of PSCs were carried out by the Class A solar simulator (ABET Sun 2000) under AM 1.5 sunlight and 100 mW/cm  $  ^{-2}  $  irradiance. The light intensity was calibrated by the reference Silicon cell (RERA Solutions RR-1002) and a Keithley 2400 as a source-meter. The incident photo-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra measurement was performed by Solar Cell Scan 100 measured system (Zolix Instruments, Inc.). The surface and cross-section scanning electron microscope (SEM) measurement were employed by a SIRION field-emission SEM with an energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) system (Nova NanoSEM430). The crystal structure was performed by a Rigaku D/max 2550 X-ray diffractometer (XRD) with the monochromatized Cu target radiation source. The roughness of the layers was performed using atomic force microscopy (AFM) (5500, Agilent, Santa Clara, CA). The photoluminescence (PL) were performed by the fluorescence luminescence spectrometer. Electrochemical Impedance spectra (EIS) were measured on the model Princeton electrochemical workstation in the frequency range of 0.1 Hz–1 MHz. The TRPL (Fluorolog-3 spectrometer, Horiba Jobin Yvon, America) were measured by using an emission wavelength of 770 nm.

To study the distribution of local electromagnetic field, the Au NSs are calculated by FDTD stimulation. The FDTD method is a powerful computational technique which is widely used to calculate the optical properties of nanostructures [37]. Fu et al. used the FDTD method to elaborate the LSPR effects of Au@Ag on PSCs. The results of calculation indicated that the Au@Ag (15 nm) exhibits the stronger near field intensity and the largest field distribution size of transverse dipolar mode, which is agreement with the experimental results [23]. Liu et al. used the FDTD to theoretically simulate the electric field distribution in Au arrowhead nanorods. The calculated results indicated that Au arrowhead nanorods had strong near field which can be contributed to light-trapping enhancement [38]. It can be seen that the average electric field strength for Au NSs is significantly stronger than that of the Au SPs. The bare device (without loading) has the Jsc of a 20.97 mA/cm $ ^2 $ , a Voc of 1.11 V and a FF of 75.8%, resulting in a PCE of 17.64%. For the optimum Au SPs and Au NSs proportions, the optimum PCE of devices loaded with Au SPs and Au NSs with different sizes of 18, 30, 40 and 50 nm are 18.54%, 19.10%, 20.06% and 18.25%, respectively, corresponding to relative improvements of 5.1%, 8.2%, 13.7% and 3.4%. In the loaded devices, the Jsc increases significantly to 20.97–22.30 mA/cm $ ^2 $ , Voc increases from 1.11 to 1.13 V and FF increases from 0.76 to 0.79. It should be noted that for the PSCs loaded with different Au SPs and NSs, the device loading with 40-nm Au NSs is optimum. As the size of Au NSs further increases to 50 nm, the device permanence decreases quickly. It can be attributed to the two possible reasons. First, too large Au NSs can reflect light and affect light transmission of the m-TiO $ _2 $  film [40]. In addition, it increases the roughness of the m-TiO $ _2 $  film, leading to reduced charge transfer (Fig. S10). Furthermore, Fig. 4c shows the statistical PCE histogram of PCEs of different PSCs devices. The average PCEs of the control device (L1), devices loaded with Au NSs (L2), devices loaded with SnO $ _2 $  (L3) and the PSCs loaded with Au NSs/SnO $ _2 $  (L4) are 16.9%, 17.4%, 18.5%

and 19.2%, respectively. This demonstrates that Au NSs or/and SnO  $  _{2}  $  both can improve the PCE of the PSCs. To study the steady-state power output of both control device and Au NSs/SnO  $  _{2}  $  modified device, the Jsc and PCE of PSCs are measured as a function of time when their maximum power outputs are 0.88 V and 0.91 V, respectively, as shown in Fig. 4d. The devices loaded with the Au NSs/SnO  $  _{2}  $  achieve a stabilized Jsc of 21.18 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  and PCE of 19.24%. The steady-state Jsc and PCE are similar to the results obtained from J-V curves, indicating the reliability of the measured property. This also shows that the PSCs have a good response to simulated sunlight, which can achieve maximum efficiency in a very short time. The hysteresis curves of two kinds of PSCs are displayed in Fig. 4e at different scan directions, which indicates that the hysteresis influence is inhibited by the insulating of Au NSs/SnO  $  _{2}  $ . The high electron mobility of SnO  $  _{2}  $  can increase electron transport from perovskite to ETL, decreasing charge accumulation therefore the cells loaded with Au NSs/SnO  $  _{2}  $  exhibit suppressed hysteresis [44]. In fact, the Au NSs loading induced PCE enhancement in the PSCs depends strongly on the thickness of perovskite layer. We fabricate the different thicknesses of the perovskite layer by the precursor solution with different concentrations of 0.8, 1.0, and 1.2 M, the corresponding cross-sectional SEM images were displays in Fig. S11. Here, the PCE enhancement of the PSCs with different perovskite layer thicknesses (250 nm, 430 nm, and 550 nm) was compared before and after the same 40-nm Au NSs loading, as shown in Fig. 4f. The starting PCE values for the based devices with 250 nm, 430 nm, and 550 nm active layers are 15.34% 17.64% and 15.05%, respectively. After loading with Au NSs, the PCEs of the corresponding devices increased to 18.50%, 20.06% and 15.49%, respectively. They are with absolute PCE enhancement of 3.16%, 2.42% and 0.44%, relative enhancement of 20.6%, 13.7% and 2.9%, respectively. It is apparent that in the PSCs, the decreased thickness of the perovskite layer induces more intense PCE enhancement after the Au NSs loading.

To elucidate the mechanism of PCE improvement, the IPCE is shown in Fig. 5a. In contrast to the extinction spectra of the Au SPs and Au NSs, we can conclude that the optimum enhancement appears exactly corresponding to the central position of LSPR. Therefore, the increased IPCE is essentially attributed to the broadband light scattering, which promotes the perovskite film to absorb the incident light more efficiently. As we pointed out in the introduction part, there exist two possible reasons for the metal-induced PCE enhancement of PSCs, light scattering to incident light, and the metal-induced enhancement of absorption cross section of the perovskite materials. According to the systemic characterization, the optical scattering enhancement of light in the range of 400–800 nm for the Au-NSs (40 nm) is about 20%. The largest absorbance increase of the perovskite layer (Fig. S13) ranging of 400–800 nm is also around 20%. Therefore, both the two factors may contribute to the metal-induced extra light harvesting and improvement of PCE.

In order to investigate the more detailed reason of increased Voc and FF by the Au NSs/SnO $ _2 $ , we studied carrier transportation and recombination behavior by EIS of L1, L2, L3 and L4. The equivalent circuit in the inset of Fig. 5c was used to fit the data. The devices loaded with SnO $ _2 $  and Au NSs/SnO $ _2 $  has larger carrier recombination resistance (Rreb) of (359, 329  $ \Omega $ cm $ ^2 $ ) than that of the control device and device with Au NSs (219, 223  $ \Omega $ cm $ ^2 $ ), revealing that the introduction of SnO $ _2 $  enhances the charge transport performance and reduces recombination. To further explore the effect of the Au NSs or/and SnO $ _2 $  on charge transfer, the PL and TRPL of control perovskite, perovskite film with Au NSs, SnO $ _2 $  and Au NSs/SnO $ _2 $  are also measured, as shown in Fig. 5d and e. Fig. 5e shows the TRPL of four kinds of perovskite films. # 4. Conclusion

In summary, the Au NSs with wide, tunable and super-strong LSPR were successfully fabricated and incorporated into PSCs devices. Several new contributions should be highlighted. First of all, in order to prevent the direct contact of Au NSs with perovskite active layer and promote the carrier extraction,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2 / SnO_2} $  double electron modification layers were introduced in the devices, which resulted in a large Voc of  $ 1.13\mathrm{V} $ , FF of  $ 79.6\% $  and considerable improvement of UV light stability. Second, contributed by the extra light harvesting, the ultra-thin PSCs using an only  $ 250~\mathrm{nm} $  perovskite as the absorption layer displayed a PCE of  $ 18.50\% $  after  $ \mathrm{AuNSs / SnO_2} $  modification, accounting for a maximum enhanced factor of  $ 20.60\% $ , which is the most effective enhanced factor for PSCs based on various metal nanostructure reported until now. Simultaneously, the modified PSCs exhibit a best reported PCE of over  $ 20\% $  for the plasmonic PSCs, with an enhanced factor of  $ 13.70\% $  and enhanced light stability. Lastly, flexible, large area and semi-transparent
