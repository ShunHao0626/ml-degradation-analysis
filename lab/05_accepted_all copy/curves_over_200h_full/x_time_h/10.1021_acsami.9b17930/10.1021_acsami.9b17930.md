# Introduction

Hybrid metal halide perovskites possess many attractive characters, such as high absorption coefficient, $ ^{1-3} $  ultra-long carrier diffusion length, $ ^{4-6} $  and low-cost solution processability, $ ^{7} $  enabling it an inherent semiconductor material for solar cells. Up to the present, the certified PCE has excessed  $ 25\% $ . $ ^{8} $  However, numerous studies have revealed that polycrystalline perovskite film deposited from a low temperature solution process is always accompanied with a large amount of defects locating on the perovskite film surface or within grain boundaries (GBs) inevitably $ ^{9, 10} $ . These defects, on one hand, serve as charge recombination centers, leading to a large photovoltage loss of PSCs. $ ^{11-14} $  On the other hand, they are the main channels for ion migration and moisture permeation, thus initiating device degradation. $ ^{15-18} $  Therefore, developing effective passivation methods is vital to further improving the PCE and stability of PSCs.

In general, organic cation vacancies are easily generated due to organic components, such as methylamine cation (MA  $  ^{+}  $  ), tend to release from the surface during the film formation process.  $  ^{19,20}  $   Therefore, replacing these volatile components with bulkier organic components should be an effective way to inhibit the formation of such vacancies.  $  ^{21-23}  $   Meanwhile, previous studies have

More importantly, the 2D layered perovskite, obtained through the reaction between   $  PbI_{2}  $   and long chain organic ammonium iodide, seems to be promising to solve the stability issue of PSCs.  $  ^{27-30}  $   Therefore, utilizing long chain organic ammonium iodide to in situ growing 2D perovskite atop of the 3D perovskite film surface is not only efficient to passivate defects on the 3D perovskite, but also improves the device stability with big significance. Mathews et al. reported that the butylammonium iodide (BAI) based 2D layered perovskite can passivate interfacial defects and vacancies, moreover, the corresponding PSCs revealed an enhanced moisture tolerance.  $  ^{28}  $   Chen et al. demonstrated that the moisture durability of 2D/3D stacking structure based PSCs has been drastically improved without compromising their high performance as compared with 3D perovskite.  $  ^{31}  $

In this work, it is found that the chain length of organic ammonium iodide is a crucial factor on the defect passivation effect. Compared with BAI, the 2D perovskite surface based on HAI possesses much lower surface defects and a more prolonged photoluminescence lifetime, resulting in higher PCE (20.62%). More importantly, the 2D capping layer based on HAI with long organic chain can function as moisture passivation and ion migration blocking layers, thus the corresponding device exhibits significantly enhanced moisture and thermal stability in comparison with 3D perovskite device.

$$
\mathrm{Y} (\mathrm{t}) = A _ {1} \exp \left(- \frac {\mathrm{t}}{\tau_ {1}}\right) + A _ {2} \exp \left(- \frac {\mathrm{t}}{\tau_ {2}}\right) + A _ {0}
$$

(1)

# Conclusions

In summary, hexylammonium iodide (HAI) is introduced to in situ constructing a 2D perovskite layer atop of the 3D perovskite surface. The 2D/3D perovskite structure has been verified by characterization methods of SEM, XRD, and TA spectrum. Moreover, compared with 3D and 3D+BAI films, the 3D+HAI owns lower interfacial defect corresponding to an enhanced photoluminescence lifetime and much more suppressed interfacial charge recombination. As a consequence, the PCE is enhanced from  $ 18.83\% $  (3D) to  $ 20.62\% $  (3D+HAI). Moreover, the long-term durability of PSCs against moisture and heat is simultaneously elevated. Our work further demonstrates that 2D/3D structure is promising for further improving the PCE and stability of PSCs.
