# Efficient Gradient Potential Top Electron Transport Structures Achieved by Combining an Oxide Family for Inverted Perovskite Solar Cells with High Efficiency and Stability

Boping Yang,  $  ^{\#}  $   Ruiman Ma,  $  ^{\#}  $   Zishuai Wang, Dan Ouyang, Zhanfeng Huang, Jinlian Lu, Xiaohui Duan, Lu Yue, Ning Xu, and Wallace C. H. Choy  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Although inverted (p–i–n) structure perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved high efficiency by commonly using fullerenes or their derivatives as electron transport layers (ETLs), the device stability and cost of fullerene materials are still of great concern. Herein, we demonstrate inorganic top ETLs simply composed from a family of metal oxides including   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   and its derivative of   $  Sn:In_{2}O_{3}  $   with a gradient potential structure. For inverted PSCs, the typical film formation process of   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   will damage or degrade perovskite materials underneath; thus, we report a low temperature synthesis approach for obtaining   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   and   $  Sn:In_{2}O_{3}  $   nanoparticles that can form effective top ETLs without any post-treatment. The one-

family oxide-based top ETL features with the enhanced built-in potential, high electron extraction, and low interfacial recombination, offering a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 20.65% in PSCs constructed from oxide-only carrier (both hole and electron) transport layers (CTLs), which is the highest efficiency in oxide-only CTL-based inverted PSCs to the best of our knowledge. Equally important, the inverted PSCs based on the   $  Sn:In_{2}O_{3}/In_{2}O_{3}  $   ETL show the excellent operational stability and remain 90% of the initial value of PCE over 2000 h. Consequently, this work contributes to the robust strategy of all oxide-only CTLs in developing rigid and flexible PSCs for practical photovoltaic applications.

KEYWORDS: low temperature, solution process, inorganic metal oxides, electron transport layer, perovskite solar cells

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Inverted structure planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been widely investigated due to their advantages such as long device stability, cost-effectiveness, low-temperature processability, and suppressed hysteresis characteristics.  $  ^{1-6}  $   Typically, there are several requirements such as chemical compatibility,  $  ^{5,7}  $   high electron transport ability, and low-temperature treatment  $  ^{2,5,8-10}  $   to form electron transport layers (ETLs) on top of the perovskite layer because perovskite thin films are vulnerable to high-temperature processes and polar solvents. Besides, carrier (electron and hole) transport layers (CTLs) play an indispensable role in planar PSCs not only for effectively transporting charges and reducing carrier recombination but also for preventing the damage of devices from ambient environments, such as humidity, oxygen, and light.  $  ^{10-14}  $   As the most commonly used ETLs,   $  C_{60}  $   and its derivative [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) possess suitable energy alignment and effective charge collection ability, which resulted in a high power conversion efficiency (PCE) of about 22% in p-i-n planar PSCs.  $  ^{15-19}  $   Although high performances were obtained for these organic ETLs, their high cost, complicated synthesis, and purification process are highly unsuitable for large-scale applications.  $  ^{20}  $   Additionally, there is a concern in their long-term operational and thermal instability for future commercialization.  $  ^{21,22}  $

Inorganic ETLs, featuring material abundance, low cost, and excellent stability, are potentially superior alternatives. Some inorganic n-type metal oxides, such as   $  \mathrm{Ti(Nb)O_{9}}^{23}  $     $  SnO_{2}  $  , Al-doped ZnO (AZO),  $  ^{25}  $     $  ZnO  $  ,  $  ^{26-29}  $   and   $  CeO_{x}  $  ,  $  ^{30}  $   have been reported to be a layer material as a part of the ETL structures. While most of the metal oxide-only ETLs are reported in n–i–p PSCs,  $  ^{31-36}  $   there are no clear studies of top ETLs for inverted planar PSCs composed from simply a family of metal oxides with a gradient potential structure for improving the performance and stability of PSCs. Indeed, most of the well-performed and stable inverted PSCs are made from the ETL structure composed of organic films such as PCBM and   $  C_{60}  $   and the metal oxide layer.  $  ^{37-39}  $

In this work, we demonstrate inorganic top ETLs simply from combining one family of metal oxides including   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   and its derivative of Sn-doped   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   (hereafter named as   $  Sn:In_{2}O_{3}  $  ) with a gradient potential structure in p-i-n PSCs with high efficiency and stability. To assess the energy band alignment of the Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  bilayer, we analyze the Tauc plots and ultraviolet electron spectroscopy (UPS) of pristine In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  and Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ . From the Tauc plots (Figure 2a) and absorbance spectra (Figure S2), the band gap energy increases from 3.81 eV (In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ ) to 3.89 eV (Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ ) after Sn doping. As discussed previously, partly substituting In $ ^{3+} $  by smaller size Sn $ ^{4+} $  will reduce the lattice size and enhance the crystal field due to stronger coupling of In–O, resulting in the broadened band gap of the Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  film. The valance band energies ( $ E_{VB} $ ) of In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  and Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  calculated from UPS spectra shown in Figure 2b are  $ -8.32 $  and  $ -8.21 $  eV, respectively. Then, the conduction band energy ( $ E_{CB} $ ) is calculated by combining the band gap and  $ E_{VB} $ , where the  $ E_{CB} $  ( $ -4.51 $  eV) of Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  decreases from  $ -4.32 $  eV of In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  after doping with Sn into In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ . Figure 3d shows the band alignment diagram of In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  and Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  and materials used in p–i–n structure PSCs. Notably, the small energy offset of

0.2 eV between the Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  layer (-4.32 eV) and perovskite film (-4.12 eV) $ ^{12} $  is beneficial for electron extraction from the perovskite layer.

The electrical properties of Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  ETLs, including carrier concentration ( $ n $ ), mobility ( $ \mu $ ), and conductivity ( $ \sigma $ ), are investigated and calculated from the Hall effect measurements as a function of Sn doping concentration, as shown in Figure 2c. The electrical properties all show an increase at first and then decrease with increasing the doping concentration. It should be noted that conductivity is calculated from the equation  $ \sigma = ne\mu $ , where  $ e $  is an electron charge. As shown in Figure 2a, the maximum of carrier concentration, mobility, and conductivity are  $ 1.65 \times 10^{16} $  cm $ ^{-3} $ , 14 cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  s $ ^{-1} $ , and 0.037 S cm $ ^{-1} $ , respectively at 5% doping concentration. Notably, the higher conductivity of Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  compared to In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  contribute to enhancing the charge transport and collection at the interface. Moreover, as shown in Figure 2d, the designed Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  bilayer ETL with a layer of In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  contacting directly with the Ag electrode, where the Schottky barrier (0.09 eV) between the conduction band minimum of In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  and work function of the Ag electrode is very small, which can promote electron extraction from ETL to the cathode efficiently. Consequently, the gradient energy band alignments in the ETL region and at the cathode formed by the Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  bilayer structure can (1) contribute to the carrier separation by offering a higher built-in voltage, which comes from the higher carrier concentration and (2) benefit the carrier extraction by the cascade energy alignment structure, which suppresses the recombination loss and contributes to the higher fill factor (FF) and  $ V_{OC} $ .

The most critical issue that limits the commercialization of PSCs is their long-term stability. It is confirmed that the poor device stability resulting from the moisture- or  $ \mathrm{O}_2 $ -induced perovskite decomposition can be controlled by good encapsulation. However, under realistic operating conditions, the stability of the PSC is inevitably affected by light and high temperature, which is the focus of this work. As shown in Figure 3d, the time-related variation of the device performance is collected by testing the devices at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 12\mathrm{h} $  and then at room temperature in the dark for another  $ 12\mathrm{h} $ , which is similar to the realistic operational condition with day and night alternates. Notably, in terms of the temperature stability test, the PSC is transferred in an oven rather than being placed on a hot plate in a  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $  glove box for characterization. This arrangement ensures that the whole device is in a high-temperature atmosphere ( $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ ) rather than only one side of the device being heated by the hot plate. After 69 days (1656 h), all PSC PCEs maintain  $ >90\% $  of their initial values, but PSCs based on the  $ \mathrm{Sn:In_2O_3 / In_2O_3} $  bilayer ETL show a slightly higher remaining PCE ( $ 91.9\% $ ), which is attributed to the excellent chemical inactivity of inorganic charge transport layers sandwiching the perovskite layer. Meanwhile, the advantages of more efficient charge extract and transport ability as well as enhanced built-in field of the used  $ \mathrm{Sn:In_2O_3 / In_2O_3} $  bilayer ETL are beneficial for separating photoexcited charges and then impeding the degradation of the perovskite. Furthermore, the time-related variation of the device performance under  $ 12\mathrm{h} $  continuous 1 sun illumination and then  $ 12\mathrm{h} $  interval in the dark is shown in Figure S5. Obviously, after  $ 2000\mathrm{h} $  operation, the devices with the  $ \mathrm{Sn:In_2O_3 / In_2O_3} $  bilayer ETL remain  $ 91.8\% $  PCEs of their initial values, preceding that of  $ \mathrm{In_2O_3} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Sn:In_2O_3} $ . The improved photostability is considered to be resulted from less recombination due to more efficient electron extraction.

To investigate the improved performance of PSCs, especially for the Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  bilayer ETL, dynamics and device physics are discussed. To evaluate charge extraction from the devices, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) are measured and presented in Figure 4a,b. By inserting one layer of the In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  ETL on top of perovskite layers, the PL intensity is significantly quenched due to the charge transfer from perovskite layers to the In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  ETL. The charge transfer in the Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /perovskite interface is more efficient than that of the In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ -based interface, and the Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  bilayer has the most efficient charge extraction. As shown in Figure 4b, TRPL spectra are collected for better understanding of the charge extraction process. As expected, we can observe that the PL decay lifetime (fitted with a biexponential decays model) is reduced from 9.6 ns of the pristine In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /perovskite sample to 7.4 ns of for the Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /perovskite sample and 5.8 ns of the Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /perovskite sample. The results indicate a more efficient charge transfer for Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  bilayer ETL-based PSCs, $ ^{44,45} $  agreeing well with the PL spectra and the favorable energy band level. Charge extraction and recombination information can also be derived from the transient photocurrent and photovoltage (Figure S6a,b). It can be calculated that the charge-extraction lifetimes (CELs) are 0.52, 0.44, and 0.31  $ \mu $ s, while the charge-recombination lifetimes (CRLs) are 1.2, 1.8, and 2.1  $ \mu $ s for In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ , Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ , Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ -based devices, respectively. Importantly, the shortest

CEL and the longest CRL indicate a better charge extraction and lower recombination at the Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /perovskite interface than that of the In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /perovskite and Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /perovskite interfaces. The result is well consistent with the PL and TRPL measurements as well as PSC performances. The conductivity of the single In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  and Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  ETLs and Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  bilayer ETL is also confirmed by characterizing the current density ( $ J $ ) voltage ( $ V $ ) characteristics of ITO/ETL/Au under dark conditions (as illustrated in Figure 4c). It is clear that the conductivity of the bilayer is much higher than that of the monolayer of In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  and Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ . Notably, all the thickness of ETL is 60 nm, where the Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  bilayer ETL consists of 30 nm In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  and 30 nm Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ .

Capacitance–voltage(C−V) measurements are performed to further understand the effect of different ETLs on   $  V_{OC}  $  . The built-in potential of the device can be obtained from the x-intercept of the linear regime in the Mott–Schottky plot (Figure 4d) by fitting the experiments with the Mott–Schottky equation:  $  ^{43}  $     $  1/C^{2} = (2/\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon N)(V - V_{\mathrm{bi}})  $  , where   $  \varepsilon  $   is the dielectric constant of the material,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the permittivity of the vacuum, e is the element charge, and N is the carrier concentration in the device. The   $  V_{bi}  $   of the device with the   $  Sn:In_{2}O_{3}/In_{2}O_{3}  $   bilayer ETL is 1.17 V, which is 0.08 and 0.24 V higher than that of   $  Sn:In_{2}O_{3}  $   (1.09 V) and   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   (0.93 V), respectively. Typically, the high   $  V_{bi}  $   not only offers a strong driving force to carrier separation and transport in PSCs and promotes   $  V_{OC}  $   of the device but also contributes to the effectively suppressed recombination as well as improved device performance. The enhancement of   $  V_{bi}  $   of the device with the   $  Sn:In_{2}O_{3}/In_{2}O_{3}  $   bilayer contributes to an increase in   $  V_{OC}  $   in PSCs, which results from the large difference of charge concentration between   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   and   $  Sn:In_{2}O_{3}  $  .

To obtain more information about charge transport and recombination mechanisms, the dependence of the   $  J_{SC}  $   and   $  V_{OC}  $   on the light power intensity is determined; as plotted in Figures S7 and 4c,   $  J_{SC}  $   has a power-law relationship with the light intensity (  $  J_{SC} = kI^{\alpha}  $  ). When the perovskite film is trap-free, the recombination in the perovskite film is the bimolecular recombination and   $  \alpha  $   is equal to 1. The slope based on the bilayer ETL is 0.93, the closest value to 1, indicating the low nonradiative recombination. In addition, from the   $  V_{OC}  $   versus light intensity as shown in Figure 4e, the ideality factor is estimated by the formula   $  n = \frac{q}{kT} \frac{dV_{oc}}{d\ln(\varphi)}  $  , which also provides information about the recombination process in PSCs. Since the slope of   $  V_{OC}  $   versus the light intensity is greater than 1, it indicates the presence of additional trap-assisted Shockley–Read–Hall (SRH) recombination.  $  ^{46-48}  $   As shown in Figure 4e, the slopes of 1.52 KT/q, 1.31 KT/q, and 1.18 KT/q correspond to the devices based on   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $  , Sn:In  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $  , and Sn:In  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $  /In  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $   bilayer ETL, respectively. The decreased slope indicates a significant reduction in the trap-assisted SRH recombination in devices with the Sn:In  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $  /In  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $   bilayer ETL, which is consistent with the increment of   $  V_{OC}  $   and FF. Furthermore, the charge recombination in PSCs based on different ETLs is also compared by their dark J-V curves. As shown in Figure S8, the Sn:In  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $  /In  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $   bilayer ETL-based PSCs exhibit the smallest dark current density compared to the single In  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $   and Sn:In  $  _{2}  $  O  $  _{3}  $   ETL-based PSCs, which corresponds to the lowest recombination loss in devices. # 3. CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  NPs are synthesized through a low-temperature solution process method, and a post-treatment-free Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  stepped structure of the top bilayer ETL is proposed and demonstrated for achieving high-efficiency and stable p–i–n PSCs. Electrical properties are improved by doping Sn in In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ , and the formation of gradient carrier structure and their energy bands with enhanced built-in electric field is proposed. After optimization, PSCs based on the Sn:In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $ /In $ _2 $ O $ _3 $  bilayer ETL exhibit a high PCE of 20.65% with improved  $ J_{\text{SC}} $ ,  $ V_{\text{OC}} $ , and FF on account of the enhanced charge separation, extraction and transfer, and suppressed recombination. PSCs with this all-oxide ETL structure also show high thermal stability and photostability. Consequently, the PSCs constructed from the oxide-only HTL and ETLs achieve high efficiency, stability, and completely ambient condition process, which promotes the development of inverted PSCs for practical applications.

# 4. EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS

4.1. Material Synthesis and Device Fabrication. Indium(III) acetate monohydrate   $  \left(\mathrm{In}\left(\mathrm{C}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{3}\mathrm{O}_{2}\right)_{3}\cdot\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O},99.99\%\right)  $   and tin(IV) chloride   $  \left(\mathrm{SnCl}_{4}\right)  $   were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and Macklin Reagent, respectively. Ethanol absolute, methanol, chlorobenzene (CB, 99.8%, extra dry), hydrochloric acid (37%), dimethylformamide (DMF, 99%, extra dry), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99%, extra dry), 1,2-dichlorobenzene (DCB, 98+, extra dry), isopropanol (99.8%, extra dry), and ammonium hydroxide solution (28% in water) were purchased from Acros Organics. Methylammonium iodide and formamidinium iodide were purchased from Dyesol. Lead(II) iodide   $  \left(\mathrm{PbI}_{2},99\%\right)  $   was purchased from TCI. All chemicals were used as

received unless otherwise noted.   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   and   $  Sn:In_{2}O_{3}  $   NPs are synthesized via a modified hydrothermal approach, which is similar to our previously reported method.  $  ^{[12]}  $   Typically, 0.62 g of   $  \mathrm{In(C_{2}H_{3}O_{2})_{3}\cdot H_{2}O}  $   was dissolved in 12 mL of absolute ethanol and then 0.4 mL of NaOH (1 g) aqueous solution was added. After stirring for 15 min, the mixed solution was transferred into an 18 mL Teflon-lined autoclave, which was sealed and kept at 140 °C for 5 h. In terms of the synthesis of   $  Sn:In_{2}O_{3}  $  , a different amount of   $  SnCl_{4}  $   was added with   $  \mathrm{In(C_{2}H_{3}O_{2})_{3}\cdot H_{2}O}  $   together, and the other process was the same as   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   synthesis. The NPs were dried and kept in a glovebox for further use.

According to our previous work,  $  ^{10}  $  the perovskite film is fabricated first. Subsequently, the ethanol solution of  $  In_{2}O_{3}  $  or/and  $  Sn:In_{2}O_{3}  $  NPs with different concentrations (20, 30, and 40 mg/mL) is dynamically spin-coated on top of the perovskite layers through different speeds (800–2000 rpm). Importantly, before the formation of the ETL films, the well-dispersed metal oxide NP solution was prepared from the purified ethanol by removing possibly residual water. The purified ethanol was obtained through vacuum distillation and cooling in the glovebox. This combined processes could impede the damage of the perovskite film by its direct contact with water. Additionally, it prevents the contact between the perovskite film and water after the volatilization of ethanol due to its lower boiling point than water. Different amounts of NPs were added in the evaporated ethanol in a glovebox and then ultrasonic treatment was performed. Finally, 100 nm thick Ag electrodes are directly deposited on the top ETL.

4.2. Characterization. Elemental analysis was carried out by ICP-OES. Photocurrent and photovoltage decay curves were measured using a 450 W Xenon lamp and a Keithley 2400 source meter. For Hall effect measurements, Sn-doped   $  In_{2}O_{3}  $   films with different concentrations were spin-coated on a glass substrate, and the Au electrode in the Van der Pauw configuration was thermally evaporated. TPV and TPC decays were recorded using a digital oscilloscope (Tektronix TBS 1202B). To obtain the TPV data, 530 nm laser pulse was used and the PSCs were exposed to 0.3 sun background illumination. To obtain TPC measurements, the short-circuited PSCs were illuminated by a 530 nm laser pulse with a pulse width of 120 fs. The capacitance measurements were processed according to the previous report  $  ^{4}  $   based on the capacitors with the structure of   $  ITO/NiO_{x}/ETL/Ag  $  . The details of other characterization methods have been described in our previous work.  $  ^{12}  $
