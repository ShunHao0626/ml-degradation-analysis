# $  NH_{4}Cl  $  -Modified ZnO for High-Performance   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   Perovskite Solar Cells via Low-Temperature Process

Huaxin Wang, Siliang Cao, Bo Yang, Haiyun Li, Ming Wang, Xiaofei Hu, Kuan Sun, and Zhigang Zang*

Recently, the thermally stable and facilely fabricated inorganic   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted tremendous attention where the electron transport layer (ETL) is vital. However, the typical sintering temperature for the widely used electron transport material, that is,   $  TiO_{2}  $  , is more than 400 °C, elevating the cost and hindering the application. Due to high electron mobility and low fabrication temperature, ZnO becomes a desirable alternative for   $  TiO_{2}  $  , as the ETL in   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs, albeit with low open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ). Herein, this work introduces a trace of   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   to the sol–gel-derived ZnO precursor to decrease the work function of the ZnO film, tune the surface morphology of the perovskite film, and thus suppress the density of trap states in the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   films. Consequently, full-coverage and pure-phase   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   films consisting of micron-size and high-crystallinity grains are obtained. More importantly, for the optimal   $  NH_{4}Cl  $ -modified ZnO, a shining improvement in   $  V_{oc}  $   from 1.08 to 1.27 V boosts the champion   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs to obtain a power conversion efficiency of 10.16%, which is the highest value reported among pure-  $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs under a low fabrication temperature of 160 °C. In addition, the   $  NH_{4}Cl  $ -modified ZnO ETL reduces the severe hysteresis and increases the device stability significantly.

# 1. Introduction

The prominent organic–inorganic halide perovskite solar cells (PSC) have been spotlighted in the past decade, with the skyrocketing power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 3.8% to 25.2%.  $  ^{[1-5]}  $   Although the splendid optical and electronic properties are instrumental in the prosperity of the widely studied   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (  $  MAPbI_{3}  $  ) PSCs,  $  ^{[6-8]}  $   the thermally unstable organic cation methylammonium (  $  MA^{+}  $  ) becomes the Achilles' heel.  $  ^{[9,10]}  $   Therefore, one solution is to replace the organic cations by the

bandgap.  $  ^{[20]}  $     $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs with a bandgap of 1.92 eV reported by Yan et.al. reached a PCE of 14.3% by interface engineering,  $  ^{[21]}  $   but   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   is still unstable when exposed to high temperature or high humidity.  $  ^{[22]}  $   Meanwhile, another cesium lead mixed-halide perovskite,   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $ , has been integrated into the photovoltaic application, due to its proper bandgap of 2.08 eV and superior thermal stability.  $  ^{[23,24]}  $   Unfortunately, the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs face two major obstacles, i.e., high fabrication temperature and low open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ).  $  ^{[25-27]}  $   The high fabrication temperature increases the cost and impedes the flexible, wearable application. In contrast,   $  V_{oc}  $   is related to the energy losses, which is defined as the difference between perovskite bandgap and the corresponding   $  V_{oc}  $ .  $  ^{[21]}  $   The   $  V_{oc}  $   of   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs are typically lower than 1.2 V, demonstrating much severe energy losses in those devices and resulting in a relatively poor PCE below 9%.  $  ^{[24-33]}  $   The energy losses have been evidenced to be related with the radiative and nonradiative recombination properties of the PSCs.  $  ^{[34-36]}  $   As the radiative recombination mainly refers to the perovskite absorber, high-quality perovskite films fabrication is the key to the reduced energy losses and improved   $  V_{oc}  $ .  $  ^{[37-40]}  $   Zhang et al. used a light-soaking process before the perovskite film annealing, demonstrating that at large sizes, high crystallinity and preferential orientation resulted in an improved   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.28 V.  $  ^{[41]}  $   An efficient way to suppress the nonradiative

inorganic analogs (e.g.,   $  Cs^{+}  $  ,   $  [11]  $     $  Rb^{+}  $  ,   $  [12]  $   etc.) to improve the thermal stability. The first reported all-inorganic cation PSCs used cesium tin iodide (  $  CsSnI_{3}  $  ) perovskite for the photo-active layer despite quite low PCE and unstable   $  Sn^{2+}  $  .   $  [13,14]  $   Then, the   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   devices were proven that their PCE is comparable with the PCE of   $  MAPbBr_{3}  $   devices.  $  ^{[11]}  $   However, the complicated fabrication process and intrinsic low absorption ability arising from the wide bandgap (  $  E_{g}=2.3\ eV  $  ) become the unwilling impediments.  $  ^{[15,16]}  $   The   $  \alpha  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   material exhibits suitable bandgap (  $  E_{g}=1.73\ eV  $  ) for photovoltaics,  $  ^{[17]}  $   where the corresponding PSCs attained a high PCE of 17%.  $  ^{[18]}  $   Unfortunately, its rebarbative yellow nonperovskite   $  \delta  $  -phase would suffer the transition from the desirable black perovskite   $  \alpha  $  -phase at room temperature.  $  ^{[19]}  $   Alternatively, replacing some iodine with bromine to form a mixed-halide   $  CsPbI_{3-x}Br_{x}  $   perovskite can improve the thermal stability albeit with the increased

The planer-type PSCs are typically deposited on a compact ETL for facile fabrication process. In this work, for the first time, we present that the photovoltaic parameters, i.e.,   $  V_{oc}  $  , fill factor (FF), and PCE, of the inorganic   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSC can be improved at the same time by introducing ammonium chloride (  $  NH_{4}Cl  $  ) into a sol–gel-derived ZnO as the ETL.  $  ^{[50]}  $   The   $  NH_{4}Cl  $  -modified ZnO thin film exhibits a higher electron mobility and reduced work function, facilitating desirable cascade energy-level alignment between perovskite and ETL. The high-quality   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   film can be spin-coated on the ZnO and   $  NH_{4}Cl  $  -modified ZnO surfaces, whereas the latter increases the grain size and the crystallinity of the overlying   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   film. Finally, we fabricate the devices with the structure of Glass/ITO/ZnO (or   $  NH_{4}Cl  $  -modified ZnO)/  $  CsPbIBr_{2}/2,2',7,7'  $  -tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD)/Ag via a low-temperature process. The PCE is increased from 8.29% to 10.16%, whereas the outstanding enhancement of   $  V_{oc}  $   is from 1.08 to 1.27 V. Moreover, reduced hysteresis and significantly enhanced stability are accomplished. To the best of our knowledge, the PCE of 10.16% is the highest value for   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs when the fabrication temperature is lower than 160 °C.

# 2. Results and Discussion

First, we investigate the surface morphology of the ZnO ETLs using atomic force microscopy (AFM) (Figure 1a,b) and the top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM) as shown in Figure S1, Supporting Information. The SEM results present the compact films were spin-coated on the ITO substrates using the ZnO precursor with or without   $  NH_{4}Cl  $  . Hereinafter, they are abbreviated as ZnO and   $  ZnO-NH_{4}Cl  $  , respectively, for convenience. By comparing Figure S1a,c, Supporting Information, the introduction of   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   tunes the wrinkle-textured ZnO thin film to be smoother and more uniform. This upgraded surface morphology was further confirmed by the AFM measurements.

The results shown in Figure 1a,b and Figure S2, Supporting Information, reveal that all the ZnO films exhibit a smooth surface, but the roughness of the ZnO film (3.10 nm) is larger than that (1.65 nm) of the ZnO-NH $ _4 $ Cl (adding 2 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  NH $ _4 $ Cl) film. The smoother substrates contribute to a more favorable morphology for the overlying perovskite film, $ ^{[52,53]} $  contributing to a higher  $ V_{\text{oc}} $ . $ ^{[54]} $  Then, we compared the transmittance of different ZnO ETLs on the ITO/glass substrates, and the results are shown in Figure S3, Supporting Information. All the ETL films except the over-introduced sample (4 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  NH $ _4 $ Cl) presents higher transmittance than the ITO/glass substrate over a wide spectrum range of 440–690 nm. This high transmittance of ZnO-NH $ _4 $ Cl films ensure the light absorption in the overlying photo-active material.

The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) is used to examine the element composition of the different ETLs, whereas the global spectra are shown in Figure S4, Supporting Information. The XPS signals remain unchanged of Zn 2p core level as shown in Figure S5, Supporting Information. However, the Cl 2p core-level spectra shown in Figure 1c presents two significant peaks which can be indexed to the Cl 2p orbital in the  $ \mathrm{ZnO - NH_4Cl} $  film, whereas those signals are absent in the ZnO film. This comparison reveals the existence of Cl in the ZnO ETL. Figure 1d shows the local high-resolution XPS spectra of O1s core level, where the presence of two peaks is highlighted by the deconvolution. The lower-binding-energy peak (529.7 eV) is attributed to the  $ \mathrm{O}^{2-} $  ions, bonded with  $ \mathrm{Zn}^{2+} $  to form ZnO. The higher-binding-energy peak (531.3 eV) is indexed to the oxygen-vacancy defects or hydroxyl O-H groups.[50,55-58] Intriguingly, after  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $ -modified, the intensity of the higher-binding-energy component significantly increased, suggesting that more oxygen vacancies in the ZnO films are formed.[59-61] The photoluminescence (PL) emission spectrum of the two ZnO films further evidenced the increase in oxygen vacancies after  $ \mathrm{NH_4Cl} $  modification, as shown in Figure S6, Supporting Information. The peak located at  $ 390\mathrm{nm} $  associating with a higher exciton binding energy is indexed as the direct recombination. In addition, the amplitude of defect-energy-levels-introduced peak at  $ 530\mathrm{nm} $  corresponds to the quantity of oxygen vacancies.[60] In this work, the ZnO is the product of the hydrolyzed  $ \mathrm{Zn(OH)_2} $  during thermal annealing (more details can be found in the Experimental Section). We suspected that the reaction between  $ \mathrm{NH_4^+} $  and the  $ \mathrm{OH^-} $  contributes to the oxygen vacancies in the  $ \mathrm{ZnO-NH_4Cl} $  film. Meanwhile, parts of the  $ \mathrm{NH_4^+} $ -induced oxygen vacancies are passivated by the  $ \mathrm{Cl^-} $ , forming  $ \mathrm{ZnCl_2} $ . Nevertheless, it has been reported that the increased oxygen vacancies are harmless to the performance of solar cells, no matter initially or over extended illumination.[62] Furthermore, the increased oxygen vacancies introduce a high electron concentration, thus elevating the Fermi level from the center to the conduction band,[50,55,57] resulting in a cascade band structure with CsPbIBr₂. The Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) information confirms the difference in work functions between ZnO and  $ \mathrm{ZnO-NH_4Cl} $ , as shown in Figure S7, Supporting Information. The work function of Au is assumed to be  $ 5.40\mathrm{eV} $ . We therefore estimated that the work functions of ZnO and  $ \mathrm{ZnO-NH_4Cl} $  were around 4.54 and  $ 4.30\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. # 3. Conclusions

In this study, we have demonstrated a facile and efficient strategy to tune the band structure and electron mobility of the ETL by adding   $  NH_{4}Cl  $   to the sol–gel-derived ZnO precursor. Decreased work function of the   $  ZnO-NH_{4}Cl  $  , more beneficial surface morphology of the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   film, and suppressed defects in perovskite enhance the performance of the solar cells with   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.27 V and PCE of 10.16%, which is the recorded high efficiency for the   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs fabricated under low temperature (160 °C). Meanwhile, the prolonged device stability and reduced hysteresis are achieved. Our work highlights the great importance to optimize the ETL and interface property for the inorganic PSCs, and provides a low-temperature process for the realization of highly efficient and stable   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   PSCs.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: The lead (II) bromide (PbBr $ _2 $ , 99.99%), cesium iodide (CsI, 99.99%), Spiro-OMeTAD (99.8%), Bis(trifluoromethane) sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI, 99%), 4-tert-Butylpyridine (TBP, 96%), and tris (2-(1H-pyrazol-1-yl)-4-tert-butylpyridine)-cobalt(III) Tris (bis-(trifluoromethylsulphonyl)imide)) (FK209, 99%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Zinc acetate (ZnAc, anhydrous, 99%), 2-methoxyethanol (2-ME, 99%), and NH $ _4 $ Cl (99%) were provided by Damas-beta. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99%), chlorobenzene (CB, 99.8%), acetonitrile (99.8%), and ethanolamine (EA, 99.5%) were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich. All the materials were used without further purification.

Preparation of ZnO Films: The ZnO films was prepared according to a sol-gel-derived method reported in the literature.  $  ^{[50,71]}  $   2-MA (15 mL) and EA (254 μL) was used to dissolve zinc acetate dihydrate (1000 mg). Subsequently, the precursor was kept stirring at 60 °C for 1 h and then at room temperature for 48 h. For the NH₄Cl-modified ZnO, various concentration NH₄Cl (1–4 mg mL⁻¹ in deionized water) was added into the ZnO precursor to obtain NH₄Cl-modified ZnO precursor after stirring at room temperature for 30 min. The ITO-coated glass substrate (sheet resistance of 15 Ω⁻¹) was sequentially washed by ultrasonication in detergent, deionized water, ethyl alcohol, acetone, and isopropyl alcohol for 30 min, respectively. Then the ITO substrates were all dried and cleaned with nitrogen flow and treated by UV-ozone for 30 min. Finally, the corresponding ZnO precursors were spin-coated on the ITO substrates at 3000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 150 °C for 20 min to obtain ZnO and NH₄Cl-modified ZnO compact layers.

Device Fabrication: After the ETL fabrication, the substrates were moved into the glove box for perovskite film and HTL deposition. First, the   $  CsPbBr_{2}  $   precursor (440 mg   $  PbBr_{2}  $   and 312 mg CsI dissolved in 1 mL DMSO) was spin-coated on the ETL at 1500 rpm for 15 s and 3500 rpm for 30 s. Then, according to the previous literature,  $  ^{[24]}  $   the as-casted film was heated at 30 °C for 10 min and then 160 °C for 10 min to obtain high quality. Subsequently, once the films were cooled to the room temperature, the HTL was deposited on the perovskite film by spin-coating HTL precursor solution (90 mg Spiro-OMeTAD, 75 μL FK209 (170 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in acetonitrile) and 45 μL Li-TFSI (100 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   in acetonitrile) and 10 μL TBP dissolved in 1 mL CB) at 4000 rpm for 40 s. Finally, Ag electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation with the thickness of 100 nm. The PSC active area was 0.11 cm  $  ^{2}  $  .

Devices Characterizations: The XPS and UPS were conducted using a Thermo Fisher Escalab 250Xi spectrometer. XRD patterns were recorded by Rigaku D/Max 2500PC with Cu Kα radiation (1.54 Å). The surface topography and cross-section structures were scanned by a GeminiSEM 300 field-emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM). AFM and KPFM were analyzed by a Bruker Dimension Icon AFM. Optical absorption and transmission spectra were measured with an UV–vis spectrophotometer (Shimadzu UV-1800). The steady-state PL spectra were obtained by a fluorescence spectrophotometer (Cary Eclipse, Agilent) with

excitation wavelength of 360 nm for   $  CsPbIBr_{2}  $   and 320 nm for ZnO. Current density–voltage (J-V) curves were plotted using a Keithley 2400 SourceMeter under an AM 1.5G solar simulator (Newport, 2612A), which was calibrated by a silicon reference cell under a light intensity of 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  . EQE of PSCs was measured by the solar cell quantum efficiency measurement system (Newport, USA). Each solar cell device had an active area of 0.11 cm  $  ^{2}  $   and was measured in air.
