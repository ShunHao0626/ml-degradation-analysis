# Interface engineering via an insulating polymer for highly efficient and environmentally stable perovskite solar cells

Xiaoru Wen,  $  ^{a}  $   Jiamin Wu,  $  ^{b}  $   Meidan Ye,  $  ^{c}  $   Di Gao  $  ^{b*}  $   and Changjian Lin  $  ^{a*}  $

A tunnelling contact of polystyrene nanofilm was firstly conducted at the interface of perovskite/hole transfer layer, leading to a significantly reduced charge recombination. Moreover, such polymeric contact worked as a hydrophobic encapsulation layer for effectively protecting perovskite against the humidity. The resultant PSCs displayed a peak efficiency of  $ 17.80\% $  (vs.  $ 15.90\% $  of the controle cell) and an enhanced stability.

Solar cells as a promising renewable energy technology that convert sunlight directly to electricity possess cost-effective, reliable and safe attributes, and thus show great potential in solving global energy problem. Recently, the solution-processed organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) had gained worldwide attention for their amazing photovoltaic performance as well as low cost.  $  ^{1-5}  $   Pioneering work by Miyasaka and his co-workers in 2009, employed the organometal halide perovskites as sensitizers for photovoltaic cells with a liquid electrolyte, and demonstrated a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 3.81%, which makes the material particularly attractive for the photovoltaic application.  $  ^{6}  $   Within a short period of time, the advancement in PCE of PSCs progressed very rapidly, and a certified PCE over 20% was reported recently, which largely exceeds the development speed of all other thin-film solar cells such as amorphous silicon, dye-sensitized, quantum dot-sensitized as well as organic solar cells.  $  ^{4,7}  $   The explosive efficiency of PSCs is mostly attributed to superior photovoltaic properties of lead halide perovskites such as excellent absorption coefficient, tunable band gap by managing chemical compositions, ambipolar charge transport, and long electron-hole diffusion length,

which make PSCs become the most promising candidates for the third-generation photovoltaic technologies.  $  ^{3,8-11}  $

For efficient PSCs, the perovskite films are always sandwiched between the electron transport layers (ETLs) and hole transport layers (HTLs). In pursue of high performance, enormous efforts have been made on each isolated layer, with a major emphasis on perovskite film processing and relevant material design. Besides the optimization of each layer, it has been demonstrated that the precise photo-carrier control along the relevant interfaces (ETL/perovskite as well as the perovskite/HTL) becomes the main challenge for high efficiency.  $  ^{5,12}  $   To date, various interfacial strategies have been developed by doping additional interlayer including metal-based semiconductors, metal, polymer, graphene derivatives and silane to facilitate the carrier extraction and transport from the perovskite to the ETL or HTL layer for an enhanced photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{13-19}  $

The tunnelling junction, a very thin insulating layer at the interface, can act as the contact layer for suppressing the photo-carrier recombination, which has been successfully developed in the field of silicon solar cells.  $  ^{20, 21}  $   The inserted insulating layer is able to selectively conduct one type of charges while block the other one simultaneously. Thus it benefits the spatial separation of the photo-generated electrons and holes, resulting in the reduced charge recombination at the corresponding interface. However, the most widely used oxide or amorphous silicon layers are generally deposited by the relatively high-temperature vacuum process for silicon solar cells, which is not compatible for PSCs application. Quite recently, inspired by the success of applying tunnelling contacts in silicon solar cells, Huang's group proposed an insulating polymer tunnelling layer between the perovskite and ETL interface for the inverted PSCs.  $  ^{22}  $   The polymer tunnelling contact was demonstrated to selectively tunnel electrons while blocking holes, which suppressed the charge recombination of perovskite/ETL interface. Herein, we firstly proposed the insulating polymer as the tunnelling junction at the perovskite/HTL interface (Fig. 1A), and achieved a significantly improved the efficiency of PSCs. The polystyrene (PS) suspension was deposited onto the perovskite layer by the spin-coating method without any further annealing treatment. The energy level matching between the perovskite and spiro-OMeTAD allows the photo-generated holes tunnel through the ultrathin insulating PS contact from the valence band of perovskite to that of the spiro-OMeTAD, while blocks the tunnelling of photo-generated electrons. Thus, electrons and holes are spatially separated at this contact, and thus suppress the charge recombination of the perovskite/HTL interface. In addition to the efficiency, the inferior long-term stability of perovskite suffered by the humidity always becomes a big challenge for PSC application. Recently, many interfacial layers have been inserted at the relevant junction, and an evidently enhanced stability has been achieved.  $  ^{19, 23}  $   Here, the superhydrophobic PS ultrathin film may also behave as an encapsulation layer to protect the perovskite from the humidity-caused decomposition.

The commercially available PS dissolved in chlorobenzene with a concentration of  $ 10\mathrm{mg / mL} $  was used as the tunnelling contact at the perovskite/HTL junction. The entire preparation was conducted in the open air at a relative humidity of  $ 45\% $ . The tunnelling role of PS interlayer at the perovskite/HTL interface was investigated by the measurements of electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), which is a powerful technique for monitoring the interfacial change for photovoltaic devices.  $  ^{25, 26}  $   The Nyquist plots were recorded under the dark condition, applying a 5 mV AC signal over the constant applied bias of 0.9 V in the frequency range from 0.01 Hz to 100 kHz as depicted in Fig. 3A. Generally, the main arc at the low frequency region can be ascribed to the recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) as well as chemical capacitance of cells,  $  ^{27, 28}  $   and the increased diameter of semicircle is always related with an improved recombination resistance at the corresponding interface. Considering the identical cell assembling processing besides the interface engineering of perovskite/HTL junction, the difference of   $  R_{rec}  $   was proposed to be caused by the thin PS incorporation. Noteworthy, an evidently enhanced   $  R_{rec}  $   has been obtained for PSCs with the insulating PS interlayer, suggesting the sufficiently suppressed electron-hole recombination at the interface. Moreover, the transient   $  V_{oc}  $   as a function of time upon turning off the illumination was applied to monitor the electron recombination kinetics of solar cells.  $  ^{29}  $   It found that once turning off the illumination, the   $  V_{oc}  $   decayed sharply due to the photo-carrier recombination, and the   $  V_{oc}  $   decay rate directly indicated the charge recombination rate. Clearly seen in Fig. 3B, the PSCs with the PS tunnelling contact exhibited an evidently slower   $  V_{oc}  $   decay rate than the control device, suggesting that the suppressed photo-charge recombination can be achieved by the PS incorporation. Besides the high performance of PSCs, the long-term stability against the humidity becomes the main concern towards the commercial application of PSCs. In this work, we demonstrated that the hydrophobic PS nanofilm at the interface of perovskite/HTL can really act as the water-resistant layer for perovskite, and a significantly enhanced water contact angle (98.1°) has been achieved compared to that of the control device (86.4°, Fig. 4A-B). The improved water-resistant ability was proposed to be beneficial to inhibit the entrance of water molecules into the perovskite film, yielding the enhanced stability. So, the long-term durability of resultant devices was investigated by storing them in the dark air with a relative humidity of ca. 45% without any encapsulation layer. As illustrated in Fig. 4C, the devices with PS layer retained over 70% of the original efficiency after 10 days, while the control cell only maintained 23%. As expected, an evident improvement of stability against the humidity has been achieved after the insertion of a thin PS layer at the perovskite/HTL interface. So, the hydrophobic PS film is able to behave as an efficient water protector for perovskite layer. It is believed that the facile processing as well as the low cost make such interface engineering strategy possess an attractive potential for further PSCs commercialization.

# Conclusions

A facile, low-cost and efficient interfacial engineering strategy with PS interlayer at the perovskite/HTL junction has been proposed for the first time. Here, the incorporated insulating PS acted as a tunnelling contact for selectively conducting holes while blocking the electrons at the perovskite/hole transfer layer interface with a proper band alignment, resulting in the reduced carrier recombination. Moreover, the super-hydrophobic PS layer played as an encapsulation layer to protect the perovskite film against the humidity condition. The dual-functional role of such PS interlayer at the perovskite/HTL interface enabled a significantly enhanced efficiency and long-term stability of the resultant PSCs, which showed a direction for further development of high-performance and high-stability PSCs.
