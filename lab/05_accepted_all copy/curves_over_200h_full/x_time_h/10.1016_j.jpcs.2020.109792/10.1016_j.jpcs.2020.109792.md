# Abstract

Incredible progress in the efficiency of hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has nominated perovskites as a promising light-harvesting for new-generation photovoltaic devices. Additive engineering in PSCs is a valid and straightforward method to increase the surface quality of perovskite films. Herein, we prepared mixed cation of cesium (Cs)/formamidinium (FA)/methylammonium (MA) PSCs via an air-processed sequential deposition method. Acetonitrile (ACN) was used as an effective additive to improve the surface properties of perovskite layer. Our measurements demonstrated that with the incorporation of ACN in the FAI/MABr/MACl precursor surface defects on the perovskite film well passivated, and consequently, crystal grain size increased. A PCE of  $ 15.64\% $  was achieved for ACN-based PSCs higher than of  $ 13.06\% $  one for without additive devices. Additionally, ACN-containing devices showed a lower hysteresis effect as compared to the control devices. Most importantly, ACN additive based PSCs showed more stable behavior in ambient air, thermal, and humidity stability tests. This work introduces a simple approach for the fabrication of high-performance perovskite materials in ambient air.

Keywords: Acetonitrile; Humidity stability; Perovskite solar cells; Hysteresis effect

# 1. Introduction

Hybrid perovskite materials, due to their unique properties, have been introduced as favorable nominees for new-generation photovoltaic (PV) technology   $  {}^{1-7}  $  . The Perovskite materials are represented with a general form of ABX  $  _{3}  $  , wherein A is a monovalent organic cation (MA  $  ^{+}  $   (CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{2}  $    $  ^{+}  $  ), FA  $  ^{+}  $   (CH(NH  $  _{2}  $  )  $  _{2}  $    $  ^{+}  $  , or Cs  $  ^{+}  $  ), B is a metal cation (Pb  $  ^{2+}  $   or Sn  $  ^{2+}  $  ), and X is a halogen anion (Cl  $  ^{-}  $  , Br  $  ^{-}  $   or I). These materials have high absorption capacity   $  {}^{8}  $  , long-range charge

In the beginning, perovskite materials were used as a light-harvesting layer in photovoltaics by Miyasaka et al. in 2009 with a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 3.7%  $  ^{12}  $ . After that, efforts to enhance the performance of PSCs have been developed. In 2012, by employing spiro-OMeTAD as a hole-transporting layer, the PCE of PSCs reached 9.7%. PCE of PSCs swiftly raised to 23.32% and 23.48% in mesoscopic and planer based PSCs, respectively  $  ^{13-14}  $ . In the past decade, various technologies such as solvent engineering  $  ^{15}  $ , additive engineering  $  ^{16}  $ , interfacial engineering  $  ^{17}  $ , and compositional engineering  $  ^{18}  $ , have been developed to increase the performance of PSCs. Among these, additive engineering to passivate the surface defects of perovskite film has emerged as an efficient approach for fabricating high-performance and stable PSCs  $  ^{13-14,19}  $ .

Despite the advantages of PSCs, there is a significant barrier to the commercialization of PSCs, which is their low intrinsic stability and must be addressed  $ ^{20} $ . In 2016, Saliba et al. introduced cesium-containing mixed-cation perovskite materials as an effective solution to overcome the stability issue of PSCs  $ ^{21} $ . After that, cation engineering within the A-site of Cs-containing perovskite materials to enhance the stability of PSCs has emerged  $ ^{22} $ . In recent years, different composition of Cs-containing mixed perovskite materials, like  $ \mathrm{Cs_xMA_{1 - x}PbI_3} $   $ ^{23} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Cs_xFA_{1 - x}PbI_3} $   $ ^{24} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Cs_x(FA_{1 - y}MA_y)_{1 - x}PbI_3} $   $ ^{25} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Cs_xFA_{1 - x}Pb(I_{1 - y}Br_y)_3} $   $ ^{26} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Cs_xFA_{1 - x - y}MA_yPb(I_{1 - z}Br_z)_3} $   $ ^{27} $ , and  $ \mathrm{Cs_xFA_{1 - x - y}GA_yPb(I_{1 - z}Br_z)_3} $   $ ^{28} $  have been used to address stability issue of PSCs. Liu et al. used triple cation  $ \mathrm{Cs_{0.05}(FA_{0.4}MA_{0.6})_{0.95}PbI_{2.8}Br_{0.2}} $  perovskite as a photon harvester and showed that corresponding PSCs exhibit excellent thermal stability at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , over  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $ . They showed that the incorporation of Cs in perovskite could boost charge diffusion length benefiting for printable perovskite solar cells  $ ^{29} $ . In 2018, Long et al. showed that by a simple FABr postannealing treatment of  $ \mathrm{Cs_{0.17}FA_{0.83}PbI_2Br} $  could even furthermore increase the irradiation stability of Cs-containing PSCs  $ ^{30} $ . In 2019, Zhao et. al. showed that by water additive and also interface engineering by PFN-P2 layer on the inverted  $ \mathrm{Cs_xFA_{1 - x - y}MA_yPb(I_{1 - z}Br_z)_3} $  PSCs could improve both stability and performance of PV devices, caused by crystallinity and optical properties improvements of the perovskite film and also recombination reduction at the HTL/perovskite interface  $ ^{31} $ . Recently, quadruple-cations perovskite materials with Cs like RbCsMAFA and KCsFAMA perovskites were also introduced as high stable PSCs  $ ^{32-33} $ . In these reports revealed that through cation-engineering can reach to fast charge transportation, low interface defect intensity, and low non-radiative recombination in PSCs.

In this research, the Cs-containing mixed perovskite solar cells were fabricated via an air-processed sequential deposition method (AP-SDM). To improve the photovoltaic (PV) performance of fabricated PSCs via AP-SDM, additive engineering through low-cost acetonitrile (ACN) was employed. Herein, ACN solvent with different concentrations was added into the FAI/MABr/MACl solution to find out how much ACN is enough. Results revealed that through ACN additive treatment, the crystal size of the perovskite layer increased, and surface defects of it were well suppressed. It was showed that PV parameters of ACN-based PSCs show higher values as compared to without additive devices, which indicates ACN additive suppresses non-radiative recombination in perovskite film. Besides, hysteresis phenomena in passivated PSCs with ACN were lower due to better surface quality of the corresponding film. Through ACN passivation of the perovskite material, an efficiency of  $ 15.64\% $  was achieved, which is higher than of  $ 13.06\% $  one for control devices. Additionally, unencapsulated ACN-based PSCs showed more stable behavior than control devices. From obtained results in here and compare with similar works, it can be argued that the most beneficial approach of ACN usage in PSCs is adding it into cations solution as reported here.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1 Materials preparation

MA halides (MAX; X=I, Cl, and Br), and FA iodide (FAI) were prepared as mentioned in previous work   $  {}^{34-35}  $  . In brief, MAX (X= Cl, Br, and I) were prepared by using an equimolar solution of methylamine in ethanol (40% w, Merck) and hydriodic acid (HI, 67% w, Merck), hydrochloric acid (HCl, 41% w, Merck), and hydro-bromic acid (HBr, 35% w, Merck), respectively, followed by mixing at 0 °C for 2 h. FAI was prepared by mixing formamidine acetate (98%, Merck) with a double molar of HI acid. The resulting powders were collected by an evaporator at 60 °C. The light-yellow powders were rinsed repeatedly with chlorobenzene and recrystallized with methanol. Subsequently, the white products of MAX and FAI were dried in a vacuum oven.

The   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   and lead iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  ) stock solutions were prepared by a previously reported procedure. After that,   $  CsPbI_{3}/PbI_{2}  $   dispersion was synthesized through mixing   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   precursors (9:1 v/v). For the acetonitrile (ACN) additive-based method, 0~12 v% of ACN solvent was dropped wise in the FAI/MABr/MACl mixture under stirring for 40 min at RT. The FAI/MABr/MACl solution was synthesized by using previous procedure   $  {}^{36}  $  . The Spiro-OMeTAD mixture was synthesized via inserting 17.5   $  \mu  $  l bis (trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI, 99.95, Merck) in ACN (99.9%, Sigma-Aldrich) (520 mg/ml), and 28.8   $  \mu  $  l 4-tertbutylpyridine (Merck, 97%) to a 60 mM spiro-OMeTAD in chlorobenzene (99.8%, Merck).

# 2.2 Device Fabrication

The patterned fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO, 15  $ \Omega/\mathrm{sq} $ ) glasses were washed with soap, deionized water, acetone, and isopropyl alcohol, consecutively. A compact TiO $ _2 $  (c-TiO $ _2 $ ) film was deposited on the substrate at 2000 rpm for 30 s via spin coating using a mixture of titanium(IV) isopropoxide (TTIP, 97%, Merck) in ethanol, followed by annealing at 500°C for 25 min. Then, a mesoporous TiO $ _2 $  (mp-TiO $ _2 $ ) film was spinning coated on FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $  substrates using a paste TiO $ _2 $  in ethanol at 4000 rpm for 60 s, followed by baking at 440 °C for 40 min. The perovskite layer was fabricated via an air-processed sequential deposition method, as reported in  $ ^{36} $ . In brief, the CsPbI $ _3 $ /PbI $ _2 $  mixture was spun on the electron transport layer at 1500 rpm for 30 s. Then, the as-synthesized FAI/MABr/MACl with ACN solvent was deposited on the PbI $ _2 $  film at 1300 rpm for 20 s. After that, the spiro-OMeTAD material was prepared on the perovskite film as a hole transfer layer (HTL) by depositing at 4000 rpm for 30 s. Subsequently, 100 nm Au counter electrodes were thermally deposited on the HTL.

# 2.3 Characterization

The surface quality of perovskites was studied by a field emission SEM (Mira 3). The x-ray diffraction patterns of perovskite layers were collected using an x-ray diffraction spectrometer (XRD, Shimadzu). The absorbance of perovskites was obtained using a Perkin Elmer ultraviolet-visible spectrometer. Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) measurements were recorded via Varian Cary Eclipse Fluorescence (excitation at  $ 405\mathrm{nm} $ ). The electrical properties of PSCs were recorded under  $ 100\mathrm{mA/cm}^2 $  intensity using a Keithley Model 2400.

The presence of ACN in perovskite solution reduces the activation energy of perovskite crystallization, which increases crystal grain size perovskite film, and consequently, reduces grain boundaries (Figure 3e)  $ ^{33,46-48} $ . In an isothermal grain growth process of perovskite film, a simple relationship between the grain boundaries velocity ( $ v $ ), the activation energy of grain boundary ( $ Q_G $ ), temperature (T) was proposed, as follow  $ ^{49-50} $ .

$$
v = \frac {d D}{d t} = \frac {\sigma}{D} \times e x p \left(- \left. Q _ {G} \right/ _ {R T}\right)
$$

(1)

Through Integrating from the equation 1 and evaluating the integration constant at  $  t = 0  $ , can write:

$$
D ^ {2} - D _ {0} ^ {2} = \sigma \times e x p \left(- \left. Q _ {G} \right/ _ {R T}\right) t
$$

(2)

Which D is the average grain size at time of t,  $  \mathrm{D}_0  $  is the initial grain size and  $  \sigma  $  is a weakly temperature-dependent constant. If we consider that  $  \mathrm{D}_0^2  $  is negligible compared to  $  \mathrm{D}^2  $ , then can simplify equation 2 to:

$$
D = \sqrt {\sigma \times e x p \left(- \left. Q _ {G} \right/ _ {R T}\right) t}
$$

(3)

So, from the above relation can argue that when the average grain size of perovskite film increases, consequently, the activation energy of perovskite crystallization reduces. This improved crystallinity film can boost charge transport due to reducing non-radiative charge recombination   $  {}^{51}  $  . # 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) were fabricated through an air-processed sequential deposition process. Low-cost acetonitrile (ACN) solvent was introduced as an additive into the perovskite solution. By adding the ACN additive to the FAI/MABr/MACl precursor, the efficiency of PSCs increased to 15.64% from 13.06% for control devices. The obtained results showed that the ACN additive improved the surface quality of perovskite film and reduced the grain boundaries of film. Also, it was observed that in ACN-containing perovskite, non-radiative recombination suppressed, which led to a higher PCE in the corresponding cells. Also, the ACN-based devices showed more stable behavior against humidity and heat as compared to control devices. Besides, from comparing our results with similar works, it clarified that adding ACN additive into FAI/MABr/MACl precursor is the most effective approach of the ACN usage in PSCs.
