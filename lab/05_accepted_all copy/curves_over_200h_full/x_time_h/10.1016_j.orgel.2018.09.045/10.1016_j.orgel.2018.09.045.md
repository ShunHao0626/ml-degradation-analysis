# Abstract:

Three novel dopant-free hole-transporting materials (HTMs) based on phthalocyanine core containing (4-methyl formate) phenoxy or (4-butyl formate) phenoxy as the peripheral groups with copper or zinc as the core metals  $ \mathrm{(CuPcNO_2 - OMFPh} $ ,  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2 - OBFPh} $ ,  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2 - OBFPh} $  were designed and synthesized. All of the phthalocyanine complexes show excellent thermal stabilities, appropriate energy levels and suitable hole mobilities. The potential of three HTMs were tested in perovskite solar cells (PSCs) and  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2 - OBFPh} $  based PSC obtained power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 15.74\% $  under  $ 100\mathrm{mA}\cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $  standard AM 1.5G solar illumination. Most important of all, PSC based on  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2 - OBFPh} $  shows better stability than that of the other two phthalocyanines and Spiro-OMeTAD under continuous light irradiation at  $ 60^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and maximum power point tracking in ambient air without encapsulation after  $ 500\mathrm{h} $ . The results show that the introduction of appropriate peripheral groups and core metals can improve the performance and stability of PSCs dramatically, which provides an alternative way to develop HTMs for efficient and stable PSCs.

words: Hole transporting material; Perovskite solar cells; Stability; Metallophthalocyanine

# 1. Introduction

In the past few years, hybrid organic-inorganic perovskites solar cells (PSCs) are appearing as a hopeful new generation of photovoltaic technology, resulting from their low material cost, low fabrication temperature, and superb photovoltaic performance. And their power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) have rapidly increased from  $ 3.8\% $  to  $ 23.3\% $  [1-4]. The outstanding photoelectric properties of perovskite materials make them appropriate for light harvesting in photovoltaics, such as high absorption coefficient, suitable and adjustable band gap [5-6], ambipolar charge transport [7-8], and long carrier diffusion length [9].

Despite the rapid increment in PCE associated with the evolution of new perovskite materials and novel fabrication techniques, the instability of PSC remains a challenge. Perovskite are forceless against oxygen, moisture, heat stresses, light illumination and interfacial degradation, resulting in reduced device performance [10-19]. Therefore, issues concerning long-term stability of perovskite solar cells still need to be addressed. Superior HTMs play an important role in improving the performance and long-time stability of PSCs. They can not only transport holes and block electrons to retard charge recombination [20-21], but also act as an encapsulation layer to protect perovskite from degradation by oxygen and moisture within a conventional device architecture [22-24]. The excellent HTMs such as 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis (N,N'-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) [25], poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT) [26], and poly(triarylamine) (PTAA) [27] have been reported to boost device efficiencies in the past few years.

While, all the conventional HTMs suffer from many disadvantages as high synthetic cost, thermal and chemical instability [28-30], that seriously hinder the commercialization of this kind of technology. In this regard, many efforts have been made to develop alternative HTM recently. Phthalocyanines with  $ 18\pi $  electrons, have obtained increasing attention due to their prominent thermal and chemical stability, appropriate redox potential, and low cost [31-35]. The core metal and peripheral groups of the Pc ring could play a key role in improving PSC performance by altering the HOMO and LUMO energies of the phthalocyanines [36-40]. Different core metals and peripheral groups can lead to different  $ \pi-\pi $  stacking in phthalocyanine crystal lattices and can alter the charge mobility of phthalocyanine materials [41-43]. But there is few study focusing on peripheral groups and core metal for PSCs at the same time for the tuning of hole mobilities with high performance and stability under continuous sunlight soaking at high temperature.

In this work, phthalocyanine core containing (4-methyl formate) phenoxy or (4-butyl formate) phenoxy as the peripheral groups and copper or zinc as the core metals  $ (\mathrm{CuPcNO}_2\text{-OMFPh}, \mathrm{CuPcNO}_2\text{-OBFPh}, \mathrm{ZnPcNO}_2\text{-OBFPh}) $  were introduced as HTMs in PSCs. For comparison, Spiro-OMeTAD-based PSCs were also made as the control devices. The influences of both peripheral groups and core metal on the physiochemical properties and stability of the PSCs were all studied. The optimal  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO}_2\text{-OBFPh} $ -based device performance with an efficiency of  $ 15.74\% $  has been achieved, a little lower than that of the Spiro-OMeTAD device. In addition, its PCE maintained

# 2.3. Synthesis

3.3.1. Synthesis of 4-aryloxy phthalonitrile

At room temperature, 5.9 mmol 4-nitrophthalonitrile (1.021 g) was dissolved in redistilled dimethylformamide (DMF, 40 mL) and then 8.26 mmol aryl phenol (methyl 4-hydroxybenzoate 1.256 g or butyl 4-hydroxybenzoate 1.602 g) was added. After 15 min stirring, finely ground   $  K_{2}CO_{3}  $   (1.140 g, 8.26 mmol) was added for three times. The reaction was not ended until all 4-nitrophthalonitrile disappeared by thin layer chromatography (TLC) monitoring. The yellowish precipitate was obtained by pouring the mixture into cold water (100 mL). It was dealt with 1% NaOH till pH to 9-10 and precipitated for a night.

The filtered crude product (4-(4-methyl formate) phenoxy phthalonitrile) was recrystallized in DMF and ethanol to give milky white crystals. Yield: 1.60 g, 97.4%, m.p. 148–150 °C. # 2.4. Solar cell fabrication details

A 30 nm-thickness compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer, 200-300 nm mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers and perovskite layer (  $  [(FAI)_{0.85}(PbI_{2})_{0.85}(MABr)_{0.15}(PbBr_{2})_{0.15}]  $  ) were prepared according to the reported methods [46]. The HTMs layers were spin-coated on the top of   $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite films at 3000 rpm for 20 s with a concentration of 20 mg·mL  $  ^{-1}  $  . For comparison, perovskite solar cells based on Spiro-OMeTAD were also fabricated by using a chlorobenzene solution doped with Lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) and 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) under the same conditions. All the above fabrication processes were carried out in air. Finally, 80 nm-thickness Au photocathode was deposited by thermal evaporation.

In DMF solution, the three phthalocyanines showed typical maximum photoluminescence (PL) emission peaks at 678, 698 and  $ 702\mathrm{nm} $  respectively that can be seen in Fig. 2b.  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2 - OBFPh} $  exhibited the most intense PL intensity for the core metal and (4-alkyl formate) phenoxy substituent [50], while  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2 - OMFPh} $  shows the least PL intensity for the open-shell copper metal and smaller (4-methyl formate) phenoxy substituents.

After the phthalocyanines HTMs were coated on top of perovskite films, the PL intensity (Fig. 2c) was reduced roughly to 40.6, 19.9 and  $ 3.5\% $  of that obtained from pristine perovskite films for  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OMFPh,  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OBFPh and  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OBFPh, respectively, suggesting that fast interface charge separation in  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OBFPh-based devices can contribute to a better Jsc and FF in the PSCs compared to  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OMFPh-based devices but lag far behind the hole capture rate by  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OBFPh [51].

The hole mobilities of HTM plays an important role for the properties of solar cells. There are many ways to obtained the result of hole mobilities [29,52-54]. The three phthalocyanines' hole mobilities were determined by the common-used space charge-limited current (SCLC) method. The obtained intrinsic hole mobilities (Fig. 2d) of  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OMFPh,  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OBFPh and  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OBFPh are  $ 5.02 \times 10^{-5} $ ,  $ 8.21 \times 10^{-5} $ , and  $ 11.4 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~cm}^2 \cdot \mathrm{V}^{-1} \cdot \mathrm{s}^{-1} $ , respectively, which is a little lower than that of doped Spiro-OMeTAD ( $ 4.7 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~cm}^2 \mathrm{~V}^{-1} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} $ ) obtained by the same method [55]. Phthalocyanines with bulky (4-butyl formate) phenoxy groups show much higher hole mobilities than (4-methyl formate) phenoxy groups for stronger electron donating effect of butyl groups and the smaller molecular aggregation caused by large space hindrance, while,  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OBFPh brings out the most superior mobility of the three phthalocyanines for the natural of core metal [50].

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA shows) that all the three phthalocyanines have superior thermal stability. The decomposition temperatures of  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OMFPh,  $ \mathrm{CuPcNO_2} $ -OBFPh and  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OBFPh are as high as 437, 416,  $ 414^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , respectively, which are higher than that of Spiro-OMeTAD [51] (Fig. S6).

# 3.4. The illumination stability of PSCs

Another important factor for estimating new HTM materials is the stability of devices. The photo-stability of corresponding PSCs was studied by keeping the devices under continuous light irradiation at 60 °C and maximum power point tracking in ambient air without encapsulation. As shown in Fig. 6b, the PCE maintained 46.2%, 57.8% and 72.3% of the initial value in the   $  CuPcNO_{2}  $  -OMFPh,   $  CuPcNO_{2}  $  -OBFPh and   $  ZnPcNO_{2}  $  -OBFPh-based PSC after 500 h of full sunlight light exposure. While, only 7.9% initial value was maintained in Spiro-OMeTAD-based PSC, indicating that all of the phthalocyanine-based PSCs show the prominent stability [46]. We ascribed the improvement of the devices' stability mainly due to the less pinholes in the HTM layer, the higher   $  T_{d}  $   and its increased hydrophobicity [43,61,68-69] which can seen from the contact angles in Fig. 6c. As a result, the   $  ZnPcNO_{2}  $  -OBFPh-based PSC showed a much stronger resistance to degradation under ambient conditions over longer time periods than the other two corresponding HTM-based devices.

# 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we have synthesized three novel phthalocyanines with different core metals and peripheral groups (CuPcNO $ _2 $ -OMFPh, CuPcNO $ _2 $ -OBFPh and ZnPcNO $ _2 $ -OBFPh). They were successfully applied as dopant-free hole transporting materials in perovskite solar cells based on their high stability, suitable hole motilities, and appropriate HOMO and LUMO energy levels alignment. All the PSCs based on the newly synthesized metallophthalocyanine obtain higher PCE than  $ 10\% $  under AM 1.5G standard conditions. Most of all, as the HTM, ZnPcNO $ _2 $ -OBFPh based PSC affords an impressive PCE of  $ 15.21\% $  with  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of  $ 1.10\mathrm{V} $ ,  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  of  $ 21.00\mathrm{mA}\cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $  and FF of 0.66. The device based on ZnPcNO $ _2 $ -OBFPh obtained the highest stability than that of CuPcNO $ _2 $ -OMFPh, CuPcNO $ _2 $ -OBFPh at room temperature aged under continuous light irradiation in  $ \mathbf{N}_2 $  without encapsulation after  $ 500\mathrm{h} $ . The researches show that both the central metals and peripheral substituents will affect the performance of PSCs signally. These results create a new direction for designing HTMs by using aryl oxygen and nitro as the peripheral groups and different core metals in the future deployment of high performance and stable PSCs.

# Highlights

- Novel asymmetric metallophthalocyanines (CuPcNO $ _2 $ -OMFPh, CuPcNO $ _2 $ -OBFPh and ZnPcNO $ _2 $ -OBFPh) were synthesized

● They were applied in perovskite solar cells as hole transporting material

- The cell devices based on  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OBFPh show excellent stability under continuous light irradiation

- The power conversion efficiency of the device based on dopant-free  $ \mathrm{ZnPcNO_2} $ -OBFPh is up to  $ 15.74\% $
