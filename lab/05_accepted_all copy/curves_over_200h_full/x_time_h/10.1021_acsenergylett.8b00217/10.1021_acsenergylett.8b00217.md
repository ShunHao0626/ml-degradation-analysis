ABSTRACT: Modification of  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  by using fullerenes is an efficient strategy to further improve the device efficiency and enhance the cell stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, the intrinsic issues of low electron mobility and electrical conductivity of fullerene materials may restrict their potential application in PSCs. Here, we demonstrated an n-doped electron-transporting layer by doping bis(1-[3-(methoxycarbonyl)propyl]-1-phenyl)-[6,6]C62 (Bis-PCBM) with decamethylcobaltocene (DMC) to fabricate n-i-p structure PSCs. We successfully realized an n-type doping of Bis-PCBM via a solution-processed doping process. DMC-doping played a series of roles in adjusting the energy levels, improving the electron mobility and enhancing the film conductivity of Bis-PCBM. Bis-PCBM:DMC composite film could act as an underlay for the deposition and growth of subsequent perovskite layer owing to its smooth morphology. In addition, Bis-PCBM:DMC composite film also presented good solvent resistance, which could slow-down the degradation process of perovskite layer. Consequently, a champion device with a maximum power conversion efficiency of  $ 20.14\% $  was approached.

Organic-inorganic hybrid halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) demonstrate strong competitiveness in photovoltaic field by their unimaging rising in power conversion efficiency (PCE) in past several years.  $  ^{1-5}  $   Although a certified PCE of 22.1% in a single-junction device has been approached,  $  ^{6}  $   further efforts on fabricating PSCs with high-efficiency and long-term stability simultaneously are necessary to realize their commercialization. Particularly, the long-term stability of PSCs with at least 10 years outdoor operation remains to be proven. In addition to the perovskite layer itself, the construction of adjacent interfacial layers also strongly affects the device stability.  $  ^{7-12}  $   Ideal interfacial materials should possess desired physical, chemical and electronic properties including appropriate energy level, high conductivity, high solvent resistance and low optical absorption. In addition, the interfacial films are expected to have good wettability as an underlayer for the deposition and growth of perovskite films.  $  ^{13,14}  $   Recently, various interfacial materials have been employed in planar structure PSCs to prevent the erosion of moisture, oxygen, and UV light into the perovskite layer with a goal of improving the cell stability.  $  ^{10-16}  $   Planar PSCs could be divided into p-i-n (inverted) and n-i-p (normal) structures based on layer stacking sequence.  $  ^{17-21}  $   Noticeably, most reported state-of-the-art PSCs adopt an n-i-p structure, in which titanium oxide (TiO  $  _{2}  $  ) is used as the electron-transporting layer (ETL). Although TiO  $  _{2}  $   has good electron-selective properties, its sensitivity to the surface adsorption of oxygen and UV-irradiation may restrict further improvements in efficiency and stability for perovskite solar cells. Modifications of TiO  $  _{2}  $   surface using bilayer-structured ETLs, i.e., TiO  $  _{2}  $ /PCBM ([6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester)  $  ^{22}  $ , TiO  $  _{2}  $ /Sb  $  _{2}  $  S  $  _{3}  $    $  ^{23}  $ , and TiO  $  _{2}  $ /ZnS  $  ^{24}  $ , have been demonstrated as an efficient strategy to further improve the efficiency and stability of PSCs by adjusting the interfacial properties between TiO  $  _{2}  $   and perovskite layers. One strategy for improving the electron mobility and electrical conductivity of fullerene is to utilize n-type doping technology by increasing the free electron concentration through efficient electron transfer from the n-type dopant to the host material. Although p-doped hole-transporting materials have been widely employed in PSCs, few studies on n-doped electron-selective materials are reported due to the lacking of efficient and stable n-type dopants. In addition, a chemical doping of solution-processed film is more challenging compared with the vacuum-evaporated one because the issue of solvent-induced erosion during the multi-layer lm deposition process has to be considered. Compared with alkali metals or alkali salts based inorganic n-type dopants  $  ^{25-28}  $  , organic based n-type dopants are regarded as the alternative for realizing the doping of solution-processed films owing to their merits of better solubility in common organic solvents and non-occurrence of atom diffusion  $  ^{29-34}  $  . Metallocenes with low ionization energy (IE) have proven to be an efficient donor for n-type doping.  $  ^{35-37}  $   Particularly, decamethylcobaltocene (DMC) possesses an even lower IE of 3.3 eV, which makes it act as a powerful reductant to n-dope a wider range of host materials.  $  ^{37}  $   To our knowledge, there is still no reports on the DMC-doped fullerenes as ETLs in perovskite solar cells up to date. Herein, we demonstrated an n-doped ETL by doping bis(1-[3-(methoxycarbonyl)propyl]-1-phenyl)-[6,6]C62 (Bis-PCBM) with DMC to fabricate efficient and stable n-i-p PSCs with a structure as shown in Figure 1. By utilizing the strong reductant capability of DMC, an efficient n-doping of Bis-PCBM was successfully realized based on a solution-processed doping process. Bis-PCBM: DMC composite films were prepared by spin-coating the mixed precursor solution consisting of Bis-PCBM (chlorobenzene) and DMC (Ethanol) with various doping ratio in a nitrogen-filled glove box. An annealing on a hotplate at 100 °C for 20 min was carried out to activate the doping. A full scan of X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) including C 1s, O 1s and Co 2p core levels (Figure S1, Supporting Information) reveals that DMC molecules did exist in Bis-PCBM: DMC composite films. The n-type doping of Bis-PCBM by DMC is further investigated by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy, Raman spectroscopy and electron spin resonance (ESR) as shown in Figure S2. and verified efficient electron transfer from the n-dopant DMC to Bis-PCBM.

To quantify the DMC doping effect on the defect energy distribution in the active layer of PSCs, thermal admittance spectroscopy measurements (Figure S4, Supporting Information) was carried out to evaluate the trap density of states   $  (tDOS)^{45}  $   in   $  TiO_{2}  $  ,   $  TiO_{2}/Bis-PCBM  $   and   $  TiO_{2}/Bis-PCBM:DMC  $   based PSCs. As shown in Figure 3d, the measured trap density in   $  TiO_{2}/Bis-PCBM:DMC  $   based device is reduced by two orders of magnitude at the shallow trap region between 0.25 and 0.30 eV compared with the   $  TiO_{2}  $   based device. The decreased tDOS is ascribed to the insertion of Bis-PCBM:DMC between   $  TiO_{2}  $   and perovskite layers. First, smooth surface morphology of Bis-PCBM:DMC prifors to form good contact with perovskite layer with reduced interfacial traps. Second, Bis-PCBM:DMC underlayer is beneficial for the deposition of high quality perovskite film with less bulk traps. In addition, the degradation of perovskite layer may proceed due to the surface defects. Therefore, the reducing of defect states is very likely to positively improve the device stability.  $  ^{30}  $

Figure 4e plots the J-V curves in TiO  $  _{2}  $ /Bis-PCBM and TiO  $  _{2}  $ /Bis-PCBM:DMC based PSCs with forward and reverse scan directions under AM 1.5G illumination with the light intensity of 100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $ . And the utilization of pure fullerene modified TiO  $  _{2}  $  did not improve the charge collection significantly. Fortunately, DMC-doped Bis-PCBM improved the interface contact with the perovskite layer, resulting in a relative low hysteresis in TiO  $  _{2}  $ /Bis-PCBM:DMC based device. In summary, we have developed a new ETL of DMC-doped Bis-PCBM to fabricate n-i-p planar structure PSCs. Efficient n-type doping of fullerene, which was clarified by XPS, FTIR, Raman and ESR evaluations, has been realized by DMC incorporating via a solution-processed doping process. DMC doping resulted in a downward movement of the LUMO of Bis-PCBM. Also, the doping improved the electron mobility and enhanced the film conductivity of Bis-PCBM films, resulting in efficient electron extraction and transport. Smooth Bis-PCBM:DMC film acted as an underlay for the deposition and growth of subsequent perovskite layer with large grain sizes and full film coverage. In addition, Bis-PCBM:DMC composite films also presented good stability with good solvent resistance, which retarded the degradation process of perovskite layer. As a result, a champion device with a maximum PCE of 20.14% with good stability was approached. The developed novel material provides the diversification of electron-transporting layers. Also, the finding provides a new route to realize n-type doping of fullerenes by using DMC-liked metallocenes.
