# Spiro-OMeTAD:  $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   Hole Transport Layer with Triple Functions of Overcoming Lithium Salt Aggregation, Long-Term High Conductivity, and Defect Passivation for Perovskite Solar Cells

Qing Du, Zhitao Shen, Chong Chen,  $  ^{*}  $   Fumin Li, Mengqi Jin, Huilin Li, Chao Dong, Jihong Zheng, Mingxing Ji, and Mingtai Wang  $  ^{*}  $

The development of a hole transport layer (HTL) with persistent high conductivity, good moisture/oxygen barrier ability, and suitable passivation ability of perovskite defects is very important for achieving high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and long-term stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, the state-of-the-art HTL, lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)-imide (Li-TFSI)-doped 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD), does not have these abilities. Herein, the incorporation of antimony sulfide (Sb₂S₃) nanoparticles as a multifunctional additive into spiro-OMeTAD is demonstrated. The Sb₂S₃ effectively improve the compactness of composite spiro-OMeTAD:Sb₂S₃ HTL by inhibiting the Li-TFSI aggregation and effectively prevent the infiltration of moisture and oxygen into the perovskite layer, resulting in its high chemical stability. More importantly, Sb₂S₃ not only improves the conductivity and hole mobility of the spiro-OMeTAD:Sb₂S₃ through the oxidation of spiro-OMeTAD by Sb₂S₃, but also makes the high conductivity more durable and stable in the atmospheric environment. In addition, Sb₂S₃ also effectively passivates the perovskite defects and accelerates the charge transfer from perovskite layer to HTL. As a consequence, the optimized PSCs based on spiro-OMeTAD:Sb₂S₃ HTL exhibit a much higher PCE (22.13%) than that (19.29%) of the PSCs without Sb₂S₃ and show a greatly improved stability.

The traditional structure of PSCs is formed over a transparent conductive glass substrate as anode, by sequentially depositing electron transport layer (ETL), perovskite absorber layer, hole transport layer

(HTL), and metal cathode (Au, Ag, or Al) on the top of each other into a planar superstrate architecture. Therefore, the perovskite absorber layer is sandwiched between the ETL and the HTL,  $  ^{[10,11]}  $  which facilitates the extraction of the photogenerated electrons and holes inside the perovskite bulk layer into a spatial state for photocurrent and voltage generation across the cell device, and provides a certain HTL coverer to prevent the moisture and oxygen from penetrating into the perovskite layer.  $  ^{[12,13]}  $  Obviously, the ETL and HTL have important influences on the performance and stability of PSCs.

At present, the HTL materials are mainly divided into inorganic materials such as   $  NiO_{x}  $  ,  $  ^{[14,15]}  $     $  CuSCN  $  ,  $  ^{[16]}  $     $  CuO  $  ,  $  ^{[17]}  $   and   $  CuInS_{2}  $    $  ^{[18]}  $   and organic molecules/polymer materials such as poly(3-hexylthiophene-2,5-diyl) (P3HT),  $  ^{[19]}  $   polytriarylamine (PTAA),  $  ^{[20]}  $   and 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD), among which the state-of-the-art HTL is spiro-OMeTAD first reported by Grätzel and Park in 2012,  $  ^{[21]}  $   mainly due to its excellent

# 1. Introduction

In the past decade, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) increased from 3.8% to 25.5% since the first report in 2009.  $  ^{[1,2]}  $   The ABX  $  _{3}  $   organic–inorganic hybrid perovskites, in which A (A = FA  $  ^{+}  $   (NH  $  _{2}  $  CH = NH  $  _{2}  $    $  ^{+}  $  ), MA  $  ^{+}  $  (CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $    $  ^{+}  $  ), Cs  $  ^{+}  $  ) is surrounded by BX  $  _{6}  $   octahedra (B = Pb  $  ^{2+}  $  , Sn  $  ^{2+}  $  , and X = Br  $  ^{-}  $  , Cl  $  ^{-}  $  , or I  $  ^{-}  $  ),  $  ^{[3-7]}  $   possess high charge mobility, long charge diffusion length, and easy film-forming ability and have received intensive attention as a promising absorbing material for solar cells.  $  ^{[8]}  $   However, the commercialization of PSCs is still seriously hindered by the poor long-term stability of the perovskites themselves because the organic–inorganic hybrid perovskites are sensitive to oxygen, moisture, and raised temperature.  $  ^{[9]}  $

A large number of chemical additives are used as additives (or dopants) to prepare spiro-OMeTAD HTL, such as protic ionic liquids,  $  ^{[26]}  $   AgTFSI,  $  ^{[27]}  $   F4-TCNQ,  $  ^{[28]}  $     $  K_{3}[Fe(CN)_{6}]  $  .  $  ^{[29]}  $   kalium persulfate,  $  ^{[30]}  $   lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)-imide (Li-TFSI),  $  ^{[31]}  $   and 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP).  $  ^{[32]}  $   Among these additives, Li-TFSI and tBP are predominate dopants utilized in improving the electrical properties of spiro-OMeTAD,  $  ^{[33]}  $   which is realized by the conductivity of lithium ion and the oxidation promotion of spiro-OMeTAD by Li-TFSI. In addition to adding additives to spiro-OMeTAD, oxidation of spiro-OMeTAD solution in advance is also an effective method.  $  ^{[34]}  $   Unfortunately, the hygroscopic characteristics of Li-TFSI and the easily formed Li-TFSI aggregation in the spiro-OMeTAD matrix due to phase separation make the spiro-OMeTAD film easily be damaged after exposure to air, that is, more and more holes or cracks appear over time inside the spiro-OMeTAD layer to deteriorate the hole transport property therein and accelerate the infiltration of oxygen and moisture into the beneath perovskite layer for a poor long-term device stability.  $  ^{[35]}  $   In addition, the surface defect states in the perovskite active layer have an important influence on the device performance and stability.  $  ^{[36]}  $   Unfortunately, Li-TFSI/tBP-doped spiro-OMeTAD has no passivation effect on the perovskite defects, therefore, it is urgent to further improve the conductivity, water oxygen resistance, and defect passivation ability of spiro-OMeTAD HTL.

Here, we develop a strategy to dope antimony sulfide   $  (\mathrm{Sb}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{3})  $   nanoparticles, along with the tBP and Li-TFSI as additives, into spiro-OMeTAD HTL to improve significantly the performance and stability of   $  Cs_{0.05}(FA_{0.85}MA_{0.15})_{0.95}Pb(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_{3}  $   (CsFAMA)-based PSCs.   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   as a suitable photovoltaic material has many excellent photoelectric properties. For example, it has a suitable bandgap of 1.4–1.8 eV.  $  ^{[37,38]}  $   Moreover,   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   has a good conductivity,  $  ^{[39]}  $   a good hydrophobicity, and a good chemical stability in air,  $  ^{[40]}  $   which can prevent oxygen and moisture from infiltrating into the beneath perovskite layer. In addition, the valence band (VB) energy levels of   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   matches well with that of the perovskite for the efficient extraction of photogenerated holes from the perovskite layer. Therefore, considering these advantages of   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $ , we attempt to add it as an additive in the spiro-OMeTAD layer. Our research results demonstrate that, by incorporating   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   nanoparticle into the spiro-OMeTAD, the newly formed S—H bonds between them change the electron density of   $  CH_{3}^{+}  $  and the density of states (DOS) in the bandgap of spiro-OMeTAD are greatly increased, resulting in the significantly enhanced conductivity of spiro-OMeTAD. Moreover,   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   nanoparticles also greatly hinder the conductivity degradation caused by the aggregation and moisture absorption of Li-TFSI. In addition,   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   also significantly reduces the defect state on the surface of the perovskite layer, suppresses the charge recombination, and promotes the charge extraction. In consequence, after optimizing the concentration of incorporated

$  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $  , the PCE of PSC based on the spiro-OMeTAD:  $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   HTL increased from 19.29% to 22.17% and also the device stability was greatly enhanced.

The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of the spiro-OMeTAD and spiro-OMeTAD:  $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   films are shown in Figure 1c,d, respectively. The white-ring-like Li-TFSI aggregates of 50–270 nm in size formed due to phase separation are clearly observed in the spiro-OMeTAD film (left top inset of Figure 1c). For comparison, the left bottom inset of Figure 1c shows the SEM image of the spiro-OMeTAD film without Li-TFSI/tBP doping, which shows a relatively flat and uniform surface without obvious aggregates and particles. It should be noted that, for the convenience of description, the following spiro-OMeTAD generally refers to the Li-TFSI/tBP-doped spiro-OMeTAD. After dispersing   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   powder, no white-ring-like Li-TFSI aggregates are evident, except for some   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   nanoparticles with the sizes ranging from 30 to 90 nm uniformly dispersed in the spiro-OMeTAD matrix (Figure 1d), which indicates that   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   powder has been ultrasonically dispersed into nanosized particles in tBP solvent and   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   nanoparticles effectively prevent the Li-TFSI aggregation, resulting in a more smooth and compact film. To prove the existence of   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   in spiro-OMeTAD matrix, Figure 1e compares the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of pristine   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   powder and prepared spiro-OMeTAD:  $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   composite film, from which the characteristic diffraction peaks corresponding to the (310) and (221) planes of   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   were observed in the composite films, proving the existence of   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   in the spiro-OMeTAD matrix. To reveal the reason why the dispersed   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   nanoparticles prevent Li-TFSI from the aggregation due to phase separation, atomic interaction between Li-TFSI and   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   is investigated by density functional theory (DFT) calculations. As shown in Figure 1f, Li ions in Li-TFSI and S atoms in   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   can form Li-S chemical bonds. Therefore, Li-TFSI molecules are bound to   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   nanoparticles by Li-S bonds, and the aggregation of Li-TFSI due to phase separation is suppressed for a good dispersion of Li-TFSI along with the   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   nanoparticles in spiro-OMeTAD matrix. The crystal structure of synthesized CsFAMA was examined by XRD (Figure S1b, Supporting Information) and top-view images of CsFAMA film and cross-sectional images of ITO/  $  SnO_{2}-KCl/CsFAMA/spiro-OMeTAD:Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   are shown in Figure S1c, Supporting Information. The UV–vis absorption properties of the ITO/  $  SnO_{2}-KCl/CsFAMA/spiro-OMeTAD:Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   films with different   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   doping concentrations (0, 0.3, 0.5, and 0.8 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) are shown in Figure S1d, Supporting Information, showing that the light absorption intensity of the films increases with the increase in the   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   concentrations as the result of the light absorption of   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   nanoparticles.

To better reveal the origin for the higher efficiency and stability in the spiro-OMeTAD:  $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $  -based devices, the conductivity in spiro-OMeTAD and spiro-OMeTAD:  $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   films is investigated. The calculated   $  \sigma  $   is   $  8.37 \times 10^{-4} ~S~cm^{-1}  $   for fresh spiro-OMeTAD:  $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   film, much higher than that   $  (5.92 \times 10^{-4} ~S~cm^{-1})  $   for fresh spiro-OMeTAD film. The surface hydrophobicity of HTL is a significant parameter affecting device performance. Water contact angle test can determine the moisture absorption tolerance of the material. To study the effect of   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   on the hydrophobicity of the spiro-OMeTAD film, the contact angles of spiro-OMeTAD and spiro-OMeTAD:   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   films are shown in Figure S6, Supporting Information, from which it can be observed that the contact angle increases from 47.4° to 68.2° after the incorporation of   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $ , indicating that the spiro-OMeTAD:   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   layer has better hydrophobic property than the spiro-OMeTAD and thus can effectively slow down the moisture penetration into the perovskite layer in the atmospheric environment and increase the long-term stability of the PSCs.

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, we demonstrate an effective approach of modifying spiro-OMeTAD layer with   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   nanoparticles that are easily available by ultrasonically dispersing   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   powder in tBP solvent to prepare spiro-OMeTAD:  $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   composite film as new HTL for PSCs. Compared with the spiro-OMeTAD, the spiro-OMeTAD:  $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   HTL displays enhanced conductivity owing to strong electric interaction between S atoms of   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   and   $  CH_{3}  $   group of spiro-OMeTAD. Furthermore, the spiro-OMeTAD:  $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   HTL shows a more persistent and high conductivity as the result of the inhibition of the Li-TFSI aggregation by   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $  . In addition, experimental and theoretical results also show that   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   can effectively passivate the defect states of perovskite films by forming S–Pb and Sb–I bonds and accelerating the charge transfer from perovskite layer to HTL when   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   is in contact with perovskite film. Therefore, compared with spiro-OMeTAD film, spiro-OMeTAD:  $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   composite film reduces the photogenerated charge recombination more effectively and shows a better charge transport. When the spiro-OMeTAD:  $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   is applied as a HTL in PSCs, more than 22% of PCE is achieved by spiro-OMeTAD:  $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $ -based PSCs, which is one of the highest PCEs of the CsFAMA PSCs based on spiro-OMeTAD HTL. The PSC with   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   also displays a better stability than the cell without   $  Sb_{2}S_{3}  $   after 85 h of continuous light aging at 25 °C and 1100 h under 25 °C in an ambient environment. Our results provide a promising and simple route for the efficient PSCs with a high long-term stability.
