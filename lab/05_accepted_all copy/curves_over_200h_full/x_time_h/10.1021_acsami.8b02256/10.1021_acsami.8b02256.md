# ABSTRACT

A simple alcohol-soluble perylene derivative (i.e., tetramethyl ammonium salt of perylene-3,4,9,10-tetracarboxylic acid; TMA-PTC) was prepared and applied as a cathode interlayer (CIL) to modify the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM / Ag} $  interface in planar p-i-n perovskite solar cells (PeSCs). As a result, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the TMA-PTC-based PeSCs is ca.  $ 30\% $  higher than that of the devices without CIL. It was revealed that the enhancement in PCE might be attributed to the improved electron-transporting and hole-blocking properties of the  $ \mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM / TMA - PTC / Ag} $  interfaces. Moreover, the TMA-PTC devices show remarkably higher stability than those without CIL probably due to the suppressed corrosion of perovskite on Ag cathode. Our findings thus demonstrate a multifunctional and solution-processable CIL which might be a promising block for the fabrication of low-cost, high efficiency and stable planar p-i-n PeSCs.

# 1. Introduction

In recent years, there are growing concerns for perovskite solar cells (PeSCs) due to their boosting power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 3.87% to 22.7% in merely eight years.  $  ^{1-6}  $   Mesoporous and planar heterojunctions are typical structures used for PeSCs.  $  ^{7-9}  $   Among them, mesoporous and planar n–i–p PeSCs generally require a high–temperature processing for the deposition of   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer (  $  \sim500\ ^{\circ}C  $  ),  $  ^{10}  $   and suffer from severe current–voltage hysteresis.  $  ^{11}  $   In contrast, planar p–i–n PeSCs with a structure of ITO/HTL/perovskite/ETL/Ag or Al allow for low–temperature processing (  $  <150\ ^{\circ}C  $  ) and can be fabricated at reduced cost,  $  ^{12,13}  $   which make them more promising for real applications. To further improve the PCE of planar p–i–n PeSCs, substantial efforts have been devoted to optimizing the perovskite and interfacial materials  $  ^{14-18}  $   as well as the configuration and fabrication techniques.  $  ^{19-22}  $

Interfacial engineering has been considered as one of most effective approaches toward highly efficient PeSCs, through which the carrier extraction and transport from perovskite layer to electrodes can be greatly enhanced.  $  ^{23,24}  $   In view of electron transport process, a fullerene derivative   $  [6,6]  $  -phenyl-  $  C_{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (  $  PC_{61}BM  $  ) has been widely used as electron acceptor layer for planar p-i-n PeSCs.  $  ^{25}  $   Note that the LUMO energy level of   $  PC_{61}BM  $   is around -3.9 eV,  $  ^{26}  $   much lower than the work function (WF) of commonly used Ag cathode (ca. ~4.7 eV).  $  ^{27}  $   Such an energy mismatch indicates that the pristine   $  PC_{61}BM  $   layer cannot form a prefect ohmic contact with Ag cathode. To circumvent this problem, a variety of cathode interface layers (CILs), including   $  C_{60}  $   derivatives,  $  ^{28-30}  $   perylene-diimide derivatives,  $  ^{31,32}  $   BCP,  $  ^{33}  $   PFN,  $  ^{34,35}  $   PN6,  $  ^{36}  $   LiF,  $  ^{37}  $     $  PEOz^{38}  $   and doped Bphen (i.e., Bphen:Ir(MDQ)  $  _{2}  $  (acac)),  $  ^{39}  $   have been applied to modify the   $  PC_{61}BM/cathode  $   interface by either a solution or vacuum–deposition processing. As a result, the performance of PeSCs has been remarkably improved owing to a

# 2.2 Device Fabrication

The indium tin oxide (ITO) conductive glasses with a sheet resistance less than   $  15 \Omega sq^{-1}  $   (Shenzhen Hnxc) were used as the substrate of PeSCs. Before use, the ITO substrates were thoroughly cleaned using the method reported before.  $  ^{40}  $   A thin layer of PEDOT:PSS (Al 4083, Clevios H. C. Stark co.) (ca. 60 nm) was first deposited on the ITO substrate by spin-coating at 2000 rpm for 40 s followed by an annealing process at 150 °C for 15 min in air. The PEDOT:PSS-coated ITO glasses were then transferred into a   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glovebox (<1 ppm of   $  H_{2}O  $   and   $  O_{2}  $  ). The perovskite film was prepared by spin-coating the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   precursor solution onto the PEDOT:PSS layer at 4000 rpm for 60 s and annealing at 80 °C for 2 h. To prepare the precursor solution of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $  , a mixture of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (99.5%, Materwin co.),   $  PbCl_{2}  $   (99.999%, Aldrich) and   $  PbI_{2}  $   (99.999%, Alfa Aesar) with a mole ratio of 5.75:1.6:1 was dissolved in DMF and stirred at 60 °C overnight inside the glovebox. The solution of   $  PC_{61}BM  $   (99%, Nano-C Inc.) in chlorobenzene (20 mg/mL) and TMA-PTC in alcohol (0.2 mg/mL) were deposited onto the perovskite film by spin-coating at 2000 rpm for 40 s in sequence. Finally, Ag electrodes (100 nm) were thermally evaporated on the TMA-PTC layer through a shadow mask under high vacuum (  $  <1.0\times10^{-5}  $   Pa). The active area of the PeSCs is defined as   $  0.04 cm^{2}  $   by the overlap region between the Ag and ITO. The electron-only devices were fabricated for the space-charge-limited-current (SCLC) measurements using a structure of ITO/  $  PC_{61}BM  $  /Perovskite/  $  PC_{61}BM/TMA-PTC/Ag  $  .

# 2.3 Material and device characterizations

UV–Vis absorption was obtained using a Hitachi F-4600 spectrophotometer. Photoluminescence spectra were obtained with Fluoromax-4 spectrophotometer (HORIBA JobinYvon Inc.) detected in the wavelength range of 650–800 nm and by the excitation at the wavelength of 475 nm with a slit width of 5 nm. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) characterizations were performed on an ESCALAB 250 spectrometer equipped with an Al Kα excitation source with a photon energy of 1486.6 eV. # 3. Results and discussions

# 3.1 Synthesis and characterizations of TMA-PTC

TMA–PTC was synthesized by simply mixing PTCDA and TMAH in an aqueous solution and stirring for 1 h. After reaction, the extra TMAH was removed along with water by vacuum evaporation and the TMA–PTC was obtained in high yield over 90%. Since no column-based separation is involved, which is otherwise necessary for most organic synthesis and separation including the above mentioned perylene diimide derivatives,  $  ^{31,32}  $  the preparation of TMA–PTC is scalable and cost-effective. The as-prepared TMA–PTC is highly soluble in both water and alcohol (see Figure S1 in Supporting Information (SI)). The molecular structure of TMA–PTC was confirmed by a combined study of  $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR and XPS. As shown in Figure 1a, the XPS survey spectrum clearly indicates the TMA–PTC layer is composed of carbon, nitrogen and oxygen species, showing the main peaks around 284.83, 402.22 and 532.22 eV, respectively. The high-solution C 1s spectrum (Figure 1b) can be well fitted by three peaks at 284.8 eV (C=C/C-C), 286 eV (C-N) and 287 eV (COO  $  ^{-}  $ ), respectively, confirming the integration of tetramethylammonium moieties in form of COO  $  ^{-}  $  ·[N(CH  $  _{3}  $ )  $  _{4}  $ ]  $  ^{+}  $ . Besides,  $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR study (see Figure S2 in SI) demonstrates a stoichiometric ratio of 4:1 for TMA and PTC moieties of the as-prepared TMA–PTC, suggesting that the four carboxylate groups of PTC are fully neutralized by tetramethyl ammonium groups. In addition, the absorption and photoluminescence features of TMA–PTC are shown in Figure S3. The characteristic absorptions around 442, 471 nm as well as the emissions around 489, 514 nm are in good agreement with those reported for various perylene derivatives.  $  ^{40-42}  $

To assess whether TMA–PTC is suitable as CIL for planar p–i–n PeSCs, its electronic properties were investigated by means of UPS. Figure 2 shows the secondary electron cut-off and valance region photoemission spectra for the film of PC $ _{61} $ BM/TMA–PTC, in comparison with those of pristine PC $ _{61} $ BM. Accordingly, the WF of TMA–PTC is calculated to be 3.95 eV, matching well with the LUMO energy level of PC $ _{61} $ BM (i.e., 3.9 eV). On the other hand, the HOMO energy level of TMA–PTC is calculated to be 6.6 eV, much deeper than that (i.e., 5.9 eV) measured and reported for PC $ _{61} $ BM. $ ^{26} $  Accordingly, the relative energy level alignment of each layer in the PeSCs is depicted in Figure 3d. The energy levels of ITO, PEDOT:PSS, perovskite layer, and Ag are previously reported data. $ ^{31,43} $  These results indicate that TMA–PTC might be able to serve as a good CIL in planar p–i–n PeSCs, which may not only enhance the electron transport through a quasi–ohmic interface between PC $ _{61} $ BM and Ag cathode, in consequence of the Femi energy level pinning, but also more effectively block the holes migrating from CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _{3-x} $ Cl $ _x $  perovskite to Ag cathode.

Device tests were performed on 40 individual PeSCs with and without TMA–PTC CIL. The thickness of TMA–PTC CIL is optimized by varying the concentration of TMA–PTC solution (see Figure S5 and Table S1 for optimization details). Their key photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table 1, and their current density–voltage   $  (J-V)  $   characteristics are shown in Figure 4a. The average PCE of the reference devices without CIL is 10.27%, with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 0.918 V, a   $  J_{SC}  $   of 18.60 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , an FF of 60.20%. With the insertion of a thin TMA–PTC layer between the PC  $  _{61}  $  BM and Ag cathode, the average PCE of the PeSCs is increased to 13.29%, with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 0.941 V, a   $  J_{SC}  $   of 19.78 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and an FF of 71.47%. Moreover, as seen in the histogram of Figure 4b, the PCE of PeSCs with the TMA–PTC CIL shows a high reproducibility with a standard derivation of ±0.35%. The champion PCE of the PeSCs with TMA–PTC CIL reaches to 14.13%, with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 0.940 V, a   $  J_{SC}  $   of 21.46 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and an FF of 70.06%. These results convince that the thin TMA–PTC film could be used as an effective CIL at the PC  $  _{61}  $  BM/Ag interface. # 3.4 Stability of perovskite solar cells

The long-term stability of PeSCs was evaluated in the air with a low relative humidity (<10%). Plots of PCE decay versus exposure time are shown in Figure 8. Notably, the degradation of the reference device without CIL is very fast, with a PCE drop of 70% after 330 hours, which is mainly caused by the remarkable decrease in   $  J_{SC}  $   and FF (see Figure S16 in SI). This is similar to that reported in the previous literature.  $  ^{62}  $   However, the device with TMA–PTC CIL exhibits a much better stability, with 80% of the initial PCE retained after the same period, which is mainly attributed to the much more slowly decayed FF (see Figure S17 in SI). Slightly increasing the thickness of TMA–PTC CIL yields the similar result (see Figure S18 in SI). To investigate the mechanism for the improved stability, water-contact-angle measurements were firstly performed. As seen in Figure S19, the water-contact-angle on   $  PC_{61}BM/TMA-PTC/Ag  $   surface is   $  64^{\circ}  $  , even lower than that (  $  95^{\circ}  $  ) on the   $  PC_{61}BM/Ag  $   surface. As indicated, the insertion of TMA–PTC CIL may not be able to reduce the influence of humidity. The improved stability induced by TMA–PTC CIL shall be accounted for elsewhere. On the other hand, the corrosion of perovskite on metal cathode due to the chemical reaction with the migrating halide ions has been proposed.

# 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, a simple alcohol-soluble perylene derivative (i.e., tetramethyl ammonium neutralized perylene-3,4,9,10-tetracarboxylic acid; TMA-PTC) was prepared and used as the CIL for planar p-i-n PeSCs based on  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $ . The PeSCs with TMA-PTC CIL achieve an average PCE of  $ 13.29\% $  and a maximum PCE of  $ 14.13\% $ , all remarkably higher than those ( $ 10.27\% $  and  $ 11.06\% $ ) of the devices without CIL. Further investigations reveal that TMA-PTC CIL can not only enhance the electron transport from perovskite to Ag cathode but also effectively block the holes to reduce the leakage current and to improve the contact selectivity between perovskite and Ag cathode, thus well accounting for the improvement in
