# Tetra-ammonium Zinc Phthalocyanine to Construct a Graded 2D-3D Perovskite Interface for Efficient and Stable Solar Cells

Congping Li, Xudong Lv, Jing Cao,  $  ^{*}  $   and Yu Tang  $  ^{*}  $

ABSTRACT The crystallographic defects inevitably incur during the solution processed organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite film, especially at surface and the grain boundaries (GBs) of perovskite film, which can further result in the reduced cell performance and stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, a simple defect passivation method was employed by treating perovskite precursor film with a hydrophobic tetra-ammonium zinc phthalocyanine (ZnPc). The results demonstrated that a 2D-3D graded perovskite interface with a capping layer of 2D  $ (\mathrm{ZnPc})_{0.5}\mathrm{MA}_{n - 1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n13n + 1} $  perovskite together with 3D MAPbI3 perovskite was successfully constructed on the top of 3D perovskite layer. This situation realized the efficient GBs passivation, thus reducing the defects in GBs. As expected, the corresponding PSCs with modified perovskite revealed an improved cell performance. The best efficiency reached  $ 19.6\% $ . Especially, the significantly enhanced long-term stability of the responding PSCs against humidity and heating was remarkably achieved. Such a strategy in this work affords an efficient method to improve the stability of PSCs and thus probably bringing the PSCs closer to practical commercialization.

KEYWORDS Zinc phthalocyanine, Grain boundary passivation, Perovskite solar cells

# Introduction

In order to solve the energy crisis caused by the consumption of fossil fuels, the research on photovoltaic devices has been developed rapidly.  $  ^{[1]}  $   Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been attracted more attention in the field of photovoltaic research due to their high efficiencies and low-cost fabrication.  $  ^{[2-12]}  $   This attractive mainly comes from the prominent properties of the perovskite materials, such as high optical absorption coefficient and effective electron/hole transfer and separation rate.  $  ^{[13-17]}  $   Over the past few years, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has been greatly improved over 23%.  $  ^{[9]}  $   However, their practical application and commercialization process is still hindered by the poor long-term stability of humidity and thermal.

In the conventional PSCs devices, highly efficient PSCs were obtained by using three-dimensional (3D) perovskite material. However, the large number of crystallographic defects within the grain boundaries (GBs) of perovskite film will cause the easy entrance of water molecule into perovskite lattice,  $  ^{[18-20]}  $   the movement and emission of methylamine cation  $  ^{[21-23]}  $   and the oxidization of iodide ions into the trimer,  $  ^{[24,25]}  $   thus resulting in the rapid destroy of the perovskite structure. To resolve this issue, a series of defect passivators were successfully designed and employed. Compared to the corporation of small molecules as defect passivators,  $  ^{[5,18,26-30]}  $   recently, we employed a hydrophobic tetra-ammonium zinc phthalocyanine (ZnPc) with excellent charge transport property and thermal stability to post-treat the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (MA = CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  ) film.  $  ^{[31]}  $   A 2D perovskite of (ZnPc)  $  _{0.5}  $  MA  $  _{n-1}  $  Pb  $  _{n}  $    $  ^{3n+1}  $   within the GBs of perovskite film was successfully constructed. Such a situation efficiently achieved the GBs suture for passivating the defects in GBs, finally generating the fabrication of efficient and stable PSCs.

In addition, prior study revealed that the introduction of phenethylammonium iodide into perovskite precursor film can construct a 3D-2D grade perovskite interface.  $  ^{[22]}  $   This result also led to an enhanced stability in p-i-n photovoltaics and an overall PCE of 19.89%. Based on this strategy, the usage of ZnPc to treat perovskite precursor film would provide an effective route to passivate the GBs of perovskite film, further leading to the improved efficiency and stability of PSCs. Therefore, in this work, we treat the interface of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite precursor film by ZnPc. As expected, a capping layer of 2D (ZnPc)  $  _{0.5}  $  MA  $  _{n-1}  $  Pb  $  _{n}  $  I  $  _{3n+1}  $   perovskite together with 3D MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite was yielded on

the top of 3D perovskite layer, thus yielding a 2D-3D garage perovskite interface. Such a modification further achieved the efficient GBs passivation and reduced the defects in GBs, thus resulting in an improved cell performance. More importantly, the significantly increased cell stability was remarkably accomplished.

Above results demonstrated that efficient GBs passivate can yield positive effects on photovoltaic performance. Since the presence of defects associated with the formed lattice vacancies in perovskite film can drive the  $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $  in the  $ [\mathrm{PbI}_6]^{4-} $  octahedral frame out of the perovskite lattice under thermal treatment, and thereby reducing the irreversible decomposition of 3D perovskite.[32] In the above modified perovskite, the diammonium cations in ZnPc would efficient bind  $ [\mathrm{PbI}_6]^{4-} $  at both ends together, further inhibiting the  $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $  ion-migration and ensuring the structure stability. Firstly, the corresponding PSCs devices were stored in air at room temperature with a humidity of about  $ 45\% $  to monitor their moisture stabilities (all devices without encapsulation in this work). As shown in Figure 5a, the PSC with the modified perovskite displayed considerable stability by retaining  $ 85\% $  of its initial PCE over  $ 1,000\mathrm{h} $ . In contrast, the reference cells with only  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  firstly degraded gradually and then the degradation rate increased; it eventually retained only  $ < 20\% $  of the initial efficiency within  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  of operation. The corresponding perovskite films were also directly immersed into water to further clearly display the presence of excellent moisture stability for the modified

perovskite film. It is thus concluded that the GBs passivation of the 2D-3D grade perovskite interface is an effective approach to block the escape of MA  $  ^{+}  $  and entrance of water molecules, further improving the stability of PSCs.

Furthermore, the thermal stabilities of PSCs were tested at   $  85\ ^{\circ}C  $   in an   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere. As shown in Figure 5b, as expected, the PSC with modified perovskite revealed a remarkably enhanced heating stability. To assess the overall stability toward moisture and heating, the PSCs were then tested at   $  85\ ^{\circ}C  $   with a humidity of about 45%. The PSCs devices with modified perovskite retained over 90% of its original efficiency after 250 hours (Figure 6c), whereas the PSC device based on pure MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   degraded rapidly, it retained only   $  <10\%  $   of the initial PCE within 200 h. The color variety of corresponding perovskite films under the same condition was also presented in Figure 5d. Modified perovskite film was no obvious color change in 200 h. In comparison, in the same period, the cells without modification decayed greatly with the color faded from dark to yellow. Accordingly, our findings provide new insights into ongoing development of efficient approach to passivate the GBs of 3D perovskite, finally achieving the long-term stability toward moisture and heating of PSCs.

# Conclusions

In summary, the low cost tetra-ammonium zinc phthalocyanine ZnPc was employed to modify the pervovskite precursor film, thus successfully constructing a 2D-3D grade perovskite interface. After treated, 2D  $ (\mathrm{ZnPc})_{0.5}\mathrm{MA}_{n - 1}\mathrm{Pb}_n\mathrm{I}_{3n + 1} $  perovskite structure can be constructed in the GBs of 3D perovskite and passivated the GBs by reducing the defects within perovskite film. This modification provided an effective route to improve the conversion efficiency of PSCs and the best efficiency was improved up to  $ 19.6\% $  . More importantly, the PSC with modified perovskite showed greatly improved structure stability toward heating and moisture. This study highlights the importance of GBs passivation of perovskite films using phthalocyanine, finally achieving the fabrication of efficient and stable PSCs.

# Experimental

# Solar Cell Fabrication

Fluorine-doped Tin Oxide (FTO) glass substrates with dimension of   $  2.0 \times 2.0 \, cm^2  $   were etched by zinc powder and 2 M hydrochloric acid to form a pattern. The substrates were then sequentially cleaned using ultrasonication in acetone, distilled water and ethanol for 10 min, respectively. After cleaning surface impurities at   $  450 \, ^\circC  $   for 30 min, a compact   $  TiO_2  $   blocking layer was spin-coated onto the cleaned FTO glass by using 0.15 M titanium tetraisopropanolate in isopropanol solution at 2,000 rpm for 25 s. The substrate was annealed at   $  550 \, ^\circC  $   for 30 min. After cooling to the room temperature, the substrate was immersed into 20 mM   $  TiCl_4  $   solution at   $  70 \, ^\circC  $   for 30 min. After dried, a mesoporous   $  TiO_2  $   layer was deposited on the surface by spin-coating of the   $  TiO_2  $   paste (Dyesol DSL 18NR-T) with isopropanol (1:7, mass ratio) at 5000 rpm for 25 s, following heating at   $  550 \, ^\circC  $   for 30 min. After obtaining the ETM layer, the perovskite film was deposited on the glass surface by spin-coating precursor solution prepared with the reported method at 4000 rpm for 25 s, while 2 ml ether should be rapidly dripped to the rotating substrate in 8 s.  $  ^{[31]}  $   After obtaining perovskite film, the pre-prepared phthalocyanine synthesized by our previously reported method  $  ^{[27]}  $   in isopropanol solution (1 mg/mL) was spin-coated onto perovskite surface at 4000 rpm for 20 s. After annealing at   $  100 \, ^\circC  $   for 10 min, the 2D-3D graded interface was formed on the upper layer of 3D perovskite layer. For Spiro-OMeTAD HTMs, Spiro-OMeTAD in chlorobenzene solution (72 mg/1 mL) was mixed with the additives containing

# Device Characterization

The fabricated photovoltaic cells were characterized by current-voltage (J-V) characteristics, which testing by a solar simulator equipped with a Keithley 2400 source meter and 300 W collimated Xenon lamp (Newport). The simulator was calibrated by the certified silicon solar cell with the light intensity to 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  at AM 1.5 G solar light condition. Incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) was measured on a computer-controlled IPCE system (Newport) containing a Xenon lamp, a monochromator and a Keithley multimeter. The apparatus was calibrated with the certified silicon solar cell and the IPCE data were collected at DC mode. The XRD spectrum was obtained by an X-ray diffractometer (Rigaku, RINT-2500) with a CuKa radiation. The surface morphology of perovskite was measured via a SEM-4800 field-emission scanning electron microscope (SEM). UV-vis absorption spectra were obtained from a cary-5000 UV-vis spectrophotometer. The Time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) spectra were measured using an Edinburgh Instruments FLS920 spectrometer.
