# Enhanced photovoltaic performance and stability of carbon counter electrode based perovskite solar cells encapsulated by PDMS

Zhiyong Liu  $  ^{a}  $  , Bo Sun  $  ^{a}  $  , Tielin Shi  $  ^{*a}  $  , Zirong Tang  $  ^{a}  $  , Guanglan Liao  $  ^{*a,b}  $

We report the encapsulation of low temperature carbon counter electrode based hole-conductor-free mesoscopic methylammonium lead iodide perovskite/TiO $ _2 $  heterojunction solar cells with polydimethylsiloxane. The solar cells demonstrate improved photovoltaic performance, where we obtain the optimal short-circuit photocurrent  $ J_{sc}=23.5 $  mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , open-circuit photovoltage  $ V_{oc}=0.97 $  V, and a fill factor  $ FF=0.474 $ , corresponding to a light to electric power conversion efficiency of 10.8% under standard AM 1.5 solar light of 100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $  intensity. The results exhibit remarkable 54% enhancement than those without encapsulation. The cross-sectional SEM images indicate that the PDMS layer can condense the carbon electrode by filling the gaps in the mesoscopic carbon film during the solidification of PDMS, resulting in an improved MAPbI $ _3 $ /Carbon interface condition. The photoluminescence and electrical impedance spectroscopy measurements prove that the improved efficiency is ascribed to a more efficient charge transfer process and a slower charge recombination between interfaces. In addition, the robust polydimethylsiloxane isolates water in air, avoiding the degradation of CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  perovskite and leading to an impressive stability during a testing period of 3000 h. Our work paves the way for realizing low cost, highly efficient and stable hybrid photovoltaic cells.

# Introduction

The explorations on clean, inexpensive and renewable sources as alternatives to conventional fossil energy is an eternal theme, which provides a strategy for energy crisis and global warming. The emerging photovoltaic technologies converting solar energy to electricity, such as organic cells  $  ^{1-3}  $  , inorganic cells  $  ^{4,5}  $  , quantum dot cells  $  ^{6}  $   and dye-sensitized solar cells  $  ^{7-12}  $  , have been prosperous for decades and considered as one of the best approaches to meet the requirements. The organometal perovskite owns advantages of wide absorption range  $  ^{13}  $  , high extinction coefficient  $  ^{14, 15}  $  , and long electron-hole diffusion length  $  ^{16}  $  , which are regarded as one of the most promising materials  $  ^{17}  $   for solar energy conversion. Organic-inorganic hybrid solar cells using metal-halide perovskites such as methylammonium lead iodide (CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $  ) as light harvesters exhibit appealing features such as high optical cross section  $  ^{15}  $  , excellent ambipolar charge transport  $  ^{18}  $  , small exciton binding energy  $  ^{19-21}  $  , tunable band gaps  $  ^{22-24}  $  , and low-cost fabrication. Importantly, the CH  $  _{3}  $  NH  $  _{3}  $  PbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are solution-processible, which pave the way for future inexpensive

large scale commercialization. The research of the organometal perovskite solar cells have advanced by leaps and bounds, especially in the field of pursuing cost-effective high-efficiency solar power conversion  $  ^{25}  $  . The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells has skyrocketed from initial about 3.8%  $  ^{26}  $   since they were proposed for the first time in 2009 to 20.1%  $  ^{27}  $  , showing incredible momentum of development and potential value of application. No matter mesoscopic, planar, inverted designs or other kinds of PSC structures  $  ^{28-31}  $  , the most common and successful ones can be expressed as   $  TiO_{2}/perovskite/hole-transporter-material  $   (HTM)/Au. However, because of the moisture-sensitive dopants   $  Li[(CF_{3}SO_{2})_{2}N]  $   (Li-TFSI) and tert-butylpyridine (tBP), the most commonly used HTM 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) has proved to be non-stable in air, to say nothing about its long-term stability  $  ^{32-35}  $  . To improve the cell stability, stable inorganic HTMs such as   $  CuSCN^{36,37}  $   were used to replace the spiro-OMeTAD and showed a PCE of 12.4%. Even more radical, Au electrode was deposited on perovskite without the spiro-OMeTAD interlayer and a PCE of 11.5% was achieved, proving that   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite can act as both light harvester and hole conductor at the same time  $  ^{18,38,39}  $  . Those HTM-free mesoscopic   $  TiO_{2}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   solar cells have drawn increased interest, due to no requirement of high-cost HTMs and much simpler manufacturing process.

Nevertheless, the solar cells still need thermal evaporated noble metal Au as counter electrodes (CEs), which is highly energy consumptive and process complicated  $  ^{40}  $  . Recently,

Electrons are injected from perovskite to   $  TiO_{2}  $   and finally collected by fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO), and holes are extracted from perovskite to carbon electrode directly. However,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   material will degrade and form   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   under humid condition or high temperature, which may hamper the application of PSCs  $  ^{45}  $  . Cheng et al. reported degradation observations of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   PSCs at high temperatures and humidity  $  ^{46}  $  , signing the necessity of sealing process. They developed ‘partial’ and ‘complete’ encapsulation architectures to further prolong device lifetime  $  ^{47}  $  . In order to inhibit the ingress of moisture and oxygen as well, PSCs encapsulated by UV-treated epoxy  $  ^{48}  $   and polyurethane  $  ^{49}  $   are also reported. Nevertheless, detailed analysis on the encapsulation of PSCs is inadequate. Polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS), benefiting from characters of low cost, ease of moulding, biocompatibility, chemical stability and transparency, has been widely used in fabrication and encapsulation processes for electronic devices  $  ^{50-55}  $  . What’s more, the excellent ductility of PDMS makes it potential in the extensive application of flexible electronics. Importantly, the PDMS solution is water-free and shows no harm to perovskite layer, making it a perfect choice to package PSCs.

In this work, we prepare hole-conductor-free perovskite heterojunction solar cells by using a low-temperature carbon electrode. PDMS is chosen to package the perovskite solar cells and detailed analysis is carried out. We find the PDMS layer can condense the carbon electrode by filling the gaps in the carbon film during the solidification of PDMS, resulting in an improved  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/ $  Carbon interface condition. The photoluminescence (PL) measurements and electrical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements further prove a more efficient charge transfer process and a slower charge recombination occurred between interfaces after packaging. Besides, the mesoscopic structure of the carbon layer become more compact after the encapsulation, preventing the degradation of perovskite layer caused by humidity. The packaged devices show outstanding stability during the testing period of  $ 3000\mathrm{h} $ .

# Results and discussion

The schematic of the carbon CE based PSCs structure is shown in Fig. 1(a). The cell, packaged by PDMS, consists of layers of  $ \mathrm{FTO / TiO_2 / CH_3NH_3PbI_3} $  /carbon CE. As depicted in Fig. 1(b), perovskite  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  has a low exciton binding energy ( $ \sim 0.5 $  eV), leading to an easy generation of electron-hole pairs under irradiance. Sequentially, the separation of electron-hole pairs is achieved by injecting electrons into  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  (-4.0 eV) and transporting the hole to carbon CE (-5.0 eV), forming photocurrent in the device. There are 128 solar cells investigated in our study, where 67 cells are encapsulated by PDMS and 61 without encapsulation. The optimal photocurrent-voltage curves of device I from packaged cells and device II from unpackaged cells are presented in Fig. 1(c) with detailed characteristic parameters listed in Table 1. The incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) test is further implemented to understand the ability of turning incident photons to extracted electrons at a given wavelength of different devices in Fig. 1(d). As can be found, the packaged device bears higher IPCE in the range from 400 to  $ 800~\mathrm{nm} $  and reaches a peak (94%) at  $ 535~\mathrm{nm} $ , yielding remarkable  $ J_{SC} $  of  $ 23.5\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ . In addition, the electron flux of the PSC can be calculated by multiplying the incident spectral response with the measured IPCE, as displayed in Fig. S1. At shorter wavelengths, the photocurrent is cut-off by FTO and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , as shown in the UV-vis absorbance of FTO and  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  (Fig. S2). The  $ \sim 800~\mathrm{nm} $  cut-off wavelength corresponds to  $ 1.55\mathrm{eV} $  band gap of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite absorber. Figs. 1(e,f) depict the efficiencies distribution of PSCs. After sealed by PDMS, an average PCE of  $ 7.64\% $  (max.  $ 10.8\% $ ) is achieved, higher than the average efficiency of  $ 4.95\% $  (max.  $ 6.88\% $ ) of the unpackaged devices. Clearly, these devices exhibit little difference in FF under AM1.5 simulated sunlight of  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $ . The enhanced photovoltaic performance mainly comes from the increment of  $ J_{SC} $  (from 13.9 to 19.6) and  $ V_{OC} $  (from 0.83 to 0.88), leading to a marked  $ 54\% $  promotion of PCE.

Despite the widely known high efficiency, PSCs especially for thin film PSCs still suffer from hysteresis effect, which is dependent on scan direction and scan rate. Such hysteresis effect seriously causes cell stability concern and difficulty to accurately determine the cell efficiency. Both forward and reverse bias sweep are used to evaluate the hysteresis behavior of the PSCs. Fig. 2(a) shows the J-V curves of device I which are performed from forward bias to short circuit (FB-SC) and from short circuit to forward bias (SC-FB) at a scan rate of 0.01 V/s. The device exhibits a FB-SC performance with   $  J_{SC}=22.1  $   mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{OC}=0.96  $   V, FF=0.456 and PCE=9.68%, and a SC-FB performance with   $  J_{SC}=22.2  $   mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ,   $  V_{OC}=0.95  $   V, FF=0.454, yielding a PCE of 9.58%. Such results reveal unapparent hysteresis of the J-V curves for both SC-FB and FB-SC. The hysteresis effect at different scan rates is also investigated as shown in Fig. S3. To quantify the hysteresis effect, an dimensional hysteric index (HI) is introduced  $  ^{56}  $  .

$$
H I = \frac {\left| J _ {s c a n -} \left(V _ {O C} / 2\right) - J _ {s c a n +} \left(V _ {O C} / 2\right) \right|}{J _ {s c a n -} \left(V _ {O C} / 2\right)}
$$

where   $  J_{\text{scan}}(V_{oc}/2)  $   is the photocurrent density at   $  V_{oc}/2  $   bias for the reverse scan, and   $  J_{\text{scan}}+(V_{oc}/2)  $   is the photocurrent density at   $  V_{oc}/2  $   for the forward scan. The lower HI corresponds to the cells without significant hysteresis, while the higher HI corresponds to the hysteretic devices  $  ^{57}  $  . At the scan rate of 0.01 V/s, the forward and backward curves of the device nearly overlap perfectly with a lowest HI of only 0.03. When the scan rate increases up to 0.03 V/s, the HI grows to 0.06. When the scan rates further rise to 0.05 V/s and 0.1 V/s, the hysteresis effect becomes worse for the device with higher HI of 0.14 and 0.16, respectively. The reason for the hysteresis behavior possibly stems from unbalanced charge transportation and collection  $  ^{58}  $  , perovskite's ferroelectricity  $  ^{59}  $  , ion migration, or defects  $  ^{60}  $  . This is common and hard to avoid in planar heterojunction solar cells. Besides, the steady-state power output at a given constant forward bias is another important parameter to evaluate the PCE  $  ^{25}  $  . As shown in Fig. 2(b), we record the photocurrent density of device I (which is held at a forward bias of 0.61 V near its maximum output power point) as a function of time to monitor the stabilized power output under working conditions. The steady-state photocurrent consists with that derived from the J-V curve. After illumination at one sun for 500 s, the maximum power output reduced from 9.88% to 8.51%, maintaining 86% of its original performance. This drop of the photocurrent has some connections with the hysteresis of the device  $  ^{61}  $  .

The perovskite films capped by carbon and then packaged by PDMS are characterized using a confocal laser microscope, which measures the spatial distribution of the emission sites in the films  $  ^{74}  $  . As shown in Fig. 4(b), the emission sites decrease gradually after printed by carbon and packaged by PDMS. In addition, the PL measurements, which are often used to demonstrate the charge extraction ability of hole-conducting material in contact with the perovskite  $  ^{75}  $  , are performed to evaluate whether the carbon film efficiently extract photogenerated electron from the perovskite absorber or not. The perovskite layer was deposited on a bare quartz and the insulating mesoporous layer is omitted to exhibit the performance of carbon layer. Fig. 4(c) displays the steady-state

PL spectra of the perovskite/carbon and perovskite/carbon/PDMS together with the independent perovskite. Strong luminescence occurs at   $  \sim780  $   nm for the PL of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , and is suppressed with the existence of the carbon film. These results elucidate that the PDMS packaged PSCs are more likely to accelerate light-induced charge separation and restrict carrier recombination  $  ^{25}  $  .

# Conclusions

In summary, we have successfully fabricated low temperature carbon CE based HTM-free mesoscopic perovskite devices and encapsulated them by low-cost PDMS. Interestingly, the introduction of PDMS can condense the mesoscopic carbon layer and improve the MAPbI3/Carbon interface condition during the solidification of PDMS, leading to an optimal photovoltaic performance: a power conversion efficiency of 10.8%, a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 0.97 V, a   $  J_{SC}  $   of 23.5 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and a FF of 0.474. The results exhibit remarkable 54% enhancement than those without encapsulation. The improved performance is associated with the more efficient charge transfer process and the slower charge recombination between interfaces. Also, the robust PDMS acts as a protective shield to isolate water in air, avoiding the degradation of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite. The packaged devices show outstanding stability during the testing period of 3000 h. Our work paves the way for realizing low cost, highly efficient and stable hybrid photovoltaic cells.
