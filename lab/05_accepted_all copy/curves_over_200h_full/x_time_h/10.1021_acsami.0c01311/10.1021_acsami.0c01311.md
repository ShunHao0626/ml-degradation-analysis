# ABSTRACT:

Tin-based perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) have emerged as the most promising lead-free perovskite materials owing to their superior optoelectronic properties. However, the deficiency of accurate control for the tin-based perovskite crystallization process increases the possibility of unexpected perovskite film morphology and defects, resulting in inferior power conversion efficiency (PCE). Meanwhile, the poor environmental stability of tin-based perovskite film hinders its further development. In this work, a unique polymer [poly(ethylene-co-vinyl acetate) (EVA)] is introduced into anti-solvent during spin coating of formamidinium tin tri-iodide  $ (\mathrm{FASnI}_3) $  precursor solution. The  $ \mathrm{C = O} $  groups contained in EVA have a powerful Lewis acid-base complexation with uncoordinated tin atoms in perovskite grains, which can greatly improve grain size, optimize grain orientation and decrease surface defects of  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $  films. This strategy offers an impressive PCE of  $ 7.72\% $  with favorable reproducibility. More importantly, the PVSCs devices based on  $ \mathrm{FASnI}_3 $ -EVA absorber have a self-encapsulation effect, which exhibits distinguished moisture and oxygen barrier property, thereby retaining  $ 62.4\% $  of the original efficiency value after aging for  $ 48\mathrm{h} $  in air environment with humidity of  $ 60\% $ . Such convenient strategy provides a new inspiration for the establishment of stable and high performance tin-based PVSCs.

KEYWORDS: tin-based perovskite solar cells, poly(ethylene-co-vinyl acetate), crystallization, self-encapsulation, environmental stability

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) have obtained remarkable progress that the power conversion efficiency (PCE) has skyrocketed from 3.8% to 25% within just a decade.  $  ^{1}  $   However, these high-efficiency PVSCs contain toxic lead (Pb) element, which cannot be ignored in further large-scale commercial applications.  $  ^{2}  $   It is worth noting that the tin element is a more environmentally friendly substitute.  $  ^{3-5}  $   Especially, the formamidinium tin tri-iodide (FASnI  $  _{3}  $  ) based perovskite has been pursued because of its promising photoelectric properties, including suitable optical band gap (  $  \sim  $  1.4 eV),  $  ^{6}  $   low exciton binding energy,  $  ^{7}  $   and high carrier mobility.  $  ^{8}  $   Nevertheless, the inferior formation energy of tin vacancies results in a serious self-doping effect of perovskite films, which is ascribed to the Sn  $  ^{2+}  $   is easily oxidized to Sn  $  ^{4+}  $ .  $  ^{9-12}  $   Moreover, fast crystallization rate of tin-based perovskite causes the perovskite film rich in pinholes and defects, which not only promotes the non-radiative recombination of carriers inside the perovskite but also easily are penetrated by moisture and oxygen in the air.  $  ^{13,14}  $   As a consequence, the photoelectric performance and environmental stability of corresponding PVSCs is far from the lead-based PVSCs devices.

So far, various additives have been incorporated into perovskite precursor, such as tin (II) fluoride   $  (\mathrm{SnF}_{2})  $  ,  $  ^{15}  $   stannous chloride   $  (\mathrm{SnCl}_{2})  $  ,  $  ^{16}  $   hydroxybenzene sulfonic   $  (\mathrm{KHQSA})  $  ,  $  ^{16}  $   ethylenediammonium diiodide   $  (\mathrm{EDAI}_{2})  $  ,  $  ^{17}  $   hydrazinium chloride   $  (\mathrm{N}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{5}\mathrm{Cl})  $  ,  $  ^{18}  $   formamidinium thiocyanate (FASCN),  $  ^{19}  $   and trimethylamine (TMA),  $  ^{20}  $   regarding as attractive methodologies to inhibit the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $  . Besides, optimizing preparation process and precursor composition has also been considered as valid strategies to obtain tin-based perovskite films with high coverage.  $  ^{21,22}  $   Furthermore, researchers have designed 2D/3D tin-based perovskite structure through

In this work, poly(ethylene-co-vinyl acetate) (EVA) as a self-sealing polymer has been firstly adopted in the tin-based perovskite film, which can not only slow down the crystalline rate of   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite film but also play a role in self-encapsulation effect on preventing the permeation of moisture and oxygen. The Lewis acid-base complexation between carbonyl groups (C=O) and uncoordinated Sn performs a significant role in retarding the perovskite grain growth and facilitating the preferred orientation in thermodynamics,  $  ^{33}  $   leading to the formation of pinhole-free   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -EVA perovskite film with larger oriented grains and fewer defects. With the combination of the self-encapsulation effect of EVA, the   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -EVA based PVSCs exhibit the PCE up to 7.72% as well as excellent environmental stability in high-humidity air.

# 2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

# 2.1. Materials and solution preparation

All chemicals were employed directly without further treatment after purchase. Formamidinium iodide (FAI, ≥99%) and [6, 6]-phenyl- $  C_{61}  $ -butyric acid methyl ester ( $  PC_{61}BM  $ ) were obtained from Xi'an p-OLED Corp. Tin (II) iodide ( $  SnI_{2}  $ , 99.99%), tin (II) fluoride ( $  SnF_{2}  $ , 99%), bathocuproine (BCP, 99.99%), poly(ethylene-co-vinyl acetate) (EVA, containing 40wt% vinyl acetate), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, anhydrous, ≥99.9%), isopropanol (IPA, ≥99.5), chlorobenzene (CB, anhydrous, 99.8%) and N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF, anhydrous, 99.8%) were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich. Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS, Clevios VP AI 4083) was received from Heraeus. The preparation of perovskite precursor was mixed FAI,  $  SnI_{2}  $  and  $  SnF_{2}  $  into DMSO and DMF (1:4, v/v) with molar ratio of 1:1:0.1, subsequently stirred for 3 hours at 50 °C.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

The ITO substrate was washed in sequential order with acetone, abluent, deionized water and isopropyl alcohol for half an hour each time under ultrasound conditions. Afterwards, using a nitrogen gun blow dry the ITO substrate, followed by UV ozone treatment for 3-5 min. The PEDOT:PSS hole transport layer (HTL) was spun onto the ITO at 4000 rpm for 60 s, subsequently, annealed at 140 °C for 20 min. The FASnI  $  _{3}  $  precursor solution was deposited on HTL under a designed program (30 s at 5000 rpm). The chlorobenzene and chlorobenzene with different concentrations of EVA (0 mg/mL, 1 mg/mL, 2 mg/mL and 5 mg/mL) were continuously drop-casted at the 9st second after the beginning of program. The perovskite film was immediately annealed at 90 °C for 10 min. Subsequently, The PC  $  _{61}  $  BM (20 mg/mL,

# 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Suitable polymer additives are regarded as the efficient methods for fabricating perovskite films with high quality and prominent stability.  $  ^{34,35}  $   Here, we first employ a simple approach to achieve pinhole-free tin-based perovskite films with larger oriented grains and excellent environmental stability by introducing EVA (Figure S1) into anti-solvent during spin coating of FASnI  $  _{3}  $   precursor solution (Figure 1). Firstly, EVA can act as a template to both retard the crystallization rate and regulate the growth orientation of tin-based perovskite crystals. The cross-section and top-view SEM of perovskite films have been performed to evaluate the template effect of EVA on the morphology evolution. As the Figure 2a, b shown, the reference FASnI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite film is formed with relatively small size grains, which are broadly distributed in the scope of 100-350 nm with an average size of 196 nm (Figure 2c). Worse still is that the reference FASnI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite film involves scores of pinholes. Cheerfully, pinholes appear to be absent in the FASnI  $  _{3}  $   film after treated by the optimal concentration of EVA (C  $  _{EVA}  $  =2 mg/mL) (Figures S2), the average size of perovskite grain is significantly increased to 347 nm with a smooth and uniform surface (Figure 2d, e, f), which is ascribed to the template effect of EVA on the perovskite crystallization. This morphology evolution can also be observed from the atomic force microscopy (AFM) images (Figure S3). Seen from the FTIR spectra shown in Figure 2g, the stretching vibration of carbonyl groups (C=O) bond in pure EVA is occurred in 1738 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , while it shifts to 1727 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   in the EVA-SnI  $  _{2}  $   composite, confirming that C=O in EVA has a chemical interaction with SnI  $  _{2}  $   and an intermediate adduct has been formed.  $  ^{36}  $   The formation of Lewis acid-base complexation is expected to reduce the generation of tin vacancy, slow down the crystalline rate and regulate the growth direction of perovskite grain. The crystal structure of the perovskite has been further characterized by glancing incident angle X-ray diffraction (GIXRD). As shown in Figure 2h, GIXRD patterns of FASnI $ _3 $  films without and with EVA treatment are found at  $ 14.0^{\circ} $ ,  $ 24.4^{\circ} $ ,  $ 28.1^{\circ} $ ,  $ 31.6^{\circ} $ ,  $ 40.4^{\circ} $  and  $ 42.9^{\circ} $ , which belong to (100), (120), (200), (122) and (222) crystal planes of the orthorhombic (Amm2) structure, respectively. $ ^{26} $  The diffraction peak intensity of FASnI $ _3 $  film is markedly enhanced after EVA treatment with optimum concentration ( $ C_{EVA} = 2 \text{ mg/mL} $ ), suggesting higher crystallinity in consistent with the larger average crystal size. In particular, the calculated (100)/(120) peak intensity ratio increases from 1.15 to 2.83 (Figure 2i and Figure S4), illustrating that the template effect of EVA can regulate the direction of grain growth. The X-ray rocking curves ( $ \omega $  scan) of FASnI $ _3 $  perovskite films in (200) direction (Figure S5) is a reliable assessment technology to evaluate the deviation of perovskite grain from the specified orientation, which can be reflected in the full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) of rocking curves. $ ^{37} $  In terms of perfectly oriented single crystal perovskite, the FWHM approaches zero. Based the FASnI $ _3 $  and FASnI $ _3 $ -EVA polycrystalline perovskite films, the FWHM are respectively calculated as  $ 14.6^{\circ} $  and  $ 11.2^{\circ} $ , respectively. Lower FWHM value indicates that the perovskite grains tend to grow in the direction of (h00) during the crystallization of EVA treated perovskite films, which can effectively improve the photoelectric performance of PVSCs devices. $ ^{38} $

# 4. CONCLUSIONS

In summary, EVA as a self-sealing polymer has been applied to obtain high-quality and large-grained   $  FASnI_{3}  $   perovskite film by slowing down grain growth and facilitating the preferred grain orientation, enabling to improve the PCE of   $  FASnI_{3}  $  -EVA based PVSCs up to 7.72%. Moreover, the EVA treatment can prolong the environmental stability of the PVSCs device, which is benefit from the self-encapsulation effect of EVA. The devices preserve over 90% of its original efficiency with 800 h of storage in nitrogen glove box and maintain an initial value of more than 60% in high-humidity (60% RH) air for 48 h. Such a practical strategy paves the way for the construction of highly efficient and stable tin-based PVSCs.
