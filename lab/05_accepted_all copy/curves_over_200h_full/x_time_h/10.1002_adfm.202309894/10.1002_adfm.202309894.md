# Tailoring Crystallization Dynamics of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   for Scalable Production of Efficient Inorganic Perovskite Solar Cells

Nannan Sun, Sheng Fu,* You Li, Lei Chen, Jaehoon Chung, Muhammad Mohsin Saeed, Kshitiz Dolia, Amirhossein Rahimi, Chongwen Li, Zhaoning Song,* and Yanfa Yan*

All-inorganic perovskite cesium lead triiodide (CsPbI $ _3 $ ) with inorganic nature, low-temperature synthesis, and a suitable bandgap is desirable for high-performance photovoltaics. However, the scalable production of CsPbI $ _3 $  photovoltaics is still challenging due to a large nucleation energy barrier and slow phase transition during unassisted natural crystallization. Here, the crystallization dynamics of CsPbI $ _3 $  thin films is tailored via lead acetate (PbAc $ _2 $ ) substitution in the perovskite precursor ink, allowing the scalable fabrication of efficient all-inorganic perovskite solar cells and minimodules. Introducing PbAc $ _2 $  enlarges CsPbI $ _3 $  colloid size in the precursor and reduces the nucleation energy barrier. Additionally, reactions between acetate and dimethylammonium in the wet film accelerate the removal of dimethylammonium additives and generate solvent vapors for self-regulate internal solvent annealing, resulting in densely packed, uniform, and pinhole-free CsPbI $ _3 $  perovskite films over large areas. This strategy demonstrates inverted CsPbI $ _3 $  solar cells with 20.17% efficiency and good operational stability (retaining 95.5% of initial efficiency after continuous operation for 1800 h) and 15.1%-efficient CsPbI $ _3 $  minimodules with an active area of 26.8 cm $ ^2 $ .

# 2. Results and Discussion

First, we have to understand the role of DMAI in   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite formation and how   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitution tailors the crystallization dynamics of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   to produce densely packed, uniform and pinhole-free perovskite films.  $  ^{[4,38-41]}  $   Unlike the typical organic-inorganic perovskite films, solution deposition of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite thin films does not use anti-solvent or gas quenching but relies on unassisted natural nucleation. Therefore, three crucial factors: (1) regulating the colloid size and chemical phase in the precursor solution, (2) minimizing critical nucleation energy of intermediate phases during the wet film condensation, and (3) accelerating the perovskite nucleation kinetics and sustaining adequate the perovskite grain growth time are vital in governing the crystal nucleation and growth for high-quality   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films.  $  ^{[4,40,42,43]}  $   An ideal unassisted solution deposition requires the quick condensation of uniformly distributed solute nuclei with a proper packing density, a rapid in-situ transformation from the intermediate to the perovskite phase, and a prolonged grain growth period to allow these nuclei to grow into continuous, smooth, and dense polycrystalline thin films (Figure S1, Supporting Information).  $  ^{[21,39,43]}  $   However, this desired thin-film growth scheme is difficult to achieve in   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskites.

All-inorganic perovskite   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   suffers from poor solubility in the commonly used polar aprotic perovskite solvent DMF. As a result, yellow precipitates are formed in a low-concentration (0.65 M) precursor solution (Figure S2a, Supporting Information). DMAI has been generally used as a crystallization regulator to create an intermediate phase during   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film deposition. Interestingly, we find that adding DMAI powders into a turbid oversaturated   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   solution turns the precursor solution into clear ink (Figure S2b, Supporting Information), indicating that DMAI also functions as a solubilizer to enhance the solvation of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   in DMF. Despite enhanced solubility, the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films prepared using a DMAI-contained precursor solution still exhibit poor crystallinity and porosity due to the small critical nucleus size with a large nucleation barrier and slow vaporization.

Thus, regulating the condensation of the perovskite precursor, nucleation of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  perovskite phase, and vaporization of DMAI during crystal growth is critical for the scalable production of large-area and efficient  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $  PSCs.

To address this issue, we partially substituted   $  PbI_{2}  $   with   $  PbAc_{2}  $   to tailor the interplay between the DMAI regulator and   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   to achieve the desired crystallization dynamics of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films. Figure 1 summarizes the schematic of crystallization dynamics of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films with   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitution, which is a holistic strategy that can synergistically meet the criteria for uniform and smooth growth of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite thin films over large areas. In the precursor stage, substituting   $  PbAc_{2}  $   introduces acetate   $  CH_{3}COO^{-}  $  ligands on the surface of the colloidal particles, reducing the surface tension with DMF solvent via hydrogen bonds of the acetyl moiety and enlarging the critical size of colloids for lower nucleation energy barrier of the intermediate phase   $  DMA_{x}Cs_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $   (Figure S1a, Supporting Information).  $  ^{[4,44-47]}  $   During high-temperature annealing, the nucleation of the perovskite phase is accelerated by rapid evaporation of the volatile byproduct DMAAc resulting from the reaction between   $  PbAc_{2}  $   and DMAI in the precursor, which shortens the time of perovskite phase nucleation in the wet films and prolongs the growth process for higher crystalline quality (Figure S1b, Supporting Information).  $  ^{[48,49]}  $   The detailed formation dynamics are discussed in Supplementary Note S1 (Supporting Information). Lastly, DMAAc evaporation at an elevated temperature is accompanied by the release of solvent vapors, which boosts the diffusion growth of the perovskite film by a self-regulated solvent annealing effect (vide infra).

We first investigated the chemical reactions in the   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substituted precursor by hydrogen nuclear magnetic resonance (  $  H^{1}NMR  $  , Figure 2a). After mixing up the DMAI and   $  PbAc_{2}  $   at a 2:1 ratio in   $  d^{6}  $   mso solvent, noticeable chemistry shifts of H signals for   $  Ac^{-}  $   (from 1.69 to 1.72 ppm) and   $  DMA^{+}  $   (2.56 to 2.54 ppm) were observed, indicating the formation of DMAAc. Because   $  I^{-}  $   is a weaker Lewis base than   $  Ac^{-}  $  , the hydrogen bonding between   $  DMA^{+}  $   (  $  NH_{2}^{+}  $   group) and   $  Ac^{-}  $   (  $  COO^{-}  $   group) is stronger than that of   $  DMA^{+}  $   and   $  I^{-}  $  . Therefore, the formation of DMAAc results in the lowing and increasing electron density of the methyl groups for   $  Ac^{-}  $   and   $  DMA^{+}  $   ions, respectively, which is the origination of the chemical shift for   $  H^{1}NMR  $   results. Forming DMAAC after reaction changes the chemical environment of DMAI and yields a colloidal solution.  $  ^{[44]}  $   To gain an insight into colloid size change with   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitution, we conducted dynamic light scattering (DLS) measurements on the various precursors without (referred to as Ref) and with different   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitutions (  $  PbAc_{2}:PbI_{2}  $   ratios of 1:5, 1:3, and 1:2). Figure 2b shows a dramatic increase of average colloid size from 2.7 to 480 nm after introducing 25%   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitution (  $  PbAc_{2}:PbI_{2}=1:3  $  ). Figure S3a (Supporting Information) shows that the average radius of the colloidal perovskite precursor increases with increasing the substitution of   $  PbAc_{2}  $  . Additionally, the precursor inks with higher   $  PbAc_{2}  $   concentrations exhibit a more apparent Tyndall effect (Figure S3b, Supporting Information). Interestingly, precipitates spontaneously formed in the   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substituted precursor inks after storing the solution for 20 days, whereas the pristine precursor ink without   $  PbAc_{2}  $   (Ref).

According to the classic nucleation and growth theory, colloids with a radius larger than the critical size (  $  r^{8}  $  ) are more stable and tend to grow owing to a smaller surface-to-volume free energy ratio (Figure 1, top left). Large and stable colloids act as nuclei for perovskite-complex phase crystallization during the solution deposition, favoring the formation of uniform and pinhole-free precursor film. The large colloid sizes are attributed to the reaction between   $  PbAc_{2}  $   and DMAI, which reduces the nucleation energy barrier of the intermediate phase. It is reported that DMAAc molten salt can also be incorporated into the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   precursor to stabilize the perovskite precursor and improve the optoelectronic and structural quality of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films.  $  ^{[17]}  $   We synthesized DMAAc ionic liquid by reacting DMA with HAc (Figure S5, Supporting Information). Adding DMAAc to the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   precursor solution also leads to enlarged colloids (Figure S3a, Supporting Information) that favor the crystallization of the intermediates and final conversion to the perovskite phase. However, the average colloid size and distribution are still inferior to the target sample with 25%   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitution.

We then conducted the thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) measurement on DMAI and DMAAc to study their thermal properties (Figure 2c). The evaporation temperature of DMAAc at 41°C is much lower than that of DMAI at 239°C, confirming the high volatility of DMAAc. DMAAc resulting from the chemical reaction between PbAc $ _{2} $  and DMAI accelerates the evaporation of the DMA $ ^{+} $ -related substance from the wet precursor film and facilitates the accelerated supersaturation and rapid nuclearization of CsPbI $ _{3} $  perovskite during annealing. To further unveil the desorption behaviors of the volatile reaction products of DMAI and PbAc $ _{2} $  during annealing, we performed temperature-resolved mass spectrometry (TR-MS) measurements. Figure 2d displays the TR-MS of PbAc $ _{2} $ /DMAI mixed powders (at a molar ratio of 1:2). The reaction between PbAc $ _{2} $  and DMAI occurs spontaneously at close to room temperature, releasing mixed gas species, including dimethylamine (DMA), methylamine (MA), acetic acid (HAc), HI, iodomethane (CH $ _{3} $ I), and their fragments. Interestingly, a distinct signal of C $ _{3} $ H $ _{7} $ NO with a mass unit of 73 was observed at temperatures above 40°C, consistent with the vaporization temperature of DMAAc determined by TGA shown in Figure 2c. The H $ ^{1} $ NMR measurement on the mixed powders of DMAI/PbAc $ _{2} $  at a 2:1 ratio heated at 100°C for 12 h reveals a very weak peak of DMF (2.75 ppm, Figure S6, Supporting Information), indicating the formation of DMF through the reaction of DMAI and PbAc $ _{2} $ . Accompanied by the appearance of DMF, a unique signal associated with CH $ _{3} $ I was detected. We speculate that the decomposition of DMAAc can produce dimethylacetamide (DMAC, Mn = 87), which can further react with HI to form DMF, catalyzed by I and Pb, according to the Haloform reaction. $ ^{[50]} $

The thermal desorption dynamics of volatile species from the precursor films were also analyzed (Figure S7, Supporting Information). Introducing   $  PbAc_{2}  $   to the DMAI/CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   precursor ink lowers the peak evaporation temperatures of DMA and HI from 100 and 140°C to 60 and 120°C, respectively (Figure S7c, Supporting Information). Interestingly, we observed a two-stage evaporation behavior for the target film with   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitution (Figure S7d, Supporting Information). The desorption of HAc, DMA, and HI was observed at high temperatures (  $  \sim  $  120°C). However, DMF, DMA, MA, and their fragments are likely to form at

low temperatures (  $  \sim80^{\circ}C  $  ) due to the thermal decomposition of DMAAc, as discussed above. It is worth noting that the release of DMA, acetic acid, and DMF vapors during temperature ramping up produces an in-situ solvent vapor annealing (SVA) effect that promotes the diffusion-growth of perovskite grains. It is worth noting that the SVA process is self-regulated because the amount of the solvent released during annealing can be controlled by the annealing temperature (Figure S7, Supporting Information) and quantity of   $  PbAc_{2}  $   added into the precursor solution.

The fast phase transition from the   $  DMAPbI_{3}/Cs_{4}PbI_{6}  $   complex to the black-phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite in merely 30 s annealing proves the accelerated vaporization of DMA species. Additionally, the self-regulated solvent annealing benefits diffusion-growth, resulting in uniform, pinhole-free, and compact morphology.

All the fully annealed perovskite films show the characteristic XRD peaks of the   $  \beta  $   phase (Figure S13, Supporting Information) with a bandgap of 1.68 eV (Figure S14, Supporting Information). The films with   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitution feature superior crystallinity with enhanced XRD intensity compared with the Ref sample. Among them, the target film with 25%   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitution shows the highest peak intensity and strongest preferred (110)/(220) orientation. As shown in Figure 3e, the surface SEM image of the Ref film presents porous and inhomogeneous surface microstructures with many voids and irregular grains. As discussed above, this is likely attributed to the high nucleation energy of the intermediates during solution deposition and the slow perovskite phase conversion due to the slow evaporation of DMAI during annealing. After replacing 16.7%   $  PbI_{2}  $   with   $  PbAc_{2}  $   in the precursor (1:5), the porosity is significantly alleviated, and the grains become more uniform (Figure S15, Supporting Information). The target film presents a compact and dense morphology with homogenous grains (Figure 3f). There are terrains on the grain domain surface of target films under high magnification, further proving the superior crystallinity for better crystalline dynamics. The cross-section SEM image of the optimized   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films presents no pinholes and vertical grains with larger sizes (Figure S16, Supporting Information), indicating better crystallization with merit orientation growths. Further increasing the substitution ratio to 1:2 results in enlarged colloids in the precursor ink (Figure S3a, Supporting Information) and larger apparent grain domain sizes (Figure S15, Supporting Information). However, the number of colloids in the precursor ink is reduced, leading to uneven grain growth with some large voids among them. In addition, the high volatility of excess DMAAc in as-deposited precursor films can produce pinholes during film growth. These pinholes deteriorate the PV performance and stability of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs. We also conducted atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements to investigate the surface morphology (full images in Figure S17, Supporting Information). With   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitutions, the surface roughness of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films was significantly flattened from 24.2 nm (Ref, Figure 3e) to 11.7 nm (target, Figure 3f), which would provide a smooth top plate surface for better interface contact and lowered surface recombination.  $  ^{[55]}  $

We further characterized the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films to elucidate the benefits of improved film quality after implementing the   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitution strategy. Figure 4a compares the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) results of Pb 4f energy levels of the Ref and target   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films. The Ref film shows obvious metallic lead (  $  Pb_{0}  $  ) signals, whereas the target film shows no obvious   $  Pb_{0}  $   signals.   $  Pb_{0}  $   defects in perovskite have been proven detrimental to both the photoelectrical properties and stability.  $  ^{[56-57]}  $   The binding energy of Pb 4f peaks shifts slightly to lower energy, indicating the decrease in the cationic charge of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   ions, which is ascribed to the donation of lone-pair electrons in Ac  $  ^{-}  $   to passivate undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   atoms or less iodine vacancies on the surface. The rapid crystallization of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskites via the   $  PbAc_{2}  $  -DMAI reactions suppresses the formation of   $  Pb_{0}  $   and undercoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   defects, significantly improving the electronic quality of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $ . This is likely attributed to suppressed iodine gas evaporation during annealing, as evidenced by the TRMS analysis (Figure S7, Supporting Information).

Figure 4b displays the space-charge limited-current (SCLC) measurement results of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_3} $  films without and with  $ \mathrm{PbAc_2} $  substitution. The trap density  $ (N_{\mathrm{t}}) $  in  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_3} $  films fitted with the trap-filled limit voltage  $ (V_{\mathrm{TFL}}) $  is reduced from  $ 5.7\times 10^{15}\mathrm{cm}^{-3} $  to  $ 4.4\times 10^{15}\mathrm{cm}^{-3} $  after introducing the  $ \mathrm{PbAc_2} $  substitution, in agreement with the reduced  $ \mathrm{Pb_0} $  defects. The photoexcited charge carrier dynamics was investigated by the photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL (TRPL) measurements. The PL intensity shows one-folder enhancement after introducing the chemical reaction-driven crystallization (Figure 4c). In addition, the carrier lifetime is increased from 14.0 to 40.6 ns (Figure 4d), indicating reduced non-radiative recombination.

The energy levels of the Ref and target   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films were measured by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS, Figure S18a, Supporting Information), and the fitting results are displayed in Figure S18b (Supporting Information). The target   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film shows the up-shift energy levels with the Femi energy level (  $  E_{f}  $  ) closer to the conduction band edge of the PCBM electron-transport layer and the valence band edge closer to that of the P3CT-N hole-transport layer, benefiting the electron extraction and quasi-  $  E_{f}  $   splitting.  $  ^{[58]}  $   Figure 4e shows the Mott-Schottky curves of the Ref and target   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs (cross-section SEM image in Figure S19, Supporting Information). The built-in potential (  $  V_{bi}  $  ) of inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs is improved from 0.95 V to 1.04 V with the optimal   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitution, in good agreement with UPS results. The impedance spectroscopy measurements show that the target device exhibits larger recombination resistance than the Ref device (Figure 4f), corresponding to the suppressed nonradiative recombination. The improved photoelectric properties and better aligned energy levels of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films enable the superior photovoltaic performance of the devices.

The photovoltaic performances of the devices fabricated from various  $ \mathrm{PbAc}_2 $  substitution radios were investigated, and their  $ J\cdot V $  curves are displayed in Figure 5a and S20a. The Ref device delivers an efficiency of  $ 17.82\% $  with a  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of  $ 1.155\mathrm{V} $  and a  $ FF $  of  $ 76\% $  (Figure 5a). The target device shows an improved efficiency of  $ 20.17\% $ , with a high  $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $  of  $ 1.215\mathrm{V} $  and a  $ FF $  of  $ 80.89\% $ . This is among the highest reported efficiencies of inverted inorganic PSCs and is comparable to those with a regular structure (Table S1, Supporting Information). It is well known that black phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   is thermodynamically unstable and can spontaneously transform into a nonactive phase even at room temperature, especially under moisture conditions.  $  ^{[59-60]}  $   To evaluate the impact of the   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitution approach on device stability, we aged the target and Ref films in ambient air with a relative humidity (RH) of 40% (Figure S23, Supporting Information). The Ref film presented a remarked phase transition in 30 min and turned yellow after 1 h aging. In comparison, the target film displayed no color change after 1 h, which is ascribed to the promoted crystallization that suppresses defect formation in the target film, creating a strong barrier to   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   degradation. Additionally, we also tracked the long-term operational stability of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   solar cells. Figures 5d and S24 show that the Ref device suffered a pronounced efficiency drop to 80% of the initial efficiency after MPP tracking for 670 h in a nitrogen-filled glovebox. In sharp contrast, the target device maintained 95% of its initial efficiency after continuous operation at MPP for 1800 h. Furthermore, the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   absorber in the Ref device has transformed into the   $  \delta  $   phase, and the silver electrode shows apparent corrosion after operating for 1800 h. In contrast, the active area of the target device remained in the black phase (Figure S24d, Supporting Information). Thus, improving the crystallinity of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films can significantly promote the efficiency and stability of the inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs.

Scaling-up fabrication of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   is still challenging and seriously limited by inferior and heterogeneous crystallization of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   than organic-inorganic Pb halide perovskites. The   $  PbAc_{2}  $   substitution strategy utilizes colloid-driven crystallization and internal solvent-vapor annealing to overcome the obstacles of large nucleation energy barrier and slow-volatility of DMAI, paving the way for the scaling-up fabrication of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs. To demonstrate the feasibility of large-area production of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs, we used a scalable blade-coating method to deposit uniform   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films on   $  12 \times 12 \, cm^{2}  $   glass substrates (Figure S25a, Supporting Information). The Ref film presents inferior crystallization with an uneven and inferior surface, whereas the target film is uniform with an obvious mirror-like reflection. The bladed-coated Ref film mainly consists of major peaks belonging to the   $  \delta  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   with minor   $  \beta  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   peaks (Figure S25b, Supporting Information). The target film shows merely the   $  \beta  $  -phase with

strong peak intensity, indicating high crystallinity of phase pure   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite.

Using this approach, we fabricated   $  1-cm^{2}  $   inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs with a champion efficiency of 16.71% and an SPO efficiency of 15.14% (Figure S25c,d, Supporting Information), which is ahead of most reported large-area inorganic PSCs (Figure 5f and Table S3, Supporting Information). This deposition approach can also be used to fabricate regular (n-i-p) structure devices. As proof of concept, we fabricated   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   minimodules on   $  7.5 \times 7.5 cm^{2}  $   substrates with the regular device structure and demonstrated an efficiency of 15.1% with an active area of   $  26.77 cm^{2}  $   (Figure 5e). The small-area devices presented a champion efficiency of 17.08% (Figure S26, Supporting Information). The efficiency of minimodule is ahead of currently scalable-fabrication   $  CsPbX_{3}  $   PSCs (Figure 5f). These large-area cell and module results show the promise for the industrialization of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PV technology.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we investigated a   $  PbAc_{2}  $  -DMAI chemical reaction-driven crystallization approach for preparing compact and uniform all-inorganic perovskite   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films for large-area and efficient solar cells. Through a comprehensive study, we unveiled the role of   $  PbAc_{2}  $   in regulating the crystallization dynamics of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films. We found that introducing   $  PbAc_{2}  $   to the precursor forms the DMAAc, enabling enlarged colloid sizes and reducing the nucleation energy for the crystallization of the perovskite intermediate phase, resulting in uniform precursor film coating. Additionally, the volatile reaction product DMAAc with a low decomposition temperature is rapidly removed from the wet precursor film during high-temperature annealing, enabling a rapid conversion to the perovskite phase and a prolonged period for grain growth. Lastly, self-regulated internal solvent annealing promotes grain growth and aggregation, producing smooth, uniform, and dense   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films.

As a result, a champion efficiency of 20.17% was realized based on inverted   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs. The target device maintained 95.5% of its initial efficiency after MPP tracking for 1800 h in nitrogen. The approach was used for fabricating large-area   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   solar cells and minimodules, featuring an efficiency of up to 16.71%. Our work unveils the working mechanism of   $  PbAc_{2}  $   on regulating crystallization dynamics of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films and provides a novel and effective strategy for the scalable fabrication of efficient and stable   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs.
