# Stabilizing   $  \gamma  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   Perovskite via Phenylethylammonium for Efficient Solar Cells with Open-Circuit Voltage over 1.3 V

Qiufeng Ye, Fei Ma, Yang Zhao, Shiqi Yu, Zema Chu, Pingqi Gao,* Xingwang Zhang,* and Jingbi You*

Cesium lead iodide (CsPbI $ _3 $ ) perovskite has gained great attention due to its potential thermal stability and appropriate bandgap ( $ \approx $ 1.73 eV) for tandem cells. However, the moisture-induced thermodynamically unstable phase and large open-circuit voltage (V $ _{OC} $ ) deficit and also the low efficiency seriously limit its further development. Herein, long chain phenylethylammonium (PEA) is utilized into CsPbI $ _3 $  perovskite to stabilize the orthorhombic black perovskite phase ( $ \gamma $ -CsPbI $ _3 $ ) under ambient condition. Furthermore, the moderate lead acetate (Pb(OAc) $ _2 $ ) is controlled to combine with phenylethylammonium iodide to form the 2D perovskite, which can dramatically suppress the charge recombination in CsPbI $ _3 $ . Unprecedentedly, the resulted CsPbI $ _3 $  solar cells achieve a 17% power conversion efficiency with a record V $ _{OC} $  of 1.33 V, the V $ _{OC} $  deficit is only 0.38 V, which is close to those in organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Meanwhile, the PEA modified device maintains 94% of its initial efficiency after exceeding 2000 h of storage in the low-humidity controlled environment without encapsulation.

We adopted CsI,   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $  , and PEAI with the stoichiometry ratio of 1:1:2 to prepare the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   precursor as the previous study reported.  $  ^{[37]}  $   And we increased the solution concentration for improving the thickness of light absorbing layer. Afterwards, additional   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}}  $   were further added into   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   precursor to react with excess PEAI to form 2D perovskite, as shown in Figure 1a. We defined the R (ratio) =   $  \mathrm{Pb(OAc)_{2}/CsI}  $  , and a series of perovskite films with different R (1.00, 1.05, 1.10, 1.16, and 1.20) were prepared. The perovskite films were obtained by a one-step spin-coating method and subsequently annealing at 120 °C in ambient air. We performed X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurement to study the chemical composition of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films as shown in Figure S1, Supporting Information. Except for the Cs 3d, Pb 4f, and I 3d peak (Figure S1a–c, Supporting Information), the N 1s peak is also found (Figure S1d, Supporting Information), which confirms the presence of the ammonium ligands PEA in the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films. The weak signal of N 1s indicates that most PEA ligands are washed off by the solvent during the spin-coating process or evaporated in the post-annealing processes even though a large amount of PEA was added into the precursor solutions.  $  ^{[43]}  $

To determine the crystal structure of the perovskite films, we carried out X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements, as shown in Figure 1b,c. The XRD patterns in Figure 1b show the obvious peak split at around  $ 14.4^{\circ} $  and  $ 29.0^{\circ} $ , and the diffraction peaks were located at  $ 14.23^{\circ} $ ,  $ 14.40^{\circ} $ ,  $ 28.65^{\circ} $ , and  $ 29.00^{\circ} $ , which is corresponding to (002), (110), (004), and (220) of orthorhombic  $ \gamma $ -phase  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_3 $ .[44] It is remarkable that the relative peaks intensity of (110) and (220) compared to (002) and (004) show intensive with  $ R $  increase, indicating excess  $ \mathrm{Pb(OAc)}_2 $  facilitated the preferred  $ <110> $  orientation growth of the  $ \gamma $ -CsPbI\$_3\$ grains. Moreover, we further analyzed a series of Bragg reflections at the low angles ( $ <12^{\circ} $ ) of the PEA-CsPbI\$_3\$ perovskite films with a different value of  $ R $  (Figure 1c), and confirmed the formation of 2D perovskite when excess  $ \mathrm{Pb(OAc)}_2 $  was added. The black phase of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   is extremely sensitive to moisture, which is a critical challenge for stability.  $  ^{[17,50]}  $   We compared the phase stability and moisture resistance of PEA-  $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films and the   $  \alpha  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films by X-ray diffraction measurements (Figure S3, Supporting Information). It is found that the perovskite films based on PEA-  $  CsPbI_{3}  $   can be stable even more than 4 months in a dry air environment (RH = 5–10%), since the XRD pattern has no obvious change. However, under the same condition, the controlled   $  \alpha  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   is rapidly converted into the non-perovskite   $  \delta  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   as evidenced by the different XRD pattern. The PEA-  $  CsPbI_{3}  $   shows skyrocketing enhanced moisture stability may due to the increased micro-strain as we have discussed and the strong chemical bonding between functional groups of   $  PEA^{+}  $  and   $  CsPbI_{3}  $  .  $  ^{[41,51]}  $

From the SEM image, the thicknesses of SnO $ _2 $ , perovskite layer, Spiro-OMeTAD, and Au can be estimated to be about 25, 300, 240, and 120 nm, respectively. The energy level alignment is shown in Figure 3f, in which the energy levels of each layer except the perovskite absorber layer are attained from the previous report. $ ^{[30]} $

Overall, this result provided new thought for reducing the open-circuit voltage deficit caused by the charge recombination in perovskite bulk and improving moisture resistance, we firmly believe it will help improve the performance of inorganic perovskite in further research.

# Experimental Section

Materials: The   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanoparticle precursor was purchased from Alfa Aesar (tin (IV) oxide, 15% in   $  H_{2}O  $   colloidal dispersion). The Lead Iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  ), Lithium fluoride (LiF), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and Chlorobenzene were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. The PEAI, BAI, PAI, and 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-dip-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene(Spiro-OMeTAD) were obtained from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp (China). Cesium iodide (CsI) and lead acetate (  $  Pb(OAc)_{2}  $  ) were obtained from Aladdin Industrial Corporation (China).

Precursor Preparation: The electron transporting layer was using diluted   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanoparticle solution (  $  SnO_{2}  $   stock solution : deionized (DI) water = 1.3). The PEA-CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite precursors were consisted of CsI (338 mg, 1.3 mmol), different values (1.00, 1.05, 1.10, 1.16, and 1.20) of R relative to CsI for Pb(OAc)  $  _{2}  $     $  3H_{2}O  $  , and PEAI (647.4 mg, 2.6 mmol) dissolved in 1 mL pure DMSO solution. The   $  \alpha  $  -CsPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite precursor was prepared by using CsI (1 M) and PbI  $  _{2}  $   (1 M) dissolving in pure DMSO. The hole transporting layer was using 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-dip-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene(Spiro-OMeTAD) solution, which consisted of 72.3 mg Spiro-OMeTAD, 35   $  \mu  $  L bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSI) stock solution (260 mg Li-TFSI in 1 mL acetonitrile), 30   $  \mu  $  L 4-tertbutylpyridine, and 1 mL chlorobenzene.

Device Fabrication: ITO glass was washed using detergent, DI water, acetone, and isopropanol step by step to clean up. Next, the ITO substrate was disposed by UVO for 20 min and then spin-coated with the SnO₂ nanoparticle at a speed of 4000 rpm for 30s and annealed at 180 °C for 30 min just in ambient air. After that, the SnO₂/ITO substrate was disposed by UVO for 10 min and then a 3 nm LiF layer was vacuum-evaporated onto the SnO₂ surface. The PEA- CsPbI₃ precursor was spin-coated onto the SnO₂/LiF substrate at 6000 rpm for 40 s, then dried the fresh spin-coated perovskite films in glove box for 30 min, and subsequently annealed at 120 °C for 30 min in ambient air for the formation of γ-phase CsPbI₃. Before deposition, all the precursor solutions and SnO₂/ITO substrate were preheated on the hot plate at 120 °C. The hole transporting layer was deposited on top of the perovskite layer by 1500 rpm for 30 s. Conclusively, gold electrode was covered in the thickness of 80 nm by thermal evaporation using a shadow mask with an efficient device area of 0.108 cm² and a 0.0737 cm² non-reflective mask was used to define the accurate active cell area when measured.

Characterizations: XPS was also carried out on the Thermo Scientific ESCALab 250Xi with 200W monochromated Al Kα (1486.6 eV) radiation, and the XPS analysis using a 500 μm X-ray spot. The XRD patterns of the perovskite crystal structure were characterized by a Rigaku D/ MAX-2500 system using Cu Kα radiation. The SEM measurements were measured with FEI NanoSEM650 to obtain the morphology of films and cross-section structure of devices. The TRPL measurements were carried out by Edinburgh instruments F900 (UK). UV-Vis spectras were carried out by a Varian Cary 5000 spectrophotometer. UPS measurements were conducted on a Thermo Scientific ESCALab 250Xi, using Hel (21.22 eV) radiation lines, where Ni was used as the standard sample for calibration. The J-V characteristics of the solar cells were measured with a Keithley 2400 source meter under a simulated AM 1.5 G spectrum and a solar simulator (Enli Tech, Taiwan) in nitrogen glove box. Before each measurement, the solar simulator was calibrated with a Si solar cell (KG-5). The devices were both measured in reverse scan (1.4V→0V, step 0.02 V), and forward scan (0V→1.4V, step 0.02 V), the photovoltaic devices were measured both forward scan and reverse scan at a scan rate of 0.02 V s  $  ^{-1}  $  . The devices were taken out for EQE measurement, the EQE were measured by Enli tech (Taiwan) EQE measurement system. For the moisture stability, the unencapsulated PSCs were stored in ambient condition (humidity = 30–40%) and in a controllable low humidity glove box (humidity = 5–10%), respectively, and the J-V curves were measured every approximately 100 h.
