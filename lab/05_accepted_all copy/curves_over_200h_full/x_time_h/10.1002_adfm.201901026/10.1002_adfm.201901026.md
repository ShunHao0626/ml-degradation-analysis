# Zwitterion Coordination Induced Highly Orientational Order of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   Perovskite Film Delivers a High Open Circuit Voltage Exceeding 1.2 V

Weiran Zhou, Dan Li, Zhengguo Xiao, Zhilin Wen, Mengmeng Zhang, Wanpei Hu, Xiaojun Wu, Mingtai Wang, Wen-Hua Zhang, Yalin Lu, Shihe Yang,* and Shangfeng Yang*

The organic-inorganic halide   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) has been the most commonly used light absorber layer of perovskite solar cells (PSCs); however, solution-processed MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films usually suffer from random crystal orientation and high trap density, resulting in inferior power conversion efficiency (PCE) with open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) being typically below 1.2 V for PSC devices. Herein, for the first time an imidazole sulfonate zwitterion, 4-(1H-imidazol-3-ium-3-yl) butane-1-sulfonate (IMS), is applied as a bifunctional additive in regular-structure planar heterojunction PSC devices to regulate the crystal orientation, yielding highly ordered MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film and passivating the trap states of the film. Such a dual effect of IMS is fulfilled via coordination interactions between the sulfonate moiety of IMS with the Pb2  $  ^{+}  $   ion and the electrostatic interaction between the imidazole of IMS with the I  $  ^{-}  $   ion of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  . As a result, under a optimized IMS doping ratio of 0.5 wt%, the PSC device exhibits a significant increase in PCE from 18.77% to 20.84%, with suppressed current–voltage hysteresis and promoted ambient stability. Moreover, a high   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.208 V is achieved under a higher IMS doping ratio of 1.2 wt%, which is the highest   $  V_{oc}  $   for regular-structure MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   planar PSC devices based on TiO  $  _{2}  $   electron transport layer.

In this work, we synthesized an imidazole sulfonate (4-(1H-imidazol-3-ium-3-yl) butane-1-sulfonate, abbreviated as IMS) zwitterion and applied it in regular-structure planar heterojunction PSC devices for the first time. The effects of IMS additive on the crystallization, grain orientation, and trap states of the perovskite film are investigated systematically, revealing that the IMS zwitterion is not only able to regulate the crystalline grain orientation but also passivate the trap states of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film especially the iodine vacancy. As a result, a highly ordered  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film is achieved, and the PCE enhances from  $ 18.77\% $  to  $ 20.84\% $  after incorporation of IMS zwitterion with suppressed hysteresis. Moreover, the highest  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  reaches 1.208 V under a high IMS doping ratio, which is the highest  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  for regular-structure  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  planar PSC devices based on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL.

# 2.2. Effects of IMS on the Crystallinity and Orientation of Perovskite Film

The effect of IMS doping on the crystallinity of the MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite film is investigated by XRD measurements. Figure 2a compares the XRD patterns of MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite film without and with IMS doping. Clearly, the two intense diffraction peaks at 14.1° and 28.5° assigned respectively to (110) and (220) lattice planes of tetragonal MAPbI $ _3^{[12,26,27]} $  exhibit no obvious shift after IMS doping, revealing that IMS does not participate in formation of MAPbI $ _3 $  crystal lattice.

Thus, IMS addition does not affect the bandgap of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite as confirmed by UV-vis absorption spectroscopic measurements. However, the intensities of the two intense diffraction peaks increase gradually with the increase of the IMS doping ratio, suggesting the improvement of the crystallinity of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite.

It is then intriguing to investigate whether IMS doping would affect the crystalline orientation of MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite besides the crystallinity. We investigated the effect of IMS doping on the crystallographic orientation of perovskite layer by using synchrotron-based GIXRD. Figure 2b shows the 2D-GIXRD profiles of the MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite films without and with IMS doping obtained under an incident angle of 0.2°. The GIXRD profiles are similar to those reported crystallized MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite films, and the scattered rings at  $ q \approx 10 $ , 20, 22.2 nm $ ^{-1} $  correspond respectively to the (110), (220), and (310) diffractions of tetragonal perovskite crystal. $ ^{[28,29]} $  The pristine perovskite film without IMS shows a Debye–Scherrer ring for (110) plane with an almost isotropic intensity distribution along the azimuthal angle, suggesting random arrangement of the 3D crystal grains. By contrast, the discrete Bragg spots are observed after IMS doping (Figure 2b-B,C), indicating the improved orientation of the perovskite crystal grain with the (110) plane parallel to the substrate surface. $ ^{[30]} $  Similar phenomenon is also observed when the incident angle increases to 1.0°, confirming the same preferred orientation of the bulk perovskite crystal grain (see Figure S6, Supporting Information). Meanwhile, the same trend is observed for (220) and (310) crystalline planes. This improvement in the crystalline orientation is further verified by XRD rocking curves for (110) reflections at 2θ = 14.1° (see Figure S7, Supporting Information). Besides, Figure 2c shows the azimuthal scan profiles for (110) plane extracted from the 2D-GIXRD patterns. Notably, the peak at 90° becomes higher and sharper

# 2.3. Interactions of IMS with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $

In order to understand the mechanism why IMS doping induces ordered growth of MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite grain, we measured the XRD patterns of the as-prepared perovskite films without thermal annealing so as to track the status of perovskite crystallization in the initial stage (Figure 4a). Interestingly, upon IMS doping a new peak at 13.8° appears along which the diffraction peak at 14.1° correlated to (110) lattice plane of MAPbI $ _3 $ , which can be tentatively ascribed to newly formed MAPbI $ _3 $ -IMS intermediate phase. $ ^{[27,33]} $  Given that Pb $ ^{2+} $  ions within MAPbI $ _3 $  can readily coordinate with Lewis bases with lone electron pairs due to its empty electron orbits, $ ^{[16]} $  we assume that the sulfonate group with a lone electron pair on the oxygen (O) atom within IMS could function as a ligand with which Pb $ ^{2+} $  ions can coordinate, leading to the formation of MAPbI $ _3 $ -IMS intermediate. $ ^{[13,22,34]} $  To verify such an assumption, we prepared pure PbAc $ _2 $ , IMS, and blend of PbAc $ _2 $  and IMS (PbAc $ _2 $  + IMS) dispersions in

# 2.4. Effect of IMS on the PSC Device Performance

We next fabricate regular-structure (n-i-p) planar heterojunction PSC devices to examine whether the effect of IMS on promoting MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite crystalline grain orientation contributes to the PSC device performance. The device structure is indium tin oxide (ITO)/TiO $ _2 $ /perovskite/2,2',7,7'-tetrakis (N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD)/Au. Figure 5a compares the current density-voltage (J-V) curves of the champion devices based on MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite with or without IMS doping measured under one sun illumination. The photovoltaic parameters including  $ V_{oc} $ ,  $ J_{sc} $ , fill factor (FF), PCE, series resistance ( $ R_s $ ), and shunt resistance ( $ R_{sh} $ ) are summarized in Table 1. The control device without IMS exhibits a PCE of 18.77% with a  $ V_{oc} $  of 1.103 V, a  $ J_{sc} $  of 21.92 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and an FF of 77.60%. Under the optimized IMS doping ratio of 0.5 wt%, the PCE increases significantly to 20.84% with a  $ V_{oc} $  of 1.140 V, a  $ J_{sc} $  of 22.08 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and a FF of 82.82% (see Figure S9, Supporting Information for the optimization of IMS doping ratio).

# 2.5. Effect of IMS on the Stability of PSC Device

Finally, the effect of IMS doping on the stability of the PSC device is examined. The PCE of 0.5 wt% device can be stabilized at 19.24% at the maximum power point (see Figure S20, Supporting Information). Furthermore, we evaluated device stability under ambient conditions without any encapsulation (temperature: 20 °C, relative humidity: 35%) for 1000 h, as shown in Figure 7c. PCE of the control device without IMS drops by ≈40% after 1000 h storage, while the device with 0.5 wt% IMS doping shows a PCE drop of only ≈20% under the same condition, indicating that IMS doping improves dramatically the ambient stability (see also Figure S19, Supporting Information for details). It has been reported that the grain boundaries with high density of defects are vulnerable to the ambient environment and thus normally become the sites where the degradation of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  to PbI  $  _{2}  $  starts.  $  ^{[9,52]}  $  The decrease of density of grain boundaries after IMS doping leads to the improved ambient stability of PSC devices, as confirmed by the obvious suppression of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  degradation to PbI  $  _{2}  $  (see Figure S20, Supporting Information). However, 1.2 wt% IMS device shows an inferior stability with a PCE drop of ≈45%, and this may be due to the interaction between the excess IMS and moisture, leading to rapid degradation of devices.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, by doping an IMS zwitterion fulfilling coordination interaction with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and consequently reinforced interactions between zwitterion with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ , we fabricated high-efficiency PSC devices with highly ordered perovskite film and improved crystallinity. In addition to regulating the crystalline grain orientation, IMS can also passivate the trap states especially the iodine vacancy, thus playing a role as a bifunctional additive. As a result, under the optimized IMS doping ratio of  $ 0.5\mathrm{wt}\% $ , we achieved a significant enhancement of the PCE of the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ -based regular-structure planar heterojunction PSC device from  $ 18.77\% $  to  $ 20.84\% $  with suppressed current-voltage hysteresis and improved stability. A high  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  of  $ 1.208\mathrm{V} $  is achieved under a higher IMS doping ratio of 1.2 wt%, which is the highest  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  for regular-structure  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  planar PSC devices based on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  ETL. This study offers new insights into additive engineering, and can be extended to other zwitterionic additives for realizing high-efficiency perovskite solar cells with the least  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  loss.

# 4. Experimental Section

Materials: All chemicals were used as received without further purification, including imidazole (99%, Alfa Aesar), 1,4-butane sultone (99%, Alfa Aesar),   $  PbAc_{2} \cdot 3H_{2}O  $   (99.998%, Aladdin),   $  PbCl_{2}  $   (99.999%, Alfa Aesar), ethanesulfonic acid (96%, Alfa Aesar), lithium

bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI) (99%, Aladdin), 4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP) (97%, Adamas Reagent), DMF (99.8%, Sigma-Aldrich), and chlorobenzene (99.9%, Alfa Aesar). Ethanol, isopropanol, acetone, and acetonitrile were all purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. Spiro-OMeTAD was purchased from 1 M company. CH₃NH₃I was bought from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corporation.

Synthesis of IMS: IMS was synthesized following a procedure reported in literature.  $  ^{[23,24]}  $   Imidazole (680 mg, 10 mmol) was dissolved in toluene (10 mL), and equal-molar 1,4-butane sultone was slowly added. The mixture was stirred at room temperature for three days and filtered to get the white precipitate. Subsequently, the product was washed with diethyl ether and recrystallized with ethanol for several times and dried in vacuum to afford IMS as a white solid (1.53 g, 75%).

Device Fabrication: The ITO-coated glass substrate (Shenzhen Nan Bo Group, China) with a sheet resistance of   $  10 \Omega sq^{-1}  $   was ultrasonicated in detergent, deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol for 15 min before being dried at   $  60^{\circ}C  $  . A low-temperature processed   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer was prepared by spin-coating the dispersed   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticles solution onto ITO at 5000 rpm for 40 s and dried at   $  150^{\circ}C  $   for 30 min. The   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite layer was fabricated by a simple one-step spin-coating method without antisolvent extraction. The perovskite precursor solution (comprised of 2.2 M MAI, 0.8 M   $  PbAc_{2}  $  , and 0.2 M   $  PbCl_{2}  $   in 1 mL of DMF) was spin-coated onto the substrate at 3000 rpm for 40 s, followed by a thermal annealing at   $  100^{\circ}C  $   for 10 min. The IMS additive with variable doping ratio (0.2–2 wt%, weight ratio) was dispersed in DMF and added into the perovskite precursor solution before spin-coating. For the deposition of hole transporting layer, spiro-OMeTAD (73.2 mg) was first dissolved in 1 mL of chlorobenzene, and then 28.8   $  \mu  $  L of t-BP and 18.8   $  \mu  $  L of Li-TFSI solution (520 mg Li-TFSI in 1 mL of acetonitrile) were added. The spiro-OMeTAD solution was spin-coated onto the perovskite layer at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, the device was transferred into a vacuum chamber (  $  =10^{-6} torr  $  ) and the Au electrode (  $  \approx60 nm thick  $  ) was thermally deposited through a shadow mask to define the effective active area of the device (  $  0.10 cm^{2}  $  ). All device fabrication procedures were carried out in a N  $  _{2}  $  -purged glovebox (<0.1 ppm of O  $  _{2}  $   and H  $  _{2}  $  O).

Measurements and Characterization: The current density voltage (J-V) characterizations were measured by a Keithley 2400 source measurement unit under simulated AM 1.5 irradiation (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) with a standard xenon-lamp-based solar simulator (Oriel Sol 3A), which was calibrated with a monocrystalline silicon reference cell (Oriel P/N 91 150 V, with KG-5 visible color filter) calibrated by the National Renewable Energy Laboratory. The measurements were performed with a mask (well-defined area of size 0.10 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) to ensure accurate measurement under ambient conditions. Around 40 devices were fabricated and measured independently under each experimental condition to warrant the reproducibility of the result and to obtain the statistical data of the photovoltaic parameters. The EQE measurements were performed on an ORIEL Intelligent Quantum Efficiency 200TM Measurement system established with a tunable light source. The SEM images were obtained via field-emission scanning electron microscope (Zeiss Gemini SEM 500, Germany) using secondary electron. AFM images were acquired on a XE-7 scanning probe microscope in noncontact mode (Park systems, Korea). XRD patterns were attained on a Rigaku SmartLab X-ray diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (0.154 nm). The GIXRD measurements were performed at the BL14B1 beamline of Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF) employing X-ray with a wavelength of 0.6887 Å. The 2D-GIXRD patterns were acquired by a MarCCD detector mounted vertically at a distance of around 274 mm from the sample with an exposure time of 50 s at different grazing incidence angles of 0.2° and 1°. The 2D-GIXRD patterns were analyzed using software Fit 2D and displayed in scattering vector q coordinates with q = 4tsinθ/λ, where θ is half of the diffraction angle, and λ is the wavelength of incident X-ray. XPS measurements were performed on a Thermo ESCALAB 250 instrument with a monochromatized Al Kα X-ray source under vacuum. The NEXAFS measurements were performed at the Catalysis and Surface Science endstation in the National Synchrotron Radiation Laboratory, Hefei.
