The photovoltaic efficiency and stability of planar p-i-n perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) are largely dependent on charge carrier dynamics and surface properties of hole transport layers (HTLs). Herein we introduce ammonium metatungstate hydrate (AMH) into poly(3,4-ethylenedio-xythiophene): poly(-styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) for the first time. The results showed that AMH dopant could not only enhance the conductivity of PEDOT:PSS film without impact its transmissivity, but also change the surface property of HTL after the annealing process of composite HTL. The composite HTL yields the perovskite with large average grain size, smooth and dense film. Consequently, the average power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 14.61% was achieved in PVSCs based on the optimal concentration of AMH modified PEDOT:PSS, with improved short-circuit current (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) and fill factor (FF). Meanwhile, the ambient stability of AMH modified devices is also enhanced by more than 3 times compared with the control devices.

# 1. Introduction

Due to the excellent properties like facile fabrication process, low-cost and high power conversion efficiency, the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite based solar cells (PVSCs) have been considered as an impressive candidate for the next generation solar cell to solve the critical energy and environmental problems   $  [1-5]  $  . During the past few years, the PVSCs exhibit improved power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 3.8% to 22.1%   $  [6,7]  $  . Among the many reported PVSCs, the planar p-i-n PVSCs which have simple architecture and low temperature preparation processing are attracting great attention   $  [8-11]  $  . Many strategies have been adopted to optimize the performance of planar p-i-n PVSCs, including perovskite crystalline regulation   $  [12,13]  $  , design of new perovskite component   $  [14,15]  $  , geomorphology control   $  [16]  $   and modification of the charge carriers transport layers   $  [17-20]  $  . Especially, the hole transport layer (HTL) which between the perovskite layer and the ITO anode has a critical influence on hole collection and crystal formation of the perovskite layer, then determines the ultimate performance of PVSCs   $  [21]  $  .

# ABSTRACT

To realize the optimized performance of planar p-i-n PVSCs, some p-type materials have been employed in PVSCs to function as HTL, such as inorganic metal oxide materials of NiOx,   $  CuGaO_{2}  $  ,   $  MoO_{3}  $  ,   $  Cu_{2}O  $   [22–24] and organic polymers of PTAA, poly-TPD, PDPP3T, PEDOT:PSS [25–29]. As a result of the advantages of high conductivity and excellent optical transparency in the visible light, PEDOT:PSS [poly(3,4-ethylenedio-xythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate)] has become the most widely used material to fabricate HTL of planar p-i-n PVSCs [30–33]. Generally, PEDOT:PSS is fabricated upon the transparent anode to realize smooth surface and regulate the energy level between perovskite layer and electrode. However, PEDOT:PSS still has some drawbacks that are difficult to resolve, The acid and hydrophilic PEDOT:PSS would form an undesirable PEDOT:PPS/perovskite interfacial contact, leading the reduction of perovskite grain size and the degradation of atmospheric stability [34,35]. The low open circuit voltages caused by low work function of PEDOT:PSS also needs to be considered. Furthermore, the insulated PSS restricts the charge transport within the PEDOT:PSS layer, which leads to accumulation of charge carrier at the interfaces to reduce the performance of PVSCs [31]. Consequently, the modification of PEDOT:PSS layer for keeping its inherent advantages is indispensable to promote the performance and stability of planar p-i-n PVSCs.

Various materials have been reported to incorporate with PEDOT:PSS, for instance, inorganic carbon material   $  [36,37]  $  , organic small molecule and polymers   $  [35,38–40]  $  . But most of these methods face the problem of high cost, as well as the complex process of material synthesis and surface preparation, which reduces the application of these methods in the commercialization of PVSCs. So it is imperative to develop simple methods and easily available materials to solve this problem. As a low-cost and hydro soluble precursor material, ammonium metatungstate hydrate (AMH,   $  (\mathrm{NH}_{4})_{6}\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{W}_{12}\mathrm{O}_{40}\cdot\mathrm{xH}_{2}\mathrm{O}  $  ) has been extensively applied to synthesize p-type material   $  WO_{3}  $     $  [41–43]  $  . However, as potential candidates for water-soluble inorganic metal compounds, there are still short of literature to related studies about their application into PEDOT:PSS. In this work, AMH is utilized as a dopant into PEDOT:PSS to form composite HTL in planar p-i-n PVSCs. Specially, during the indispensable annealing process of PEDOT:PSS, the surface properties of AMH doped HTL can be modified, without any further treatment on it. After systematic investigating the electrical and morphological characteristics of AMH doped PEDOT:PSS films and the corresponding perovskite films upon them, the efficiency and ambient stability of AMH modified PVSCs had been measured to testify the feasibility of AMH doping into PVSCs.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Fabrication of planar  $ p $ -i-n PVSCs

The planar p-i-n PVSCs were fabricated on indium tin oxide (ITO)-coated glass substrates with a sheet resistance of 10 V/sq. The substrates were consecutively cleaned consecutively in an ultrasonic bath containing detergent, acetone, deionized water, and isopropyl alcohol for 15 min each step, and then dried at 80 °C for 1 h prior to use. Then the substrates were treated with UV-ozone (PSD-UV3, 40 W, NovaScan) for 10 min. 20 mg AMH (Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd) was dissolved into deionized water to prepare AMH solution. Then different volume ratios of AMH ((NH₄)₆H₂W₁₂O₄₀·xH₂O) solution (0, 2.5, 5, 7.5 and 10 vol %) were added into the PEDOT:PSS solution (Clevios PVP Al 4083, Heraeus, Germany) and stir for two hours. The pristine PEDOT:PSS and doped PEDOT:PSS solution were spin coated at 3000 rpm for 60 s to fabricate HTL, then the substrates were annealed on a hot plate in air at 130 °C for 35 min. After that, the samples were transferred to a N₂ glove box to fabricate perovskite layer. The perovskite precursor solution was prepared by mixing 744 mg CH₃NH₃I with 254.3 mg PbI₂ (Polymer Light Technology Corp.) in 1 mL N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF, Sigma-Aldrich). The perovskite precursor solution was deposited upon different HTL at 6500 rpm for 25 s. It was noted that 300 μL chlorobenzene (CB, Sigma-Aldrich) was quickly dropped onto the center of the spinning film as anti-solvent at 6 s after the beginning of the spin-coating program to get a smooth perovskite film. Then the CH₃NH₃PbI₃ as-prepared thin films were heated at 100 °C for 1 h on heat plate to obtain a dark-brown film. Subsequently, PC₆₁BM (Polymer Light Technology Corp.) solution in o-dichlorobenzene (DCB) with 22 mg/mL was spin coated on perovskite films at 1600 rpm for 35 s, then the substrates were annealed on a 100 °C hot plate for 1 h. Bphen (99.98%, Aldrich) layer was deposited onto the substrates at a rate of 1 Å/s at a pressure of 3 × 10⁻⁴ Pa in vacuum, followed by the deposition of Ag anode at a rate of about 4 Å/s under a pressure of 2 × 10⁻³ Pa in a vacuum chamber. 20 devices had been fabricated for each AMH concentration, respectively. The typical area of PSCs is 0.045 cm².

# 2.2. Characterization and measurement

A light source integrated with a xenon lamp (CHF-XM35, Beijing Trusttech) with an illumination power of   $  100 \, mW/cm^{2}  $   was used as a solar simulator. The current-voltage curves in the dark and under simulated AM 1.5G solar illumination were measured with a Keithley 4200 programmable voltage-current source (the step of J-V scan is 0.01 V, with start voltage is -0.5 V and finish voltage is 1.0 V), and the external quantum efficiency (EQE, Solar Cell Scan 100, Zolix) spectra were measured under the lump light passing through a monochromator. Photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy and time-resolved PL spectra were obtained using a PL microscopic spectrometer (Jobin Yvon Fluorolog-3-TAU). The impedance measurements were tested on the CHI660E electrochemical workstation (CH instruments, Inc.). TGA and DSC were taken using simultaneous thermal analyzer (STA 449 F3, Netzsch, Shanghai, China) with crucible (DSC/TG pan AI2O3). The grains of perovskite were tested via field scanning electronic microscope (FSEM, FEI Inspect F50) and X-ray diffraction (XRD, Bruker AXS GMBH D2 Phaser). The attenuated total reflectance fourier transform infrared (FTIR) measurements were conducted with a FTIR spectrometer (Thermo Scientific, Nicole-10). The ultraviolet–visible (UV–Vis) absorption spectra and transmittance spectra of the devices on quartz substrates were measured using a Shimadzu UV1700 system. The film preparation conditions for Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM) measurement (Agilent, AFM 5500) were kept the same as the device fabrication for comparison. All the measurements were carried out under ambient conditions without any encapsulation from air.

# 3. Results and discussion

# 3.1. Characterization of AMH modified PEDOT:PSS HTLs

The structure of PVSCs is ITO/PEDOT:PSS with or without AMH doping (40 nm)/CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $  perovskite layer (300 nm)/PC $ _{61} $ BM (40 nm)/Bphen (2 nm)/Ag (100 nm) (Fig. 1a). This device architecture is used to explore the effect of the AMH doped PEDOT:PSS on the perovskite crystalline quality and the device performance. An ultrathin Bphen layer is functioned as a modifier to manipulate the interface contact between PC $ _{61} $ BM and cathode [37]. The cross-sectional SEM image of the full device with AMH is shown in Fig. 1b, and the cross-sectional SEM image of pristine device is shown in Fig. S1. We could notice that the doping of AMH would not affect the film thickness.

Above all, the optical and electrical characteristics of AMH doped PEDOT:PSS film had been studied. The UV–Vis absorption spectra of pristine PEDOT:PSS film and AMH doped PEDOT:PSS film were tested and shown in Fig. 1c and Fig. S2a. It can be noted that in the visible light range (from 350 nm to 800 nm) the pristine and doped PEDOT:PSS films exhibit similar absorption intensity, indicating that moderate amount of the AMH doping would not obviously decrease the transmittance of the PEDOT:PSS film. Therefore, the perovskite layer above the doped PEDOT:PSS is still able to acquire enough photons. Subsequently, the film conductivities of HTLs with different concentration of AMH are displayed in Fig. 1d and Fig. S2b. The corresponding device structure is ITO/PEDOT:PSS with or without AMH doping (40 nm)/Au (80 nm). From Fig. 1d, 5 vol % AMH doped PEDOT:PSS film has a higher current density of   $  1.03 \times 10^{-2}  $   S/cm than that of pristine PEDOT:PSS film of   $  7.11 \times 10^{-3}  $   S/cm. However, a high concentration of AMH would decrease the conductivity of composite HTL. Hence, with the moderate dopant of AMH, the hole transport property of PEDOT:PSS can be improved, which is profitable for the performance enhancement of PVSCs [40].

# 3.2. Perovskite crystallization upon AMH modified PEDOT:PSS HTLs

As well known, the hole transport layer that below the perovskite layer can determine the quality of perovskite grain growth [10,44]. Before the study of the perovskite crystallinity, the surface morphology of PEDOT:PSS and 5 vol % AMH doped PEDOT:PSS films was evaluated via AFM (Fig. 2a–b). Compared with the pristine PEDOT:PSS film, although the AMH doped PEDOT:PSS film shows a slightly higher root means square (RMS) roughness value (0.927 and 1.039 nm, respectively), both films have similar smooth interfaces. It indicates that the doping of AMH would not destroy the dispersion property of PEDOT:PSS, which provides a desirable condition for the growth of perovskite grains. Then the corresponding SEM images of the perovskite films upon pristine and 5 vol % AMH doped PEDOT:PSS films are shown in Fig. 2c–d, and the corresponding less magnified SEM images of different concentration AMH are shown in Fig. S3. In Fig. 2c, the perovskite film which grows on pristine PEDOT:PSS film exhibits small average grain size and larger roughness. By contrast, the average grain size of perovskite upon AMH doped PEDOT:PSS film is obviously increased, and the perovskite film becomes smoother and denser. As a result, larger grains and fewer grain boundaries would decrease the charge carrier recombination, leading to an improved carrier transport properties of perovskite layer [31,45]. But as shown in Fig. S3d, the perovskite layer that upon composite HTL doped with 10 vol % AMH has an inferior crystal quality. It means

that high concentration of AMH may make some negative effect to the growth of perovskite grains.

To further study the growth of perovskite crystal films upon different HTLs, the X-ray diffraction spectrometry (XRD) of perovskite layer upon pristine or doped PEDOT:PSS films had been measured. Consistent with the SEM results, the average crystal size of AMH modified perovskite film estimated by the Scherrer equation using XRD data is approximately 66 nm, larger than that of pristine film (59 nm). As shown in Fig. 3a, after 5 vol % AMH doping, the intensities of diffraction peaks at 14.2°, 28.6°, and 32.1° (correspond to (110), (220), and (310) planes of the perovskite, respectively) [38,46] become stronger than that of the perovskite film on pristine PEDOT:PSS. It means that the PEDOT:PSS modified by AMH is favorable of the growth of the perovskite grains, which agrees well with the above-mentioned SEM results. In addition, the diffraction peak at 12.7° (correspond to (001) plane of the PbI₂) is slightly enhanced, implying the increased amount of PbI₂ residual remaining inside the perovskite film which upon the doped PEDOT:PSS. According to the previous reports, moderate amount of PbI₂ residues in the perovskite film is able to reduce the recombination probability of charge carriers due to its passivation function on the grain boundary and interface [46]. As a result, these advantages of perovskite grains which grow on the AMH doped PEDOT:PSS could improve photovoltaic performance of PVSCs. In addition, Fig. 3b exhibits the UV–Vis absorption spectra of the perovskite layers upon different HTLs. It can be seen that both the

# 3.3. Transformation of surface properties by doping AMH into PEDOT:PSS

From the above analysis, although the doping of AMH would not significantly affect the morphology of PEDOT:PSS, the crystalline quality of perovskite films is significantly improved. Therefore, the interface contact properties between PEDOT:PSS and perovskite may be optimized by AMH doping. To verify this point, the contact angle measurements had been utilized to investigate the surface properties of the PEDOT:PSS HTL with or without AMH doping (Fig. 4a–b). After annealing, the hydrophilic pristine PEDOT:PSS film gives a low water contact angle of   $  10^{\circ}  $  , and this value is

enhanced to   $  37^{\circ}  $   on doped PEDOT:PSS. This phenomenon indicates that during the annealing process (which is a necessary step for PEDOT:PSS film) of composite HTL, most of the bound water in AMH will evaporate without further treatment on it. In other words, the surface of PEDOT:PSS is changed from wetting to non-wetting via AMH doping, and non-wetting surface means lower surface energy [44]. It has been verified that the low surface energy of HTL could suppresses the heterogeneous nucleation, contributing to the increase of crystal grain size and the formation of uniform perovskite films [27]. Moreover, the hydrophobicity of HTL is favorable for stability because of preventing water molecules from penetrating into the perovskite layer, causing much slower detrimental degradation of the perovskite layer, and the details would discuss later. It is noteworthy that, although AMH is a water-soluble material, the AMH doped PEDOT:PSS film exhibits hydrophobicity after annealing. During the annealing process on a hot plate at 130 °C, 35 min, large amount of bound water evaporated out of the composite film. The hydrophobicity/hydrophilicity of AMH changed via annealing, thereby turning the surface properties of the composite film more favorable for the grain growth of perovskite.

# 3.4. Device performance of PVSCs based on AMH modified PEDOT:PSS

To explore the availability of AMH doped PEDOT:PSS as a HTL in planar p-i-n devices, the PVSCs with different volume ratios of AMH doped PEDOT:PSS were fabricated with the structure as shown in Fig. 1a. Fig. 5a exhibits the current density-voltage (J-V) characteristics of PVSCs based on pristine PEDOT:PSS and optimized ratio (5 vol %) of AMH doped PEDOT:PSS, and the corresponding devices operating parameters involving the open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ), current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ), fill factor (FF), and PCE are summarized in Table 1. In addition, the J-V curves and operating parameters of PVSCs with variant doping ratio of AMH are shown in Fig. S2 and Table S1. The control devices with pristine PEDOT:PSS as the HTL have an average PCE of 11.14%, together with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 0.905 V,   $  J_{SC}  $   of 18.22 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , FF of 67.56%, and the best PCE among them is 11.82%. While incorporating 5 vol % AMH into PEDOT:PSS as the HTL, the average   $  V_{OC}  $  ,   $  J_{SC}  $   and FF are increased to 0.910 V, 21.68 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and 74.06%, respectively. As a result, compared with the control devices, 31% enhancement in PCE is obtained from the AMH doped PEDOT:PSS HTL, with the average PCE reaches 14.61%, and the best PCE reaches 15.34%. The enhancement of   $  J_{SC}  $   and FF should be

attributed to the improved conductivity of AMH doped PEDOT:PSS, and the reduction of charge recombination which caused by an optimized grain growth quality of perovskite film. There is no obvious change to the   $  V_{OC}  $   value, so we surmise that the doping of AMH would not significantly affect the energy level of PEDOT:PSS. Also, the hysteresis behavior of the PVSCs with or without AMH doping is investigated. In Fig. 5a, the devices using pristine PEDOT:PSS as HTL shows small hysteresis. Compared with the undoped devices, negligible difference can be observed between reverse and forward scans for those AMH doped devices. The reduced hysteresis might be attributed to the improved charge transport ability and the decreasing charge recombination rate of AMH modified PVSCs which lead to a more balanced electron and hole mobility [31].

The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra and corresponding integrated current curves of PVSCs with different HTLs are exhibited in Fig. 5b. The PVSCs employed with pristine PEDOT:PSS HTL show over 70% EQE in the range from 400 nm to 750 nm. The AMH doped PVSCs have higher EQE that over 80% in this wavelength range. The trend of EQE is in agreement with the above J-V characteristics. The corresponding integrated current curves estimated from the EQE spectra are also in good agreement with the Jsc values obtained from the J-V curves. Fig. 5c shows the steady-state current output and stabilized PCE of the AMH doped PVSCs, which are tested under the maximum power point voltage (0.81 V). The devices based on AMH doped PEDOT:PSS film exhibit a steady-state current density of 21.42 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , with a stabilized PCE of 14.43%. The results match well with those values of J-V curves. Moreover, to confirm the variation tendency of the device performance for AMH doped PVSCs, the distribution of PCEs based on 20 devices were measured and the corresponding values are plotted in Fig. 5d. The majority of PCEs concentrated between 14% and 15%, implying good device repeatability of the PVSCs with AMH doped PEDOT:PSS HTL. It means that the doping of AMH into PEDOT:PSS film is stable and universal to the planar p-i-n PVSCs.

Therefore, the improved stability of AMH doped devices could be attributed to the hydrophobic surface of AMH doped PEDOT:PSS, which prevents the perovskite layer out of water and impurities diffused from HTL and electrode [47], eventually slowing down the degradation rate of perovskite materials.

# 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, we develop a generally applicable approach, by doping water-soluble tungsten compounds AMH in PEDOT:PSS HTL, to significantly improve the performance and stability of planar p-i-n PVSCs. As the evaporation of bound water in AMH during the annealing process of composite HTL, the surface property of AMH doped PEDOT:PSS becomes more hydrophobic, and thus remarkably improving the perovskite crystallization upon it and decreasing the recombination of charge carrier between perovskite layer and HTL. As a result, at the doping concentration of AMH is 5 vol %, we obtain a 31% improvement in the device average PCE from 11.14% to 14.61% with negligible hysteresis. In addition, the hydrophobic surface of composite HTL could protect the perovskite layer among the moisture ambient, polishing up the PVSCs stability. Our study implies that AMH doped PEDOT:PSS has potential to be an excellent HTL and a step toward achieving highly efficient and long stability PVSCs.
