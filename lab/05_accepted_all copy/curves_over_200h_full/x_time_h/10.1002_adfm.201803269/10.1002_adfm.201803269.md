# Precursor Engineering for All-Inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   Perovskite Solar Cells with 14.78% Efficiency

Guannan Yin, Huan Zhao, Hong Jiang, Shihao Yuan, Tianqi Niu, Kui Zhao, Zhike Liu,* and Shengzhong (Frank) Liu*

The optoelectronic properties of perovskite films are closely related to the film quality, so depositing dense, uniform, and stable perovskite films is crucial for fabricating high-performance perovskite solar cells (PSCs).  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  perovskite, prized for its superb stability toward light soaking and thermal aging, has received a great deal of attention recently. However, the air instability and poor performance of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs are hindering its further progress. Here, an approach is reported for depositing high-quality  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  films via the Lewis base adducts  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2(\mathrm{DMSO}) $  and  $ \mathrm{PbBr}_2(\mathrm{DMSO}) $  as precursors to slow the crystallization of the perovskite film. This process produces  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  films with large-scale crystalline grains, flat surfaces, low defects, and long carrier lifetimes. More interestingly,  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2(\mathrm{DMSO}) $  and  $ \mathrm{PbBr}_2(\mathrm{DMSO}) $  adducts could significantly improve the stability of  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  films in air. Using films prepared by this technique, a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 14.78\% $  is obtained in  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PSCs, which is the highest PCE value reported for  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $ -based PSCs to date. In addition, the PSCs based on DMSO adducts show an extended operational lifetime in air. These excellent performances indicate that preparing high-quality inorganic perovskite films by using DMSO adducts will be a potential method for improving the performance of other inorganic PSCs.

In particular, it is generally accepted that obtaining high-quality perovskite films is a prerequisite to making high-efficiency PSCs. Therefore, various fabrication methodologies, such as one-step-coating deposition,  $  ^{[6]}  $   sequential deposition,  $  ^{[7]}  $

spray-assisted solution-process,  $  ^{[8]}  $   and vacuum evaporation,  $  ^{[9]}  $   have been developed to produce high-quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films with flat surfaces and large grains by controlling the crystallization processes of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films. Among these methodologies, the one-step-coating deposition method is simple and solution-processed. However, according to the literature, it is still challenging to deposit a thick, uniform, and stable   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film with a low defect density and long carrier lifetime by a one-step solution-based process.  $  ^{[10,11]}  $   Therefore, a simple, low-cost, and one-step method for preparing high-quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films is highly desired for the further development of inorganic PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Recently, cesium-based trihalide perovskites (CsMX $ _3 $ , M = Pb or Sn; X = I, Br, Cl, or mixed halides) as a class of inorganic perovskites have drawn considerable attention owing to their superb compositional and thermal stability as well as their promise for use in perovskite solar cells (PSCs), perovskite

The Lewis acid-base adduct approach is an effective method for controlling the quality of perovskite film. It has been demonstrated that the existence of Lewis bases, such as dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO), in the annealing process could significantly promote perovskite grain growth.  $  ^{[12]}  $   Previously reported works have also demonstrated that DMSO can coordinate with   $  PbI_{2}  $   to form an intermediate phase   $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)  $   for retarding the rapid reaction between   $  PbI_{2}  $   and organic iodide (MAI, FAI) in the solution, which is beneficial for

based-tandem solar cells and other optoelectronic devices.  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   The optical properties and air stability of   $  CsMX_{3}  $   are varied.   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   exhibits good stability but with a large band gap (  $  \approx2.3~eV  $  ),   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   displays a relatively narrow bandgap (  $  \approx1.7~eV  $  ) but suffers from the notable phase instability, while   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   is a novel inorganic halide perovskite with a mid-range bandgap (  $  \approx1.9~eV  $  ) and the highest record efficiency among the all Cs-based inorganic solar cells, and it can operate even in the ambient atmosphere.  $  ^{[4]}  $   However, the efficiencies of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cells achieved to date are still low, principally as the result of poor   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film quality, which induces a low short circuit current and poor stability.  $  ^{[5]}  $   Therefore, it is very desirable to control the crystallization process of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   for dense, uniform, and stable perovskite films.

At high temperature, the seed crystals in the DMSO-adducted film have lower growth rate than those in the control film due to the lower activation energy of the grain boundaries in the DMSO-adducted precursors.[17] This allowed more time for crystal growth because more precursors are available as a resource for growth. In the vertical direction of films prepared with DMSO adducts, gaps between the grains are filled and single-crystalline grains span the entire film thickness, which is due to the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  grain size exceeding the film thickness. On the one hand, charge transport is believed to be more efficient in such a film than in a film with small crystals because this structure enables photogenerated carriers to be transported freely through the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film in the vertical direction without the interruption of grain boundaries, where many defects are located.[18] On the other hand, the high-quality and compact perovskite film can prevent moisture from penetrating into the interior of the perovskite layer, and thus the decomposition of the perovskite material is suppressed and the device stability is improved.

The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films have absorption onset at   $  \approx650~nm  $   (1.9 eV), which is consistent with the reported results.  $  ^{[9,19]}  $   The thicknesses of the two kinds of films are carefully controlled to be same (Figure 3c,d). To quantitatively analyze the influence of DMSO adducts on the trap density of a   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, capacitor-like devices that sandwich the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films between FTO/  $  TiO_{2}  $   and PCBM/Ag are fabricated and are characterized by the evolution of the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) at different biases (Figure 5b). The current density–voltage (J-V) curves rise sharply at the trap-filled limit (TFL) voltage, where all of the trap defects are occupied by electrons. The trap density is calculated according to the equation   $  N_{trap} = 2\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0} V_{TFL}/ed^{2}  $  ,  $  ^{[20]}  $   where   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   and   $  \varepsilon  $   are the vacuum permittivity and the dielectric constant of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, respectively, d is the thickness of the prepared   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, and e is the elementary charge. The trap densities   $  N_{trap}  $   are calculated to be   $  3.64 \times 10^{15}  $   and   $  1.94 \times 10^{16} ~cm^{-3}  $   for the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films with and without DMSO adducts, respectively. The defect density is substantially decreased in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film with DMSO adducts, which can be attributed to its high crystallinity obtained through the slow growth process.

To investigate the influence of DMSO adducts on photogenerated charge carriers in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films, the steady-state

photoluminescence (PL) spectra are recorded (Figure 5c). The PL intensity of the perovskite film prepared with DMSO adducts is much higher than that of the control film, suggesting a significantly reduced nonradiative recombination rate within the perovskite film. Moreover, a small blue shift can be observed for the PL peak of the sample with DMSO adducts (Figure 5c). This implies a lower trap density of the film near the band-edge, therefore, reduced recombination loss and improved photocurrent collection in the film with DMSO adducts can be expected. The PL decay lifetimes of the films are determined by time-resolved PL (TRPL) measurements (Figure 5d; Table S1, Supporting Information). The decay curves can be well fitted with exponential components. The  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film with DMSO adducts shows a much longer lifetime (11.6 ns) than that of the control film (1.7 ns). The prolonged PL decay time of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film is the result of suppressed nonradiative recombination channels in the perovskite film.[21] In summary, compared to the control film, the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film with DMSO adducts results in higher photoluminescence intensity and longer PL lifetimes, indicating that the improved crystalline quality can significantly reduce the defect density and nonradiative recombination and prolong the lifetimes of carriers.

Furthermore, under open-circuit conditions, the relationship between the open circuit voltage and the light intensity is utilized to elucidate the recombination mechanism within the inorganic PSCs. By plotting   $  V_{oc}  $   as a function of incident light intensity (I), as shown in Figure 6d, the recombination mechanism can be understood through applying the equation   $  V_{\lambda} = V_{s} + (nk_{B}T/q)\ln(P_{\lambda}/P_{S})^{[2,28]}  $   where   $  V_{\lambda}  $   and   $  V_{S}  $   are the open-circuit voltages at light intensity   $  P_{\lambda}  $   and at the standard light intensity   $  P_{s}  $  , respectively. n,   $  k_{B}  $  , T, and q are the ideality factor, Boltzmann constant, absolute temperature, and elementary

charge, respectively. Clearly, the   $  V_{oc}  $   of the DSMO-adducted device at various light intensities is higher than that of the control device, implying a mitigated voltage loss in the DSMO-adducted device. The fitted lines show the slopes (n) of 2.23 for the control device and 1.16 for the DSMO-adducted device. The reduced slope of the DSMO-adducted device approaches unity and affirms the suppressed Shockley–Read–Hall recombination, which agrees with the PL results (Figure 5c).

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, a high-quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film formation methodology is developed herein. The use of the DMSO adducts (  $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}(DMSO)  $  ) can dramatically influence the quality of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films. It is found that the

DMSO adducts can impede   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   crystallization, resulting in the formation of large-grain films with low defect densities, long carrier lifetimes, and high stabilities. After employing the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films prepared with DMSO adducts, inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   PSCs exhibit a high open-circuit voltage of 1.22 V and a remarkable PCE up to 14.78%, which is record-breaking among the existing   $  CsMX_{3}  $  -based PSCs. Moreover,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   solar cells based on DMSO adducts exhibited tremendous improvement in air stability in comparison to the control devices without DMSO adducts. This study demonstrates a feasible way to design high-quality inorganic perovskite films through employing DMSO adducts for high-performance all-inorganic PSCs, and this method may also be beneficial to the fabrication of other optoelectronic devices based on all-inorganic perovskites, such as light-emitting diodes, photodetectors, and lasers.

# 4. Experimental Section

Preparation of   $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}(DMSO)  $  : 5 g   $  PbI_{2}  $   or   $  PbBr_{2}  $   was dissolved in 15 mL dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) with heating and stirring at 60 °C. Then 100 mL toluene was slowly added into the   $  PbI_{2}  $   or   $  PbBr_{2}  $   solution, and a white precipitate was formed. After the precipitate was completely produced, it was filtered and then dried in a vacuum oven for 24 h to yield white   $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)_{2}  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}(DMSO)_{2}  $   adducts. The   $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}(DMSO)  $   powders were obtained by annealing   $  PbI_{2}(DMSO)_{2}  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}(DMSO)_{2}  $ , respectively, in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 24 h.

Device Fabrication: The planar inorganic PSCs were fabricated with the structure FTO/TiO $ _2 $ /CsPbI $ _2 $ Br/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au. FTO-based glass substrate was cleaned sequentially with water, acetone, isopropanol alcohol, and ethanol and then treated in an ultrasonic bath for 10 min. Then the substrates were treated under UV-Ozone for 10 min to form a hydrophilic surface. TiO $ _2 $  layers were prepared by a chemical bath deposition method according to this previously published works. The FTO/Glass substrates were placed in a glass container filled with a solution of 4.5 mL titanium chloride and 200 mL ultrapure water in a 70 °C lab oven for 1 h to deposit the TiO $ _2 $  layers. The substrates were rinsed with deionized water to clean them and annealed for 30 min at 200 °C on a hotplate. After cooling to room temperature, the TiO $ _2 $ /FTO/glass substrates were treated by UV-Ozone treatment for 20 min. CsPbI $ _2 $ Br perovskite layers were formed by spin-coating the perovskite solutions with and without DMSO adducts at 1000 rpm for 10 s and then at 4000 rpm for 45 s while dripping 150 mL chlorobenzene onto the substrate between 14 and 16 s into the second spinning step. To prepare a CsPbI $ _2 $ Br inorganic perovskite solution with DMSO adducts, 0.2338 g CsI (99.99%, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.), 0.2426 g PbI $ _2 $ (DMSO), and 0.2003 g PbBr $ _2 $ (DMSO) were dissolved into 1 mL mixed solvent of N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF) and dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) (4:1, v/v) to form a 0.9 M solution. The CsPbI $ _2 $ Br inorganic perovskite solution without DMSO adducts was dissolved with 0.2338 g CsI (99.99%, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.), 0.2075 g PbI $ _2 $  (99.99%, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.), and 0.1652 g PbBr $ _2 $  (99.99%, Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.) into 1 mL mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO (4:1, v/v) to prepare a 0.9 M solution. After the spin coating, the films were heated on a hotplate at 200 °C for 10 min. To prepare a Spiro-OMeTAD solution, 90 mg of 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis(N,N-di-pmethoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene was dissolved in 1 mL of chlorobenzene and mixed with 36 μL 4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP) and 22 μL Li-TFSI solution (bis-trifluoromethane sulfonamide lithium salt dissolved in acetonitrile, 520 mg ml $ ^{-1} $ ). It was spin-coated onto brown perovskite film at 5000 rpm for 30 s to form an hole transport layer. Finally, an 80 nm thick gold layer was thermally evaporated as a top electrode using a shadow mask to form a device active area of 9, 49, or 100 mm $ ^{2} $ . All the fabrication steps for spin-coating inorganic perovskite films and Spiro-OMeTAD films were performed in a glove box.

Device Characterization: Surface morphologies and cross-sectional images of inorganic perovskite films were characterized by field-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM, HITACHI, SU-8020). Surface roughnesses of the inorganic perovskite films were characterized by an atomic force microscope (AFM, Bruker Dimension 5000 Scanning Probe Microscope). Thermogravimetric (TG) analyses of the prepared PbI $ _{2} $ (DMSO) and PbBr $ _{2} $ (DMSO) complex powders were performed using a TA Instruments SDT Q600 (2 °C min $ ^{-1} $  from room temperature to 100 °C) under nitrogen atmosphere. X-ray diffraction (XRD) spectra were obtained using a D/MAX 2400 diffractometer with Cu Ka radiation (Rigaku). FTIR spectra were measured with a Bruker Vertex 70. Photoluminescence (PL) (excitation at 532 nm) spectra were measured using a FLS980 spectrometer (Edinburgh Instruments Ltd). Time resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra were measured with a PicoQuant FluoQuant 300. Water contact angles were obtained using a Dataphysics OCA 20. The transmittance spectra of inorganic perovskite films were measured by a UV/Vis NIR spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer, Lambda 950). The current density–voltage ( $ J-V $ ) characteristics of PSCs were collected (2400 Series SourceMeter, Keithley Instruments) under AM 1.5 illumination at 100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $  irradiance generated by a SAN-EI ELECTRIC XES-40S2-CE solar simulator, with the intensity calibrated using a NREL-traceable KG5 filtered silicon reference cell. Devices were tested in both the forward and reverse scan directions at a scan rate of 0.03 V s $ ^{-1} $ .
