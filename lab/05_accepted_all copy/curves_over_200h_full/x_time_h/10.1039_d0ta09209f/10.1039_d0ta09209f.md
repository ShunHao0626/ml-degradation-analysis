# Compositional Optimization of 2D-3D Heterojunction Interface for 22.6% Efficient and Stable Planar Perovskite Solar Cells

Maosheng He  $  ^{a}  $  , Jianghu Liang  $  ^{a}  $  , Zhanfei Zhang  $  ^{a}  $  , Yuankun Qiu  $  ^{a}  $  , Zihao Deng  $  ^{a}  $  , Heng Xu  $  ^{a}  $  , Jianli Wang  $  ^{a}  $  , Yajuan Yang  $  ^{a}  $  , Zhenhua Chen  $  ^{b}  $  , and Chun-Chao Chen  $  ^{*a}  $

The stacking of 2D perovskites on the top of 3D perovskites has been recognized as a promising interfacial treatment approach to improve the stability and efficiency of planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, traditional 2D-3D perovskite heterojunctions obtained from high-temperature annealing process still possess unsatisfactory charge transfer performance and interfacial voltage loss. Herein, we introduced isopentylammonium iodide (PNAI) as the large organic ammonium salt, and adjusted the in-situ grown processes of 2D perovskites by thermal treatments to form a multi-component capping layer composed of 2D perovskites with lots of high n-value 2D phases (n ≥ 3, n is the number of inorganic layers) and residual PNAI molecules on 3D perovskites. Such an optimized composition for 2D-3D perovskite heterojunction can remarkably improve the charge transfer performance, further suppress the interfacial ionic defects, and enlarge Fermi-level splitting, leading to a low bandgap-to-voltage loss (0.38 V). Consequently, this treatment strategy significantly improves the efficiency of planar PSCs to 22.62% with an outstanding open-circuit voltage of 1.16 V. Moreover, the unencapsulated PNAI-90 treated devices stored under a relative humidity of 30±5% for 1000 h still retain 89% of its initial PCE. This work offers a new strategy to construct a robust 2D-3D heterojunction for planar PSCs.

# Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites have been one of the most impressive photovoltaic materials due to their high carrier mobility, high absorption coefficient, long carrier lifetime, and adjustable bandgap.  $  ^{1-5}  $   Since first reported by Miyasaka in 2009, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of solution-processed PSCs has rapidly developed from 3.8%  $  ^{6}  $   to 25.2%  $  ^{7}  $   by optimizing perovskite composition,  $  ^{8}  $   device architectures,  $  ^{9,10}  $   and perovskite interface,  $  ^{11-14}  $   etc. In these studies, planar PSCs obtained at low-temperature have attracted considerable attention, benefiting from their low-cost and the application potential in flexible electronic devices.  $  ^{15-17}  $   However, the perovskite film of planar PSCs prepared by the two-step-solution method is rough and presents many distinctive grain boundaries. Those grain boundaries contain lots of defects, such as the dangling bonds generated by surface discontinuous crystal structures and under-coordinated Pb  $  ^{2+}  $  , resulting in the non-radiative recombination of carriers and poor contact with the hole transport layer.  $  ^{18,19}  $   The labile perovskite surface not only decreases the device PCE but also deteriorate the device stability.  $  ^{20,21}  $   Therefore, the interfacial treatment of perovskite film is indispensable to obtain high efficiency and stable planar PSCs.

Fortunately, various materials have been successfully employed in the interfacial engineering of perovskite, such as   $  PbI_{2}^{22}  $  , inorganic oxide materials  $  ^{23}  $  , polymer materials  $  ^{24}  $   and large organic ammonium salts  $  ^{11}  $  , etc. Among them, large organic ammonium salts (the molecule volume of which are bigger than   $  \mathrm{HC(NH_{2})_{2}^{+}}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}^{-}  $  ) have attracted tremendous attention due to their outstanding passivation performance.  $  ^{25-27}  $   Concretely, those organic ammonium salts, such as phenethylammonium iodide  $  ^{28-30}  $   and octylammonium iodide  $  ^{31, 32}  $  , etc. can passivate the perovskite surface through two different ways. For the first one, organic ammonium salts can be directly used as passivator. The Lewis base functional groups (  $  -NH_{2}  $  ) of these small molecules can suppress the defects and eliminate the charge traps through coordinating with   $  Pb^{2+}  $   by lone pair electrons.  $  ^{20, 21, 33}  $   For the other one, organic ammonium salts can react with the excess lead iodide on 3D perovskite surface at high temperatures to transform into 2D perovskites. Benefiting from the inherent high stability of 2D perovskite, the device stability will be significantly improved.  $  ^{34}  $

Furthermore, the wide-bandgap 2D perovskites and the underlying 3D perovskites can form a 2D-3D perovskite heterojunction, which not only improves the perovskite film stability but also adjust the energy band alignment for high-performance PSCs.  $  ^{35, 36}  $   Generally, 2D perovskites are formulated as   $  (R_{2}A_{n-1}M_{n}X_{3n+1}, n\geq1)  $  , where R is an additional large organic ammonium cation and n represents the number of   $  [MX_{6}]^{4}  $  -inorganic layers.  $  ^{31}  $   However, as the large organic cations are electrically insulating, charge transfer in 2D perovskites is limited by long-chain organic ligands, which hinders the charge transfer from 2D-3D heterojunction to the hole transport layer.  $  ^{37}  $   To solve this issue, an effective carrier transport pathway can be created by increasing the

# Fabrication and Characterization of the Perovskite Film

The 3D perovskite film was deposited through a two-step spin-coating method. Moreover,  $ \mathrm{KI}^8 $  was introduced into the triple-cation  $ (\mathrm{CsPbI}_3)_x(\mathrm{FAPbI}_3)_y(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_3)_{1 - x - y} $  (FA:  $ \mathrm{HC(NH_2)_2} $ , MA:  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3 $ ) perovskite precursor to increase the perovskite crystallinity. # Device Fabrication and Characterization

After investigating the composition and surface morphology of optimized 2D-3D heterojunction, we fabricate solar cells to evaluate the photovoltaic performance of the devices based on PNAI-90 treatment. The architecture of devices is FTO/SnO  $  _{2}  $  / Perovskite/with or without interfacial treatment/Spiro-OMeTAD/Ag, as shown in Figure 4a. The current density-voltage (J-V) curves of PSCs treated by different interfacial strategies are presented in Figure 4b, and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters are shown in Table 1. Compared with the best PCE of 20.26% for the control device, the PCE of devices treated by PNAI-20, PNAI-90, and PNAI-120 increases to 22.00%, 22.62%, and 21.46%, respectively. In particular, the V  $  _{oc}  $  of PNAI-90 treated devices are improved significantly from 1.08 V (control) to 1.16 V, leading to a low voltage loss (E  $  _{abs}  $  /q – V  $  _{oc}  $  ) of 0.38 V, equal with that of crystal silicon solar cells.  $  ^{49}  $  Besides, the FF of PNAI-90 treated device is increased from 78.99% to 81.87% due to the improved surface contact. Furthermore, the J  $  _{sc}  $  of the PNAI-90 treated device is also slightly higher than that of control, PNAI-20 treated, and PNAI-120 treated device. In order to investigate the versatility of optimized composition strategy, we also fabricated devices based on butylammonium iodide (BAI) and phenethylammonium iodide (PEAI) through the same procedure, and the best PCE results are also obtained after 90 °C thermal treatment. # Device Fabrication.

FTO glass substrates were cleaned ultrasonically in ethyl alcohol, detergent, deionized water, acetone, and isopropyl alcohol each for 15 min, sequentially. Prior to use, The FTO substrates were treated with UV-ozone for 25 min. Then, the diluted SnO₂ colloid precursor (2.67%, in water) was spin-coated onto FTO substrates at 3000 r.p.m. for 30 s and annealed on a hot plate at 150 °C for 30 min. After cooling to room temperature, the FTO/SnO₂ substrates were treated with UV ozone again for 25 min to improve the wettability. Afterwards, the perovskite layer was prepared through a two-step method. In the first step, 600 mg PbI₂, 30 mg CsI, 15 mg KI, and 6.4 mg MAI in 1 ml DMF/DMSO (9:1) solution was spin-coated on the SnO₂ substrate at 1500 r.p.m. for 30 s, then the samples were annealed at 70 °C for 1 min. In the second step, a solution of FAI (60 mg), MABr (6 mg) and MACl (6mg) in IPA (1 ml) was spin-coated onto the PbI₂ film at 1500 r.p.m. for 30 s, followed by being annealed at 150 °C for 20 min in ambient air conditions (25%-30% humidity). So far, perovskite film was synthesized. After that, the samples were moved into a N₂-filled glovebox for PNAI treatment. PNAI (20 mM) solution was dissolved in IPA and spin-coated onto the perovskite surface at 5000 r.p.m. for 30 s at different annealing temperatures from 20 °C to 120 °C. Then, Spiro-OMeTAD (72.3 mg ml⁻¹ in chlorobenzene) with the addition of Li-TFSI (54 μl, 170 mg/ml in acetonitrile) and tBP (30 μl) was spin-coated at 3,000 r.p.m. for 30 s. Finally, Ag back electrodes (100 nm) were deposited by thermal evaporation. The initial active area was 0.18 cm². When measuring, the accurate active area was 0.1 cm² defined by a non-reflective mask.

# Characterization.

The current density–voltage (J–V) curves of the devices (unencapsulated) were measured under AM1.5G illumination at 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  (calibrated by a standard Si solar cell) using an Abet Technologies Sun 2000 solar simulator and a Keithley 2400 source meter. The devices were measured by reverse scan (1.2 V→0.1 V, step 20 mV) and forward scan (-0.1 V→1.2 V, step 20 mV) in ambient air (30% RH). External quantum efficiency (EQE) measurements were observed on the QTEST HIFINITY 5 EQE system (the light intensity was calibrated with Si detectors). X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were taken on a D8 Advance X-ray diffractometer with Cu K  $  _{\alpha}  $  radiation as the X-ray source at a scan rate of 6° min  $  ^{-1}  $ . The grazing incidence wide angle x-ray scattering (GIWAXS) spectra were obtained at beamline BL16B1 in the Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF) and the incidence angle of the X-ray beam was 0.3°. The normal incidence of Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF) 16B is about 400 microns, and the grazing incidence spot can reach the centimeter-level (grazing incidence angle of 0.3°, the spot along the optical path can be elongated to 400/sin (0.3°) = 7.6 cm, with a spot area around 400 μm * 7.6 cm). Thus, the surface effect is magnified. Field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) images were

acquired on a TESCA MIRA3 FE-SEM. Atomic force microscopy (AFM) images were observed on a Bruker Bio-FastScan AFM, UV-Vis absorption spectra were acquired on a Lambda 35 UV-Vis spectrometer. Steady state photoluminescence (PL) spectra and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra were taken on a FLS1000 photoluminescence spectrometer with the excitation at 470 nm. X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS) were acquired on an AXIS Ultra DLD x-ray photoelectron spectrometer and calibrated based on C 1s peak (284.8eV). Ultraviolet photoelectron spectra (UPS) were measured using a Thermo Fisher ESCALAB 250Xi machine. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) spectra were observed on a Chenhua CHI660E electrochemical workstation under dark condition. Confocal laser scanning microscope (CLSM) images were measured on a Leica TCS SP8 STED 3X instrument in ambient air conditions. The water contact angle images were taken on a DSA100 contact angle analysis instrument.

# Conclusions

In conclusion, we have introduced PNAI as the large organic ammonium salt to fabricate an optimized composition of 2D-3D heterojunction with much content of high n-value 2D phases in 2D perovskites and a small amount of residual PNAI molecules through a simple thermal treatment procedure. In the optimized 2D-3D heterojunction, high n-value 2D phases can enhance the charge transfer performance of 2D capping layer and a small amount of residual PNAI molecules can be regarded as defect passivators to reduce nonradiative recombination by further suppressing the interfacial ion defects in the layers of 2D and 3D perovskites. The optimized composition for 2D-3D perovskite heterojunction can also induce an enlarged Fermi-level splitting followed by an improvement in the  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  of solar cell. As a result, the optimized heterojunction increased the  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  from 1.08 V to 1.16 V, and the FF from 78.99% to 81.87%. Furthermore, the optimized 2D-3D heterojunction can significantly increase the moisture stability and photo stability of perovskite films and devices. In short, the optimized compositional strategy for 2D-3D heterojunction provides a simple and effective method for obtaining highly efficient and stable planar perovskite solar cells.
