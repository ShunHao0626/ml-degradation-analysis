# GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT

A fullerene-based molecular passivator, namely   $  C_{60}-tBu-I  $  , has been firstly developed as interfacial layers between a perovskite and electron transport layers in planar invert perovskite solar cells (PVSCs), which exhibit high efficiency up to 17.75% with negligible hysteresis and good device stability.

# A B S T R A C T

Interfacial defects result in a limitation to the development of highly efficient and stable perovskite solar cells. The passivation of these defects by adopting various interfacial defects passivation agents is a common method for boosting device performance. However, most existing interfacial defects passivation agents form poorly conductive aggregates at the perovskite interface with the electron transport layer (ETL), hindering the transport of charge carriers. In addition, the electron mobility of passivation agents is an important factor that affects the electron communication between the adjacent layers. Herein, a fullerene-based molecular passivator, [60]fullerene-4-(1-(4-(tert-butyl)phenyl)pyrrolidin-2-yl)benzena minium  $ (C_{60} - t\mathrm{Bu - I}) $ , is designed and synthesized. This novel  $ n $ -doping fullerene ammonium iodide is developed as an interfacial modification agent to accelerate charge transport from the perovskite active layer into the ETL while hindering the nonradiative charge carrier recombination. Hence, compared with the control devices  $ (15.66\%) $ ,  $ C_{60} - t\mathrm{Bu - I} $ -modified device presents a higher efficiency of  $ 17.75\% $ . More

# 1. Introduction

Perovskite has promising application prospects in photovoltaic devices due to its fantastic photoelectric properties, and the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has received rapid progress[1-6]. However, the organic components of perovskite can easily evaporate from the surface during the thermal annealing process. Thus, the surface of perovskite films always exhibits numerous positively and negatively charged surface defects, exerting a strong negative effect on the charge extraction and stability of devices[7]. Defect passivation is an effective approach for improving the efficiency and enhancing the durability of perovskite solar cells (PSC). To date, surface modification by coating the defect passivation layer at the perovskite surface is the most concise and efficient methods for controlling defect density[8-11].

Organic salts are particularly effective passivation agents because of their high solubility in normal organic polar solvents and their capability to tune compositions by alerting negatively or positively charged components. Yang et al used toluenesulfonate as an effective passivation agent on the top of perovskite film. Their work indicated that this novel passivation agent can effectively passivate the negatively and positively charged defects of CsMAFA perovskite[6]. A recent work of Lan et al. determined that iodobenzene diacetate can mitigate defects and improve charge carrier transport at the interface, increasing the PCE and stability of devices[12]. Jiang et al first reported the application of phenylethylammonium iodide (PEAI) as a surface passivation agent and they obtained a high PCE value of  $ 23.3\% $  [13]. To date, PEAI is widely used as a passivation agent in PSCs. To further enhance the hydrophobicity of PEAI and reduce the formation of poorly conductive aggregates at the perovskite interface, Grätzel et al. introduced the 4-tert-butyl-benzylammonium iodide (tBBAI) as a surface passivation layer on the top of perovskite films. Their study indicated that the introduction of bulky tert-butyl groups prevents the molecular aggregation via steric repulsion[14]. Although the aforementioned organic passivation agents exert a positive effect on defect passivation and increase long-term stability, the mobility of the materials is typically disregarded and low mobility apparently introduces severe energy disorder that reduces device performance. Fullerene and their derivatives have been widely applied in inverted PSCs due to their low-temperature solution fabrication, good electron mobility, well-matched energy levels, and effective defect passivation.

Here, we designed and synthesized a simple and low-cost fullerene tert-butyl phenylamine salt, namely,   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I, and applied it as a surface passivation agent to modify surface morphology, reduce energy level barrier and trap state, and promote charge communication between the active layer and the electron transport layer (ETL). The I  $  ^{-}  $  anion and   $  NH_{3}^{+}  $  cation of   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I were found to interact with the   $  Pb^{2+}  $   and   $  I^{-}  $  ions of perovskite, respectively, resulting in the surface passivation of perovskite. As a result, the optimized inverted PSCs presents simultaneous improvement in overall device parameters, significantly increasing PCE from 15.66% (without   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I) to 17.75% (with   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I). Moreover, the hydrophobicity of perovskite was significantly improved due to the introduction of bulk tert-butyl, and the corresponding device ambient stability was evidently enhanced. The aforementioned

research results demonstrated that the molecular aggregates of the interlayer can be effectively reduced by rationally tailoring the molecular structure.

# 2. Results and discussion

# 2.1. Synthesis of conductive fullerene interlayers

The synthesis route and chemical structure of   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I are presented in Scheme 1. Synthesis can be easily achieved via the Prato reaction with a satisfied yield (  $  \sim64\%  $  ). Scheme S1 of Supporting Information indicates the detailed synthesis procedure. The molecular structure of the final product   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I was clearly confirmed by matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectroscopy,   $  {}^{1}  $  H-nuclear magnetic resonance (  $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR) and   $  {}^{13}  $  C NMR spectroscopy (Figures S1-6 of Supporting Information). The   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I presents a high solubility (at >10 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) in trifluoroethanol (TFE), allows for solution-processed PSCs. As shown in Figure S7, C60-tBu-I possesses high decomposition temperature (186 °C), which indicates   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I presents superb thermal stability and enables to tolerate strict operating conditions.

# 2.2. Optical and electrochemical properties

The UV-Vis spectra of  $  C_{60}  $ -tBu-I was measured in o-dichlorobenzene. The optical bandgap was then estimated at 1.7 eV from the absorption boundary ( $  \lambda_{\text{abs}} = 728  $ ) of  $  C_{60}  $ -tBu-I with the common used equation of  $  Eg = \frac{1240}{c}  $  (Fig. 1a). The electrochemical properties of the  $  C_{60}  $ -tBu-I were determined via cyclic voltammetry (Fig. 1b). The lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) levels of  $  C_{60}  $ -tBu-I can be estimated at -4.0 eV from the onset reduction potential (-0.38 V) in the cyclic voltammetry from the widely used equation of  $  E_{\text{LUMO}} = -[E_{\text{red}}^{1/2} + 4.4]  $  eV, which indicated that  $  C_{60}  $ -tBu-I exhibits similar LUMO levels with the conduction band of MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite (-3.9 eV). The well-matched energy levels can promote efficient electron communication and decrease the energy level barrier between perovskite and ETL. Furthermore, deeper highest occupied molecular orbital levels (-5.7 eV) can be easily obtained from optical bandgaps (Fig. 1c), suggesting more effective blocking holes.

The electron transfers between halides atoms and adjacent fullerene sphere caused the intramolecular n-doping and stimulates higher conductivity in fullerene films. The occurrence of intramolecular n-doping can be verified through the formation of radical signals from the reported literature[15-17]. As a result, we conducted the electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopy to manifest the existence of intramolecular n-doping in  $ C_{60} $ -tBu-I[18,19]. As indicated in Fig. 1d, an obvious radical signal intensity could be detected in  $ C_{60} $ -tBu-I. The conductivity of  $ C_{60} $ -tBu-I will be inherently improved by the formation of these anions. Consequently, more efficient charge extraction at the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3/ $  PCBM interface can be realized due to the improved conductivity of  $ C_{60} $ -tBu-I.

# 2.4. Solar cell performance characterization

A normal configuration with and without   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I passivation was constructed to investigate the effect of using   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I as an interlayer on device performance (Fig. 3a). The passivation agent   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I was applied via spin coating onto the perovskite surface with 0.3 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   solution in TFE without further annealing (Supporting Information Table S1). The device without   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I passivation presents inferior performance with an optimal PCE of 15.66%. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, we developed a new surface passivation agent, namely,  $  C_{60}  $ -tBu-I. The introduction of  $  C_{60}  $ -tBu-I can reduce nonradiative charge carrier recombination and defect density, achieving a significantly improved charge extraction from the perovskite film to the ETL. Electro-optical characterizations showed that this novel fullerene-based passivation agent can efficiently passivate positively and negatively charged surface defects on perovskite

Moreover,   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I-modified device exhibited enhanced environmental stability compared with bare cells due to the hydrophobicity of   $  C_{60}  $  -tBu-I. Our work indicated that the performance and stability of PSCs can be further improved via rational molecular engineering. The application of a fullerene-based passivation agent can help further explore the novel surface modification material of inverted PSCs.
