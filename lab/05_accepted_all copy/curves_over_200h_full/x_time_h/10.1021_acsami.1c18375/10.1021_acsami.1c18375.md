# All-Inorganic Perovskite Solar Cells with Tetrabutylammonium Acetate as the Buffer Layer between the   $  SnO_{2}  $   Electron Transport Film and   $  CsPbI_{3}  $

Hang Zhong,  $  ^{II}  $   Wenbo Li,  $  ^{II}  $   Yin Huang, Duoling Cao, Congqiang Zhang, Huaxi Bao, Zhiguang Guo, Li Wan,  $  ^{*}  $   Xu Zhang,  $  ^{*}  $   Xiuhua Zhang, Yuebin Li, Xiaoming Ren, Xianbao Wang, Dominik Eder, Kai Wang, Shengzhong Frank Liu, and Shimin Wang

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: All-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskites have great potential in tandem cells in combination with other photovoltaic devices. However,   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells (PSCs) still face a huge challenge, resulting in a low power conversion efficiency (PCE) relative to organic–inorganic PSCs. In this work, we introduced tetrabutylammonium acetate (TBAAc) as a buffer layer between the   $  SnO_{2}  $   electron-transport layer (ETL) and   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   all-inorganic perovskite film interface for the first time. TBAAc not only improved the conductivity of   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL but also formed a 1D   $  TBAPbI_{3}  $   layer between the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL and the 3D   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   all-inorganic perovskite film, thereby enhancing the stability and

passivating the surface defects of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite to fabricate high-efficiency carbon-counter electrode (CE)-based   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   solar cells. We fabricated carbon-CE-based hole-transporting layer (HTL)-free PSCs with an FTO/  $  SnO_{2}  $  /TBAAc/CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  /C structure. The open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ), short circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ), PCE, and fill factor of the champion   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs simultaneously enhanced to 1.08 V, 17.48 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $  , 12.79, and 67.8%, respectively. This PCE is currently one of the high efficiencies reported for the above planar-structured carbon-CE-based   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs to date. Moreover, the optimized device exhibits excellent stability, which retained over 83% of its initial PCE after 350 h. This work provides a facile way of simultaneous optimization of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL and the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite layer to fabricate stable and high-efficiency carbon-CE-based   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs.

KEYWORDS: inorganic perovskite,   $  CsPbI_{3}  $  , carbon counter electrode, interface engineering, stability

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Organic–inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have experienced an unprecedented rapid development in the power conversion efficiency (PCE) that has now reached 25.5%,  $  ^{1}  $  which has approached the performance of commercial crystalline silicon solar cells.  $  ^{2}  $  However, the poor stability under thermal, humidity, and light stress conditions due to organic cations has restricted their further commercial application, although perovskite materials have many advantages, such as a suitable band gap and excellent absorption coefficients.  $  ^{3-5}  $  Recently, some researchers have found that using  $  Cs^{+}  $  to replace the organic cations can improve the Goldschmidt ideal factor, which is one of the good solutions for the problem of thermal instability.  $  ^{3}  $  Among inorganic cesium PSCs (  $  CsPbX_{3}  $  ), cubic-phase (  $  \alpha  $  -phase)  $  CsPbI_{3}  $  with a band gap of 1.73 eV  $  ^{6,7}  $  and strong visible-light absorption capabilities  $  ^{3}  $  has great potential in tandem cells in combination with other photovoltaic devices and other field of optoelectronics. However,  $  CsPbI_{3}  $  has low lattice strength, making it easy to degrade from the photoactive perovskite phase (  $  \alpha  $  -

The   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL has a wider band gap and higher electron mobility compared with the   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL. In addition, the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL could be prepared using a low-temperature method, implying that it could be better applied in flexible and tandem PSCs.  $  ^{16,17}  $   However, the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL still faces the problem of low conductivity, limiting the electron collection efficiency of PSCs.  $  ^{18,19}  $   Nonetheless, interface passivation is a useful method to improve the conductivity and electron extraction efficiency between the perovskite and ETL.  $  ^{20-22}  $   Hole-transporting layers, such as Spiro-MeOTAD and metal electrodes, are expensive, which can increase the production cost of PSCs. The migration of halide and metal ions frequently occurs between organic hole-transporting layers (HTLs) and the metal electrode, and it also has a significant influence on the device stability. Hence, the development of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs without a HTL and carbon counter electrode (CE) is very important.  $  ^{23}  $

In this study, tetrabutylammonium acetate (TBAAc) was first introduced between the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite film and the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer to enhance the electron transport of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL and modulate the perovskite crystal growth to fabricate efficient carbon CE-based all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs. Finally, a champion PCE of 12.79% was obtained relative to 10.52% of the control device. The TBAAc buffer layer suppresses heterogeneous   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite nucleation, which leads to larger grain sizes, better charge transfer, and the enhancement of the photovoltaic performance of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs. In addition, interfacial trap-assisted recombination was reduced and the charge extraction efficiency was remarkably improved.

# 2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

2.1. Materials. Cesium iodide (CsI, 99.99%) and hydrogen lead iodide (HPbI  $  _{3}  $  , 99.99%) were bought from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 99.9%), N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.9%), TBAAc (C  $  _{18}  $  H  $  _{39}  $  NO  $  _{2}  $  , 97%), SnCl  $  _{2}  $  ·2H  $  _{2}  $  O (98%), and urea (99.9%) were acquired from Shanghai Aladdin Bio-Chem Technology Co. Low-temperature conductive

carbon paste was purchased from Shanghai Maituowei Technology Co., Ltd. All chemical reagents were used without further purification.

2.2. Device Fabrication. The device structure comprises glass/FTO/SnO $ _2 $ /TBAAc/CsPbI $ _3 $ /C in this work. First, FTO conductive substrates were sequentially washed with detergent, deionized water, propanone, ethyl alcohol, and propan-2-ol and further cleaned by UV/ozone for 15 min. For the preparation of the SnO $ _2 $  colloidal solution, 0.05 M CO(NH $ _2 $ ) $ _2 $  and 0.05 M SnCl $ _2 $  $ \cdot $ 2H $ _2 $ O were mixed and stirred in 10 mL of deionized water to obtain a light-yellow colloidal solution. The prepared SnO $ _2 $  precursor solution was spin-coated on FTO glasses once and twice at 4000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at 180 °C for 20 min. 0.25, 0.5, and 1 mg of TBAAc were dissolved in 1 mL of deionized water. Then, the TBAAc solution was spin-coated on top of the SnO $ _2 $  layer at 4000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 150 °C for 20 min. 0.3254 g of CsI and 0.26 g of HPbI $ _3 $  were dissolved in a mixed solvent of DMF and DMSO at room temperature to prepare the CsPbI $ _3 $  precursor solution. Then, the CsPbI $ _3 $  solution was spin-coated on the TBAAc layer at 3500 rpm for 35 s and annealed at 165 °C for 90 min. Finally, the carbon CE was screen-printed using the carbon paste and dried at 120 °C for 15 min.

2.3. Device Characterization. In this experiment, the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were recorded by a Bruker-AXS D8 ADVANCE X-ray diffractometer with Cu Kα1 irradiation from 5° to 80° at room temperature. The ETL powder and KBr were mixed for the Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy test in the transmittance mode. The UV–vis absorption spectra were measured on a UV-3600 ultraviolet–visible spectrophotometer at the spectral test range of 300–900 nm, with a light source slit width of 5 nm and a test interval of 1 nm. A Zeiss electron microscope produced in Germany was used to obtain the film morphologies. The band energy was tested by an X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) instrument of Escalab 250Xi. The J-V characteristics of the PSCs were measured with a NEWPORT, 91,192-1000 solar simulator, and a Keithley 2400 Source Meter. A Fluo Time 300 produced in Germany was used to measure the photoluminescence (PL) spectra and time-resolved PL (TRPL) decay curves under the test parameters given as follows: room temperature, 485 nm picosecond pulsed laser, and 2 mW total output average power. The original data were analyzed using FluoFit software. # 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

In this work, TBAAc with a chemical formula of   $  C_{18}H_{39}NO_{2}  $   was introduced between the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer and the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film to modulate the perovskite crystal growth. Figure 1a shows the schematic of the fabrication process. Figure 1b shows the effect mechanism of TBAAc on the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer and   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite film. TBAAc contains the tetrabutylammonium

(TBA  $  ^{+}  $  ) cation and carboxylic acid (Figure 1c). Previously related literature studies have reported that the carboxylic acid group could react with the hydroxyl groups on the surface of the SnO  $  _{2}  $  film. Therefore, the electronic conductivity was improved and the carrier recombination between the SnO  $  _{2}  $  layer and the CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskite film was suppressed.  $  ^{24}  $  Moreover, the TBA  $  ^{+}  $  cation could be inserted into CsPbI  $  _{3}  $ , which can effectively intercalate into the [PbI  $  _{6}  $ ]  $  ^{4-}  $  octahedra, replacing part of the Cs  $  ^{+}  $  cation and inducing the formation of a one-dimensional (1D) TBAPbI  $  _{3}  $  layer between the SnO  $  _{2}  $  layer and the three-dimensional (3D) CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  layer. To explore the effects of TBAAc on   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL, the XRD patterns of   $  SnO_{2}/TBAAc  $   and pure   $  SnO_{2}  $   film were analyzed to investigate whether the TBAAc has influence on the crystallization of   $  SnO_{2}  $  . In Figure 2a, the XRD patterns have three strongest diffraction peaks at 26.5, 33.7, and 51.7°, which can be indexed to the (110), (101), and (211) crystal planes in the rutile   $  SnO_{2}  $  . Furthermore, the peak position of the XRD diffraction peaks without noticeable offset and the intensity of diffraction peak slightly increase. This notion indicates that TBAAc was not incorporated into the   $  SnO_{2}  $   lattice. However, the crystallization performance of   $  SnO_{2}  $   slightly improved, and the excess hydroxyl on the surface of   $  SnO_{2}  $   was reacted by the carboxylic acid in TBAAc.

The surface chemical bonding and elements of   $  SnO_{2}  $   with and without modification by TBAAc were also analyzed to verify whether the photoelectric properties of   $  SnO_{2}  $   were affected by TBAA. The FTIR spectra of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film with and without modification by TBAAc are shown in Figure 2b. Mainly, two peak positions shifted: peaks of C=O in TBAAc and Sn-O shifted from 1676 to 1642 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   and from 550 to 555 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , respectively.  $  ^{25,26}  $   This phenomenon indicated that the excess hydroxyls of   $  SnO_{2}  $   are reacted by the carboxylic acid in TBAAc and made the arrangement of   $  SnO_{2}  $   closer.  $  ^{27}  $   Moreover, Figure S1a,b shows the XPS spectra of O 1s peaks and Sn 3d peaks, respectively. For the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL modified by TBAAc, the binding energy of Sn 3d decreased, indicating that the negative charges around Sn atoms enhanced, and the

TBAAc was successfully combined with   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETLs. Meanwhile, the XPS spectra of the O 1s peak increased, indicating that the oxygen content in the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL increased. The structure of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs with the   $  SnO_{2}/TBAAc  $   ETL is shown in Figure 3a. The crystalline quality is critical to the photovoltaic device performance. XRD is used to investigate the crystallization and phase purity of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $ . The total XRD patterns of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite thin films with the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL modified by different concentrations of TBAAc and without TBAAc are displayed in Figure 3b. The XRD analysis shows diffraction peaks at   $  2\theta = 26.6  $   and   $  27.8^{\circ}  $   belonging to the diffraction peaks of the FTO and   $  SnO_{2}  $   film, respectively. Diffraction peaks at   $  2\theta = 14.4  $   and   $  29^{\circ}  $   were observed, which could be expressed as the (110) and (220) lattice planes of the   $  \beta-CsPbI_{3}  $   with thermal annealing at   $  165^{\circ}C  $   for 90 min and were consistent with recent reports.  $  ^{33,34}  $   The diffraction peaks at   $  9.9^{\circ}  $   assigned to the orthorhombic yellow   $  \delta  $ -phase were also observed. When the TBAAc concentration was changed from 0 mg/mL to 0.25, 0.5, and 1 mg/mL, the intensity of the   $  \delta  $ -phase diffraction peaks was significantly reduced, and the (110) and (220) lattice planes of the   $  \beta-CsPbI_{3}  $   phase were significantly enhanced. Meanwhile, the peak intensity became the strongest when the TBAAc concentration was 0.5 mg/mL, indicating the strongest crystallization. Furthermore, the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL modified by TBAAc exhibited an almost identical diffraction pattern with the control layer. These results indicated that   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL modified by TBAAc does not change the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   crystal structure. Simultaneously, Figure S2a,b shows a diffraction peak at   $  2\theta = 7.3^{\circ}  $   belonging to the diffraction peaks of the 1D TBAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite.  $  ^{14}  $

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was used to characterize the morphology of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   thin films. The top-view SEM images of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films deposited on the pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL and   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL with 0.25, 0.5, and 1 mg/mL of TBAAc are shown in Figure 3c–f, respectively. Some pinholes were observed in the control film. After TBAAc was used to modify the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL,   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   films with uniform and large grain sizes, high coverage was observed. Extraction of statistical grain size distribution from SEM results was used to clearly compare the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film grain morphology. Figure S3a–d shows the statistical grain size of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film spin-coated on the pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL and   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL with 0.25, 0.5, and 1 mg/mL of TBAAc, respectively. The average grain sizes of   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite films deposited on the pristine   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL and   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL modified by different concentrations of TBAAc were 280, 399, 478, and 472 nm, respectively. The grain sizes of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film gradually became larger after being deposited on the TBAAc-modified   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL. However, the grain sizes of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film were slightly reduced when the concentration of TBAAc exceeded 0.5 mg/mL. This result implied that the TBAAc-modified   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL can effectively increase the average grain sizes of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film. When the TBAAc concentration was 0.5 mg/mL, the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite film had a maximum average grain size, which increased from 280 to 478 nm. Therefore, the   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL modified by TBAAc

can produce a high-quality   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film. Evidence shows that fewer pinholes and a larger grain size can efficiently reduce the shunt paths of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite; thereby, the carrier recombination was reduced and the stability was improved and caused fewer trap states in grain boundaries in the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film.  $  ^{35}  $   The cross-sectional SEM images of the device also showed that the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film with   $  TBA^{+}  $  cation treatment has no noticeable pinholes and the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   grains appear well connected compared with the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film on a pure   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer (Figure 3g,h). As evidenced by UV–vis spectroscopy, modification by TBAAc can effectively enhance the light absorption capacity of the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film, as shown in Figure S4a. # 4. CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we successfully introduced TBAAc between the   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite layers and synthesized the   $  \beta  $  -phase   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite at 165 °C. Furthermore, the   $  TBA^{+}  $  cation could replace part of the   $  Cs^{+}  $  cation and form a 1D   $  TBAPbI_{3}  $   layer between the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer and the 3D   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite film. Consequently, the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite has higher phase stability. The test result analysis showed that the charge transport efficiency at the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer and   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   film interface significantly improved. Moreover, the defect density and nonradiative reorganization of the grain boundary surface remarkably decreased. We finally achieved 12.79% of the champion PCE and a steady-output PCE of 12.44%. Thus, this work demonstrated the simultaneous modification of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer and the   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   perovskite film, which is a promising way for carbon-CE-based   $  CsPbI_{3}  $   PSCs with high stability and performance.
