# A new co-solvent assisted CuSCN deposition approach for better coverage and improvement of the energy conversion efficiency of corresponding mixed halides perovskite solar cells

# Abstract

In this research, inorganic copper thiocyanate (CuSCN) hole transport layer (HTL) was applied in conventional structure of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Besides, mixed halides perovskite   $  \left(\mathrm{Cs}_{0.05}\left(\mathrm{MA}_{0.17}\mathrm{FA}_{0.83}\right)_{0.95}\mathrm{Pb}\left(\mathrm{I}_{0.83}\mathrm{Br}_{0.17}\right)_{3}\right)  $   was utilized as the light absorbing layer and deposited on FTO/compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   substrates through a one-step coating method in ambient condition. The mentioned perovskite is more stable against high temperature, high irradiation and humidity compared to commonly applied   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite. Nevertheless, the CuSCN could not be well dissolved in usual dipropyl sulfide solution and should be deposited for several times to achieve suitable thickness, this could reduce the quality of CuSCN layer and corresponding interfaces with the other layers. Here, Acetonitrile was applied as a co-solvent to increase the CuSCN concentration in mixed solvent. Consequently, the proper thickness was achieved for just one step of spin coating process. In addition, the CuSCN layer seemed smooth and pinholes free and the interfaces were well-formed. The PSCs with CuSCN HTLs prepared by usual low concentration CUSCN solution were also fabricated and characterized. The results show that the PSCs with improved CuSCN layer deposited from the acetonitrile assisted solution demonstrated a power conversion efficiency (PCE) 11%. This value was nearly close to that of the reference cell with Spiro-OMeTAD organic HTL. The corresponding stabilities were also comparable according to the carried out measurements. In addition, the PCE was increased about 45% compared to the optimized situation of the PSCs with CuSCN layer prepared from low concentration CuSCN solution.

# 1 Introduction

Organic–inorganic perovskites have vastly investigated due to their unique optoelectronic properties   $  [1, 2]  $  . These materials were successfully applied in perovskite solar cells (PSCs) because of their broad band light absorption, high absorption coefficient, direct and tunable band gap energy, low exciton binding energy and long diffusion length of the photogenerated charge carriers   $  [3–5]  $  . The crystalline structure of perovskite materials is   $  ABX_{3}  $   while A is an organic like methylammonium (MA,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}  $  ), formamidinium

$ (\mathrm{FA}, \mathrm{CH}_3(\mathrm{NH}_2)_2) $ , and CS, B is a divalent metal such as  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  or  $ \mathrm{Ge}^{2+} $  and X is a halogen anion like  $ \mathrm{Cl^-} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Br^-} $  and  $ \mathrm{I^-} $  [5-7]. In spite of the considerable characteristics of the perovskite, their degradation and decomposition under moisture and temperature have been a big challenge in recent studies [8]. In order to improve thermal and humidity stability of the perovskite layer, some combinations of cations (FA, MA and Cs) and halides (Br and I) have been utilized [9, 10]. These structures could lead to a higher quality, better tunable band gap energy and improved charge carriers transport properties for the perovskite materials. Therefore, stability and power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the PSCs have been increased.

Different structures have been introduced and applied for fabrication of the PSCs [11, 12]. Among them, the conventional n-i-p type structures have been widely utilized and studied [2]. There, the n-type metal oxide scaffold is deposited on a transparent conductor (TCO) substrates and acts as an electron transport layer (ETL). The light absorbing perovskite film is subsequently over-coated and finally the hole transport layer (HTL) and metallic contact are deposited.

Generally, the electron and hole transport layers are used as the blocking layers for the opposite charge carriers in the PSCs [13]. Titanium dioxide  $ (\mathrm{TiO}_2) $  scaffold has been frequently utilized as the ETL in conventional structure of PSCs. This is due to wide band gap energy (3.20 eV), high transparency, suitable band edges positions, excellent electron-extraction ability and low electrical resistivity [14]. The appropriate choice of HTL as well as its quality could mainly decrease the charge carriers recombinations and influence the cell performance [15]. Properties such as high hole mobility, thermal stability, wide bandgap energy and suitable valence band maximum (VBM) are significant factors for the hole transport materials (HTMs). Furthermore, simple deposition method and reduction of damages to the light absorbing layer are another effective factors which should be considered [16].

The highest efficiencies of PSCs have been reported about  $ 22\% $  and achieved for organic HTMs [17]. Nevertheless, despite of the proper performance of these organic materials, the corresponding synthesis processes are difficult and HTMs are too expensive for commercialization. In addition, p-type dopants of organic HTMs like Lithium Salt Libis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI) are extremely hydrophilic [18]. This leads to aggravate degradation of the perovskite structure over the time and also reduction of the stability [19]. Besides, Photo-oxidation and deformation of Spiro-OMeTAD as the most commonly used HTM under illumination and temperature are another main factors in performance deterioration of the cells [20, 21]. As a result, several studies have been performed to replace the inexpensive and more stable HTMs in the PSCs [22]. According to literature, inorganic HTMs demonstrate high hole mobility and low cost synthesis processes compared to the organic materials. Therefore, the inorganic HTMs are promising materials in manufacturing low-cost and stable PV devices.

NiO, CuI, CuO,   $  Cu_{2}O  $   and CuSCN, are p-type semiconductors, which are typically used as HTM in different structures of solar cells [22–26]. Among them, CuSCN demonstrates a high hole mobility about 0.01–0.1 cm  $  ^{2}  $  /Vs compared that of the Spiro-OMeTAD which is reported as   $  10^{-3}  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $  /Vs HTM [27, 28]. It shows a proper transparency, thermal and chemical stability and is compatible with low cost and low temperature solution-processed methods [25, 27, 29]. Therefore, this HTL is a noteworthy and promising option for the application in the PSCs [30]. CuSCN was applied in dye synthesized solar cells as the HTL and resulted in energy conversion efficiencies around 3.5% [31]. Recently, this material has been utilized as the HTL in planar, conventional and inverted structures of the solid state PSCs. The first application of CuSCN in the PSCs had resulted in a power conversion efficiency of 5.4%. The corresponding deposition was carried out by a simple doctor-blade method [32]. Nevertheless, the most commonly applied solvents of inorganic HTMs could usually create a

surface destruction for the perovskite layer [33]. As a result, the proper selection of the solvents and deposition techniques are the main barriers in application of the inorganic HTMs [34]. CuSCN is hardly soluble in dipropyle sulfide solution with a maximum concentration around  $ 0.05\mathrm{M} $ . The deposition methods for this material and other inorganic HTMs are reported as spin coating [30] doctor-blading [32] and screen printing [23] approaches. Consequently, several deposition stages should be carried out to achieve sufficient thickness of the CuSCN layer in PSCs [35]. This would result in partial destruction of the perovskite film as well as increased surface roughness and creation of pinholes and cracks in HTM layer.

In this work, we propose a facile and effective approach to reduce the number of CuSCN deposition stages. It is shown that this change can result in lower surface roughness of the CuSCN layer, decreased demolition of the perovskite surface and improvement of the interfaces. Here, acetonitrile is applied as a co-solvent in dipropyle sulfide solution to increase the concentration of CuSCN in the mixed solvent. It was shown that the mentioned value could be enhanced from 0.05 to  $ 0.3\mathrm{M} $  by addition of the acetonitrile solution. Two kinds of CuSCN solution was prepared with explained concentrations. Then they were deposited as the HTM layers in conventional structure of the PSCs. These layers were named as CuSCN  $ 0.05\mathrm{M - X} $   $ (\mathrm{X} = 1 - 6) $  and CuSCN  $ 0.3\mathrm{M - Y} $   $ (\mathrm{Y} = 1 - 2) $  while the letters X and Y represent the number of spin coating times.  $ \mathrm{Cs}_{0.05}(\mathrm{MA}_{0.17}\mathrm{FA}_{0.83})_{0.95}\mathrm{Pb(I_{0.83}Br_{0.17})_3} $  mixed halides perovskite was applied as a light absorbing layer and deposited on FTO/compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  substrates through a one-step coating method [36]. This perovskite structure demonstrates more stability against the higher temperatures, light irradiation and humidity compared to the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite material [37]. Finally, the Au metallic contact was vacuum evaporated and PSCs were characterized for their photovoltaic performance. The effects of lower number of deposition times on the quality of the CuSCN layers and their interfaces were investigated. Besides, the photovoltaic parameters of the corresponding PSCs were measured and compared with the reference cells. The results demonstrated a power conversion efficiency of  $ 11.0\% $  for the PSC with CuSCN  $ 0.3\mathrm{M - 1} $  HTM layer. This efficiency was increased about  $ 45\% $  compared to the optimized reference cell with CuSCN  $ 0.05\mathrm{M - 4} $  hole transport layer. The reason was attributed to the better quality of the CuSCN  $ 0.3\mathrm{M - 1} $  HTL due to the lower deposition times as well as the well-formed interfaces.

# 2 Experimental details

# 2.1 Preparation of   $  TiO_{2}  $   mesoporous scaffold

The Fluorine doped Tin Oxide (FTO) glass transparent conductor substrates were cleaned under sonication with

ethanol, Acetone, DI water and 2-Propanol, respectively. Then, they were heated at 450 °C for 15 min and subjected to a UV-Ozone treatment to remove the traces of organic residues. A compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   (C-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) layer was deposited on the cleaned substrates by spin coating of a 0.15 M Titanium iso-propoxide (TTIP-Merck 97%) solution in ethanol at 2000 rpm for 30 s. Then the layers were annealed at 500 °C for another 1 h for better crystallization and stickiness to the substrate. A 20 wt%   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste was prepared using hydrothermally grown   $  TiO_{2}  $   NPs with 25 nm size and specific amount of terpineol and ethyl cellulose (5–15 mPa s, #46070, Fluka and 30–50 mPa s, #46080, Fluka; with weight ratio of 45/35) as we reported earlier [38, 39]. Then the paste was diluted in ethanol by 1:5.5 wt ratio and spin coated at 4000 rpm for 30 s on the FTO glass/C-  $  TiO_{2}  $   substrates. Finally, the double layer mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   (mp   $  TiO_{2}  $  ) scaffold were annealed at 500 °C for 30 min for the sintering and crystallization.

# 2.2 Preparation and deposition of mixed halides perovskite solution

Perovskite solution was prepared of FAI   $  (\mathrm{CH}(\mathrm{NH}_{2})_{2}\mathrm{I})  $   (Dyesol 98%), CsI (Sigma-Aldrich 99.999%),   $  PbI_{2}  $   (TCI 99.99%), MABr   $  (\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{Br})  $   (Dyesol 98%), and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   (TCI 99.99%) with 1 M, 0.05 M, 0.22 M, 1.1 M and 0.2 M concentrations in N-N-dimethylformamide (DMF-Merck 99.5%) and dimethyl sulfoxide, (DMSO-Merck 99.9%) mixed solvent (DMF:DMSO, 4:1 volume ratio). Then, this solution was coated on the FTO/C-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate by two sequential spin-coating procedures at 1000 rpm for 10 s and then 6000 rpm for 30 s. In the last 15 s, 200   $  \mu  $  l anti-solvent chlorobenzene solution was injected and layers were quickly annealed at 100 °C for 1 h.

# 2.3 Deposition of CuSCN hole transport layer and cell fabrication

A 0.05 M solution of CuSCN (Sigma-Aldrich 99%) was made by dissolving 6 mg of CuSCN into 1 mL of dipropyl sulfide (Sigma-Aldrich 97%) solvent. Another 0.3 M solution of CuSCN was also prepared by dissolving 36 mg of CuSCN into 1 mL of dipropyl sulfide/acetonitrile (Merck 99.9%) mixed solvents. The as-prepared solutions were deposited in the range one to six times to achieve different thicknesses of the CuSCN layers. The concentration of the applied solutions and number of deposition times were also mentioned at the end of the layers name. As a result, FTO/C-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /Perovskite/CuSCN 0.05 M-X (X=1-6) and FTO/C-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /Perovskite/CuSCN 0.3 M-Y (Y=1-2) multilayers were fabricated in this stage, finally, these layers were annealed at 65 °C for 5 min. In order to Spiro-OMeTAD deposition, the mixture solution includes 72.3 mg of solvent.

Spiro-OMeTAD (99.0%) in 1 mL of chlorobenzene (Merck 99%) with 17.5   $  \mu  $  L of lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI) (Sigma-Aldrich 99.5%) solution in acetonitrile (530 mg/mL) and 22.5   $  \mu  $  L of 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP-Sigma-Aldrich 96%) was prepared. Then, this solution was spin coated at 5000 rpm for 30 s and Au metallic contact was over-deposited by thermal evaporation technique.

# 2.4 Characterization

X-ray diffraction pattern in normal and grazing-incidence mode were recorded by PANalytical, X'Pert Pro MPD system using Cu Kα radiation. Avaspec 2048 TEC spectrophotometer was used to measure optical properties, including diffuse spectroscopy (DTS) and steady state photoluminescence, PL measurements. The samples were excited by a LCM-DTL-374QT laser equipment with a 350 nm wavelength. Then the emissive light was collected through the optical fibers and transferred into the spectrophotometer. The UV–Visible absorption spectra were recorded by a Perkin Elmer Lambda 25 equipment. The Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) and energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) analyses were carried out using a TESCAN-Mira3 field-emission scanning electron microscope. Photovoltaic characteristics were recorded by a Keithley2400 source meter under Am 1.5 (100 mW/cm²) simulated light radiation in a Sharif solar SIM-1000 system (calibrated by a Thorlabs photodiode). Incident photon-to current conversion efficiencies (IPCE) were measured by a Sharif solar IPCE-015 equipment.

According to the results the EDS peaks related to the C, N, S, Cu, Br, I, Cs and Pb elements could be observed in the spectrum. Figure 3a demonstrates the X-ray diffraction pattern of the perovskite/CuSCN double-layer. According to the results there are several peaks located at 2θ angles of 14.2°, 20.1°, 23.57°, 24.54°, 28.5°, 31.9°, 35.0°. These peaks belong to the (110), (112), (211), (202), (220), (310) and (312) crystalline planes of the tetragonal phase   $  (\mathrm{Cs}_{0.05}(\mathrm{MA}_{0.17}\mathrm{FA}_{0.83})_{0.95}\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{I}_{0.83}\mathrm{Br}_{0.17})_{3})  $   perovskite layer [41]. It means that there are not any XRD peaks related to the CuSCN crystalline lattice in the spectrum. This could be attributed to the low thickness of the CuSCN layer and low intensity of the corresponding XRD peaks. Consequently, grazing-incidence XRD (GIXRD) analysis was performed for the perovskite/CuSCN double-layer. This method has less penetration depth in the layer due to the small angle of incidence irradiation. As a result, it can create more information about the crystalline properties of the surface layers. The result in Fig. 3b indicates three extra peaks located at 2θ angles of 15.9°, 26.6° and 34.03°. These peaks are related to the (003), (101) and (104) planes in β-phase crystal lattice of the CuSCN using JCPDS card No. 29-0581 [30, 42–44].

# 4 Conclusions

In this research we proposed a novel acetonitrile assisted CuSCN deposition as an inorganic HTL in

conventional structure of the PSCs. Mixed halide   $  (\mathrm{Cs}_{0.05}(\mathrm{MA}_{0.17}\mathrm{FA}_{0.83})_{0.95}\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{I}_{0.83}\mathrm{Br}_{0.17})_{3})  $   perovskite layer was utilized due to its more stability against the higher temperatures, light irradiation and humidity compared to the   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   material. The maximum accessible concentration of CuSCN in dipropyl sulfide solution was about 0.05 M in several reports. Here, this concentration was increased to 0.3 M by using acetonitrile as a co-solvent, resulted in lower number of CuSCN deposition times for a proper thickness. The interface of CuSCN/perovskite layer and surface quality of CuSCN layer were quite improved compared to those of the optimized situation created by 0.05 M CuSCN solution. The photovoltaic parameters of the PSCs with CuSCN layer prepared in different number of deposition times from two CuSCN solution were measured and investigated. Besides, the optimized cell was compared with the standard PSC with Spiro-OMeTAD hole transport layer. The results showed that a power conversion efficiency of 11.0% was achieved for the PSC with CuSCN layer prepared from the 0.3 M solution in one time of deposition process. This efficiency was enhanced about 45% compared to that of the best cell with CuSCN layer deposited from 0.05 M solution in 4 deposition times. The reason was referred to the better quality of CuSCN layer deposited from 0.3 M CuSCN solution as well as the well-formed interfaces.
