Recently, organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cell (PSC) receives increasing attention as a cutting-edge photovoltaic technology owing to its low production cost, straightforward fabrication and high power conversion efficiency (PCE) [1-3]. So far, with the great efforts of researchers worldwide, PSCs had undergone expeditious development with the PCEs astonishing increased from  $ 3\% $  to  $ 24.2\% $  [4-6]. Typically, the highly efficient PSCs possess a well layer-by-layer structure with the perovskite light harvesting material sandwiched between electron and hole transport materials. The hole transport materials (HTMs) are found playing a crucial role in hole extraction and transport, and reducing the energy barrier between the perovskite and electrode [7-10]. Up to now,  $ 2',7,7' $ -tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) is still the most commonly used HTM. However, the complicated synthesis and purification procedures lead to the extremely high price, limiting their commercial viability. Therefore, it is highly appreciated to develop low-cost and highly efficient alternative HTMs. Up to now, a great number of novel alternatives have been reported. # ABSTRACT

Two novel hole transport materials (HTMs) with indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b']dithiophene (IDT) as core building blocks, termed IDT1 and IDT2, were designed and synthesized. The side alkyl chains were introduced to regulate and control the morphology and stacking behavior of HTMs, and the peripheral triarylamine arms were introduced to adjust the energy levels and to facilitate efficient hole transport. Applied in mesoporous structured perovskite solar cells (PSCs), HTM IDT1 achieved higher power conversion efficiency (PCE, 19.55%) and better stability than Spiro-OMeTAD (19.25%) and IDT2 (15.77%) based PSC. These results suggest the potential of IDT1 as a promising HTM for PSCs.

Herein, indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b']dithiophene (IDT) core unit which has succeeded in the non-fullerene ETM design for organic solar cell was selected, varying the configuration of the backbone by introducing different side chains, two HTMs termed IDT1 and IDT2 (see Fig. 1) were designed and synthesized. The nearly planar rigid conjugated IDT ring is beneficial for intramolecular π-π stacking, which has been manifested that it can enhance charge carrier mobility and conductivity. Applied in PSCs with   $  (\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3})_{0.85}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})_{0.15}  $   as light harvesting material, HTM IDT1 achieved an impressive PCE as high as 19.55% without hysteresis. Moreover, IDT1 based device without any encapsulation presents better stability than IDT2 and Spiro-OMeTAD by maintaining 85% of the initial efficiency after 500 h degradation in ambient conditions.

These two new HTMs were easily synthesized through Suzuki reaction with good yields (59.4% and 57.9% for IDT1 and IDT2, respectively), and the detailed synthetic routes are shown in the Supplementary Information. Both HTMs IDT1 and IDT2 are characterized by NMR and high-resolution mass spectrum (HRMS) (see Figs. S1–S6). Herein, different side chains were also introduced to adjust the configuration of HTMs. As shown in Fig. 1, density functional theory (DFT) calculation results indicate that HTM IDT1 exhibits a better planar configuration compared with IDT2, which is favorable for intramolecular  $ \pi-\pi $  stacking, therefore the higher hole mobility and conductivity could be expected for IDT1. In addition, the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of HTMs IDT1 and IDT2 are both distributed along the main backbone and exhibit an overlap to some extent, implying a strong Coulomb interaction, which is beneficial for hole extraction and hopping. By DFT calculation, the HOMO energy levels of HTMs IDT1 and IDT2 are evaluated to be  $ -4.31 $  and  $ -4.28\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. Theoretically the side chains are found no conspicuous effects on energy levels of these two HTMs.

The UV–vis absorption spectra of HTMs IDT1 and IDT2 are presented in Fig. 2(a) and the corresponding optical and electrochemical characteristics were summarized in Table 1. The maximum absorption peak (  $  \lambda_{max}  $  ) of HTMs IDT1 and IDT2 were detected to be 441 and 439 nm, respectively. The semblable absorption profiles of IDT1 and IDT2 were derived from the similar chemical backbone structure. By calculating from the UV–vis spectra absorption edge, the bandgap of IDT1 and IDT2 are equal (2.51 eV). In combination with the cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements (Fig. 2(b)), the energy levels of IDT1 and IDT2 were further evaluated. The HOMO energy levels of IDT1 and IDT2 were calculated to be -5.24 and -5.17 eV, respectively, supplying a sufficient driving force for hole extraction and transfer from perovskite to HTMs. By contrast, the IDT1 shows a slightly deeper HOMO energy level, which may be attributed to the moderately weaker electron-donating ability of alky phenyl side chains on IDT1 molecule, weakly adjusting the electron cloud distribution on the back bone and then lowering the energy level of molecule. Furthermore, the LUMO energy levels of IDT1 and IDT2 were calculated to be -2.73 and -2.65 eV,

respectively, which is much more positive than the conduction band (CB) of perovskite (-4.05 eV), facilitating efficiently electron blocking and suppressing the charge recombination. The measured results are basically consistent with the DFT calculation and further verify that the side chains on IDT core unit have nearly no significant influence on the energy levels of materials as discussed above.

To evaluate the hole transport properties of HTMs IDT1 and IDT2, the hole mobility was further studied by utilizing space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method, with a hole-only device structure of ITO/NiO/HTM/Au. As shown in Fig. 3(a), the hole mobility of IDT1 is much higher than that of IDT2, evaluated to be  $ 1.02 \times 10^{-4} $  and  $ 2.65 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~cm}^2 \mathrm{~V}^{-1} \mathrm{~S}^{-1} $ , respectively. However, the conductivity of IDT1 is evaluated to be  $ 2.60 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~S} \mathrm{~cm}^{-2} $ , which is even an order of magnitude higher than that of IDT2 ( $ 3.92 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~S} \mathrm{~cm}^{-2} $ ), as shown in Fig. 3(b). The dramatically improved conductivity may cause from more favorable stacking orientation [26-28]. On account of the superior conductivity of IDT1, a higher fill factor (FF) for IDT1 could be further expected in the photovoltaic performance test. In addition, the thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) measurements (see Fig. S7) show that IDT1 exhibits a much higher decomposition temperature (Td) of  $ 415^{\circ} \mathrm{C} $  than IDT2 ( $ T_{\mathrm{d}} = 345^{\circ} \mathrm{C} $ ), suggesting a better thermal stability of IDT1.

To investigate the photovoltaic characteristics of HTMs IDT1 and IDT2, PSC devices based on IDT1 and IDT2 were fabricated with a device configuration of FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $ /m-TiO $ _2 $ /(FAPbI $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.85} $ (MAPbBr $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.15} $ /HTM/Au. From the cross-sectional SEM image of IDT1 based PSC (see Fig. 2(d)), we can see that the device displays a good layer-by-layer structure. The thickness of perovskite film and HTL are around 400 and 80 nm, respectively. The perovskite film can be fully and homogenously covered by HTM IDT1, while the IDT2 film exhibits obvious apparent cracks (see Fig. 2(e) and (f)). To make sure the J-V measurements are accurate, the steady-state current densities of IDT1 and IDT2 based PSC devices at maximum power output point and hysteresis behavior were further measured and presented in Fig. 4(c). There is nearly no hysteresis for both IDT1 and IDT2 based devices. The IDT1 and IDT2 based PSC devices show the steady-state current densities of 21.19 and 19.32 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , respectively, matching well with the J-V test results. As the incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra shown in Fig. 4(b), the IDT1 and IDT2 based PSC devices both exhibit a wide identical absorption region from 350 to 800 nm. Moreover, IDT1 based PSC device receive a higher IPCE values than that of IDT2, correspondingly leading to a higher   $  J_{sc}  $  , consisting with the J-V measurements. The greatly improved   $  J_{sc}  $   of IDT1 can be ascribed to the excellent hole mobility and better film morphology of IDT1 as discussed above. It is also further supported by the steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) measurements as shown in Fig. 3(c) and (d). Fairly obvious PL quenching is detected in case of IDT1 and IDT2 coated perovskite films. Simultaneously, IDT1 coated perovskite film presents a higher quenching efficiency (85.7%) than that of IDT2 (79.5%), indicating more effective hole extraction of IDT1. Furthermore, the pristine perovskite film shows

a PL peak at 796 nm, while the PL quenched emission peaks of IDT1 and IDT2 coated perovskite films are both located at 771 nm, generating a comparatively distinct blue shift. It suggests that HTMs IDT1 and IDT2 can passivate the trap states on the surface of perovskite film as well because of the lone pair of sulfur atom on the molecule. The PL decay lifetime of IDT1 and IDT2 coated perovskite film are 55.32 and 53.56 ns, respectively, indicating a similar hole extraction rate and smoother hole transport process for IDT1 and IDT2.

Fig. 5(a)–(d) shows the aging test results of the IDT1, IDT2 and Spiro-OMeTAD based PSCs in ambient condition (relative humidity around 45%, room temperature) without encapsulation. At the initial stage, all devices showed improved PCEs. On the one hand, the improvements can be partly attributed to the passivation of perovskite surface defects by TBP in hole transport layer (HTL) [29–31]. On the other hand, for our designed materials IDT1 and IDT2, the sulfur atoms on IDT ring can passivate the perovskite surface defects as well, which is confirmed by PL measurements as discussed above [32]. Both the passivation effect of TBP and sulfur atom is gradual process, which take some times, correspondingly, manifesting an increased PCE at the initial stage. However, the additive LiTFSI in HTL may also give rise to the degradation of perovskite, which is unfavorable for the device stability, correspondingly, a decreased PCE is observed after a long period of time. The IDT1 based devices exhibit better stability than IDT2, maintaining 85% of initial efficiency. The main reasons are IDT1 film is more homogenous and slightly stronger hydrophobicity (see Fig. S9).
