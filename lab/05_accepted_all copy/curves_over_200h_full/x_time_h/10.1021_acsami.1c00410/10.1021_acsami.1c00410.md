# Increasing Stability of   $  SnO_{2}  $  -Based Perovskite Solar Cells by Introducing an Anionic Conjugated Polyelectrolyte for Interfacial Adjustment

Chao Tan, Wenting Xu, Yihong Huan, Bo Wu,  $  ^{*}  $   Tianshi Qin, and Deqing Gao  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Despite the fact that power conversion efficiency (PCE) has been greatly improved in recent years, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) need to overcome some challenges, like stability, for the commercial application. Herein, an anionic conjugated polyelectrolyte, sulfonic-containing polyfluorene (abbreviated to SPF), has been developed to modify the interface between the electron-transporting layer (ETL)   $  SnO_{2}  $   and the optoelectronic active layer   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   in the n-i-p cells. After 40 days of storage in atmospheric environment in the dark with exposure to a controlled humidity of about 10%, PCE of the SPF-modified cells with the structure of ITO/  $  SnO_{2}  $  /SPF/MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /spiro-OMeTAD/Au still remained 94% of the initial value. In contrast, the control cell without SPF only remained 31.1% of its initial efficiency after 29

days. The main reason for the stability enhancement is the adjustment of interfacial energy level, the crystallinity enhancement, and the removal of the interfacial defect of the perovskite layer by introducing the hydrophobic and smooth SPF interfacial layer. Deep electrical study on the PSCs discloses that the cell has low carrier transfer resistance, low leakage current density, and minor interfacial charge accumulation. What's more, the short-circuit current density is improved, and PCE of  $ 20.47\% $  is achieved.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

In recent years, organic–inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have become one of the hot spots in photovoltaic research due to their high efficiency and easy manufacture. In a short decade, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) has rapidly increased from 3.9%  $  ^{1}  $  to 25%.  $  ^{2,3}  $  High PCE is mainly attributed to the excellent optoelectronic properties of the perovskite materials (for example,  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbX_{3}  $ , X = Cl, Br, and I) as optoelectronic active materials, such as long carrier lifetime, high mobility, strong absorption coefficient, and bipolar transmission.  $  ^{4-7}  $  However, although PCE has been greatly improved, the commercial application of PSCs is limited due to the low stability.

As an electron-transporting layer (ETL) in the traditional n-i-p PSCs,   $  SnO_{2}  $   has shown great advantages, such as low-temperature preparation, high transparency, high electron mobility, and large bandgap.  $  ^{8,9}  $   However, due to the mismatching energy level and the existence of defect states,  $  ^{10,11}  $   the   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite interface features ineffective charge extraction and severe charge recombination, resulting in low efficiency and stability of the cells. There are methods to modify the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer: (1) improve the quality of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   film to make it a better contact with the perovskite layer by doping metal ions, (2) passivate the defect state of the surface,

A conjugated polyelectrolyte refers to a conjugated polymer with an ionic group (for example, a sulfonic acid group, a carboxyl group, and an ammonium group) on the side chain. In the past few years, several types of conjugated polyelectrolytes were introduced as ETL, HTL, or dopant in PSCs, achieving improved device performance with suppressed trap density and better charge extraction.  $  ^{20-22}  $   The ionic conjugated polymer electrolytes can form a strong interface dipole and may adjust the interface work function.  $  ^{23-25}  $   An anionic conjugated polyelectrolyte, a block copolymer polyfluorene (abbreviated to SPF), is composed of two fluorene monomers, one fluorene containing two sulfonic groups at the side chain and another containing two octyl side chains. SPF is both hydrophilic due to sulfonic groups and hydrophobic due to octyl chains. It is conjugated and arranged in an orderly fashion through the fluorene main chain, being partially strengthened by the long octyl side chains. This copolymer as a comparison was utilized to be a cathode interface layer for OPVs and presented a slight performance improvement  $  ^{26}  $   but has not been reported in PSCs. In the work, the polyelectrolyte SPF was introduced to modify the interface between the   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer and the optoelectronic active layer   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   in the n-i-p PSCs (in Scheme 1). # 2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

Materials. All reagents and chemicals were purchased from commercial suppliers without further purification, unless otherwise stated. Tin dioxide (IV), 15% hydrocolloid dispersion (  $  SnO_{2}  $  ), methylammonium iodide (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $  , purity   $  \geq 99.5\%  $  ), and lead iodide (  $  PbI_{2}  $  , purity   $  \geq 99.999\%  $  ) were obtained from Alfa Aesar. Spiro-OMeTAD (purity   $  \geq 99.8\%  $  ) was purchased from Xi'an Bao Laite. 4-tert-Butylpyridine (tBP, purity   $  \geq 99.5\%  $  ) was purchased from Maxtor. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, purity   $  \geq 99.9\%  $  ), and N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, purity   $  \geq 99.9\%  $  ) were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich.

Synthesis of SPF. The synthetic route for the anionic conjugated polyelectrolyte SPF is demonstrated in Scheme 1 of the Supporting Information. The starting material fluorene was brominated to give 2,7-dibromo-9H-fluorene in a yield of 90%. 2,7-Dibromo-9H-fluorene reacted with 1,4-butane sultone to give 2,7-dibromo-9,9-bis(4'-sulfonatobutyl) fluorene disodium in yield of 57.2%. By following the literature,  $  ^{[27]}  $  the anionic conjugated polyelectrolyte SPF was synthesized from a Suzuki coupling reaction between 2,7-dibromo-9,9-bis(4'-sulfonatobutyl) fluorene disodium and 2,2'-(9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluorene-2,7-diyl)bis(4,4,5,5-tetramethyl-1,3,2-dioxaborolane) in a snack reactor in a yield of 40%. As shown in Figure S4, the weight-average molecular weight (  $  M_{w}  $  ) of SPF was found to be 36633 g mol  $  ^{-1}  $  with a polydispersity index (PDI) of 1.48. The thermal property was analyzed with thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) under flowing argon. As shown in Figure S5, corresponding to an initial 5% of weight loss, SPF exhibited superior thermal stability with a decomposition temperature (  $  T_{d}  $  ) of 213 °C.

Device Fabrication. The perovskite solar cells with the architecture of ITO/SnO $ _2 $ /SPF/MAPbI $ _3 $ /Spiro-OMeTAD/Au and the control cell without SPF were fabricated as following. The patterned indium tin oxide (ITO) substrates were cleaned as reported. $ ^{21} $  The SnO $ _2 $  precursor was spin-coated onto the ITO substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at 150 °C for 30 min. The interfacial layer SPF was made by spin coating the DMSO solution in 0.5 mol/L at 4000 rpm for 30 s and then baked in air at 120 °C for 15 min. The perovskite layer was prepared by one-step spin coating at 1000 rpm for 10 s and then at 3500 rpm for 20 s, with the precursor solution including MAI, PbI $ _2 $ , and DMSO in DMF. Ethyl acetate was dropped to the sample at the last 10th second during the spin coating. The perovskite film was annealed at 130 °C for 10 min. Then the spiro-OMeTAD solution (72.3 mg/mL) with Li-TFSI and TBP additive was spin-coated onto the perovskite layer, and Au electrodes (80 nm) were thermally evaporated at a rate of 1 Å/s. The active area of the cell was 5 mm $ ^2 $ .

Characterization. The molecular weight was characterized with gel permeation chromatography (GPC). The optical absorption and photoluminescence spectra measurements were performed with the UV–vis spectrometer (SHIMADZU UV-1750) and fluorescence spectrophotometer (Hitachi F-4600). The contact angle characterization was performed by using the microscopic contact angle meter (KRUSS GmbH DSA1005). Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) measurement was carried out on a thermogravimetric analyzer (Mettler Toledo TGA STAR System) under an Ar flow. The surface morphology was measured by the atomic force microscopy (AFM, Park XE-70) in tapping mode. The crystal characterization of perovskite on different layers was carried out by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Smartlab 3KW) measurements with a Cu Kα beam and scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-7800F). The lifetime of the carriers was obtained from the time-correlated single-photon counting (TCSPC, FLS980). Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurements were conducted on an ultrahigh vacuum UPS system (Kratos Axis Supra) with a He Iα (21.2 eV) ultraviolet source. The cells were characterized as our report.  $  ^{24}  $

# 4. CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we have demonstrated the sulfonic-containing fluorene-based conjugated polyelectrolyte SPF as the interfacial layer between   $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite in PSCs. It is found that high quality perovskite film with a better morphology was formed with a SPF underlayer. Moreover, SPF can reduce the work function of   $  SnO_{2}  $   and facilitates efficient electron transport. As a result, the champion PCE of SPF-modified PSCs has reached 20.46%. Moreover, due to the long side chains endowing the polymer with hydrophobic property and its UV-blocking ability, the stability of the SPF-modified device has been greatly improved. After 40 days, PCE of the SPF-modified cell still remained 94% of the initial optimized value, which is about 60% better than the stability of the control cell.
