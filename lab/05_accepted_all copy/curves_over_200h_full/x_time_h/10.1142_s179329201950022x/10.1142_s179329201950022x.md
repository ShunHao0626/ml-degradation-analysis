ABSTRACT: Remarkable progress has been achieved in organometallic halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs). However, poor environmental stability of PSCs has been proved challenging and needs further improvement. Here, a strategy of butylated hydroxyltoluene (BHT) antioxidant additive with hydrophobic tert butyl and hydrophilic hydroxyl is developed to enhance the hydrophobicity, control nucleation rate and passivate the defect states of perovskite. The tert butyl group effectively prevents the penetration of moisture, as well as the formed hydrogen bond between hydroxyl group and iodine controls the assembly process of BHT and perovskite. The By optimizing weight ratio of BHT and  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  in precursor solution, the power conversion efficiency of PSCs increased from  $ 14.93\% $  to  $ 18.28\% $ , and greatly enhanced device stability compared with control device. We believe our tactic of BHT additive can be applied to various perovskite films and will facilitate the realization of stable perovskite optoelectronics.

Keywords: Perovskite solar cells; BHT additives; Hydrophobicity; Stability; Defect states.

# 1. Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) show the promising as the next-generation photovoltaic technology and the power conversion efficiency (PCE) has been dramatically increased from 3.8% to 23.3%.  $  ^{1-5}  $   Brilliant performance of PSCs attributes to the superior optoelectronic properties of perovskite materials, such as tunable bandgap, high absorption coefficient, ambipolar charge transport, long carrier diffusion length and low exciton binding energy.  $  ^{6-10}  $   Although rapid and tremendous improvement of perovskite solar cells has been achieved, the critical challenge for commercial PSCs is still the degradation of perovskite films and solar cells under relative humidity and oxygen.  $  ^{11, 12}  $

In order to improve the anti-humidity and film stability, three kinds of tactic have been developed: synthesizing novel perovskite structure, enhancing the anti-moisture ability of hole transport layer (HTL) and protecting the perovskite film. Firstly, different additive and organic cations were added into pristine perovskite to obtain novel perovskite. For example, two dimensional (2D) perovskite structures was fabricated by adding additives into three dimensional (3D) perovskite to improve the humidity resistant ability.  $  ^{13}  $   Pang et al introduced the water-resistant organic group,   $  NH_{2}CH=NH_{2}  $  , to replace part of MAI, obviously improved the device stability.  $  ^{14}  $   Wu et al. introduced the addition of Lewis base organic component (2-NAM) to fabricate the perovskite containing triple organic cations and enlarge crystal size, which has strong Lewis acid and base interaction (between C=O and Pb) with perovskite.  $  ^{13}  $   Bi et al. mixed 1,1,1-trifluoro-ethyl ammonium iodide (FEAI) with intrinsic   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   to improved environmental stability with sacrificing the device performance. Another way is adding hydrophobic materials into HTL, which is the upper layer of perovskite films in conventional PSCs structure.  $  ^{15}  $   Liu et al. added highly hydrophobic 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) as the surface modification agent to enhance the performance and stability of   $  MAPbI_{3}  $  -based (  $  MAPbI_{3}  $  ) perovskite solar cells fabricated in ambient air.  $  ^{16}  $   Jia et al. reported a fluorine-containing hydrophobic Lewis acid dopant for PTAA HTL and achieved stable device, which also

outperformed the state-of-the-art bi-dopants Li-TFSI/tBP.  $  ^{[11]}  $   Meanwhile, capping layer were explored to enhance the anti-humidity of perovskite film. Huang et al. inserted a tunneling/encapsulation layer between perovskite and electron transport layer (ETL) to prevent perovskite film from damage caused by water or moisture.  $  ^{[17]}  $   Wang et al. introduced fluoroalkyl silane with a super-hydrophobic C-F strong polar bond at the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  /HTL interface to prevent perovskite layer against degradation in air.  $  ^{[18]}  $   The capping layer on top of perovskite effectively resisted moisture penetration and accelerated charge carrier transporting.

However, there are still some problems with these methods, such as insufficient improvement of hydrophobic properties, ineffective passivation of ionic migration and defect states, restrained grain growth and complicated processes. In order to further solve above problems, induced crystalline additives and antioxidants were explored. The additives of benzoquinone (BQ) and thio-semicarbazide (TSC) were added into precursor solution of perovskite to improve morphology and stability. Enlarged grain size and eliminated grain boundary contributes to the improvement of perovskite stability. As reported, the penetrative moisture firstly reacts at grain boundary of perovskite film, which is demonstrated as the origin of perovskite degradation.  $  ^{19}  $   Gao et al. added antioxidants citric acid (CA) into perovskite precursor solution, leading to the formation of an extremely uniform and continuous layer. It derives from the synergistic effect of CA and chloride anion, which modulate crystal growth of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  .  $  ^{20}  $   Although the nucleation rate and grain size of perovskite crystals were controlled, the hydrophobicity of perovskite film was not improved. Therefore, Hwang et al. improved the stability of perovskite through hydrophobic passivation. However, the defects were not effectively passivated and the ion migration was not inhibited.  $  ^{21}  $

Inspired by above-mentioned reports, an additive of antioxidant butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT), which contains hydrophobic group and hydroxyl was added into MAI and   $  PbI_{2}  $   precursor solution. Compared to pure   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   films, the BHT based perovskite films were smoother and more hydrophobic, meanwhile, obtained larger crystal grain. As a result, with 4.2 wt% BHT additive, the maximum efficiency of PSCs reached 18.28%, and the devices showed an improved long-term stability.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1.Materials

Isopropyl alcohol titanium, methylammonium iodide (MAI), 2,2',7,7'-Tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD), PbI $ _2 $ , lithium bis[(trifluoromethyl) sulfonyl] imide salt (Li-TFSI), titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) (75 wt% in isopropyl alcohol) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. All of the other salts and anhydrous solvents were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, including N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), ethanol, isopropyl alcohol, tert-butylpyridine (tBP), chlorobenzene, acetonitrile, 1-butanol, diethyl ether, and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). All the above chemical products were used directly without further purification or other treatment.

# 2.2. Device Fabrication

Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glasses substrates were ultrasonically cleaned with detergent, acetone, deionized water, ethanol for 15 min, respectively. And then, the conductive substrates were dried with a nitrogen stream and treated with UV-ozone for 15 min. The cleaned FTO glasses were titanium dioxide solution by the spin-coating method at 2000 rpm for 55 s, followed by heating at 150 °C for 15 min. And then the films were calcined in a muffle furnace at 500 °C for 30 min. When the base was cooled from the muffle furnace to room temperature, the electron transport layer was modified by TiCl₄ hydrolysis, which was then calcined at 500 °C for 0.5 h. BHT were dissolved in 0.5 mL DMF, which was heated at   $  60^{\circ}  $  C for 2 h under magnetic stirring, and the mixture was then spin coated on the as-prepared   $  TiO_{2}  $   film at 3000 rpm for 55 s. Afterward, the as-prepared films were heated at   $  100^{\circ}  $  C for 20 min until the color changed to dark brown. Finally, a 100 nm thick silver (Ag) electrode was thermally evaporated under vacuum of   $  4\times10^{-5}  $   Pa, at a rate of   $  \sim0.9  $   nm/s.

# 2.3. Device Characterization

The morphology and structure of the films were characterized by Field Emission Scanning Electronic Microscope (SEM) tested by FEI Czech Republic S. r. o (FEI Quanta 650 FEG), X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the samples were obtained using a diffractometer Panaco Netherlands (X Pert PRO MPD). The optical properties of samples were measured by UV-Vis spectrophotometer (UV-2700). The current density-voltage (J-V) curves were measured under AM 1.5G one sun illumination (100 mW/cm²) with a solar simulator (Zolix SS150). The light intensity was adjusted by an NREL-calibrated silicon solar cell. During measurement, the device area of 0.16 cm² was defined by an aperture. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) was measured with an EQE system America Bentham (PVE300) containing a xenon lamp, a monochromator, a Si detector and a dual-channel power meter. The time-solve photoluminescence (PL) spectra were measured at 765 nm with 450 nm excitation at a pulse frequency of 1MHz (FLS980, Edinburgh Instruments). Atomic force microscope (AFM, Agilent Technologies 7500) was used to detect surface topography of perovskite films.

With  $ 4.2\mathrm{wt}\% $  BHT, the largest average grain size of  $ 277.9\mathrm{nm} $  was achieved. And the film become more dense and compact. This large grain size means that the density of grain boundaries becomes much lower, which is advantageous to the transport and collection of photogenerated charges. However, the cracks and pinholes caused without BHT, lead to reduced coverage of the perovskite film on the substrate, which decreases device performance. Therefore, the addition of appropriate amount of BHT can slow down the nucleation rate of crystals and improve the quality of thin films. This is attributed to the hydrogen bond formed internally, which increases the direct cohesion of adjacent perovskite.

The perovskite films were further tested by AFM. It was found that the roughness decreased from 9.6 nm to 8.1 nm [Fig. 1(h)] after adding 4.2 wt% BHT, which indicates that optimized concentration of BHT additive could effectively improve the smoothness of perovskite film. Bigger grain and smoother surface were beneficial to reduce charge recombination at grain boundary and improve the performance of the device  $  ^{22}  $  .

In order to confirm the existence of BHT in perovskite film, the FTIR spectra are measured, as shown in Fig. 3(a) and 3(b). Four peaks appear at  $ 1500~\mathrm{cm^{-1}} $ ,  $ 1675~\mathrm{cm^{-1}} $ ,  $ 3450~\mathrm{cm^{-1}} $ , which derive from the stretching vibration of the C-O,  $ \mathrm{C = C} $ , and  $ \mathrm{N - H},^{23,24} $  respectively. In Fig.3(b), the stretching of C-O  $ (1500~\mathrm{cm^{-1}}) $  occurred when the additive amount are  $ 2.1~\mathrm{wt\%} $ ,  $ 4.2~\mathrm{wt\%} $ ,  $ 8.4~\mathrm{wt\%} $ ,  $ 12.6~\mathrm{wt\%} $ , and the stretching is enhanced with the increasing of BHT concentration. Moreover, there is no C-O stretching in pure perovskite films. It is directly proved that BHT molecules are completely mixed with perovskite, which also formed a coating layer on perovskite surface and grain boundary to resist the penetration of moisture.

To verify the enhanced hydrophobicity of perovskite films after adding BHT additive, contact angle measurements were adopted on perovskite films with different BHT concentration. As shown in Fig. 3(c), with the increasing of additive amount in perovskite precursor from 0 wt% BHT to 12.6 wt% BHT, the contact angles of perovskite films are 46.5°, 52.8°, 59.4°, 56.9°, 55.6°, respectively. As shown in Fig. 3(d), the contact angle increased from the original 46.5° to 59.4° after adding 4.2 wt% BHT. The hydrophobic properties of perovskite layer can be easily tuned by introducing different amount of BHT into perovskite film, which leads to the possibility to achieve high performance and stability perovskite solar cells in ambient air. It has been reported that this hydrophobicity is beneficial to the stability of perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{25, 26}  $  Pure perovskite surface would be more likely to be wetted by water than perovskite surface composed of hydrophobic BHT. The surface-assembled BHT of perovskite could effectively prevent moisture or water penetration from grain boundary and restrained the degradation of perovskite film.

Water and moisture in the environment are the main influence factors of perovskite instability, so it is urgent to explore tactics to prevent moisture penetration. Previous reports have proved that the moisture stability of perovskite can be markedly improved by increasing its hydrophobicity.  $  ^{16, 27}  $   Here,

In this way, the purpose of ion migration control is achieved.

# Conclusion

In conclusion, it has been proved that adding appropriate BHT into perovskite can effectively improve the stability of PSCs. By optimizing the incorporation concentration of BHT, the PSCs exhibited a maximum efficiency of  $ 18.28\% $  compared with the controlled PSCs  $ (14.93\%) $ . Moreover, the BHT based devices is more stable in air after  $ 500\mathrm{h} $ , demonstrating that BHT additive improved the hydrophobicity and suppressed the degradation process of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films. It was found that the formation of hydrophobic group and hydrogen bond effectively protected the perovskite from moisture and regulated the crystal quality. The results showed that the addition of BHT is a promising strategy to improve the perovskite quality and photoelectric performance.
