# 1. Introduction

Organometallic halide perovskites are now in the spotlight of solar energy harvesting research aiming to realize highly-efficient, cost effective solar cell devices (Chen et al., 2017b; Liu et al., 2019; G. Xu et al., 2018; Y.L. Xu et al., 2018; You et al., 2018; Zheng et al., 2017). Remarkably, the certified efficiency of power conversion for perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has gone through a rapid improvement from  $ 3.8\% $  to  $ 25.2\% $  within the last decade (https://www.nrel.gov/pv/cell-efficiency.html; Jiang et al., 2019; Yang et al., 2019; You et al., 2017), owing to the superior optical characteristic, the good coefficient of absorption, and the highly mobile charge carriers in the material, in addition to the long path of diffusion that the charge carriers need to travel before their recombination (Aydin et al., 2019; Li et al., 2016; Park et al., 2019; Zheng et al., 2019). Depending on the type of the layer transporting carriers on the electrode, the structure of a PSC device assumes either a normal (also known as n-i-p) or an inverted (p-i-n) configuration (Jeon et al., 2018; Jiang et al., 2017; Shi et al., 2015). # ABSTRACT

Among the factors that lead to the reduction of the efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) using [6,6]-phenyl C61 butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) versus those of conventional structure, is the difficulty involved in realizing a high-quality film, and the non-radiative recombination that takes place at the interface between PCBM and perovskite. Our present work proposes a fabrication technique capable of overcoming these issues. Organic small molecule material was introduced along with PCBM, to obtain a homogeneously seamless film that plays the role of a dipole layer. The incorporated triethyl citrate (TEC) boosts the power conversion efficiency (PCE) from  $ 14.56\% $  to  $ 17.86\% $  with suppressed hysteresis, which originates primarily from the efficient electron transfer between the perovskite/PCBM and the Ag cathode interface. The results of electrochemical impedance spectroscopy measurements imply efficient electron transfer, low series resistance, and large recombination resistance in the photovoltaic device employing PCBM@TEC as the cathode interfacial layer. Specifically, the highly hydrophobic PCBM@TEC cathode interfacial layer (CIL) showed higher resistance to moisture, leading to a more environmentally-stable device. The results also showed the efficiency of the device retained  $ 84\% $  of its initial value after 30 days of operation under ambient conditions, which shows a visibly superior stability compared to a reference device that, under the same environment, retained only  $ 37\% $  its initial efficiency. Such advancement in the operational efficiency and stability of the device, will contribute to the longevity and cost-effectiveness of inverted PSC devices.

One of the strategies for enhancing the performance of inverted PSCs, is to optimize the properties of the CIL. Among other candidates, [6,6]-phenyl C61 butyric acid methyl ester (abbreviated as PCBM), is one of the frequently investigated materials (Chen et al., 2017a; Chiang and Wu, 2016; Ciro et al., 2017; Jiang et al., 2016; Li et al., 2019). To successfully use PCBM, it is important to take into consideration the issue of the dependence of the PCBM CIL morphology on that of the underlying perovskite, whose roughness may cause the formation of a non-uniformly coated PCBM, layer, which in turn causes problematic non-radiative carrier recombination (Yang et al., 2019). The strategies tackling the issue involved several approaches for engineering the interface between the electrode and PCBM. Modifying the interface reflects on the behavior of carrier along the structure of the cell, starting with the active layer and all the way to the electrode, across the interfacial layer of the cathode. So far the reports indicate that interfacial modification strategies on a base PCBM layer have been indispensable for obtaining planar PSC devices with the desired efficiency in performance. Unfortunately, the scalability of such strategies faces challenges stemming from either the need for processing under vacuum, or their use of costly starting materials.

Herein, we propose a strategy that combines cost-effectiveness and stability of the product, to process triethyl citrate (TEC) via a method that relies on solution processing, without the need for any heat treatment. The obtained material served as a high-efficiency interfacial layer coated on PCBM, for the fabrication of an inverted planar heterojunction perovskite device.

# 2. Experimental

# 2.1. Materials

We purchased triethyl citrate (99%   $  C_{12}H_{20}O_{7}  $  ) from Alfa Aesar, PCBM from Luminescence Technology Cor. Ltd., and   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   powders from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. We used the materials without additional purification.

# 2.2. Device fabrication

We washed the ITO-coated glass substrates in detergent solution,

deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol, for 15 min each, using an ultrasonic bath. We then oven-dried the samples overnight. We spin-coated NiO $ _{x} $ , nickel (II) acetate tetrahydrate (Sigma-Aldrich,  $ \ge $ 99.0%, 50 mg/ml in ethanol) at 4000 rpm on the clean dry substrates, for 45 s, then we annealed the coated substrates at 300  $ ^{\circ} $ C for 1 h. To prepare MAPbI $ _{3} $ , we dissolved PbI $ _{2} $  and CH $ _{3} $ NH $ _{3} $ I (mixed with a molar ratio of 1:1) in a solvent composed of DMSO and DMF, mixed with the volume ratio 1: 4. We spin-coated the precursor solution of perovskite on the surface of the ITO/NiO $ _{x} $  substrate. We coating process continued for 10 s at a speed of 1000 rpm, then another 40 s at a speed of 4000 rpm. The second stage involved a treatment step in which we drop-casted chlorobenzene (CB) on the surface of the substrate. After that, we carried out thermal annealing of the films at 100  $ ^{\circ} $ C for 45 min. After cooling the films down to room temperature, we dissolved PCBM powder into CB with a concentration of 20 mg/ml with different concentrations of triethyl citrate (TEC), and spin-coated the obtained solution on the obtained layers of perovskite, then annealed that film at 100  $ ^{\circ} $ C. We transferred the finished device to a vacuum chamber (10 $ ^{-5} $  Torr), where we carried out the thermal deposition of the Ag electrode with thickness of ca. 80 nm.

Surface potential images were carried out on the same microscope using potential mode. # 3. Results and discussion

Fig. 1a shows an illustration of the inverted perovskite solar devices, and the chemical structure of triethyl citrate (TEC), the interfacial material employed in the present study. Our device configuration consisted of PCBM as the electron extracting layer, and  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  for the role

of the layer transporting holes. From the scanning electron microscopy (SEM) of the cross-sectional layout of the device (Fig. 1b), we estimate the average thickness of the layer of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  perovskite to be around  $ 310~\mathrm{nm} $ . Such thickness guarantees that the device will effectively utilize the incident solar spectrum. The image also shows very smooth layer of  $ \mathrm{NiO_x} $  at the surface, fully covering the film of perovskite. Additionally, a PCBM layer, ca.  $ 37~\mathrm{nm} $  in thickness, is seen covering the whole surface of perovskite. The PCBM layer works to prevent the perovskite film from damage. The PCBM is followed by the TEC coating, which has previously been reported to be of critical importance for achieving ohmic contact (Jiang et al., 2016, 2019; Chen et al., 2016). We employed ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (SKPM) for measuring the work function for a PCBM sample with the layer of TEC, and one without it, to assess the change that the interfacial TEC layer inflicts on PCBM's work function. Fig. 1d shows that the surface potential of PCBM visibly increased to ca.  $ 500\mathrm{mV} $  after the modification using the TEC thin layer. That increase upon TEC spin-coating shows a direct correlation with the reduction in the PCBM@TEC film work function, if we assume macroscopic-scale uniformity in the film surface potential. A previous study using a buffer layer of PVP, related the considerable reduction in the value of the work function to the formation of a surface dipole that causes a cushion-like effect, as one can see from the energy levels available in literature and given by the diagram in Fig. 1c (Li et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2018; Xia et al., 2015; Yang et al., 2019). The diagram shows that such interface dipoles that form at the interfaces between Ag electrode, PCBM, and TEC, generate negative charge in the direction of the PCBM layer, and a correspondingly positive charge in the opposite direction (the metal) (Zhou et al., 2017). Therefore, the built-in potential across the device may be enhanced as the TEC dipole layer forms, stimulating in turn the process electron transfer between PCBM and the Ag electrode (Chen et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2017).

Fig. 2a and b show the curves of the current density plotted versus the voltage (J-V), measured under an illumination of AM1.5G, and Table 1 lists the corresponding photovoltaic parameters. For the highest-performing device with PCBM as CIL, the reverse scan had a  $ 14.56\% $  power conversion efficiency (PCE), a  $ 0.997\mathrm{V} $  open-circuit voltage  $ (\mathrm{V_{oc}}) $ , a  $ 68.07\% $  fill factor (FF), and a  $ 21.46\mathrm{mA}\cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $  short-circuit current density  $ (\mathrm{J_{sc}}) $ . The forward scan, on the other hand, had a PCE of  $ 13.22\% $ . The index of hysteresis, calculated from the formula  $ (\mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{reverse}} - \mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{forward}}) / \mathrm{PCE}_{\mathrm{reverse}} $  is 0.101 (Yang et al., 2019). In contrast, for the PSCs using the PCBM@TEC as CIL, the reverse scan for the highest-performing device had a  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $  of  $ 1.023\mathrm{V} $ , a FF of  $ 76.19\% $ , and a  $ \mathrm{J_{sc}} $  of  $ 22.92\mathrm{mA}\cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $ , translating to a  $ 17.86\% $  PCE. The forward scan delivered a comparable PCE value of  $ 17.79\% $ , and a negligibly small value of 0.004 for the index of hysteresis. The higher performance of the PCBM@TEC-based device, originates from the improvement in the value of  $ \mathrm{V_{oc}} $ , FF, and  $ \mathrm{J_{sc}} $ . This results predominately from the

improvement in the matching in the alignment of the energy bands, and the reduction in the level of defects at the interface between the CIL and the perovskite, as we will address in more detail below. Using the spectra of external quantum efficiency in Fig. 2c, we find that the integrated PCBM-based PCBM@TEC-based Jsc for the PSC devices is 21.15 and  $ 22.4\mathrm{mA}\cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $ . These values are similar to those obtained using the J-versus-V plot. The PCE statistic distribution for PCBM-based and PCBM@TEC-based devices (Fig. 2d) shows considerably improved PCE values. In addition, we evaluated the performance of each PSC versus the TEC concentrations, and plotted the results in Fig. S2 and Table S1.

The performance of perovskite solar cells is boosted with the improvement in the interfacial contact between the films and the top electrode due to the uniformity in morphology. Fig. S4a and 4b show that the formation of crystalline domains with multi-faced sharp structure causes non-uniform coverage as those nanocrystals start to connect in the film of the perovskite, rendering the PCBM coating ineffective. The non-uniform coverage gives rise to voids in the active layer, which in turn causes the device to suffer from a reduction in the resistance of the shunting paths, consequently leading to the reduction in the fill factor. The PCBM/TEC coating (Fig. S4c) fills those voids, improving the smoothness and packing density of the film. The compact structure of films is beneficial to reduce the leaking current, in other words, they decrease the chance for the carriers to recombine (Yang et al., 2019). We performed tapping-mode atomic force microscopy (AFM) imaging, for insight on the influence of TEC interlayer. The results in Fig. S4d-f probe a  $ 5\mu \mathrm{m}\times 5\mu \mathrm{m} $  scan area of the surface of the layer of bare, PCBM-based, and PCBM@TEC-based perovskite. From Fig. S4d, we see that the root mean square roughness (RMS) for the layer of bare perovskite is  $ 10.4\mathrm{nm} $ . From the steady-state photoluminescence spectra (PL) with the diverse CILs-coated perovskite (Fig. 3a), we see that the intensity of PL PCBM@TEC-based perovskite is visibly quenched in comparison with the PL intensity of PCBM-based perovskite, which is indicating the higher charge carrier transfer efficiency at those interfaces. In order to evaluate the bimolecular recombination process of the free electrons and holes, the time-resolved photoluminescence spectra (TRPL) for perovskite are shown in Fig. 3b. The TRPL decay constants  $ (\tau_{1} $  and  $ \tau_{2}) $  are calculated using a bi-exponential equation. Generally, a faster decay time  $ (\tau 1) $  is related to interfacial charge transfer, whereas a slower decay time  $ (\tau_{2}) $  is related to radiative recombination in the bulk of the perovskite (Yang et al., 2019; You et al., 2017). For ITO/NiO $ _{x} $ /Perovskite/PCBM, the faster decay  $ (\tau 1) $  is  $ 8.36~\mathrm{ns} $ . As for ITO/NiO $ _{x} $ /Perovskite/PCBM@TEC,  $ \tau 1 $  is shortened to  $ 7.58~\mathrm{ns} $ . The result suggests the more rapid interfacial charge transfer to be the dominant reason behind the decay in the photoluminescence spectra of the two samples, rendering PCBM@TEC an efficient CIL candidate to boost the performance improvement of these inverted PSC devices.

The current values measured in the dark for PCBM-based and

PCBM@TEC-based devices (Fig. 3c) are similar within lower reverse bias values, but differ considerably as the bias voltage increases, with the PCBM@TEC-based device exhibiting the higher current value. This dissimilarity in the characteristics of dark J-V stems from the corresponding differences in the interlayer modification effects. The curve of PCBM@TEC based device showed a smaller dark current in negative bias than the PCBM based device. This reflects the higher suppression in the rate of carrier recombination at the PCBM@TEC-based perovskite/Ag interface, compared with the case of PCBM-based perovskite, and is in consistence with previously reported results concerning the increase in the shunt resistance (You et al., 2017). The PCBM@TEC-based device also had improved injection current (1.0–2.0 V) during forward bias, and produced better diode behavior, which is consistent with the reduced Rs value that is assumed to originate from the improvement in the interfacial contact that PCBM@TEC-based perovskite has with Ag electrode, due to the existence of the PCBM@TEC interlayer.

In addition, Fig. 3d presented the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy data (EIS) and the equivalent electrical circuit for PCBM-based and PCBM@TEC-based perovskite electrodes. The internal series resistance  $ (\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{s}}) $  arises from the contribution of the resistance to the carrier recombination  $ (\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{rec}}) $  within the thin film of perovskite, and at the interface between the perovskite and the layer that transports electrons, and the contribution from the electrodes sheet resistance  $ (\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{s}}) $ . The  $ \mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{s}} $  in PCBM@TEC-based PSCs is  $ 10.2\Omega \cdot \mathrm{cm}^2 $ , which is lower than the  $ 13.0\Omega \cdot \mathrm{cm}^2 $  obtained for the bare perovskite-based device, which may also be attributed to the above mentioned improvement in the smoothness and compactness, and hence the interfacial contact that the presence of TEC creates between the PCBM@TEC-based perovskite and Ag, and is in agreement with the results seen from the J-V

The enhanced recombination resistance   $  R_{rec}  $   of the PCBM@TEC-based electrode, supports the postulation of reduced carrier recombination in the PCBM@TEC-based electrode, since   $  R_{rec}  $   relates primarily to the perovskite's structure and interfacial layer, and correlates inversely with the ratio of carrier recombination throughout electron transfer (Wang et al., 2018). These results support the suitability of PCBM@TEC as an interfacial layer for planar perovskite solar cell cathodes.

Finally, we evaluated the dark-condition stability of pristine PCBM-based and PCBM@TEC-based unsealed devices without encapsulation, and found the PCBM@TEC-based device to maintain approximately 84% and 95% of its initial PCE after for about 1000 h of storage in the dark, in ambient air (Fig. 4a), or inside a glove box (Fig. 4b), respectively, versus 8% and 30% of the initial PCE being retained by the pristine PCBM-based device, in the same ambient air and glove box conditions, respectively. In addition, we investigated the operational stability (Fig. S5) of the control and target devices without encapsulation aged at maximum power point (MPP) tracking with a continuous one-sun irradiation (AM 1.5 G, 100 mW·cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) in ambient air (relative humidity in the laboratory was ~40%). Compared with the PCBM/Ag device, the PCBM@TEC/Ag device exhibited less PCE loss. Remarkably, the PCBM@TEC/Ag device maintained 98% of the initial PCE, whereas that of the PCBM/Ag device decreased to 87% after continuous operation for 48 h full solar light soaking of non-encapsulated devices in ambient air. This is likely because of the PCBM@TEC cathode-interfacial layer partially encapsulating the perovskite film, by inhibiting ingress of atmosphere and loss of degradation products (Bush et al., 2017; Cao et al., 2020; Lin et al., 2020).

Upon comparing the hydrophobicity of a perovskite film coated with PCBM versus PCBM@TEC (Fig. 4c and d), we see that angle of contact between water and the PCBM@TEC film is  $ 91^{\circ} $ , versus  $ 68^{\circ} $  for the pristine PCBM. The more hydrophobic PCBM@TEC-based film is more resistant to the penetration of moisture through the device, which in turn increases the stability we obtained from the PCBM@TEC-based PSC.

# 4. Conclusion

To summarize, we devised a strategy to produce a seamless PCBM@TEC capped perovskite film for the inverted planar perovskite solar cells, which showed an impressive increase of the efficiency to  $ 17.86\% $ , due to the improvement in the morphology and smoothness of PCBM@TEC CIL, the alignment of the energy levels, and the efficient electron transfer between pervoskite and Ag electrode interface. Non-encapsulated devices based on the more hydrophobic PCBM@TEC CIL enjoyed improved stability over their pristine PCBM counterparts. These improvements in performance and stability highlight the merit of using PCBM@TEC as interfacial layer in planar perovskite solar cells.
