# ABSTRACT

Organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cells have high power conversion efficiency; however, their poor environmental stability is still a problem that needs to be solved. In this study, a tetrabutylammonium cation is introduced into mixed-cation lead a mixed-halide   $  Cs_{0.05}(FA_{0.83}MA_{0.17})_{0.95}Pb(I_{0.83}Br_{0.17})_{3}  $   perovskite as a sacrificial cation to enhance the stability of the corresponding solar cells. Tetrabutylammonium cations located at grain boundaries are beneficial for the formation of two-dimensional perovskite grains perpendicular to the substrate during heat treatment, which can enhance the stability of solar cells against moisture. By optimizing the content of tetrabutylammonium iodide ions, a maximum power conversion efficiency of 12% is achieved for the solar cell with the structure of FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}/m-TiO_{2}/perovskite/carbon  $  . Without encapsulation, the tetrabutylammonium contained perovskite solar cell does not obviously degrade at ambient atmosphere (60% relative humidity and 27 °C) for over 1000 hours. # 1. Introduction

Hybrid inorganic–organic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted significant attention due to their high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and other excellent properties [1–7]. Despite the high efficiency, the long-term stability of PSCs remains to be the biggest hurdle for its potential commercialization [8,9]. In recent years, a lot of research work was focused on the enhancement of the stability of PSCs. Firstly, mixed cations were introduced to enhance the intrinsic stability of the perovskite material. The widely used MA $  ^{+}  $  showed intrinsic instability and poor adaptability to moisture, UV irradiation and high temperature [10–13]. FA-based and Cs-based perovskite materials have better thermal stability [14–17]. Saliba et al. [18,19] developed complicated triple-and quadruple-cation perovskite compositions of Cs $  _{0.05}  $ (MA $  _{0.17}  $ FA $  _{0.83}  $ ) $  _{0.95}  $ Pb(I $  _{0.83}  $ Br $  _{0.17}  $ ) $  _{3}  $  and Rb $  _{0.05}  $ [Cs $  _{0.05}  $ (MA $  _{0.17}  $ FA $  _{0.83}  $ ) $  _{0.95}  $ ] $  _{0.95}  $ Pb(I $  _{0.83}  $ Br $  _{0.17}  $ ) $  _{3}  $  (RbCsMAFA) with improved phase stability. However, Duong et al. [20] put forward that the Rb in perovskite segregates when the films are stressed under light and high temperature. Since an inadequate materials interplay between the 3D methylammonium lead halide compositions which are prone to heat, moisture and light-induced degradation, they also show poor stability [21–24].

Secondly, the development of low dimensional perovskite materials is an

effective way to improve the long-term stability of PSCs [25–27]. On the one hand, 2D, 1D or even 0D perovskites are fundamentally more stable owing to the van der Waals interactions of the spacer cations [28,29]. On the other hand, the large organic cation helps to block the moisture, oxygen and ion migration [30,31]. Wei et al. [32] reported a series of 2D Ruddlesden–Popper perovskite materials that comprise cyclohexane methylamine (CMA) as a spacer cation. They prepared   $  (\mathrm{CMA})_{2}(\mathrm{MA})_{8}\mathrm{Pb}_{9}\mathrm{I}_{28}  $   perovskite solar cell with a long-term stability for 4600 h in ambient air with a relative humidity of 40–70%. Zheng et al. [33] introduced   $  NH_{4}^{+}  $  into   $  (\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3})_{0.85}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})_{0.15}  $   to form a novel 2D-3D mixed structure. They designed and prepared the hybrid perovskite materials of   $  [(\mathrm{NH}_{4})_{2.4}(\mathrm{FA})_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_{n}\mathrm{I}_{3n+1.4}]_{0.85}  $   (  $  MAPbBr_{3})_{0.15}  $   (n=3, 5, 7, 9 and 11) and the unsealed PSCs maintained 90% of their initial PCE when they were aged at high humidity conditions over 1000 h. Lai et al. [34] and Jiang et al. [35] demonstrated that 2-thiophenemethylammonium (ThMA  $  ^{+}  $  ) and phenethylammonium iodide (PEAI) contribute to the high stability of PSCs, respectively. So far, a series of large organic cations have been explored, including n-butylammonium (BA  $  ^{+}  $  ), guanidinium (Gua), iso-butylammonium (isoBA  $  ^{+}  $  ), iodoethylammonium (IEA  $  ^{+}  $  ), cyclohexylmethylammonium (CHMA  $  ^{+}  $  ), propylphenylammonium (PPA  $  ^{+}  $  ), ethylenediammonium (EdA  $  ^{2+}  $  ), butyldiammonium (BdA  $  ^{2+}  $  ), hexyldiammonium (HdA  $  ^{2+}  $  ), octyldiammonium (OdA  $  ^{2+}  $  ), polyethylenimine (PEI  $  ^{+}  $  ), 5-ammoniumvaleric acid (5-AVA  $  ^{+}  $  ), and so on [29,36–40]. However, 2D perovskite materials generally show relatively lower photovoltaic performance than 3D perovskite materials.

Therefore, the challenge for 2D perovskite solar cells is to enhance their efficiency. Complicated triple-cation perovskite materials have confirmed high thermal stability and 2D perovskite materials have demonstrated high moisture stability. Therefore, the stability of perovskite materials can be greatly improved by the 2D-3D mixed perovskite structure [41–43].

Thirdly, carbon electrodes were often used to enhance the stability of PSCs. Recently, carbon electrodes have been successfully demonstrated in a variety of perovskite solar cells and show excellent stability. For instance, Sheng et al. [44] introduced mixed cation   $  (5-\mathrm{AVA})_{0.034}\mathrm{MA}_{0.966}\mathrm{PbI}_{2.95}(\mathrm{BF}_{4})_{0.05}  $   perovskite to fabricate high-performance carbon electrode based PSCs. Wu et al. [45] combined thermally stable FA/Cs-based perovskite and a hydrophobic carbon electrode to successfully fabricate stable PSCs, which maintained on average 77% of the initial efficiency after being aged for 192 h under the conditions of 85 °C and 85% relative humidity without encapsulation. Meng et al. [14] designed an all-inorganic perovskite solar cell based on an ITO/  $  SnO_{2}/CsPbI_{2}Br  $  /carbon structure with an efficiency of over 10% with remarkable thermal stability. Nevertheless, there is few reports on large organic cations introduced into triple-cation perovskite solar cells with carbon electrodes.

In this paper, we fabricate mixed-cation PSCs based on carbon electrodes. The cell structure is  $ \mathrm{FTO / c - TiO_2 / m - TiO_2 / Cs_{0.05}(FA_{0.83}MA_{0.17})_{0.95}Pb(I_{0.83}Br_{0.17})_3 / carbon} $ . To improve the stability of the perovskite material, an organic amine tetrabutylammonium  $ (\mathrm{TBA^{+}}) $ , which contains four alkyl chains with t (tolerance factor)  $ >1 $ , was introduced into the perovskite precursor solution. The introduction of

TBA results in the formation of a 2D-3D mixed structure during the subsequent thermal treatment. The best PCE of  $ 12\% $  was achieved by optimizing the ratio of TBA in the perovskite films. In addition, under the synergistic effect of carbon electrodes and the 2D-3D perovskite structure, the stability of HTM-free carbon-based PSCs were significantly improved. We find that PSCs with a TBA cation located at the grain boundaries can retain over  $ 95\% $  of its initial efficiency after 1000 hours of storage in air without encapsulation, and it can maintain over  $ 80\% $  of its initial efficiency after 500 hours in  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  and in dry air without encapsulation.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1 Preparation of carbon paste

Carbon paste was prepared by mixing  $ 2\mathrm{g} $  carbon black powders,  $ 6\mathrm{g} $  graphite powders,  $ 1\mathrm{g}50\mathrm{nm} $   $ \mathrm{ZrO_2} $  nanopowders,  $ 1\mathrm{g} $  ethyl cellulose and  $ 30~\mathrm{mL} $  terpineol.

# 2.2 Preparation of the substrate

FTO (7 Ω/sq) glasses were purchased from Asahi Glass Company (AGC). Before spin coating different layers, the glasses were cleaned by sonication in the cleaning agent, deionized water, acetone and ethanol. Then, the glasses were treated by ultraviolet-ozone (UV- $  O_{3}  $ ) for 15 min. The compact  $  TiO_{2}  $  layers were deposited on the cleaned FTO glasses by home-made  $  TiO_{2}  $  sol at 3000 rpm for 20 s. After sintering at 510 °C for 30 min, the substrates were treated with 80 mM of  $  TiCl_{4}  $  solution at 70 °C for 1 h and then rinsed with deionized water and ethanol, respectively. After drying and sintering at 510 °C for 30 min, the  $  TiO_{2}  $  mesoporous layer was deposited onto the compact layer by spin-coating  $  TiO_{2}  $  paste (diluted by ethanol with the weight ratio of

1:3) at 3000 rpm for 20 s, followed by sintering at 510 °C for 30 min. At last, the substrate was cleaned by UV-  $  O_{3}  $  treatment for 15 min to remove the organic residues.

# 2.3 Fabrication of carbon-based perovskite solar cells

The FA/MA precursor solution was firstly prepared by dissolving FAI (1M),   $  PbI_{2}  $   (1.1 M), MABr (0.2 M), and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   (0.2 M) in a mixed solvent of anhydrous DMF/DMSO (  $  V_{DMF}  $  :   $  V_{DMSO}=4:1  $  ), respectively. Then, the FA/MA precursor solution was stirred overnight. An appropriate amount of CsI and TBA-I solution (1.5 M of CsI in DMSO and 1.5 M of TBA-I in DMSO) was separately added to the FA/MA precursor solution. The amount of substance of the added TBA was 0, 0.0125, 0.025, 0.05, 0.075, and 0.1 of the total amount of FA and MA cations. For instance, TBA0.0125 represents that the amount of added TBA is 1.25% of the total amount of FA and MA cations. The TBA/Cs/FA/MA perovskite precursor solution was vigorously stirred with 810 rpm at 65 °C for 1 h. The mixed perovskite films were spun at 0 rpm for 5 s, 1200 rpm for 12 s, and then 6000 rpm for 20 s. At the 15  $  ^{th}  $   second before the end of spin-coating, 500 μL chlorobenzene was dropped onto the films, and finally the substrates were annealed at 100 °C for 1 h. After that, a 32 μm thick carbon electrode was prepared by a screen-printing method on the perovskite film and was solidified by thermal treatment at 100 °C. In detail, the carbon paste was screen-printed on the perovskite film and then heated at 100 °C for 5 min to evaporate terpineol and solidify the carbon layer. After cooling to room temperature, another layer of carbon paste was screen-printed and heated again. After several repetitions, a carbon electrode of approximately 32 μm was prepared.

# 2.4 Characterizations

X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements were completed on the form of thin films by Ultima IV X-ray diffractometer using Cu Kα radiation under the operation of 40 kV and 40 mA at room temperature. The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of PSCs were recorded by a field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM: FEI, Magellan 400). Ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) absorption spectra were measured using Shimadzu UV-2550PC spectrometer. For solar cell device, J-V curves were recorded under a simulated AM 1.5 sunlight at 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  with a Yokogawa-7563 source meter in conjunction with a solar simulator (YSS-150A, Japan), and the mask area was 0.07 cm  $  ^{2}  $ .

# 3. Results and discussion

A series of large molecules (R-NH $ ^{3+} $ ) were used to fabricate 2D/2D-3D perovskite materials, such as BA, PEA, etc. In this research, TBA was introduced into the perovskite precursor solution. When the perovskite film is heated, the short-chain organic cations will be first volatilized from the perovskite film. Compared with the cations mentioned above, TBA is a large-sized alkylamine cation with low volatility. Under the same conditions, TBA has a slower volatilization rate from the perovskite film than that of BA and PEA. The retained large-sized cations was fabricated to form the 2D structured perovskite film to decrease the entropy of the whole film system [46]. The 2D structure was arranged along the 3D grain boundaries to obtain the lowest energy and maximum stability. On the other hand, TBA has a long carbon chain, which is more hydrophobic. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of perovskite film on FTO/TiO $ _2 $  substrates were measured, which are shown in Fig. 2(a). The sharp peaks located at 14.12°, 20.04°, 24.5°, 28.50°, 31.92°, 35.01°, 40.70°, and 43.32° correspond to (110), (112), (202), (220), (310), (312), (224) and (314) crystal planes of the perovskite structure, respectively. Strong diffraction peaks at 26.51° and 37.83° are attributed to the FTO substrate. The intensity of the diffraction peaks of the films with different TBA ratios is different. Compared with the film without TBA (TBA0), the diffraction peak intensity of (110) increases when the content of TBA increases, and the diffraction intensity of the (112), (310) and (224) peaks increases first and then decreases. These results indicated that the crystallinity of the perovskite film was improved with the introduction of TBA. Therefore, in the following studies, the representative components of the TBA0.0125 film and TBA0.025 films will be further investigated based on the film morphology and the cell performance. Ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) absorption spectra were measured to analyze the change in optical properties of the perovskite films as a function of the TBA content. As shown in Fig. 2(b), the absorbance of the TBA cation blended perovskite film did not show obvious blue or red shifts in the visible spectra, and only a slight difference in absorption strength can be observed.

# 4. Conclusions

In summary, an effective method based on the introduction of TBA ions into Cs/FA/MA halide perovskite through a multistep spin coating method has been developed for PSCs with high stability, combined with carbon electrodes instead of metal electrode. An optimal PCE of  $ 12\% $  is obtained for  $ \mathrm{FTO / c - TiO_2 / m - TiO_2 / Cs_{0.05}(FA_{0.83}MA_{0.17})_{0.95}Pb(I_{0.83}Br_{0.17})_3} $  /carbon PSC under one sun illumination. PSCs with TBA addition performs well in terms of thermal and humidity stability. Under heating conditions, a 2D perovskite structure can be formed at the grain boundaries because of the sacrificial effect of large TBA cations, while in humid conditions, TBA cation can block water molecules because of its hydrophobic property. Additionally, combined with the carbon electrode, the perovskite solar cell
