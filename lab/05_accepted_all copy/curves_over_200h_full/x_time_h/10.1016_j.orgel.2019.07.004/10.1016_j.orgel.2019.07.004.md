# Abstract

Carbon-based perovskite solar cells aroused huge attention for their simple, low-cost device fabrication processing and excellent stability. However, the fully low-temperature preparation and flexibility of carbon-based perovskite solar cells remain huge challenges. Here we demonstrate the fabrication of carbon-based perovskite solar cells processed at fully low-temperature, which take thermally evaporated cadmium sulfide as efficient electron transport layer, and obtain an optimal power conversion efficiency of  $ 13.22\% $ . With the decoration of PCBM, the separation of charges at the interface is facilitated and the recombination is hindered, so a higher fill factor is obtained and the efficiency is further improved to  $ 14.28\% $ . # 1. Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are known as one of the best potential photovoltaic technology for their striking power conversion efficiency (PCE), simple fabrication and low manufacture cost [1-4]. PSCs have witnessed tremendous progress with PCE skyrocketed broadly from  $ 3.8\% $  to  $ 23.7\% $  during the last decade [5-14], which outperform all the other emerging photovoltaic devices, e.g. quantum dot solar cells, organic solar cells, dye-sensitized solar cells, etc. However, PSCs' stability should be improved before being commercialized. Carbon-based PSCs are considered with the best potential for improving the stability due to the water-resistant, inert to ion migration (which originates from perovskite and metal electrodes) and inherently stable carbon counter electrode [15, 16]. According to the device structure, carbon-based PSCs can be primarily classified into two types, namely 1) mesoscopic carbon-based PSCs and 2) low-temperature printable carbon-based PSCs. The mesoscopic carbon-based PSCs were initially developed by Han's group in 2013 [17, 18]. After years of hard work, the efficiency has increased to  $ 17\% $  [19-22]. Low-temperature printable carbon-based PSCs are prepared by firstly depositing a

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

perovskite layer and then printing a carbon electrode with a pre-prepared carbon paste, which also arouse huge attention from researchers [23-27]. Thus far, the best printable carbon-based PSCs have reached an efficiency up to 19.2% [28], which is comparable to the traditional gold electrode-based PSCs. However, both of these carbon-based PSCs need high-temperature annealing processes, and the report of fully low-temperature processed carbon-based PSCs is rare, which limits their application on flexible plastic substrates. In particular, for the mesoscopic carbon-based PSCs, almost all the functional layers (e.g.   $  ZrO_{2}  $   mesoscopic layer and mesoscopic carbon layer,   $  TiO_{2}  $   mesoscopic layer,   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer) require an annealing process at high temperature (above 400 °C) to form the mesoscopic scaffold for perovskite filling. For the low-temperature printable carbon-based PSCs, only the electron transport layer (ETL) requires a 500 °C annealing process to be better crystallized. Accordingly, to prepare the fully low-temperature processed and flexible carbon-based PSCs, the high-temperature processed ETL should be replaced by a low-temperature processed ETL if we choose the printable carbon-based PSCs as our device structure.

The common ETL,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $ , has been reported to have low-temperature preparation methods. However, owing to the large charge barrier and low electron mobility (<1 cm $ ^2 $ V $ ^{-1} $ s $ ^{-1} $ ), devices based on  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  often suffer from inefficient transfer and huge accumulation of charge at the interface [29]. Besides,  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  owns highly catalytic activity, which will cause serious stability problem when the device is illuminated by ultraviolet (UV) light [30]. ZnO, with a high mobility of 205-300 cm $ ^2 $ V $ ^{-1} $ s $ ^{-1} $ , has been

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

applied as ETL in PSCs by numerous researchers and a highest PCE over 17% was achieved [31-34]. ZnO based PSCs can also be low-temperature processible, nevertheless, the chemically non-inertness of ZnO is a fatal flaw for achieving long-term stability. In recent years,   $  SnO_{2}  $  -based PSCs also got breathtaking achievement with a best PCE up to 21%, due to   $  SnO_{2}  $  's high mobility (  $  240\ cm^{2}V^{-1}s^{-1}  $  ) and transparency [35-38]. But the usual   $  185^{\circ}C  $   annealing process of the   $  SnO_{2}  $   thin film is still damaging for the flexible substrates.

Cadmium sulfide (CdS), a common n-type semiconductor, has aroused much people's attention because of its low-cost, low-temperature processible, good stability and broad use in  $ \mathrm{Cu(In,Ga)Se_2(CIGS),CuZnSnS_4(CZTS)} $  and CdTe solar cells. CdS is reported to have a superior electron mobility  $ (4.66~\mathrm{cm}^2\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1}) $  and a similar conduction band position to  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  [39]. In 2015, Liu and co-workers introduced low-temperature wet chemical processed CdS thin film into the planar PSC as ETL for the first time and achieved a maximum PCE of  $ 11.2\% $  [39], which has been further increase to  $ 15\% $  [40] thereafter. Whereas, cadmium-containing waste during the wet chemical preparation process of CdS would be a hidden danger for ambient environment and people's health. Thus, the approaches of radio-frequency sputtering [41] and ultra-high vacuum thermal evaporation [42] are also reported, but the efficiencies are still unsatisfactory.

Here, a carbon-based PSC processed at fully low temperature (  $  \leq100^{\circ}C  $  ), with the thermally evaporated CdS ETL, mixed-cation lead mixed-halide perovskite absorber, small molecular organic CuPc hole transport layer (HTL) and low-temperature

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

printable carbon counter electrode, is demonstrated. To our knowledge, this is the first time CdS ETL has been introduced into carbon-based PSCs. The prepared films' photoelectric, optical, morphological and structural properties are detailed studied. PCE of the optimal CdS ETL based device can reach 13.22%. Combined with the decoration of PCBM, the efficiency of our optimal planar PSC is further improved to 14.28%. Flexible devices with an optimal PCE over 9% and considerable mechanical stability are also obtained. The results here reveal that CdS can be an excellent alternative ETL material to prepare efficient, stable and flexible carbon-based PSCs.

# 2. Experimental

2.1. Materials: The fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) substrate with a 14 Ω/□ sheet resistance (NSG-14) were provided by Nippon Sheet Glass Co. Ltd. in Japan. Anhydrous ethanol and acetone were provided by Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. The anhydrous chlorobenzene (ACS, 99.5%) was provided by Alfa Aesar. The N,N-Dimethylformamide (≥99.9%), dimethyl sulfoxide (ACS, 99.9%), Copper(II) phthalocyanine (99%) and cadmium sulfide (≥99.999%) were provided by Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co. Ltd. All the perovskite materials (e.g. FAI, MABr, PbI₂, PbBr₂, CsI and PCBM) were provided by Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. The low-temperature processed commercial carbon paste was provided by Shenzhen DongDaLai Chemical Co., Ltd. Poly(dimethyl siloxane) (PDMS, Sylgard 184 elastomer kit) and curing agent were provided by Dow Corning (Midland, MI, USA). All materials were employed without being further purified.

2.2. Device Fabrication: By successively using acetone, ethanol and deionized water, we first ultrasonically washed the laser patterned FTO substrates. After further treated by UV-ozone for another 30 min, the CdS ETL was deposited on the substrate by thermal evaporation (vacuum degree  $ <1 \times 10^{-3} $  Pa). The film's deposition rate and thickness were detected using an INFICON SQM-160 quartz crystal monitor. For the flexible devices, the indium tin oxide (ITO)/PEN flexible substrates were fixed on the PDMS coated quartz glass rigid substrates before the deposition of CdS ETL. To be specific, PDMS and curing agent were mixed at a weight ratio of 10:1. Subsequently, the solution was continuously vacuumized in a vacuum drying oven for several times for the removal of bubbles in the PDMS solution. Afterwards, the PDMS mixed solution was spin coated on the quartz glass and baked at 110°C till the entire solidification. The ITO/PEN substrates were adhered to the PDMS surface by Van der Waals forces. The 15 mg/mL PCBM chlorobenzene solution was spin-coated on the CdS film with 2000 rpm spin rate for 30 s and then heat-treated at 100°C for 5 min. The formula of the perovskite precursor can be found anywhere. In brief, the mixed-cation lead mixed-halide perovskite solution was prepared by dissolving 215 mg FAI, 28 mg MABr, 637 mg PbI $ _2 $  and 101 mg PbBr $ _2 $  in a mixture of 1 ml DMF and 0.25 ml DMSO. Then 65.75 μL1.5 M DMSO solution of CsI was dropped into the upper solution. 60 μL perovskite precursor was deposited by a two-step spin coating process (10 s at 1000 rpm and 20 s at 5000 rpm) with dripping 135 μL chlorobenzene as anti-solvent during the second step, 5 s before the end. The uncrystallized perovskite film was then transferred onto a hot plate for annealing at 100°C for 1 h.

Afterwards,  $ 30~\mathrm{nm} $  thermally evaporated CuPc was deposited above the perovskite film to serve as the hole conductor layer. The final step was to deposit carbon electrode using a doctor blade method and subsequently dried at  $ 100^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 15min.

# 4. Conclusions

The fully low-temperature processed carbon-based PSCs with thermally evaporated CdS ETL, mixed-cation lead mixed-halide perovskite absorber, small molecular organic CuPc HTL and printable carbon counter electrode are demonstrated in the present study. The prepared films' photoelectric, optical, morphological and structural properties are specifically performed. PCE of the optimal CdS ETL based device can reach  $ 13.22\% $ . By combining with the use of PCBM, a higher fill factor can be obtained and the efficiency is further improved to  $ 14.28\% $ . According to the PL studies, the decoration of PCBM can promote the separation and hinder the recombination of charge at the perovskite/ETL interface. Besides, the current leakage measurements and the EIS analysis indeed reveal the superior capabilities of PCBM in facilitating electron extraction and transportation. Our devices also display good

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

long-term durability above 500 hours and excellent ultraviolet stability for 1000 min. Benefiting from the fully low-temperature fabrication process, the carbon-based flexible devices are further developed with an optimal efficiency over  $ 9\% $  and considerable bending proof performance. In summary, these moderate efficient CdS ETL based PSCs with simple, economical architecture and procedures are considered more potential for the roll-to-roll large-area industrial production and can serve as power sources and roof-top photovoltaics for wearable devices.
