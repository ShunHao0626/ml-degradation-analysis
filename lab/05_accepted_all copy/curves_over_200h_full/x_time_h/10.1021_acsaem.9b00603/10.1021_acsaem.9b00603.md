# ABSTRACT

Development of low-cost solution processable inorganic hole-transporting materials (HTM) in n-i-p-perovskite solar cells (PSC) is still a challenge towards stable and cost-effective devices. Here, we report the synthesis, surface functionalization and application of hydrophobic nickel oxide nanocrystals (ho-NiO $ _x $ ) as HTM in planar n-i-p PSC. The morphological and electrical properties of ho-NiO $ _x $  layers were evaluated by atomic force microscopy (AFM) and conductivity measurements, as well as ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) and surface photovoltage (SPV) measurements. Compared to the state-of-the-art Spiro-OMeTAD, our results suggested a better energy band alignment between ho-NiO $ _x $  and (FAPbI $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.78} $ (MAPbBr $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.14} $ (CsPbI $ _3 $ ) $ _{0.08} $  perovskite. Noticeable, ho-NiO $ _x $  base devices exhibit a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 12.71% and stabilized power output (SPO) of 10.99%, the best performance reported so far employing NiO $ _x $  as sole top transport layer. Notably, a low photovoltage suggests that the V $ _{oc} $  could be enhanced by reducing possible recombination paths at perovskite/HTM interface. Moreover, un-encapsulated PSC employing ho-NiO $ _x $  exhibited an outstanding stability under high moisture levels (~65% RH) retaining ~90% of initial PCE after 1008 h of fabrication, paving the way towards scalable and solution processed fully-inorganic blocking layers PSC.

KEYWORDS: Nickel oxide, inorganic hole-transporting material, n-i-p perovskite solar cells, interfaces, stability.

# INTRODUCTION

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on organic-inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskites have become one of the most promising candidates for efficient and cost-effective solar energy since their rapid power conversion efficiency (PCE) increase from 3.8% to ~23% in just one decade. $ ^{1-4} $  Charge collection materials and specifically hole-transporting materials (HTM) have been recognized as a crucial constituent of PSC to achieve the highest PCE especially in n-i-p architecture. However, high performance HTMs are commonly composed of expensive organic conjugated molecules such as octakis(4-methoxyphenyl)-9,9'-spirospirobi[9H-fluorene]- 2,2',7,7'-tetramine (spiro-OMeTAD) $ ^{5} $  or semiconductor polymers $ ^{6,7} $  which limits cost-effectiveness and large-scale fabrication. Moreover, hygroscopic additives are often required to enhance their hole mobility and proper Fermi level alignment, such as lithium and/or cobalt salts, leading to device instability thorough water assisted photoactive material degradation. $ ^{8-11} $  In this framework, inorganic p-type semiconductors have emerged as low-cost, stable and dopant-free alternatives. Nevertheless, the research has been mostly focused on p-i-n architectures employing materials such as CuSCN, Cu $ _{2} $ O, CuO, CuI, CuZnSnS $ _{2} $ , WoO $ _{3} $ , CuInS $ _{2} $  or NiO $ _{x} $ , $ ^{12} $  due to the perovskite incompatibility with a wide range of chemical precursors and deposition processes. Consequently, only few works have investigated inorganic HTM that are compatible with wet deposition methods for low-cost n-i-p architectures, e.g. CuI $ ^{13} $ , CuSCN $ ^{14} $ , Cu $ _{2} $ O $ ^{15} $ , CuS $ ^{16} $ , NiO $ ^{17} $ , graphene oxide $ ^{18} $ , carbon nanotubes (CNTs) $ ^{19} $ , CIGSSe nanocrystals $ ^{20} $  or FeS $ _{2} $  $ ^{21} $ . Unfortunately, , most of these methods require high crystallization temperatures or still need Spiro-OMeTAD as a bilayer, in order to reach relevant PCE. $ ^{16,22,23} $  In addition, some of them

In this work, we synthesize   $  NiO_{x}  $   nanocrystals by an initial water-based methodology and then functionalized them with oleylamine molecules to obtain stable colloidal dispersions in non-polar solvents. Moreover, we found that thanks to the hydrophobic nature of the functionalized   $  NiO_{x}  $  , un-encapsulated cells showed outstanding high-stability under very high levels of relative humidity in air, retaining ~90% of initial PCE after 1008 h.

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The inorganic HTM fabrication consisted in two steps. First, we employed a synthetic route reported by Ciro at al. $ ^{34} $  to obtain NiO $ _{x} $  nanocrystals with hydrophilic groups at the surface (hl-NiO $ _{x} $ ). Second, we carried out a ligand exchange process in a highly concentrated oleylamine solution prepared in chlorobenzene, in order to functionalize the nanocrystals towards a hydrophobic material suitable to disperse in non-polar solvents (ho-NiO $ _{x} $ ). In addition, transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images, presented in Figure 1a, revealed a spherical shape for the NiO $ _{x} $  nanocrystals, with a diameter between 3-8 nm,

This situation can be attributed to oleylamine capping agent which is beneficial in terms of colloidal stability but detrimental for charge transport and interaction between NiO  $  _{x}  $   nanocrystals.  $  ^{12}  $   Additionally, surface photovoltage measurements (SPV) were performed to analyze the contribution of Spiro-OMeTAD and ho-NiO  $  _{x}  $   to hole-carrier extraction. The ITO/SnO  $  _{2}  $  /CsFAMAPbIBr/HTM structure was tested under different illumination intensities. As shown in Figure 2c, SPV is positive for both cases indicating a desirable p-type nature of the two materials and a proper hole collection and transport from the light harvester bulk to the HTM.  $  ^{44}  $   Interestingly, SPV magnitude employing Spiro-OMeTAD and ho-NiO  $  _{x}  $   differs around ~280 mV meaning that a most efficient photogenerated free-charge collection is present at the perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD interface compared to the perovskite/ho-NiO  $  _{x}  $   interface.  $  ^{45}  $
