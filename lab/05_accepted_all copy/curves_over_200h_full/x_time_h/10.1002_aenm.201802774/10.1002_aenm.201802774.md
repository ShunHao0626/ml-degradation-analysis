# Improved Efficiency and Stability of Pb/Sn Binary Perovskite Solar Cells Fabricated by Galvanic Displacement Reaction

Zonglong Zhu, Nan Li, Dongbin Zhao, Liduo Wang, and Alex K.-Y. Jen*

# 1. Introduction

Here, a simple and generally applicable method of fabricating efficient and stable Pb-Sn binary perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) based on a galvanic displacement reaction (GDR) is demonstrated. Different from the commonly used conventional approaches to form perovskite precursor solutions by mixing metal halides and organic halides such as   $  PbI_{2}  $  ,   $  SnI_{2}  $  , MAI, FAI, etc., together, the precursor solutions are formulated by reacting pure Pb-based perovskite precursor solutions with fine Sn metal powders. After the ratios between Pb and Sn are optimized, high PCEs of 15.85% and 18.21% can be achieved for   $  MAPb_{0.4}Sn_{0.6}I_{3}  $   and   $  (FAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3})_{0.85}(MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}Br_{3})_{0.15}  $   based PVSCs, which are the highest PCEs among all values reported to date for Pb-Sn binary PVSCs. Moreover, the GDR perovskite-based PVSCs exhibit significantly improved ambient and thermal stability with encapsulation, which can retain more than 90% of their initial PCEs after being stored in ambient (relative humidity (RH) ≈50%) for 1000 h or being thermal annealed at 80 °C for more than 120 h in ambient conditions. These results demonstrate the advantage of using GDR to prepare tunable bandgap binary perovskites for devices with greatly improved performance and stability.

In spite of demonstrated high efficiencies, the reliability of the binary Pb-Sn PVSCs is still a serious problem that needs to be addressed. The main reason for the instability is the easy oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   to   $  Sn^{4+}  $ , which degrades both the semiconducting properties and film quality of binary perovskites.  $  ^{[15-18]}  $   To alleviate this deficiency, numerous composition engineering or precursor formulations have been attempted, such as the introduction of reducing additives or 2D organic components into perovskite precursor to suppress   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation.  $  ^{[19,20]}  $   For example,   $  SnF_{2}  $   has been used in forming Sn or Pb-Sn binary perovskites to reduce   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation in the solution. However, the existence of   $  SnF_{2}  $   additives will adversely impact the film morphology of Sn-containing perovskite films, which is detrimental to the long-term stability of devices.  $  ^{[21-24]}  $   The modulation of 3D Sn-based perovskite into 2D/3D layered structures can slow down oxygen permeation to retard   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation  $  ^{[25,26]}  $ ; however, the resulting perovskites still suffer from self-oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   to   $  Sn^{0}  $   and   $  Sn^{4+}  $   and the absorbers degrade even under an inert environment.

In this work, we report a simple but effective method of fabricating Pb-Sn binary perovskite films by utilizing a galvanic displacement reaction (GDR). # 2. Preparation of Pb-Sn-Based Perovskite Solution via GDR and the Film Characterization

A typical GDR  $  ^{[27]}  $  is a redox process occurred between metal and metal ions in solution, driven by the difference between redox potentials.  $  ^{[28-30]}  $  Hence, when this method was adopted to prepare binary Pb-Sn precursor solutions, the driving force for the GDR is the difference between the redox potential of  $  Sn^{2+}  $  and  $  Pb^{2+}  $ . The standard redox potentials for the involved materials are given in Equations (1)–(3)  $  ^{[31]}  $

$$
\mathrm{Sn} ^ {2 +} + 2 \mathrm{e} ^ {-} \rightarrow \mathrm{Sn} _ {\left(\mathrm{s}\right)}, \quad E ^ {\ominus} = - 0. 1 4 \mathrm{V}
$$

(1)

$$
\mathrm{Sn} ^ {4 +} + 2 \mathrm{e} ^ {-} \rightarrow \mathrm{Sn} ^ {2 +}, \quad E ^ {\ominus} = 0. 1 5 \mathrm{V}
$$

(2)

$$
\mathrm{Pb} ^ {2 +} + 2 \mathrm{e} ^ {-} \rightarrow \mathrm{Pb} _ {\mathrm{s}}, \quad E ^ {\ominus} = - 0. 1 3 \mathrm{V}
$$

(3)

Based on the above equations, the final balanced equation in the binary Pb-Sn precursor solution should be

$$
\mathrm{Sn} (\mathrm{s}) + \mathrm{Pb} ^ {2 +} \rightarrow \mathrm{Sn} ^ {2 +} + \mathrm{Pb} (\mathrm{s}); \quad E ^ {\ominus} = 0. 0 1 \mathrm{V}, \Delta G <  0
$$

(4)

Therefore, when the Sn powders were added into the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  precursor solution (as depicted in Figure 1a), a suspension is formed initially. The Sn atoms on the metal surface were oxidized by the  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  ions owing to the GDR while the  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  ions were gradually replaced by the  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  ions in the precursor solution. Meanwhile, the Pb atoms could be deposited on the Sn metal surface to form Pb/Sn alloy or aggregating together to form Pb nanoparticles. Note that the reaction will keep the tin ions at the  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  state because further oxidation of  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  to  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{4+} $  needs higher driving force but the electrochemical dynamics between  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  and  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  lack such driving voltage due to the positive  $ \Delta G $  in the equation shown below

$$
\mathrm{Sn} ^ {2 +} + \mathrm{Pb} ^ {2 +} \rightarrow \mathrm{Sn} ^ {4 +} + 2 \mathrm{Pb} (\mathrm{s}) E ^ {\ominus} = - 0. 0 2 \mathrm{V}, \Delta G > 0
$$

(5)

The GDR was performed by stirring the solution at 80 °C for several hours and the reaction mixture was then filtered to remove the solid Pb products and excessive Sn powders from the solution. The detailed experimental procedures are described in the Experimental Section. As shown in Figure 1a, clear solutions could be obtained after different reacting times in forming the MAPb $  _{1-x}  $ Sn $  _{x}  $ I $  _{3}  $  precursor solutions. The clear yellow color of the prepared solutions indicates there was no Sn $  ^{4+}  $  formed during the GDR because the existence of Sn $  ^{4+}  $  will turn the solution into reddish color. $ ^{[32]} $  Subsequently, these precursor solutions were spin-coated onto poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) hole-transporting layer via an anti-solvent dripping process and then annealed at 100 °C to prepare the MAPb $  _{1-x}  $ Sn $  _{x}  $ I $  _{3}  $  perovskite thin films, wherein the x ranges from 0, 0.2, 0.4. to 0.6, as confirmed by the XPS analysis (Table S1, Supporting Information).

Figure 1b shows the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the studied   $  MAPb_{1-x}Sn_{x}I_{3}  $   films. It has been documented that   $  MASnI_{3}  $   and   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   possess pseudo-cubic (P4mm)   $  \alpha  $  -phase and tetragonal (I4cm)   $  \beta  $  -phase crystal structure, respectively, at room temperature (298 K).  $  ^{[6]}  $   The XRD patterns of these as-prepared   $  MAPb_{1-x}Sn_{x}I_{3}  $   films all showed intense characteristic peaks of the perovskite crystal structure. However, these feature peaks gradually decreased as the x increased, indicating the Sn incorporation in the perovskite film would reduce film crystallinity. Notably, the   $  MAPb_{1-x}Sn_{x}I_{3}  $   films with x = 0, 0.2, and 0.4 showed two obvious peaks within the   $  2\theta  $   range of   $  22^{\circ}-25^{\circ}  $   corresponding to the (211) and (202) planes in the tetragonal I4cm space group while there is only one peak observed at   $  \approx24^{\circ}  $   for the   $  MAPb_{0.4}Sn_{0.6}I_{3}  $   film.  $  ^{[6]}  $   This transition can be attributed to the higher symmetry of the pseudo-cubic P4mm space group than the tetragonal I4cm space group.

Shown in Figure 1c are the Tauc plots of these MAPb $ _{1-x} $ Sn $ _x $ I $ _3 $  films that have been derived from the UV–vis absorption spectrum from Figure S1 (Supporting Information). As shown, the band edges of these Sn-incorporated films redshifted compared with the pristine MAPbI $ _3 $  film. The increase of Sn substitution resulted in anomalous band-gap variation with  $ E_g $  shift, which has also been reported previously in the literature. $ ^{[15]} $  The steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy was next conducted to probe the charge dynamics of these MAPb $ _{1-x} $ Sn $ _x $ I $ _3 $  films deposited on the glass substrates. As shown in Figure 1d, the PL peak of the film gradually redshifted as  $ x $  increased, in good agreement with the  $ E_g $  variation observed in Figure 1c. The lowest emission wavelength, corresponding to a band gap of  $ \approx $ 1.23 eV, was observed for the MAPb $ _{0.4} $ Sn $ _{0.6} $ I $ _3 $  film. Presented in Figure 1e are the corresponding time-resolved PL spectra measured by exciting the prepared film with a 470 nm pulsed laser and the PL decay was fitted by the single-exponential decay model. As shown in Table S2 (Supporting Information), the carrier lifetime was 156, 102, and 22 ns for the MAPb $ _{0.8} $ Sn $ _{0.2} $ I $ _3 $ , MAPb $ _{0.6} $ Sn $ _{0.4} $ I $ _3 $ , and MAPb $ _{0.4} $ Sn $ _{0.6} $ I $ _3 $  films, respectively. They are all shorter than the value (203 ns) of the pristine MAPbI $ _3 $  film, suggesting that the Sn incorporation will unavoidably induce the defect density in the film.

# 3. Device Performance

To examine the photovoltaic performance of these binary Pb-Sn perovskite films, inverted p-i-n PVSCs with the device configuration of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/MAPb $ _{1-x} $ Sn $ _x $ I $ _3 $ /PCBM/Bis-C $ _{60} $ /Ag were fabricated. The corresponding energy level diagram of the device is illustrated in Figure 2a. Their resultant photocurrent-voltage (J-V) curves measured under AM 1.5 G irradiation at 100 mW cm $ ^{-2} $  are compared in 2b and the related photovoltaic

parameters are summarized in Table S3 (Supporting Information). As shown, the control MAPbI $ _{3} $  PVSCs showed a decent PCE of 14.15% with an open-circuit voltage ( $ V_{oc} $ ) of 0.96 V, a short-circuit current density ( $ J_{sc} $ ) of 19.20 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and a fill factor (FF) of 76.8 %. It is noteworthy that the photocurrent was apparently raised for the Sn-containing PVSCs and increased with the Sn-containing ratio due to the anomalous  $ E_{g} $  bowing effect as reported in the literature. $ ^{[6,34]} $  The  $ J_{sc} $  for the MAPb $ _{0.8} $ Sn $ _{0.2} $ I $ _{3} $ , MAPb $ _{0.6} $ Sn $ _{0.4} $ I $ _{3} $ , and MAPb $ _{0.4} $ Sn $ _{0.6} $ I $ _{3} $  PVSCs are 22.87, 25.51, and 27.94 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , respectively. The champion device performance is based on the MAPb $ _{0.6} $ Sn $ _{0.4} $ I $ _{3} $  composition, which delivered a PCE of 15.76% with a  $ V_{OC} $  of 0.82 V, a  $ J_{SC} $  of 25.51 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and an FF of 75.60% without showing hysteresis even under a scan rate of 100 mV s $ ^{-1} $  (Figure S3, Supporting Information).

These results confirm the high PCE extracted from  $  J - V  $  curve. The stabilized photocurrent measured under a bias near the maximum power point of the champion device is shown in Figure 2d to confirm the reliability of performance. As shown, a steady  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  of  $  \approx 25.42 \, \mathrm{mA} \, \mathrm{cm}^{-2}  $  corresponding to a PCE of  $  15.1\%  $  was clearly observed. # 4. Stability Test

As the Sn incorporated PVSCs are notoriously prone to oxidation, we have carefully examined the stability of these Sn-containing PVSCs processed through the GDR approach. We first investigate the ambient stability of the perovskite films

fabricated from the solution with or without GDR. All of the films were stored in air with a relative humidity of 40%–50% at room temperature. Presented in Figure 3a are the XRD spectrum and real pictures of the degraded films. As can be seen, the reference   $  MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3}  $   film totally decomposed into a yellow phase after being stored for 15 d whereas the   $  MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3}  $   film prepared by the GDR-derived solution remained intact in black, showing much improved ambient stability. The XRD patterns of those degraded films as a function of stored time in ambient unraveled the possible degradation pathways. Intense   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  SnI_{2}  $   peaks at   $  \approx12.5^{\circ}  $   appeared in the reference   $  MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3}  $   film after 15 d storage in ambient whereas they were absent in the GDR-derived   $  MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3}  $   film after the same period of time and also over 40 d in ambient (shown in Figure S11 of the Supporting Information).

Besides, the XRD peaks broadened in the reference   $  MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3}  $   film, indicating its decreased crystallinity after aging. On the contrary, the GDR-derived   $  MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3}  $   film showed retarded conversion into   $  PbI_{2}  $   yellow phase after being stored in ambient for 15 d.

To investigate the underlying mechanisms of the improved ambient stability in the GDR-derived   $  MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3}  $   film, we have resorted to chemical state analysis and elemental analysis using XPS. Figure 4a presents the Sn states of the reference and GDR-derived   $  MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3}  $   films. As shown, the Sn 3d peak in these films was deconvoluted into   $  Sn^{4+}  $   and   $  Sn^{2+}  $   ions, respectively, and the   $  Sn^{4+}/Sn^{2+}  $   ratio was calculated accordingly as illustrated in Figure 4b. As shown, the GDR-derived   $  MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3}  $   possesses lower   $  Sn^{4+}/Sn^{2+}  $   ratio compared to the reference   $  MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3}  $   film. This indicates that the film prepared by the GDR-derived solution possesses a lower degree of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation, which might play a pivotal role for the enhanced performance observed.

Figure 4a also presents the Pb states of the studied films, where two feature peaks of Pb   $  4f^{7/2}  $   and Pb   $  4f^{5/2}  $   were clearly observed. The Pb   $  4f^{7/2}  $   core level comprises two deconvoluted peaks centered on   $  \approx137.9  $   and 136.3 eV. The more intense one is associated with the   $  Pb^{2+}  $ , while the other one is associated with metallic   $  Pb^{0}  $ , resulting from the decomposition of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $ .  $  ^{[35,36]}  $   Surprisingly, the GDR-derived MAPb  $  _{0.6}  $  Sn  $  _{0.4}  $  I  $  _{3}  $   film revealed

a higher   $  Pb^{0}/Pb^{2+}  $   ratio than the reference film. This might be traced to the fact that more residual   $  Pb^{0}  $   was formed in the GDR-derived solution according to Equation (4). We next compare the   $  Sn^{4+}/Sn^{2+}  $   and   $  Pb^{0}/Pb^{2+}  $   ratios of both films as a function of storage time as plotted in Figure 4b,c. Taking the evidence revealed by device characteristics and XPS spectroscopy, we propose that the residual   $  Pb^{0}  $   in GDR-derived film can further reduce the formed   $  Sn^{4+}  $   back to   $  Sn^{2+}  $   to enhance the film robustness against the oxidation portrayed in Figure 4e. As shown, the   $  Sn^{4+}/Sn^{2+}  $   ratio of the reference   $  MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3}  $   film quickly raised after being exposed to air while the value of the GDR-derived   $  MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3}  $   film remained almost the same. This clearly illustrates the advantage of GDR-derived film in inhibiting   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidization while being stored in ambient. Interestingly, the   $  Pb^{0}/Pb^{2+}  $   ratio of the GDR-derived film gradually reduced after being exposed to air while the value of the reference film only shows slight decrease.

# 5. Conclusion

We have demonstrated a simple and generally applicable approach in preparing stable Pb-Sn binary perovskite films via a GDR. By tuning the reaction time, the   $  MAPb_{x}Sn_{1-x}I_{3}  $   films with different Pb/Sn ratios could be obtained to exhibit improved ambient stability. The PVSCs derived from these films showed a PCE of 15.76%. The GDR method can also be applicable to make other mixed perovskite compositions for PVSCs. The   $  (FAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}I_{3})_{0.85}(MAPb_{0.6}Sn_{0.4}Br_{3})_{0.15}  $   based PVSC showed a promising PCE of 18.21%, which is the highest PCE reported to date for PVSC based on the Pb-Sn binary perovskites. # 6. Experimental Section

Materials: All materials including MAI (98%), FAI (  $  \geq  $  99%, anhydrous), MABr (98%), SnI  $  _{2}  $   (anhydrous, beads, -10 mesh, 99.99% trace metals basis), PbI  $  _{2}  $  (99%), dimethylformamide (DMF) (for high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC),   $  \geq  $  99.9%), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (99.7%), Sn powder (powder, -100 mesh, 99.99% trace metals basis), and phenyl-C  $  _{61}  $  -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) (  $  \geq  $  99.9%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used without further purification except notice.

Solution Preparation: The  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  precursor solution was prepared by dissolving  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{l} $  (MAI) and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  with a molar ratio of  $ 1:1.08\mathrm{M} $  in DMF and DMSO (volume ratio of 4:1). The reference  $ \mathrm{MAPb}_x\mathrm{Sn}_{1 - x}\mathrm{I}_3 $

was prepared with a similar method; except different ratio of   $  PbI_{2}/SnI_{2}  $  , the total molar density of   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  SnI_{2}  $   is 1.08 M. The   $  [HC(NH_{2})_{2}]_{0.85}[CH_{3}NH_{3}]_{0.15}[Pb[0.85Br_{0.15}]_{3}]  $   solution was prepared by mixing   $  HC(NH_{2})_{2}I  $   (FAI),   $  (\mathrm{PbI}_{2}, \mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{NH}_{3}\mathrm{Br}(\mathrm{MABr}), \mathrm{and}\mathrm{PbBr}_{2}  $   in a co-solvent of DMF and DMSO (volume ratio 4:1) with the molar concentration of 1.35 M   $  Pb^{2+}  $     $  (\mathrm{PbI}_{2}  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}  $  , the molar ratio of   $  PbI_{2}/PbBr_{2}=85/15  $  ,   $  PbI_{2}/FAI=1.05  $  , and   $  PbBr_{2}/MABr=1/1  $  ).

The GDR-based perovskite solution was prepared by mixing solid Sn powders into the prepared Pb-based perovskite solutions with a molar ratio of 3:1 between  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  and  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $ . Normally, the solution was kept and stirred at  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ . The  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  concentration would increase with the reaction time. For the typical  $ \mathrm{MAPb}_{0.6}\mathrm{Sn}_{0.4}\mathrm{l}_3 $ , the solution was heated and stirred at  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 3\mathrm{d} $ . The mixed solution was filtered with a  $ 0.45\mu \mathrm{m} $  polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) filter before use.

As reported previously, 15 mg PC  $  _{61}  $  BM was dissolved into 1 mL chloroform and bis-C60 solution was prepared by dissolving 2 mg bis-C  $  _{60}  $  into 1 mL isopropyl alcohol (IPA), followed by sonication for 1 h.  $  ^{[37]}  $
