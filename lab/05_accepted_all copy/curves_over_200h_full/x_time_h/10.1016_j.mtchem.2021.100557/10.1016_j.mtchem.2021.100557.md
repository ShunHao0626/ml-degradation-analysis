# ARTICLEINFO

In this study, we used ytterbium   $  (\mathrm{Yb}^{2+})  $   as a dopant in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   inorganic perovskite thin film and stabilized its black phase. Here, we varied the   $  Yb^{2+}  $   doping concentration in the   $  CsPb_{1-x}Yb_{x}I_{2}Br  $   (x = 0 -0.04) perovskite phase through simple solution method. The optimum concentration of   $  Yb^{2+}  $   showed improved morphology and crystal growth. The fabricated all-inorganic perovskite solar cells (IPVSCs) having   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $  -based champion device showed the highest 15.41% power conversion efficiency (PCE) for a small area of 0.09 cm  $  ^{2}  $   and 14.04% PCE for a large area of 1 × 1 cm  $  ^{2}  $   with excellent reproducibility, which is higher than the controlled   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   device. Detailed photovoltaic analysis revealed that the PCE, open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ), short circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) and fill factor (FF) of the final IPVSC device attributed to the suppressed charge recombination, better film quality, and well growth orientation of the perovskite film. Moreover, the champion   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $   device retains >85% initial efficiency after 280 h under 85 °C thermal annealings. Our results provide a new method to boost the performance of the photovoltaic application.

# 1. Introduction

In recent years, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (HPVSCs) have been attracted more attention due to long diffusion length, tunable bandgap energy, high carrier mobility, low exciton binding energy, high absorption coefficient and highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.5% [1-5]. However, organic cations are still facing enormous challenges to get better long-term stability, which impedes their further marketable application and improvement [6,7]. Furthermore, these organic-inorganic hybrid PVSCs is seriously affected under high humidity and thermal heat [7-9]. Recently, the cesium based fully inorganic perovskites present superior thermal stability [10] compared with organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites [7]. Therefore, all cesium lead halide-based inorganic perovskites have been attracted great interest in the perovskite photovoltaic group [11-13]. Amid,  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_3} $  is considered a capable perovskite material to get the highest PCE because of its solar-friendly band gap of  $ 1.73\mathrm{eV} $  and broadest absorption spectrum [14]. To date, the highest PCE of  $ >20\% $  has been achieved for  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_3} $ -based inorganic perovskite solar cells (IPVSCs)

Whereas, by the suitable quantity of I and Br, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite reveals a twosome of advantages such as reasonable band gaps of 1.91 eV and superior phase stability at room temperature because of the lowered phase transition temperature [16]. The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite-based devices showed higher stability and PCE of   $  >16\%  $   [17,18]. Still, the PCE and stability issues are challenging for the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based IPVSCs.

Many researchers focusing on perovskite absorbing layers   $  [19,20]  $  , electrodes interface   $  [18,21]  $   and device structures   $  [22,23]  $  , and so on, were proposed to enhance the performance of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based IPVSCs. A small quantity of calcium chloride (0.5%   $  CaCl_{2}  $  ) was added to the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   precursor solution as a stabilizer. The   $  CaCl_{2}  $   has a crucial role to increase the crystal quality. Amongst, to get better stability and optoelectronic properties of perovskites, metal ions doping has been proved to be an effective approach because of its effect on crystal growth and passivating defects   $  [25–31]  $  . This partial incorporation facilitating the grain size, passivating grain boundaries, trap states and reducing non-radiative losses [28,30–36]. Particularly, a record PCE of 17.46% was achieved for the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based IPSC using the hot air-assisted method and the   $  In^{3+}  $   doping strategy [29].

Up to now, metal ions doping in all-inorganic perovskite lattice using compositional engineering is stated to be an effective approach to reduce the lattice strain and passivate defects. Nonetheless, the metal ion doping quantity should be accurately controlled. An extra amount of metal ion doping will create unwanted nucleation centers in the perovskite lattice construction, which leads to a degradation of the IPVSC device performance [37]. However, maintaining uniform nucleation sites to achieve high grain size and uniformity by the solvent engineering method is a challenging task. Therefore, though the solvent engineering method has reached high PCE, the method itself is poorly reproducible and restricted to small areas. Hence, the use of an optional anti-solvent-free deposition method is required to synthesis high-quality perovskite. For the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  perovskite deposition, optional methods have been used, but those methods still showed limited PCE. Currently, deposition of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI_2Br} $  perovskite with greater than  $ 1\mu \mathrm{m} $  grain size through the conventional method is not a challenging task but to get enough thickness is challenging [29]. Therefore, we used the anti-solvent free, low cost and easy hot air method to get high-quality perovskite film and increased performance for IPVSCs [38]. The hot air method is an effective method to obtain uniform grain, controls the thickness and well-ordered crystallization that produces stable perovskite films which facilitates the formation of relatively stable orthorhombic  $ \gamma $ -phase perovskite in ambient conditions [38].

Here, we used the hot air method to deposit the high-quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite with various concentrations of   $  Yb^{2+}  $   doping. We varied the concentrations of Yb in   $  CsPb_{1-x}Yb_{x}I_{2}Br  $   (x = 0–0.04) perovskite. The optimal   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $  -based IPVSC device shows a higher PCE of 15.41% with a short circuit current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 15.94 mAcm  $  ^{-2}  $  , open circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) of 1.267 V and a fill factor (FF) of 76.35%, whereas the controlled device revealed a PCE of 12.34%. This work exposes that the   $  Yb^{2+}  $   doping can grow the dense, compact with the pinhole-free surface of the perovskite and, therefore, successfully confine the non-radiative recombination in IPVSCs. Moreover, superior stability is achieved for the champion   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $  -based device without any encapsulation.

# 2. Results and discussion

All inorganic perovskite thin films with various amounts of   $  Yb^{2+}  $   were deposited via the spin coater. For the deposition of the perovskite thin films, we used the hot air method during the spinning as shown in Fig. S1. The hot air method has a substantial influence on the surface coverage of deposited thin films and the thickness of the perovskite samples. When these samples were annealed at 280 °C for 10 min, the perovskite films have converted into dark brownish color (Fig. S1). The 280 °C annealing temperature leads to the formation of black   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film. Fig. S2 shows the time-dependent photographs of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $   perovskite films. From Fig. S2, it is observed that the   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $   perovskite film is highly stable than the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film. Further, we recorded scanning electron microscopic (SEM) images of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ ,   $  CsPb_{0.99}Yb_{0.01}I_{2}Br  $ ,   $  CsPb_{0.98}Yb_{0.02}I_{2}Br  $ ,   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $   and   $  CsPb_{0.96}Yb_{0.04}I_{2}Br  $   perovskite films to analyze the influence of   $  Yb^{2+}  $   doping on the surface morphology, Fig. 1(a–e). For the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film, the pinholes and ununiformed surface morphology were observed, Fig. 1

(a). When we added 1% Yb doping (CsPb $  _{0.99}  $ Yb $  _{0.01}  $ I $  _{2}  $ Br), the decreased rough surface with pinhole-free morphology was observed, Fig. 1 (b). Hence, the Yb $  ^{2+}  $  doping controlling the crystal growth and perovskite morphology. Fig. 1 (c) shows the smooth and uniform surface morphology for the CsPb $  _{0.98}  $ Yb $  _{0.02}  $ I $  _{2}  $ Br perovskite thin film. When we used 3% Yb doping (herein CsPb $  _{0.97}  $ Yb $  _{0.03}  $ I $  _{2}  $ Br) (Fig. 1 (d)) film which exhibits more increased smooth surface, pinhole-free and compact nature of perovskite film, which resulted in fewer grain boundaries. No grain boundaries were found in the perovskite samples that means the Yb $  ^{2+}  $  doping in the CsPbI $  _{2}  $ Br perovskite effectively decreased charge recombination. Furthermore, for the 4% Yb $  ^{2+}  $  doping (CsPb $  _{0.96}  $ Yb $  _{0.04}  $ I $  _{2}  $ Br), the film surface started to decrease the smoothness. Besides, we observed increased roughness and disturbed surface morphology, which indicating multiple nucleation sites for crystal growth at the higher concentration, Fig. 1 (e). Furthermore, the UV–visible analysis was characterized to study the effect of Yb $  ^{2+}  $  on the CsPbI $  _{2}  $ Br perovskite, Fig. 1 (f). From Fig. 1(f), it is observed that the absorption intensity of the CsPb $  _{0.97}  $ Yb $  _{0.03}  $ I $  _{2}  $ Br perovskite film is significantly greater than the CsPbI $  _{2}  $ Br perovskite. The considerable changes have been observed after the Yb $  ^{2+}  $  doping amount enhanced from 1 to 4% in CsPbI $  _{2}  $ Br. With an increase of the Yb $  ^{2+}  $  concentration in the CsPbI $  _{2}  $ Br perovskite, the absorption edges slowly blue shifted. Which is possibly ascribed to the enhancement in the CsPb $  _{0.97}  $ Yb $  _{0.03}  $ I $  _{2}  $ Br perovskite film morphology and crystallinity. Furthermore, we performed the X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis of the controlled CsPbI $  _{2}  $ Br and different concentration of the Yb $  ^{2+}  $  doped CsPbI $  _{2}  $ Br perovskite films to check the influence of the Yb $  ^{2+}  $  doping on its crystal structure, Fig. 2 (a). From the XRD data, it is clear that XRD peaks showed the orthorhombic γ-phase, which is consistent with the previous report [38]. The improved intensity was observed after the Yb $  ^{2+}  $  doping, signifying an increased perovskite crystallinity of the perovskite film. Nevertheless, once the Yb $  ^{2+}  $  concentration increased from 1 to 4%, considerably reduced-intensity has been observed. Fig. 2 (b) exhibits the magnified view of different diffraction surfaces of corresponding samples. Also, the characteristic XRD peaks at 14.6, 20.8 and 29.5 of the Yb $  ^{2+}  $  doped CsPbI $  _{2}  $ Br perovskite films shifted towards a higher 2θ value. These peaks slightly shifted to a higher 2θ value for the Yb $  ^{2+}  $  doping, which suggests that the marginal lattice contraction in the CsPbI $  _{2}  $ Br perovskite phase [39,40]. Because of its suitable ionic radii of Yb $  ^{2+}  $  (0.93 Å), it can be easily replaced with Pb $  ^{2+}  $  (1.20 Å) [41] and, therefore, contributing in its phase stabilization. Also, owing to the smaller ionic radii of the Yb $  ^{2+}  $  dopant, the partial substitution leads to a reduction in the tolerance factor, which increases the phase stability.

To depth understanding of the   $  Yb^{2+}  $   doping effect on the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite, we characterized X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). Full XPS survey spectrum of the   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $   perovskite films as shown in Fig. 2 (c). All elements are well present in the survey spectrum of   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $   perovskite. Fig. 2 (d) reveals the Pb 4f core-level spectra of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $   perovskite films. It is noteworthy that the Pb   $  4f_{7/2}  $   and Pb   $  4f_{5/2}  $   core levels of the   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $   perovskite slightly shifted to lower binding energy than the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite film [38]. These shifts signifying chemical bonding properties have been changed via the   $  Yb^{2+}  $   doping. Comparing the XRD and XPS results, it can be concluded that the   $  Yb^{2+}  $   is effectively doped in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   lattice at the Pb site rather than the physical mixing [41,42]. The presence of the   $  Yb^{2+}  $   doping is confirmed by peaks located at 181.5 and 188.1 eV, which are assigned to the Yb   $  4d_{5/2}  $   and Yb   $  4d_{3/2}  $   core levels, respectively [43–46]. It can be concluded that the   $  Yb^{2+}  $   ions successfully doped in the perovskite lattice, Fig. 2 (e). # 3. Conclusions

In this work, we successfully fabricated IPVSC devices based on different amount of   $  Yb^{2+}  $   doped   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite thin films and utilized as an absorber. The   $  Yb^{2+}  $   doping in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite is a promising approach to obtain high-quality perovskite films. The highest PCE of 15.41% was achieved for the champion   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $   composition, which is much higher than the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ -based device (12.34%). The charge recombination is considerably decreased because of an increased average lifetime and decreased trap states for   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $   perovskite composition, which is advantageous for enhanced performance of the corresponding device. More significantly, the long-term device stability was revealed for   $  CsPb_{0.97}Yb_{0.03}I_{2}Br  $ -based IPVSC device via retaining >85% of its initial PCE at 280 h without encapsulation under 85 °C thermal annealings. This work delivers a favorable and well-designed approach to get efficient and stable IPVSCs. Accordingly, the work exhibits a viable path to further boost the phase stability and electronic properties of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films toward efficient and trustworthy photovoltaic applications.
