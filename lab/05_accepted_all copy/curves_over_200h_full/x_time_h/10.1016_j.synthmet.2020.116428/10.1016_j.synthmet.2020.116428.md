# 1. Introduction

Hybrid organic-inorganic perovskite stand out as foremost light harvesting materials owing to their superior optoelectronic properties   $  [1-10]  $  . Hence, the development of perovskite solar cells (PeSCs) has been flourished and the power conversion efficiency (PCE) has been greatly improved to 25.1 % in the past ten years   $  [11]  $  . For the construction of PeSCs, three main structures have been adopted including mesoporous structure, n-i-p planar structure, and p-i-n planar structure   $  [12-15]  $  . In the early stages of study, mesoporous structure of PeSCs with   $  TiO_{2}  $   layers as electron hole transport layer have beyond 20 % PCE easily. However, the high-temperature processing (  $  >450^{\circ}C  $  ) required to prepare the charge transport layer is unfav reducing the production cost of PeSCs and its application on flexible substrates.   $  [6,16]  $  . Among the alternative device structures, p-i-n planar structure has attracted significant attention for industrial production due to its advantages of low-temperature annealing process (  $  100^{\circ}C  $  ), shorter process time and inconspicuous hysteresis with highest PCE of 21 %   $  [15,17-21]  $  . The hole-transport layer (HTL) plays a pivotal role in the preparation of p-i-n PeSCs, which acts as the p-type layer for affecting the morphology of the upper perovskite layer   $  [22]  $  . Hence, it is important to improve the performance of p-i-n PeSCs via reasonable selection of HTL.

For p-i-n perovskite solar cells (PeSCs), hole transport layer (HTL) plays a key role in its performance since it will affect the crystallization of the perovskite deposited on it. In this work, the insulating polymer polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) was introduced into polytriarylamine (PTAA) matrix as mixed HTL to fabricate PeSCs. The efficiency and stability of PeSCs were significantly improved by employing PMMA as dopant compared to the control device (without PMMA). After optimizing the concentration of PMMA, the champion device achieved a peak power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 14.56\% $   $ 33\% $  higher than the control device  $ (10.92\%) $ . The performance improvement can attribute to the increased hole conductivity of mixed HTL after ultraviolet ozone (UVO) treatment, and the optimized crystallization properties of perovskite. Meanwhile, the mixed HTL has better ability to block moisture from penetrating into the perovskite layer than pure PTAA layer. Hence, PeSCs based mixed HTL could keep good stability, retaining  $ 80\% $  of their initial performance after  $ 700\mathrm{h} $  under ambient conditions in comparison to the control device, which degrade to  $ 64\% $  within the same time.

Since PEDOT:PSS is prone to degrade the PeSCs because of hygroscopic property for relatively better device performance [21]. Compared with PEDOT:PSS, PTAA has huge potential in improving the PCE and prolonging device life since it can facilitate the growth of large-sized perovskite crystal and isolate the moisture into perovskite, respectively [23–26]. However, it is difficult to deposit perovskite precursor on the PTAA film directly via solution-based processes due to the hydrophobicity of PTAA. To alleviate this non-wetting problem of polymer interface, many strategies have been adopted for perovskite growth on non-wetting PTAA, such as surface addition engineering, pre-wetting method, and ultraviolet-ozone (UVO)/oxygen plasma treatment.

For surface addition engineering, Wang et al. employed small organic molecule 4,4',4"-Tris[phenyl(m-tolyl)amino] triphenylamine (m-MTDATA) as surface addition to improve the wettability of the PTAA surface [27]. For the pre-wetting method, Xiong et al. used the pure N,N-dimethyl formamide (DMF) solvent to wet the PTAA film before depositing perovskite layer [28]. As for UVO/oxygen plasma treatment, it is a simple, inexpensive and well-established method, which is usually used as cleaning the surface of inorganic metallic oxide, such as indium tin oxide (ITO) [29–31], NiO [32], ZnO [33] via high energy of

The UVO treatment in our previous work was used to deal with the mixed polymer HTL consisting of PTAA and poly 9, 9-dioctyfluorene-co-benzothiazole (F8BT) to improve the wettability [35]. The larger wettability of hydrophobic polymer film is mainly caused by an increase in the polar part such as -OH, C-O and C=O group after UVO treatment [36,37]. As every con has two sides, the chain scission of PTAA is unfavorable for its conductivity, which is critical to the performance of PeSCs. Hence, it is necessary to find an effective method to suppress the conductivity decay of PTAA caused by UVO treatment while keeping the perovskite deposition smoothly. Since the doping of HTL has been thoroughly investigated in the metal oxide-based HTLs in p-i-n PeSCs [38–40], which could be a facile way to solve the above problem.

Therefore, in this work, we first introduce insulating polymer polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) into the PTAA matrix (P-PTAA) as HTL for fabricating planar p-i-n PeSCs. With the doping of PMMA, the electrical conductivity of P-PTAA HTL has almost no change after UVO treatment in comparison to the control device, which degrades sharply. Besides, the crystallinity of perovskite films upon P-PTAA HTL has also been optimized exhibiting larger grain size for the better hydrophobicity of P-PTAA after UVO treatment. Adopting the appropriate ratio of PMMA, the champion device achieved a peak power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 14.56\% $ ,  $ 33\% $  higher than the control device  $ (10.92\%) $ . Moreover, the PMMA doping can improve the stability of the device, with the PCE of the champion device maintaining over  $ 80\% $  of the initial value after storing the device for over  $ 700\mathrm{h} $ .

# 2. Experimental

Fig. 1 shows the structure of PeSCs (ITO/PTAA or P-PTAA (20 nm)/MAPbI $ _3 $  perovskite layer (300 nm)/ Phenyl-C $ _{61} $ -butyric acid methyl ester (PC $ _{61} $ BM) (40 nm)/ 4,7-diphenyl-110-phenanthroline (Bphen) (3 nm)/Ag (100 nm)). The ITO substrates were sequentially cleaned in an ultrasonic bath with detergent, acetone, deionized water, and isopropanol. After drying in an oven, the substrates were treated with UVO for better wettability. Then, different concentrations (0–10 wt%) of PMMA doped PTAA solutions (5 mg/mL in chlorobenzene) were spin-coated at 3000 rpm for 35 s and annealed at 60 °C for 20 min. The HTL then underwent a UVO exposure of 1 min. Then, for all samples, perovskite films were deposited onto the HTL by a one-step spin-coating method as our previous report [21]. Afterwards, PC $ _{61} $ BM solution (22 mg/mL in chlorobenzene) was spun at 2000 rpm on the perovskite film for 40 s. Finally, the PeSCs were completed by consecutive vacuum deposition of Bphen (3 nm) and Ag cathode (100 nm) using a shadow mask to define the effective device area of 0.02 cm $ ^2 $ . Both the pure PTAA and P-PTAA films are   $  \sim 20 \, nm  $   thick, which is verified by the cross-section SEM. Hence the corresponding conductivity of PTAA and P-PTAA HTLs is summarized as Table 1 and Table S1. Before UVO treatment, the conductivity of HTL decreases gradually as the concentration of PMMA increases owing to the insulating property of PMMA. To deposit perovskite films on the hydrophobic PTAA and P-PTAA films, UVO treatment was adopted to modify the surface energy. When the PTAA and P-PTAA based HTL are exposed to the UVO environment, their electrical conductivity all shows reduction, especially for the pure PTAA film, which drops sharply from   $  4.24 \times 10^{-7} \, S \, cm^{-1}  $   to   $  1.62 \times 10^{-7} \, S \, cm^{-1}  $   since the serious structural degradation of PTAA due to the high energy of the UV light. However, the P-PTAA films can still maintain higher conductivity compared to the pure PTAA film after UVO treatment. For example, the electrical conductivity of 3 wt% P-PTAA drops slightly from   $  2.13 \times 10^{-7} \, S \, cm^{-1}  $   to   $  2.05 \times 10^{-7}  $  , which is the highest value compared to the other samples. According to the above description, we can see the P-PTAA-based HTL has higher conductivity compared to the pristine samples after UVO treatment, which will decrease the resistance leading to the enachnement of charge transport. Besides, as depicts in Fig. 2(c)-(d), Atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurement is adopted to study the effect of PMMA doping on the morphology of HTL. The root-mean-square (RMS) roughness of pure PTAA film is 1.314 nm. And the RMS roughness of P-PTAA shows a slight increase to 1.324 nm. Hence, both the polymer films show the smooth interface, which is conducive to the growth of perovskite.

The J-V hysteresis is also an important parameter of solar cells. Hence, we also evaluated the hysteresis behaviors of PeSCs with pure PTAA and P-PTAA HTLs as shown in Fig. 3(c). As we can see, the PeSC with P-PTAA HTL shows more negligible hysteresis than pure PTAA devices. In addition, maximum steady-state photocurrent out is carried out to test the photovoltaic efficiency in a steady-state, which is measured under the maximum power point voltage (0.78 V) as shown in Fig. 3(d). The photocurrents of PeSCs with pure PTAA and P-PTAA HTLs rise directly to steady state under illumination of AM 1.5 G then stay steady for the next 200 s of light exposure at ambient condition. The PTAA-based and P-PTAA based PeSCs both exhibit a steady-state current density of 14.37 and  $ 18.52\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ , respectively. These results are also corresponding to the J-V curves.

In order to examine the efficiency of hole extraction and transport between perovskites and the two types of HTLs, steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence were measured as shown in Fig. 5(c)-(d). The spectra of the perovskite films on pure PTAA and P-PTAA HTLs exhibited an emission peak at  $ 769\mathrm{nm} $  and  $ 772\mathrm{nm} $  which is typical of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ , which is in accordance with the reports that that small grain size polycrystalline films have a blue-shifted PL peak compared to large grain polycrystalline films [49]. Besides, it is found that an obvious quenching effect was observed when the perovskite layer was deposited on the P-PTAA HTL, indicating the mix polymer blend facilitated hole extraction and transport from perovskite to the HTL [13]. To monitor the charge carrier dynamics of perovskite/HTL interfaces, the TRPL of perovskite films upon pure PTAA and P-PTAA have been measured and shown in Fig. 5(d). The PL decay lifetime of perovskite film on P-PTAA HTL is shorter than that of perovskite on PTAA layer, which indicates that the P-PTAA HTL has a stronger ability to extract holes from perovskite layer than the pristine PTAA HTL since carrier lifetime can represent the carrier extraction capability of HTL [43]. These results also explain why the the PeSCs with P-PTAA HTL discussed above have

negligible hysteresis behavior.

In addition to the enhancement of efficiency, the P-PTAA HTL also improves the stability of PeSCs. The J-V curves of PeSCs with PTAA and P-PTAA HTLs before-and-after air storage are shown in Fig. 6(b)-(c) and the photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table 3. Wherein, PCE of the PeSCs with pure PTAA and P-PTAA HTLs as a function of storage time in ambient air are shown in Fig. 6(a). After  $ 700\mathrm{h} $  of exposure to air, the relative efficiency of the PeSC with the pure PTAA HTLs degraded to  $ 64.2\% $  versus initial value, whereas the device with P-PTAA HTL maintained the relative efficiency of  $ 81.7\% $ . We attributed this result to the fact that the perovskite layer deposited on P-PTAA with larger grain size and lower surface trap, which could provide effective moisture protection [50,51].
