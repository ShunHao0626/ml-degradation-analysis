Abstract: The defect in perovskite film is one of the most non-negligible factors that can attenuate the performances of perovskite solar cell. This work fabricates defects-reduced perovskite film by using the lead indicator (dithizone) as an additive of perovskite functional layer. The dithizone can retard the crystallization rate of perovskite films, passivate the defects, and enhance the structure stability of perovskite by coordinating with lead atoms. As a result, the device doped with dithizone yields outstanding power conversion efficiency and stability.

Keywords: dithizone; additive; defects-reduced; perovskite solar cell

# 1. Introduction

The perovskite solar cell (PSC) has achieved a record-breaking power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.2%,  $  ^{[1]}  $  which benefits from the tunable band gap, high absorption coefficient and carrier mobility of perovskite.  $  ^{[2]}  $  Even though the PSC has achieved the excellent PCE, the stability is still one of the most critical issues for its industrialization and commercialization.  $  ^{[3]}  $  As we all know, the PCE and stability of devices are closely related to the crystallization quality of perovskite films.  $  ^{[4,5]}  $  However, the defects are always ineluctably introduced into the perovskite films due to the rapid crystallization process and the complex crystallization environment.  $  ^{[6,7]}  $  And these defects are always the source of recombination and the cause of device performances degradation.  $  ^{[6,7]}  $  Therefore, improving the crystallization quality and reducing the defects of perovskite films are requisite for highly efficient and stable devices.

Some approaches have been used to improve the crystallization quality and reduce the defects of perovskite films: such as regulating the precursor compositions,  $  ^{[8-10]}  $  controlling the crystallization environment (including humidity and annealing temperature),  $  ^{[11,12]}  $  post-processing with the regents that can accelerate the dissolve and recrystallization of crystallized perovskite,  $  ^{[13-15]}  $  and regulating the interfaces to increase the nucleation density.  $  ^{[16]}  $  Besides, applying additives can also reduce the defects by regulating the crystallization kinetics process or passivate the defects effectively.  $  ^{[17-19]}  $  And the common additives are the polymers or small organic molecules with hybrid atoms, such as sulfur (S), oxygen (O), or nitrogen (N)

atoms.  $  ^{[17-19]}  $   The lone pair electrons of hybrid atoms make it possible to chelate with Pb atoms to produce intermediate complex, which is conducive to reduce the defects by regulating the crystallization rate. Therefore, the introduction of additives can reduce the recombination and enhance the comprehensive device performances further.  $  ^{[17-19]}  $   Thus, choosing a suitable additive is vital to reduce the defects.

As a typical lead indicator, dithizone (DTZ) can coordinate with Pb along with a color change from green to red, which originates from the hybrid atoms in DTZ can act as electron donors for Pb empty orbital.  $  ^{[20]}  $   And because of this property, DTZ has been widely used for lead content determination and heavy metal elements treatment in sewage.  $  ^{[21-23]}  $   Therefore, DTZ has the potential to be an additive of PSC.

In this work, DTZ plays roles by adding it into the   $  PbI_{2}  $   precursor solution. The chelating action of DTZ and Pb is conducive to slow down the crystallization rate of perovskite, passivate the defects, and reduce the recombination. The fourier transform infrared spectra (FTIR), X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), and theoretical and experimental ultraviolet-visible absorption spectra (UV-vis), were used to study the coordination mode between DTZ and Pb; The field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM), state photoluminescence (PL), time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL), and space-charge-limited current (SCLC) were measured to interpret the effects of DTZ on carrier transport dynamics. The device with DTZ additive yields enhanced PCE and stability.

In order to confirm the final coordination pattern between DTZ and   $  PbI_{2}  $  , we optimized and obtained four possible coordination structures by theoretical computational chemistry methods. As shown in the insets of Figure 2 c-f, the coordination patterns between DTZ and   $  PbI_{2}  $   varies with the adding content of DTZ and the types of ligand atoms. And when the DTZ content is relatively small, the DTZ will coordinate with the Pb atom at 1:1 to form complex-1 by the electron donating roles of two N atoms or to form complex-3 by the electron donating roles of one N atom and one S atom in DTZ (the insets of Figure 2 c and 2 e). When the DTZ content is increased, the coordination ratio between DTZ and Pb is going to 2:1. And four N atoms coordinate with a Pb atom in complex-2, while two N and two S atoms chelate with a Pb atom in complex-4 (the insets of Figure 2 d and 2 f). # 3. Conclusions

In this work, we reported an effective DTZ additive for improving the performances of perovskite solar cell. The N atoms in DTZ can coordinate with the Pb in  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  precursor, which retards the transfer rate from  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  to perovskite, improves the morphologies, passivates the defects, decreases the non-radiative recombination, and

And the device stability was enhanced after adding the DTZ. This finding proves that the indicators are viable additives for highly efficient and stable PSCs owing to their abundant hybrid atoms can coordinate with Pb.
