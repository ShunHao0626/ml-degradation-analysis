# One-Source Strategy Boosting Dopant-Free Hole Transporting Layers for Highly Efficient and Stable   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   Perovskite Solar Cells

Xinqi Li, Weijie Chen, Shuhui Wang, Guiying Xu, Shuo Liu, Yaowen Li,* and Yongfang Li

All-inorganic perovskites have emerged as promising photovoltaic materials due to their superior thermal stability compared to their organic–inorganic hybrid counterparts. However, the inferior film quality and doped hole transport layer (HTL) have a strong tendency to degrade the perovskite under high temperatures or harsh operating conditions. To solve these problems, a one-source strategy using the same polymer donor material (PDM) to simultaneously dope   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films via antisolvent engineering and fabricating the HTL is proposed. The doping assists perovskite film growth and forms a top-down gradient distribution, generating   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   with enlarged grain size and reduced defect density. The PDM as the HTL suppresses the energy barrier and forms favorable electrical contacts for hole extraction, and assemble into a fingerprint-like morphology that improves the conductivity, facilitating the creation of a dopant-free HTL. Based on this one-source strategy using PBDB-T as PDM, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite solar cell with a dopant-free HTL achieves a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 16.40%, which is one of the highest PCEs reported among all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs with a dopant-free HTL. Importantly, the devices exhibit the highest thermal stability at 85 °C and operational stability under continuous illumination even with Ag as the top electrode and present good universality.

hybrid pero-SCs  $  ^{[7,8]}  $  because the solution-processed  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  often suffers from poor crystallization stemming from nonuniform nucleation and growth which leads to high radiative recombination and nonradiative charge recombination.  $  ^{[9,10]}  $  Solvent engineering has been widely adopted to control  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  perovskite growth.  $  ^{[11]}  $  The antisolvent method has been proven to quickly remove the residual precursor solvent, triggering homogeneous nucleation and fast deposition-crystallization which facilitate the growth of perovskite films with large, compact grains and smooth surfaces.  $  ^{[12,13]}  $  Still, the  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ -based pero-SCs exhibited inferior  $  V_{oc}  $  and  $  J_{sc}  $ , indicating that numerous defects still exist in the  $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  films. Thus, organic additives  $  ^{[14,15]}  $  and interfacial engineering  $  ^{[16,17]}  $  are synergistically incorporated to passivate the defects in the bulk perovskite and optimize the band alignment between perovskite and the HTL. # 1. Introduction

Lead halide perovskite semiconductors are promising photovoltaic materials due to their excellent optoelectronic properties including their high absorption coefficients, facile tunable bandgaps, and long carrier-diffusion lengths.  $  ^{[1]}  $   All-inorganic perovskites composed of cesium (Cs  $  ^{+}  $  ) instead of volatile organic

components (MA $  ^{+}  $  and FA $  ^{+}  $ ) have attracted extensive attention due to their excellent thermal stability compared with organic–inorganic counterparts. $ ^{[2,3]} $  The black phase ( $  \alpha  $ ) of CsPbI $  _{3}  $  exhibits a favorable bandgap of  $  \approx  $ 1.7 eV, enabling the design of solar cells with power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) exceeding 19%, $ ^{[4]} $  but exhibits an unstable black ( $  \alpha  $ ) phase even at room temperatures due to the intrinsically low Goldschmidt tolerance factor ( $  \tau = 0.81  $ ). $ ^{[5]} $  Cesium lead mixed-halide perovskite, CsPbI $  _{2}  $ Br, with a relatively low iodine content and a reasonable wide bandgap of 1.92 eV exhibits an increased  $  \tau  $  of 0.84 and high  $  \alpha  $  phase stability, offering great potential as a front cell to achieve high-performance tandem solar cells. $ ^{[6]} $  However, the inferior CsPbI $  _{2}  $ Br film quality, poor electronic contact at the perovskite/hole transport layer (HTL) interface, and the lithium salt-doped HTL are still big challenges to improve their efficiency and stability.

Li et al. introduced a polymer donor material (PDM), poly[(2,6-(4,8-bis(5-(2-ethylhexyl)thiophe-2-yl)-benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']dithiophene)]-alt-(5,5-(1',3'-di-2-thienyl-5',7'-bis(2-ethylhexyl)benzo[1',2'-c:4',5'-c']dithiophene-4,8-dione)] (PBDB-T), into the antisolvent chlorobenzene (CB) to trigger heterogeneous nucleation over the perovskite precursor film then attached on the grain boundary in a gradient.  $  ^{[18]}  $   This method was demonstrated to passivate the trap states of the hybrid perovskite film and improve energy level matching and electronic contact at the perovskite/HTL interface. Unfortunately, few studies investigating organic/polymer dopants have been carried out in all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs.

Dopant-free HTLs also play an important role in improving the stability of n–i–p type pero-SCs. Up to now, high-efficiency, all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs have been produced by employing a 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis[N,N-bis(pmethoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) HTL doped with Li-bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (Li-TFSI) and 4-tert-butyl pyridine (tBP).  $  ^{[19,20]}  $   However, under working or heating condition, the hygroscopic and diffusive nature of these dopants degrade device performance despite the thermostability of all-inorganic perovskites. Thus, a dopant-free HTL is crucial for improving pero-SC stability.  $  ^{[21]}  $   Poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT), which is inexpensive and exhibits excellent hole mobility, has been used as an alternative dopant-free hole transport material (HTM).  $  ^{[22,23]}  $   The unencapsulated P3HT-based devices with a   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   active layer maintained ≈95% of their initial PCE after 1300 h of storage in ambient conditions.  $  ^{[24]}  $   Tian et al. used another PDM (PDCBT) with a deeper-lying highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level as a dopant-free HTL in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ -based pero-SCs.  $  ^{[25]}  $   A high photostability with an efficiency drop of less than 10% was realized under continuous 1 sun equivalent illumination for 400 h. Similar behavior was achieved by adopting other hydrophobic PDMs as the HTL, such as PBDB-T-Si,  $  ^{[26]}  $   poly[[5,6-difluoro-2-(2-hexyldecyl)-2H-benzotriazole-4,7-diyl]-2,5-thiophenediyl[4,8-bis[5-(tripropylsilyl)-2-thienyl]benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']dithiophene-2,6-diyl]-2,5-thiophenediyl] (J71),  $  ^{[27]}  $   and poly[(dithieno[3,2-b:2',3'-d]silolethieno[3,4-c]-pyrrole-4,6-dione]-random-(2,2'-bithiophenethieno[3,4-c]-pyrrole-4,6-dione)] (poly(DTSTPD-r-BThTPD)).  $  ^{[28]}  $   Although the dopant-free strategy improves device stability, PCEs still encounter insurmountable bottlenecks toward high values. Therefore, it is crucial to fabricate high-quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films and develop dopant-free HTMs at the same time, and to form good electronic contact between them.

Here, we proposed a one-source strategy using the same PDM to simultaneously act as both the dopant in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and the HTL to prepare efficient and highly stable all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs. The PDM was dissolved in antisolvent to diffuse into the pre-deposited   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film. This PDM acts as a soft template to enable the growth of high-quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   crystals and results in a downshifted Fermi level (  $  E_{F}  $  ) of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ , optimizing band alignment in the device. Additionally, using the same PDM as the HTL strengthens the electrical contact with   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   due to the ultrathin fibrous PDM film on top of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   surface. Meanwhile, during the HTL forming process, the underlying fibrous PDM film induces self-assembly into a highly ordered, fingerprint-like HTL, which significantly increases the conductivity of the

HTL in the absence of dopant. Finally, based on this one-source strategy, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs with dopant-free HTLs achieve a PCE of 16.40%, which is one of the highest PCEs reported for the all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs with dopant-free HTL. The device also exhibited robust stability, maintaining almost 85% of its initial PCE after aging at 85 °C for 500 h without encapsulation, and 81% after almost 1000 h under continuous operation at maximum power point (MPP) irradiating under 1 sun equivalent illumination. More excitingly, the one-source strategy is universally applicable in multiple PDMs, providing a new pathway for fabricating dopant-free HTL all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs with high efficiencies and stabilities.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The precursor   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films were fabricated by the fast deposition-crystallization method.  $  ^{[12]}  $   To assist the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film growth, antisolvent CB with or without various PDMs like PBDB-T, poly[4,8-bis(5-(2-ethylhexyl)-thiophene-2-yl)benzo[1,2-b;4,5-b']dithiophene-2,6-diyl-alt-(4-(2-ethylhexyl)-3-fluorothieno[3,4-b]thiophene)-2-carboxylate2-6-diyl] (PTB7-Th), poly[(2,6-(4,8-bis(5-(2-ethylhexyl-3-fluoro)thiophen-2-yl)-benzo[1,2-b;4,5-b']dithiophene)]-alt-(5,5-(1',3'-di-2-thienyl-5',7'-bis(2-ethylhexyl)benzo[1',2'-c:4',5'-c']dithiophene-4,8-dione)] (PM6), and poly[(2,6-(4,8-bis(5-(2-ethylhexyl-3-chloro)thiophen-2-yl)-benzo[1,2-b;4,5-b']dithiophene)]-alt-(5,5-(1',3'-di-2-thienyl-5',7'-bis(2-ethylhexyl)benzo[1',2'-c:4',5'-c']dithiophene-4,8-dione)] (PM7) (see Figure 1a) was used to treat the perovskite surface. These PDMs containing functional groups with Lewis base characteristic, such as thiophene, could passivate the under-coordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   defects in perovskite films. The HOMO levels of PTB7-Th, PBDB-T, PM6, and PM7, determined by cyclic voltammetry, were calculated to be -5.22, -5.30, -5.48, and -5.51 eV, respectively (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The consistent absorption spectra of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films confirm that the antisolvent with or without PDMs does not affect the crystal lattice (Figure S2, Supporting Information), in agreement with previous work.  $  ^{[14]}  $   To determine the quality of the PDM-treated   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films, cross-sectional and top-view scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images were acquired. As shown in Figure 1b, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film with pure CB antisolvent treatment (control) exhibits disordered grain boundary stacking with random grains. After adding PDM PBDB-T into the antisolvent, the grain boundaries were substantially reduced, and the enlarged grains became perpendicular to the substrate. Based on this observation, we inferred that the PBDB-T PDM acts as a soft template, triggering heterogeneous nucleation over the perovskite precursor film, improving the grain size and directing perovskite growth in a preferred orientation.  $  ^{[29]}  $   As shown in the top-view SEM images, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film exhibits an inhomogeneous morphology consisting of multiscale grains, which is consistent with the cross-sectional SEM images. Interestingly, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film treated with CB containing PBDB-T (  $  CsPbI_{2}Br-PT  $  ) exhibits a fibrous morphology and the grains become blurry. Similar morphologies were also observed by changing PBDB-T to PTB7-Th, PM6, or PM7 (Figure S3, Supporting Information). These behaviors suggest that the PDMs may distribute both in the perovskite film and on top of the perovskite surface.

To determine the effects of incorporating PDMs by antisolvent treatment on device performance, we fabricated pero-SCs with an n–i–p structure of ITO/TiO  $  _{2}  $  /SnO  $  _{2}  $  /CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br/SpiroOMeTAD/MoO  $  _{3}  $  /Ag. As shown in Figure 1e and Table S1, Supporting Information, the PCEs were significantly increased

after incorporating PBDB-T with concentrations in the range of 0.1–2 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  . At a PBDB-T concentration of 0.5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , the pero-SC exhibited the highest PCE of 14.97% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.20 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 15.82 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and a fill factor of 78.81%. Similar device performance was also observed when using other PDMs such as PTB7-Th, PM6, or PM7, and all these devices showed superior PCEs compared with the device without PDM dopants (Figure 1f). Thus, the PDM processing by the antisolvent has a positive effect on device performance due to the improved perovskite quality and charge carrier transport ability. We also tried to use high PDM concentration to passivate the perovskite and act as HTL simultaneously, but the corresponding devices didn't work well. It suggests that a high PDM concentration is detrimental to the crystallization during the antisolvent treatment (Figure S8 and Table S2, Supporting Information).

To evaluate the electronic states at the perovskite interface, the surface potentials of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   films with or without PBDB-T were characterized using a Kelvin probe force microscope.  $  ^{[41]}  $   As shown in Figure 2b and Figure S11, Supporting Information, the average surface potential of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film with PBDB-T dramatically decreased to 705 mV compared to the film processed with pure CB antisolvent (1783 mV). The decreased surface potential of the perovskite film brings it closer to the pristine PBDB-T HTL (326 mV), suggesting an improvement in energy band alignment in the device. The reduced energy barrier facilitates hole extraction at the perovskite/HTL interface.  $  ^{[42]}  $   To further confirm the band structure, the surface electronic properties of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ ,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br-PT  $ , and pristine PBDB-T HTL were characterized by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS), as shown in Figure 2c. The work function of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   increased from 3.74 to 3.84 eV with respect to vacuum after incorporating PBDB-T. The valence band maximum (  $  E_{VB}  $  ) was determined to be -5.54 and -5.36 eV for the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film and the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br-PT  $   film, respectively. The conduction band maximum (  $  E_{CB}  $  ) was estimated by the   $  E_{VB}  $   value and the optical bandgap. As shown in Figure 2d, the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br-PT  $   film shows a downshifted Fermi level (  $  E_{F}  $  ) (-3.84 eV) which is closer to   $  E_{VB}  $   than that of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, which implies more p-type nature for the PBDB-T-processed   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film. The more p-doped perovskite surface may induce a band bending compensation to the increased work function when contacting the HTL, which is beneficial to interfacial charge separation and collection due to the increased built-in potential.  $  ^{[41]}  $   These results indicate that this one-source strategy efficiently improves band alignment, continuous hole channel transport, and electronic contact at the interface.

In addition to fine-tuned electronic states at the perovskite/HTL interface, the generation of high-conductivity HTLs without dopants is another critical issue for obtaining highly stable pero-SCs. As seen from the atomic force microscopy (AFM) topography images (Figure S12, Supporting Information), the PBDB-T HTL deposited on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br-PT  $   film exhibited a smooth morphology with a root mean square (RMS) roughness of 14.33 nm, compared to the control   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film (RMS = 21.75 nm), which is beneficial for the formation of Ohmic contact with electrode. We also observed that the PBDB-T HTL deposited on   $  CsPbI_{2}Br-PT  $   exhibited a highly ordered morphology with a fingerprint-like texture. We hypothesize that this self-assembled fingerprint-like texture is induced by the underlying fibrous PBDB-T formed during the antisolvent process through weak molecular inter-

actions.  $  ^{[43]}  $   As seen from the cross-sectional SEM image, these fingerprint-like PBDB-T HTLs compactly covered the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br-PT  $   surface, forming a 60-nm-thick HTL (Figure 3a). This HTL may effectively prevent   $  H_{2}O/O_{2}  $   from permeating into the film, enhancing device stability. Conductive atomic force microscopy (C-AFM) (Figure 3b) was carried out to evaluate the conductivities of the PBDB-T HTLs.  $  ^{[44]}  $   The electrical current maps (Figure 3c,d) suggest that the PBDB-T HTL grown on the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br-PT  $   surface has a much higher average current value of -109.7 pA than the control   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film (-15.7 pA). The significantly increased surface current is attributed to the highly ordered PBDB-T HTL and the continuous one-source PBDB-T transport channel.  $  ^{[18]}  $

To demonstrate the effect of the one-source strategy on the performance of pero-SCs, the pero-SCs with a configuration of ITO/TiO $ _2 $ /SnO $ _2 $ /CsPbI $ _2 $ Br-PT/PBDB-T/MoO $ _3 $ /Ag were fabricated. As shown in Figure 4a, all the photovoltaic parameters were improved (i.e., a  $ V_{oc} $  of 1.24 V, a  $ J_{sc} $  of 16.16 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and a fill factor of 82.05%) compared to conventionally configured devices. The resultant devices exhibit a PCE of 16.40%, which is one of the highest PCE observed for the CsPbI $ _2 $ Br-based pero-SCs with a dopant-free HTL (Table S3, Supporting Information). The devices also exhibit excellent thermal and operational stability (Figure S13 and Tables S3 and S4, Supporting Information), and an ultrahigh fill factor of 82.05%, indicating the superior interfacial properties between perovskite and PBDB-T HTL enabling efficient charge carrier separation and extraction. We also found that the one-source pero-SC exhibits an increased  $ V_{oc} $  compared with the other devices, possibly due to the suppression of carrier recombination and gradient band alignment in the device. In contrast, when using conventional Spiro-OMeTAD as the HTL, the resultant device (ITO/TiO $ _2 $ /SnO $ _2 $ /CsPbI $ _2 $ Br-PT/Spiro-OMeTAD/MoO $ _3 $ /Ag) exhibited an inferior PCE of 15.35% with a  $ V_{oc} $  of 1.20 V, a  $ J_{sc} $  of 16.27 mA cm $ ^{-2} $ , and a fill factor of 78.81% (Figure 4a and Table S4, Supporting Information); when replacing CsPbI $ _2 $ Br-PT with CsPbI $ _2 $ Br but maintaining PBDB-T as the HTL, the resultant device exhibits an even lower PCE of 13.48%. These results confirm the criticality of adopting the same PDM as the hole transporting channel in CsPbI $ _2 $ Br and the HTL to improve interfacial contact and hole transport capability in the devices. The external quantum efficiency spectra (Figure S14, Supporting Information) exhibited an integrated  $ J_{sc} $  of 15.55 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  for the one-source device, agreeing well with the  $ J_{sc} $  value obtained from the  $ J-V $  curve within ≈5% deviation. In addition, the one-source devices exhibited a high average PCE of 16.07% with a narrower distribution than control devices (Figure 4b), demonstrating the universality of this strategy. Meanwhile, the one-source pero-SC exhibited a steady-state PCE of 16.12% with a stable current density of 15.50 mA cm $ ^{-2} $  under a constant bias voltage of 1.04 V for over 200 s (Figure 4c), which is very close to that obtained from the  $ J-V $  curves. In addition, the one-source pero-SC showed smaller hysteresis factor (0.09) than the control device (0.15) (Table S5, Supporting Information).

To study the effects of the one-source strategy on the charge-carrier dynamics of pero-SCs, steady-state photoluminescence measurements were carried out. As illustrated in Figure 4c, the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  PT film exhibited pronounced photoluminescence quenching compared with the control  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film. This suggests that the PBDB-T in the  $ \mathrm{CsPbI}_2\mathrm{Br} $  film effectively builds up hole-transporting channels throughout the perovskite film, enhancing the hole extraction capability. We then replaced PBDB-T HTL with other conventional PDMs, such as PTB7-Th, PM6, and PM7, to evaluate the universality of the one-source strategy under the same fabrication conditions. All these PDMs exhibit mismatched HOMO levels with the   $  E_{VB}  $   of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  . As shown in Figure S18 and Table S7, Supporting Information, the PTB7-Th, PM6, and PM7-based devices exhibited much higher PCEs of 15.67%, 16.34%, and 16.14%, respectively, than the control device with the SpiroOMeTAD HTL. Among these various one-source devices, the slight difference in PCEs may be due to the varying perovskite quality observed in XRD patterns (Figure S4, Supporting Information) and the varying passivation effects of the PDMs (Figure S7, Supporting Information). We also noted that the PTB7-Th-based devices with the largest mismatch level exhibited higher efficiencies than the control devices, suggesting that the one-source strategy effectively weakened the energy barrier. Thus, these results reveal that the one-source strategy can efficiently weaken the energy offset between the HTL and perovskite, reducing the importance of the intrinsic HOMO of the HTL.

Based on above results, three principles are proposed when selecting PDMs for their application in one-source strategy. 1) The functional group with Lewis-base characteristic such as thiophene should be incorporated for passivating the prevailing  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  defects in perovskite films; electron-withdrawing atoms, such as F and Cl, are suggested to be avoided whatever on backbone or functional group, because the decreased electron density may make side effects to the passivation. 2) The PDMs with high coplanar backbone are needed to ensure high hole mobility for smooth hole transport. 3) The HOMOs of PDMs should be shallower compared to the valence bands of perovskite for sufficiently extracting holes from perovskite to HTL.

We next determined the effect of the one-source strategy on the overall device stability. First, the unencapsulated devices were stored in an ambient atmosphere with a relative humidity

of ≈30%. After 500 h, the pero-SCs with the Spiro-OMeTAD HTL exhibited a dramatically dropped PCE, retaining 60% of their initial PCE values (Figure S19, Supporting Information). In contrast, when using the dopant-free PBDB-T as the HTL, the devices with control   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   or   $  CsPbI_{2}Br-PT  $   active layers exhibited improved stabilities, retaining 78% and 91% of their initial PCE values, respectively. This result suggests that dopant-free PBDB-T HTL prevents moisture penetration into the perovskite film because the lithium salts doped in Spiro-OMeTAD adsorb water from the air, forming widespread pinholes (Figure S20, Supporting Information) and decomposing the underlying perovskite layer.  $  ^{[54]}  $   In comparison, the dopant-free PBDB-T HTL on the perovskite film exhibited a homogeneous and full-coverage morphology, and there were no changes after exposure to atmospheric air for 7 days. Moreover, the PBDB-T HTL deposited on the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br-PT  $   film exhibits a higher contact angle than the Spiro-OMeTAD, indicating that the PBDB-T HTL is more hydrophobic (Figure S21, Supporting Information), which contributes to the high moisture stability for the one-source device. The thermal stability study was conducted on an 85 °C hot plate in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere (Figure 6a). The PCEs of the pero-SCs with the Spiro-OMeTAD HTL dramatically decayed to 30% of their initial values within 160 h, but the one-source pero-SCs maintained 85% of their initial PCEs after 500 h, which are also much higher than that (60%) of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br/PBDB-T  $  -based devices. To determine the source of the differences in thermal stability, the heating-aged control   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and   $  CsPbI_{2}Br-PT  $   films with various HTLs were investigated by XRD analysis. As shown in Figure 6c, the characteristic peaks (100) and (200) of perovskite in   $  CsPbI_{2}Br/PBDB-T  $   and   $  CsPbI_{2}Br-PT/Spiro-OMeTAD  $   were dramatically reduced after continuous heating at 85 °C for 7 days, especially for the perovskite coated with the Spiro-OMeTAD HTL, where the color of the sample also changed from black to dark yellow. This indicates that the lithium salts and TBP dopants in Spiro-OMeTAD degrade the HTL morphology and accelerate perovskite decomposition at high temperatures.  $  ^{[55]}  $

Finally, we also monitored the long-term operational stability of unencapsulated devices under continuous 1 sun equivalent illumination in a   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glovebox at 45–55 °C (Figure 6b). The one-source pero-SC exhibits excellent operational stability, retaining 81% of its initial PCE after almost 1000 h, while the device with the Spiro-OMeTAD HTL only maintained 50% of its initial PCE. We hypothesize that the considerable difference in operational stability was due to the dopant in the Spiro-OMeTAD, where   $  I^{-}  $  can escape from the perovskite through the cave in Spiro-OMeTAD caused by volatile tBP, then reacting with the silver electrode under illumination.  $  ^{[44]}  $   We also employed 2D GIWAXS to characterize the changes in molecular stacking of HTLs before and after illumination. As shown in Figure 6d, the photo-aged Spiro-OMeTAD film exhibits a (010) diffraction peak at 1.29 Å  $  ^{-1}  $   in the out-of-plane direction with a crystal coherence length (CCL) of 15.3 Å (Table S8, Supporting Information), which is lower than that of the fresh Spiro-OMeTAD film (CCL = 16.6 Å). The aged PBDB-T film exhibits similar CCL values before and after illumination. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, we successfully explored a one-source strategy employing PDM to address the bottleneck issues, that is, inferior   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film quality and doped HTL, which seriously limited the efficiency and long-term stability of all-inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs. We found that the one-source PDM could be incorporated into   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film by antisolvent with a top-down gradient distribution and induced perovskite growth with enlarged grain size and reduced defect density; the PDM deposited on the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -PDM surface assembled into a fingerprint-like HTL, which enhanced the conductivity and electrical contact for hole extraction and transportation without any dopant. As a result, the pero-SC based on this one-source strategy with PBDB-T PDM exhibited a remarkable PCE of 16.40%, which is one of the highest PCE reported for the

CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br-based pero-SCs with a dopant-free HTL. This strategy works with a wide range of PDMs (e.g., PBDB-T, PTB7-Th, PM6, and PM7) demonstrating universality. Importantly, the device employing the one-source strategy made a significant breakthrough in device stability, realizing outstanding thermal and MPP operational stability. We believe that this strategy will open new avenues toward fabricating high-performance all-inorganic CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br pero-SCs and displays promising potential for their future industry applications.
