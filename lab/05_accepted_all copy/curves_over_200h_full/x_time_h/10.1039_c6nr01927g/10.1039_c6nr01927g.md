# High-Performance Inverted Planar Heterojunction Perovskite Solar Cells Based on Solution-Processed   $  CuO_{x}  $   Hole Transport Layer

Weihai Sun,  $  ^{a,b}  $   Yunlong Li,  $  ^{a}  $   Senyun Ye,  $  ^{a}  $   Haixia Rao,  $  ^{a}  $   WeiBo Yan,  $  ^{a}  $   HaiTao Peng,  $  ^{a}  $   Yu Li,  $  ^{b}  $   Zhiwei Liu,  $  ^{*a}  $   Shufeng Wang,  $  ^{b}  $   Zhijian Chen,  $  ^{b}  $   Lixin Xiao,  $  ^{b}  $   Zuqiang Bian  $  ^{*a}  $   and Chunhui Huang  $  ^{a}  $

# Abstract

During the past several years, methylammonium lead halide perovskites have been widely investigated as a light absorber for thin-film photovoltaic cells. Among various device architectures, the inverted planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells attract special attention for a relatively simple fabrication with high efficiencies. Although the promising efficiencies have been obtained in the inverted planar geometry based on poly (3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly (styrenesulfonate) sulfonic acid (PEDOT:PSS) as hole transport materials (HTMs), the hydrophilicity of PEDOT:PSS is a critical factor for the long-term stability. In this paper,   $  CuO_{x}  $   hole transport layer from a facile solution-processed method was introduced in the inverted planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells. After the optimization of devices, a champion PCE of 17.1% was obtained with an open circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 0.99 V, a short-circuit current (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 23.2 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and a fill factor (FF) of 74.4%. Furthermore, the stability test for the unencapsulated device cooperating with   $  CuO_{x}  $   film exhibited superior performance than that of device involved with PEDOT:PSS layer, indicating   $  CuO_{x}  $   could be a promising HTM for replacing PEDOT:PSS in inverted planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells.

# Introduction

In the past few years, organometal halide perovskites are considered as promising absorber materials for thin-film photovoltaic cells because of their low cost, simple fabrication, and superior photonic properties such as high charge carrier mobility and long diffusion length.  $  ^{1-8}  $   Owing to the breakthrough of replacing the liquid hole transport electrolyte with the first solid hole transport materials (HTMs) [2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenamine)-9,9'-spiro-bifluorene] (spiro-MeOTAD), perovskite solar cells exhibited a power conversion efficiency (PCE) beyond 9%.  $  ^{9}  $   Remarkable efforts have been made to advance the PCE exceeding 20%.  $  ^{10}  $

Two architectures (mesoscopic and planar heterojunction) have been successfully developed for perovskite solar cells. Regardless of the mesoscopic or planar heterojunction configuration, the structures of perovskite solar cell can be divided into two types (conventional and inverted structure), depending upon the electron or hole transport layer which is employed as perovskite layer's substrate. Currently the conventional perovskite solar cells consist of a compact or mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   electron-transport layer, which requires sintering up to 500 °C before use, making the device fabrication infeasible for plastic or flexible substrates. Given that the diffusion length of hole is shorter than that of electron in the perovskite layer,  $  ^{1,2}  $   the inverted planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells were reported by depositing the perovskite film on a planar layer of HTMs such as poly(3,4-ethyl-needioxythiophene):poly(styrene-sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) and followed with fullerene (  $  C_{60}  $  ) or

The corresponding devices exhibited a decent PCE approaching 18% with small variability.  $  ^{20}  $   Yet, PEDOT:PSS is not a perfect HTM owing to its high cost, relatively low hole mobility, acidic and hygroscopic nature.  $  ^{21}  $   Therefore, we selected a series of conductive polymers such as polythiophene,  $  ^{22}  $   poly(p-phenylene) and poly(4,4'-bis(N-carbazolyl)-1,1'-biphenyl)  $  ^{23}  $   prepared via electrochemical polymerization as HTMs in inverted perovskite solar cells, exhibiting superior device performance comparable with the conventional perovskite solar cells incorporating with PEDOT:PSS. In addition, inorganic HTMs, such as   $  NiO_{x}  $  ,  $  ^{24-27}  $   graphene oxide,  $  ^{28}  $     $  MoO_{3}  $  ,  $  ^{29}  $   CuSCN,  $  ^{30}  $   et al,  $  ^{31}  $   are potential substitutes for PEDOT:PSS in perovskite solar cells due to their advantages of excellent stability, high hole mobility and low cost. However, the PCEs of inverted perovskite solar cells based on inorganic HTMs are still lower than that of classical devices using PEDOT:PSS. Very recently, Han et al proposed heavily p-doped   $  Ni_{x}Mg_{1-x}O  $   and n-doped   $  TiO_{x}  $   selective contacts to extract photogenerated charge carriers effectively from the inverted planar perovskite-PCBM film architecture.  $  ^{32}  $   Although a PCE exceeding 18% was achieved by enhancing the electrical conductivity of both hole selective layer and electron selective layer, the deposition of NiO using spray pyrolysis still need high-temperature processing, which is not appropriate to a large scale production. Hence, seeking for better HTM is an important research area of the perovskite solar

Stable copper oxides (CuO $ _x $ ), namely, cupric oxide (CuO) and cuprous oxide (Cu $ _2 $ O), are well-known p-type semiconductors. Due to the natural abundance of copper, CuO $ _x $  becomes a promising alternative for photovoltaic system. $ ^{33} $  Recently, CuO $ _x $  has been used as a hole transport layer in polymer solar cells (PSCs), since it could form the ohmic contact between the polymer active layer and the anode. $ ^{34-36} $  Nejand  $ et $   $ al $  introduced Cu $ _2 $ O as HTM by magnetron sputtering technique in conventional perovskite solar cells with PCE of 8.93%. $ ^{37} $  Zuo  $ et $   $ al $  proposed a facile low-temperature method to prepare Cu $ _2 $ O and CuO films by in situ conversion of CuI film in aqueous NaOH solution and employed them as HTMs in inverted planar perovskite solar cells, achieving PCEs of 13.35% and 12.16% for Cu $ _2 $ O and CuO devices, respectively. $ ^{38} $  To simplify the film process of HTM and improve the PCE of perovskite solar cells, CuO $ _x $  layer by a facile solution-processed method was introduced into a planar inverted perovskite solar cell as a hole transport layer in this work. The solution-processed CuO $ _x $  layer exhibits a smooth surface and high transparency in the visible region. A maximum PCE over 17% was achieved by employing CuO $ _x $  as the hole transport layer. Remarkably, the device based on CuO $ _x $  layer shows the excellent stability in air. The results demonstrate that CuO $ _x $  has great potential as an alternative HTM for highly efficient perovskite solar cells.

To study the photocurrent hysteresis in perovskite solar cells using   $  CuO_{x}  $   hole transport layer (with   $  UV-O_{3}  $   treatment for 15 min), we measured the J-V curves of our devices using both reverse and forward scans with a scan rate of 300 mV/s (Fig. 4a). # Conclusions

In summary, we fabricated a planar inverted perovskite solar cell by incorporation of solution-processed  $ \mathrm{CuO_x} $  hole transport layer, where the perovskite layer was deposited by a one-step fast deposition-crystallization method. A highest PCE of  $ 17.1\% $  was obtained after the further optimization for the  $ \mathrm{CuO_x} $  based device. It was demonstrated that the high performance of  $ \mathrm{CuO_x} $  based device could be originated from the fast hole carriers transfer from the perovskite to  $ \mathrm{CuO_x} $  layer and the lower contact resistance of  $ \mathrm{CuO_x} $  layer. Furthermore, the inverted perovskite solar cells employing  $ \mathrm{CuO_x} $  as a hole transport layer showed superior stability than that of perovskite solar cells with PEDOT:PSS. Significantly, the solution-processed  $ \mathrm{CuO_x} $  layer is a simple, inexpensive and controllable way to provide a potential and the

# Experimental section

Materials and method. Copper(II) acetylacetonate (Cu(acac) $ _2 $ , purity >99.9%), PEDOT:PSS (Clevious P VP AI 4083), PbI $ _2 $  (99.9985%), C $ _{60} $  (99.9%) and 2,9-dimethyl-4,7-diphenyl-1,10-phenanthroline (BCP) (99.9%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, H. C. Stark Company, Alfa Aesar, Puyang Yongxin Fullerene Technology Co., Ltd and Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. (PLT), respectively. Indium tin oxide (ITO) coated glass substrates with a sheet resistance of 8 Ω/sq and ITO thickness of 180 nm were purchased from Huayulianhe Co., Ltd. Methylammonium iodide (CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I) was synthesized according to the procedure reported previously $ ^{19} $ . All these commercially available materials were used as received without any further purification.

Device fabrication. The ITO coated glass substrates were cleaned ultrasonically in detergent, deionized water, acetone and isopropyl alcohol sequentially and ultraviolet-ozone treated for 10 minutes. The  $ \mathrm{CuO_x} $  film was deposited by spin-coating  $ \mathrm{Cu(acac)_2} $  1,2-dichlorobenzene solution (1.5 mg/mL) at 4000 rpm for 60 s on the ITO electrode, and then annealing at  $ 80^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  for 20 min in air. After washed by spin-coating methanol, the  $ \mathrm{CuO_x} $  film was transferred into ultraviolet-ozone for  $ \mathrm{O_3} $  treatment with several min (0 min, 15 min, 30 min, 60 min). Afterwards, a layer of perovskite was deposited by either the fast crystallization-deposition (FDC) method or the two-step coating method.
