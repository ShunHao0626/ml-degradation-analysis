# 1. Introduction

Recently, tremendous research enthusiasm has been increasingly stimulated by the promising newcomer of the photovoltaic family, namely organometal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) due to its outstanding merits of high-efficiency, low-cost and solution processability   $  [1,2]  $  . Over the past few years, an unprecedented rapid improvement of the power conversion efficiency (PCE) for PSCs has been witnessed from the initial only 3.8% in 2009 to the present value of exceeding 23%   $  [3,4]  $  . Essentially, a series of intrinsic properties of the organometal halide perovskite materials can primarily be responsible for the rapid progress on the PCE, such as high absorption coefficient, long charge carrier diffusion lengths and tunable bandgap by tuning the composition of cation and halide, etc.   $  [1,5–7]  $   Besides, crystallization of the perovskite materials is another important determining factor for high efficiency of PSCs, which can mainly influence the morphology and crystallinity of the resultant perovskite film   $  [8]  $  .

# ABSTRACT

As we known, high-efficiency of the solution-processed perovskite solar cells can be achieved by modulating the perovskite film quality. Here, the prevalent 4-tert-butylpyridine is firstly utilized as an additive of  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  solution for improving the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film quality fabricated with two-step deposition method. Due to the strong polarity and interaction between N atom on TBP and Pb atom on  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $ , this strategy leads to a substantial enhancement of the  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film quality with larger grains, higher crystallinity and more complete coverage on the substrate than that of the pristine one. Thereby, the TBP-based  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  film simultaneously exhibits superior optical properties and surface hydrophobicity. By optimization, the perovskite solar cells fabricated with TBP addition at a volume ratio of  $ 7/1000 $  (v/v, TBP/isopropanol) exhibits significant performance enhancement with a power conversion efficiency reaching  $ 17.06\% $ , while the pristine one merely obtains a lower value of  $ 14.56\% $ . More importantly, this type of cells also shows a desirable efficiency reproducibility and long-time stability in air atmosphere. In conclusion, our work demonstrates another meaningful application for the TBP additive and provides a facile and effective strategy for further improving the performance of perovskite solar cells.

Currently, there are primary two types of developed deposition technologies for the perovskite film, namely precursor solution deposition (PSD) and precursor vapor deposition (PVD) methods, respectively [9,10]. In comparison with the PVD method, the PSD is relatively low-cost, easy-processability, and more importantly compatible with large-scale roll-to-roll production for the large-area device panel [11]. In general, the PSD method is mainly divided into two categories, namely one-step precursor spin-coating deposition (OSPSD) and two-step sequential precursor spin-coating (TSSPSD) [12]. By further comparing with the above-mentioned PSD methods, although the TSSPSD method needs relatively complex fabrication procedure, it can easily lead to a higher photovoltaic performance for the PSCs with superior PCE, good reproducibility and long-time stability. To our knowledge, the present record PCE has still been held by the PSCs fabricated by TSSPSD method. However, similar with OSPSD method, the traditional TSSPSD is not easy to obtain an optimal efficiency due to the inferior perovskite film quality including incomplete conversion of precursor

Therefore, with the aiming at improving the perovskite film quality, some effective strategies have also been successfully utilized to modify the TSSPSD method, such as solvent engineering   $  [16,17]  $  , addition additive in precursor solution,   $  [18–21]  $   composition modulation   $  [22]  $  , and the effective control of the annealing process   $  [23–26]  $  , etc. However, these strategies mainly concentrated on regulating the crystallization process of   $  PbI_{2}  $   film and the annealing process of the resultant perovskite film. Actually, a typical TSSPSD method, such as the fabrication of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) film, is basically divided into two primary stages, (i) the deposition of   $  PbI_{2}  $   and its corresponding post-annealing treatment, (ii) the coating of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (MAI) solution for completing the conversion of   $  PbI_{2}  $   into MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   by spin-coating or dipping methods as well as the post-annealing for the final perovskite film   $  [12,27]  $  . Therefore, it can easily be speculated that manipulating the deposition process of MAI is also an effective approach for improving the performance of PSCs fabricated with TSSPSD method. However, as far as I know, there hitherto are rare reports on the manipulation of the deposition process of MAI.

4-Tert-butylpyridine (TBP) is a prevailing additive which is commonly used in the liquid electrolyte of dye-sensitized solar cells and the hole transport materials of PSCs   $  [28–31]  $  . To the best of our knowledge, it up to now has never been reported for the utilization of additive into the MAI solution for fabricating the perovskite film with TSSPSD approach. In this report, we found that utilization of a small amount of TBP as an additive of MAI solution in TSSPSD approach can be beneficial for the formation of high-quality MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   film with larger grains, higher crystallinity and more complete coverage on the substrate, etc. After optimizing the volume ratio (v/v, TBP/isopropanol) of TBP addition in MAI solution, the TBP-based PSCs at a volume ratio of 7/1000 (v/v, TBP/isopropanol) yields a higher PCE up to 17.06% than that of the pristine one (mere 14.56%). Furthermore, these TBP-based PSCs also show a superior PCE reproducibility and long-time stability in air atmosphere. All in all, our findings herein demonstrate another meaningful application of the TBP additive and provide a facile strategy for further improving the performance of the PSCs.

# 2. Experimental section

# 2.1. Devices preparation

The perovskite photovoltaic devices were constructed on the fluorine-doped   $  SnO_{2}  $   glass (FTO, 15 Ω/square, NSG, Japan). After etching with Zn powder and 3 M HCl into a certain pattern, the FTO substrates were further treated by a continuous ultrasonic rinsing with detergent, deionized water, acetone and isopropanol for each of 10 min, and ultraviolet ozone cleaning for 10 min, respectively. Then, a compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   layer (abbreviated as C-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) was fabricated on the pre-cleaned FTO substrates by spin-coating a solution of 360 μL titanium isopropoxide together with 60 μL 3 M HCl addition in 5.0 mL isopropanol at 3500 rpm for 30 s and then heating at 500 °C for 30 min. After cooling to room temperature, the as-constructed C-  $  TiO_{2}  $   film were necessarily passivated with a 20 mM   $  TiCl_{4}  $   solution by immersion method at 70 °C for 30 min, and then cleaned with deionized water and ethanol. After sintering at 500 °C for 30 min and then cooling into room temperature, the above substrates were further deposited with a diluted   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste (Dyesol 30 NR-D) in ethanol (v/v, 1/7) via a spin-coating procedure at 5000 rpm for 30 min. Sequentially, the above   $  TiO_{2}  $   paste coated substrates were sequentially annealed at 500 °C for 30 min and cooled into room temperature.

It is noted that all the following spin-coating procedures were performed in the glove-box with   $  N_{2}  $   filling. Initially, the substrates were deposited by spin-coating a 1.2 M   $  PbI_{2}  $   solution at a speed of 3000 rpm

for 5 s and 5000 rpm for 5 s, respectively. The PbI₂ solution was prepared by dissolving 550 mg PbI₂ into 1 mL mixture of dimethylformamide (DMF) and dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) (v/v, 4/1) and energetically stirring at 70 °C for 12 h. When the PbI₂ coated substrates were heated at 100 °C for 10 min on a hotplate and cooled to room temperature, they were sequentially exposed into a MAI solution (8 mg/mL in isopropanol without and with different content of TBP addition) and then spin-coated at 5000 rpm for 30 min. After annealing at 100 °C for 30 min on a hotplate, the conversion of PbI₂ into MAPbI₃ was completed. Typically, the MAI solutions with different volume ratio of TBP addition were obtained by adding a small amount of TBP into the above MAI solution at a volume ratio (v/v, TBP/isopropanol) of 0/1000, 1/1000, 5/1000, 7/1000, 10/1000 and 25/1000, respectively. After that, a doped solution of 72.3 mg spiroOMeTAD (Toronto Research Chemicals Inc.) in 1 mL chlorobenzene was spin-coated onto the as-formed MAPbI₃ film at 4000 rpm 20 s, which was used as the hole transport layer (HTL) of the final PSCs. In this work, the spiroOMeTAD solution was simultaneously doped with 17.5 μL bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (Li-TFSi) solution (520 mg/mL in acetonitrile) and 29 μL 4-tert-butylpyridine (t-BP). Finally, the device was completed by thermally evaporating 80 nm Au film onto the HTM film with a metal aperture under low vacuum.

# 2.2. Characterization and measurements

2.2.1. Film characterization

It initially need to be noted that all the film samples used in the following film analysis were prepared utilizing the MAI solution with TBP addition at fixed volume ratio (v/v, TBP/isopropanol) of 7/1000. The film morphology and surface roughness were analyzed by investigating the scan electron microscope (SEM) and atomic force microscope (AFM) images, which was recorded by using a New Generation Cold Field Emission SEM (Rili SU 8000HSD Series Hitachi) and a Bruker Dimension ICON-PT with Co/Cr tips, respectively. The Contact angle was measured using a sessile drop technique and a surface contact angle measurement instrument (the type JYSP-360, USA, needlepoint  $ 1.5\mathrm{mm} $ ). Here, the samples for the surface morphology and contact angle measurements were prepared onto the  $ \mathrm{M - TiO_2} $  coated FTO substrates. Additionally, X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were obtained with Shimadzu XRD-6000 X-ray Diffraction instrument with Cu-Karadiation. UV-vis absorbance characterization was carried out via PerkinElmer 750 spectrophotometer with  $ \mathrm{BaSO_4} $  powder as the baseline correction material. Steady-state photoluminescence (ss-PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (tr-PL) measurement were conducted via the FLS 920 luminescence spectrometer in combining with an optical filter for filtering the emission light at  $ < 650\mathrm{nm} $ , and the excitation wavelength was fixed at  $ 475\mathrm{nm} $ . The samples for UV-vis, XRD and PL measurements were fabricated by directly depositing the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  films on the cleaned glass.

2.2.2. Photovoltaic device measurements

The photocurrent-voltage (I-V) curves and electrochemistry impedance spectroscopy (EIS) of the PSCs were measured via an electrochemical workstation (Gamry, America) under standard AM 1.5 G simulated sunlight (  $  100\ mW\cdot cm^{-2}  $  ) originated from a AAA Class 300 W solar simulator (Newport, USA). Before utilization, the light intensity was calibrated with a Newport Oriel PV standard reference cell and meter (model 91,150 V). A metal shadow mask with aperture of   $  0.06\ cm^{-2}  $   was used to define the photo active area. The scan rate for I-V measurement is 100 mV/s. A set of IPCE measurement system (model 2931-C, Newport, USA) was applied to check the incident photon to electron conversion efficiency (IPCE).

As demonstrated in otherwise, the bigger arcs at the low frequency are ascribed to the charge recombination resistance (  $  R_{tr}  $  ) between the interface of   $  TiO_{2}/MAPbI_{3}  $  , and the smaller arcs at high frequency are correlated to the charge transport resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) between the interface of   $  MAPbI_{3}/spiroOMeTAD  $   [40,41]. As shown in Fig. 5b, the intersection values of the extension of the arcs at high frequency with real

Significantly, we can easily observe that the TBP-based PSCs at a volume ratio of 7/1000 (v/v, TBP/isopropanol) exhibits some characteristics of a smaller semicircle at high frequency, a smaller intersection value in combination with a bigger semicircle at low frequency in the EIS spectra, indicating a smaller   $  R_{tr}  $   and   $  R_{s}  $   together with a bigger   $  R_{rec}  $   compared with that of the pristine cell. Commonly, these can lead to a substantial PCE enhancement for corresponding PSCs [41,43]. Quite obviously, due to the same   $  TiO_{2}  $   electron transport layer and spiroOMeTAD hole transport layer adopted in both of the PSCs regardless of the TBP addition, we can identify the TBP addition in MAI solution as the main reason of the PCE enhancement of the resultant PSCs, especially the enhanced perovskite film morphology and crystallinity directedly induced by TBP addition.

By comparison, we can find that the TBP addition not only enhance each of the photovoltaic parameters, but also lead to a small deviation, indicating that the photoelectric property reproducibility of the PSCs can also be effectively enhanced by our proposed MAPbI $ _3 $  crystallization modulation process with TBP addition in the MAI solution. According to this, we consider that the perovskite film quality is one of the primary determining factors for the high efficiency of the PSCs.

# 4. Conclusions

In conclusion, we developed an effective and flexible strategy to improve the perovskite film quality via a widely utilized TSSPSD method, which was accomplished by adding the well-known TBP additive into the MAI solution. In this procedure, the TPB can play two main roles for promoting the perovskite film quality enhancement. On one hand, TBP cannot only render a more complete conversion of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  into  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ , but also enlarge the grain size of the perovskite crystal, both of which are mainly due to the polarity nature of TBP. On the other hand, the nitrogen atom on TBP and the  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  on  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  can form a strong coordination interaction, finally resulting in a more compact film with fewer defects and crystal grain boundaries. Accordingly, the resultant PSCs fabricated with TBP addition at a volume ratio of 7/1000 (v/v, TBP/isopropanol) exhibits significant device

performance enhancement with a PCE up to  $ 17.06\% $ , while the pristine one merely obtains a lower PCE of  $ 14.56\% $ , achieving a  $ 17.2\% $  of the PCE enhancement. Additionally, this type of the TBP addition-based PSCs also show a desirable efficiency reproducibility and long-time stability in air atmosphere. In conclusion, our work demonstrates another meaningful application for the prevalent TBP additive and provides a facile and effective strategy for further improving the performance of the PSCs.
