# Introduction

Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskites have recently attracted enormous interest in the photovoltaic field due to its low exciton dissociation energy, large charge diffusion length and bipolar carrier properties. The efficiency of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have risen from 3.8% to 24.2% via crystal growth control, device architecture optimization, composition engineering and interface engineering.  $  ^{1-15}  $   The outstanding performance of PSCs is closely related to the optoelectrical properties of each functional layers, including the light-absorbing layer, the electron transport layer (ETL) and the hole transport layer (HTL). Various metal oxides and organic materials with wide bandgap and a good transmittance have been developed for the ETL of PSCs, such as fullerene derivatives,   $  TiO_{2}  $   and ZnO. However, on the one hand, the low-electron mobility and high costs of organic ETLs are not

Energy level alignment and carrier mobility of the charge transport layer is of crucial importance for the electron extraction and transport in planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells (PSCs). In this work, a carbonitride-modified  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  nanocomposite,  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ /graphitic carbon nitride  $ (\mathrm{g - C_3N_4}) $  quantum dots, was designed as the functional electron transport layer (ETL) to precisely regulate the interfacial charge dynamics for high-performance PSCs. It is demonstrated that the high-conductive  $ \mathrm{g - C_3N_4} $  could recast the electronic density distribution around the neighboring  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  crystal unit, thus effectively eliminating the oxygen-vacancy-reduced trap centers and promoting the interface and bulk electron transport. Consequently, this carbonitride-modified  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  nanocomposite exhibits proper electrical properties including suitable energy level alignment and high conductivity. By employing the typical hybrid  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ -based ETL, a maximum power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 22.13\% $  ( $ V_{oc} = 1.176\mathrm{V} $ ,  $ J_{sc} = 24.03\mathrm{mA/cm^2} $ , FF=0.783) can be achieved for planar heterojunction PSCs, with negligible hysteresis and long-term stability (only  $ 10\% $  PCE decay after 1500h in relative humidity of  $ 60\% $ ). Our work offers a hybrid ETL design strategy to promote the efficiency and stability of the PSCs.

More direct insight into the recombination kinetics within the device can be obtained by measuring   $  V_{sc}  $   and   $  V_{oc}  $   at various light intensities. The monomolecular recombination refers to any first-order process including the geminate recombination of a bound electron-hole pair before dissociation and the Shockley-Read-Hall (SRH) recombination at the shallow traps created by defects and impurities in the interfacial layer, while the bimolecular recombination refers to the recombination of free electrons and holes in the photoactive layer.  $  ^{51,52}  $   Fig. 6e presents the dependence of   $  J_{sc}  $   on the incident light intensity (I) in logarithmic scale according to   $  J_{sc} \propto \alpha I  $  , from which the slopes of the bare   $  SnO_{2}  $   and G-  $  SnO_{2}  $   devices are 0.97 and 0.99, respectively. At   $  \alpha = 1  $  , all carriers are swept out prior to recombination. The slightly larger (closer to 1) slope of the G-  $  SnO_{2}  $   device indicates that the bimolecular recombination during sweeping out the carriers is suppressed at the G-  $  SnO_{2}  $ /perovskite interface when compared with the bare-  $  SnO_{2}  $ -based control device, which is due to the much more efficient carrier extraction and transport. Besides, the dependence of   $  V_{oc}  $   on the logarithm of the incident light intensity (  $  \log(I)  $  ) is shown in Fig. 6f, which can be fitted according to the equation   $  V_{oc} = \varepsilon kT \ln(I)/e + constant  $  , where   $  \varepsilon  $   is the ideal factor, k is the Boltzmann constant and T is the temperature. For monomolecular recombination,   $  V_{oc}  $   versus   $  \ln(I)  $   would be 2kT/e. The value between kT/e and 2kT/e indicates the presence of bimolecular recombination and monomolecular SRH recombination.  $  ^{53,54}  $   As shown in Fig. 6f, the G-  $  SnO_{2}  $ -based PSC possesses a weaker   $  V_{oc}  $   dependence on the light intensity (1.09 KT/q) than the bare-  $  SnO_{2}  $ -based device (1.36 KT/q), demonstrating that G-  $  SnO_{2}  $   effectively reduces the monomolecular SRH recombination (interfacial trap-assisted recombination), which is largely attributed to a reduction of defect density and resulting in improved device performance.

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was utilized to analyze the core-level chemical shift of tin. As shown in Fig. 7a and Fig. S9, the signal peaks of Sn 3d at 486.4 eV and 494.9 eV correspond to the featured bond of Sn 3d  $  _{3/2}  $  and Sn 3d  $  _{5/2}  $ , respectively. In addition, the signal peaks of Sn 3d in G-SnO  $  _{2}  $  slightly shift to higher binding energies at 486.6 eV and 495.0 eV. The blue shift (toward higher binding energy) of Sn 3d demonstrates electron transfer from g-CNQD to the surface tin atoms  $  ^{35}  $ . To investigate the change of band positions induced by g-CNQD incorporation, the ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) of SnO  $  _{2}  $  and G-SnO  $  _{2}  $  are performed and shown in Fig. 7b. The Fermi level (E  $  _{F}  $ ) of SnO  $  _{2}  $  and G-SnO  $  _{2}  $  are calculated as -4.85 eV and -4.55 eV, by using the formula of E  $  _{F}  $  = 21.22 eV- E  $  _{cut-off}  $  (cut-off binding energy). From Fig. 7b the E  $  _{VBM}  $  of SnO  $  _{2}  $  and G-SnO  $  _{2}  $  was determined to be -8.39 eV and -8.26 eV, respectively, using E  $  _{VBM}  $  = E  $  _{F}  $  - E  $  _{F,edge}  $  (Fermi edge). The bandgap (E  $  _{B}  $ ) of SnO  $  _{2}  $  and G-SnO  $  _{2}  $  are found to be 3.91 eV through Taucplot, showing that the g-CNQD dopant did not affect E  $  _{B}  $  (Fig. S2). E  $  _{CBM}  $  is determined from E  $  _{B}  $  and E  $  _{VBM}  $ , which are found to be -4.48 eV and -4.35 eV, respectively, as depicted in Fig. 7d. Due to the shift of the work function and the change in the surface electron density of SnO  $  _{2}  $  after g-CNQD doping, the energy band alignment at the perovskite/G-SnO  $  _{2}  $  interface is expected to be more favorable. The UPS and absorption measurements of mixed cation CsFAMA perovskite are also obtained as shown in Fig. 7c and Fig. S4, and the E  $  _{F}  $ , E  $  _{CBM}  $  and E  $  _{VBM}  $  are -4.41, -4.36 and -5.95 eV, respectively. The energy band level alignments of SnO  $  _{2}  $  or G-SnO  $  _{2}  $  with the perovskite layers deposited on them are shown in Fig. 7d. According to the basic theory of band alignment of heterojunctions, the band bending of each material at the perovskite/SnO  $  _{2}  $  interface leads to an energy barrier at the interface, which then suffers from charge accumulation and energy loss.  $  ^{49,55}  $  Although the E  $  _{CBM}  $  of G-SnO  $  _{2}  $  is slight higher (0.01 eV) than that of the CsFAMA, considering the multifactors (interface traps and energy band alignment) influenced on the interface extraction/transporting efficiency, the electron transporting behavior at ETL/perovskite interface could be improved in G-SnO  $  _{2}  $  based PSCs. At the same time, The V  $  _{oc}  $  in a photovoltaic device is determined by the quasi-Fermi level splitting of electrons and holes in the whole device under illumination, which is affected by the occupation of the available electronic states by photogenerated charge carriers, not only in the perovskite layer but also in the charge transport layers. Thus, the E  $  _{F}  $  upshift of ETL can also increase the quasi-Fermi level splitting of electrons and holes and enlarge the V  $  _{oc}  $ , that in accordance with the G-SnO  $  _{2}  $  devices demonstrated the higher flat-band potential in the Mott-Schottky plots (Fig. 6b).  $  ^{36,48}  $

method and the DFT-D3 method. to incorporate the van der Waals interactions between the g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  molecule and SnO  $  _{2}  $  (110) surface.  $  ^{58-59}  $  Adsorption configurations of g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  on SnO  $  _{2}  $  (110) surface with and without oxygen vacancy concentration of 1/217 were considered. Details of our DFT calculations are available in the experimental section of the Supporting Information. The DFT-calculated charge density difference of SnO  $  _{2}  $  with oxygen vacancy was shown in Fig. 8a and b. The cyan region represents charge accumulation, and the yellow region indicates charge depletion. It is clearly seen that the center of oxygen vacancy show intensity electron depletion and the three Sn atoms surrounding the vacancy show electron accumulation. So the oxygen vacancy is positively charged, always as the electron trap center. The charge density difference of G-SnO  $  _{2}  $  with oxygen vacancy was shown in Fig. 8d and e. As it shows, the charge redistribution occurred around the oxygen vacancy, and clear interaction between the g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  and the three Sn atoms surrounding the vacancy, more clear interaction can be seen by 2D views in Fig. 8c and f. As a result of g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  doping, the number of delocalized electrons on Sn atoms of around oxygen vacancy is remarkably augmented after the absorption of g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $ . This calculation result confirms our speculation that oxygen vacancy defect passivation and the signal peaks of Sn 3d shift. The charge density difference of G-SnO  $  _{2}  $  was also calculated (Fig. S10). The charge redistribution at the SnO  $  _{2}  $  (110)/g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  interface region is observed. The change at interfaces indicated that the electron transfer from the g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $  side to the SnO  $  _{2}  $  side across the interface, that in accordance with the doping density increase observed in Mott-Schottky plots.  $  ^{60-64}  $

It is known that the stability of perovskite films always poses a challenge for PSCs commercialization. The commonly used   $  TiO_{2}  $   ETL has been found to cause a rapid degradation of pervskite film under UV light, that due to desorption of   $  O^{2-}  $   at oxygen vacancies destroy the interface and perovskite. Comparing to   $  TiO_{2}  $ , low oxygen vacancies density   $  SnO_{2}  $   shows much better performance in oxygen and moisture condition. To study the stability of the device based on typical hybrid ETL, related investigations on unencapsulated PSCs is carried out. The stability test is shown in Fig. 9, and the stability test of previous reported using   $  SnO_{2}  $   and modified   $  SnO_{2}  $   as ETL are also listed in Table S5. It is clear that the device based on G-  $  SnO_{2}  $   maintains 90% of its initial efficiency exposed to an ambient atmosphere after 1500 h, and remain 80% of its initial efficiency under the condition of the 60% relative humidity at 85 °C after 72 hours aging. The XRD of perovskite film after ageing is shown in Fig. 9c and Fig. 9d, the   $  PbI_{2}  $   peak of G-  $  SnO_{2}  $   is much weaker than that of bare   $  SnO_{2}  $ . Here the excellent stability of G-  $  SnO_{2}  $   based PSCs can be attributed to two aspects: Firstly, as the typical carbonitride material, g-  $  C_{3}N_{4}  $   has the features of excellent corrosion resistance and chemical stability; Secondly, the elimination of the surface/bulk defects of   $  SnO_{2}  $   could further restrain the material decomposition, G-  $  SnO_{2}  $   with reduced oxygen vacancies effectively prevent the hydroxides adsorption, restraining the material decomposition.

# Conclusions

In summary, we demonstrated an innovative designing scheme of electron transporting layer, that is, the hybrid nanocomposites consisting of g-CNQD and   $  SnO_{2}  $  , for applying to the PSCs with high-performance and long-term stability simultaneously. Theoretical and experimental studies show that with the incorporation of g-CNQD into   $  SnO_{2}  $  , charge redistribution could be induced at the   $  SnO_{2}/g-C_{3}N_{4}  $   interface region for passivating the oxygen vacancies effectively. Correspondingly, energy band alignment and charge extraction behavior at the ETL/perovskite interface was regulated. As for the best device by utilizing the G-  $  SnO_{2}  $   hybrid ETL, a favorable 22.13% PCE was obtained compared with the controlled device with the PCE of 20.21%. Furthermore, G-  $  SnO_{2}  $   based device also present the considerable long-term stability, with 90% of the original efficient after 1500 h aging (at the humidity of 60%). We think that the explorations in our work could provide a facile ETL-design strategy in thriving perovskite photovoltaic field.

# Experimental Details

# Materials.

N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloid precursor from Alfa Aesar., Acetonitrile (ACN), bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide lithium (Li-TFSI), tert-butylpyridine (tBP), and CsI were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich.   $  PbI_{2}  $   and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   were purchased from TCI. MABr, FAI were synthesis as previous reports, Spiro-OMeTAD and tris(2-(1H-pyrazol-1-yl)-4-tertbutylpyridine)-cobalt(III)tris(bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide) (FK209) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. chlorobenzene(CB) was obtained from Arcros. All materials above were used as received.

# Preparation   $  SnO_{2}  $   and G-  $  SnO_{2}  $   precursor.

Disperse vary g-CNQD particles in 50 ml deionized water, ultrasonic for 8 hours, then filter with 0.45um filter obtained g-CNQD water solution. SnO  $  _{2}  $  colloid precursor was diluted by deionized water and g-CNQD water solution with 1:2 ratio, then ultrasonic for 2 hours. Then the SnO  $  _{2}  $  and G-SnO  $  _{2}  $  precursor were obtained.

# Device Fabrication.

ITO glass was cleaned by sequentially washing with detergent, deionized (DI) water, and isopropanol (IPA). Before using, the ITO was cleaned by UVO for 10 min. Then, the substrate was spin-coated with SnO $ _2 $  and G-SnO $ _2 $  precursor (1500 r.p.m., 30s). baked on a hot plate in ambient air at 180 °C for 30 min. After cooling to room temperature, the substrate was treated by UVO for 15 min. Here, CsFAMA mixed perovskite precursor solution was prepared by mixing PbI $ _2 $ , PbBr $ _2 $ , FAI and MABr in DMF/DMSO (v/v:4/1) where the mole concentration of PbI $ _2 $  was kept at 1.3 M with 0.1 M excess, and the mole ratio of I/Br and FA/MA were maintained at 0.85/0.15. 47 μl of CsI (1.5 M in DMSO) was added and stirred for 3 hours. CsFAMA perovskite films were deposited by spin coating (2000, 10s/6000, 30s) and 250 μl CB was used as antisolvent 15 s prior to the end of the spinning. And then anneal for 1h at 100 °C. The Spiro-OMeTAD solution was coated at 1500 r.p.m. for 30 s, where 1ml Spiro-OMeTAD/CB (90 mg/ml) solution with the addition of 17.8 μl Li-TFSI/ACN (520 mg/ml), 28.5 μl tBP and 20μl FK209/ACN (200 mg/ml). Finally, Au back electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation. The active area was 0.0706 cm $ ^{2} $ .

# Characterization.

UPS and XPS measurements were performed on an ESCALAB 250Xi, Thermo Fisher (by using Al Kg x-ray source) under high vacuum (  $  10^{-9}  $   be derived). For work function measurement with UPS, 10 V bias was applied and Au as reference. The XPS spectra were calibrated by the binding energy of C 1s. UV measurement was completed by UV/Vis/NIR Spectrometer (HITACHI U-3010, Japan). J-V measurements were carried out using a Keithley 2400 sourcemeter in ambient environment of 24 °C and 30% RH. Illumination was provided by an XES-301S, SAN-EI solar simulator with AM1.5G spectrum and light intensity of 100 mW/cm  $  ^{2}  $   was calibrated by means of a KG-5 Si diode. The devices were measured both in reverse scan (1.2 Vs-0.05 V, step 0.01 V) and forward scan (-0.05 Vs 1.2 V, step 0.01 V) with 0.1 V/s scan rate. Devices were taken out for EQE measurements using an solar cell Spectral Response/QE/IPCE Measurement System (Solar Cell Scan 100, ZOLIX) with the light intensity was calibrated with a standard single-crystal Si photovoltaic cell. The X-ray diffraction patterns were taken on BRUKER ECO D8 series. PL and time resolved PL spectra were carried out by Spectrofluorometer (FSS, Edinburgh instruments) and 405 nm pulsed laser was used as an excitation source for the measurement. Motte-shotty were tested with electrochemical workstation (chi 660E) at 1000Hz. Electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) of the cells were obtained using a CHI-660E over the frequency range of 1 Hz to 100 KHz under dark.

# Computaional Details.

The atomic structure of both   $  SnO_{2}  $   and   $  g-C_{3}N_{4}  $   were optimized with density functional theory (DFT) using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP). A   $  g-C_{3}N_{4}  $   molecule (22 atoms) was put on a 4-layer (110)   $  SnO_{2}  $   surface, with the unit-cell lattice parameters of the slab model   $  12.749\ \AA \times 13.395\ \AA \times 23.348\ \AA  $  . A vacuum space of   $  15\ \AA  $   thickness is added to the third lattice vector (that is, vertical to the surface) to minimize interactions between neighboring slabs. DFT calculations were performed using the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof exchange-correlation functional implemented in the VASP. The energy cutoff was selected to 500 eV. Both the lattice parameters and the atomic coordinates were optimized until the forces on each atom are less than   $  0.02\ eV\mathring{A}^{-1}  $  . The van der Waals interaction between the   $  g-C_{3}N_{4}  $   molecule and   $  SnO_{2}  $   (110) surface was taken into account via a damped vdW correction using the DFT–D3 approach. The Monkhorst–Pack k-point sampling was   $  2\times2\times1  $  . Surface dipole correction was employed to avoid artificial interactions between neighboring slabs.
