# 1. Introduction

One of the most auspicious materials applied lately to the configuration of new generation solar cells in order to gain high-performance photovoltaic devices, is the organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite materials, because of their exceptional optoelectronic features, such as high absorption coefficient   $  [1,2]  $  , strong light-harvesting capability   $  [3]  $  , tunable direct bandgap   $  [4]  $   and long charge carrier diffusion lengths and mobilities   $  [5]  $  .

Optimizing compositional, morphology, and improvement of selective contact materials, together with the design of advanced device constructions and interface engineering, are considered critical parameters to achieve high power conversion efficiency (PCE), which reached almost 25.5% in the last decade   $  [6–11]  $  . These record PCE are now on par with those of the conventional Si-based solar cells and be an adjunct to foremost benefits from the direction of processing

Boosting the stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) is necessary to the industrialization of them. It is believed the dimensional engineering of the perovskite layer may tackle the low stability of PSCs. In this research, a 3D/2D heterostructure layer based on 4-Fluoro-Phenethylammonium iodide (4FPEAI) was successfully fabricated to improve the humidity resistance and the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the 3D PSCs. After that, the research aims to boost the quality of the fabricated 3D/2D layer and further improving the photovoltaic performance and stability of devices. For this purpose, the formamidinium bromide (FABr) salt was introduced as an additive to the 2D precursor. The FABr additive treated the interfacial of the 3D/2D perovskite/HTL and significantly increased the charge transport processes in the PSCs. Results showed with  $ 5\% $  of FABr additive in a molar ratio, the PCE of 3D/2D PSCs raised to  $ 21.79\% $  from the  $ 19.61\% $  for the net 3D/2D PSCs. Besides, the humidity and irradiance stability of the PSCs dramatically were improved with the FABr modification of the 3D/2D perovskite layer. The suggested approach, i.e., modification of the 3D/2D layer to achieve a more desirable 3D/2D layer, may provide a practical step towards the PSC development.

The FABr additive drastically passivated the grain boundaries and suppressed the charges recombination at hybrid perovskite/HTL layers, indicating origins of PCE and stability improvements of 3D/2D PSCs. In conclusion, the current work exemplifies that engineering the interfaces between 3D and 2D perovskite levels is essential for concurrently accomplishing PSCs with extraordinary PCE and stability.

# 2. Experimental details

# 2.1. Materials and chemicals

Unless otherwise, materials and solvents were purchased from Greatcell Solar and Sigma-Aldrich, respectively, and used without further purification. Lead iodide  $ (\mathrm{PbI}_2) $  and lead bromide  $ (\mathrm{PbBr}_2) $  were purchased from TCI. Cesium iodide (CsI) was purchased from Alfa Aser.  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  paste was purchased from Dyesol.

# 2.2. Solution preparation

A 1.4 M Cs $ _{0.05} $ (FA $ _{0.95} $ MA $ _{0.05} $ ) $ _{0.95} $ Pb(I $ _{0.95} $ Br $ _{0.05} $ ) $ _{3} $  stock solution was prepared by solving formamidinium iodide (FAI, 99.99%), PbI $ _{2} $  (99.99%), methylammonium bromide (MABr, 99.99%), PbBr $ _{2} $  (99.99%), and CsI (99.999%) in DMF:DMSO mixed solvent (4:1 v/v). The mesoporous TiO $ _{2} $  (mp-TiO $ _{2} $ ) precursor was obtained from diluting of TiO $ _{2} $  paste (30 NR-D) in ethanol (1:6 wt%). The 4-Fluoro-Phenethylammonium iodide (4FPEAI, 99%) precursor was prepared with different concentrations in isopropanol (IPA, 99.5%). Besides, 10 mg of formamidinium bromide (FABr, 99.99%) was solved in 1 ml IPA and used as additive source. The hole transport layer (HTL) precursor was prepared with dissolving 73 mg of Spiro-OMeTAD (99%) in 1 ml of chlorobenzene (CB, 99.8%) mixed with 17.5

$ \mu \mathrm{L} $ ,  $ 28.8~\mu \mathrm{L} $  and  $ 8\mu \mathrm{L} $  of bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide lithium salt (Li-TFSI,  $ 98\% $ ) solution (520 mg/mL in acetonitrile), 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP,  $ 98\% $ ) and tris(2-(1H-pyrazole-1-yl)-4-tertbutylpyridine)-cobalt(III)tris(bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide) (FK209,  $ 98\% $ ) solution (180 mg/mL in acetonitrile), respectively.

# 2.3. Device fabrication

The etched F-doped   $  SnO_{2}  $   (FTO, Pilkington, TEC15) substrates were cleaned with an ultrasonic bath using soap, distilled water, acetone, and IPA successively for 10 min. As compact blocking   $  TiO_{2}  $   (c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  ) layer, 40 nm   $  TiO_{2}  $   was sprayed on FTO substrates at 450 °C using titanium diisopropoxide bis(acetylacetonate) (TAA; Sigma-Aldrich) in IPA. The diluted mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   paste in ethanol (1:6 wt%) was spin-coated on c-  $  TiO_{2}  $   at 6000 rpm for 30 s to fabricate electron transport layer (ETL), followed by annealing at 500 °C for 30 min. The perovskite films were fabricated through a one-step deposition process inside an   $  N_{2}  $  -filled glovebox. In brief, the triple cation perovskite solution was poured on the mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   layer and spin-coated with a two-step program, first at 1000 rpm for 10 s and then 6000 rpm for 30 s. In the 25 s of the faster step, 200 μL of CB immediately was dripped on the intermediate perovskite layer. After that, the perovskite layers were annealed at 100 °C for 60 min. To fabricate 3D/2D bi-layers, the 3D perovskite layers were dipped into a 4FPEAI precursor with or without FABr additive for 15 s. After post-dipping treatment of the 3D perovskite layers, they were spin-coated at 4000 rpm for 30 s, followed by annealing at 110 °C for 5 min. As an HTL layer, the Spiro-OMeTAD precursor solution was spin-coated at 4000 rpm with an acceleration of 2000 rpm/s for 30 s on top of the perovskite layers. Finally, an 80 nm gold was evaporated on the HTL as electrode layer.

# 2.4. Characterization

The surface morphologies of the 3D/2D perovskite layers were monitored with FE-SEM (TESCAN, Mira 3) images. The X-ray diffraction spectra of the hybrid perovskite layers were examined to study the crystal structure with a Bruker, D8 advance X-ray diffraction spectrometer. The UV–vis spectra of the hybrid 3D/2D perovskite thin films were investigated by a spectrophotometer (LAMBDA 1050, Perkin Elmer). The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) response of films was measured using a HORIBA Fluorolog-III system. Time-resolved PL (TRPL) response of different perovskite layers were recorded with FLS980 spectrofluorometer (Edinburgh) by exciting the samples with a pulsed diode laser at 485. The atomic force microscope (AFM) was employed to further study of topographic of perovskite films (XE 70, Park System). The photovoltaic characteristics of the perovskite solar cells under simulated irradiation of AM 1.5 (450 W Xenon light source from Oriel) were monitored with a Keithley Model 2400. The PV devices were masked with an active area of   $  0.1 \, cm^2  $  . The voltage step in I-V tests was fixed at 50 mV/s. The light of the simulator was calibrated with a Si-reference solar cell.

# 4. Conclusion

In this study, 3D/2D PSCs were fabricated by immersing 3D perovskite layers into a 4FPEAI solution in IPA with desirable concentration. It was found via 2D passivation of 3D PSCs, PV properties and stability of devices improved. In the next step, research focused on the engineering of the formation of the 3D/2D hybrid perovskite layer to improve the mixed-dimensional approach. For this aim, FABr salt was separately dissolved in IPA and added to the 2D precursor in different molar ratios. Amazingly, with  $ 5\% $  of the FABr additive, GBs passivation caused by 2D solution drastically increased, and better interface engineering at perovskite/HTL layers is obtained. The presence of FABr additive in the 2D source solution caused the formation of a more oriented 2D capping layer, while properties of the bottom 3D perovskite layer not only keep without damages but also improve. Such 3D/2D formation is a missing achievement in most researches based on mixed-dimensional techniques. In practice, the FABr additive increases the carriers' lifetime in the perovskite layer, indicating a suppressed charges recombination in it. Besides, the nonradiative recombination is reduced, suggesting more electron-hole generation in the perovskite layer. Through 3D/2D engineering with FABr dopant, a champion PCE of  $ 21.79\% $  was obtained for the treated 3D/2D PSCs, higher than the control 3D/2D PSCs with a

champion of  $ 19.61\% $ . Notably, the humidity resistance of the treated 3D/2D devices increased compared to the control 3D/2D devices, which is due to robust GBs passivation and better formation of a 2D layer on top the 3D perovskite layer.
