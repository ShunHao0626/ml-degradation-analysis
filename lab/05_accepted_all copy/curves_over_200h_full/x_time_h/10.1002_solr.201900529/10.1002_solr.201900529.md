# Abstract

The perovskite solar cell is a highly competitive candidate for the next-generation photovoltaic technology. Defects in the perovskite grain boundaries and on the film surfaces however have significant impact on both the device efficiency and environmental stability. Here, we report a strategy using organic dyes as additives to passivate the defect states and produce more n-type perovskite films, thereby improving charge transport and decreasing charge recombination. # Introduction

Organic-inorganic halide perovskites have drawn worldwide attention due to their exceptional optoelectronic properties, e.g., high absorption coefficients, $ ^{[1, 2]} $  adjustable bandgaps $ ^{[3, 4]} $  and long carrier diffusion lengths, $ ^{[5, 6]} $  which enable them to be very promising applications in photovoltaic technology. Today, the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of single-junction perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been exceeded  $ 25\% $  thanks to a series of strategies including optimizing composition, morphology and interface engineering, and it is now in parity with crystalline silicon solar cells ( $ 26\% $ ). $ ^{[7-11]} $

High-quality perovskite film is critical to achieve highly efficient and stable PSCs. Perovskite films are generally fabricated by precursor solution deposition at low temperature and are inevitably polycrystalline with a large number of defects such as uncoordinated ions and dangling bonds on the surface, along the grain boundaries, and even inside the bulk grains.  $  ^{[12-14]}  $   These defects can act as charge recombination centers, leading to energy loss and thus limiting device performance.  $  ^{[15-17]}  $   Furthermore, these defects are sensitive to illumination and heat (having low activation energy), and can also initialize permeation of moisture/water, significantly decreasing the device operational

In order to achieve high-quality perovskite films for high-performance PSCs, it is natural idea to overcome these serious issues. Additive components have been introduced into the perovskite precursor to modulate the film growth kinetics and passivate defects. $ ^{[22-24]} $  The selection of additives spans from small organic molecules, $ ^{[25-27]} $  inorganic salts, $ ^{[28]} $  long-chain polymers, $ ^{[29]} $  to ionic liquids, $ ^{[18]} $  and so on. It has been proved that the halide vacancies, uncoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  and  $ \mathrm{Pb}-\mathrm{I} $  anti-site defects at grain boundaries can be passivated by hydrogen bonding and coordination interactions with the additives. As a result, significant improvement in both efficiency and stability of the corresponding PSCs devices can be achieved. $ ^{[30, 31]} $  Furthermore, the long-chain polymers can cross-link perovskite grain boundaries, leading to enhance the mechanical stability and durability of the PSCs. $ ^{[32]} $

Organic dyes, used for coloring textiles for thousands of years, have seen a revival in modern electronics due to their low cost, excellent photochemical properties, high environmental stability, high charge mobility and ease of chemical structure modification.  $  ^{[33-35]}  $   For example, organic dye indigo-based biocompatible organic field-effect transistors can yield ambipolar mobilities approaching   $  0.5 \, cm^{2} \, V^{-1} \, s^{-1}  $   with excellent air stability due to the high crystalline structures and reversible redox properties.  $  ^{[36]}  $   Besides the exceptional mobility and stability, organic dyes can provide sites for hydrogen bonds, which makes them great candidates to passivate the perovskite film defects through hydrogen bonding. Furthermore, the optical band gaps of organic dyes can facilitate additional light capture and subsequent energy transfer to the perovskite films.  $  ^{[37, 38]}  $

Consequently, organic dyes are promising additives for perovskite-based devices.

Here, we present a simple and cost-effective organic dye as an additive for passivating defects to realize high-quality  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $  (MAPbI₃) films with low trap density. The organic dye we employed is 5-Chloroisatin (Isatin-Cl) and its chemical structure is displayed in Figure 1a. The effect of Isatin-Cl on crystallization, surface electronic structures, trap states, mobilities, charge dynamics and stability of the perovskite films are systemically investigated. The results clearly revealed that the Isatin-Cl not only effectively passivates uncoordinated  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  defects but also produces a more n-type perovskite film. Correspondingly, the PCE of the Isatin-Cl-modified PSCs is enhanced from  $ 18.13\% $  to  $ 20.18\% $  with a significant improvement in Voc, Jsc and FF as well as a negligible hysteresis compared to control PSCs. Furthermore, attributed to hydrophobic Isatin-Cl in the grain boundaries and on the top film surface attached by hydrogen bonding with the perovskite, the stability of the PSCs towards humidity and thermal stress is remarkably enhanced.

Device stability is a critical factor for commercialization in the future for PSCs. To assess the impact of the Isatin-Cl on the stability of the PSCs, we first probe the stability of the perovskite devices without encapsulation under ambient air in 45% relative humidity at room temperature (Figure 6a). The PCE of the pristine devices drops by 45% after 350 h storage, while the devices with Isatin-Cl show a PCE drop of only 10% for the same time, indicating the Isatin-Cl additive dramatically improves the humidity stability of the PSCs. Moreover, we examine the thermal stability of the PSCs under nitrogen atmosphere at 85 °C temperature (Figure 6b). It is found that 70% of the PCE of the Isatin-Cl modified PSCs can be maintained after thermal aging for 24 h, whereas the pristine PSCs is nearly completely degraded. These results suggest a strong positive impact of the Isatin-Cl additive on the operational stability of PSCs.

# Conclusion

In summary, we introduce organic dyes as additives into perovskite films and find coordination and hydrogen bonding interactions between  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  and the organic dye Isatin-Cl that significantly improve photovoltaic performance and stability of the PSCs. The carbonyl groups and hydrogen-bond structures on Isatin-Cl effectively passivate the defect states in the perovskite grain boundaries and on the top perovskite film surface, which facilitate the charge transport. By passivating the defects, the Isatin-Cl additive suppresses the rate of the trap-assisted recombination and moves the  $ E_{\mathrm{F}} $  closer to the conduction band, creating a more n-type perovskite film. As the result, we achieve a significant enhancement of the PCE of PSCs from  $ 18.13\% $  to  $ 20.18\% $  with a large improvement in Voc, Jsc and FF as well as a negligible hysteresis. # Film Characterization:

SEM (Carl Zeiss, Merlin) and AFM (Asylum Research, Oxford) measurements were performed to examine the film top surface morphology. XRD patterns were measured by an X-ray diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation. The FTIR transmission spectra was carried out with an ART-FTIR spectrometer (PerkinElmer) at 400-4000 cm $  ^{-1}  $ . The UV–vis absorption spectra were measured via a TU-1901 instrument. PL spectra were received by a fluorescence spectrometer (PerkinElmer LS 55). TRPL decay process was monitored via a TCSPC system. The NMR spectra were recorded in N, N-dimethylformamide d7 by liquid nuclear magnetic resonance spectrometer (Bruker Avance 500 MHz).
