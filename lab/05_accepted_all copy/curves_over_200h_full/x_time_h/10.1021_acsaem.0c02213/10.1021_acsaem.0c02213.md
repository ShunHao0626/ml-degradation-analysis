# Improving the Performance of Carbon-Based Perovskite Solar Modules (70 cm  $  ^{2}  $  ) by Incorporating Cesium Halide in Mesoporous TiO  $  _{2}  $

Kavya S. Keremane, Sateesh Prathapani, Lew Jia Haur, Annalisa Bruno, Anish Priyadarshi,  $  ^{*}  $   Airody Vasudeva Adhikari,  $  ^{*}  $   and Subodh G. Mhaisalkar  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: We present the fabrication of highly efficient large-area carbon-based perovskite solar cells (C-PSCs) using CsX (X = Cl, Br, and I)-modified mesoporous (mp)   $  TiO_{2}  $   beads of 40 nm size as an electron transport material. Here, triple-layered scaffolds made of cesium halide-modified   $  TiO_{2}  $   exhibit efficient charge extraction as confirmed by enhanced photoluminescence quenching and inhibit the UV-activated degradation processes of perovskite, leading to an enhanced operational stability. Among the three cesium halide modifications, devices containing CsBr-modified   $  TiO_{2}  $   showed the highest short-circuit current density, yielding a photoconversion efficiency (PCE) of 12.59% of the device, with   $  0.7\ cm^{2}  $   active area and 11.55% for a large-area module (  $  70\ cm^{2}  $  ). These devices are stable in an ambient atmosphere (  $  25\ ^{\circ}C  $  , 65–70% RH) over 2700 h as well as at a high temperature (  $  85\ ^{\circ}C  $  ) over 750 h with virtually no hysteresis.

KEYWORDS: perovskite solar cells, carbon-based, MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  , hysteresis, CsBr doping, improved stability

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Recently developed organic–inorganic hybrid perovskite semiconductor materials have received great attention as an important light harvesters to convert solar energy into electricity, owing to their superior properties such as solution-processability, low-exciton binding energy, long free charge diffusion lengths, high charge carrier mobilities with long lifetimes, outstanding cost effectiveness, and energy-efficient light absorption.  $  ^{1-4}  $   In the past decade, relentless efforts have been made towards the development of new device architectures for the fabrication of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with attractive materials, achieving photoconversion efficiencies (PCEs) from low (3.8%) to very high (25.2%) values.  $  ^{5-8}  $   Although the achieved PCE is high, PSCs face challenges with the stability of perovskite, reproducibility in device performance, and hence, large-scale production.

In order to overcome the challenges of the stability of perovskite, significant efforts have been made towards the development of new materials and device engineering.  $  ^{9,10}  $   Such efforts include the investigations on crystal formation, precise control over morphology, the selection of solvents and additives, computational studies, and interface engineering.  $  ^{11}  $   Furthermore, numerous attempts have been made to improve the long-term stability of PSCs by achieving high-quality perovskite layers through surface coverage with grain size modifications via solvent annealing, Ostwald ripening, gas treatment, and additives.  $  ^{12}  $   Nevertheless, to date, the stability

The conventional PSC architecture consists of a transparent electrode   $  TiO_{2}  $   as an electron transport layer (ETL), perovskite as a light harvester, Spiro-OMeTAD or PEDOT:PSS as the hole transport material (HTM), and the back electrode Au/Ag.  $  ^{14,15}  $   Although this architecture leads to the highest PCEs, the problem of instability of the organic HTM as well as perovskite in the presence of moisture in ambient air challenges the long-term use of the device, creating many hurdles for commercialization.  $  ^{16}  $   Also, organic HTMs are expensive, increasing cost constraints for their production.  $  ^{17}  $   In order to overcome the challenges of the material stability and cost constraints, a new PSC device architecture with fully printable carbon-based mesoporous electrodes has been developed.  $  ^{18,19}  $   The fully printable mesostructured carbon-based PSC (C-PSC) architecture consists of a triple-layered screen-printed mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $  ,   $  ZrO_{2}  $  , and carbon scaffold, which is expected to play a pivotal role for the scale-up of low-

The introduction of Cs or related compounds in   $  TiO_{2}  $   mesoporous and compact layers has been studied by various groups for the performance enhancement of planar devices.  $  ^{[11,39]}  $   Cesium introduction into the   $  TiO_{2}  $   compact layer helps in enhancing the conduction band maximum and reducing interfacial resistance.  $  ^{[40]}  $   By introducing CsX (X = Br and I) modification (coating) to mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $ , a shift of titanium (Ti) and oxygen (O) to lower energy is induced and the areal density of pinholes is reduced, which further implements the suppression of oxygen vacancies operating as the electron trap.  $  ^{[41-43]}  $   Also, under UV light exposure, the photogenerated holes in mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $   react with oxygen, which further becomes deep traps and lead to charge recombination.  $  ^{[44-46]}  $   The size of the   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticles in the mesoporous layers play an important role in enhancing the efficiency of planar PSCs.  $  ^{[47-49]}  $   Recently, as an improvement, the porous   $  TiO_{2}  $   beads of 40 nm size with an increased surface area of 200 m  $  ^{2}  $  /g have been used in the conventional planar architecture.  $  ^{[42]}  $

Encouraged by a previous study on   $  TiO_{2}  $   modification in planar architectures, we employed the mesoporous layer of   $  TiO_{2}  $   with CsX as an active interface modifier in the fabrication of carbon-based PSCs with an active area of   $  0.7\ cm^{2}  $  . Furthermore, monolithic perovskite solar modules with a large-area of   $  70\ cm^{2}  $   were fabricated for further tuning them to the industrial needs. The 40 NR-D-unmodified and CsX-modified mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   pastes were provided by EPFL, Switzerland, and the detailed preparation of the paste has been reported earlier.  $  ^{42}  $   In the proposed PSCs, the composition of the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  -AVAI crystal phase and light absorption properties were comprehensively investigated by powdered X-ray diffraction (PXRD) and UV/visible absorption spectra. The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) decay experiments were also conducted to reveal the effect of cesium halide-modified   $  TiO_{2}  $   on charge extraction and carrier lifetime, respectively. The quantum efficiency measurements were performed for various CsX-modified   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based C-PSCs to obtain their incident photon to current conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra. Finally, the improved stability aspects were presented by evaluating the current–voltage curves on C-PSCs exposed to ambient and high-temperature conditions for an extended period of time.

The kinetics of charge transfer between electron selective layers (ESLs) and perovskite absorbers can be efficiently measured using steady-state PL and TRPL spectroscopic studies.  $  ^{53-55}  $   Figure 3a displays the steady-state PL quenching of the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   films deposited on “glass/c-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $   (with and without CsX)”. As observed, the MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   perovskite layer shows characteristic PL emission with an emission peak at 766 nm, which corresponds to its band gap.  $  ^{46}  $   Also, the observed PL emission peak is rather broad, which spans in the

range of 700–825 nm; this broad emission is attributed to the nanocrystalline nature of perovskite in the mesoporous structure. When the perovskite is deposited on the ETL, the photoelectrons in the conduction band of perovskite would transfer to the conduction band of mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $  or modified mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $ . The extent of electron transfer from perovskite to mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $  reduces radiative recombination in perovskite and hence causes the reduction in PL intensity, which is termed as PL quenching.  $  ^{40}  $  From Figure 3a, it is clear that the PL quenching for the CsX@TiO  $  _{2}  $  samples is higher than that for unmodified mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $ . This observed high PL quenching clearly indicates the faster electron extraction at the CsX-modified mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $ /perovskite interface. Among the three halides, the electron extraction at the “CsBr-modified mp-TiO  $  _{2}  $ /perovskite” is expected to be superior.

In general, PSCs often exhibit the phenomenon of hysteresis where the forward scan does not trace back to the backward scan in their J-V curves. This is due to the presence of mobile ions in perovskite and the electronic traps at the charge collection layers.  $  ^{59}  $   The C-PSCs normally exhibit low hysteresis because the ionic motion in perovskite is hindered by the entire mesoscopic structure. Furthermore, for the reduction of hysteresis, it is necessary to mitigate the trap density that minimizes interfacial recombination.  $  ^{60}  $   Because the CsX surface modification on the   $  TiO_{2}  $   nanoparticles is expected to reduce the surface traps, the fabricated devices are tested for hysteresis. To evaluate the hysteresis quantitatively, the hysteresis index (HI) is defined using eq 1:  $  ^{33}  $

$$
\mathrm{HI} = \frac {J _ {\mathrm{RS}} (0 . 5 V _ {O C}) - J _ {\mathrm{FS}} (0 . 5 V _ {O C})}{J _ {\mathrm{RS}} (0 . 5 V _ {O C})}
$$

(1)

where  $  J_{\mathrm{RS}}  $  ( $  0.5\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{OC}}  $ ) and  $  J_{\mathrm{FS}}  $  ( $  0.5\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{OC}}  $ ) represent the photocurrent density at  $  50\%  $  of  $  V_{\mathrm{OC}}  $  for the reverse scan (RS) and forward scan (FS) directions, respectively; normally, HI takes values between "zero to one." The value "zero" indicates no

hysteresis with the meaning that there is no difference in the voltage-dependent photocurrent in the RS or FS. The value "one" indicates high hysteresis, underlining a significant difference in the voltage-dependent photocurrent (a magnitude of   $  J_{RS}  $  ) in FS and RS.  $  ^{60,61}  $   The HI values were determined for C-PSCs made of unmodified and CsX-modified mp   $  TiO_{2}  $  , and their values are tabulated in Table S3. As anticipated, CsX-modified C-PSCs display relatively lower HI than that of standard 40NR-D and standard 30NR-D. Furthermore, the CsBr-modified C-PSCs show the least HI value of 0.0065, producing the best performance among all.

In summary, we developed highly efficient and stable C-PSCs using the CsBr-modified mp-  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  beads of  $ 40\mathrm{nm} $  size as a superior electron transport material, with a PCE of  $ 12.59\% $  (active area  $ 0.7~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ ) and  $ 11.55\% $  (active area  $ 70~\mathrm{cm}^2 $ ). In the device, the use of high surface area 40NR-D mp-  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  beads allows effective infiltration of perovskite into the ETL
