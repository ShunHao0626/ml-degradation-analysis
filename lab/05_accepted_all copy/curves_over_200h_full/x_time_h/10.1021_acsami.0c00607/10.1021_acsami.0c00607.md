# Abstract

Broadening the near-infrared (NIR) spectrum of device is critical to further improve the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the perovskite solar cells (PSCs). In this work, novel   $  Cu_{2}Cd_{x}Zn_{1-x}SnS_{4}  $   (CZTS: Cd) film prepared by thermal evaporation method was employed as the NIR light-harvesting layer to complement absorption of the perovskite. At the same time, Au nanorods (NRs) were introduced into the hole transporting layer (HTL) to boost the utilization of CZTS: Cd to NIR light through localized surface plasmon effect. The perovskite/CZTS: Cd and Au NRs integrated PSCs can extend the photoelectric response until to 900 nm. And more, the well-matched energy levels between CZTS: Cd and perovskite can effectively extract holes from perovskite and depress the charge-carrier recombination. As a result, the champion PSC device insulating with CZTS: Cd and Au NRs demonstrates a remarkably increased PCE from 19.30% to 21.11%. The modified PSC devices also demonstrate highly improved long time stability. The device retains the PCE of 87% after 500 h even under air with a relative humidity of 85%, implying the superior humidity stability of the devices with CZTS: Cd. This work suggests that perovskite/inorganic integrated structure is a promising strategy to broaden and boost the NIR response of the PSCs.

# Introduction

Organic-inorganic PSCs have received great attentions because of their outstanding photovoltaic properties and low cost fabrication methods   $  {}^{1-4}  $  . The certified PCE of PSCs is 25.2%, which is comparable to that of commercialized crystalline silicon solar cells, making it highly competitive in the future photovoltaic market   $  {}^{5}  $  . However, current perovskite absorber materials only absorb a small fraction of the ultraviolet (UV) and visible ranges (400–800 nm) due to its optical band gap of about 1.60 eV, which only occupy 45–50% in whole solar spectrum (280–2500 nm)   $  {}^{6}  $  . Clearly, the major reason for limiting the PCE of PSCs is that their ineffective utilization to the NIR region in solar spectrum   $  {}^{7-8}  $  . Therefore, efficiently utilizing the NIR light has become an important approach to further improve the PCE of PSCs. One common strategy is replacing Pb by Sn to form hybrid perovskite material of   $  (\mathrm{MASn}_{1-x}\mathrm{Pb}_{x}\mathrm{I}_{3})  $  , which can decrease the band gap of perovskite and extend the NIR spectral absorption of device   $  {}^{9}  $  . Although the photocurrent response of   $  MASn_{1-x}Pb_{x}I_{3}  $   extends to over 1000 nm, the stability of device becomes rather worse in contrast to that of the Pb-based device due to the poor Sn (II) stability   $  {}^{10,11}  $  . A more serious problem is that reducing the band gap will result in the decreasing open circuit voltage (Voc) of PSCs, which deteriorate the device performance   $  {}^{12}  $  . Therefore, it is critical to find other methods to effectively broaden the NIR spectral absorption of device for the development of PSCs.

The combination of perovskite materials and complementary absorbing materials is another effective way to improve the utilization of NIR of PSCs. Inorganic chalcogenide material can be considered as the light-harvesting materials to broaden the NIR spectral absorption  $ ^{13} $ . In addition, it can be also as an efficient HTL for PSCs due to the consideration of suitable energy level alignment and hole mobility  $ ^{14,15,16,17} $ . HTL plays an important role in effective charge extraction  $ ^{18-21} $ . It is well known that CZTS has suitable valence band position, p-type conductivity and high carrier mobility, which can be selected as an effective HTL. In addition, CZTS can also be as a very promising light-absorbing material considering its high optical absorption coefficient of  $ 10^{5} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1} $ , and the intrinsic p-type doping with a carrier concentration of  $ \approx 10^{15}-10^{18} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3} $   $ ^{21} $ . However, the band gap of CZTS is about  $ 1.55 \mathrm{eV} $ , not suitable be used as a complementary absorbing material to broaden the NIR spectral absorption of PSCs. Some literatures have reported that the band gap of CZTS can be easily adjusted by the cation substitution  $ ^{22,23} $ . Therefore, it is very important to find an ion to decrease the band gap of CZTS, which can make CZTS not only be used as an efficient HTL but also as light-harvesting materials to complement absorption of perovskite and extend the photoresponse of PSCs to NIR region. Moreover, the long time stability of the modified device also largely increases.

# Experimental

ITO-coated glass substrates are etched by zinc powder and HCl to define the electrode patterns and washed in deionized water, acetone, and ethanol for 20 min, respectively. The FTO substrates were bought from Ying kou you xuan Trade co., Ltd. The ultraviolet-ozone was used to remove the organic residues of ITO surface. To fabricate the compact   $  SnO_{2}  $   layer, the   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloid solution was by water to the concentration of 2.14 wt% and was spin-coated on ITO substrates at 5000 rpm for 30 s and then annealed at 150 °C for 30 min. In order to study the effect of Cd doping content on the band-gap of CZTS film, optical absorption properties of the CZTS: Cd films were characterized. Figure S4 shows the UV–vis absorption spectra of the CZTS film doped with different concentration of Cd. Compared to that of the CZTS, the absorption edge for the Cd doped film red shifts, which is consistent with the previous work   $  {}^{24}  $  . Figure 2a shows the corresponding curves of   $  (\alpha h\nu)^{2}-h\nu  $  , which is used to determine the band gap of CZTS: Cd films. The optical band gap reduces from 1.55 to 1.44 eV as Cd doping content increases from 0 to 15%. The band-gap of CZTS is mainly dominated by conduction band minimum (CBM) and valence band maximum (VBM). VBM is mainly affected by anti-bonding of p-d hybridization between   $  Cu^{+}  $  cation and   $  S^{2-}  $   anion, while CBM is mainly controlled by s-p and s-s hybrid anti-bonding states between the   $  S^{2-}  $   and   $  Sn^{4+}  $     $  {}^{24}  $  . In this work, the lattice expansion of CZTS caused by replacing of   $  Zn^{2+}  $   with   $  Cd^{2+}  $   can weaken the antibonding states of the s-s and s-p between the   $  S^{2-}  $   and   $  Sn^{4+}  $  , leading to a decrease in CBM. Therefore, the band gap of CZTS decreases as the content of Cd doping increases. The influence of film thickness on the device performance was firstly investigated. The PCE of device increases as the thickness of CZTS changes from 0 to 80 nm, and decreases as it further increases to 100 nm (Figure S9). The influence of doping contents of   $  Cd^{2+}  $   in CZTS on device performance of PSCs was also studied (Figure 4a). For the commercialization of PSCs, the most critical issue is to improve the device stability. The long-term stability of device with Spiro-OMeTAD and CZTS: Cd/Spiro-OMeTAD at 25 °C was measured, as shown in Figure 6a. The PCE of the control device retains only 10% of its initial PCE after being stored under ambient air for 2000 h, reflecting quite severe performance degradation. Dramatically, the device with CZTS: Cd maintains 90% of its initial PCE after aging for 3000 h. We also measured humidity stability of device with CZTS: Cd, as shown in Figure 6b. The device retains over 87% of its initial PCEs after 500 h under relative humidity of 85%, which is much better than the Spiro-OMeTAD based device with the same environment. In order to explore the intrinsic mechanism of enhanced stability of devices, the structural change of the perovskite absorber layer with different HTLs during the degradation was characterized by XRD characterizations. Figures 6c and d show XRD patterns of the perovskite film with Spiro-OMeTAD and CZTS: Cd/Spiro-OMeTAD, respectively. For the control film, an extra XRD diffraction peak appears at 12.49° under maintaining at ambient atmosphere for 3000 h, which can be attributed to the (101) diffraction peak of PbI₂, indicating the partial decomposition of the perovskite film. However, the perovskite film with CZTS: Cd does not show a distinct peak at 12.49°, implying that the perovskite film has not been decomposed yet. The enhanced device stability should be firstly ascribed to the inherent stability of CZTS compared to organic Spiro-OMeTAD. To further investigate the reason, we measured the contact angle of perovskite/CZTS and perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD films (Figure 6c-d). The result indicates that the perovskite film with CZTS has an improved water contact angle (84°) than that of the Spiro-OMeTAD film (55°). This result implies that the CZTS: Cd film is more hydrophobic than organic Spiro-OMeTAD film, which can effectively suppress the moisture permeation into the perovskite film, improving the stability of PSCs. In addition, the present CZTS: Cd film owns dense structure due to the preparation of thermal deposition method, which can also prevent the across of water molecular  $ ^{39} $ . The light-soaking stability of control device and the devices with CZTS: Cd was measured under ambient condition without any encapsulation for 500 hours in Figure S13. The PCE of the device with CZTS: Cd maintained more than 90% of its initial efficiency. However, the PCE of the control device reduced to 60% of its initial efficiency, indicating that CZTS: Cd hole transfer layer showed a better light-soaking stability than control device.

# Conclusions

In summary, Cd doped CZTS films were prepared and employed as both a light-harvesting material and an efficient inorganic HTL of PSCs. On one hand, the doping of Cd decreased the band gap of CZTS, and made the energy levels of CZTS matching well with the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskite, leading to more effective charge transformation and collection. On the other hand, the introduction of Cd doped CZTS into the PSCs also expanded the light harvesting range of the PSCs from  $ 800~\mathrm{nm} $  to  $ 900~\mathrm{nm} $ . More importantly, in order to further boost the NIR light harvesting of Cd doped CZTS, Au NRs were further introduced into the Spiro-OMeTAD layer of the PSCs. As a consequence, the utilization of infrared light around  $ 830~\mathrm{nm} $  was highly enhanced due to LSPR effect of Au NRs. The champion device with Cd doped CZTS and Au NRs demonstrated extended photoelectric response to  $ 900~\mathrm{nm} $  by integrating the CZTS: Cd and perovskite layers, and a PCE improvement from  $ 19.30\% $  to  $ 21.08\% $ . The present work indicates that a perovskite/semiconductor integrated structure combining LSPR effect of noble metal nanostructure is a novel and promising strategy to enhance utilization of NIR for PSCs.
