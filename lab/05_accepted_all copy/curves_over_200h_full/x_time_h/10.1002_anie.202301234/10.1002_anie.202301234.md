# Crystallinity Regulation and Defects Passivation for Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells Using Fully Conjugated Porous Aromatic Frameworks

Yege Jing $ ^{+} $ , Chen Wang $ ^{+} $ , Yi Chen, Lin Liu, Liying Yin, Fengchao Cui, Ning Zhang, $ ^{*} $  Shanpeng Wen, $ ^{*} $  and Guangshan Zhu $ ^{*} $

Abstract: Fully conjugated porous aromatic frameworks (PAFs) have been constructed through Gilch reaction. The obtained PAFs have rigid conjugated backbones, high specific surface area, and excellent stability. The prepared PAF-154 and PAF-155 have been successfully applied in the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) by doping into the perovskite layer. The champion PSC devices afford a power conversion efficiency of 22.8% and 22.4%. It is found that the PAFs can be used as an efficient nucleation template, thus regulating the perovskite crystallinity. Meanwhile, PAFs can also passivate defects and promote carriers transporting in the perovskite film. By the comparative study with their linear counterpart, we unravel that the efficacy of PAFs is highly related to their porous structure and rigid fully conjugated networks. The unencapsulated devices with PAFs doping exhibit outstanding long-term stability, retaining 80% of their initial efficiencies after half-year storage in ambient conditions.

# Introduction

Porous aromatic frameworks (PAFs) are an important category of porous organic polymers (POPs) constructed from C–C bond linked aromatic-based building units. The structural and composition features endow PAF materials with rigid skeletons, ultra-high surface areas, and remarkable stability.  $  ^{[1]}  $   Because of their tunable chemistry plus their chemical stability, PAFs are regarded as a new class of porous frameworks, distinct from inorganic porous solids and metal organic frameworks (MOFs). The past decade has

witnessed the rapid development of PAFs. They have exhibited great potential in adsorption, separation, biochemistry, sensing, photochemistry, electrochemistry, catalysis, and photovoltaic devices.  $  ^{[2-4]}  $

The organo-metal-halides perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are one of the most successful photovoltaic devices over the past ten years. This is originated from their excellent properties, such as low exciton binding energy  $  ^{[7]}  $  and high absorption coefficient.  $  ^{[5]}  $  The power conversion efficiency (PCE) has rapidly increased from 3.8% to 25.7%.  $  ^{[5-18]}  $  Additive engineering plays a critical role in improving the PSCs device efficiency and stability, including the co-solvents, organic/inorganic ions/salts, Lewis acid or base and the zwitterion.  $  ^{[19,20]}  $  Distinct from the traditional additives, POPs have drawn attention in the perovskite photovoltaic community due to their unique physicochemical properties in terms of porosity and large specific surface area. For instance, covalent organic frameworks (COFs) were used as scaffolds to template perovskite growth,  $  ^{[21]}  $  channels for ions interexchange,  $  ^{[22]}  $  or inhibitors for halogens segregation in perovskite film.  $  ^{[23]}  $  However, most of the COFs reported are usually based on B–O, C=N node, and reactions involved are highly reversible, which may result in relatively low hydrolytic and oxidative stability.  $  ^{[24,25]}  $  In contrast, porous aromatic frameworks (PAFs) synthesis is carried out through an irreversible coupling reaction that creates strong carbon-carbon covalent bonds to link the rigid aromatic groups together. This ensures a stable framework and therefore high chemical resistance to acidic, alkaline and humid environments. This indicates that PAFs may be more advantageous when applied in PSCs to enhance the device performance and stability simultaneously.

Another challenge for the successful application of PAFs in PSCs is the presence of extended   $  \pi  $  -conjugation throughout the porous materials, since similar materials with an aromatic conjugated architecture (fullerene or graphene) have exhibited the capability in passivating defects and improving the carrier extraction in PSCs.  $  ^{[26,27]}  $   Nevertheless, most of the aromatic fragments in PAFs are connected by   $  sp^{3}  $   carbon, fully conjugated PAFs, i.e. aromatic fragments connected by   $  sp^{2}  $   carbon are less reported.  $  ^{[28,29]}  $   The presence of   $  sp^{3}  $   carbon centers in PAFs inevitably leads to the presence of rigid tetrahedral structural units in the backbone. This blocks the extension of conjugation along the backbone and disrupts electron departure domains along the PAFs backbone.  $  ^{[30]}  $   Constructing fully conjugated PAFs

In this work, we construct C=C-linked fully conjugated PAFs i.e. PAF-154 and PAF-155 with high specific surface area, excellent thermal and chemical stability, and an extended rigid conjugated structure by Gilch reaction using tetraphenylene and pyrene as the core, respectively. By simply doping with PAFs, the perovskite film quality is improved, and so is the photovoltaic performance of the PSCs devices. We demonstrate that the porous PAFs can improve the crystallinity and grain size of perovskite film by templating the nucleation stage. The fully conjugated PAFs can also passivate the perovskite defects and increase the charge transport within the perovskite film. Finally, the PAFs doped PSCs deliver champion efficiency of 22.8% (PAF-154) and 22.4% (PAF-155). The device shelf lifetime is also significantly improved, devices doped with the PAF-154 and PAF-155 can maintain 80% and 70% of the initial PCE after aging for 4000 h under ambient conditions.

# Results and Discussion

The Gilch reaction was first described by Gilch et al. and has become one of the most popular techniques for producing high molecular weight poly(p-phenylene-vinylene)s (PPVs) due to its readily available monomers and mild reaction conditions.  $  ^{[31-33]}  $   First, we designed and synthesized two monomers i.e. 1,1,2,2-tetrakis (4-(bromomethyl)phenylethene (TBPE) and 1,3,6,8-tetrakis (4-(bromomethyl)phenyl) pyrene (TBPPy) (Figure S1–S9). Successively, we prepared PAF-154 and PAF-155 by the condensation polymerization of TBPE and TBPPy in the presence of potassium tert-butoxide (t-BuOK) via the Gilch route (Scheme 1). PAF-154 was obtained as a yellow powder in 82% yield, and PAF-155 was acquired as a brown powder in 72% yield.

The chemical structures of the formed polymer powders were examined by Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy. As shown in Figure 1a, the C–Br symmetric stretching vibrations (600 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ) and asymmetric stretching vibrations (1200 cm  $  ^{-1}  $  ) of TBPE and TBPPy disappear after the condensation polymerization, indicating the removal of Br from the monomer and the completion of condensation reactions. In addition, energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) shows that the content of unreacted Br end groups is almost not detectable in the PAF-154 and PAF-155 units (Figures S10 and S11). This indicates that the Br end groups were completely removed from the monomers. The successful synthesis of the target PAFs was further confirmed by   $  {}^{13}  $  C solid-state NMR (ssNMR) spectroscopy (Figure 1b). In the ssNMR spectrum of PAF-154, the peak at about 129 ppm corresponds to the characteristic signal of the carbon atom in the benzene ring, and the characteristic signals at about 134 ppm and 140 ppm are assigned to carbon atoms for the C=C functionality in two different chemical environments. The morphologies of PAF-154 and PAF-155 were examined by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). SEM images show that both PAF-154 and PAF-155 exhibit morphologies with minute irregular nanoparticle accumulation (Figure S12). As shown in TEM images, both PAF-154 and PAF-155 exhibit irregular particle morphology (Figure S13), which is consistent with the SEM results. In addition, the powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) characterization also shows that both materials are amorphous (Figure S14).

The intrinsic porosities of the two PAFs were investigated by nitrogen adsorption characterization at 77.3 K. As shown in Figure 1d, both PAF-154 and PAF-155 exhibit mixed sorption isotherms of type I. The   $  N_{2}  $   adsorption-desorption isotherms show steep adsorption at low pressure, which indicates the presence of micropores in the prepared PAFs. The Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) specific surface area was calculated to be   $  610 \, m^{2} \, g^{-1}  $   and   $  255 \, m^{2} \, g^{-1}  $   for PAF-154 and PAF-155, respectively. Based on the pore size distribution calculated by nonlocal density functional theory (NL-DFT), both PAF-154 and PAF-155 show a wide pore size distribution. The pore size are 1.2 nm for PAF-154 and 1.7 nm for PAF-155 (Figure S15). The presence of other pore sizes may be caused by the accumulation of nanoparticles and the crossover of pore channels.

The thermal stability of PAFs was investigated by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA). As shown in Figure 1c, the initial decomposition temperature for PAF-154 and PAF-155 is around   $  450^{\circ}  $  C, and the complete degradation temperature is around   $  580^{\circ}  $  C and   $  610^{\circ}  $  C. This indicates that both materials have remarkable thermal stabilities, due to the presence of rigid backbone of aromatic benzene rings and conjugated double bonds. The chemical stability of the materials was assessed by immersing both PAFs in different media such as boiling water (  $  H_{2}O  $  ), dichloromethane (  $  CH_{2}Cl_{2}  $  ), tetrahydrofuran (THF), HCl (6 M) solution, and NaOH (6 M) solution. After three days, FT-IR spectra for PAF-154 and PAF-155 show no significant changes, indicating that the skeletal structures of the PAFs were well maintained under the harsh conditions as described above (Figure S16). The structural feature guarantees the superior thermal and chemical stability of the prepared PAFs.

We further investigated the effect of PAF on the perovskite film crystallization and the PSCs performance. The saturated PAFs dispersion in DMF could be stably dispersed for days. The preparation procedure is detailed in the Supporting Information. Figure S20 shows the  $ J - V $  characteristics of devices fabricated from different PAFs doping in the precursor solution. The detailed parameters for these devices are summarized in Table S1. The best device performance was obtained at  $ 10~\mu \mathrm{L} $  PAFs doping. Further addition of PAFs beyond  $ 10~\mu \mathrm{L} $  caused a negative performance change. We studied the morphology of the perovskite films with different PAF-154 dispersion addition through SEM. As shown in Figure S21, the perovskite films show a measurable increase in perovskite grain size and a more uniform grain size after the PAF-154 doping for 5 and  $ 10~\mu \mathrm{L} $ . However, when the doping amount of PAF-154 was increased to more than  $ 20~\mu \mathrm{L} $ , rod-like nanostructures were observed. Pinholes formed in the films when doped with  $ 30~\mu \mathrm{L} $  PAF-154. This leads to the inhibition of charge collection and possible leakage, which explains the losses of overall performance observed in the corresponding devices.

We posited that the remarkable effects of PAFs doping in improving the PSCs performance was originated from the specificity of the pore structure of PAFs. To verify this, we chose a structurally analogue i.e. linear poly[2-methoxy-5-(2-ethylhexyloxy)-1,4-phenylenevinylene] (MEH-PPV) with no porosity as a dopant to investigate its device performance under identical conditions. The  $ J - V $  characteristics are shown in Figure 3b, and the detailed parameters are listed in Table 1. MEH-PPV doping led to a decreased PCE to  $ 18.76\% $ , with an overall loss in performance metrics. The EQE spectrum and the integrated  $ J_{sc} $  confirm the decreased photocurrent density. Statistical PCE distribution also exhibits a clear drop in the average efficiency. These results demonstrate that the efficacy of PAFs is highly related to

their porous structure. We speculate the unique structural advantage of PAFs may help to improve the film quality and enhance optoelectronic properties, which will be discussed later. The geometrical dynamics determine the softer nature of the linear MEH-PPV, which are easier to aggregate and charges tend to be localized. MEH-PPV doping may induce additional “hot spots” for carrier recombination, thus deteriorating the device performance.  $  ^{[41]}  $

The long-term stabilities of the control and PAFs-doped PSCs without encapsulation in ambient atmosphere (20 RH %, 25°C) were examined. Figure 3e shows the normalized stability records of the devices with and without PAFs. It is obvious that the efficiency of all devices increases in the initial period, reaching the highest after about 300 h. This corresponds to the increased conductivity of SpiroOMeTAD hole transport layer caused by the oxygen doping. The PCE of the control device starts to decay rapidly after 300 h and loses over 70% efficiency after 2000 h aging. In contrast, after a short period of rapid decay at about 400 h (burn-in loss),  $  ^{[42]}  $  the degradation rate of PAFs-doped devices slows down significantly. Encouragingly, the PAF-154 and PAF-155 doped devices can still maintain 80% and 70% of their initial PCEs after about half-year aging.

As the operational stability is crucial to the possible commercialization of PSCs in the future, we assessed the long-term operational stability of encapsulated devices at the maximized power point (MPP) under continuous AM 1.5G illumination in the air (room temperature, RH≈25%). Figure S22 shows the normalized PCE degradation of devices. A nonmonotonic evolution of PCE with time was observed, the rise in efficiency during the initial 10 h may stem from light-induced trap healing.  $  ^{[43]}  $   After this stage, the degradation commenced. The PCE of the control device quickly degraded to below 80% within 80 h and lost half of the initial PCE after 360 h aging. In contrast, the stability of PAFs doped devices significantly improved. After 360 h, the PAF-154 and PAF-155 doped devices retained 89.4% and 84.5% of their maximum PCEs, respectively.

# Conclusion

Two fully conjugated novel PAFs were prepared using the Gilch reaction with tetraphenylene and pyrene as the core building blocks, respectively. Both PAFs exhibited high specific surface area, excellent chemical and thermal stability. Due to their extended  $ \pi $ -conjugation, the PAFs were successfully applied as dopant in perovskite solar cells. The incorporation of PAFs prevented carrier recombination and facilitated charge transport by improving the crystallization and reducing the defects of perovskite film. We demonstrated that the rigid porous backbone of PAFs can be used as an efficient nucleation template and promoted rapid formation of circular nuclei. This induced growth of high-quality perovskite film with coarsening and compact perovskite grains. The champion PSCs with adding PAF-154 exhibited the highest PCE of  $ 22.8\% $  and steady-state power output of  $ 22.5\% $ . The device shelf lifetime is also significantly improved, still retaining  $ 80\% $  of the initial PCE after half-year storage in ambient conditions. This work paves the way for the fabrication of efficient and stable PSCs using porous aromatic frameworks with novel conjugated structures.
