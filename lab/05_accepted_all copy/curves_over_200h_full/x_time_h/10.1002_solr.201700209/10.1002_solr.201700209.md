# Low-Temperature Processed, Efficient, and Highly Reproducible Cesium-Doped Triple Cation Perovskite Planar Heterojunction Solar Cells

Chunhua Wang, Chujun Zhang, Shitan Wang, Gang Liu, Huayan Xia, Sichao Tong, Jun He, Dongmei Niu, Conghua Zhou, Kongxian Ding, Yongli Gao, and Junliang Yang*

# 1. Introduction

Highly efficient and reproducible cesium (Cs) doped triple cation (Cs, methylammonium (MA) and formamidinium (FA)) lead trihalide perovskite planar heterojunction (PHJ) solar cells are fabricated via low-temperature process with a simple architecture of ITO/SnO  $  _{2}  $  /Perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Ag, of which the power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to 20.51% with negligible hysteresis and a steady output PCE of 20.22% can be achieved. Cs-intercalation is useful for forming high-quality Cs-doped triple cation perovskite films with larger gains and band gap as compared with perovskite films without Cs doping, leading to impressively enhanced photoluminescence lifetime and open circuit voltage (V  $  _{oc}  $  ). Meanwhile, incorporating Cs  $  ^{+}  $  into perovskite structure can result in lower charge-extraction time and prolonged charge-recombination lifetime, which are advantageous to improve the device performance. More importantly, Cs-doped triple cation PHJ perovskite solar cells (PSCs) exhibit better stability. They could maintain about 80% original PCE even exposed to air environments (humidity ≈40%) for over 500 hr without any encapsulation, while similar ones without Cs-doping only maintain about 60% original PCE. The research work demonstrates that triple or multiple cation mixture is an effective strategy for structuring highly-efficient and stable PHJ-PSCs via low-temperature process, which may accelerate the commercialization of PSCs fabricated via large-scale printing techniques.

MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  would exhibit moisture-induced degradation, thermal decay over 100 °C and light-induced trap-state,  $  ^{[15,18]}  $  while solid-state mesoscopic heterojunction MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  solar cells showed the breakthrough results in 2012 with excellent stability over 500 hr in air without encapsulation.  $  ^{[8]}  $  FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  has the highly unfavorable non-perovskite hexagonal δ-phase (yellow phase) and a perovskite α-phase (black phase), of which α-phase could transfer to a photoinactive α-phase and/or coexist of α-phase and δ-phase at room temperature.  $  ^{[19-22]}  $  Similarly, the photoactive CsPbI  $  _{3}  $  perovskite would just exist stable over 300 °C and it is a photoinactive orthorhombic δ-phase at room temperature.  $  ^{[19,23]}  $

Organic-inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskite material as a super star has been attracting much attention and revolutionize solar cells since the first report by Kojima et al. in 2009.  $  ^{[1]}  $   Thus, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has developed dramatically and the certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to 22.7% have been achieved due to the distinguished characteristics such as ambipolar charge transport, direct band gap, large diffusion length, and so on.  $  ^{[2-6]}  $   Meanwhile, PSCs could be fabricated via large-scale, low-cost roll-to-roll (R2R) printing process.  $  ^{[6,7]}  $   Now there are many researches focusing on the fabrication of high-quality perovskite thin film,  $  ^{[8-11]}  $   interface modification,  $  ^{[12-15]}  $   and large-area fabrication techniques,  $  ^{[7,16,17]}  $   which would greatly accelerate the development and commercialization of PSCs.

Considering the composition of cation, organic-inorganic hybrid metal halide perovskite material has three typical types of fundamental mono-cation, that is, methylammonium lead trihalide (MAPbX $ _3 $ ), formamidinium lead trihalide (FAPbX $ _3 $ ), and cesium lead trihalide (CsPbX $ _3 $ ) (X = I, Cl, or Br). Therefore, it is very important to design humidity and thermal stable perovskite materials for high-performance PSCs.

Mixing cations is a feasible route to achieve high-performance and stable perovskite materials. For example, a small quantity of MA is beneficial to induce a preferable crystallization of photoactive phase of FA perovskite, resulting in a more thermally and structurally stable composition than pure MA or FA compounds.  $  ^{[24,25]}  $   Besides, the trace of Cs is especially effective to curb the yellow phase impurities, which is advantageous to fabricate high-purity, defect-free perovskite thin films, and potentially improve the stability of PSC devices.  $  ^{[19,22,24]}  $   It was reported that porous structural PSCs with the PCE of 20.8% could be achieved via a triple Cs/MA/FA cation mixture,  $  ^{[24]}  $   in which   $  TiO_{2}  $  /perovskite heterojunction was normally used and the spray pyrolysis or sintering of   $  TiO_{2}  $   component requires a high-temperature processing (  $  \approx450^{\circ}C  $  ). In some cases, antisolvent treatment could be used to fabricate high-quality perovskite thin film. However, high-temperature process and antisolvent are not regarded as to be acceptable process for fabricating large-area PSCs via printing process and flexible PSCs, as well as the commercialization. Furthermore, interface layers are key factors for achieving high-performance and stable PSC devices as well.  $  ^{[26-28]}  $   Recently, inorganic metal oxide   $  SnO_{2}  $   as electron transport layer (ETL) has captured great attention owing to its suitable bandgap (over 3.6 eV), deeper conduction band than   $  TiO_{2}  $ , highly mobility (  $  240 cm^{2} V^{-1} s^{-1}  $  ), and low-temperature treatment.  $  ^{[27,29,30]}  $   Thus, it would be an appropriate candidate for obtaining high-performance PSCs.

Herein, planar heterojunction (PHJ) PSCs with a simple architecture of ITO/SnO  $  _{2}  $  /Perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Ag were fabricated via low-temperature, solution process, in which perovskite is composed of triple cations including MA, FA, and Cs. The Cs-doped perovskite thin film exhibits higher crystallinity, longer PL lifetime, stronger light absorption, and slightly larger bandgap as compared to perovskite thin film without Cs-doping, resulting in the improvement of PCE up to 20.51%. Furthermore, the stability of PHJ-PSCs with triple caitons presents a great improvement even exposure in air (humidity ≈40%) without encapsulation. The research work provides a good route to fabricate high-performance, stable, and simply structural PHJ-PSCs via a low-temperature, solution process, which would match with large-scale R2R printing techniques, as well as further understanding the role of Cs  $  ^{+}  $  for achieving high-quality perovskite thin film and device performance.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The perovskite film was fabricated via two-step spin-coating process through inter-diffusion of organic lead (and/or cesium) and organic salts for achieving high-quality thin film. Purposefully,  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  was used due to its low bandgap ( $ \approx 1.48\mathrm{eV} $ ) for extending the absorption edge and consequently heightening the light harvesting. Meanwhile, a small fraction of bromide (Br) and chlorine (Cl) were incorporated into  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  perovskite for enhancing the crystallinity. The only difference between FA/MA binary perovskite and FA/MA/Cs ternary perovskite is that the  $ \mathrm{Cs}^+ $  is pre-embedded into the  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  framework before forming

triplet coexist FA/MA/Cs perovskite film. The detailed process is described in Section 4 and the fabrication process of device is presented in Figure S1, Supporting Information. The chemical formula of FA/MA binary and FA/MA/Cs ternary perovskites are proved to be  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.976}\mathrm{MA}_{0.024}\mathrm{Pb(I_{0.976}B_{r0.024})_3} $  and  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.945}\mathrm{MA}_{0.025}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.03}\mathrm{Pb(I_{0.975}B_{r0.025})_3} $  according to the XPS results, which will be discussed in details.

The quality of perovskite film is a key factor to greatly influence the device performance. The surface morphology of perovskite films characterized by SEM is shown in Figure 1a and b. It do form dense and uniform perovskite films thanks to the pre-existed   $  PbI_{2}  $   or the mixed   $  PbI_{2}-CsI  $   framework. But, the FA/MA/Cs film presents more uniform coverage and larger size grains than that of FA/MA film. The grain size distributions of both films are shown in Figure S2a, Supporting Information. The statistical grain size of FA/MA film changes from 400 to 1300 nm with an average value of about 830 nm, while the grain size of FA/MA/Cs film varies from 500 to 1600 nm with an average value of about 970 nm. The grain feature of enhanced uniformity and larger grain size in the FA/MA/Cs film is likely associated with enhanced film reconstruction and mass transport due to the   $  Cs^{+}  $  incorporation, where a promoted crystallization and grain growth occurs. It could be deduced that the perovskite film with larger crystal grains can reduce the number of grain boundaries, which are beneficial to the carrier transport and reduce carrier recombination.  $  ^{[22,31]}  $   The cross-sectional SEM image with the stack structure of ITO/  $  SnO_{2}  $  /perovskite is presented in Figure S2b (Supporting Information), indicating that the thickness of FA/MA/Cs film is about 750 nm and the vertical perovskite is also well-distributed. Besides, the AFM images of FA/MA and FA/MA/Cs films show the root mean square roughness (RMS) of 30.1, and 28.7 nm, respectively, with a scan area of   $  5\times5\mu m^{2}  $   (Figure S3, Supporting Information), which further reflects the superior film quality.

The crystalline structure of FA/MA and FA/MA/Cs perovskite films is exhibited in Figure 1c. It obviously shows that both perovskites present typically similar peaks, and the impurity  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  peak occurred at  $ 2\theta = 12.7^{\circ} $  could be observed as well, resulting from the excess  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  used in the precursor solution (1.3M). It has been demonstrated that slightly excess  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  could passivate the defects and decrease recombination in the perovskite film.[32,33] But, the magnified XRD patterns show that the peak of FA/MA/Cs film has a little right shift from  $ 14.12^{\circ} $  to  $ 14.16^{\circ} $  (Figure 1d). It means that the observed peak shifts toward higher angles because of partially substituting of  $ \mathrm{FA}^{+} $  or/ and  $ \mathrm{MA}^{+} $  cation with smaller  $ \mathrm{Cs}^{+} $ , which is attributed to the reduced lattice parameter of the FA/MA/Cs unit cell (the ionic radii of  $ \mathrm{FA}^{+} $ ,  $ \mathrm{MA}^{+} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Cs}^{+} $  are 2.53, 2.16, 1.67 Å, respectively). Furthermore, the different growth dynamics of perovskites would influence the optical properties. Thus, the effects of perovskite with and without Cs on the absorption properties were characterized using UV-vis absorption, as depicted in Figure 1e. Both perovskites exhibit strong absorption between 500 and  $ 900\mathrm{nm} $ . But, the FA/MA/Cs film shows a slight blue shift of about  $ 5\mathrm{nm} $ , indicating that it would have a minor effect on the bandgap  $ (E_{\mathrm{g}}) $  of perovskite and result in a slightly larger value than that of FA/MA film. # 3. Conclusions

In summary, triple cation mixtures (FA $  ^{+}  $ , MA $  ^{+}  $ , Cs $  ^{+}  $ ) perovskite films and consequently PHJ-PSC devices were fabricated via modified two-steps, low-temperature process. The characterized results indicated that the triple cation FA/MA/Cs perovskite film and PHJ-PSCs showed an impressive improvement in crystallinity with decrease crystal defects, longer PL lifetime, a slightly increased light absorption, together with lower charge-extraction time and larger charge-recombination lifetime than that of FA/MA film and devices. Additionally, the smaller ionic radii of Cs $  ^{+}  $  partially replace the larger cation leads to a larger bandgap, thereby a slightly increased  $  V_{oc}  $  was obtained for PHJ-PSCs with the simple architecture of ITO/SnO $  _{2}  $ /Perovskite/Spiro-OMeTAD/Ag. The champion PCE of 20.51% was achieved, which is better than FA/MA PHJ-PSCs with a PCE of 19.56%. Besides, the average PCE was elevated from 18.59 to 19.50% (the average  $  V_{oc}  $  was improved from 1.09 to 1.12 V) with highly reproducibility. Furthermore, the stable property of photovoltaics was also improved markedly after the Cs was doped into the perovskite system, and ca.80% of original PCE could be retained as the devices were stored in ambient

environment for 530 hr without encapsulation. This work demonstrated that low-temperature (<150 °C), ease-of-fabrication (without anti-solvent), highly efficient and air-stable PHJ-PSCs could be achieved by adopting mixed cation (triple or multiple) compositional engineering, which may pave a boulevard for large-scale fabrication and thereby promoting its industrialized production.

To form the HTL, Spiro-OMeTAD in CB solution (90 mg mL⁻¹) with 45 μL Li-TFSI in acetonitrile solution (170 mg mL⁻¹) and 10 μL 4-TBP was spin-coated onto the perovskite layer at 3000 rpm for 30 s. Finally, 100 nm Ag electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation under a vacuum of about 8.0 × 10⁻⁶ mbar, and the active area is 0.093 cm².

Characterization: Ultraviolet-visible spectrophotometer (UV-vis, Puxi, T9, China), X-ray diffractometer (XRD, Rigaku D, Max 2500, Japan) was used to obtain the absorption spectra and crystallographic properties of perovskite thin films. Surface morphologies was characterized by scanning electron microscope (SEM, FEI Helios Nanolab 600i, USA), film thickness and surface roughness of perovskite films were obtained by atomic force microscope (AFM, Agilent Technologies 5500AFM/SPM System), respectively. Time-resolved PL was achieved through time-correlated single-photon counting (TCSPC, MS3504I) measurements. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS, He I, 21.22 eV) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Al Kα X-ray source, 1486.6 eV) were employed to investigate the detailed energy-level and element distribution. Transient photocurrent (TPC, Keysight DSO-X 3104A, 1MW and 50W) decay and

photovoltage (TPV, Keysight DSO-X 3104A, NL100, 500 nm) decay were carried out to detect the carrier extraction and recombination properties. J-V characteristics of PHJ-PSCs devices were measured by digital Source Meter (Keithley, model 2420, USA) and the solar simulator (Newport 91160s, AM 1.5G, USA) was calibrate using standard silicon simulator with the standard value 100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  .
