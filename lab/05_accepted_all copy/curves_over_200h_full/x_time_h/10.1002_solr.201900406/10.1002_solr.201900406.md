# Synergistic Benefits of Cesium-Doped Aqueous Precursor in Air-Processed Inverted Perovskite Solar Cells

Guangcheng Wu, Rui Sun, Linqun Hu, Xuting Dong, Guanglei Cui,  $  ^{*}  $   Min Gu, Tong B. Tang, Zewen Zuo, and Yong Liu

Air-processed perovskite solar cells (PSCs) allay the need for costly fabrication in a controlled atmosphere but currently suffer from disadvantages in power conversion efficiency (PCE) and device stability. Herein, the systematic investigation into   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   prepared with an antisolvent-assisted process in a relative humidity of   $  40\pm5\%  $   is reported, using cesium-containing aqueous solutions of various strengths. Diffractometry and microscopy reveal how grain sizes vary among the samples, suggesting an optimum concentration for grain size. Those films are fabricated in air into solar cells, and their electrochemical impedance and current–voltage characteristics under light are measured. The films demonstrating optimum strength achieve PCEs of up to 16.6%, compared with the maximum of 14.4% achieved by untreated films. The air-processed films also exhibit mitigated hysteresis, with indices decreasing to 0.021. Photoluminescence characterization reveals reduced defect densities in Cs-doped materials and, together with photoelectron spectroscopy, suggests an upward shift in energy bands. Such changes explain the improvement in photovoltaic performance. Stability tests on unencapsulated cells over 14 days show that those made from the perovskite with optimum Cs-doping degrade slowest, with their conversion efficiencies falling by   $  <10\%  $   every 100 h. Our findings may contribute to the low-cost commercialization of PSCs.

As an outstanding candidate for photovoltaic application,  $  ^{[1-4]}  $  light-emitting diodes,  $  ^{[5,6]}  $  and photodetectors,  $  ^{[7-9]}  $  organic–inorganic lead halide perovskite has drawn a tremendous amount of interest in recent years. In particular, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) occupy a key focus of the current research.

One-step solvent–antisolvent ambient processing is one such approach.  $  ^{[18,21-24]}  $   The excellent performance of PSCs produced in this manner is attributed to the higher crystallinity and larger grain size of the perovskite films, which, as a result, contain fewer intra- and intergrain bound-

The maximum power conversion efficiency (PCE) of PSCs has rapidly risen from 3.8% to over 25% in recent years.  $  ^{[1,10-12]}  $   Perovskites possess many desirable properties, such as long charge carrier diffusion length,  $  ^{[13]}  $   efficient optical absorption,  $  ^{[14,15]}  $   and a tunable bandgap, which combine to provide high performance.  $  ^{[16]}  $   Nevertheless, the commercial exploitation of PSCs faces obstacles, the most crucial of which relates to their industrial-scale fabrication. Due to the low stability of perovskites, the laboratory preparation of PSCs often proceeds inside a controlled environment to minimize moisture and oxygen.  $  ^{[17]}  $   An alternative economical method of quality PSC fabrication must be established before the success in commercialization is achieved.  $  ^{[18-20]}  $

Another process that provides substantial benefits is cesium doping. The electronic properties of the perovskite can be strongly influenced by the partial substitution of cesium to one of its cations,  $  ^{[35,36]}  $  and Choi et al. reported that Cs-doped materials exhibit remarkably enhanced light absorption and film morphology.  $  ^{[37]}  $  In this study, we pursued a synergistic strategy of improvement by incorporating both cesium and water into the perovskite precursor. Their additions promote crystallinity and reduce trap states. Due to its better charge transportation and energy-level alignment, the new perovskite enables devices based on a traditional inverted structure and without a 2,9-dimethyl-4,7-diphenyl-1,10-phenanthroline (BCP) buffer layer to achieve a PCE as high as 16.6%, as well as exhibit negligible hysteresis and enhanced stability. As shown in Figure 2a, all prepared films exhibited strong diffraction peaks at 14.1° and 28.4°, ascribed to the perovskite planes (110) and (220).  $  ^{[3]}  $   Residue PbI  $  _{2}  $   peaks were absent, and any ITO features would lie outside the angular range.  $  ^{[33]}  $   As film thicknesses and measurement conditions were virtually identical, the peak intensities should be indicative of the perovskite crystallinity, which synergy treatment seems to enhance. The use of a 4% Cs aqueous solution appears to be optimum, consistent with another indicator, namely, the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the 14.1° peak. This FWHM is estimated as 0.096°, 0.092°, 0.096°, and 0.099° for 2%, 4%, 6%, and 8% Cs, respectively, with the narrowest FWHM at 4% and all cases appearing narrower than the pristine (0%) case of 0.105°. Corroboration also comes from UV–vis region absorption, which, as shown in Figure 2b, was strongest for 4% Cs and weakest for 0% Cs. Their bandgaps, estimated by the Tauc plot (Figure S1, Supporting Information),  $  ^{[38]}  $   are 1.604 and 1.607 eV, respectively.

All the films displayed smooth, compact, and pinhole-free growth (Figure 3); however, histograms plotted for their grain size distributions reveal that the most probable size reached the greatest value at 550 nm for 4% Cs, with those for 0%, 2%, 6%, and 8% peaking at 250, 310, 310, and 260 nm, respectively. Larger grains reduce grain boundaries, defects, and, thus, carrier recombination, leading to better photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{[39,40]}  $

During perovskite precursor spin-coating, the intermediate phase,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I-PbI_{2}/PbCl_{2}-N,N  $  -dimethylformamide (DMF), was formed.  $  ^{[51]}  $   As water is only slightly soluble in the nonpolar antisolvent, it coexisted in the intermediate phase and acted to retard crystallization.  $  ^{[52]}  $   Meanwhile, during the stage of annealing, as water has a boiling point lower than that of DMF, in excessive amounts, it accentuated the supersaturation in the intermediate phase and induced the secondary nucleation of the perovskite, leading to smaller crystals. There is, therefore, an optimum concentration of water to achieve the desirable grain size.  $  ^{[33,34,53]}  $

PL spectroscopy and photoelectron spectroscopy suggest that Cs doping lifts the valence band edge in   $  MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   to match more closely with that in PEDOT:PSS, which should then result in the more optimal extraction of holes across the lower energy barrier. Meanwhile, the suggested upshift of the conduction band edge should raise the barrier on the   $  C_{60}  $   side and facilitate the injection of electrons. These energy-level tunings may explain the observed improvement in photovoltaic performance.  $  ^{[54]}  $

# Experimental Section

Materials and Preparation:   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}I  $   (of stated purity >99.5%),   $  PbI_{2}  $   (>99.99%),   $  PbCl_{2}  $   (>99.99%), CsI (>99.9%), and   $  C_{60}  $   (>99.9%) powders, and PEDOT:PSS solution were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. and used as received. Sigma-Aldrich supplied anhydrous DMF (>99.9%). The first two iodides and the chloride were dissolved in DMF, according to a molar ratio of 1.3:1.0:0.1, such that the total solutes reached 42 wt%. A part of this   $  MAPbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $   precursor was set aside. The balance was divided into four portions for the preparation of additional Cs-doped samples. Various amounts of 1.0 mol L  $  ^{-1}  $   aqueous solution of CsI were drawn with a pipette and mixed with quantified portions of the precursor in the volume ratios of 2%, 4%, 6%, or 8%, then stirred at 70°C for 12 h before clarification by passing through 0.45 μm polyvinylidene fluoride filters.

Device Fabrication: ITO glass plates were successively washed with ethanol, acetone, and deionized water; then, they were dried before final cleaning in UV ozone plasma for 20 min. Each cleaned substrate contained PEDOT:PSS spin-coated in air at 4000 rpm for 30 s, followed by annealing at 150 °C for 15 min. On top of this hole-transport layer, a selected perovskite precursor was deposited, using an antisolvent-assisted method: at 1000 rpm for 6 s, then at 4000 rpm for 15 s with 120 μL of ethyl acetate dropped after 10 s. All the spin-coating and annealing processes were conducted in air with a relative humidity of 40 ± 5%. After annealing in air on a hot plate at 100 °C for 10 min, the coated substrate was either subjected to a number of measurements or fabricated into a PSC. In the latter case, C $  _{60}  $  (to a thickness of 20 nm) and then Ag (80 nm-thick) were thermally evaporated on top in a chamber evacuated to 6.0 × 10 $  ^{-4}  $  Pa to act as the cathode.

Measurements and Characteristics: XRD, UV–vis absorption spectra, SEM, steady-state PL spectra, time-resolved PL, and UPS measurements were taken of the coated plates. The instruments used were Bruker D8 ADVANCE diffractometer, Shimadzu UV-3600 spectrometer, Zeiss Sigma 300 microscope equipped with a field-emission cathode, Edinburgh FLS920 fluorescence spectrophotometer, Edinburgh FLS1000 fluorescence spectrophotometer, and Thermo Fisher 250Xi spectrometer. The diffractometer was operated with Cu Kα radiation of 1.5406 Å, and the spectrophotometer was used with a 405 nm pulsed laser as the excitation source. A plate without any perovskite deposited, that is, PEDOT:PSS/ITO, served as the reference in absorption spectroscopy.

EIS observations of fabricated PSCs were performed with an Autolab PGSTAT 302N workstation, and the current density–voltage (J–V) characteristics were obtained by a Keithley 2400 SourceMeter. EIS spectra were recorded in the dark under a bias of 1.0 V. J–V curves of cells covered by a shadow mask measuring 4 mm  $  ^{2}  $  were scanned, under an illumination of simulated 100 mW cm  $  ^{-22}  $  AM1.5G, calibrated by a Si reference cell. Finally, to obtain the device stability, crucial in practical applications, we stored unencapsulated PSCs in the dark, inside a sealed box kept at a temperature of 20 ± 5 °C and a relative humidity of 45 ± 10%, and subjected them to periodic tests extending to 14 days.
