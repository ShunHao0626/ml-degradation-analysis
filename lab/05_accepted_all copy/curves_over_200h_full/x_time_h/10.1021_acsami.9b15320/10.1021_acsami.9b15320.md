# $ \mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm{Te}_3 $  topological insulator as a new and outstanding counter electrode material for high-efficiency and endurable flexible perovskite solar cells

Mei Wang, Qiuyun Fu*, Liang Yan, Wenbo Pi, Geng Wang, Zhiping Zheng and Wei Luo

School of Optical and Electronic Information, Engineering Research Center for Functional Ceramics of the Ministry of Education, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, Wuhan 430074, P. R. China

* Corresponding author: fuqy@hust.edu.cn

# ABSTRACT

Inverted flexible perovskite solar cells (PSCs) typically employ expensive metals as the counter electrodes, which are brittle and corrodible by perovskite, leading to a sharp performance drop under continuous bending, air exposure, thermal stress or light illumination and eventually retards the commercialization. Herein, a low-cost   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   counter electrode was employed by using a simple thermal evaporation process. The resultant device achieved an excellent power conversion efficiency of 18.16%, which was among the highest reported efficiencies, much higher than the reference Ag PSC (15.90%). The improvement should be attributed to the intrinsic suppressed electron backscattering in   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   topological insulator. Simultaneously, the   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   device obtained a significantly improved mechanical flexibility and long-term operational stability. The present strategy will help to open up a new avenue for future commercialization of flexible photovoltaic applications.

KEYWORDS: Counter electrode,  $ \mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm{Te}_3 $ , Topological insulator, Flexible perovskite solar cells,  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{PbI}_3 $

# INTRODUCTION

To cope with the increasing electricity energy demands for the emerging wearable electronic devices and portable chargers, it is pre-requisite to develop highly efficient, lightweight and flexible photovoltaic cells. Perovskite solar cells (PSCs), most promising next-generation photovoltaics, have aroused a wide concern form people in academia and industry due to the features of high efficiency, solution processibility and low manufacturing cost, showing to be one of the top priorities for volume production and commercialization in the short run.  $  ^{1-8}  $   The attractive advantages of perovskite materials are the low-temperature process capability, the intrinsic bendability and the solution processibility, which creates an opportunity for the fabrication of flexible PSCs. Since 2013, there has been significant progress in the field of flexible PSCs, with the power conversion efficiency (PCE) record of 21.3% obtained in flexible tandem PSCs, 19.11% in regular structured flexible PSCs and 19.0% in inverted structured flexible PSCs,  $  ^{9-11}  $   hugely exceeding the efficiency of other flexible solar cells. Although high PCE has been confirmed, the large-scale commercialization of flexible PSCs still faces many practical challenges, such as cost, stability and flexibility, et al.  $  ^{12}  $

In high performance flexible PSCs, silver (Ag) and aluminum or even gold are the most frequently used counter electrode, the high cost of them may hinder perovskite's superiority in rapid energy recovery and retard the large-scale production of PSCs. On the other hand, the long-term stability of PSCs will be weakened by the use of metal electrodes. It has been reported in the literature a chemical reaction occurred at the interface of the metal electrode and the perovskite layer which caused a significant drop in the efficiency of PSCs. $ ^{13-15} $  Although spatially separating the metal electrode and the perovskite layer by inserting organic buffer layers could alleviate performance degradation caused by the metal electrodes to a certain degree, $ ^{16, 17} $  but it just slowed the rate of degradation and won't get to the root of the problem. Researchers demonstrated that the metal electrode tend to permeate across the organic buffer layers into the perovskite layer after long-term operation under thermal stress or exposure to

In addition to the efficiency, cost and stability, flexibility is another issue we must focus on. Various methods have been actively conducted to enhance the flexibility of PSCs, among them introducing new flexible transparent bottom electrodes to replace ITO/FTO were the most common means.  $  ^{22-29}  $   However, these new transparent bottom electrodes were usually prepared by using complex and immature techniques which were not compatible with mass production. Moreover, studies on the mechanical flexibility of the counter electrode have been quite sparse up to date. Therefore, in order to realize a flexible PSC with both high efficiency, low cost, excellent mechanical flexibility and operational stability, developing a novel counter electrode material who possesses all the features of electrically conductive, low-cost, highly flexible and chemical stable remains highly desirable.

In recent years, great efforts have been devoted to search new counter electrodes such as graphene, carbon nanotubes (CNTs), carbon black and NiO@carbon spheres, etc.  $  ^{30-34}  $   But PSCs fabricated with these carbon-based counter electrode only achieved a relative low PCE of up to 16%.  $  ^{35}  $   Moreover, the effect of these new counter electrodes on the mechanical durability of PSCs have not been discussed in these reports. Although cross-stacking CNTs (CSCNTs)  $  ^{36}  $   or SnO  $  _{2}  $  @CSCNT  $  ^{37}  $   have ever been employed to fabricate flexible PSCs, a very low PCE of 11.9% was obtained. Therefore, new counter electrodes need to be developed. As an exotic state of quantum matter, topological insulators can effectively suppress unwanted electron backscattering due to their insulating bulk and highly conductive surface states protected by time reversal symmetry, can enable excellent transport properties such as high carrier mobility, have received extensive attention in recent years.  $  ^{38,39}  $   2D-Layered bismuth telluride (Bi  $  _{2}  $  Te  $  _{3}  $  ), who has recently been demonstrated to be a topological insulator,  $  ^{40-44}  $   possesses the following merits. Here, we first reported an inverted   $  NiO_{x}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) planar flexible PSC, in which   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   is used as the counter electrode. The device has a PCE of 18.16% with no hysteresis, which is much higher than the corresponding device using Ag as the counter electrode whose optimal PCE is 15.90%. Furthermore,   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $  -based flexible PSCs were proven to have significantly improved mechanical endurance and considerably enhanced device stability in air, under thermal stress or light illumination, compared with the standard inverted Ag-based flexible PSCs.

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The structure of   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $  -based flexible PSC is schematically illustrated in Fig. 1a. The device was fabricated on an ITO/PEN substrates.   $  NiO_{x}  $   nanocrystals film was used as the hole transporting layer as deposited according to the previous reports with some modification.  $  ^{48, 49}  $   MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   was used as the absorber. The organic material [6, 6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) was used as an electron transport layer and bathocuproine (BCP) was a hole blocking layer.   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   was used as the counter electrode. The structure of Ag-based flexible PSC was identical to that of   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $  -based flexible PSC, except for the counter electrode material. Fig. 1b shows the energy level of each layer in the device. It indicated that both of Ag and   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   match well with the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital energy level of PCBM. Figure S1 provided the corresponding SEM image of both electrodes. Both of them were uniformly deposited. The process of performance-optimizing of two kinds of flexible devices were shown in Fig. 1c, d. Since the electrode was prepared by the thermal evaporation technology, the increase in thickness of the electrode was controlled by prolonging the evaporation time. The thicker the electrode signified that the longer time the high temperature steam contacted the sample. As a result, the temperature of the sample would be higher. However, it is well known that perovskite materials are easily decomposed at high temperature, which would worsen the performance of PSCs. Both electrodes mentioned in this paper were prepared by using these optimal parameters.

For the purpose of investigating the electrical properties of both electrodes, we primarily investigated the sheet resistance of   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   electrode using the four-point probe method. The average sheet resistance was   $  350\ \Omega/\square  $  , which was comparable to the value reported before.  $  ^{47,55}  $   Then we examined the series resistances (Rs) of both devices by measuring the electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS). By fitting the Nyquist plots, the Rs of the   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   PSC was obtained to be   $  211.6\ \Omega  $  , much higher than that of the Ag PSC (  $  102.2\ \Omega  $  ) (Figure S3). Furthermore, the electrical conductivity of the electrodes were measured, which was   $  5.77\pm0.08\times10^{3}S/cm  $   for   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $  , lower than that of Ag (  $  7.93\pm0.13\times10^{5}\ S/cm  $  ). Therefore, the charge collection efficiency of   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   electrode would be lower than that of Ag electrode, resulting in lower   $  J_{sc}  $   of   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   PSCs.  $  ^{29}  $   However, it was remarkable that the champion   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   PSC using the less conductive cathode was measured to have much higher   $  J_{sc}  $   value (  $  22.90\ mA\ cm^{-2}  $  ), compared with that of the Ag PSC (  $  20.62\ mA\ cm^{-2}  $  ). This could be ascribed to the more effective electron transfer from perovskite to the   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   electrode. According to the Nyquist plots in Figure S3, a lower transfer resistance (  $  R_{tr}  $  ) and a higher recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) was obtained for the   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   PSC than the Ag PSC, indicating that replacing the Ag electrode with   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   electrode was efficient in charge transportation. To investigate the carrier transportation and recombination behavior in Ag PSC and   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   PSC, the light-intensity-dependent Voc measurement was carried out, as shown in Figure S4. In principle, when the slope of the Voc versus light intensity deviates from the value of kT/q, the trap-assisted recombination plays a dominant role in charge transfer.  $  ^{56}  $   Herein, the Ag PSC exhibited a slope of   $  1.83\ kT/q  $  , this strong Voc-to-light dependence reflected a strong energy loss and the recombination behavior in the device should be a combination of bimolecular recombination (free-carrier recombination) and trap-assisted recombination.  $  ^{57}  $   However, in the case of   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   PSC, the slope was decreased to   $  1.14\ kT/q  $  , which indicated that there was no obvious trap-assisted recombination. When it comes to the practical commercial feasibility of flexible power applications, the flexing tolerance of flexible PSCs becomes as important as efficiency. The mechanical durability was evaluated by repeatedly bending the cells at various bending radius (R). The relation between the PCE and the bending radius (R=∞, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 3, 2 mm) for the Bi₂Te₃ PSC after bending 100 cycles was shown in Fig. 4a. For comparison, the reference Ag PSC was also tested. Encouragingly, the PCE of the Bi₂Te₃ PSC had negligible degradation even after bending at a small radius of 4 mm. When R was decreased to 2 mm, 78% of its initial PCE was remained. In contrast, the performance of Ag PSC degraded gradually with the decrease of the bending radius, decreased to 85% of its original PCE when the radius was lowered to 4 mm and preserved only 18% after a further bending to 2 mm. To figure out whether or not the flexible devices can be used for harsh bending applications, their mechanical stability under 1000 cycles bending test at R=4 mm was further evaluated. Surprisingly, the PCE of Bi₂Te₃ PSC still remained up to 95% of its original value (Fig. 4b), which was on par with some of the best-reported results.  $  ^{[29,61]}  $   In striking contrast, the PCE of Ag PSC decreased rapidly upon increasing the bending cycles, preserving only 22% of its original value, which was mainly attributed to the decreased JSC and FF as displayed in Figure S6. To explore the source of performance degradation of the devices after they were bent repeatedly, we examined the relative resistance change (  $  \Delta R/R_{0}  $  ) of the Ag film and Bi₂Te₃ film against bending at R=4 mm using a probe station system and the results were shown in Fig. 4c. For Ag, the   $  \Delta R/R_{0}  $   increased slightly at first and rapidly increased after bending 100 times, rising up to about 5 times of its original value after 1000 times, suggesting the failure of the Ag cell. This is in stark contrast to Bi₂Te₃, the   $  \Delta R/R_{0}  $   was only 0.6 fold of its original value after 1000 times of bending. The relative resistance change trend of the Ag film and Bi₂Te₃ film was consistent with the PCE declining trend for the Ag PSC and Bi₂Te₃ PSC, respectively. # CONCLUSIONS

In summary, the layered topological-insulator   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   film was firstly used in the inverted flexible   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}/NiO_{x}  $   PSCs as an effective non-metallic counter electrode. The   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   PSCs showed much more efficient electron transfer than the Ag PSCs benefiting from the suppressed electron backscattering. As a result, the champion   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   PSC achieved an inspiring PCE of 18.16% with negligible hysteresis, much higher than the Ag PSC (15.90%). In addition, the   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   PSC exhibited superior mechanical durability, retaining over 95% of its original PCE even suffering 1000 bending cycles at R=4 mm, outperforming the reference Ag PSC. This excellent mechanical flexibility was on par with the best-reported results. Furthermore, the air, thermal and light stability of the flexible PSC were significantly improved when the Ag electrode was substituted with the   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   electrode. The PCE of the   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   PSC showed little loss for up to 1000 hours in air with constant temperature and humidity controlled, remained about 90% of its initial value after a continuous heat treatment at 85 °C for 1000 hours in   $  N_{2}  $   and retained about 85% of its initial value after a continuous light illumination for 1000 hours in controlled air condition. Owing to the outstanding flexibility, promising PCE and long-term stability of   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   PSCs, the topological-insulator   $  Bi_{2}Te_{3}  $   with low cost exhibited great potential to be a counter electrode material for foldable photovoltaic devices.
