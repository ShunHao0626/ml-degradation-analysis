# Interfacial Molecular Doping and Energy Level Alignment Regulation for Perovskite Solar Cells with Efficiency Exceeding 23%

Chuansu Yang, Haoxin Wang, Yawei Miao, Cheng Chen, Mengde Zhai, Qinye Bao, Xingdong Ding, Xichuan Yang, and Ming Cheng*

Cite This: ACS Energy Lett. 2021, 6, 2690–2696

Read Online

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Development of hole transport materials (HTMs) with comprehensive passivation effects and appropriate energy levels are urgently desirable for constructing highly efficient and stable perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Herein, we report an effective interfacial molecular doping strategy and energy level regulation approach to improve the performance of PSCs with ultrasimple carbazole-based HTMs CZ-As and CZ-Py. The pyridine-substituted HTM CZ-Py exhibits a stepped energy level with perovskite and CZ-As, effective passivation of   $  Pb^{2+}  $   defect, as well as the assistance of the formation of high-quality perovskite film and hole transport layer (HTL). By utilizing CZ-Py as interfacial doping material and CZ-As as HTM, the undesired charge carrier recombination at the perovskite/HTM interface are significantly restricted. Finally, the fabricated PSCs feature an impressive power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to 23.5% and good long-term stability. This work demonstrates a facile and highly efficient way to

reduce nonradiative recombination and further improve photovoltaic performance of PSCs.

Recently, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cell (PSC) has rocketed from barely 3.8% to a certified 25.5% in the past decade, making PSC a promising new-generation photovoltaic technology.  $  ^{1-8}  $   In the future, boosting efficiency, enhancing long-term stability, and reducing the material and fabrication cost of PSC will be constantly pursued to promote the commercial application of PSC. As a vital interfacial layer of PSCs, hole transport materials (HTMs) play a crucial role in hole extraction and transport and the device stability.  $  ^{9-21}  $   Up to now, 2,2',7,7'-Tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) and poly[bis(4-phenyl)-(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA) are still the mainstream HTMs.  $  ^{22-27}  $   However, the costs of Spiro-OMeTAD and PTAA are extremely high because of the complicated synthesis and purification process.  $  ^{28,29}  $   Moreover, the large energy gap between the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of HTM (-5.2 eV for Spiro-OMeTAD and -5.1 eV for PTAA) and the valence band (VB) of perovskites severely influences the hole extraction and transport efficiency, leading to a high open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) loss.  $  ^{30,31}  $   In addition, defects existing at the grain boundaries and the surface of polycrystalline perovskite film, such as interstitial/uncoordinated species and ion vacancies, are recognized as the nonradiative recombination center at the perovskite/HTL

The synthetic routes for CZ-As and CZ-Py are shown in Scheme S3, and the detailed description and characterization are supplied in Supporting Information (SI, Figures S1–S6). CZ-As and CZ-Py can be facilely synthesized with total yields of 89% and 70%, respectively. The synthesis costs of HTMs CZ-As and CZ-Py are calculated to be 10.14 \$/g and 16.03 \$/g (Tables S1–S2), respectively, which are much lower than the classical HTM Spiro-OMeTAD (57.07 \$/g, see Table S3), $  ^{34,35}  $  making CZ-As and CZ-Py superior to Spiro-OMeTAD at the material cost level.

As shown in Figure 1, CZ-As and CZ-Py showed similar molecular geometries, with the HOMO levels distributing throughout the main skeleton, including the central carbazole unit and peripheral arms. However, the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) levels of CZ-As and CZ-Py transfer to the central carbazole unit. Especially, for CZ-Py, the charge density on N-substitution becomes higher than CZ-As because of the stronger electron-withdrawing character of pyridine. The distinct separation but partial overlap of HOMO and LUMO is favorable for hole extraction and transport. The HOMO and

LUMO levels are calculated to be -4.36 and -0.82 eV for CZ-As and -4.38 and -0.88 eV for CZ-Py, respectively. The down-shift of HOMO level for CZ-Py can be ascribed to the weakening of the electron-donating ability of carbazole with the introduction of the electron-deficient pyridine substitution. Compared with CZ-As, the electrostatic potential (ESP) mapping of CZ-Py shows that the negative charges tend to concentrate on the pyridine unit, making CZ-Py function as an effective Lewis base to passivate the uncoordinated   $  Pb^{2+}  $   defects and further enhancing the device performance and stability.

Furthermore, the optical and electrochemical properties of CZ-As and CZ-Py were investigated with the corresponding data summarized in Figure S7 and Table S4. CZ-As and CZ-Py showed weak absorbance in the visible region, suggesting that they would not compete to absorb visible-light with perovskite. The optical bandgaps  $ (E_{\mathrm{g}}) $  of CZ-As and CZ-Py are estimated to be 3.10 and  $ 3.12\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. From the cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements, the HOMO energy levels  $ (E_{\mathrm{HOMO}}) $  of CZ-As and CZ-Py are estimated to be -5.16 and  $ -5.32\mathrm{eV} $ , respectively. After deciphering the intrinsic properties of CZ-As and CZ-Py, we found these two materials could be favorable HTM alternatives of Spiro-OMeTAD from energy level and material cost points. Therefore, we preliminarily explored the potential application of CZ-As and CZ-Py as HTMs in PSC with the configuration of FTO/c-TiO $ _2 $ /mp-TiO $ _2 $ /perovskite layer/HTM/Au. The corresponding cross-sectional scanning electron microscopy (SEM) image is shown in Figure S8b, exhibiting well layer-by-layer structure. As shown in Figure S8c,d and Table 1, the CZ-As-based PSC achieved a champion PCE of 20.9% ( $ V_{\text{oc}} = 1.08 $  V,  $ J_{\text{sc}} = 24.4 $  mA.cm $ ^{-2} $ , FF = 79.3%) with the hysteresis index (HI) of 0.024, which overperformed Spiro-OMeTAD (19.0%, HI = 0.042) and CZ-Py-based PSCs (18.2%, HI = 0.027). The superior photovoltaic performance of the CZ-As-based device mainly derives from the higher  $ J_{\text{sc}} $  and FF. The higher  $ J_{\text{sc}} $  might be triggered from the higher hole extraction and transport efficiency of CZ-As than CZ-Py and Spiro-OMeTAD because of its higher hole mobility (1.71 × 10 $ ^{-4} $  cm $ ^2 $ ·V $ ^{-1} $ ·S $ ^{-1} $ , see Figure S7c), which is consistent with the incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) measurements. It was further confirmed by the photoluminescence (PL) decay measurements (Figure S9 and Table S5). HTM CZ-As exhibited not only higher PL quenching efficiency but also faster hole extraction and transport rate than CZ-Py and Spiro-OMeTAD. The especially higher FF for CZ-As-based PSC mainly results from the higher hole conductivity of CZ-As (see Figure S7d). For CZ-Py-based PSCs, although the PCE is inferior compared with CZ-As, the  $ V_{\text{oc}} $  is 50 mV higher. To effectively illustrate it, we tested the defect density of CZ-As-, CZ-Py-, and Spiro-OMeTAD-based samples, as the  $ V_{\text{oc}} $  is closely related to the defect density of the device. $ ^{36-41} $  As shown in Figure S10, the defect density of CZ-Py-based sample is calculated to be 3.47 × 10 $ ^{15} $  cm $ ^{-3} $  with a

lower trap-filled limit voltage (  $  V_{TFL}  $  ) of 0.327 V, which reveals that CZ-Py is effective on surface defect passivation, further leading to higher   $  V_{oc}  $  .

Considering the excellent hole extraction and transport property of CZ-As, the effective surface passivation effect of CZ-Py, and their well-stepped energy level alignment with perovskite, we further fabricated the PSC by combining CZ-Py into antisolvent as passivator and using CZ-As as HTM. The schematic structure and schematic energy levels alignment are shown in Figure 2a,b. Encouragingly, the resulted PSC (termed PVK: CZ-Py/CZ-As) achieved an impressive PCE of  $ 23.5\% $  ( $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} = 1.15 \mathrm{~V} $ ,  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} = 25.2 \mathrm{~mA} \cdot \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $ , and  $ \mathrm{FF} = 81.2\% $ ) (Figure 2c). The IPCE spectrum indicates the optimized device showed wide response to solar spectrum, and the integrated  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  was calculated to be  $ 24.5 \mathrm{~mA} \mathrm{~cm}^{-2} $ , matching well with the  $ J-V $  experimental data and thus further verifying the reliability of the  $ J-V $  curves (see Figure 2d). Moreover, as shown in Figure 2e,f, the device can work stably at the maximum power point (MPP, bias  $ 0.97 \mathrm{~V} $ ) with a steady PCE of  $ 23.1\% $  and more than  $ 50\% $  of the sample obtained PCEs exceeding  $ 23\% $ .

To better illustrate the enhanced performance of CZ-Py-treated PSC, we first checked the effect of CZ-Py on perovskite energy level and film quality. The ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) results (see Figure S11) indicate the CZ-Py can act as p-type dopants on perovskite surface, leading to the bending of perovskite VB. With the CZ-Py treatment, the Fermi energy level  $ (E_{\mathrm{F}}) $  slightly increases from  $ -4.32\mathrm{eV} $  to  $ -4.12\mathrm{eV} $  after CZ-Py treatment. VBM are calculated to be  $ -5.32 $  and  $ -5.72\mathrm{eV} $  for CZ-Py-treated and the bare perovskite film, respectively. By employing CZ-Py passivation, the bending of the perovskite energy band has been achieved, which is more conducive to hole transport and suppresses the charge nonradiative recombination. Moreover, the characteristic peak of PbI₂ was well restricted in CZ-Py-treated perovskite film because of the effective passivation effect of CZ-Py through Lewis acid-based action. Subsequently, the coordination of CZ-Py to the bared Pb²⁺ was further characterized by using Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra (Figure 3h). It is found that the stretching vibration of the C–N bond of CZ-Py-treated perovskite located at 1574 cm⁻¹ is blue-shifted by 13 cm⁻¹ relative to CZ-Py, indicating the formation of the C–N…Pb coordination bond. The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis (see Figure 3i,j) also confirms the interaction between the CZ-Py and PbI₂. With the CZ-Py treatment, the characteristic peaks of Pb 4f₇/₂ and Pb 4f₅/₂ shifted from 138.39 and 143.29 eV to lower binding energies of 138.26 and 143.14 eV, respectively, indicating CZ-Py could passivate the Pb²⁺ involved surface defects of perovskite. Meanwhile, the C=O peak detected around 288 eV for the pristine perovskite significantly disappeared in the CZ-Py-treated case, suggesting that CZ-Py treatment can also effectively isolate the perovskite from oxygen and moisture, therefore, the good long-term stability for CZ-Py-treated device can be expected.

Therefore, as the CZ-Py showed multiple effects on surface doping and defect passivation, which is reported to be crucial for efficient charge transport, the effect of CZ-Py treatment on charge transport and recombination was investigated by measuring the light-intensity dependency of  $  J_{\mathrm{sc}}  $  and  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $ . As plotted in Figure 4a, for CZ-Py-treated device, the  $  \alpha  $  value that related to charge recombination is fitted to be 0.99, which is

much closer to 1 than the control device only using CZ-As as HTM (  $  \alpha = 0.91  $  ), indicating the more efficient charge extraction property in CZ-Py-treated PSC. This result is consistent with the PL decay test results. As shown in Figure 4c, although the same HTM CZ-As was used, the PL intensity of perovskites at 775 nm was further quenched with CZ-Py treatment, indicating the higher hole extraction efficiency. Moreover, the CZ-Py-treated film shows a 3 nm blue-shift compared with the control sample, indicating the surface defect passivation effect of CZ-Py.  $  ^{42}  $   In TRPL measurements (see Figure 4d, Table S5), the lifetime   $  \tau  $   for the CZ-Py-treated sample was fitted to be 9.65 ns, which is slightly shorter in comparison with the control sample (10.98 ns), suggesting the faster hole collection and transport rate at the PVK:CZ-Py/CZ-As interface. From the light-intensity dependency of   $  V_{oc}  $   test results (Figure 4b), we found the CZ-Py-treated device shows a smaller slope of 1.94 KT/e compared with the control device (2.23 KT/e), demonstrating the charge recombination was well suppressed for PVK:CZ-Py/CZ-As device. The restricted charge recombination is also confirmed by the electrical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements performed at open-circuit voltage (see Figure 4e). The CZ-Py-treated PSC shows much larger charge recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ) of 214   $  \Omega  $   than the control device (137   $  \Omega  $  ). All the discussions above give a good explanation of higher   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.15 V for CZ-Py-treated PSC.

The enhancement of long-term stability, on the one hand, can be ascribed to the effective defect passivation with CZ-Py treatment as mentioned above. On the other hand, it mainly derives from the better isolation of oxygen and moisture with CZ-Py treatment and HTM CZ-As on the top, which possess excellent hydrophobicity property, as confirmed by water contract angles (Figure 5b–f). From the results, we found that although all devices showed a noticeable decline in PCEs, the CZ-Py-treated PSC exhibited slower degradation rate and retained slightly higher PCE after 500 h measurements.

In summary, two ultrasimple yet efficient HTMs CZ-As and CZ-Py with matched energy levels were reported and successfully applied in PSCs. With rational designation of N-substitution, CZ-As possesses excellent hole extraction and transport property and CZ-Py displays excellent defect passivation and surface doping effects. By employing CZ-As as HTM and CZ-Py as an interfacial engineering layer, we demonstrate a facile way to improve hole transport process and suppress detrimental nonradiative recombination at the interface. As a result, the optimized device termed PVK: CZ-Py/CZ-As achieved an impressive PCE of 23.5% with negligible hysteresis and excellent long-term stability, maintaining over 95% of its original PCE after 1000 h aging in ambient condition with RH of 50–60%.
