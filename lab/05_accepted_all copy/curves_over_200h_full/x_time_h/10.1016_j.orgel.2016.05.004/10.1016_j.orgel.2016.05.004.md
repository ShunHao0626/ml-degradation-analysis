# 1. Introduction

The solid-state organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite was firstly used in 2009 as light absorber for liquid electrolyte based dye-sensitized solar cells with merely power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 3.81%. Up to now, perovskite solar cells are extensively explored as the recent major breakthrough in low-cost photovoltaics   $  [1-5]  $  . The PCE of perovskite solar cells has increased to 22% according to the report from the National Renewable Energy Laboratory in a matter of 7 years   $  [6]  $  . However, the stability of devices has become the growing problem   $  [7,8]  $  . The rapid development of perovskite solar cells could be attributed to the efficient and easy processing of the perovskite layer   $  [9]  $  . The hole transport material (HTM) plays a critical role in charge carrier transportation and retardation of charge recombination in efficient perovskite solar cells   $  [10]  $  . As a mostly used organic HTM, spiro-OMeTAD has been developed for about two decades. However, this material suffers from desirable electrical properties including low conductivity, ambient degradation, multiplex synthetic approach, which limit its application in perovskite devices   $  [11-13]  $  . To find a better solution for the HTM in perovskite solar cells, other small molecular materials have been developed and showed PCEs in the range of 9–16%

# ABSTRACT

The   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  -based perovskite solar cells using   $  \alpha,\alpha^{\prime}  $  -diethoxyethyl-oligothiophenes (DEPT-SC) doped with 2,3,5,6-Tetrafluoro-7,7,8,8-tetracyanoquino-dimethane (F4TCNQ) as hole transport material (HTM) exhibited a power conversion efficiency of 11.52%. Compared to the pristine devices, the perovskite solar cells using the new synthesized HTM showed an increased efficiency by about 18% and exhibited better photo-stability, indicating that the organic dopant is an effective method for DEPT-SC toward stable perovskite solar cells.

Most of reported HTM materials are featured with methoxy-phenylamine functional group, which the inherent triangular pyramid configuration formed by   $  sp^{3}  $   nitrogen atom leads to large steric hindrance and intermolecular distances. Consequently, these HTMs are usually anisotropic in their pristine form with low mobility and low conductivity [20–22]. Therefore, dopants such as LiTFSI,   $  \mathrm{Co(PzPy)_{3}(TFSI)_{3}}  $  ,   $  \mathrm{Co(TPzPy)_{3}(PF6)_{3}}  $  , have been widely applied to increase the conductivity of the HTMs and improve the photovoltaic property [23,24]. However, the deliquescent behavior of HTMs with dopants accelerates device performance degradation and obvious reduces device's lifetime, which are fatal for their practical applications. In this case, it is significant to develop suitable dopants for enhancing PCE of solar cells without sacrificing of the device stability.

F4TCNQ has been proved to be a strong electron acceptor   $  [25,26]  $  . It is widely used in organic light-emitting diodes for the reducing the hole injection barrier by forming a narrow space-charge region near the metal contact, then improving device performance   $  [27–30]  $  . The deep LUMO (-5.24 eV) of the F4TCNQ is accessible to the HOMO (-5.14 eV) of our previously reported HTM-DEPT-SC   $  [31–33]  $   (Fig. 1 and Fig. S1), which can form molecular charge transfer complexes (CTCs)   $  [34–36]  $  . The CTCs play important roles in quenching excited state fluorescence in recombination processes and leave holes in HTMs to facilitate the charge transport   $  [37–39]  $  . In addition the doping will increase the work function and lower the Fermi level of the HTMs to enhance open voltage   $  [40]  $  . # 2. Device fabrication

Fluorine-doped tin oxide (F:SnO $ _2 $ ) coated glass (Pilkington TEC 15, 15 Ω/square) was cleaned by ultrasonication and rinsed with acetone and ethanol for 30 min sequentially, then dried by nitrogen. The substrate was placed in a UV–O $ _3 $  chamber for 30 min prior to spray the mixed liquids of titanium diisopropoxidebis (acetylcetonate) solution (75% in 2-propanol, Sigma-Aldrich) and ethanol (1:39, v/v) at 450 °C. The TiO $ _2 $  compact layer thickness was ~50 nm. Then the compact layer was spin-coated with a TiO $ _2 $  paste (Dyesol 30 NRD, 30 nm average particle size) diluted in anhydrous ethanol (1:3, weight ratio) at 5000 rpm for 30 s. Porous TiO $ _2 $  film was about 250 nm. After drying at 125 °C, the mesoporous TiO $ _2 $  were heated to 500 °C for 30 min. Then PbI $ _2 $  solution in  $ N,N $ -dimethylformamide (500 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ ) was spin coated on the mesoporous TiO $ _2 $  films at 6500 rpm for 90 s followed by a thermal annealing on the hotplate for 30 min in the glove box. After cooling, the films were dipped into a solution of CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I (diluted in 2-propanol, 10 mg mL $ ^{-1} $ ) for 30 s, rinsed with 2-propanol and dried at 70 °C for 30 min. After drying, the aforementioned films were covered with a HTM layer by spin-coating at 3000 rpm for 30 s. The HTM recipes were prepared by dissolving 20 mg of DEPT-SC in 1 mL of chlorobenzene, or 20 mg of DEPT-SC doped with 0.2 mg of F4TCNQ in 1 mL of chlorobenzene, or 20 mg of DEPT-SC doped with 28.8 μL of TBP, 17.5 μL of a stock solution of 520 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  LiTFSI in acetonitrile in 1 mL of chlorobenzene. Finally, 65 nm of gold was thermally evaporated on top of devices in a vacuum chamber.

A solar cell device was fabricated with spiro-MeOTAD for comparison. The HTM recipe was prepared by dissolving  $ 72.3\mathrm{mg} $  of spiro-MeOTAD,  $ 28.8~\mu \mathrm{L} $  of 4-tert-butylpyridine, and  $ 17.5~\mu \mathrm{L} $  of a stock solution of lithium salt in  $ 1\mathrm{mL} $  of chlorobenzene.

# 3. Results and discussion

In order to prove the CTC effect between DEPT-SC and F4TCNQ, proton nuclear magnetic resonance (  $  ^{1}  $  H NMR) characterization was carried out as a unique tool to probe the chemical shift of hydrogen atom according to the change of extranuclear electron. The change of chemical shift is determined by the shielding effect and de-

shielding effect, while the strong electronegativity of F4TCNQ component plays a role of de-shielding effect by reducing electron density of DEPT-SC, resulting in signal of de-shielding-effect proton occurring in lower external magnetic field and the opposite to the shielding-effect proton. Fig. 2 shows   $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR spectroscopy of a pristine DEPT-SC with and without different molar ratio of F4TCNQ in chloroform-d solution. With increase of the amount of F4TCNQ, the monolithic chemical shift moved to low external magnetic field and spin-spin splitting became overlapped. These may be caused by the hydrogen-bond interaction (C–H...F) between the hydrogen atom of thiophene and the fluorine atom of F4TCNQ, and/or the coordination interaction among the carbon atoms from thiophene and C(CN  $  _{2}  $  ) group of F4TCNQ. As a result, the electron cloud density around the hydrogen atom can be reduced [42].

Fig. 3a shows UV–visible absorption spectra of DEPT-SC with and without dopants in thin film state. The three films exhibited similar absorbance at 430 nm. However, the film of DEPT-SC doped with F4TCNQ forms obvious shoulder peak at 470 nm than the other two, which may be caused by the change of molecular configuration and the enhanced intermolecular forces by addition of F4TCNQ [41]. We applied static state photoluminescence measurements to investigate the charge-hole recombination on the perovskite/HTM interface. Fig. 3b shows the photoluminescence (PL) emission spectra of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   on quartz glass before and after addition of different HTMs. The emission spectra reveal that the intense fluorescence exhibited by the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film has been quenched quantitatively after the addition of HTMs. The PL intensity of perovskite film with DEPT-SC (F4TCNQ) is weaker than that of the case with the pure DEPT-SC and DEPT-SC (TBP + LiTFSI). This indicates an efficient charge extraction when doping DEPT-SC with F4TCNQ. It suggests F4TCNQ as effective dopant to promote hole transport. The results are also well agreement with the following experimental result.

As shown in Fig. 4a, it is the current density-voltage  $ (J - V) $  curves for perovskite solar cells using various HTMs tested under AM 1.5G simulated irradiation  $ (100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2}) $ . The corresponding photovoltaic parameters are presented in Table 1. The solar cells were fabricated with a typical configuration of FTO/compact TiO $ _2 $ /mesoporous TiO $ _2 $ /CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ /HTM/Au. # 4. Conclusion

In this work, the charge transfer between F4TCNQ and DEPT-SC was proved by the change of chemical shift in   $  {}^{1}  $  H NMR. The formed CTC can increase the hole mobility, surface potential and reduce HOMO energy lever of HTM, resulting a PCE of 11.52% with DEPT-SC adding 1 wt% F4TCNQ as the HTM, which increased 18% to that of pristine device (9.73%). The present results show that F4TCNQ is an effective dopant for DEPT-SC HTM to enhance the PCE without the cost of cells stability.
