# Abstract

Power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) was reported to be over 23% with a variety of configurations including the optimization of photoactive material, selection of carrier transport layers and interface engineering. Critical concerns pertaining to the instability, hysteretic effects, reproduction, flexibility, large area and transparency, which may potentially hinder their commercialization still remain. In this work, an effective low-temperature electron beam (E-beam) approach for depositing lanthanides (  $  Y^{3+}  $  ,   $  La^{3+}  $  ,   $  Ce^{3+}  $  ,   $  Nd^{3+}  $  ,   $  Sm^{3+}  $  ,   $  Gd^{3+}  $  ,   $  Tm^{3+}  $  ,   $  Yb^{3+}  $  ,   $  Lu^{3+}  $  ) doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   as electron transport layer was developed for planar PSCs application. The lanthanide dopants, especially for   $  Gd^{3+}  $  , could facilitate the charge transport behaviour and band gap optimization of   $  TiO_{2}  $  . Additionally, small molecule DRCN5T was selected as an effective additive in anti-solvent to fill grain boundary and modify the quality of the perovskite film with a grain size of 1.3-2.0   $  \mu  $  m. The modified PSCs exhibit a noticeably increased PCE from 19.0% to 20.53% with excellent long-term and light stability. More importantly, flexible, large area and transparent PSCs were also achieved. The flexible devices show more than 20% of their initial PCE values after 1000 bending cycles. The dual interfacial modification mechanism represents an attractive approach to achieve full functional PSCs.

# 1. Introduction

PSCs have attracted extensive interests due to their attractive advantages, including low cost, solution process-ability, large-scale and flexible production.  $  ^{[1-4]}  $   Despite, several challenges including effective spectral response, air-stability and flexibility still remain before PSCs become competitive commercial technologies.  $  ^{[5, 6]}  $   The performance of PSCs depends strongly on the energy level alignment and carrier-transfer properties between the perovskite photoactive layer and carrier transport layer. Thus, a lot of efforts have been devoted to the surface morphology, crystallization control, band alignment, conductivity of the perovskite photoactive layer and carrier transport layer. Uncontrolled film morphology, associated with the poor crystallinity of perovskites and more negative grain boundaries usually result in poor device performance and reproducibility. Higher carrier mobility and a flat band alignment will effectively promote the migration of photo-generated carriers and thus reduce carrier recombination at the interface. Improving the quality of the perovskite absorber by reducing the defects and increasing grain sizes has been considered as the major factors to improve the photovoltaic performance and stability of the PSCs.  $  ^{[3, 7]}  $   Various routes have been studied to optimize the morphology of perovskite photoactive films. Poly(methyl methacrylate) have been selected as a template to control nucleation and crystal growth, resulting in shiny smooth perovskite films with excellent electronic quality and a considerable increase in PCE and stability.  $  ^{[8]}  $   Poly-vinylpyrrolidone doped in perovskite photoactive films could suppress light diffusion and increases the electronic quality of the PSCs.  $  ^{[9]}  $   Other studies used additives, like   $  \alpha  $  -bis-PCBM alkali metal ions, have been used as templating agents in chlorobenzene anti-solvent to enhance high quality film formation.  $  ^{[7, 10, 11]}  $   However, very few investigations achieve the devices with stability, flexibility and large-area, which will boost the practical application of PSCs. In comparison with polymer materials, small molecules offer several advantages such as better-defined structure, less batch-to-batch variation, fine tunable energy level and deep-understanding the relationship

Therefore, DRCN5T with favorable absorption coefficient and carrier migration ability can be used as an additive or complementary absorber to improve the film quality and reduce the vacancies and grain boundaries of the perovskite layer to achieve high  $ PCE $ .

Carrier transport materials have been wildly studied in that doping could affect the carrier mobility and charge diffusion lengths.  $  ^{[19-21]}  $   Recently, metal-oxide-based electron transport materials have been studied with emphasis on modifications of morphology, physical properties and the electronic band structure.  $  ^{[19]}  $   Those approaches were effective for adjusting electron transport and recombination properties and device stability. Due to the favorable position of its conduction band, long electron lifetimes and low fabrication costs,   $  TiO_{2}  $   is frequently used as electron transport layer in PSCs.  $  ^{[19, 22]}  $   Nevertheless, the relatively high density of electronic trap states below the conduction band, which have a large influence on charge recombination and charge transport, are significant drawbacks for the application of   $  TiO_{2}  $   electrodes in PSCs.  $  ^{[19]}  $   It is well known that doping in   $  TiO_{2}  $   electron transport layers has been considered as a promising avenue to control over the electronic and optical properties of   $  TiO_{2}  $   and thus the based PSCs. The incorporation of   $  Ni^{2+}  $ ,   $  Nb^{5+}  $ ,   $  N^{3+}  $ ,   $  Cl^{-}  $ ,

Moreover, moving along the lanthanides series from Y to Lu, a gradual change in the decrease of ionic radius and the variations of electronic and chemical characterizations provide us the opportunities to unveil the underlying mechanism of the doping effect and consequently establish a general principle to conduct the further doping study on electron transport layer.  $  ^{[26, 27]}  $  At this stage, most of the research groups reported the synthesis of  $  TiO_{2}  $  electron transport layer by spray pyrolysis and precursor annealing for efficient PSCs application.  $  ^{[1, 28]}  $  These methods usually require long-term annealing process at high temperatures (450-500 °C). The low temperature preparation process of  $  TiO_{2}  $  layer based on sol-gel method has poor repeatability, and it is complicated. Additionally, residual organic additives remain in  $  TiO_{2}  $  layers will affect the transport properties of the carriers and the stability of the perovskite layer.  $  ^{[29]}  $  E-beam deposition is a very promising method because it provides large area coatings with excellent uniformity on various substrates including flexible and nonflexible substrates. Moreover, E-beam deposition has capabilities of maintaining the doping characteristics of the original targets.  $  ^{[29-31]}  $  In this paper, lanthanides ( $  Y^{3+}  $ ,  $  La^{3+}  $ ,  $  Ce^{3+}  $ ,  $  Nd^{3+}  $ ,  $  Sm^{3+}  $ ,  $  Gd^{3+}  $ ,  $  Tm^{3+}  $ ,  $  Yb^{3+}  $ ,  $  Lu^{3+}  $ ) doped  $  TiO_{2}  $  electron transport layers were prepared on FTO ( $  SnO_{2}:F  $ ) glass or flexible substrates by low-temperature E-beam method. Among the lanthanides ions, a suitable amount of  $  Gd^{3+}  $  doping (2 at.%) in  $  TiO_{2}  $  films as electron transport layer in PSCs could favorably lead to effective PCE enhancement by reducing electron recombination and photocurrent loss. # 2.2 Device fabrication

Devices were fabricated on fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) coated glass (NSG, 7Ω–1). Initially FTO was removed from regions under the anode contact, to prevent shunting upon contact with measurement pins, by etching the FTO with 2 M HCl and zinc powder. Substrates (2.0 cm×2.5 cm) were then cleaned sequentially in washing liquid (water), water, ethanol and acetone and oxygen plasma. Each substrate can produce 5 devices with the same preparation conditions. The active area of the device is 0.1 cm $  ^{2}  $  (0.2 cm×0.5 cm). The TiO $  _{2}  $  electron transport layer with thickness of 40 nm was prepared by the E-Beam method. The antisolvent assisted perovskite crystallization process by spin-coating the perovskite precursor solution is divided into two steps. The total process lasts for 50 s at a speed of

4000 rpm. Firstly, 200 ul perovskite solution was added dropwise and evenly spread on the surface of the substrate. The first spin coating step was performed for 15 s without operation. Secondly, 120 μL chlorobenzene with different amount of DRCN5T was immediately dropped onto the above film to template the nucleation and growth of the perovskite crystals. Specially, the dropping process of chlorobenzene needs to be controlled within 1 s. After spin coating process, the perovskite films need to be annealed on a heating station (Smart lab, Strider Instrument & Application Co.(shanghai), Ltd) at 150 °C for 10 minutes. A hole-transporting layer was deposited in glove via spin-coating a spiro-OMeTAD solution at 3000 rpm for 35 s. In this spin coating process, the substrate needs to reach 3000 rpm first. The spiro-OMeTAD precursor was prepared by adding 144.6 mg of spiro-OMeTAD, 57.6 μL of 4-tert-butylpyridine and 35 μL Li-TFSI (pre-dissolved in acetonitrile at 520 mg/mL) to 2 mL of chlorobenzene and then stirred for 12 h. Devices were then left overnight in the dry air for 24 hours to oxidize the spiro-OMeTAD sufficiently before depositing Au, Ag or NiO/Ag/NiO contact.

# 2.4 Theoretical calculation

The density functional theory (DFT) calculations were done by VASP software with a periodic slab. The Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) functional with the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) was used to describe the electronic structures, and the projector augmented wave method was applied to calculate the interaction between electrons and atomic cores. All the calculations used a cutoff energy of 500 eV with the force threshold of 0.05 eV Å  $  ^{-1}  $  . The lattice parameter of TiO  $  _{2}  $   was optimized and one of the Ti atoms is replaced by a Gd atom to build a Gd-substituted TiO  $  _{2}  $  -Gd structure. For all of the structures considered, the Brillouin zone was described with a 3 × 3 × 3 k-point grid.

# 4. Conclusion

In conclusion, we employed a one-step spin-coating method to prepare CsFAMA based PSCs by dual interfacial modifications with small-molecules and lanthanides doping. The low-temperature E-beam evaporated lanthanides doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   exhibits excellent electron transporting characteristics for efficient PSCs, accompanied with an optimized PCE of 19.6%. The excellent performance is attributed to the excellent optical and electronic properties of the lanthanides doped   $  TiO_{2}  $   material, such as high electrical conductivity and reasonable band gap matching. Furthermore, by introducing an appropriate amount of DRCN5T in the anti-solvent of chlorobenzene, the high-quality perovskite films can be prepared, resulting in a noticeable PCE of 20.53% with fewer hysteresis effects. Our results show that the DRCN5T (i) templates the nucleation and growth of the CsFAMA perovskite with large grain size, (ii) passivates the interface defects and reduces the carrier recombination at grain boundaries (iii) generates carriers as complementary light-absorbing layer under the light irradiation, accompanied with enhanced spectral response complementation and enhanced device performance. This work provides a technique towards the widespread use of the one-step solution process and the fabrication of efficient and stable PSCs for commercial applications.
