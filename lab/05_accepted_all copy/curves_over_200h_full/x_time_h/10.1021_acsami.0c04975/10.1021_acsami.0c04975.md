# Abstract

Flexible perovskite solar cells (PSCs) are currently one of the most attractive flexible thin-film photovoltaic technologies. Despite achieving remarkable progress in power conversion efficiencies (PCEs), flexible PSCs have not yet kept pace with rigid PSCs. Defect passivation is of crucial importance to further enhance the PCEs of flexible PSCs. Here, highly-dispersed fluorographene quantum dots (FGQDs) are exfoliated from graphite fluoride with the aid of stirring and sonication and used to passivate the grain boundaries and surface of the perovskite films for high-performance flexible PSCs. Photoluminescence spectroscopy (PL) and time-resolved PL decays indicate that the FGQDs are beneficial for suppressing carrier recombination. Space-charge-limited current measurements prove that the passivated perovskite film exhibits reduced trap densities. As a result, a best PCE of 20.40% is achieved from the flexible PSCs, owing to significantly reduced charge recombination. Moreover, the champion device delivers an outstanding steady-state PCE exceeding 20%. The flexible PSCs with the FGQDs also exhibit enhanced thermal stability and environmental stability. Our work not only highlights the importance of passivating the defects within the perovskite films for high-efficiency flexible PSCs but also offers a promising future for the commercialization of flexible PSCs.

Keywords: defect passivation; liquid-phase exfoliation; fluorographene; quantum dots; flexible; perovskite solar cells

# 1. Introduction

Tremendous efforts have been devoted to develop high-efficiency flexible solar cells in recent years to meet the energy demands of various moving objects, wearable devices, and buildings with curved surfaces.  $  ^{1-3}  $   Of flexible thin-film solar cells, flexible lead halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have gained worldwide attention owing to their low-cost and high power conversion efficiencies (PCEs).  $  ^{4-6}  $   Though the state-of-art PCE of the flexible single-junction PSC has been reported as 20.01%,  $  ^{7}  $   further enhancement is required as the champion PCE of flexible   $  CuIn_{1-x}Ga_{x}Se_{2}  $   solar cells has already exceeded 20%.  $  ^{8,9}  $   Defects within perovskite absorber play a crucial role in determining the PCEs of PSCs, especially those of the flexible PSC. Those defects are inevitably distributed at the surface and grain-boundaries or in the bulk of the perovskite films, which not only result in nonradiative charge recombination and limited PCEs but also cause the rapid degradation of the perovskite absorber.  $  ^{10-13}  $   For the flexible PSC, the defects are more likely to form owing to the low surface energy, the wrinkle of the flexible substrates.  $  ^{4,14}  $

The defects at the perovskite surface are easily passivated. Many surface-treatment methods have been well developed to passivate the surface to enhance device performance.  $  ^{13, 15-17}  $   Compared with the surface passivation, the passivation of grain boundaries and the elimination of the bulk point defects are more challenging, which are inside the perovskite films. Considering the liquid fabrication process of the perovskite films, researchers employed various organic or inorganic materials as precursor additives to passivate the grain-boundary defects and thus reduce the bulk

Hence, it is imperative to find a capable grain-boundary defect passivating strategy for high-efficiency flexible PSCs. As indicated in previous studies, most of the grain-boundary defects can cause organic-cation evaporation and ion migration.  $  ^{28, 29}  $   The defects not only lead to the formation of carrier traps but also result in the decomposition of the perovskite grains, which are detrimental to the device PCE and stability.  $  ^{30, 31}  $   Fluorine atoms could be one of the most outstanding candidates to address the problems.  $  ^{21, 31, 32}  $   When F atoms were doped, they were likely to distribute at the grain boundaries and form strong hydrogen bonds with organic cations, which could lock the surface organic cations and thus significantly improve the device performance and long-term stability. Thereby, a suitable defect-passivation additive with fluoride atoms for perovskite passivation should be promising for the realization of high-efficiency flexible PSCs.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The FGQDs were obtained by simply stirring and sonicating commercial ultrafine graphite fluoride powders (shown in Figure S1a) in a N-Methyl pyrrolidone (NMP) solution. The use of NMP as the exfoliation solvent is ascribed to two reasons: 1) NMP has been proven as the best solvent for the dispersibility and stability of exfoliated FGQDs, owing to its free  $ \mathfrak{p}_{\mathrm{z}} $  orbital forming pseudo-hydrogen bonds with FGQD's  $ (\mathrm{C_nF})_{\mathrm{x}} $ -F groups;  $ ^{44} $  and 2) NMP is one of the commonly-used solvents in the perovskite precursor solutions.

It can be seen that the   $  R_{re}  $   decreases exponentially with the increased bias voltage for both PSCs. The   $  R_{re}  $   of the PSC based on the 4-F perovskite is always larger than that of the counterpart, indicating the successfully suppressed recombination by the FGQDs. The results are in good agreement with the PL measurement results.

# 4.2 Device Fabrication

Chemically etched PEN/ITO substrates were sonicated in deionized water, ethanol, and isopropanol for 15 min successively.
