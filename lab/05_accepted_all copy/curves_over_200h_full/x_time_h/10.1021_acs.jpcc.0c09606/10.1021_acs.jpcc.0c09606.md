# Tuning the Interfacial Dipole Moment of Spacer Cations for Charge Extraction in Efficient and Ultrastable Perovskite Solar Cells

Yuankun Qiu, Jianghu Liang, Zhanfei Zhang, Zihao Deng, Heng Xu, Maosheng He, Jianli Wang, Yajuan Yang, Lingti Kong, and Chun-Chao Chen*

Cite This: J. Phys. Chem. C 2021, 125, 1256–1268

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: The two-dimensional (2D)/three-dimensional (3D) heterojunction perovskite solar cell (PSC) has recently been recognized as a promising photovoltaic structure for achieving high efficiency and long-term stability. Rational design of the 2D spacer cation is important to achieve a win-win situation for defects' passivation and photogenerated carrier extraction. Herein, we carry out first-principles calculation to analyze the dipole moment of phenethylamine-type molecules and their resulting 2D/3D perovskites. Based on the results of theoretical calculation, the dipole moment of 2D cations can be well tuned by varying the number of fluorine atoms on the para-position of the benzene ring, which further determines the interfacial dipole across the 2D/3D heterojunction interface. A high dipole

2D perovskite layer at the interface between the 3D perovskite and hole-transporting material is found to promote charge transport and suppress charge trapping efficiently. As a result, our 2D/3D PSCs exhibit a champion power conversion efficiency over  $ 22\% $  and a fill factor over  $ 83\% $ . Moreover, our solar cells also show a remarkable stability, maintaining  $ 80\% $  of its initial efficiency for more than  $ 1400\mathrm{h} $  without encapsulation under a  $ 30 \pm 5\% $  relative humidity.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Organic–inorganic perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved huge success after only 10 years of active investigation, which have now reached a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.5%. $  ^{1-4}  $  This significant breakthrough is mainly due to their advantages of optoelectronic properties, such as high absorption coefficient, long diffusion length of carriers, and high defect tolerance. $  ^{5-7}  $  However, perovskites are still suffering from uncontrollable defects, heavily affecting their performance and stability. Due to the facile low-temperature solution process, $  ^{8,9}  $  perovskites are normally accompanied by intrinsic point defects, such as vacancies, substitutions, and interstitial defects in the bulk as well as those at grain boundaries and interfaces. $  ^{10,11}  $  These defects may act as trap recombination centers and induce ion migration, $  ^{12,13}  $  which inevitably affect the transportation of carriers and thus limit PSCs to approach their theoretical efficiency.

To mitigate the negative effect of defects on the performance of devices, a wide range of technologies have been implemented in order to enhance the efficiency and stability.  $  ^{14-24}  $   For example, additive engineering,  $  ^{25}  $   interfacial engineering,  $  ^{26}  $   and compositional engineering  $  ^{27}  $   are adopted to passivate the defects, align the energy level, suppress the nonradiative recombination, and enhance the stability. Among these methods, layered two-dimensional (2D) perovskite thin films formed by 2D spacer cations were widely reported owing to their strong air stability and tunable spacer cations, which

In addition, compared with linear alkylammonium, 2D perovskites made of alkylammoniums with benzene can form a more orderly and oriented layer, which benefits the longitudinal transportation of carriers.  $  ^{39}  $   Despite 2D/3D perovskites being an effective structure to passivate the defects of perovskite devices, the incorporation of 2D spacer cations can also induce unfavorable quantum-well structures.

In the perovskite quantum-well structures,  $  ^{40}  $   3D perovskite layers are sandwiched by 2D perovskite layers consisting of organic cations, resulting in electron–hole pairs being confined within these 2D perovskite wells.  $  ^{41,42}  $   Though the 2D/3D perovskite structure is not a real quantum-well heterojunction because the band extrema of the wave functions of the 3D and 2D perovskites may not have the same symmetry,  $  ^{43}  $   this 2D/3D perovskite structure is found to share similar quantum-well effects, such as the enlargement of band gap and binding energy of excitons.  $  ^{44,45}  $   Besides, the 2D perovskite framework can also cause dielectric confinement and anisotropic charge transportation properties because of its insulating features and relatively low carrier mobility.  $  ^{46}  $   As a result, exciton separation and carrier transportation are discouraged in the 2D perovskite-incorporated structures.  $  ^{42}  $   Therefore, the trade-off between defects' passivation and interfacial charge transport efficiency calls for the careful selection for 2D spacer cations.

One promising method to address the problems of charge confinement and localization is to induce an additional electrical driving force, such as a dipole layer, across the interface of the device. For example, a pair of electric dipole layers (PFN and p-PFP-O) was introduced between the transporting layers and metal electrodes in order to adjust the surface potentials and enhance the driving force for photogenerated carriers.  $  ^{47}  $   In another report, para-substituted benzenethiol molecules with high dipole moments were adopted to assist charge extraction and suppress carrier recombination.  $  ^{48}  $   Recent reports also show that the adoption of 2D spacer cations with a strong dipole moment is one of the effective methods to address the charge confinement penalty in 2D/3D perovskites.  $  ^{30,37,46}  $   However, the origin of dipole moment and the effect of dipole moment on a built-in electric field are still far from clear. Therefore, systematic understanding and tuning of the functionalities of the interfacial dipole moment are urgently required to improve the efficiency of 2D/3D heterojunction PSCs.

In this work, we carried out a joint theoretical—experimental study on the dipole moment of 2D organic spacer cations from phenethylamine-type molecules to unveil its influence on the built-in electric field, device performance, and stability. Three types of 2D cations, PEA  $  ^{+}  $  , FPEA  $  ^{+}  $  , and F  $  _{3}  $  MPEA  $  ^{+}  $  , are investigated to understand the effect of fluorine numbers on the properties of the 2D/3D perovskite heterojunction and the performance of the devices. Owing to fluorine's high electronegativity and strong electron-withdrawing ability, the strength of the dipole moment inside the 2D cations as well as resulting 2D perovskites can be fine-tuned. According to first-principles calculation based on the density functional theory (DFT), F  $  _{3}  $  MPEA presents 3.44 times increase in electric dipole moment (EDM) as compared to the weakly polar phenylethylamine molecule, such as PEA. The enlarged dipole moment inside molecules is the origin of interfacial dipoles, which can significantly upshift the Fermi level and enlarge the

built-in electric field across the 2D/3D perovskite heterojunction to facilitate selective charge transport. Moreover, effective defects' elimination and improved stability are observed owing to the aromatic moiety and hydrophobic property of the trifluoromethyl unit. Compared to pristine 3D perovskite with a PCE of 19.2%, a high-performance 2D/3D heterojunction PSC with a champion PCE of 22.4% was achieved. In addition, this 2D/3D PSC exhibits superior thermal and moisture stability, maintaining 80% of its initial efficiency for more than 1400 h without encapsulation under a   $  30 \pm 5\%  $   relative humidity. The quality of the 2D/3D layer is nearly unaffected at   $  85^{\circ}C  $   under a nitrogen atmosphere or at room temperature with an 85% relative humidity for 144 h.

# 2. MATERIALS AND METHODS

2.1. Materials. Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) was purchased from Liaoning Huite Optoelectronics Technology Co., Ltd.   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloid precursor (tin oxide, 15% in   $  H_{2}O  $   colloidal dispersion) was purchased from Alfa Aesar. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO 99.8%), chlorobenzene (99.8%), and N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. 4-tert-Butylpyridine (TBP, 96%) was obtained from J&K Chemical, isopropanol (IPA, 99.8%) was purchased from Innochem, and 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD, 99%) was purchased from Lumtec. All chemicals and reagents were used without further purification.   $  F_{3}MPEAI  $   was synthesized according to an optimized recipe from a previously reported literature in Supporting Information.  $  ^{49}  $

2.2. Device Fabrication. FTO glass was first cleaned by anhydrous ethanol for 15 min, followed by a detergent solution at   $  50^{\circ}  $   for 30 min; after that, the glass was cooled down to room temperature and cleaned by deionized water, acetone, and IPA for 15 min, respectively.

After cleaning, the glass was dried by nitrogen gas and then treated by ultraviolet ozone for 15 min. Then, the substrate was spin-coated by a compact thin layer of   $  SnO_{2}  $   (2.67%, diluted by DI water) at a speed of 4000 rpm for 30 s, and then it was annealed at 150 °C for 30 min.

After depositing   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanoparticles, the substrate was cleaned with UV again to be used for deposition of perovskite thin films. 1.3 M   $  PbI_{2}  $   in a solvent of DMF and DMSO (9:1) was spin-coated onto   $  SnO_{2}  $   at a speed of 1500 rpm for 30 s. Then, the substrate was annealed on a hot plate at 70 °C for 1 min and then cooled down to room temperature. A solution of FAI/MAI/MABr/MACl (40 mg:19 mg:6 mg:5 mg in 1 mL IPA) was spin-coated onto the substrate of   $  PbI_{2}  $   at a speed of 2000 rpm for 30 s, followed by annealing at 150 °C for 15 min out of the glovebox at a humidity of 30%. After perovskite layer formatting, the samples were transferred back to the glovebox for further fabrication. A PEAI, FPEAI, and   $  F_{3}MPEAI  $   solution was spin-coated on the 3D perovskite layer at their optimized concentration at a speed of 5000 rpm for 30 s, and then the samples were annealed at 100 °C for 10 min to form a 2D/3D perovskite layer. For each 2D perovskite model, optimized lattice constant parameters are listed in Table S1.

# 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Electric dipole is physically defined as two point charges separated from each other, with the dipole moment vector pointing from the negative charge to the positive charge.  $  ^{54}  $   We aimed to investigate the charge distribution inside these molecules, which can guide us to the direction and magnitude of the dipole moment of our cation molecules. Therefore, we performed electrostatic surface potential (ESP) and EDM analysis for PEA, FPEA, and   $  F_{3}MPEA  $  . Geometric optimization was carried out for the three molecules by the Gaussian 09 package,  $  ^{55}  $   which are optimized using the PBE0 density functional along with the B3LYP/6-31G(d) basis set.

Having presented theoretical evidence of dipole moment incorporation by polar spacer cations, we aimed to directly confirm the surface potential modification through KPFM measurements. We measured the contact potential difference (CPD) between the tip and samples, which is associated with their relative work function difference. That is, a positive (negative) CPD indicates a deeper (shallower) Fermi level of samples as compared to the tip.  $  ^{61}  $   The results are shown in Figure 2, where surface potential mappings are shown for the pristine 3D perovskite layer and three types of the 2D/3D perovskite heterojunction. In the PEA-based 2D/3D perovskite layer, no obvious CPD shift is observed (548 mV for pristine and 58 mV for the PEA-based 2D/3D layer), due to negligible EDM difference between MA and the PEA molecule. In contrast, when a FPEA-based 2D/3D perovskite layer is measured, the surface potential of the film shifts toward the negative direction (233 mV), suggesting an upward shift of Fermi level. Furthermore, when the   $  F_{3}  $  MPEA-based 2D/3D perovskite is measured, the mean value of CPD decreases even more significantly (4.7 mV). These results indicate that an interfacial dipole is formed between the 2D and 3D interface in the case of FPEA- and   $  F_{3}  $  MPEA-based 2D/3D perovskite films. The interface dipole is even stronger in the latter one. The downward shift CPD indicates that the direction of the dipole vector is from the 3D perovskite pointing to the 2D perovskite,  $  ^{62,63}  $   which is well consistent with our computational result. UPS (Figure S2) was also carried out to further verify our results. We calculated the work function of 3D and 2D/3D perovskite layers on account of the expression   $  W_{F} = 21.22 \, eV - E_{cutoff}  $  . The work function shift of the   $  F_{3}  $  MPEA-based 2D/3D perovskite heterojunction is about 0.5 eV, which is aligned with KPFM results. We also measured the CPD value for the above-mentioned four structures after depositing hole-transporting

layers. In this case, however, no significant difference of the CPD value is observed, thus proving that the electric dipoles of the heterojunction structure mainly originate from the 2D/3D perovskite interface, not from the 2D perovskite/hole transport layer (HTL) interface.

To exclude the influence of film quality on the CPD value, we compare XRD patterns of the 2D/3D perovskite film formed by these three types of cations (Figure S3). Apart from the 2D perovskite characteristic peak near   $  5^{\circ}  $   due to the different size of cations, all three films show almost identical patterns, indicating the same components and orientations for the 3D part in different 2D/3D perovskite heterojunctions. The absorption spectra for these three samples are almost the same, by which the Tauc curves were also plotted (Figure S4). We also compare SEM and AFM images (Figures S5 and S6) for the above samples, in which we find similar roughness and grain size for the three films. Therefore, we conclude that the film quality formed by different 2D cations is almost the same, and thus, it does not contribute to CPD difference.

Based on KPFM and UPS measurements, we plotted energy level schematic diagrams for 3D, 2D/3D (without interfacial dipole), and 2D/3D (with interfacial dipole) perovskite films. In a conventional 3D PSC (Figure 3a), the 3D perovskites are in direct contact with HTL and commonly suffer from two types of defects. In the case of PEA-based 2D/3D heterojunction devices (Figure 3b), 2D perovskites with a wide band gap is applied as a passivation layer on top of the 3D perovskite, resulting in a band gap enlargement at the interface. When the F  $  _{3}  $  MPEA-based 2D perovskite is formed at the interface, its negative trifluoromethyl end may point toward the 3D perovskite and its positive ammonium group may point toward HTL, resulting in favorable interfacial dipole moment. Owing to the reinforcement of the built-in electric field, the carriers trapped by those intrinsic defects can be effectively extracted and swept out toward transporting layers. Therefore, the negative effect of both intrinsic and interfacial defects can be successfully mitigated by the incorporation of this 2D perovskite layer with a strong dipole moment. The eliminated trapping, reduced recombination, and reinforced built-in potential across the device are expected to improve the devices' fill factor (FF) and V  $  _{OC}  $  .  $  ^{[36]}  $

To understand the enhanced operational stability under humid conditions and increased hydrophobicity, we simulated the interaction between the water film and pure 3D perovskite as well as 2D perovskite. We performed AIMD simulation by constructing an initial structure of the water/perovskite interface, in which eight water molecules are absorbed onto the perovskite film tetragonal surface (0 0 1). As shown in Figure 7c, the unmodified 3D perovskite surface shows an inferior moisture stability during the simulation. After 5 ps, water molecules diffuse to the nearby MAI surface and start interacting with  $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $  cations. The oxygen of water molecules forms hydrogen bonds with the hydrogen of MA, which breaks the hydrogen bonds between  $ \mathrm{PbI}_3^- $  and  $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $  units and causes the desorption of MA molecules and the degradation of perovskite films. We also notice that more hydrogen bonds are formed between the water film and the perovskite surface as simulation time goes on. This means that the sole 3D perovskite surface cannot resist water molecules from infiltrating. In contrast, the structure of 2D perovskites keeps rigid as time proceeds. The  $ \mathrm{MA - PbI_3} $  framework is not affected by water molecules in this 2D perovskite case. We notice that the  $ \mathrm{F}_3\mathrm{MPEA}^+ $  cations change into an upright configuration gradually with the hydrophobic trifluoromethyl units aligning together to form a tight network as the simulation time prolongs. Therefore, the water film cannot penetrate into the 2D perovskite framework during the total simulation period. We also calculated the radial distribution function (RDF) of two structures to quantify their stability. Four types of bonds between  $ \mathrm{PbI}_3^- $  and  $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $  units are considered (Figure S14). We find that all RDFs show a higher and narrower peak in the  $ (\mathrm{F}_3\mathrm{MPEA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $  structure as compared to that in  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ , indicating again its robust structure and enhanced moisture resistance.

# 4. CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we carried out a joint theoretical and experimental study to clarify the origin of dipole moment for the 2D/3D perovskite heterojunction structure. The origin of an interfacial dipole moment can be traced to the formation of a dipole moment inside the 2D spacer cation. By changing the number of fluorine atoms in the 2D spacer cation, we can effectively tune the interfacial dipole across the device. Among three 2D/3D heterojunctions, the  $ \mathrm{F}_3\mathrm{MPEA} $ -based 2D/3D perovskite heterojunction presents the largest dipole moment both inside the molecule and across the 2D/3D interface. We revealed that the enlarged interfacial dipole moment is of critical importance for the enhancement of the built-in potential across the 2D/3D perovskite heterojunction. The reinforced electric field can effectively extract the carriers trapped by intrinsic defects and move them to transporting layers. Due to the advantages of this polar molecule incorporated in the system, over  $ 22\% $  efficiency of the champion cell was achieved, with a FF exceeding  $ 83\% $ , which is almost the highest in planar n-i-p structure devices. Moreover, the  $ \mathrm{F}_3\mathrm{MPEA}^+ $  spacer cation increases the surface hydrophobicity and thermal stability of perovskite layers and devices.
