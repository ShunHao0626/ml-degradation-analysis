# Ultracompact, Well-Packed Perovskite Flat Crystals: Preparation and Application in Planar Solar Cells with High Efficiency and Humidity Tolerance

ABSTRACT: Planar perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have exhibited a promising alternative for high efficiency recently because of a new record of certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 21.5%. At the present stage of PSCs, achieving further improvements for long-term stability and efficiency are the key issues. It is necessary to fabricate PSCs with good interface and film crystallinity. Here, planar PSCs are fabricated by a synergistic effect of dimethyl sulfoxide additive and a dropwise addition method in a two-step deposition method. Under the optimizing procedure, a large-grain perovskite film with well-packed flat crystals is induced with a largest grain size of about 5   $  \mu  $  m, which reveals decreased defect states owing to reduced grain boundaries. Moreover, the film has a highly compact and flat surface, leading to high crystallinity, enhanced

light absorption, and low carrier recombination rates. Corresponding PCE is significantly promoted to 20.63% from the original 16.02%. More importantly, the PSCs even without encapsulation exhibit excellent humidity stability, retaining 93% of its initial efficiency after 500 h in air with 40% humidity, whereas the pristine devices degrade by approximately 65% after only surviving for 256 h. Therefore, the result provides a promising route for preparation of large-grain perovskite films and high-performance and stable PSCs.

KEYWORDS: planar PSCs, DMSO additive, dropwise addition method, large-grain perovskite film, high-performance and stable PSCs

# INTRODUCTION

Organic–inorganic halide perovskite materials have attracted widespread attention and exhibited numerous optoelectronic applications in the last few years. Because methylammonium lead triiodide (MAPbI  $  _{3}  $  ) perovskite was initially used as an active layer in solar cells and got a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 3.8% in 2009,  $  ^{1}  $  the PCE of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has now risen to a certified value of 23.7%,  $  ^{2}  $  which rivals the established technologies such as silicon, CdTe, and GaAs. In planar PSCs, although the titanium oxide (TiO  $  _{2}  $  ) is often as an electron transport layer used to generate high performance and stable power output,  $  ^{3,4}  $  the tin oxide (SnO  $  _{2}  $  ) still offers the highest certified PCE of 21.52% because of its higher electron mobility and more suitable energy level.  $  ^{5}  $

The defect states of perovskite films are considered as a critical factor for affecting the performance and stability of PSCs.  $  ^{6-8}  $   Many pieces of evidence have indicated that grain boundaries in polycrystalline perovskite thin films cause increasing of charge recombination owing to the presence of defects located in grain boundaries.  $  ^{9-11}  $   Therefore, the

perovskite film of large crystalline grains with less grain boundaries has been manifested to improve efficiency of PSCs.  $  ^{10,12,13}  $   Besides, PSCs of increased crystalline grain sizes also exhibit better stability. For example, by introducing a Lewis base organic component, the perovskite particle sizes are enlarged with one-step spin coating method because of the Lewis acid and base interaction, which reduces defect densities and elevates efficiency and moisture stability of mesoporous-type PSCs.  $  ^{14}  $   However, the reported largest size of crystal is just close to 2   $  \mu  $  m. This limited size is one of negative factors to restrict the PCE and long-term moisture stability of corresponding PSCs. Numerous approaches, including additive engineering,  $  ^{11,13}  $   antisolvent engineering,  $  ^{15}  $   and solvent vapor post-annealing  $  ^{12}  $   have been employed to enlarge the grain sizes. However, the desired grain sizes are still not obtained for improving performance of PSCs.

Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) is demonstrated a usual cosolvent for forming the intermediate phase. It can be used to retard the crystallization process and then result in high-quality perovskite films. $ ^{16-19} $  For instance, by exchange of DMSO intercalated in  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  ( $ \mathrm{PbI}_2(\mathrm{DMSO}) $  complex) with formamidinium iodide (FAI) (MABr), a flat and dense  $ \mathrm{FAPbI}_3 $  film of large grain sizes is fabricated with using the intramolecular exchange method, which produces mesoporous-type devices with a PCE over  $ 20\% $ . $ ^{19} $  Nevertheless, the final products still present an unstable phase issue in humidity environment. $ ^{20,21} $  In addition, the DMSO is also used during the annealing process. $ ^{22} $  In the process,  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  crystals can develop to larger sizes in the DMSO atmosphere than those in the  $ \mathrm{N}_2 $  atmosphere. $ ^{12} $  However, the sizes of perovskite crystal are not over micrometer scale, and the PCE of solar cell is just  $ 17.64\% $ .

The two-step deposition method has been adopted to directly fabricate various mixed-cation perovskite films.  $  ^{[19,23,24]}  $   Generally, a   $  PbI_{2}  $   precursor solution is first coated on the electron transport layer. Second, the organic salt solution is spin-coated on the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film, and ultimately the perovskite phase is formed via annealing because of diffusion of the quasi-bilayer.  $  ^{[23,25]}  $   Recently, a simplified two-step deposition method with multidrop coating is developed to improve the grain sizes and film quality.  $  ^{[26]}  $   However, in this method, the mesoporous scaffolds and complex process of high-temperature sintering are still employed, just contributing a PCE of 19.3%. These limit the commercialization application of PSCs in flexible and tandem devices.

Herein, a compact and smooth perovskite (FAP-  $ \mathrm{bl}_3)_{0.97}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_3)_{0.03} $  layer is developed on the top of a low-temperature processed  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $  layer without a mesoporous scaffold, through a dropwise addition method with synergy of DMSO additive in two-step deposition method. It is demonstrated that the large perovskite grains can be obtained with the dropwise addition method by controlling dosage of organic salts during the second step deposition. When this method and DMSO additive are simultaneously employed, the well-packed flat crystals are generated, and the largest crystal size can reach  $ 5\mu \mathrm{m} $ . The corresponding perovskite film possesses high crystallinity, strong light absorption, and reduced trap states. Furthermore, the PCE of planar PSCs based on the perovskite film with well-packed flat crystals is dramatically improved up to  $ 20.63\% $ , relative to  $ 16.02\% $  of the pristine PSCs. Further, the unsealed devices still sustain  $ 93\% $  PCE of its initial value even after  $ 500\mathrm{h} $  in  $ 40\% $  relative humidity at room temperature. In consequence, these results suggest that the developing large-grain, well-packed flat crystals is a helpful strategy to obtain highly efficient and stable PSCs.

It is crucial to understand the origins of the optimizing way of the synergistic effect for leading to the enhanced grain sizes and improved interface. First, it is demonstrated that organic iodides reacting with   $  PbI_{2}  $   from the surface to the inner crystalline regions of   $  PbI_{2}  $   is a sequential process. The process is not sufficient when the high-performance perovskite films have the thickness of over 500 nm because of some unreacted   $  PbI_{2}  $   residue and uncontrolled surface roughness.  $  ^{19}  $   By the dropwise addition method, the organic iodide solution infiltrates the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film continuously, allowing sufficient infiltration of the solution across the entire thickness of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   film. Second, the ionic radius of an   $  FA^{+}  $  cation is larger than that of an   $  MA^{+}  $  cation,  $  ^{39}  $   and therefore, FAI penetration of the   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer is more difficult, manifesting that the longer reaction time is needed. The dropwise addition method ensures enough reaction time. Third, in solution, molecules of the particle surface with fewer bonded neighbors are less stable than those in the interior. Large particles have fewer surface molecules and more interior molecules in contrast to small particles.  $  ^{40}  $   As the density of free molecules is gradually increased by dropwise addition method, the free molecules are inclined to redeposit on the large-particle surface resulting in particle sizes augmenting further. When the dropwise addition method introduces the DMSO additive, first, the dissolution of the small particles are promoted, which generates more free molecules redepositing on the surface of large particles leading to larger particles than that without the DMSO additive. Next, because DMSO can dissolve   $  PbI_{2}  $ , the organic iodide solution in IPA/DMSO permeates the   $  PbI_{2}  $   layer easily relative to the solution in IPA, which facilitates the complete conversion of the remainder   $  PbI_{2}  $   yielding the smooth perovskite surface. Finally, as the DMSO volatilizes gradually by the dropwise addition method, the crystallized speed of the perovskite is accelerated. Then, the black   $  \alpha  $  -phase perovskite forms gradually at room temperature. Because the stable vacant terminations of the perovskite surface have to expose cations  $  ^{29}  $

$$
Y (t) = A _ {1} \exp (- t / \tau_ {1}) + A _ {2} \exp (- t / \tau_ {2}) + A _ {0}
$$

The fast decay component   $  (\tau_{1})  $   corresponds to the trap recombination, and the slow decay component   $  (\tau_{2})  $   is associated with bimolecular radiative recombination of perovskite film in bulk. $$
\tau_ {\mathrm{ave}} = \frac {\sum A _ {i} \tau_ {i} ^ {2}}{\sum A _ {i} \tau_ {i}}
$$

Planar Device Performance Characterization and Stability. The current density–voltage (J-V) characteristic curves of four types of devices are measured by a scan speed of 150 mV s  $  ^{-1}  $  under standard AM 1.5G solar illumination in air. Figure 6a presents the J-V curves and corresponding performance parameters for the best devices under reverse scan. It is exhibited that the PCEs of 16.02, 18.09, 19.73, and 20.63% are achieved from the sample-A-D. It suggests that the cell performances are obviously improved by introducing DMSO into the perovskite layer or using the dropwise addition method. Particularly, the device derived from the optimizing way of synergistic effect exhibits the excellent performance of highest PCE of 20.63% with  $  J_{SC}  $  of 24.81 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ ,  $  V_{OC}  $  of 1.09 V, and FF of 76.28%. It is observed that increased PCEs of the sample-A-D come from the increased  $  J_{SC}  $ ,  $  V_{OC}  $ , and FF, which is primarily ascribed to the formation of a dense and smooth perovskite thin film of the large crystalline regions without pinholes. The above results manifest that the optimizing way of synergistic effect efficiently boosts the performance of corresponding PSCs, that is, higher  $  V_{OC}  $ ,  $  J_{SC}  $ , and FF values, which are in consistent with larger crystal size, higher crystallinity, stronger light absorption, longer carrier lifetime and decreased surface and bulk defects observed in SEM of perovskite thin films.  $  ^{[11,36]}  $  To evaluate the device performance of the sample-D correctly, Figure 6b shows that the sample-D delivers the PCE of 20.30% with  $  J_{SC}  $  of 24.20 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ ,  $  V_{OC}  $  of 1.09 V, and FF of 76.96% under reverse scan and has a PCE of 19.64% with  $  J_{SC}  $  of 24.16 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $ ,  $  V_{OC}  $  of 1.07 V, and FF of 75.97% under forward scan, exhibiting a weakened hysteresis characteristic. # CONCLUSION

In conclusion, we introduce the DMSO additive into the FA/MA mixed solution in IPA, enlarging perovskite particle sizes. Besides, by the dropwise addition method, the growth of perovskite grains is also boosted. Further, under interaction of the DMSO additive and the dropwise addition method, the well-packed flat crystals are induced, and the champion crystal size can reach 5   $  \mu  $  m. The large-grain perovskite film with well-packed flat crystals possesses a highly compact and smooth surface, resulting in high crystallinity. Compared with the pristine sample, the large-sized film dramatically decreased grain boundaries and associated charge recombination, which is beneficial for the improvement of photovoltaic parameters. Because of high-quality perovskite films with low defect states, performance and stability of PSCs are significantly promoted. Finally, we have successfully fabricated planar PSCs that reach an outstanding PCE up to 20.63% and retain 93% of its original PCE value.

# EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

Materials. The   $  SnO_{2}  $   colloid precursor (15 wt %), IPA (>99.5%), and acetonitrile (>99.8%) are obtained from Alfa Aesar. FAI (>99.5%), methylammonium bromine (MABr, >99.5%), methylammonium chloride (MACl, >99.5%), and spiro-OMeTAD (>99.5%) are purchased from Lumtec, Taiwan. DMSO (>99.9%) and N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, >99.8%) are purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Li-bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI), 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP), ethanol, and acetone are received from Aladdin. All the chemicals and solvents reagents are directly used without further purification.

Solar Cell Fabrication. ITO glass (Yingkou You Xuan Trade Co., Ltd) is sequentially cleaned by using detergent, deionized water, acetone, IPA, and ethanol for 20 min in sequence. Then, the ITO substrate is cleaned further by using UV–ozone for 25 min. SnO  $  _{2}  $  colloid precursor is diluted by deionized water to 2.67 wt %, followed by spin-coating on the ITO substrate at spinning speed of 4000 rpm for 30 s and then anneal at 150 °C for 30 min. Afterward, the obtained planar SnO  $  _{2}  $  film is transferred into a nitrogen filled glovebox. PbI  $  _{2}  $  (1.3 M) in the mixture solvent of DMF/DMSO (9.5:0.5, by volume) is coated on the SnO  $  _{2}  $  layer at 1500 rpm for 30 s and anneal at 70 °C for 60 s. When the PbI  $  _{2}  $  film has cooled down, 50  $  \mu  $  L of mixture solution of FAI/MABr/MACl (1:0.15:0.25, molar ratio, in 1 mL IPA) is poured on the yellow PbI  $  _{2}  $  film during the spin-coating process, and the substrate is continuously spun at 1400 rpm for 30 s. The resulting film is transferred into ambient air (40–50% humidity) and annealed on the hot plate by keeping 150 °C for 16 min. The pristine film is named sample-A. To produce perovskite films with large grains, the 50  $  \mu  $  L mixture solution of FAI/MABr/MACl is dissolved in 1 mL of IPA, and DMSO was added at an optimized concentration of 25  $  \mu  $  L mL  $  ^{-1}  $ , and other steps are the same as above. The obtained film is named sample-B. For further obtaining larger-grain films, 50  $  \mu  $  L of mixture solution of FAI/MABr/MACl without the DMSO additive is added dropwise onto the PbI  $  _{2}  $  layers with carefully controlling of dropped numbers. The substrate is spun continuously at 1400 rpm for 30 s in the dropwise addition process, and mixture solution is added dropwise as one drop for every 1

s(about 10   $  \mu  $  L). Other steps are the same as sample-A. The film changes to the red-brown color in the spin-coating process. This resulting film is named sample-C. The largest-grain sample-D is fabricated by dual-influence of the DMSO additive and dropwise addition method. After the preparation of the perovskite layer, the 70 mM spiro-OMeTAD solution in chlorobenzene is spin-coated on perovskite thin films at 4000 rpm for 20 s. The spiro-OMeTAD is doped with the TBP, tris(2-(1H-pyrazol-1-yl)-4-tert-butylpyridine)-cobalt(III)tris(bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide) (FK209), and Li-TFSI. The molar ratio of additives for spiro-OMeTAD is 3.30, 0.03, and 0.50 for TBP, FK209, and Li-TFSI, respectively. In the last step, the top gold electrode of 80–100 nm is thermally evaporated in high vacuum.

Characterization. The morphologies of samples are characterized by SEM (S-4800). Statistical grain size of samples are calculated by Nano Measurer (Fudan). Absorption spectra are executed with an UV–vis spectrometer (U-4100). XRD patterns are collected with a SmartLab (9 kW). Stable-state PL spectra are performed using the standard 450 W xenon continuous wave lamp and 470 nm laser as an excitation source, and the TRPL are recorded by the pulsed nitrogen/dye laser (QM400, Photo Technology International, USA). Current density–voltage (J–V) curves and stable power output are measured by CHI660E electrochemical workstation (Shanghai Chenhua Instrument, China) with ABET Technologies Sun 3000 solar simulator. The solar simulator is calibrated by the standard crystalline silicon solar cell. All devices are illuminated in AM 1.5G illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) with the active area of 0.101 cm  $  ^{2}  $  . EQE is used by an Enli Tech (Taiwan) EQE measurement system, and light intensity is calibrated by using a standard single crystal Si photovoltaic cell. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) is measured using the CHI660E electrochemical workstation (Shanghai Chenhua Instrument, China) at potentials of 0.85 V in AM 1.5G illumination at frequencies ranging from 0.1 Hz to 0.1 MHz.
