Tin hybrid perovskite solar cells (TPSCs) have emerged as the most promising candidate to the lead counterparts for the low toxicity and high theoretical efficiency. However, the easy  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  oxidation and related defects hinder further efficiency and stability improvement. Therefore, a facile Lewis base molecule of 4-amino-3-hydroxybenzoic acid (4A3HA) is employed in the perovskite precursor to coordinate with the under-coordinated  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $  ions and passivate the Sn defects. The effective suppression of the formation of Sn vacancy also has been verified from the density functional theory calculations. The 4A3HA modification significantly suppresses the trap-mediated recombination of Sn perovskite film. Accordingly, these benefits deliver a high power conversion efficiency (PCE) of  $ 13.43\% $  for TPSCs with notably improved on-shelf and thermal stability.

# 1. Introduction

Tin (Sn) hybrid perovskites act as the most promising lead-free alternative with a record power conversion efficiency (PCE) over 14% now, [1,2] though the PCE is still far below those of lead (Pb) hybrid perovskite solar cells with a current PCE record of 25.7% [3]. Compared to   $  Pb^{2+}  $  ,   $  Sn^{2+}  $   has a similar outer shell electron configuration with lower toxicity and superior semiconductor properties such as nearly ideal band gap (  $  \sim  $  1.3 eV) for single-junction photovoltaic devices, long diffusion length, small excitation energy, and high carrier mobility [4–8]. However, the structural stability of Sn perovskites lag significantly behind Pb analogs due to the low defect formation energies. The easy oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   and p-type self-doping characteristics result in severe non-radiative recombination and therefore causes poor device performance, especially the deficit in open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) [9,10].

To remedy these issues, from the initial attempt of   $  SnF_{2}  $   to compensate the   $  Sn^{2+}  $   stoichiometry, to the development of other   $  SnX_{2}  $   (such as   $  SnCl_{2}  $  ,   $  SnBr_{2}  $  ,   $  SnI_{2}  $  ) and then to tin(II) acetylacetonate that could suppress the self-oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $  , various approaches have been explored [11–15]. Meanwhile, the introduction of large organic cations

to form Dion-Jacobson (DJ) or Ruddlesden-Popper (RP) phases further retarded external   $  H_{2}O  $   or   $  O_{2}  $   ingression and suppressed the formation of Sn defects for reduced charge carrier non-radiative recombination [16, 17]. To overcome the   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation, some functional additives also can manipulate the perovskite thermodynamics and direct the orientation during the film formation.

Reducing agents such as hydrazine, hydrazinium tin iodide, and other Lewis-acid type additives have been reported to protect the   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation and suppress defects in Sn perovskites [18–21]. Thereinto, the reducing agents containing Lewis-base groups (e.g., N-donor, O-donor, S-donor, and P-donor) not only mitigate the formation of Sn vacancy (  $  V_{Sn}  $  ) in perovskites by coordinating with   $  Sn^{2+}  $   but also suppress the phase segregation of   $  SnX_{2}  $   [22–25]. Yan et al. introduced gallic acid (GA) to interact with   $  SnCl_{2}  $   by forming Lewis acid-base adduct and maintained 80% of the original PCE for 1000 h in ambient air [14]. The C=O group, owing to the two lone electron pairs, could coordinate with   $  Sn^{2+}  $   to passivate trap states and suppress the surface defects. Recently, Li et al. enabled the modulation of perovskite crystallization and obtained compact morphology through strong O…Sn chelation by adding n-butylammonium acetate [26]. The full elucidation and modulation of the defect passivation, as well as non-radiative recombination channel for Sn perovskite, are needed to address the bottleneck of efficiency and stability of TPSCs.

Here, 4-amino-3-hydroxybenzoic acid (4A3HA) was employed in the Sn perovskite precursor to modulate the defect and micro-strain. The C=O group acts as the additional Lewis-base source, which could reinforce the coordination with   $  Sn^{2+}  $   to reduce the   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation and self p-doping level defects. This approach was beneficial to increase the formation energy of Sn vacancy, as revealed by the density functional theory (DFT) calculations. # 3. Conclusion

In this work, we developed a novel Lewis base to alleviate the structural disorder and under-coordinated   $  Sn^{2+}  $   defects in the Sn perovskites. The reduced   $  V_{Sn}  $   defect by Lewis acid-base strategy rendered slow   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation and long-term structural stability of the Sn perovskites. We achieved a high PCE up to 13.43% in inverted TPSCs through substantially suppressed charge carrier non-radiative carrier recombination. Meanwhile, the target devices retained over 80% of the initial PCE for 1000 h after 85 °C thermal heating and showed almost no change of the initial PCE for 2000 h in   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere. Therefore, our work provides an effective protocol for approaching the theoretical   $  V_{oc}  $   limit in TPSCs through tin vacancy regulation.
