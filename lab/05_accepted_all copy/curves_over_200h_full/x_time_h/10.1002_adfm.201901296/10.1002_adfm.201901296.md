# Hole Transport Materials Based on 6,12-Dihydroindeno[1,2-b]fluorine with Different Periphery Groups: A New Strategy for Dopant-Free Perovskite Solar Cells

Fan Liu, Fei Wu, Zongxiao Tu, Qiuyan Liao, Yanbin Gong, Linna Zhu,* Qianqian Li,* and Zhen Li*

Although several hole-transporting materials (HTMs) have been designed to obtain perovskite solar cells (PSCs) devices with high performance, the dopant-free HTMs for efficient and stable PSCs remain rare. Herein, a rigid planar 6,12-dihydroindeno[1,2-b]fluorine (IDF) core with different numbers of bulky periphery groups to construct dopant-free HTMs of IDF-SFXPh, IDF-DiDPA, and IDF-TeDPA is modified. Thanks to the contributions of the planar IDF core and the twisted SFX periphery groups, the dopant-free IDF-SFXPh-based PSCs device achieves a device performance of  $ 17.6\% $ , comparable to the doped  $ 2,2',7,7' $ -tetrakis( $ N,N $ -di-p-methoxyphenylamine)- $ 9,9' $ -spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD)-based device ( $ 17.6\% $ ), with much enhanced device stability under glovebox and ambient conditions.

In the last nine years, as an important approach to solve the current energy crisis, organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted increasing attention and improved their power conversion efficiency (PCE) from 3 to  $ 23\% $ , with the optimization of material and device structure and fabrication conditions.[1-5] Typically, in PSCs, a bilayer of perovskite and hole-transporting material (HTM) is sandwiched between a titanium dioxide semiconductor/working electrode and a counter electrode, in which the efficient HTM can facilitate

For example, Qi and co-workers reported that Li-TFSI would form some pinholes with different sizes when it migrates to the top surface, and these pinholes could decrease the device stability.  $  ^{[11]}  $   Moreover, in 2016, Park and co-workers observed the rapid efficiency decrease of the spiro-OMeTAD device with additives after 400 h and even the vanishing after 900 h.  $  ^{[12]}  $   This vital drawback of spiro-OMeTAD has hindered its further commercial development, thus, the development of high-performance PSCs device based on HTMs without any additives is badly needed.

Clearly, without any additives, HTMs should exhibit suitable energy level, high hole mobility, and conductivity.  $  ^{[13-18]}  $   To this end, many different types of core structure, such as truxene, triazatruxene, anthanthrone, fluoranthene, diacenaphtho[1,2-b:1',2'-d]thiophene, and so on, have been extensively investigated in the dopant-free HTMs.  $  ^{[19-23]}  $   As demonstrated in Figure 1, the core structures of small organic dopant-free HTMs possess the polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon rings, which can improve the intermolecular   $  \pi-\pi  $   interactions and the corresponding hole mobility. Similarly, 6,12-dihydroindeno[1,2-b]fluorine (IDF) unit has a pentacyclic plane structure with two fluorene units sharing one benzene and has demonstrated strong intermolecular interactions and excellent mobilities.  $  ^{[24]}  $   Accordingly, IDF may be a suitable aromatic core to construct high-mobility dopant-free HTMs. To the best of our knowledge, however, this structure has never been incorporated into HTM and its application in PSCs has never been evaluated. Cyclic voltammetry was measured in dichloromethane to study the electrochemical properties of the new IDF-HTMs (Figure S16b, Supporting Information). The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels, versus   $  Fc/Fc^{+}  $  , were calculated to be -5.22, -5.16, and -5.07 eV for IDF-SFXPh, IDF-DiDPA, and IDF-TeDPA, respectively (Table 1). The downward shifted HOMO energy level of IDF-SFXPh relative to that of IDF-DiDPA can be associated with the weak electron donor SFX unit replacing methoxyphenyl for 4,4'-dimethoxy-diphenylamine group, moreover, the additional two electron-donating 4,4'-dimethoxydiphenylamine (IDF-TeDPA) can lead to a higher HOMO energy level. Under the same conditions, the HOMO energy level of IDF-SFXPh is slightly deeper than that of spiro-OMeTAD (-5.13 eV), however, the HOMO energy levels of these three IDF-HTMs are still positive compared to the valence band (VB) of the perovskite (-5.43 eV). The lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels of IDF-SFXPh, IDF-DiDPA, and IDF-TeDPA were estimated to be -2.43, -2.33, and -2.28 eV, respectively. On the whole, the energy levels of these IDF-HTMs matched well with VB and conduction band (CB, -3.93 eV) of perovskite, which can efficiently extract holes from the perovskite layer to the metal back contact and block the electron transfer to metal back contact.

Figure 3c displays the frontier molecular orbital distribution of IDF-HTMs and spiro-OMeTAD. The apparent difference can be found in frontier molecular orbital distribution among the IDF derivatives, and the trend of HOMO and LUMO matches the electrochemical data well. In detail, we divided the structures into the IDF core and terminal groups of two SFXPh units, two DPA units, four DPA units for IDF-SFXPh, IDF-DiDPA, and IDF-TeDPA, respectively (Tables S2–S4, Supporting Information). The HOMO of IDF-SFXPh is located on SFXPh moiety (59.80%), a relatively smaller contribution toward the IDF moiety (41.20%), whereas, HOMO-1 is mainly localized on SFXPh (76.80%) and less contribution on the IDF moiety (23.20%). LUMO has major contribution on the IDF moiety (69.08%), oppositely, LUMO+1 is mainly localized on SFXPh (93.90%). The distribution of HOMO and HOMO-1 of IDF-DiDPA is similar to those of IDF-SFXPh, however, the distributions of LUMO and LUMO+1 in DPA for IDF-DiDPA are 18.24 and 67.68%, respectively, lower than those of SFXPh for IDF-SFXPh. The distributions of HOMO and HOMO-1 of IDF-TeDPA are mainly localized on the DPA moiety (74.09 and 87.14%, respectively), while the distribution percentages of LUMO and LUMO+1 in DPA for IDF-TeDPA, with 16.18 and 40.05%, respectively, are the lowest ones among these three HTMs.

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) were carried out to measure the thermal properties of IDF-HTMs (Figure S16c,d and Table S5, Supporting Information). IDF-HTMs have the good thermal stability with the decomposition temperature  $ (T_{\mathrm{d}}) $  higher than

410 °C, especially, IDF-SFXPh exhibits the excellent thermal stability with   $  T_{d}  $   up to 438 °C. Heating from 30 to 320 °C and then cooling to 30 °C, the DSC curves of IDF-DiDPA and IDF-TeDPA indicate a mixture of crystalline and amorphous state, both of them show the crystallization temperatures (  $  T_{c}  $  ) of 87 °C for IDF-DiDPA and 118, 122 °C for IDF-TeDPA. Moreover, a relatively lower melting peak (  $  T_{m}  $  ) is observed at 229 °C for IDF-DiDPA, while interestingly, IDF-TeDPA has two kinds of   $  T_{m}  $ : a   $  T_{m}  $   of crystallites at 154 °C and another   $  T_{m}  $   of remaining amorphous state at 203 °C. Whereas, only one   $  T_{m}  $   is observed for IDF-SFXPh, as detected to be 279 °C. These results demonstrated that IDF with the relatively smaller DPA fragments have a stronger tendency to crystallization rather than the larger analogues with SFXPh fragments.

As mentioned above, the energy levels of IDF-HTMs are suitable to fabricate the PSC devices with the standard planar n-i-p device configuration of fluorine doped tin oxide (FTO)/  $  C_{60}  $  /perovskite (  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3-x}Cl_{x}  $  )/HTM/MoO  $  _{3}  $  /Ag, where   $  C_{60}  $   is used as a compact electron transporting layer and IDF derivatives are used as dopant-free HTMs. Also, the reference device was prepared with doped spiro-OMeTAD (with t-BP and LiTFSI) as HTM. All devices were fabricated by solution spin-coating processes and the detailed device fabrication procedures are presented in the Supporting Information. Besides the hole transporting, the hole extraction ability of the HTMs also plays an important role in the device performance. Steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) spectra of the perovskite films on different HTM substrates were measured (Figure 6a,b) to investigate the influence of IDF derivatives on the hole extraction in perovskite. Upon the addition of IDF-HTMs, an enhanced PL quenching effect is observed with the pristine perovskite film and the largest degree of PL quenching appeared for the perovskite with IDF-SFXPh, meaning that the perovskite/IDF-SFXPh bilayer exhibits the highest hole extraction ability for the corresponding best device performance. Moreover, the pristine perovskite film shows a longest-living decay  $ (\tau) $  and the perovskite with IDF-SFXPh has a quicker  $ \tau $  (8.85 ns) than those of other HTMs ( $ \tau = 11.83 $  and 14.03 ns for IDF-DiDPA and IDF-TeDPA, respectively), which implies more efficient hole extraction and can contribute to the higher  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  and FF for IDF-SFXPh. Thus, the results from both steady PL spectra and time-resolved PL spectra give a full explanation for the high performance of the IDF-SFXPh-based device. The perovskite layer without or with deposited onto the IDF-HTMs was also characterized by atomic force microscopy (AFM). The root mean squares (RMS) for perovskite/HTMs bilayers are 7.93, 5.25, and  $ 5.55\mathrm{nm} $  for IDF-SFXPh, IDF-DiDPA, and IDF-TeDPA, respectively, exhibiting the more smooth and uniform surface than that of pristine perovskite layer (RMS = 21.42 nm) (Figure 6c,d and Figure S21, Supporting Information).

Figure 7a and Figure S22 (Supporting Information) show the stabilized photocurrent and PCE at near the maximum power point within 200 s for IDF-HTMs-based devices. IDF-SFXPh has a stable PCE output of 17.2% and the photocurrent of 19.6 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , while the stable PCE outputs of 14.2 and 12.3% and the photocurrents of 17.7 and 17.1 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   for IDF-DiDPA and IDF-TeDPA, respectively. These results reveal the high reliability of the device J-V curves. The stability of nonencapsulation devices with dopant-free IDF-SFXPh and

reference doped spiro-OMeTAD was also investigated, with the corresponding PCE decay curves shown in Figure 7b and Figure S23 (Supporting Information). The IDF-SFXPh device without any additives exhibits the superior stability sustaining 92% of its original PCE after 21 d in   $  N_{2}  $  , while the doped spiro-OMeTAD device only retains 76% of its original PCE. Once being stored in ambient air under a relative humidity of 20–30%, the doped spiro-OMeTAD device decreased quickly and the PCE dropped to 58% after aging a week, however, the undoped IDF-SFXPh device can still obtain about 80% of its original PCE. Thus, the IDF-core dopant-free HTMs not only achieve a high device performance as good as the doped spiro-OMeTAD but also exhibit an excellent device stability.

# 3. Conclusions

In this work, we have developed and synthesized three new planar IDF-core molecules of IDF-SFXPh, IDF-DiDPA, and IDF-TeDPA as hole-transporting materials, in which different steric hindrance and numbers of periphery groups (SFXPh, DiDPA, and TeDPA) were introduced to the IDF core. As expected, the different periphery groups lead to many different properties, such as absorption and emission, thermally stability, the corresponding energy levels, and hole-transporting properties. IDF-SFXPh, containing 3D twisted SFX units, shows a much higher hole mobility (up to  $ 10^{-4}\mathrm{cm}^2\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1} $ ) than those of other two IDF-HTMs and spiro-OMeTAD as well as a remarkable PCE of  $ 17.6\% $  for dopant-free PSCs device with the excellent device stability, demonstrating the importance of the chemical modification. Our work demonstrates that the combination of twisted periphery groups with planar core units not only guarantees the intermolecular  $ \pi-\pi $  stacking but also effectively regulates the state of molecular aggregation, which can ensure higher hole mobility and device performance. Thus, this work might provide a new material design strategy for the efficient dopant-free HTMs with high performance.
