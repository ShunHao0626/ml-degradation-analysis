Abstract: Device instability has become an obstacle for the industrial application of organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite solar cells which has already demonstrated over 23% laboratory power conversion efficiency (PCE). It has been discovered that the sliding of A-site cations in the perovskite compound through and out of three dimensional   $  [PbI_{6}]^{4-}  $   crystal frame is one of the main reasons that are responsible for decomposition of the perovskites compound. Herein we report an effective method to enhance the stability of   $  FA_{0.79}MA_{0.16}Cs_{0.05}PbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}  $   perovskite film through incorporation of n-propylammonium iodide (PAI). Both density functional theory calculation and the X-ray diffraction patterns have confirmed the formation of two dimensional   $  (PA)_{2}PbI_{4}  $   with ruddlesden-popper perovskite as a result of reaction between PAI and   $  PbI_{2}  $   in the perovskite film. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy reveals less -COOH (carboxyl) groups on the surface of the perovskite film containing   $  (PA)_{2}PbI_{4}  $ , which indicates the suppressed penetration of oxygen and moisture to the perovskite material. This is further confirmed by surface water wettability test of the   $  (PA)_{2}PbI_{4}  $   film which exhibits excellent hydrophobic property with over 110° contact angle. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy demonstrates introduction of PAI additives resulted in upshift of the conduction band minimum of perovskite by 160 meV, leading to a more favourable energy alignment with adjacent electron transporting material. As a consequence, enhanced 17.23% PCE with suppressed hysteresis was obtained with the 5% PAI additive (molar ratio) in perovskite solar cell which retained nearly 50% of the initial efficiency after 2000 h in air without encapsulation under 45% average relative humidity.

Keywords: perovskite solar cells, device stability, 2D ruddlesden-popper perovskites, n-propylammonium iodide, aging against moisture

PSCs are considered as one of the most cost-effective and promising photovoltaic technologies in the future, owing to their outstanding photoelectric properties and simple solution-processed fabrication procedure. $ ^{8} $  However, the unsatisfactory stability of perovskite materials in the presence of

The perovskite compound adopts a chemical formula of  $ \mathrm{ABX}_3 $  where A = monovalent cation such as methylammonium ( $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $ ), formamidinum ( $ \mathrm{FA}^+ $ ) and  $ \mathrm{Cs}^{+} $ ; $ ^{13-15} $  B = metal cation such as  $ \mathrm{Pb}^{2+} $  and  $ \mathrm{Sn}^{2+} $ ; $ ^{16,17} $  X = halide anion such as  $ \mathrm{Cl^-} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Br^-} $  and  $ \mathrm{I^-} $ . $ ^{18-20} $  In this structure, the A-site cations that has small ionic radius is believed to be one of the main reasons that cause the material decomposition, thus device instability. A-site cations are located in the interstitial space of  $ [\mathrm{PbI}_6]^{4-} $  octahedral frame and has relatively smaller decomposition activation energy. $ ^{21-23} $  Therefore, changes of external environment such as temperature, humidity can easily trigger the vibration of the A-site cation that increases lattice defects and even leads to molecular dissociation. $ ^{24} $  So far, attempts have been made to address this issue. One of the methods is to substitute the A-site cations with relatively stable ions or ligands. $ ^{25-27} $  Recently, two dimensional (2D) ruddlesden-popper perovskites (RPPs) with a generic structural formula of  $ \mathrm{BA}_2\mathrm{MA}_{n-1}\mathrm{Pb}_n\mathrm{I}_{3n+1} $  (BA is n-butylammonium) have drawn attention of researchers because of their better material stability. Cao et al. firstly demonstrated the good stability of the 2D RPPs in PSCs although the initial PCE of  $ 4.02\% $  was poor with  $ \mathrm{BA}_2\mathrm{MA}_2\mathrm{Pb}_3\mathrm{I}_{10} $  based cells. $ ^{28} $  Tsai et al. optimized the material synthesis method for 2D  $ \mathrm{BA}_2\mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Pb}_4\mathrm{I}_{13} $  and obtained a near-single-crystalline material quality. $ ^{29} $   $ 12.5\% $  PCE was achieved owing to strongly preferential out-plane alignment to fulfil efficient charge transport. Most importantly, the devices showed impressive stability under light, humidity and heat stress test.  $ 90\% $  efficiency retention was reported with the encapsulated  $ \mathrm{BA}^+ $  based solar cells under  $ 65\% $  relative humidity after 2250 h. Furthermore, Zhang et al. incorporated  $ \mathrm{Cs}^+ $  to  $ \mathrm{BA}_2\mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{Pb}_4\mathrm{I}_{13} $  and achieved  $ 13.7\% $  PCE with outstanding humidity resistance. $ ^{30} $  The optimized devices could remain  $ 89\% $  of initial PCE without encapsulation under  $ 30\% $  humidity after  $ 1400\mathrm{h} $ . Meanwhile, Wang et al. found  $ \mathrm{BA}^+ $ -doped Cs-FA based PSCs retained  $ 80\% $  of initial average efficiency ( $ 18.1\% $ ) after  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  in air and  $ 4000\mathrm{h} $  when the device was encapsulated. $ ^{31} $  Besides  $ \mathrm{BA}^+ $ , other aliphatic or aromatic ammonium groups have also been introduced to form a hydrophobic layer on top of perovskites in order to hinder penetration of water to the perovskite film. Smith et al. fabricated 2D-3D  $ (\mathrm{PEA})_2\mathrm{MA}_2\mathrm{Pb}_3\mathrm{I}_{10} $  ( $ \mathrm{PEA}^+ = \mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_5\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{H}_4\mathrm{NH}_3^+) $  mixed perovskites films which maintained stable crystal phase for 46 days in an ambient environment. $ ^{32} $  Based on this work, Chen et al. employed  $ (\mathrm{PEA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $  as a capping layer for  $ \mathrm{Cs}_{0.05}(\mathrm{FA}_{0.83}\mathrm{MA}_{0.17})\mathrm{Pb(I}_{0.83}\mathrm{Br}_{0.17)}_3 $  perovskite which achieved  $ 18.5\% $  PCE and retained nearly  $ 90\% $  efficiency after  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  in ambient conditions. $ ^{33} $  More recently,  $ (5-\mathrm{AVA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $  ( $ 5-\mathrm{AVA}=5-\text{ammoniumvaleric acid} $ ) was reported

Despite the efforts mentioned above, the efficiency of PSCs with 2D perovskite material is generally lower compared to PSCs using 3D only materials. According to the Goldschmist's equation, only cations or ligands that have suitable ionic radius to maintain perovskite crystal phase with tolerance factor in the range of 0.78-1.03 can form stable perovskite phase. $ ^{35} $  Thus, when doping perovskite with long-chain ligands such as BA and PEA, secondary phases such as (BA) $ _{2} $ PbI $ _{4} $  and (PEA) $ _{2} $ PbI $ _{4} $  which are photo-inactive and weaken the light harvesting of perovskite films will form in the perovskite. $ ^{36,37} $  Furthermore, the inhibition of out-plane charge transport of 2D perovskite induced by the large ionic radius of BA or PEA acts like insulating layers which reduces device efficiency. $ ^{38} $  To solve these problems, controlling the crystal growth orientation of BA $ ^{+} $  based RPPs by using a hot-cast method was reported to improve device performance. $ ^{39} $  Nevertheless unexpected defects or cracks on the film surface are often observed during the hot cast process which is probably due to different crystallization rate between BA $ ^{+} $  and MA $ ^{+} $  components under high temperature ( $ \sim $ 150°C). The complexity of controlling the crystallization process of 2D-3D perovskite materials makes it difficult to repeat the result. Furthermore, regarding to the interfacial engineering of PSCs via 2D RPPs intercalation, it is still an open question regarding the location of the 2D RPPs. The lack of clear evidence that demonstrates the 2D RPPs form an intermediate layer with satisfactory morphology gives rise to the speculation that PEA-based 2D RPPs compounds may grow at grain boundaries of bulk 3D perovskites. $ ^{40} $  This is because in situ formation of the 2D RPPs relies on reaction of PEAI with excess PbI $ _{2} $  which often exists at grain boundaries in the perovskite film. Recently, a novel 2D RPP was synthesized by using the shorter-chain n-propylamine (PA) to form PA $ _{2} $ MA $ _{4} $ Pb $ _{5} $ I $ _{16} $ . Although solvent engineering was used to optimize the material properties, the solar cells based on this material showed efficiency of merely 10.4% due to the low fill factor. $ ^{41} $  Furthermore the large bandgap (1.85 eV) of this material limits the conversion efficiency of single phase PA $ _{2} $ MA $ _{4} $ Pb $ _{5} $ I $ _{16} $  based solar cells. Ahmad et al. reported Dion-Jacobson 2D organometal halide perovskite phase by incorporation of 1,3-propanediamine (PDA) in MAPbI $ _{3} $  to form (PDA)(MA) $ _{3} $ Pb $ _{4} $ I $ _{13} $ . The related device shows better efficiency of 13.3% and excellent stability owing to elimination of van der Waals gap in 2D perovskite framework. $ ^{42} $  Nevertheless, compared with conventional 3D perovskite based

Herein, we report a 2D-3D mixed PSC using n-propylammonium iodide (PAI) as additive in  $ \mathrm{FA}_{0.79}\mathrm{MA}_{0.16}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.05}\mathrm{PbI}_{2.5}\mathrm{Br}_{0.5} $  perovskite films. The perovskite film was fabricated via a simple one-step method without hot-cast treatment (synthesis details in supporting information). We have found that the shorter-alkyl-chain of PAI gradually forms a capping layer through reaction with  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  with ruddlesden-popper crystal structure of  $ (\mathrm{PA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $  on top of the perovskite film with the increase of doping concentration. The hydrophobic  $ (\mathrm{PA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $  showed large water contact angle of  $ 115^{\circ} $  which dramatically enhanced the stability of the perovskite against moisture. As a result, optimum  $ 17.23\% $  PCE with suppressed hysteresis was obtained in our unencapsulated solar cells which retained nearly  $ 50\% $  of initial efficiency under  $ 45\% $  average relative humidity after  $ 2000\mathrm{h} $ .

# Results and discussion

Figure 1a shows the X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern of the synthesized   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2x}\mathrm{FA}_{0.79}\mathrm{MA}_{0.16}\mathrm{Cs}_{0.05}\mathrm{Pb}_{1+x}\mathrm{I}_{2.5+4x}\mathrm{Br}_{0.5}  $   (2x = 0, 0.025, 0.05, 0.075, 0.1) mixed perovskite films on FTO substrates. The characteristic peaks at around 14.1°, 28.5° and 31.8° are consistent with typical (101), (202) and (211) lattice planes of   $  \alpha  $  -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   reported before.  $  ^{43}  $   Meanwhile, no observable   $  \delta  $  -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (yellow phase) peak at around 11.6° indicates high phase purity of the perovskite film which is attributed to the incorporation of Cs  $  ^{+}  $  that can effectively suppress the presence of the photo-inactive   $  \delta  $  -FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   phase. Although no new peak is observed with the perovskite with PAI additive, a clear shift of all of FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   peaks representing (101), (202), (211) lattice planes towards lower angles (Figure 1b-d) by over 0.08° (much more than resolution of 0.02° applied step size) is noted with increase of the amount of PA  $  ^{+}  $  from 0% to 10%. This demonstrates the enlargement of the d-spacing and lattice distortion of perovskite crystals. However, whether this peak shift is due to PA  $  ^{+}  $  incorporation into perovskite lattice or overlapping of PA based material characteristic patterns is still unresolved.

To gain further insight into this aspect, we measured XRD patterns of the two possible compounds formed by interaction of PAI and  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . One is  $ (\mathrm{PA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $  and the other is  $ \mathrm{PAPbI}_3 $  compound which were made by mixing stoichiometric ratio of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and PAI (molar ratio of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2:\mathrm{PAI} = 1:2 $  for  $ (\mathrm{PA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $  and 1:1 for  $ \mathrm{PAPbI}_3 $ ) (Figure S1). Where   $  R_{A}  $  ,   $  R_{B}  $   and   $  R_{X}  $   are the radii of PA, Pb, I ions with values of   $  R_{PA}=3.70\ \AA  $  ,   $  R_{Pb}=1.02\ \AA  $   and   $  R_{I}=2.20\ \AA  $  . We further employed density functional theory (DFT) to determine the structure configuration of possible PA based compounds. In this case, to focus on the coordination between A-site ion and   $  [BX_{m}]_{n}  $   ligand, two independent crystal forms of PAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (ABX  $  _{3}  $   perovskite, Figure S2a) and   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{4}  $   (AX·nABX  $  _{3}  $   ruddlesden-popper, Figure 2) and two composite structures including PAFA  $  _{7}  $  Pb  $  _{4}  $  I  $  _{24}  $   (PA replacing FA, Figure S2b), PAFA  $  _{8}  $  Pb  $  _{4}  $  I  $  _{24}  $   (interstitial PA, Figure S2c) are considered in the calculation. Comparison of the formation energy of these four models (methodology seen in supporting information) shows only PAPbI  $  _{3}  $   (perovskite, formation energy -5.40 eV) and   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{4}  $   (ruddlesden-popper, -7.17 eV) may exist because they both have negative energy of formation. The large positive energy of formation values for the PAFA  $  _{7}  $  Pb  $  _{4}  $  I  $  _{24}  $   (replacing, 8.67 eV) and PAFA  $  _{8}  $  Pb  $  _{4}  $  I  $  _{24}  $   (interstitial, 4.95 eV) indicate that they are thermodynamically less probable. This result suggests PA is preferential to form an independent compound instead of incorporation into the FAPbI  $  _{3}  $   lattice. The thermal stability of PAPbI  $  _{3}  $   and   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{4}  $   were then evaluated via Ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations with canonical ensemble. Both   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{4}  $   and PAPbI  $  _{3}  $   were optimized from their crystal structures with the lowest energy of formation.  $  ^{45}  $   It is found that the model of PAPbI  $  _{3}  $   collapsed within 1.5 ps at 500 K temperature, (Figure S2d), while   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{4}  $   maintained the initial crystal structure for 10 ps (Figure S2e). Clearly, the lower energy of formation and apparent thermal stability of   $  (\mathrm{PA})_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{4}  $   indicate that it is the most favourable product when PbI  $  _{2}  $   reacts with PAI. This is consistent with the observed XRD results and the tolerance factor calculation above.

To further reveal the effect of PAI additive on interfacial charge carrier transport and film surface recombination, Impedance spectroscopy (IS) of the devices under different light illumination intensities was measured (Figure 10a-e). It is found that there are two semi-circles in all the Nyquist plots.  $  R_{2}  $  of the first semi-circle at high frequency represents charge transport resistance in bulk perovskites, while  $  C_g  $  is dielectric capacitance of perovskite layer.  $  R_I  $  of the second semi-circle at low frequency serves as resistance at perovskite/electrode interfaces while  $  C_s  $  corresponds to interfacial charge accumulation capacitance. In this equivalent circuit, surface recombination in the PSCs is mainly determined by  $  R_{2}  $ . $  ^{52,53}  $  As shown in Figure 10f, the related  $  R_{2}  $  shows a decreasing trend with the enhancement of light illumination. Though the trends of  $  R_{2}  $  under open circuit voltage are similar in both the cells, compared with the ref-0% PA $  ^{+}  $ cell, a larger value with 5% PA $  ^{+}  $ is obtained at the same illumination intensities. This result suggests charge extraction process is more efficient in the device in the PAI-contained perovskite films which is in agreement with the improved efficiency in J-V plots. On the other hand, it is worth to note that  $  C_s  $  increases linearly with the enhancement of illumination intensities and open-circuit voltage (Figure 10g). Zarazua et al. report that photo-generated holes build up the measured photovoltage upon light illumination which can accumulate at interfaces. $  ^{53}  $  Hence increased  $  C_s  $  in the 5% PA $  ^{+}  $ -doped device can be associated with the enhanced  $  V_{oc}  $  and FF.

The ideality factor of the solar cells, which is indicatively dominated by trap-related recombination, is evaluated from the plots of the interfacial charge accumulation capacitance   $  (C_{s})  $   according to the below equation.  $  ^{54}  $

$$
C = C _ {0} e ^ {\frac {q V}{m _ {c} k _ {B} T}}
$$

The fitted curves show that the ideality factor   $  (m_{c})  $   of the 5% PA  $  ^{+}  $  -doped cell is 1.58 which is smaller than that of the ref-0% PAI sample (1.79). The reduced value indicates higher photovoltage at interface and less trap-associated charge recombination in the device with the incorporation of PAI.

# Conclusions

We have demonstrated introduction of a suitable amount of PAI additives into  $ \mathrm{Cs_{0.05}MA_{0.16}FA_{0.79}PbI_{2.5}Br_{0.5}} $  perovskite film effectively forms 2D  $ (\mathrm{PA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $  capping layer with ruddlesden-popper crystal structure. Owing to the relatively long and immobile alkyl chain, the 2D material can significantly change the wettability of the film surface to be hydrophobic against the ambient moisture. Further investigation indicates the  $ (\mathrm{PA})_2\mathrm{PbI}_4 $  capping layer also led to an improved energy alignment between  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and perovskite layers as a result of upshifting the conduction band minimum of the perovskite materials, which benefits the interfacial charge transfer between the perovskite film and the charge extraction layer. In addition, the PAI additive also reduce recombination and trap states in the perovskite film. As a result, the performance of the PSCs improves from  $ 16.86\% $  for  $ 0\% $  PA to  $ 17.23\% $  for  $ 5\% $  PAI with negligible hysteresis.
