# A copper coordination polymer with matching energy level for modifying hole transport layers to improve the performance of perovskite solar cells

Lele Qiu, $ ^{[a]} $  Xubin Zheng, $ ^{[a]} $  Yulin Yang, $ ^{*[a]} $  Yayu Dong, $ ^{[a]} $  Guohua Dong, $ ^{[b]} $  Debin Xia, $ ^{[a]} $  Xianda Liu, $ ^{[a]} $  Qingyin Wu, $ ^{*[c]} $  and Ruiqing Fan $ ^{*[a]} $

Abstract: Spiro-OMeTAD is currently the most widely used hole transport material for the preparation of high performance perovskite solar cells (PSCs), usually requiring the addition of additives to achieve the desired electrical conductivity. However, the quality of the film is degraded due to the addition of additives. Obvious holes and defects can be observed and the dispersion of the additives are uneven inside. Here, a copper coordination polymer Cu-bix with matching energy level and fluorescent properties was screened for use as an additional additive to dope Spiro-OMeTAD. The doping of Cu-bix effectively improved the dispersion state of the additives in hole transport layers and alleviated the aggregation of Li-TFSI or/and lithium salt complexes in the film. Thanks to better dispersion of the additives, Spiro-OMeTAD was more fully and uniformly oxidized, while the possibility of recombination was reduced in the devices. Furthermore, the flat and tightly bonded film layer obtained by optimization of the doping amount can efficiently transfer holes from the perovskite layers to the hole transport layers. The possible interaction mechanisms between additives and the copper coordination polymer are proposed and discussed. The resulted PCE for Cu-bix doped PSCs was improved from  $ 16.52\% $  to  $ 18.47\% $  compared with the pristine devices and this type of PSCs also showed a long stability in air due to the increased hydrophobicity of the Cu-bix based hole transport layers.

# Introduction

Since organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite compounds had been made an attempt to use in the preparation of dye-sensitized solar cells by sensitizing mesoporous titanium dioxide in 2006,  $  ^{[1]}  $  the photovoltaic technology and industry based on perovskite materials have made a leap forward. Due to the relatively low preparation cost and simple fabrication process,

perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted enormous attention and achieved certified PCE exceeding 23%.  $  ^{[2-4]}  $   Typically, high efficient PSCs have a sandwich structure, and the perovskite light absorbing layer is sandwiched between an electron transport layer (ETL) and a hole transport layer (HTL).  $  ^{[1,5]}  $   Obviously, in the development of PSCs, the introduction of 2,2',7,7' -Tetrakis(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) is of epoch-making significance, which solves the problem of rapid decomposition of the cells due to the liquid electrolyte.  $  ^{[6]}  $   Nevertheless, Spiro-OMeTAD suffers from a relatively low conductivity and low hole mobility in its pristine form, which is incompatible with the high efficiency of the PSCs. Therefore a variety of organic or inorganic hole transport materials (HTMs) have been continuously developed and explored, but Spiro-OMeTAD is till now the dominant HTM for the high efficient devices because of its desirable advantages, such as reasonable solubility, good film-forming ability, proper energy levels and appropriate ionization potential.  $  ^{[7-11]}  $   Besides, large varieties of additives have been utilized to overcome the disadvantages of Spiro-OMeTAD.  $  ^{[12-15]}  $

Among the numerous additives available reported in previous works, lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulphonyl)imide (Li-TFSI) is the most prevalent one, which is usually applied as an effective additive in combination with the 4-tert-butylpyridine (TBP). Actually, the above-mentioned doping process still require another oxidative process in air because Li-TFSI merely plays a promotion role for the oxidation between Spiro-OMeTAD and oxygen, together with the generation of lithium oxides.  $  ^{[12,16,17]}  $   However, Li-TFSI must be dissolved in acetonitrile before use since it is insoluble in chlorobenzene. Although the phase separation between the Li-TFSI/acetonitrile solution and the Spiro-OMeTAD/chlorobenzene solution can be improved by adding TBP, a portion of Li-TFSI or/and lithium salt complexes still re-aggregate and re-form defects during device preparation and operation.  $  ^{[13,18-20]}  $   Apart from this, the Li-TFSI also shows strong hygroscopicity. To our knowledge, both of them are detrimental to the photovoltaic performance and long-term stability of the resulting devices.  $  ^{[21]}  $   Hence, for enhancing the efficiency and stability of PSCs, it is urgent to improve the dispersion state of Li-TFSI or/and lithium salt complexes throughout the HTLs and increase the hydrophobicity of the films. In addition, the oxidation of Spiro-OMeTAD simultaneously requires the presence of Li-TFSI and sufficient oxygen exposure. It can be considered that the better dispersion of Li-TFSI can lead to more uniform oxidation of the Spiro-OMeTAD. Recently, in the development of new materials with excellent optoelectronic properties, coordination polymers have shown a growing and promising tendency. By modulating the central metal coordination atoms, they display more molecular structure selectivity, better environmental stability, excellent fluorescence and adsorption properties and altered electronic properties compared with the organic compounds.[26] Copper is abundant in nature, cheap, and has an excellent coordination environment. Meanwhile, copper complexes have been successfully used in devices to improve the performance of PSCs.[27-31] All of these offer a possibility for the application of copper-based coordination polymers in solar cells. Herein, the one-dimensional coordination polymer named as Cu-bix  $ \left(\left[\mathrm{Cu}_{4}\mathrm{l}_{4}(\mathrm{bix})\right]_{\mathrm{n}},\mathrm{bix}=1,3-\right. $  bis(imidazol-1-yl-methyl)-benzene) were synthesized by simple hydrothermal-solvothermal method and for the first time applied as a new additive of Spiro-OMeTAD, which solves the problem of additives aggregation and promotes the oxidation of Spiro-OMeTAD. Due to the matching energy level and excellent fluorescence property of Cu-bix, we have successfully increased the utilization of light and restrained the recombination of the PSCs. Accordingly, an enhanced photovoltaic performance with an average PCE of  $ 17.62\% $  and a peak value of  $ 18.48\% $  can be demonstrated for the Cu-bix doped PSCs. In addition, the introduction of Cu-bix can effectively alleviate the deliquescence problem of the devices and improve the stability of the devices in air due to the hydrophobicity of Cu-bix.

# Results and Discussion

The coordination polymer Cu-bix was prepared by hydrothermal and solvothermal method  $  ^{[32]}  $  (see Experimental Section). Figure 1 shows the chemical formula structure and crystal structure of the as-prepared Cu-bix determined by performing the single crystal X-ray diffraction measurement. It can be seen that the crystal skeleton of Cu-bix is a one-dimensional chain structure, which is primarily constructed by coordination between copper atoms and nitrogen atoms of ligands. In this structure, a copper atom coordinates with three iodine atoms and one nitrogen atom of the ligand. Four copper atoms coordinate with four iodine atoms to form a skeleton structure and meanwhile the skeleton subunits are connected by coordination of copper atoms with nitrogen atoms in ligands. Bond valence sum calculations indicate that copper atoms are in the oxidation states of +1. These one-dimensional chains form a three-dimensional supramolecular structure through  $  \pi  $ - $  \pi  $  interactions between the imidazole rings of adjacent chains (Figure S1).

To illustrate the possible interactions between Cu-bix and hole transport materials, the UV-vis absorption spectroscopy, X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS) and the Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR) were conducted. When the colorless Cubix chlorobenzene solution was mixed with the pale yellow Spiro-OMeTAD solution, the solution turned light red. After the Li-TFSI was added, the solution instantly turned dark red (Figure 3a). This color change in the solutions means the charge transfer between the additives and Spiro-OMeTAD.[33] The UV-vis absorption spectra shown in Figure 3a better illustrates this phenomenon. The appearance of a distinct absorption peak at  $ 525\mathrm{nm} $  indicates the presence of singly oxidized Spiro-OMeTAD when the Li-TFSI and Cu-bix are simultaneously added to the Spiro-OMeTAD solution.[28,34] Figure S2a gives the UV-vis absorption spectra of Li-TFSI doped Spiro-OMeTAD solution with different amounts of Cu-bix. Obviously, as the amount of Cu-bix increases, the oxidation peak becomes stronger, indicating the effectiveness of Cu-bix for Spiro-OMeTAD oxidation. The conductivity of the films with different amount of Cu-bix were also investigated (Figure S2b) and as the amount of addition increases, the conductivity shows a consistent trend. The influence of Cu-bix on the hole mobility of Spiro-OMeTAD was investigated through space-charge-limited current (SCLC) based on hole-only devices with the structure of

ITO/PEDOT:PSS/HTL/MoO $ _3 $ /Ag. $ ^{[35,36]} $  The hole mobility of Spiro-OMeTAD with and without Cu-bix (Figure S3) were calculated to be  $ 4.6 \times 10^{-4} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  S $ ^{-1} $  and  $ 3.0 \times 10^{-4} $  cm $ ^2 $  V $ ^{-1} $  S $ ^{-1} $ , respectively, according to the Mott-Gurney law. In order to verify the appearance of Spiro-OMeTAD oxidation state, we conducted XPS measurements on Spiro-OMeTAD and Spiro-OMeTAD films with different additives. As shown in Figure S4, when Cubix was added to Spiro-OMeTAD, no significant difference was observed compared with the XPS spectrum of Spiro-OMeTAD. However, when Li-TFSI was added to Spiro-OMeTAD, large amounts of elements related to Li-TFSI were detected due to the phase separation between the Li-TFSI/acetonitrile solution and the Spiro-OMeTAD/chlorobenzene solution. On the other hand, the XPS full range spectrum of Spiro-OMeTAD film with Cu-bix and Li-TFSI is similar to that of Spiro-OMeTAD, and only a small amount of fluorine and sulfur elements were detected, indicating that the addition of Cu-bix is beneficial to the dispersion of Li-TFSI. The detected C, O, N in the XPS full range spectra is mainly from Spiro-OMeTAD. As shown in Figure 3b, the maximum peak of the C 1s core level for Cu-bix and Li-TFSI codoped Spiro-OMeTAD film (284.6 eV) is shifted toward lower binding energy compared with the pristine Spiro-OMeTAD film (285.2 eV). This change in core level position of C 1s corresponds to the shift of the Fermi level toward the HOMO in Spiro-OMeTAD, which is often observed during p-type doping. $ ^{[24,37]} $  In other words, the shift of C 1s maximum peak position to lower binding energy suggests that Spiro-OMeTAD molecules are oxidized. Furthermore, the maximum peaks of the C 1s core level for Spiro-OMeTAD films only with Cu-bix or Li-TFSI do not change significantly compared to that of Spiro-OMeTAD. This shift can also be observed for the XPS of N 1s core level (Figure 3c). These observations confirm that Spiro-OMeTAD molecules are oxidized with assistance of Cu-bix and Li-TFSI. To illuminate the role of Cu-bix, the Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR) was utilized to analyze the possible interactions between Cu-bix and Li-TFSI. As shown in Figure 3d, an absorption peak at 1280.9 cm $ ^{-1} $  for Cu-bix is attributed to the stretching vibration of C-N bond. $ ^{[38]} $  It can be known from the structure of Cu-bix that there is a lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom, and when the Li-TFSI is mixed therewith, the shift of the peak of C-N bond from 1280.9 cm $ ^{-1} $  to 1256.3 cm $ ^{-1} $  can be observed. This change in the FT-IR signal can be thought of the interaction between the metal ion and the nitrogen atom. $ ^{[39]} $  According to experimental observations, the possible chemical reactions are as follows:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \text {Cu - bix + Spiro - OMeTAD \rightleftharpoons Cu - bix^{-} + Spiro - OMeTAD^{+}} \\ \text {Cu - bix^{-} + Spiro - OMeTAD^{+} + Li - TFSI = Cu - bix - Li + Spiro - } \\ \text {OMeTAD^{+} TFSI^{-}} \end{array}
$$

In order to further confirm the performance of Cu-bix in hole transport, it was used as a separate additive for Spiro-OMeTAD. The prepared n-i-p type   $  TiO_{2}  $  -based PSCs achieved an efficiency of 11.61% (Figure S5), which was higher than 7.32% for the device without additives. This proved the superiority and reliability of Cu-bix in hole transport.

Based on the previous research, we constructed planar PSCs with a typical architecture of FTO/TiO $ _2 $ /Cs $ _{0.05} $ FA $ _{0.81} $ MA $ _{0.14} $ PbI $ _{2.55} $ Br $ _{0.45} $ /HTL/Au in which the Cubix was used as an additive of Spiro-OMeTAD based HTL. As can be easily observed, the HOMO of Cu-bix is matching with that of perovskite materials and Spiro-OMeTAD, which leads to a better transport of the photogenerated holes from the perovskite layer to Spiro-OMeTAD and meanwhile alleviates hole accumulation at the interface. Figure 5b and S7 show the cross-sectional SEM of Cu-bix doped PSCs and the top-view SEM image of the perovskite film on TiO $ _2 $ , respectively. Obviously, we can clearly see that a thickness of  $ \sim $ 550 nm mixed cation-halide perovskite layer is sandwiched between the compact TiO $ _2 $  film and a  $ \sim $ 220 nm thick Cu-bix doped HTL. The formed perovskite film exhibits better morphology with smooth and pinhole-free large grains. Due to the stoichiometric ratio of the precursors, no apparent PbI $ _2 $  and impurity phases in the film were observed from the XRD pattern (Figure S8). The results of SEM and XRD confirm that the highly crystallized pure phase perovskite film was successfully prepared. Furthermore, it should be noted that the HTL and the perovskite layer are closely connected without voids and cracks, which also indicates that the introduction of Cu-bix does not affect the device structure. Statistical analysis of PCE for different amounts of Cu-bix doped PSCs shows that the proper

amount of Cu-bix modified HTL can greatly improve the efficiency of the cells, and it can be seen that the excess or less Cu-bix is interior (Figure S9). After optimizing the Cu-bix doping amount, the current density-voltage (J-V) curves of Cu-bix doped PSCs compared with the pristine PSCs were obtained (Figure 5c). Unless otherwise stated, the pristine PSCs refer to the reference devices fabricated using Li-TFSI and TBP co-doped Spiro-OMeTAD. As shown in the J-V curves, the Cu-bix doped PSCs gave a PCE of 18.47%, with an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.05 V, a short-circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 23.46 mA/cm², and a fill factor (FF) of 0.75 while the pristine PSCs showed a PCE of 16.52% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.02 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 23.01 mA/cm², and a FF of 0.70. It can be seen that due to the introduction of Cu-bix, the overall performance of the cells has been greatly improved, especially for the FF. As previously assumed, Cu-bix doped HTL can facilitate the extraction and transport of holes from the perovskite layer to Spiro-OMeTAD. The resulted decrease of interfacial recombination behaviors gives a good explanation for the improvement of FF and   $  V_{oc}  $  . Besides, the fluorescence properties of Cu-bix itself and the scattering of light are beneficial to enhance the management and utilization of light by the devices, as we reported previously.  $  ^{[42]}  $   Figure S10a gives the UV-vis diffuse reflectance spectra of the hole transport material with and without Cu-bix doping and it is clear that the Cu-bix doped HTL has a relative higher reflectance value in the long-wavelength region. This increase in reflectance can increase the use of light by perovskite films, and the improvement for the UV-vis absorption spectrum of the device with Cu-bix doping (Figure S10b) give the consistent result. This can be well reflected from the incident photon to electron conversion efficiency (IPCE) spectra of the PSCs with and without Cu-bix doping (Figure S11). The IPCE value for the Cu-bix doped PSCs in the long-wavelength region is improved and there is a slight decrease in the ultraviolet light region. The reduced interfacial recombination and the light scattering lead to better utilization for long-wavelength light.  $  ^{[43-45]}  $   The calculated integrated   $  J_{sc}  $   are 23.12 mA/cm² and 22.65 mA/cm² respectively, which approximates to the measured   $  J_{sc}  $  . The steady-state power output at the maximum power point for the PSCs with Cu-bix doping was obtained and the corresponding PCE is almost the same with the measured PCE, suggesting the accuracy of the measured results (Figure S13). # Conclusions

In conclusion, we have proposed a strategy to improve the overall performance of the hole transport layer by incorporating the one-dimensional coordination polymer Cu-bix to improve the photovoltaic performance and air stability of the devices. Cu-bix, as a new additive, was used as the dispersant for Li-TFSI or/and lithium salt complexes in the hole transport layer to solve the problem of lithium salt aggregation, promote uniform oxidation of Spiro-OMeTAD and reduce the recombination caused by excessive oxidation. Furthermore, the matching energy level of Cu-bix minimizes interfacial recombination while unique optical properties maximize solar utilization. Because of the doping of Cu-bix, the hole transport layers can better extract and transport holes, thereby increasing the PCE of the devices from 16.52% to 18.47%. Due to the interaction between Cu-bix and Li-TFSI, the aggregated Li-TFSI or/and lithium salt complexes on the surface of the hole transport layers is tremendously reduced, and the rapid decomposition of the devices caused by water absorption is alleviated. Benefiting from the inherent hydrophobicity of Cubix, device stability is further enhanced.

# Preparation of Cu-bix

The preparation of Cu-bix was according to the literature.  $  ^{[32]}  $   Typically, 0.4 mmol CuI was mixed with 0.2 mmol 1,3-bis(imidazole-1-yl-methyl)-benzene (bix), 6 mL concentrated KI solution and 2 mL CH  $  _{3}  $  OH. Then, they were sealed in a Teflon-lined autoclave (25 mL) and heated to 140 °C for 3 days. After cooling to room temperature, pale yellow crystals were obtained.

# Device Fabrication

FTO-coated glass (14 Ω per square, NSG, Japan) substrates were partially etched by zinc power and 4 M HCl, and then ultrasonically washed with deionized water, acetone and ethanol for 30 min, respectively. Then, substrates were treated with UV ozone for 15 min before devices preparation. The compact TiO₂ (c-TiO₂) was formed through spin-coating the precursor solution at 3000 rpm for 30 s and annealing at 500 °C for 30 min. The precursor solution was obtained by mixing 17.5 μL dilute HCl (3 M) and 175 μL titanium isopropoxide in 2.5 mL isopropanol. After cooling to room temperature, the perovskite films were prepared by spin-coating a perovskite precursor solution. The preparation was carried out according to the literature.[40] Briefly, The Cs₀.₀₅FA₀.₈₁MA₀.₁₄PbI₂.₅₅Br₀.₄₅ precursor solution (1.2 M) was prepared by mixing CsI, FAI, MABr, PbI₂ and PbBr₂, and then heated at 60 °C. The perovskite films were deposited in glovebox at 2000 rpm for 10 s with an acceleration of 200 rpm/s and 4000 rpm for 30 s with an acceleration of 1000 rpm/s. During the second step, 200 μL of chlorobenzene was dropped on the spinning substrates at 20 s before the end of the procedure. After heated at 100 °C for 15 min, the substrates were cooled down to room temperature. Then, the hole transport layer was deposited at 4000 rpm for 20 s using Spiro-OMeTAD solution. The solution was obtained by dissolving 72.3 mg Spiro-OMeTAD in 1 mL of chlorobenzene (CB) and mixing 17.5 μL Li-TFSI solution (520 mg/mL in acetonitrile), 28.8 μL TBP. Finally, 60-80 nm gold was deposited by thermal evaporation. The modified hole transport solution was prepared by dissolving Spiro-OMeTAD in CB solution containing Cu-bix and mixing Li-TFSI, TBP. The CB solution containing Cu-bix was obtained through a cell pulverizer for 2 h and then filtered through a 0.22 μm filter. Different concentrations of Cu-bix doping amount were obtained by dilution with CB and the amount for Cu 1-4, Cu 2-4, Cu 3-4, Cu 4-4 were 0.1mg/mL, 0.2 mg/mL, 0.3 mg/mL and 0.4 mg/mL.
