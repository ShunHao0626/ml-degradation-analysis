# Chiral Stereoisomer Engineering of Electron Transporting Materials for Efficient and Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Su-Kyo Jung, Jin Hyuck Heo, Byeong M. Oh, Jong Bum Lee, Sung-Ha Park, Woojin Yoon, Yunmi Song, Hoseop Yun, Jong H. Kim,  $  ^{*}  $   Sang Hyuk Im,  $  ^{*}  $   and O-Pil Kwon  $  ^{*}  $

A series of chiral stereoisomers of electron transporting materials with two chiral substituents is rationally designed and synthesized, and the influence of stereoisomerism on their physical and electronic properties is investigated to demonstrate highly efficient and stable perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Compared to mesomeric naphthalene diimide (NDI) derivatives, which have heterochiral side groups with centrosymmetric molecular packing of symmetric-shaped conformers in the crystalline state, enantiomeric NDI derivatives have homochiral side groups that exhibit non-centrosymmetric molecular packing of asymmetric-shaped conformers in the crystalline state and exhibit better solution processability based on one order of magnitude higher solubility. A similar trend is observed in different rylene diimide stereoisomers based on larger semiconducting core perylene diimide. The PSCs based on NDI enantiomers with good film-forming ability and a very high lowest phase transition temperature ( $ T_{\text{lowest}} $ ) of 321 °C exhibit a high and uniform average power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 19.067 ± 0.654%. These PSCs also have a high temporal device stability, with less than 10% degradation of the PCE at 100 °C for 1000 h without encapsulation. Therefore, chiral stereoisomer engineering of charge transporting materials is a potential approach to achieve high solution processability, excellent performance, and significant temporal stability in organic electronic devices.

Here, a series of chiral stereoisomers of organic electron transporting naphthalene diimide (NDI) derivatives with two chiral substituents is designed and synthesized (Figure 1). The influence of stereoisomerism of NDI derivatives on their physical and electronic properties is investigated to achieve PSCs with high power conversion efficiency (PCE), good solution processability and high temporal device stability. Compared to mesomeric NDI derivatives, which have heterochiral side groups, enantiomeric NDI derivatives have homochiral side groups that exhibit more asymmetrical crystal characteristics and have one order of magnitude higher solubility in various organic solvents. # 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Design of Chiral Electron Transporting Stereoisomers

Figure 1a,b shows the chemical structure of chiral organic electron transporting naphthalene diimide (NDI) derivatives with two chiral N-substituents; homochiral side groups (i.e., both R configurations) are denoted here as “(RR),” and heterochiral side groups (i.e., one R and the other S configuration) are denoted here as “(RS).” Recently, introducing homochiral (R)-1-phenylethyl (PhE) groups into NDI derivatives for NDI-PhE(RR) results in good solution processability and good film-forming ability, led to high PCE of PSCs.  $  ^{[6]}  $   Chiral groups including a carbon atom, that links to four substituents with different shapes and sizes, exhibit asymmetric characteristics. Therefore, chiral N-substituents, as a solubilizing groups, reduce molecular symmetry and increase void volume, which results in the improved solubility of the molecules.  $  ^{[6]}  $

On the other hand, introducing 1-indanyl (ID) groups which have simultaneous alicyclic and aromatic groups into NDI derivatives for NDI-ID(RS) results in high temporal device stability of PCE of PSCs.  $  ^{[13]}  $   In this work, to achieve high device performance (high PCE), excellent solution processability, and enhanced temporal device stability of PSCs by controlling stereoisomerism of N-substituted chiral side groups of NDI derivatives, we investigate six NDI-based stereoisomers with three side groups: ID, 1,2,3,4-tetrahydro-1-naphthyl (TN), and PhE groups. # 2.2. Influence of Stereoisomerism on the Physical and Electronic Properties

To investigate the effects of stereoisomerism of chiral NDI derivatives on their physical and electronic properties, we studied solution processability by taking solubility measurements in various solvents, phase transition behavior by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), and molecular conformation and crystal structure by X-ray single crystal structure analysis. In addition, space-charge-limited current (SCLC) electron mobility of NDI derivatives is measured using electron-only devices (EODs). The results are summarized in Table 1 and Figure 2.

The crystal characteristics and physical properties of NDI enantiomers are in sharp contrast to those of corresponding NDI mesomers. As listed in Table 1, enantiomeric NDI derivatives with homochiral  $ (RR) $  side groups exhibit non-centrosymmetric space group symmetry  $ (P2_{1}) $  in the crystalline state. In addition, the molecular conformation of all NDI enantiomers is asymmetric-shape; i.e., no mirror and symmetric plane of molecular conformation (Figure 3a). In contrast, mesomeric NDI

derivatives with heterochiral (RS) side groups in the crystalline state exhibit centrosymmetric space group symmetry   $  P2_{1}/n  $   and   $  P2_{1}/c  $   with symmetric-shaped molecular conformations (Figure 3b).

# 2.3. Good Solution Processability with High Phase Transition Temperature

As discussed above, the achievement of both solution processability and temporal device stability of organic semiconductors is challenging because the phase transition temperature of materials is inversely proportional to solubility. From the NDI derivatives investigated in this work, the general inverse proportionality between the first-order phase transition temperature and solubility was also observed. Highly soluble NDI enantiomers exhibit a lower melting temperature,   $  T_{m}  $   than corresponding NDI mesomers, as shown in Figure 2b.

However, the long-term temporal and (high-temperature) device stability of electronic devices is mainly correlated to the lowest phase transition temperature   $  T_{lowest}  $   rather than melting temperature   $  T_{m}  $  .[12] As listed in Table 1, five out of six NDI derivatives having either simultaneous alicyclic and aromatic side groups[13] (enantiomeric and mesomeric ID and TN series) and/or heterochiral mesomeric side groups (i.e., (RS) series) exhibit a very high   $  T_{lowest}  $   of 285 °C or higher. This result indicates that introducing homochiral side groups (i.e., (RR) series) that have simultaneous alicyclic and aromatic side groups or heterochiral side groups (i.e., (RS) series) is vital for achieving high temporal device stability.

Interestingly, among the enantiomeric NDI derivatives exhibiting high solubility, NDI-ID(RR) exhibits the highest   $  T_{\text{lowest}}  $   (321 °C), which is identical to the melting temperature, owing to the absence of a polymorphic phase transition, as shown in Figure 5a. Every 10 h, we measured J-V curves and plotted the maximum power point of each

device, as shown in Figure 7a. The perovskite solar cell with NDI-ID(RR) ETL exhibited better thermal stability than the device with NDI-ID(RS) ETL. The  $ J - V $  curves corresponding to the initial and final state are shown in Figure 7b,c, respectively, and photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table 2. The PSCs with NDI-ID(RR) ETL exhibited  $ 20.1\% $  PCE for the initial stage and  $ 18.2\% $  PCE for the final stage, and the device with NDI-ID(RS) ETL had  $ 19.6\% $  PCE for the initial stage and  $ 16.2\% $  for the final stage. Accordingly, the perovskite solar cell with NDI-ID(RR) ETL and NDI-ID(RS) ETL exhibited  $ 9.5\% $  and  $ 17.3\% $  of degradation for the  $ 1000\mathrm{h}^{\prime} $  thermal stability test.

The higher temporal device stability of PSC with NDI-ID(RR) ETL compared to that of NDI-ID(RS) ETL may be attributed to a higher   $  T_{\text{lowest}}  $   of NDI-ID(RR) (321 °C) than that of NDI-ID(RS) (294 °C) and a better surface coverage based on a solubility in DCB (1.44 and 0.11 g/100 g DCB) that is one order of magnitude higher than that in NDI-ID(RS). A higher   $  T_{\text{lowest}}  $   leads to a lower morphological change of ETL with low molecular migration. In addition, a high solubility with an excellent film formation property results in a more effective coverage of the perovskite layer. This covering protects the perovskite layer from oxygen and moisture diffusion and prevents the degradation of the perovskite layer. For comparison, the temporal stability of PCBM ETL-based PSC is also presented in Figure 7 and Table 2. In PCBM, phase transition is observed from 240 °C.  $  ^{[12]}  $   PSCs based on NDI-ID(RR) ETM with a higher   $  T_{\text{lowest}}  $   or solubility than PCBM and NDI-ID(RS) have an enhanced temporal stability.

# 2.5. Crystalline Characteristics of Chiral Semiconductors in Solution Process

The crystal characteristics of organic  $ \pi $ -conjugated materials can be varied with the solution process conditions due to polymorphism and the tendency to different thermodynamic phases. Two crystalline samples (samples A and B) were prepared to investigate the crystal characteristics of NDI-ID(RR) in PSCs. Sample A was prepared by recrystallization of NDI-ID(RR) in the DCB solution (see Figure S6a, Supporting Information). Figures S6b and S7 (Supporting Information) show the differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) thermodiagrams and powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) pattern of sample A, respectively. As shown in Figure S6b (Supporting Information), the weight loss near  $ 80^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  might be connected with the escaping DCB molecules in sample A crystals. The corresponding molar ratio between NDI-ID(RR) and DCB in sample A was NDI-ID(RR):DCB  $ \approx 1:1 $  (mol/mol). In addition, sample A optically shows high quality as single crystals (Figure S6a, Supporting Information) and exhibits high diffraction peaks of X-ray (Figure S7, Supporting Information). Consequently, sample A was NDI-ID(RR)·DCB cocrystals (NDI-ID(RR):DCB = 1:1 (mol/mol). Therefore, at the initial state of eliminating the solvent in the solution process for electronic devices, NDI-ID(RR) molecules form NDI-ID(RR)·DCB cocrystal-like phase in the presence of DCB solvent molecules.

To replicate the postdrying process state (i.e., after completely eliminating the solvent molecules from the solvent-included crystals), sample B was prepared by heating at 130 °C of sample A (NDI-ID(RR)·DCB cocrystals). As shown in Figure S7 (Supporting Information), the PXRD pattern of sample B shows good agreement with the crystal structure data of NDI-ID(RR) single crystals having triclinic space group P1 we reported. From the DSC thermodiagram in Figure S6 (Supporting Information), the melting temperature   $  T_{m}  $   of sample B (i.e., the region exceeding 150 °C in DSC of sample A after solvent-elimination) is identical to that of NDI-ID(RR) crystals (321 °C), as displayed in Figure 5a. In addition, the NDI-ID(RR) enantiomer showed a reduced tendency to form polymorphs because NDI-ID(RR) enantiomer do not exhibit thermal-induced polymorphism (Figure 5a). Consequently, NDI-ID(RR) homocrystals were stable in the thermodynamic phase in the absence of DCB solvent molecules, whereas NDI-ID(RR)·DCB cocrystals were stable in the thermodynamic phase in the presence of DCB solvent molecules. Therefore, after completely drying the DCB solvent in the solution process of electronic devices, NDI-ID(RR) layer might exhibit NDI-ID(RR) homocrystals-like structure.

As the crystal perfectness of the organic semiconductors can be varied with the solution process conditions, XRD and DSC measurements of NDI-ID(RR) on   $  (\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3})_{x}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})_{1-x}  $   layer were determined. Figure S8 (Supporting Information) shows DSC thermodiagrams of NDI-ID(RR)/(FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{x}  $  (MAPbBr  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{1-x}  $   powder obtained from NDI-ID(RR)/(FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{x}  $  (MAPbBr  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{1-x}  $  /glass film. As shown in Figure S8 (Supporting Information), the endothermic peak at 297 °C indicated by an arrow appears. This peak is attributed to the melting transition of NDI-ID(RR) crystals. Thus, NDI-ID(RR) on the perovskite layer is in a crystalline state. However, X-ray diffraction peak in NDI-ID(RR)/(FAPbI  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{x}  $  (MAPbBr  $  _{3}  $  )  $  _{1-x}  $  /glass film could not be observed. For another NDI derivative with achiral N-benzyl groups  $  ^{[12]}  $   on the perovskite layer, a clear diffraction peak was noticed. Therefore, the crystalline NDI-ID(RR) layer on the perovskite layer may possess low crystallinity owing to the low crystal perfectness. This shows a good agreement of the DSC experiments in Figure S8 (Supporting Information); the melt transition peak of NDI-ID(RR) on the perovskite layer is remarkably broader than NDI-ID(RR) crystals (Figure 5a). Note that the decreased melting temperature of NDI-ID(RR) on the perovskite layer (297 °C) compared to NDI-ID(RR) crystals (321 °C) may be attributed to the small crystalline NDI-ID(RR) thin-layer. Therefore, because the crystal characteristics of enantiomeric organic semiconductors were varied with the solution process conditions, and enantiomeric homocrystals were in stable thermodynamic form (in the case of NDI-ID(RR)), the controlling solution process could improve the crystal characteristics of the corresponding organic electronic devices.

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, the novel function of chiral stereoisomer engineering for organic n-type semiconducting enantiomers and mesomers with two chiral substituents for optimizing solubility, device performance, and temporal device stability is

demonstrated in this study. Controlling the chiral stereoisomerism of organic semiconductors is a promising approach for 1) achieving higher solution processability of enantiomeric semiconductors compared to mesomeric semiconductors and 2) achieving solution processability, device performance, and temporal device stability when highly soluble enantiomeric semiconductors having high enough lowest phase transition temperature are used in optoelectronic devices, including perovskite solar cells. Based on highly soluble NDI-ID(RR) enantiomers with very high lowest phase transition temperature (  $  T_{lowest} > 320\ ^{\circ}C  $  ), the PSCs exhibit good solution processability, high PCE and high temporal stability.
