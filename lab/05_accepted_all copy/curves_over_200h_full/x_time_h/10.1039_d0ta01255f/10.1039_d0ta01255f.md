# Thermally Stable Perovskite Solar Cells with Efficiency over  $ 21\% $  via Bifunctional Additive

Xiaoqiang Shi,  $  ^{1}  $   Yahan Wu,  $  ^{1}  $   Jieqiong Chen,  $  ^{1}  $   Molang Cai,  $  ^{*1}  $   Yi Yang,  $  ^{1}  $   Xuepeng Liu,  $  ^{1}  $   Ye Tao,  $  ^{1}  $   Mina Guli,  $  ^{1}  $   Yong Ding,  $  ^{1}  $   Songyuan Dai  $  ^{*1,2}  $

# Abstract

The rapid improvements in performance of organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells has been astonishing but its commercialized production requires further achievements on device stability and efficiency. Herein, we introduce a bifunctional additive, biuret, with multiple Lewis base groups to regulate the crystallization process of perovskite crystals and passivate the defects at grain boundaries. Compared with the control, methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI $ _3 $ ), films processed with biuret exhibit increased grain size, reduced trap states density, and more uniform local photoluminescence. The addition of biuret leads to suppressed trap-assisted nonradiative recombination and an efficiency improvement from 18.26% to 21.16%, which is among the highest efficiency for MAPbI $ _3 $  solar cells with the mesoscopic structure. Meanwhile, as biuret interacts with uncoordinated Pb $ ^{2+} $  and iodide from the iodoplumbate complex on two adjacent perovskite grains, the thermal durability of MAPbI $ _3 $  film is enhanced due to the crosslink through chemical bonding. Under 85°C annealing in nitrogen, the biuret-modified device preserves 94% of its initial efficiency after 12 days while the control cells lose more than half the efficiency.

# 1. Introduction

Solar cells based on organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite absorbers have received tremendous interest due to their cost-effective processing and high power conversion efficiency (PCE). Within the great efforts of the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) community, the PCEs of PSCs have been dramatically improved from the initial 3.8% to the presently certified 25.2% during the last decade.  $  ^{1,2}  $   However, the enhancement of photovoltaic performance and stability is still required for commercialization of PSCs.  $  ^{3-6}  $   It is widely recognized that a high density of charged ionic defects will be inevitably generated at the surface and grain boundaries of polycrystalline grains during the thermal annealing process, due to the low thermal stability and low formation energy of perovskite materials containing organic components.  $  ^{7,8}  $   The degradation of perovskite films has been reported to preferentially occur at grain boundaries owing to the trap-assisted carrier recombination.  $  ^{9-11}  $   Therefore, regulation of the perovskite crystallization behavior to increase the grain size and reduce the grain boundaries density is considered as an active strategy for enhancing the device efficiency and stability.

In this context, numerous strategies including solvent/anti-solvent engineering, composition engineering, and additive engineering have been developed.  $  ^{12, 13}  $   Among these approaches, the introduction of additives has made significant progress in this goal over the last few years  $  ^{14, 15}  $  . For instance, liquid additives such as dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO)  $  ^{16}  $   and N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP)  $  ^{17}  $   were reported to retard the perovskite crystallization rate thus enlarge the grain size and improve the film crystallinity. However, this kind of volatile additive easily escapes during the film formation process and cannot perform defect passivation and/or stability enhancement functions. Some additives can achieve stability enhancement by in situ forming a grain encapsulation layer, in which defect passivation was absent yet due to lack of functional groups that could effectively interact with charged defects.  $  ^{18, 19}  $   These defects can still reduce the photovoltaic performance and cause device instability issues, including ion migration and related hysteresis phenomenon, and device degradation in ambient air.  $  ^{7}  $   To

passivate these ionic defects, molecules with Lewis acid or base groups have been reported to perform as electron acceptors or donors that can interact with the charged defects, therefore eliminating the associated defect-induced charge traps.  $  ^{20-22}  $

Here, we introduce biuret, a compound with multiple Lewis base groups, as the perovskite precursor additive. Biuret acts as an O-donor to strongly coordinate with lead iodide (PbI $ _2 $ ) and form an intermediate phase, which further slows down the perovskite crystallization rate. The connection between adjacent perovskite crystals is enhanced by the crosslinking structure of biuret, which induces excellent thermal durability of MAPbI $ _3 $  film. Meanwhile, the residual biuret remaining in the final perovskite film efficiently passivates charged ionic defects. As a result, additive molecular modulation simultaneously increases the perovskite grain size and crystallinity as well as decreases the density of defects acting as centers for nonradiative charge carrier recombination. Accordingly, we achieve a PCE of over 21% for MAPbI $ _3 $  solar cells with almost constant efficiency under 85%°C for 12 days.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Morphology and Crystallization Behavior

As shown in Figure 1A, the biuret additive was added into the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  precursor solution, which was then spin-coated onto the mesoporous titanium dioxide  $ (\mathrm{TiO}_2) $  layer. As can be seen from scanning electron microscope (SEM), the biuret-modified perovskite films exhibit remarkably enlarged grains compared to the control sample while the surface morphology becomes rough with the amount of biuret exceeds  $ 2\mathrm{mol}\% $  (Figure 1B,C and Figures S1). The averaged grain size (Figure S2) was estimated to be about  $ 375~\mathrm{nm} $  for the pristine perovskite film and approximately  $ 729~\mathrm{nm} $  for the perovskite film with improved morphology at  $ 2\mathrm{mol}\% $  biuret. The increase of grain size is attributed to the formation of a methylammonium iodide  $ (\mathrm{MAI})\cdot \mathrm{PbI}_2\cdot \mathrm{DMSO}\cdot \mathrm{biuret} $  adduct, leading to the increased activation energy, which decreases the number of nucleations and slows down crystal growth.[22, 23] The XRD pattern of this adduct was shown in Figure S3. Retarding of crystallization rate was further observed from Figure S4 where the color variation of the biuret-modified perovskite film during the annealing process is much slower than the control film.

The influence of biuret on crystalline properties of perovskite films was investigated by X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurement (Figure 1D). The XRD patterns in Figure 1D show no additional peaks or peak shifts after the addition of biuret, indicating that the addition of biuret in the precursor does not influence the crystal structure. The main peaks located at   $  14.1^{\circ}  $   and   $  28.4^{\circ}  $   are corresponding to the (110) and (220) crystal planes of cubic perovskite phase, respectively.  $  ^{24}  $   The enhanced diffraction peak intensities and reduced full width half-maximum (FWHM) values of (110) plane indicated that the perovskite crystallization was enhanced after the addition of biuret (Figure 1E). According to the Scherrer equation (see Supporting Information for calculation details), we calculated the crystallite size with the FWHM of the (110) peak. At the optimum ratio of 2 mol% biuret, the crystallite size increased from 51.8 nm to 66.3 nm, which is consistent with the SEM results (Figure 1B,C). In addition, the ratio of the (110) peak intensity to the (222) peak intensity increased from 8.1 to 10.9 upon incorporation of 2 mol% biuret. This underlines that biuret plays an important role in the preferred orientation of perovskite crystals. The occurrence of the preferred orientation towards the (110) direction would be beneficial for enhanced charge transport.  $  ^{25}  $

The attenuated total reflection Fourier transform infrared (ATR-FTIR) measurements were conducted to investigate whether biuret remained in the perovskite film after the annealing process (Figure 2A). Compared to the pristine  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film, the biuret-modified  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film presents characteristic peaks around 3600 to  $ 3300~\mathrm{cm^{-1}} $  and 1800 to  $ 1600~\mathrm{cm^{-1}} $ , which were ascribed to the N-H stretch and  $ \mathrm{C = O} $  stretch of biuret, respectively, confirming the presence of biuret after film formation. The N-H stretching vibration peaks of the biuret were shifted in combination with  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $ , which indicates a chemical interaction of the ammonium group in biuret with the perovskite component (Figure S5). A magnified fingerprint region containing the characteristic peaks of the  $ \mathrm{C = O} $  bond is shown in Figure 2B. The peak for  $ \mathrm{C = O} $  stretching vibration of biuret was observed to shift from 1722 to  $ 1713~\mathrm{cm^{-1}} $  in the biuret-modified  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  film, which is related to the extent of electron delocalization in the N-C=O-N system. The coordination effect of oxygen atom is considered to decrease the double bond character of the  $ \mathrm{C = O} $  bonds and conduct a shift of the carbonyl stretching mode to lower frequencies.[26] This result indicates that there is interaction between the carboxyl groups of biuret and perovskite component. This interaction was further confirmed by the shift of the Pb 4f orbital in the X-ray

photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) of biuret-modified MAPbI $ _3 $  film (Figure 2C). Furthermore, an evident O 1s peak centered at about 532.7 eV was observed for biuret-modified MAPbI $ _3 $  film (Figure 2D). A negligible O 1s peak was also observed for the pristine MAPbI $ _3 $  sample, possibly a result of a small amount of oxygen adsorbed on the sample surface and/or diffused into the sample during sample preparation. $ ^{27} $  As for C 1s spectra of the control MAPbI $ _3 $  film (Figure 2E), a peak component at about 286.2 eV is assigned to the carbon from the methyl group in MAPbI $ _3 $ . These observations further confirm that biuret remained in the surface and grain boundaries of perovskite film and the passivation of Pb $ ^{2+} $  defects by the carbonyl groups.

Based on the above analyses, the main chemical reactions involved in the formation process of perovskite grains are proposed. As illustrated in Figure 3A, MAI,  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , and biuret are dissolved in the mixed solvent of dimethylformamide (DMF) and DMSO, then the light yellow and transparent precursor solution is spin-coated on the  $ \mathrm{TiO}_2 $  coated fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO)/glass substrate.  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  as a Lewis acid can form coordination bond with O-donor type of Lewis base (DMSO and biuret). After anti-solvent (chlorobenzene) treatment, the MAI· $ \mathrm{PbI}_2\cdot $ DMSO·biuret intermediate phase is obtained. DMSO that could help to obtain a high-quality perovskite film, will be thoroughly removed after annealing for a few minutes, but biuret still exists in the perovskite film at this time and in the following annealing process. The residual biuret can further promote the ripening process of the perovskite films, contributing to the formation of larger size grains. Moreover, the remnant biuret molecules in the films interacted with the perovskites through a coordinative covalent bond to passivate defects at the surface and grain boundaries.

# 2.2. Optical and Electronic Properties

To gain more insight into the optoelectronic properties of perovskite films before and after adding biuret, a series of optical and electrical measurements were conducted. Figure S6 shows the UV-vis absorption spectra of the perovskite films. The film with biuret additive shows stronger absorption than the control one because of the improved morphology and crystallinity (Figure 1). Then, we plotted the curves of the logarithmic absorption coefficient (  $  \alpha  $  ) as a function of the photon energy to investigate the Urbach energy (  $  E_{u}  $  ), as illustrated in Figure 4A. Generally, higher   $  E_{u}  $   correlates to more trap states (subgap tail states) in the perovskite films. Here, the   $  E_{u}  $   is derived from the Urbach tail near the optical band edge. In the lower photon energy region,   $  \alpha  $   follows the Urbach empirical rule by the equation of,  $  ^{32}  $

$$
\alpha = \alpha_ {0} \mathrm{exp} \left(\frac {\mathrm{h} \nu}{E _ {\mathrm{u}}}\right)
$$

(1)

where   $  \alpha_{0}  $   is a constant, h is Planck's constant, and v is the frequency of light. Therefore, the   $  E_{u}  $   can be estimated from the slope above by fitting and calculating from Urbach empirical equation. The relatively low   $  E_{u}  $   is indicative of a low degree of energetic disorder, leading to

reduced subgap tail states and thereby decreasing nonradiative recombination. We found a lower of  $  E_{\mathrm{u}}  $  values for the biuret-modified sample (28.5 meV) than that of the control sample (33.9 meV), which indicates the passivation of subgap tail states by biuret.

The trap states density of the perovskite films were quantitatively measured based on space-charge-limited current (SCLC) model by measuring the dark current-voltage   $  (J-V)  $   characteristics of the electron-only devices with a structure of FTO/TiO  $  _{2}  $  /perovskite/PCBM/Ag. Figure 4C,D shows the dark J-V curves of the corresponding samples, in which three regions can be distinctly observed. The current signal increases linearly with the voltage at the Ohmic regime, then shows dramatically rapid nonlinear growth in the trap-filled limited (TFL) regime, and eventually exhibits quadratic voltage dependence in the trap-free SCLC (Child) regime. The trap state density can be estimated from the trap-filled limit voltage   $  (V_{\mathrm{TFL}})  $   which is corresponding to the onset voltage of the TFL region, as shown in the following equation  $  ^{33}  $

$$
V _ {\mathrm{TFL}} = \frac {e n _ {\mathrm{t}} L ^ {2}}{2 \varepsilon_ {0} \varepsilon_ {\mathrm{r}}}
$$

(2)

where e is the unit charge   $  (1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C})  $  , L is the thickness of the perovskite layer,   $  \varepsilon_{0}  $   is the vacuum permittivity   $  (8.854 \times 10^{-12} \text{ F/m})  $  ,   $  \varepsilon_{r}  $   is the relative dielectric constant of the perovskite film,  $  ^{34}  $   and   $  n_{t}  $   is the trap state density. The   $  V_{TFL}  $   of the control and biuret-modified devices are evaluated to be 0.35 and 0.21 V, respectively. Therefore, the trap states density had declined from   $  7.7 \times 10^{15}  $   (control device) to   $  4.6 \times 10^{15}  $   cm  $  ^{-3}  $   (target device), consistent with the trend of   $  E_{u}  $   values. These results imply that the addition of biuret could effectively enhance the quality of the perovskite films by suppressing surface defects.

We then conducted photoluminescence (PL) measurements to investigate the influence of trap density reduction on perovskite emission properties. As shown in Figure 4B, the PL peak intensity of the biuret-modified perovskite film is more than doubled compared with the control film, indicating reduced nonradiative charge recombination of the biuret-modified perovskite film. Meanwhile, the biuret-modified film displayed a blue-shifted PL peak compared with that of the control one, which indicates that biuret can passivate the trap states near to the surface or along grain boundaries.  $  ^{21}  $   To fully understand the observed phenomenon, micro-PL mapping of these perovskite films were recorded. Figure 4E,F displays the mapping of the PL peak position over   $  100 \times 100 \mu m^{2}  $   regions. Compared with the control samples (Figure 4E), a much more

# 2.3. Photovoltaic Performance

The photovoltaic performance of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  PSCs was assessed with different amounts of biuret additive (Figure S7). The corresponding photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table S1. The amount of added biuret varied from  $ 0\mathrm{mol}\% $  to  $ 4\mathrm{mol}\% $  with respect to  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ . As the biuret additive increased from  $ 0\mathrm{mol}\% $  (control) to  $ 2\mathrm{mol}\% $ , the  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  and FF of the PSCs were gradually enhanced. With the addition of  $ 2\mathrm{mol}\% $  biuret, the average  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  and FF were improved from  $ 1.06 \pm 0.01\mathrm{V} $  to  $ 1.11 \pm 0.01\mathrm{V} $  and  $ 75.33 \pm 0.42\% $  to  $ 79.42 \pm 0.37\% $ . This resulted in an enhancement in the average PCE from  $ 18.06 \pm 0.17\% $  to  $ 20.70 \pm 0.75\% $ , while further increasing the amount of biuret additive leads to the decrease of photovoltaic performance. The excess biuret in the perovskite films may impede charge transport along the grain boundaries, which also accounts for the poor surface morphology in Figure S1B.

Figure 5A shows the  $ J-V $  curves of the best-performing devices, with the photovoltaic parameters summarized in Table 1. The control device exhibits a PCE of  $ 18.26\% $  with short-circuit current density  $ (J_{\mathrm{sc}}) = 22.69 \, \mathrm{mA cm}^{-2} $ ,  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} = 1.06 \, \mathrm{V} $ , and  $ \mathrm{FF} = 76.07\% $ . In contrast, when  $ 2 \, \mathrm{mol}\% $  biuret was introduced, the  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $ ,  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $ , and FF are increased to  $ 23.55 \, \mathrm{mA cm}^{-2} $ ,  $ 1.11 \, \mathrm{V} $ , and  $ 80.73\% $ , yielding a promising PCE up to  $ 21.16\% $ , which to our knowledge is the highest PCE for  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  solar cells with a mesoporous electron transport layer (Table S3). Figure S8 shows the incident photon to current conversion efficiency (IPCE) of the champion devices. The integrated current densities for the control and biuret-containing devices are 21.64 and 22.65  $ \mathrm{mA cm}^{-2} $ , respectively, which is consistent with the  $ J_{\mathrm{sc}} $  values obtained from the  $ J-V $  curves within  $ 5\% $  deviation. Figure 5B,C shows the  $ J-V $  curves measured under both reverse- and forward-scan directions with the corresponding photovoltaic parameters listed in Table S2. It was found that the device with biuret additive has almost identical  $ J-V $  characteristics with

# 2.4. Charge Recombination

Nonradiative charge recombination is an important process that influences photovoltaic performance, hysteresis behavior, and long-term stability of PSCs. To further understand the improvement of device performance, charge recombination process in the complete devices was characterized. Figure 5F shows the plots of -dV/dJ versus   $  (J_{\mathrm{sc}} - J)^{-1}  $   and the linear fitting curves for the control and biuret-modified devices. The ideality factor m can be derived from the linear fitting results.  $  ^{24}  $   The biuret-modified device exhibits decreased m value (1.69) compared with the control device (2.02). The ideality factor m depends on charge diffusion and recombination processes.  $  ^{36}  $   The smaller the value of m, the less recombination is caused by traps in the device. Hence, a smaller m means that the charge recombination in the biuret-modified device was reduced due to the reduction of trap states.

To provide more solid evidence on the charge recombination process, the dependence of the  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $  on light intensity for the control and biuret-modified devices was measured. The slope

deviation of   $  V_{oc}  $   versus the natural logarithm of light intensity from   $  k_{B}T/q  $  , indicates the occurrence of nonradiative trap-assisted recombination (monomolecular recombination), which has been reported to be dominant recombination pathway in PSCs.  $  ^{37,38}  $   As shown in Figure 6A, a slope of 1.88   $  k_{B}T/q  $   and 1.34   $  k_{B}T/q  $   was estimated for the control and biuret-modified cells, respectively. This suggests that the trap-assisted charge recombination process was effectively suppressed by the biuret. Open-circuit voltage decay measurements were also performed to investigate the transient process of charge recombination. As shown in Figure 6B, compared to the control device, the biuret-modified device demonstrates a prominently slower   $  V_{oc}  $   decay. The   $  V_{oc}  $   decay time constant of the biuret-modified device (1.43 ms) was estimated to be   $  \sim5  $   times larger than that of the control device (0.28 ms). The electron lifetimes (  $  \tau_{n}  $  ) are also calculated by the following equation  $  ^{39}  $

$$
\tau_ {n} = - \frac {k _ {B} T}{q} \biggl (\frac {d V _ {o c}}{d t} \biggr) ^ {- 1}
$$

(3)

where   $  k_{B}  $   is the Boltzmann constant, T is temperature, and q is the positive elementary charge. As shown in Figure S9, the biuret-modified device displays a longer   $  \tau_{n}  $   with less charge recombination, which leads to the improvement of photovoltaic performance, especially promotion of the   $  V_{oc}  $  .

# 2.5. Device Stability

The device thermal stability was evaluated under   $  85^{\circ}  $  C continuous annealing in nitrogen glovebox. The introduction of biuret, demonstrated a strong positive effect on the stabilization of the unsealed   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   devices. As shown in Figure 7A, the biuret-modified devices showed excellent thermal stability, preserving 94% of its initial efficiency after 288 h (12 days). In contrast, the control cells retained only 50% of the original efficiency after 192 h (8 days), the degeneration of which is similar with other reports.  $  ^{30}  $   Moreover, the device storage stability was also evaluated in ambient air (temperature of   $  20-25^{\circ}  $  C; relative humidity of 30-40%) using unencapsulated cells under dark (Figure 7B). In contrast to the observed efficiency degradation (that maintains 81% over 1000 h), in the control devices, the biuret-modified devices remained more than 93% of the original efficiency over 1000 h, demonstrating obviously enhanced stability under the natural environment. The enhancement of air stability could be ascribed to

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated a simple and effective approach by using bifunctional additive biuret to obtain high-performance  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  solar cells. The strong interactions between biuret and perovskite components could retard the grain growth rate, which facilitates the growth of high-quality perovskite films with large grain size, preferred orientation, reduced defects density, and superior electronic properties. Meanwhile, the remnant biuret in the final films can passivate ionic defects and act as a molecular lock to prevent thermal degradation. As a result, we achieve greatly enhanced solar cell performance with efficiencies exceeding  $ 21\% $  for  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  solar cells, accompanied by outstanding thermal and environmental stability. We believe that the present work will stimulate other successful developments on bifunctional additive molecules in the future.
