# Bis(diphenylamine)-Tethered Carbazolyl Anthracene Derivatives as Hole-Transporting Materials for Stable and High-Performance Perovskite Solar Cells

Ashutosh Singh,  $  ^{\dagger}  $   Seid Yimer Abate,  $  ^{\dagger}  $   Ch. Pavan Kumar, Wen-Ti Wu, Jye-Chian Hsiao, Feng-Ling Wu, Jiann T'suen Lin,  $  ^{*}  $   and Yu-Tai Tao  $  ^{*}  $

ACCESS

ABSTRACT: Three new hole-transporting materials (HTMs), composed of   $  N^{3},N^{6}  $  -bis(di-4-anisylamino)-9H-carbazole and anthracene moieties, have been developed for perovskite solar cell (PSC) application. Two of the new HTMs have better hole mobility and hole-extraction ability compared to spiro-OMeTAD. Accordingly, the best PSC based on mixed ion perovskite of   $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.79}MA_{0.16}PbI_{2.49}Br_{0.51}  $   and doped HTMs has better power conversion efficiency (18.65%) than the corresponding PSC based on doped spiro-OMeTAD (17.90%). Moreover, the PSCs based on these HTMs have negligible hysteresis and good temporal stability.

KEYWORDS: hole-transporting materials, perovskite solar cells, anthracene derivatives, triphenyl amine-carbazole-anthracene, organic hole transport

# INTRODUCTION

Due to drastically reducing supply of fossil fuels and the accompanying environmental degradation from burning gasoline, harvesting renewable energy as an alternative energy source has become one of the most urgent tasks to our society in the coming years. In this regard, the massive energy that we receive from the sun stands out to be the most abundant and sustainable source of power. Converting it efficiently into electricity is a major concern. Presently, solar cell devices based on silicon are a common choice, transforming over 26.7% of the incident sunlight into electric power.  $  ^{1,2}  $   However, the high production cost of these silicon solar cells has encouraged the development of new low-cost and efficient photovoltaic devices for solar energy conversion.

Currently, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted intensive interest among researchers because of their rapidly improved power conversion efficiency (PCE), which has surpassed 22% in the past several years, and a record PCE of 25.2% has been reported.  $  ^{3-5}  $   A PSC device normally consists of a conductive substrate, an electron-transporting layer, a perovskite layer, a hole-transporting layer, and a back electrode (Ag or Au).  $  ^{6}  $   Hole-transporting materials (HTMs) play a crucial role in hole extraction, hole transport, and electron blocking from perovskites to the metal anode for efficient PSCs. Compared with other HTMs, small molecular HTMs have distinct advantages, such as easy synthesis and purification, and good film-forming property during device fabrication. Among small molecular HTMs, compounds with planar and rigid skeletons are promising due to efficient intermolecular   $  \pi  $  -stacking, which facilitates hole transport.  $  ^{26-28}  $   Other widely

used small molecular HTMs consist of a linear or star-shaped molecular skeleton and arylamines, which may facilitate hole hopping.  $  ^{29}  $   So far, spiro-OMeTAD-based devices are still the leading material in terms of performance, though some arylamine-based HTMs have been reported to have PSC efficiencies higher than 18%.  $  ^{30-36}  $   Complicated synthesis and high cost of spiro-OMeTAD greatly restrict its industrial manufacture and implementations, however. Consequently, development of highly stable, low-cost small molecular HTMs as alternates of spiro-OMeTAD for highly efficient PSCs is imperative.

We were interested in developing new HTMs for PSCs.  $  ^{37,38}  $   In continuation of our interest on arylamine-based HTMs, we set out to synthesize new HTMs with arylamine linked to an anthracenyl moiety. We reasoned that the arylamine moiety may help with hole-hopping, and the planar anthracenyl moiety may help with intermolecular assembly, leading to increased intermolecular electronic coupling.  $  ^{39}  $   Some recent reports on HTMs using   $  N^{3}, N^{6}  $  -bis(di-4-anisylamino)-9H-

Fabrication and Measurement of PSCs. Device Fabrication and Performance Measurement.   $  TiO_{2}  $   films (including compact   $  TiO_{2}  $   and mesoporous   $  TiO_{2}  $  ) on FTO were prepared by following the same procedures described in the literature.  $  ^{51,55}  $   Lithium doping was carried out following a previous report,  $  ^{56}  $   and the film was annealed at 450 °C (30 min). The film was cooled down to 150 °C and then transferred to a glove box (with controlled   $  H_{2}O  $   and an   $  O_{2}  $   level of <1 ppm) for spin-coating of perovskite and HTM layers. Triple-cation perovskite, with a nominal formula of   $  Cs_{0.05}FA_{0.79}MA_{0.16}PbI_{2.49}Br_{0.51}  $   (Cs/FA/MA), was prepared and spin-cast on   $  TiO_{2}  $   as described before.  $  ^{51}  $   After annealing at 100 °C for 1 h, a smooth and thick film of black perovskite layer formed.

The HTM solution was prepared from 2-AF (10 mg), 2,6-diAF (10 mg), 9-AF (10 mg), or spiro-OMeTAD (40 mg) in 0.5 mL of chlorobenzene. These solutions were doped with TBP and Li-TFSI (from a stock solution of 0.05 mg Li-TFSI in 100 μL acetonitrile). The optimized doping amounts of TBP and Li-TFSI (x, y) for 2-AF, 2,6-diAF, 9-AF, and spiro-OMeTAD were (12, 2 μL), (12, 2 μL), (12, 2 μL), and (14.5, 7.5 μL) respectively. The same procedures described previously  $  ^{53}  $  were followed for spin-coating of the doped HTM and deposition of the back electrode, Ag. The active area of each device was 0.04 cm  $  ^{2}  $ . The solar cell efficiencies were measured following the same procedures described in the literature.  $  ^{52}  $

The devices without encapsulation were stored in a desiccator (relative humidity: 20%; temperature: 28–35 °C) and were checked for temporal stability at different time intervals up to 500 h. The devices were taken out during measurements in the atmosphere (relative humidity: ~65–75%; temperature: ~25 °C) and returned to the desiccator immediately. The stability was also checked in ambient conditions (relative humidity: ~65–75%; temperature: 28–35 °C) for the best performed HTM, 2,6-diAF, and spiro-OMeTAD. The temporal stability was checked at different time intervals up to 120 h.

Theoretical Approach. Time-dependent density functional theory calculations at the B3LYP/6-311G* level using the Gaussian 16 program package were applied on the HTMs in order to correlate their molecular structures with the physical data and the performance of PSCs. All results are collected in the Supporting Information (Tables S1–S4). The ground-state dihedral angles between the neighboring units of the skeleton for each compound were calculated (Figure S6, Supporting Information). The angles between the anthracenyl unit attached and the carbazolyl unit are 54.7° in 2-AF and 54.1°/53.4° in 2,6-diAF. In contrast, the anthracenyl unit is nearly perpendicular to the carbazolyl unit in 9-AF (dihedral angle = 91.2°). There are similar dihedral angles from 42.6 to 45.8° obtained between diphenylamino (the plane is defined by the nitrogen and two ipso carbons) and carbazolyl units for the three compounds: 2-AF, 40.9°/44.7°; 2,6-diAF, 42.8, 43.7, 42.9, and 45.8°; and 9-AF, 42.6 and 45.2°. Molecular orbitals near the frontier were calculated (shown in Figure S7), with the HOMO and LUMO shown in Figure 2. The HOMOs are centered on arylamine with extension to the carbazole ring. In contrast, the LUMOs are localized at the central or terminal anthracenyl entity. The  $  S_0 \rightarrow S_1  $  transition is nearly 100% of the HOMO to LUMO transition, indicating the charge-transfer character (vide supra). The nearly orthogonal anthracenyl and carbazolyl entities in 9-AF are consistent with the extremely low intensity of the ICT band. Photocurrent–Voltage Characteristics. Mixed perovskite, (MA/FA/Cs)Pb(I/Br) $ _{3} $ , is becoming a popular active layer in recent years. $ ^{67} $  For example, a stabilized PCE above 21\% was reported for a PSC using Cs $ _{0.05} $ FA $ _{0.79} $ MA $ _{0.16} $ PbI $ _{2.49} $ Br $ _{0.51} $  as the active layer. After an initial drop to about 18\% in a few hours, the PCE remained stable for up to 250 h of continuous operation at a maximum power point. $ ^{51} $  Incorporation of Cs was suggested to enhance the stability and reduce the trap states of perovskite. Therefore, Cs $ _{0.05} $ FA $ _{0.79} $ MA $ _{0.16} $ PbI $ _{2.49} $ Br $ _{0.51} $  (Cs/FA/MA) was used in our study. We first tested the conventional PSC structure of FTO/ compact TiO $ _{2} $ /mesoporous TiO $ _{2} $ /(Cs/FA/MA)/nondoped HTM/Ag, and the PCEs of the cells were below 11\%. Then, TPB and Li-TFSI were added to the HTMs as the dopants. The corresponding device structure and the energy levels of relevant components are shown in Figure 8a. The photocurrent density–voltage ( $ J-V $ ) characteristics and incident photon-to-current conversion efficiencies (IPCE) are shown in Figure 8b,c, respectively, with the corresponding statistical data presented in the Supporting Information (Figure S9). The relevant cell performance data are listed in Table 3. To check the reliability of the cell  $ J-V $  plots, PCE and  $ J_{sc} $  at maximum power were measured and found to be stable for 100 s (Figure S10). Hysteresis phenomena, that is, mismatch of currents measured for the forward scan and reverse scan, were frequently reported in PSC studies. Several factors such as bulk/surface defects, ferroelectric properties, and interstitial ion migration of perovskite are considered to be related to hysteresis phenomena, $ ^{68} $  which may lead to unstable PSCs. The  $ J-V $  plots of forward and reverse scans for 2,6-diAF are shown in Figure 8d, and those for other HTMs are provided in the Supporting Information (Figure S11). All new HTMs except 9-AF exhibited negligible (2,6-diAF, 2-AF, and spiro-

OMeTAD) hysteresis, while 9-AF exhibited a very small hysteresis. The hysteresis index, HI (defined by   $  (J_{\mathrm{RS}}(0.8V_{\mathrm{oc}})-J_{\mathrm{FS}}(0.8V_{\mathrm{oc}}))/J_{\mathrm{RS}}(0.8V_{\mathrm{oc}})  $  ),  $  ^{69}  $   is 0.002, 0.004, 0.011, and 0.006 for 2,6-diAF, 2-AF, 9-AF, and spiro-OMeTAD, respectively. Very low hysteresis for all HTMs including spiro-OMeTAD indicates that bulk/surface defects, ferroelectric properties, or interstitial ion migration can be minimal in Cs/FA/MA. Certainly more studies are needed in order to have a complete understanding of the exact reason for the low J-V hysteresis of PSCs. The efficiency of 2,6-diAF reached 18.64 and 18.47% for reverse and forward scans, respectively. Both efficiencies were better than that of spiro-OMeTAD (reverse scan: 17.90%; forward scan: 17.74%). It is important to note that the spiro-OMeTAD film used here is thicker than the other three HTMs (see Figure 4) as the cell of spiro-OMeTAD with similar film thickness (78 nm; 20 mg/1 mL chlorobenzene) is much inferior (Figure S12). Compared with spiro-OMeTAD, the efficiency of 2-AF is slightly better for the reverse scan (18.11%), whereas slightly lower for the forward scan (17.61%). In contrast, the efficiencies for 9-AF were significantly lower than those of spiro-OMeTAD. # CONCLUSIONS

In summary, three new HTMs composed of  $ N^3,N^6 $ -bis(di-4-anisylamino)-9H-carbazole and anthracene entities have been synthesized and used as the HTMs for high performance PSCs using  $ \mathrm{Cs}_{0.05}\mathrm{FA}_{0.79}\mathrm{MA}_{0.16}\mathrm{PbI}_{2.49}\mathrm{Br}_{0.51} $  perovskite as the light absorber. The best HTM offers better cell efficiency (18.65%) and better temporal stability than spiro-OMeTAD (17.90%). The cell also exhibited negligible hysteresis. These good characteristics together with the low-cost preparation render the new HTMs promising candidates for future applications.
