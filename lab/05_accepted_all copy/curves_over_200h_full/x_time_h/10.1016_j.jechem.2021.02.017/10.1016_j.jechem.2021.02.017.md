# 1. Introduction

Due to the tailorable bandgap   $  [1,2]  $   high charge carrier mobility   $  [3,4]  $   long electron/hole diffusion length   $  [5,6]  $   and small exciton binding energy (  $  \approx  $  2 meV)   $  [7]  $   the emerging organic–inorganic lead halide perovskites have been regarded as appealing candidates for future photovoltaics   $  [8–10]  $   and the record power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has been rapidly raised to 25.5%   $  [11]  $   in just a decade since the seminal work that has been done in 2009   $  [12]  $  .

# ABSTRACT

Solvent residue is inevitable to occur in solution processed thin films, but its influence on the thin film quality has not been identified and addressed to date. Methylammonium acetate (MAAc) ionic liquid has recently been realized as an environmentally friendly solvent for solution processed perovskites. The specific high viscosity, low vapor pressure and strong association with perovskite precursor of the MAAc solvent is a double-edged sword, which endowed an advantageously ambient air operational and anti-solvent free perovskite deposition, but the MAAc is likely to be retained within the film and bring in detrimental effects on device performance of the corresponding solar cells. Herein, we reported a novel route to eliminate the residual solvent via a facial hydrochloric acid (HCl) annealing post-treatment (HAAP). In particular, chemical displacement reaction between the incorporated HCl and residual MAAc can be initiated to form volatile MACl and HAc, efficiently extracting MAAc residue. In the meanwhile, the stimulated mass transport via downward penetration and upward escape can trigger secondary perovskite growth with enlarged grain size and smoothened surface, leading to reduced defect state and improved interfacial contact intimacy, and also partial chloride ions are able to enter the crystal lattice to stabilize perovskite phase structure. As a result, a champion efficiency up to  $ 20.78\% $  originating from enhanced  $ V_{\mathrm{oc}} $  was achieved, and more than  $ 96\% $  of its initial efficiency can be maintained after  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $  shelf-storage.

The perovskite thin films were usually deposited via a cost-effective solution process in which the precursor solvents e.g. dimethylformamide (DMF) [13,14] or dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) [15] and anti-solvents e.g. chlorobenzene (CB) [16,17] or toluene [18] play a crucial role on the perovskite crystallization and hence the as deposited thin film quality [19,20]. Although the use of the above conventional solvents has resulted in highly efficient perovskite solar cells, the harm of the solvents to the environment and human body cannot be ignored, and also the whole fabrication procedure has to be carried out in an closed environment to get rid of the influence of moisture and oxygen, which seriously restricted their practical applications [21]. Therefore, it is highly desirable to find out some nontoxic solvents and anti-solvent free process for the perovskite fabrication. For examples, some green solvents such as ethyl acetate (EA) [22]

Ideally, an applicable procedure to deposit perovskite should imply green precursor solvent in the absence of anti-solvent, but it is rarely reported.

Recently, Chao et al. [31,32] used an environmentally friendly ionic liquid (IL) methylamine acetate (MAAc) as precursor solvent, which endowed the perovskite deposition at ambient conditions without anti-solvent. In particular, the MAAc is prone to self-assemble with the perovskite precursor via chelation and hydrogen bonding to generate homogeneously distributed micelles in the precursor solution. During the perovskite crystallization, the strong interaction between the MAAc and the perovskite precursor enables fast perovskite formation and effective passivation at the grain boundaries by eliminating voids and trap states. The unique properties of the MAAc in terms of low vapor pressure, high viscosity and strong association with perovskite precursor can advantageously regulate the high quality perovskite growth with preferred orientation and uniform grain size. Even though a feasible operational process and a relatively high PCE can be obtained by using MAAc as solvent, a certain amount of MAAc is likely to remain in the as-deposited film as evidenced by using MAAc as perovskite precursor solvent or additives [33,34]. It was known that a reasonable amount of  $ \mathrm{MA^{+}} $ ,  $ \mathrm{Ac^{-}} $  groups in the perovskite can effectively passivate antisite and under-coordinated defects, and therefore boost the device performance [35,36]. While in case of excess  $ \mathrm{Ac^{-}} $ , the lone pair of electrons on the ester groups can reduce the moisture resistance of the perovskites and shorten their operational stability [37]. In the meanwhile, the existence of the residual MAAc, chemically attached at the grain boundaries, can impede the crystal growth into larger size due to their steric hindrance. In addition, the ionic radius of  $ \mathrm{Ac^{-}} $  (0.162 nm) is smaller than that of  $ \mathrm{I^{-}} $  (0.220 nm) [38] the existence of excess  $ \mathrm{Ac^{-}} $  in the device might cause serious hysteresis issues due to the ion migration. While the traditional passivation scheme seems not applicable to the residual solvent [34] and prolonging the annealing time or increasing the annealing temperature to facilitate the solvent escape will lead to the growth of perovskite crystals with irregular orientations [39,40]. Nevertheless, solvent residue is inevitable to occur in solution processed thin films, but its influence on the thin film quality has not been identified and addressed to date.

# 2. Results and discussion

We developed a novel route to eliminate the residual solvent via an ions displacement method as illustrated in Fig. 1. Briefly, the  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  perovskites were firstly prepared by spin coating the precursor solution of  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  and MAI with mole ratio of 1:1 in MAAc solvent (Fig. S1), after thermal annealing at  $ 100\mathrm{C} $  for  $ 5\mathrm{min} $  which was optimized from our previous work [31,32] a diluted hydrochloric acid (HCl) in isopropanol alcohol (IPA) solution was then spin coated on the as-cast perovskite and followed by a post-annealing treatment at moderate temperatures to improve the quality of the spin-cast film, which was denoted as HAAP (hydrochloric acid annealing post-treatment). The schematic illustration of HAAP can be demonstrated in Fig. 1, the residual  $ \mathrm{Ac}^- $  ions were probably located at the crystal surface or grain boundaries (Fig. 1(a)). After the spin coating of HCl solution on top of the perovskite, a thin layer of HCl can accumulate at the top surface and some  $ \mathrm{Cl}^- $  ions can penetrate into the solid films along the grain boundaries (Fig. 1(b)). Upon thermal annealing, the ions exchange can occur as the HCl penetrating into the grain boundaries, for examples, the hydrogen ions  $ \mathrm{H}^+ $  and chloride ions  $ \mathrm{Cl}^- $  can react with  $ \mathrm{Ac}^- $  and  $ \mathrm{MA}^+ $  to form volatile HAc and MACl, respectively, and also partial  $ \mathrm{Cl}^- $  can enter the crystal lattice and generate  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  (Fig. 1(c)). The displacement reaction can be expressed as the following formula:

$$
\mathrm{MAAc} + \mathrm{HCl} \rightarrow \mathrm{HAc} \uparrow + \mathrm{MACl} \uparrow
$$

$$
\mathrm{MAPbI} _ {3 - x} \mathrm{Ac} _ {x} + \mathrm{HCl} \rightarrow \mathrm{MAPbI} _ {3 - x} \mathrm{Cl} _ {x} + \mathrm{HAc} \uparrow
$$

In contrast, a smooth film with enlarged grain size was observed in the perovskite after HAAP (Fig. 2(b)) and the average grain size was increased from 400 to  $ 700\mathrm{nm} $  (Fig. S2), indicating that the post treatment can induce a secondary crystal growth or coalescence

In addition, the root-mean-square (RMS) of the HAAP treated perovskite thin films was reduced from 31.93 to 16.26 nm according to atomic force microscopy images (AFM) (Fig. 2(c)). The RMS is a major benchmark for the roughness of thin film, and a smaller RMS means a smooth thin film surface with less sharp traps or pin-holes, which allows enhanced interfacial contact and reduced voids between the perovskite and charge-transporting layers [41]. The reduced surface roughness was ascribed to the crystal re-orientation and secondary growth during the ion displacement process. Subsequently, the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were performed to analyze the chemical composition of the perovskites after HAAP. It was noticed in Fig. 2(d) that a dominant peak for O 1s at 532.2 eV was observed, which was assigned to C=O or O-C=O groups, indicating the existence of residual MAAc within the MAPbI₃ films, and the peak at 530.6 eV was attributed to physically adsorbed oxygen/carbonates, in accordance with previous report (Fig. S3(a)) [42]. In contrast, the peak at 532.2 eV was disappeared in the HAAP treated samples, suggesting the successful removal of residual MAAc. We further performed Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) measurements on the pristine and HAAP perovskite films. It was noticed in Fig. S3(b) that the peak at 1627 cm⁻¹, which is assigned to the stretching vibration of C=O from Ac⁻ [31,32] was clearly present in the pristine perovskite film, indicating the presence of the residual solvent. Whereas, the peak was disappeared in HAAP perovskite film, confirmed the removal of residual MAAc. It was worth to mention that a distinct peak at 199.08 eV in Fig. 2(e), which was assigned to Cl 2p [43] was appeared for the sample after HAAP indicating that some Cl elements were retained within the film after the HAAP treatment. To ensure that the crystal quality and how the Cl existed in the perovskite film, X-ray diffraction (XRD) spectra measurements were carried out. Two typical peaks at 14.1° and 28.2° corresponding to the (110) and (220) crystal planes of 3D MAPbI₃ [44] respectively, were observed in the pristine perovskite as shown in Fig. 2(f). The characteristic peaks were obviously shifted to higher

diffraction angles (Fig. S4) after HAAP, which was ascribed to the lattice shrinkage after the incorporation of Cl into the crystal lattice and the formation of  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_{3 - x}\mathrm{Cl}_x $  [45].

Therefore, the functions of the incorporated HCl can be understood from different aspects: Firstly, the ions displacement between the incorporated HCl and residual MAAc can effectively eliminate the solvent residue; secondly, the   $  Cl^{-}  $   ions are more diffusive, and the downward penetration of   $  Cl^{-}  $   ions and upward escape of gaseous MACl facilitate the mass transport and enhance the ions exchange; and also, the incorporated   $  Cl^{-}  $   were accumulated on the grain boundaries and hence enhance the grain surface tension, which is favorable to trigger secondary crystal growth or coalescence, leading to the formation of enlarged perovskite crystals. Furthermore, we performed the steady state photoluminescence (PL) and time resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) measurements on the bare perovskite on glass, to compare the quality of the perovskite thin films before and after HAAP. Correspondingly, the perovskite films after the post-treatment exhibited higher PL intensity and prolonged charge carrier lifetime (Fig. S5) compared to that of the pristine perovskites, indicating the reduction of non-radiative recombination sites.

To further evaluate the impact of HAAP process on the photophysical properties of the perovskite thin films, we conducted optical and photoluminescence measurements on perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD and perovskite-HAAP/spiro-OMeTAD films. In order to explore the ultrafast charge transition dynamics at the perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD interface, the femtosecond transient absorption (TA) spectroscopy at different delay times was further conducted. Fig. 3(d and e) present representative TA spectra of the pristine and HAAP treated perovskites, respectively. The negative PB band was related to the fluorescent emission of the perovskite as revealed by the steady-state PL in Fig. 3(b), and it was noticed that the intensity of the PB was clearly decreased in HAAP treated samples compared to that of pristine perovskite, confirmed the quench effect in the steady-state PL. It was found that the pristine perovskite film exhibited a decay time constant of 0.2787 ps. In comparison, a faster decay with time constant of 0.1446 ps was fitted for the HAAP treated film, indicating efficient hole extraction from perovskite to spiro-OMeTAD layer.

To investigate the effect of HAAP on the photovoltaic performance of the corresponding perovskite solar cells, we fabricated a series of devices with a planar n-i-p heterojunction configuration of ITO/SnO  $  _{2}  $  (40 nm)/butylamine hydrochloride

(BACI 5 nm)/perovskite (540 nm)/spiro-OMeTAD (80 nm)/MoO  $  _{3}  $  (22 nm)/Ag (200 nm) as indicated in Fig. 4(a) (BACI was introduced as a self-assembled layer on top of the SnO  $  _{2}  $  electron transport layer (ETL) to modify the SnO  $  _{2}  $ /perovskite interface [49,50]), in which the perovskite layer was prepared by using MAAc as solvent with or without HAAP treatment. The photovoltaic metrics and typical J-V characteristics measured under a simulated AM1.5G solar irradiation were summarized in Tables S2 and S3 and plotted in Fig. 4(b), respectively. The control device with pristine perovskite exhibited a PCE of 18.65% with a short circuit current density (  $  J_{sc}  $  ) of 22.85 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $ , an open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) of 1.058 V and a fill factor (FF) of 77.09%. In contrast, the optimized HAAP treatment significantly boosts the device performance and a champion efficiency of 20.78% with a  $  J_{sc}  $  of 23.68 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $ , a  $  V_{oc}  $  of 1.11 V and a FF of 79.35% was obtained. The statistical deviations of  $  V_{oc}  $ , FF,  $  J_{sc}  $  and PCE from 30 individual devices were summarized in Fig. 4(d and e) and S6, and it was noticed that the reproducibility of the device after HAAP treatment was improved and the performance enhancement was mainly originated from the increase of  $  V_{oc}  $ . Specifically, the averaged PCE was enhanced from 18.01% to 20.14% (Fig. 4(e)) with a dramatic increase of averaged  $  V_{oc}  $  from 1.08 to 1.12 V (Fig. 4(d)) after HAAP. Furthermore, the steady-state output current densities of the devices at the maximum power point for 5 min were measured under continuous light illumination, and a stabilized PCE of 20.76% was obtained in the HAAP treated device, substantially superior than 17.81% PCE of the pristine device (Fig. S7). We further checked the shelf stability of device in a closed   $  N_{2}  $   atmosphere. As demonstrated in Fig. 4(f), more than 96% of the initial efficiency can be retained after 1000 h storage for the HAAP treated device, compared to only 82% maintenance of the pristine device. The worse stability of the control device was ascribed to the existence of numerous defect states within the film where the degradation was likely to occur, and hydrophilic lone electron pair in residual MAAc. Furthermore, the hysteresis of J-V curves was examined by measuring the J-V curves in different scan directions. As shown in Fig. 4(c), the HAAP treatment can significantly suppress the hysteresis. It was known that the hysteresis was mainly attributed to the ions migration through the device and charge recombination at the interface, and the elimination of the hysteresis suggested that the HAAP treatment can significantly boost the crystal quality and reduce non-radiative recombination at the perovskite layer and the perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD interface, which can be confirmed by the reduction of trap assisted current leakage in the dark J-V measurements (Fig. S9). In addition, the release of MAAc can reduce the mobile small ions. Therefore, the post-treatment on the MAAc-based perovskites has greatly improved the device performance of the corresponding perovskite device.

In order to gain insight into the performance enhancement resulting from HAAP, the transient photovoltage (TPV) decay under the open-circuit conditions was measured. Upon a pulse light illumination, a transient photovoltage can be created as the generation of free electrons and holes. Once the light was off, the photovoltage started to decay via instantaneous charge recombination. A longer decay time offered more chance for charge extraction from perovskite to respective electrodes. As demonstrated in Fig. 5(a), the TPV lifetime  $ (\tau_{\mathrm{r}}) $  of the HAAP treated device was substantially longer than that of the pristine device, indicating reduced non-radiative recombination pathways. Furthermore, the

transient photocurrent (TPC) decay of the device at short-circuit condition was also measured to investigate the charge extraction process. As shown in Fig. 5(b), the shorter TPC lifetime in HAAP treated device compared to that of the pristine device, indicated efficient charge transfer and extraction.

To study how the charges were recombined in the device, the light dependent   $  V_{oc}  $   and   $  J_{sc}  $   was measured. For a conventional p-n junction type photovoltaic cell, the   $  V_{oc}  $   is a function of cell temperature (  $  T_{c}  $  ) and   $  J_{sc}  $ , namely,   $  V_{oc} = (n k T_{c}/q) \ln(J_{sc}/J_{0} + 1)  $ , where n, k, q, and   $  J_{0}  $   are ideality factor, Boltzmann' constant, elementary charge, and saturated current density (in the dark), respectively. It was known that the slope (n) of   $  V_{oc}  $   respected to the light intensity is equal to unity when all carriers recombine via band-to-band transition, and increases as the increasing of trap-assisted recombination [51,52]. As can be seen in Fig. 5(c and d), the slope (n = 1.895) of the HAAP treated device was smaller than that of the pristine device (n = 2.096), suggesting the reduced trap-assisted nonradiative recombination. Furthermore, the relationship between the   $  J_{sc}  $   and light intensity can be expressed as   $  J \propto I^{2}  $ , where   $  \alpha  $   is an exponential factor. In an ideal case, the   $  \alpha  $   is equal to 1 when monomolecular recombination dominates and its deviation from 1 is mainly ascribed to the presence of bimolecular recombination [53–55]. As shown in Fig. 5(d), the values of   $  \alpha  $   are 0.986 and 0.948 for the HAAP and pristine device, respectively, indicating reduced bimolecular recombination in HAAP treated device.

To quantitatively evaluate the trap density  $ (N_{\mathrm{t}}) $  in the perovskite layer, the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) measurements were performed, where the  $ N_{\mathrm{t}} $  can be calculated using the following equation:  $ N_{\mathrm{t}} = 2\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}V_{\mathrm{TFL}} / (qL^2) $ . Where,  $ \varepsilon_0 $  is the vacuum permittivity,  $ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}} $  is the relative dielectric constant of perovskite,  $ V_{\mathrm{TFL}} $  is the trap-filled limit voltage,  $ q $  is the elemental charge and  $ L $  is the layer thickness [56]. The hole-only devices with structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD/MoO₃/Ag were fabricated, and the typical double logarithmic  $ J - V $  curves were shown in Fig. 5(e and f).

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, we reported a facile but highly efficient approach to improve device performance of perovskite solar cells when using ionic liquid MAAc as precursor solvent. It was found that a small

amount of MAAc can be retained within the as-deposited perovskite films, which was detrimental to the device performance and stability. The introduced HCl annealing post-treatment (HAAP) can initiate ions displacement between the HCl and MAAc and form volatile MACl and HAc, effectively releasing the residual MAAc solvent. In the meanwhile, the downward penetration of HCl and upward escape of gaseous MACl and HAc can facilitate mass transport and induce secondary perovskite growth, resulting in enlarged grain size and smoothened surface. Therefore, the efficiency of the corresponding solar cells can be significantly improved due to the enhancement of  $  V_{\mathrm{oc}}  $ , which was ascribed to the reduction of nonradiative recombination centers at the perovskite bulk and perovskite/spiro-OMeTAD interface. As a result, a champion efficiency above  $  20\%  $  with eliminated hysteresis and prolonged shelf-storage stability was obtained with the facial HAAP treatment.
