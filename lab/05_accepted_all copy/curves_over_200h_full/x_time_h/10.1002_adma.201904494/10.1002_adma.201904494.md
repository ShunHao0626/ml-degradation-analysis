# Extending the Photovoltaic Response of Perovskite Solar Cells into the Near-Infrared with a Narrow-Bandgap Organic Semiconductor

Xiaoming Zhao, Chao Yao, Tianran Liu, J. Clay Hamill, Jr., Guy Olivier Ngongang Ndjawa, Guangming Cheng, Nan Yao, Hong Meng, and Yueh-Lin Loo*

Typical lead-based perovskites solar cells show an onset of photogeneration around 800 nm, leaving plenty of spectral loss in the near-infrared (NIR). Extending light absorption beyond 800 nm into the NIR should increase photocurrent generation and further improve photovoltaic efficiency of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). Here, a simple and facile approach is reported to incorporate a NIR-chromophore that is also a Lewis-base into perovskite absorbers to broaden their photoresponse and increase their photovoltaic efficiency. Compared with pristine PSCs without such an organic chromophore, these solar cells generate photocurrent in the NIR beyond the band edge of the perovskite active layer alone. Given the Lewis-basic nature of the organic semiconductor, its addition to the photoactive layer also effectively passivates perovskite defects. These films thus exhibit significantly reduced trap densities, enhanced hole and electron mobilities, and suppressed illumination-induced ion migration. As a consequence, perovskite solar cells with organic chromophore exhibit an enhanced efficiency of 21.6%, and substantively improved operational stability under continuous one-sun illumination. The results demonstrate the potential generalizability of directly incorporating a multifunctional organic semiconductor that both extends light absorption and passivates surface traps in perovskite active layers to yield highly efficient and stable NIR-harvesting PSCs.

Manipulating perovskite absorption can be achieved by designing and formulating new materials compositions, such as those reported for narrow-bandgap Sn-containing perovskites.  $  ^{[18,19]}  $   However, these strategies have met limited success due to the low chemical stability of Sn-containing perovskites, which have been shown to be more prone to oxidation compared to their Pb-based counterparts.  $  ^{[20]}  $   Recently, serially-connected tandem structures composed of two or more sub-cells with complementary absorption have demonstrated enhanced photoresponse compared to single-junction Pb-based perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{[21-25]}  $   However, the creation of a tandem structure complicates processing and dramatically increases device complexity. There currently does not exist a simple and effective strategy to enhance the NIR photovoltaic response of PSCs.

Lead halide perovskites exhibit excellent materials properties for photovoltaics, such as suitable optical bandgaps, high absorption coefficients, low exciton binding energies, long charge-carrier diffusion lengths, and ambipolar charge transport.  $  ^{[1,2]}  $   These attributes, coupled with their potential to be produced from solution at low costs, have motivated rampant research in metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs). This increased activity in the community has in turn led to rapid increases in power-conversion efficiencies (PCEs).  $  ^{[3]}  $   Since the first introduction of perovskite solar cells by Miyasaka and co-workers in 2009, the power-conversion efficiency has dramatically improved from 3.8%, to over 24% owing to the development of new functional materials and creative modes of device optimization, such as composition engineering, solvent engineering, morphological engineering of the active layer, and interface engineering of the solar cell.  $  ^{[4-14]}  $   However, typical lead-based perovskites show an onset of light absorption around 800 nm. In this study, we demonstrate a facile approach to incorporate a NIR-chromophore that is also a Lewis-base into perovskite absorbers to broaden their photoresponse and increase their photovoltaic efficiency. Due to band alignment, efficient charge transfer takes place between the organic chromophore and the perovskite. Compared with pristine PSCs without such an organic chromophore, these solar cells generate photocurrent in the NIR beyond the band edge of the perovskite active layer. Given the Lewis-basic nature of the NIR-chromophore, its addition to the photoactive layer also effectively passivates perovskite defects. These films thus exhibit significantly reduced trap densities, enhanced hole and electron mobilities, and suppressed illumination-induced ion migration. As a consequence, perovskite solar cells with the NIR-chromophore exhibit an enhanced PCE of  $ 21.6\% $  relative to those without the NIR-chromophore at  $ 20.0\% $ , and substantively improved operational stability under continuous one-sun illumination. This approach is extendable to other classes of perovskite active layers. Our results demonstrate the potential generalizability of directly incorporating a multifunctional organic semiconductor that both extends light absorption and passivates surface traps to yield highly efficient and stable NIR-harvesting PSCs.

The narrow-bandgap organic semiconductor studied in this work is 2,2'-(2Z,2'Z)-(((4,4,9,9-tetrakis(4-hexylphenyl)-4,9-dihydro-s-indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b']dithiophene-2,7-diyl)bis(4-((2-ethylhexyl)oxy)thiophene-5,2-diyl))bis(methanylylidene))bis(5,6-difluoro-3-oxo-2,3-dihydro-1H-indene-2,1-diylidene))dimalononitrile, or IEICO-4F, whose molecular structure is shown in Figure 1a. It is a donor–acceptor narrow-bandgap organic semiconductor; when incorporated in OPVs as an electron acceptor, the solar cells show absorption beyond 800 nm to afford both high short-circuit current (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) and PCE.  $  ^{[29]}  $   Given that the absorption profile of IEICO-4F complements those of perovskite active layers (Figure 1b), we explored the direct incorporation of IEICO-4F in the active layers of PSCs with a goal to increase photocurrent generation. Figure 1c shows the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) levels of IEICO-4F.  $  ^{[29]}  $   That these energy levels align well with those of the perovskite absorber should ensure efficient charge transfer during photoexcitation between these two constituents. To make an effective IEICO-4F-perovskite hybrid solar cell, IEICO-4F needs to be in intimate contact with the perovskite without destroying the crystalline structure of perovskite grains. It follows that IEICO-4F should ideally be located at the perovskite grain boundaries to facilitate intergrain charge transfer while preserving intragrain crystallinity. The average   $  J_{SC}  $   of CsFAMA solar cells increased from   $  (23.2 \pm 0.4)  $   to   $  (24.4 \pm 0.4)  $   mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   with IEICO-4F addition, while the   $  J_{SC}  $   of MAIBr solar cells improved from   $  (16.6 \pm 0.5)  $   to   $  (17.8 \pm 0.7)  $   mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  . This observation is consistent with enhanced photocurrent generation with photo-absorption of IEICO-4F beyond the band edge of the pristine perovskite active layers. Directly supporting this assertion are the EQE spectra shown in Figure 2b. Compared to the EQE spectrum of pristine PSCs, we observe enhancement of EQE in the range of 700–850 nm for both MAIBr and CsFAMA solar cells when IEICO-4F is present, whereas we do not observe any marked difference in the EQE spectra of pristine PSCs and PSCs with PMMA. Increases in   $  J_{SC}  $   that result from a reduced bandgap of the active layers have typically come at the expense of   $  V_{OC}  $   in organic photovoltaics and perovskite solar cells.  $  ^{[27,46,47]}  $   To the contrary, the   $  V_{OC}  $   and FF in our PSCs have remained largely invariant with the addition of IEICO-4F. Considering the Lewis-basicity of IEICO-4F, we attribute the preservation of   $  V_{OC}  $   and FF to surface passivation, which we elaborate below. With enhancements in   $  J_{SC}  $   and preservation of   $  V_{OC}  $   and FF, CsFAMA solar cells that incorporate IEICO-4F exhibit a PCE of 21.6%, a PCE that is significantly higher than that of pristine CsFAMA devices (20.0%) and PMMA-containing CsFAMA devices (20.4%). A similar trend is observed with MAIBr solar cells, suggesting the potentially broad applicability of this strategy. The stabilized PCEs we obtained from tracking the maximum power point (MPP) for all PSCs fabricated in this study match those obtained when we scanned their J-V characteristics (Table 1). These results demonstrate the effectiveness of IEICO-4F addition on improving the overall performance of perovskite solar cells.

To understand the origin of the enhanced performance in our PSCs, we conducted a series of photophysical, morphological, and electrical studies on the perovskite active layers with and without IEICO-4F. As the enhancements are qualitatively similar in both CsFAMA and MAIBr solar cells, we opted to focus these studies on CsFAMA. The UV–vis absorption spectra of CsFAMA perovskite active layers with and without IEICO-4F are shown in Figure S4 in the Supporting Information. In the presence of IEICO-4F, the UV–vis spectrum of CsFAMA exhibits enhanced absorption in the range of 700–850 nm, consistent with the enhancement observed in its corresponding EQE spectrum. Apart from enhanced absorption, changes in the efficacy of charge-carrier separation and transfer can also impact device performance. To elucidate the charge-carrier dynamics in CsFAMA-IEICO-4F active layers, we conducted PL spectroscopy. Here, we separately studied the dynamics of the charge carriers that are generated from the perovskite and IEICO-4F; the results of which are shown in Figure 2c,d, respectively. Figure 2c presents the steady-state PL spectra of the pristine CsFAMA film, the control CsFAMA sample with PMMA, and the CsFAMA film with IEICO-4F, all excited at 470 nm and detected at 760 nm, corresponding to the PL response of CsFAMA. The steady-state PL spectra of the same films, excited at 680 nm and detected at 855 nm, a detection wavelength that is unique to the PL response of IEICO-4F, are shown in Figure 2d. Figure 2c shows efficient PL quenching of the CsFAMA in the presence of IEICO-4F; this observation suggests that charge transfer from CsFAMA to IEICO-4F is effective. Ultrafast time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) was conducted to understand energy transfer from CsFAMA to IEICO-4F. These studies were performed by pumping at 374 nm to selectively excite CsFAMA, and detecting emission at 760 nm (corresponding to CsFAMA) and 855 nm (corresponding to IEICO-4F). Since device stability remains a significant concern for perovskite solar cells, we also examined whether and how IEICO-4F addition affects the operational stability of CsFAMA PSCs. Following the test conditions suggested in the literature,  $  ^{[63]}  $  we tracked the MPP to quantify the operational stability of encapsulated devices with and without IEICO-4F under continuous one sun illumination at ambient (  $  \approx30\ ^{\circ}C  $  ,   $  \approx35\%  $  R.H.). The normalized performance parameters are shown in Figure 4d and Figure S8 (Supporting Information). The characteristics of solar cells with and without IEICO-4F uniformly exhibit a two-stage decay: a rapid initial burn-in in the first few hours of testing followed by a much slower secondary decay in the device parameters. While both cell types degrade over time, we observe slower degradation with IEICO-4F-containing devices, especially during the initial burn-in. In particular, we retain 76% of the initial PCE after burn-in with the IEICO-4F-containing device, compared to 66% of the initial PCE of the pristine CsFAMA solar cell. This burn-in has been attributed to illumination-induced ion migration in the literature.  $  ^{[64,65]}  $  We thus hypothesize that the improved operational stability of encapsulated devices with IEICO-4F to stem from suppressed ion migration at the grain boundaries.  $  ^{[66-68]}  $  As a follow up, we recorded the PL spectra of pristine CsFAMA and IEICO-4F-containing CsFAMA films as a function of illumination time to track the extent of ion migration.  $  ^{[69-71]}  $  As depicted in Figure 4e, we observe a gradual increase in the PL intensity with illumination with pristine CsFAMA. By comparison, the increase in PL intensity is modest and takes place over a more extended period of 120 min with the IEICO-4F-containing CsFAMA film (Figure 4f). The slower PL intensity increase with the IEICO-4F-containing perovskite film suggests ion migration to be substantially retarded.  $  ^{[72]}  $  These results indicate that the addition of IEICO-4F into perovskite active layers not only improve solar cell PCE, but can also enhance operational stability, showing the potential for our strategy to improve commercialized perovskite photovoltaic products.

In summary, we have demonstrated a simple and effective strategy to simultaneously enhance the NIR photovoltaic

response and passivate the active layers of perovskite solar cells. The introduction of IEICO-4F, a narrow-bandgap small molecule that is also a Lewis base, into the perovskite active layer forms a hybrid perovskite-IEICO-4F materials pair that when incorporated as the active layer in solar cells exhibit enhanced photocurrent without incurring any loss in photovoltage and fill factor relative to those without IEICO-4F. The proposed strategy provides a facile route to extend the photoresponse of broad-band perovskites beyond their band edge without increasing fabrication steps or complexity. As an added benefit, the addition of IEICO-4F passivates the perovskite film effectively, producing high quality crystalline domains with reduced trap states and enhanced mobility, resulting in suppressed ion migration in devices that incorporate such active layers. Accordingly, the resulting perovskite solar cells deliver an enhanced PCE as high as 21.6% and improved stability under continuous one-sun illumination. The developed strategy paves a new way to enhance the NIR photovoltaic response of perovskites solar cells and opens a new application field for the rational design of organic semiconductors that are (i) Lewis bases, (ii) have strong and efficient absorption in the NIR, and (iii) soluble in commonly used antisolvents, like chlorobenzene and toluene, to enable its introduction into the perovskite layer.
