1.5–1.6 eV bandgap Pb-based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) with 30–31% theoretical efficiency limit by the Shockley–Queisser model achieve 21–24% power conversion efficiencies (PCEs). However, the best PCEs of reported ideal-bandgap (1.3–1.4 eV) Sn–Pb PSCs with a higher 33% theoretical efficiency limit are <18%, mainly because of their large open-circuit voltage (  $  V_{oc}  $  ) deficits (>0.4 V). Herein, it is found that the addition of guanidinium bromide (GABr) can significantly improve the structural and photoelectric characteristics of ideal-bandgap (  $  \approx  $  1.34 eV) Sn–Pb perovskite films. GABr introduced in the perovskite films can efficiently reduce the high defect density caused by   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation in the perovskite, which is favorable for facilitating hole transport, decreasing charge-carrier recombination, and reducing the   $  V_{oc}  $   deficit. Moreover, the GABr-modified PSCs exhibit significantly improved environmental and thermal stability. This work represents a noteworthy step toward the fabrication of efficient and stable ideal-bandgap PSCs.

Here, we discover guanidinium bromide (GABr) is an ideal candidate for effectively reducing the   $  V_{oc}  $   deficit of the ideal-bandgap Sn–Pb binary PSCs. Partial replacement of formamidinium (FA) and methylammonium (MA) by guanidinium(  $  CH_{6}N_{3}^{+}  $  ) is beneficial to form a low-dimensional structure with   $  Pb^{2+}  $   and   $  Sn^{2+}  $ . At the same time, guanidinium cation is inserted into the crystal unit to form a 3D perovskite with enhanced environmental and thermal stability.  $  ^{[53]}  $   On the other hand, the introduction of Br into I-based perovskite generally increases bandgap, while also having other advantages such as improved crystallinity and large grain size, decreased disorder and advantageous defect passivation, increased charge extraction, and environmental stability.  $  ^{[22]}  $

Herein, we report utilizing GABr to improve the structural and photoelectric characteristics of ideal-bandgap (1.34 eV) Sn–Pb binary perovskite thin films (FA $  _{0.7}  $ MA $  _{0.3}  $ Pb $  _{0.7}  $ Sn $  _{0.3}  $ I $  _{3}  $ , where FA = formamidinium and MA = methylammonium). We found that GABr introduced in the perovskite films can efficiently passivate the defect density caused by Sn $  ^{2+}  $  oxidation, increase carrier lifetimes, reduce the V $  _{oc}  $  deficit, and enhance the stability of ideal-bandgap perovskite films. As a result, the best PCE of 20.63% with a certificated efficiency of 19.8% was achieved in the 1.35 eV Sn–Pb binary PSCs, which are the highest PCEs among all values reported to date for ideal-bandgap (1.3–1.4 eV) Sn–Pb binary PSCs. In addition, the device has a record small V $  _{oc}  $  deficit of 0.33 V, and the corresponding V $  _{oc}  $  is 1.02 V. Furthermore, the GABr-modified PSCs exhibit significantly improved environmental and thermal stability without encapsulation, which can maintain over 85% of their original PCEs after being stored in air (humidity ≈50–60%) for 1000 h and retain over 90% of the initial efficiency after being heated at 80 °C in air (humidity ≈50–60%) for 24 h. This work manifests a noteworthy step toward the fabrication of highly efficient and stable ideal-bandgap Sn–Pb binary PSCs.

In this work, we used the   $  FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   (1.34 eV; Figure S1, Supporting Information) as the basic composition for the ideal-bandgap (1.3–1.4 eV) Sn–Pb binary perovskite. The ideal-bandgap perovskite films were fabricated by spin-coating a stoichiometric mixture of components (MAI, FAI,   $  PbI_{2}  $  , and   $  SnI_{2}  $  ) and   $  SnF_{2}  $   in a mixed solvent of dimethyl formamide and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMF/DMSO). These perovskite films were introduced by adding GABr at various molar ratios with respect to MAI. The inverted PSCs in this study were structured as glass/indium tin oxide (ITO)/1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium chloride-modified poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) poly(styrenesulfonate) (EMIC-PEDOT:PSS)/perovskite/S-acetylthiocholine chloride passivation layer /fullerene(  $  C_{60}  $  )/bathocuproine (BCP)/silver (Ag),  $  ^{[6]}  $   as Figure S2, Supporting Information, shows. In order to determine the optimal introducing concentration of GABr, the perovskite films with different GABr introducing concentrations (0, 8, 12, 15, 25%) are fabricated and characterized, which are marked as {GABr}   $  FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $  . The device with 12% introducing

concentration shows the best efficiency, as shown in Table S1, Supporting Information. A detailed study of the introducing effects of GABr on the growth and photoelectronic characteristics of the perovskite films was executed in this work. Figure 1a depicts the bandgap of the   $  FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   perovskite films without and with 12% GABr. Compared to the   $  FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   film without GABr, the absorption onset of the   $  \{GABr\}FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   perovskite film with 12% GABr was blue-shifted, reflecting that the bandgap of 1.34 eV for 0% GABr was increased to 1.35 eV for 12% GABr (see Figures S1 and S3, Supporting Information). The absorption spectra of the   $  \{GABr\}FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   with different concentrations of GABr were shown in Figure S4, Supporting Information. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the perovskite films with and without GABr were also studied (see Figure S5, Supporting Information). Figure 1b,c depicts the XRD results of the perovskite films with and without 12% GABr. Compared to the perovskite film without GABr, the reflection peak slightly shifted to the lower angle corresponding to the (110) lattice plane of the perovskite film with 12% GABr, which denotes the slight expansion of the unit-cell volume.  $  ^{[19,23]}  $   For GABr concentrations of 15% or 25%, some more GA ions and Br ions were forced into the lattice of Sn–Pb binary perovskite, and further increased the lattice constant  $  ^{[19]}  $   (see Figure S5, Supporting Information). Moreover, the XRD peaks for the   $  \{GABr\}FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   perovskite film with 12% GABr are stronger and sharper from the (110) and (220) planes than the unintroduced perovskite film, which indicates that the perovskite film with 12% GABr possesses higher phase purity and well-preserved crystal structure. This implies that the crystallinity was increased with amplified grain size by introducing GABr into the   $  FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   perovskite film, as the grain morphologies given in Figure 1d,e. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analysis was conducted to study the introducing effect of GABr on the morphology of the   $  FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   perovskite film. Figure 1d,e depicts the SEM surface morphologies of the perovskite films without and with 12% GABr, respectively. The perovskite films without GABr contains many small grains and pinholes, where defects can easily form that leads to high levels of non-radiative recombination.  $  ^{[19]}  $   With 12% GABr introduced, the grain size of the perovskite film is significantly enlarged and the film becomes more compact. Higher concentration of GABr up to 25% leads to more structural defects and obvious gaps between the adjacent grains (Figure S6, Supporting Information), mainly because of greater strain made by the large GA ions relative to MA and FA during grain growth. Perovskite crystal quality chiefly dominates the optoelectric properties and then the device performance. In order to deeply comprehend the crystalline characteristics, the 2D grazing-incidence X-ray diffraction (GIXRD) was executed. Figure 1f,g depicts the GIXRD profiles of   $  FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   without and with 12% GABr. Compared to the   $  FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   film without GABr, the existence of strongly dispersed secondary blots and bright rings in the   $  FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   perovskite film with 12% GABr reveals that the crystalline realms retained good orientation compared to the substrate plane. In order to study the GABr introducing effect on the external trap positions and the distribution of defect energy in the perovskite films, the trap density of states (tDOS) was extracted from the thermal admittance spectroscopy results measured for the   $  FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   without and with 12% GABr-based PSCs. As Figure 3a shows,   $  FA_{0.7}MA_{0.3}Pb_{0.7}Sn_{0.3}I_{3}  $   without GABr-based device shows high trap density. With 12% GABr introduction, the trap density is reduced in the entire trap depth area. This indicates that the introduction of GABr to the ideal-bandgap Sn–Pb binary perovskite films is beneficial to reduce the defect density caused by   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation, which is favorable for facilitating hole transport and decreasing charge-carrier recombination. We have also provided the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy of the corresponding perovskite devices (Figure S9, Supporting Information).

In order to further determine the suppression effect of GABr on the oxidation of   $  Sn^{2+}  $   in the perovskite, we have further studied the time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) decay measurements of the perovskite films with and without 12% GABr, and at the same time, we have also provided the photoluminescence (PL) spectra of the corresponding perovskite films (Figure S10, Supporting Information). As Figure 3b shows, the PL lifetime value of 156 ns for 0% GABr-based perovskite film was increased to 821 ns for the 12% GABr-based perovskite film (Table S2, Supporting Information). This increase of carrier lifetime is consistent with the much-reduced tDOS. The introduction of GABr in the ideal-bandgap Sn–Pb binary perovskite layers could effectively increase the charge-carrier lifetime. These results directly demonstrate the excellent passivation effect of GABr on the defect density caused by   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation in the ideal-bandgap Sn–Pb binary perovskite films.

In summary, a novel and effective strategy of doping GABr molecule into the ideal-bandgap Sn–Pb binary perovskite films is demonstrated. The GABr-introduced ideal-bandgap Sn–Pb binary perovskite films display increased grain size, higher charge-carrier concentrations, and reduced trap state densities. In addition, this defect passivation also reduces the open-circuit-voltage deficit and current hysteresis of the PSC devices. As a result, perovskite films introduced with 12% GABr result in a magical enhancement of device performance with the PCE from 16.71% to 20.63%. The

best PCE of 20.63% with a certificated efficiency of 19.8% was achieved in the 1.35 eV PSCs, coupled with a stabilized PCE of 20.21%, which are the highest PCEs among all values reported to date for ideal-bandgap (1.3–1.4 eV) Sn–Pb binary PSCs. In addition, the device has a record small   $  V_{oc}  $   deficit of 0.33 V. Furthermore, the GABr-modified PSCs exhibit significantly improved environmental and thermal stabilities without encapsulation, which can maintain over 85% of their original PCEs after being stored in air (humidity   $  \approx  $  50–60%) for 1000 h and retain over 90% of the initial efficiency after being heated at 80 °C in air (humidity   $  \approx  $  50–60%) for 24 h. This work manifests a noteworthy step toward the fabrication of highly efficient and stable ideal-bandgap Sn–Pb binary PSCs. We believe that GABr introduction in the Sn–Pb binary perovskite films can efficiently reduce the defect density caused by   $  Sn^{2+}  $   oxidation and enhance the   $  V_{oc}  $   of the devices. Our introducing strategy will offer a new direction for the fabrication of highly efficient and stable ideal-bandgap Sn–Pb binary PSCs.
