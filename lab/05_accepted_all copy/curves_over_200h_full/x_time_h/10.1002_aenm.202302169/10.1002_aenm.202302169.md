# Surface Cleaning and Passivation Strategy for Durable Inverted Formamidinium–Cesium Triiodide Perovskite Solar Cells

Jing Wang, Kongxiang Wang, Chenhui Zhang, Siyu Liu, Xiang Guan, Chunjun Liang,  $  ^{*}  $   Chun-Chao Chen,  $  ^{*}  $   and Fengxian Xie  $  ^{*}  $

Formamidinium-cesium triiodide (FA $ _x $ Cs $ _{1-x} $ PbI $ _3 $ ) perovskite exhibits excellent phase stability, making it the most promising candidate for commercial perovskite solar cell (PSC) applications, particularly those with inverted structures present a promising contribution to the field of perovskite production. However, this composition often forms small grain sizes and has a large number of defects and PbI $ _2 $  residues on its surface, which can damage device performance. In this study, a post-surface engineering strategy called the “clean-passivation” method is proposed to address the interfacial problem between the perovskite and the electron transport layer (ETL). This method significantly reduces surface and grain boundary defects and eliminates unreacted PbI $ _2 $ , resulting in suppressed iodine decomposition and ion migration during operation. As a result, an excellent power conversion efficiency of 24.27% with superior stability is achieved, as the unencapsulated device maintains 97.12% of its initial efficiency after 1500 h of continuous light soaking. Furthermore, this new surface clean-passivate strategy can be universally applied to other typical perovskite compositions.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. The Process of Sweeping Issue at Perovskite Surface

We first improve the crystallinity of FA/Cs perovskite by optimizing the content of   $  PbI_{2}  $  ,  $  ^{[35,36]}  $   and the device efficiency increases to 21.53% with 10%   $  PbI_{2}  $   excess (Figures S1–S4, Supporting Information). Through XRD and GI-XRD analysis, we find that excessive doping of   $  PbI_{2}  $   effectively enhances the crystallinity of perovskite films (Figures S2 and S3, Supporting Information). Additionally, it simultaneously reduces the internal lattice pressure, playing a favorable role in stabilizing the perovskite film. However, we also observe that these films exhibit varying degrees of   $  PbI_{2}  $   precipitation on the surface and grain boundary of the perovskite (Figures S3 and S4, Supporting Information), with device

hysteresis observed for all devices (Figure S5 and Table S1, Supporting Information). As the doping level of   $  PbI_{2}  $   increases, the efficiency difference of forward scan and reverse scan increases to 15.5%, indicating the presence of numerous issues on the perovskite surface.

To solve the interfacial problem, we propose a post-surface engineering strategy called the “clean-passivation” method. The schematic of process was shown in Figure 1. The first step of clean-passivation method is to use a multi-functional amino acid salt ApaCl with end groups of carboxyl (—COOH) and amino group (  $  —NH^{3+}  $  ) as an intermediary. The prepared perovskite films were treated by ApaCl/IPA solution, called “A” step. Dynamic spin coating of different concentrations of ApaCl on top of perovskite films, transferred to a heating plate at 100 °C for annealing to ensure sufficient contact and binding between residual   $  PbI_{2}  $   and ApaCl. Then, IPA is used to wash the perovskite surface for several times, called “W” step. To prove the necessity of the ApaCl salt in our method, we made   $  PbI_{2}  $   samples with IPA washing and ApaCl treated. According to Figure S6 (Supporting Information), there was no change in the absorbance of   $  PbI_{2}  $   film after only washing with IPA. However, the absorbance of ApaCl treated (covered and heating) sample drop clearly, proving that the thickness of   $  PbI_{2}  $   film could be attenuated by the ApaCl. This result can prove that the key to removing excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   on the surface of perovskite using the “Clean-Passivation” method is ApaCl.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and atomic force microscopy (AFM) reveal a clean FA/Cs perovskite surface induced by the clean-passivation method (Figure 2c,f). As a reference, the control film shows a large amount of unreacted  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  (the bright crystals) at the grain boundary (Figure 2a). After A step process, a striking appearance disappears of most  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $  nanoclusters on the film surface. The device structure is shown in Figure 3a. Figure 3b shows the  $ J-V $  characteristics of the champion PSCs measured using both reverse and forward scan directions. While the cell using the 10% PbI $ _{2} $  displays a clear hysteresis, as shown in Figure 3b, it is surprising to see that the  $ J-V $  hysteresis is eliminated when the device employs clean-passivation treatment and the high efficiency of 24.27% is achieved. Finally, the PCEs from the reverse and forward scan directions are 24.27% and 24.02%, respectively. # 3. Conclusion

In summary, an effective process to solve the problems of perovskite interface was demonstrated, a multifunctional amino acid salt ApaCl was used. The principle is that the   $  -NH_{2}  $   group in the amino acid salt can interact with   $  I^{-}  $  , -COOH can interact with   $  Pb^{2+}  $   at the same time, then wash the product and residual passivator with solvent IPA. Simultaneously the ApaCl can passivation surface and grain boundary of perovskite film, thus we obtain a clean and passivated perovskite surface. With clean-passivation process, the PCE is further improved to 24.27%   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.17 V,   $  J_{SC}  $   of 24.84 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and FF of 83.49%, which is a impressive PCE of inverted   $  FA_{x}Cs_{1-x}PbI_{3}  $  , as showed in Table S7 (Supporting information). More importantly, the hysteresis is obviously suppressed to 1% from 16%. We further studied the stability of the devices by this process. The light stability is significantly promoted by removing the excess   $  PbI_{2}  $   from the surface. The efficiency remains 97.12% from original performance for MPPT after 1500 h. In addition, with   $  \approx90\%  $   of their original PCEs were retained under either 85 °C or air ambient conditions after 500 h, respectively. These results from clean-passivation progress are instructive for accelerating the commercialization of perovskite solar cells.
