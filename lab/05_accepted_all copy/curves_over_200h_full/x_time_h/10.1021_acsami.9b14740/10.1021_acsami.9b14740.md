# Inorganic  $ \mathrm{CuFeO_2} $  Delafossite Nanoparticles as Effective Hole Transport Material for Highly Efficient and Long-Term Stable Perovskite Solar Cells

Seckin Akin  $  ^{a,b,c}  $  , Faranak Sadegh  $  ^{d}  $  , Servet Turan  $  ^{c}  $  , Savas Sonmezoglu  $  ^{a,b,*}  $

$ ^{a} $  Nanotechnology R&D Laboratory, Karamanoglu Mehmetbey University, Karaman, Turkey  
 $ ^{b} $  Department of Metallurgical and Materials Engineering, Karamanoglu Mehmetbey University, Karaman, Turkey  
 $ ^{c} $  Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Eskisehir Technical University, Eskisehir, Turkey  
 $ ^{d} $  Department of Chemistry, Catalysis Division, University of Isfahan, Isfahan 81746-73441, Iran

# Abstract

The regular architecture (n-i-p) of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) has an ascendant interest in the renewable energy field, owing to high certified efficiencies in the recent years. However, there are still serious obstacles of PSCs associated with spiro-OMeTAD hole transport material (HTM), such as: i-) prohibitively expensive material cost ( $ \sim 150 - 500 $  \$/g) and ii-) operational instability at elevated temperatures and high humidity levels. Herein, we have reported the highly photo-, thermal- and moisture-stable and cost-effective PSCs employing inorganic  $ \mathrm{CuFeO_2} $  delafossite nanoparticles as HTM layer, for the first time. By exhibiting superior hole mobility and additive-free nature, the best-performing cell achieved a power conversion efficiency ( $ PCE $ ) of  $ 15.6\% $  with a negligible hysteresis. Despite exhibiting the lower  $ PCE $  as compared to spiro-OMeTAD-based control cell ( $ 19.1\% $ ), non-encapsulated  $ \mathrm{CuFeO_2} $ -based cells maintained above  $ 85\% $  of their initial efficiency, while the  $ PCE $  of control cells dropped to  $ \sim 10\% $  under continuous illumination at maximum power point (MPP) tracking after  $ 1000\mathrm{h} $ . More importantly, the performance of control cells was quickly degraded at above  $ 70^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  whereas  $ \mathrm{CuFeO_2} $ -based cells, retaining  $ \sim 80\% $  of their initial efficiency after  $ 200\mathrm{h} $ , was highly stable even at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $  in ambient air under dark conditions. Besides appearing on significant improvement in stability against light soaking and thermal stress,  $ \mathrm{CuFeO_2} $ -based cells exhibited superior shelf stability even at  $ 80 \pm 5\% $  relative humidity and retained over  $ 90\% $  of their initial  $ PCE $ . Overall, we strongly believe that this study highlights the potential of inorganic HTMs for the commercial deployment of long-term stable and low-cost PSCs.

# Keywords

Perovskite solar cell (PSC), Inorganic hole transport material (HTM),  $ \mathrm{CuFeO_2} $  delafossite nanoparticles, Operational stability, Thermal and moisture stability.

# INTRODUCTION

Over the past few years, organic-inorganic metal halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have received a considerable attention owing to their photo-physical properties including high absorption coefficient ( $ >10^{5} $  cm $ ^{-1} $ ), large diffusion length ( $ \sim $ 1  $ \mu $ m), and high charge carrier mobility ( $ >100 $  cm $ ^{2} $ /Vs) $ ^{1-3} $ , resulting in an impressive power conversion efficiency ( $ PCE $ ) over 25% $  ^{4}  $ . In the majority of PSCs, TiO $ _{2} $  as electron transport material (ETM) and spiro-OMeTAD as hole transport material (HTM) have been employed in the normal (n-i-p) device configuration $ ^{5-8} $ . However, such organic based HTMs are fairly expensive and instable under light soaking, thermal stress and/or humid environment rendering the cost and limiting the lifetime of the device $ ^{9-14} $ . The cost problem of organic HTMs comes from complex synthesis conditions whereas instability issue is due to the hygroscopic additives inside such as lithium and/or cobalt salts. Spiro-OMeTAD in bare form has relatively low hole mobility and conductivity for an efficient charge transfer. However, in the presence of additives, it becomes hydrophilic as well as permeable to metal electrode diffusion into perovskite layer, causing an irreversible degradation $ ^{5,7,15} $ . Furthermore, these additives result in a considerable decline in glass-transition temperature (T $ _{g} $ ) of the current HTM, promoting ease of crystallization $ ^{16-17} $ . All of these are actually obstacles that trigger each other like dominoes. Therefore, these challenges need to be addressed by finding cost-effective HTM alternatives, while maintaining reasonable efficiency and simultaneously enhancing the long-term stability.

For transferring photo-generated holes from perovskite to metal electrode, a decent HTM should meet some requirements such as appropriate ionization energy near or just above the valence band maximum (VBM) of perovskite, high hole mobility, high electrical conductivity and low-cost synthesis for large-scale applications  $  ^{18-22}  $  . Regarding these parameters, the use of inorganic HTMs could be one of the most promising approaches to tussle the cost and instability issues  $  ^{16, 23}  $  . Although different inorganic HTMs have been reported so far  $  ^{9, 11, 18, 24-26}  $  , very few HTMs could simultaneously exhibit the advantages of superior electrical properties and long-term thermal and operational stability. However, some of these inorganic HTMs are processed in toxic and/or high polarity of solvents that can easily deteriorate perovskite film  $  ^{26-27}  $  . For instance, a promising efficiency of over 20% has been obtained in a regular architecture employing CuSCN by inserting a reduced graphene oxide (rGO) as a spacer layer between CuSCN and Au contact  $  ^{10}  $  . Recently, eco-friendly delafossite structures, which are member of ternary metal oxide (TMO) nanoparticles, with desirable electrical properties and proper low-lying VBM have been investigated as promising hole-conductors.  $  ^{28-31}  $   Firstly, Zhang et al. reported the utilization of inorganic   $  CuGaO_{2}  $  -based HTM in normal configuration and obtained a substantial PCE of 18.5% and an extensive shelf-stability under ambient conditions  $  ^{11}  $  . Very recently, we reported the similar results by employing hydrothermally synthesized   $  CuCrO_{2}  $   nanoparticles as HTM in PSCs  $  ^{9}  $  . Our   $  CuCrO_{2}  $   HTM-based cells achieved 16.7% efficiency with negligible hysteresis. More importantly,   $  CuCrO_{2}  $   HTM showed a significant improvement in shelf and operational photo-stability compared with spiro-OMeTAD HTM. However, these stable PSCs using inorganic HTMs when subjected to the realistic operational conditions, i.e., light soaking and thermal/moisture stress, still remain an uncertainty. Comparing with other Cu-based delafossite materials, particularly,   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   delafossite materials employing as photocathode material for photoelectrochemical water reduction and solar fuel production applications  $  ^{32-34}  $   lead to many perfect performances, such a wide optical band gap, high hole mobility, proper valence band edge position with superior chemical and thermal stability  $  ^{32, 35-36}  $  . Despite the appearing a good candidate as alternative HTM in PSCs, surprisingly, there is no study about the use of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   as HTM in PSCs. Therefore, for the first time, we attempted to utilize stable and low-cost   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   as an efficient inorganic HTM in PSCs. We speculate that such material has contributed long-term stability and photovoltaic performance in PSCs.

In this report, we propose the use of hydrothermally-processed inorganic   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   delafossite material as an efficient inorganic HTM for cost-effective and highly stable perovskite devices. The optimized condition led to a comparable efficiency owing to the higher   $  J_{SC}  $   relative to spiro-OMeTAD HTM-based control cell. The best-performing PSC employing   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   HTM showed a PCE of 15.6% with a   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.01 V,   $  J_{SC}  $   of 23.6 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and FF equals to 0.65. More importantly, the cell with   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   HTM indicates the excellent photo-, thermal- and moisture-stability compare to the cell with spiro-OMeTAD HTM. Photo-stability of the PSCs has been tested under continuous irradiation with full solar intensity and maximum power point (MPP) tracking with a nitrogen flow over 1000 hours. Thermal stability has been carried out over 200 hours at different temperatures (60 °C, 70 °C, and 85 °C) under dark conditions in ambient air whereas moisture stability has been tested at various

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Crystallographic information of the as-synthesized   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   was acquired by X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis and obtained pattern was displayed in Fig. 1a. The most intense peak located at   $  35.4^{\circ}  $   is assigned to the (012) crystal plane. Other distinct peaks are assigned to the (006), (104), (009), (018), and (113) crystal planes. The annular position of the corresponding peaks well-matches with the standard pattern of rhombohedral delafossite   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   with JCPDS card no 12-0752. Notably, no additional peaks arising from impurity/secondary phases were observed, indicating a pure phase.

To evaluate the crystal size of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $  , the Scherrer equation was applied to the (012), (006) and (009) planes, the average crystallite sizes were determined as 74.4, 10.9, and 11.5 nm, respectively. This inconsistency in different planes indicates an asymmetric geometry of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $  , promoting smaller (thinner) crystals along the c-axis. This phenomenon has been confirmed by transmission electron microscopy (TEM) image and displayed in Fig. 1b. It could be seen that   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   nanoparticles have an average diameter of   $  \sim89  $   nm with a nanoplate-like morphology (Fig. S1). Similarly, high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) image in Fig. 1c revealed an excellent crystallinity with a lattice fringe with interplanar distances approximately 2.88 Å, which can be assigned to the (006) crystal plane of the delafossite   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   structure. Moreover, the surface topography analysis of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   nanoplates on glass substrate indicated a slightly high roughness (RMS) value of   $  \sim21  $   nm (Fig. 1d), which may not be conducive a good contact on perovskite layer.

The elemental chemical states and surface compositions of the as-synthesized   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   were examined by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) study (Fig. S2). The survey spectrum shows the only Cu, Fe, O, and C at the surfaces, indicating the formation of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $  . Clearly,  $ \mathrm{CuFeO_2} $  exhibited a superior hole mobility of  $ 5.9 \times 10^{-1} \mathrm{~cm}^2 \mathrm{~V}^{-1} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} $ , which is much higher than that of spiro-OMeTAD in the presence of additives ( $ 6.2 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~cm}^2 \mathrm{~V}^{-1} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} $ ). To test the hole extraction ability of a new HTM, the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   was calculated by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS). The HOMO level of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   was estimated as -5.13 eV (Fig. 1f), according to the equation reported in our previous works  $  ^{5,9}  $  . This level matches well with HOMO level of   $  Cs_{0.05}(MA_{0.15}FA_{0.85})_{0.95}\mathrm{Pb(I_{0.85}Br_{0.15})_{3}}  $   perovskite (-5.50 eV), indicating an effective hole extraction. Moreover, the conduction band minimum (CBM) induced by bandgap (3.14 eV), shown in Fig. S3b, was determined to be -1.99 eV. This reveals a good electron selectivity through perovskite and   $  CuFeO_{2}  $  , resulting in a good device performance as depicted in Fig. 2a.

To elucidate the effect of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   as HTM, PSCs with the device configuration of FTO/c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $  /Cs  $  _{0.05}  $  (MA  $  _{0.15}  $  FA  $  _{0.85}  $  )  $  _{0.95}  $  Pb(I  $  _{0.85}  $  Br  $  _{0.15}  $  )  $  _{3}  $  /CuFeO  $  _{2}  $  /Au were assembled according to the model in Fig. 2b. Following our solvent-engineering process, we obtained a uniform and smooth perovskite film on the top of mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   scaffold (Fig. 2c) and subsequently deposited   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   (or spiro-OMeTAD) on perovskite layer by spin-coating. Fig. 2d displays the fully-coverage of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   layer on perovskite film. The corresponding perovskite film exhibited a typical perovskite XRD pattern and an absorption onset, indicating the successful formation of perovskite phase (Fig. S4). To investigate the thickness and interconnection behavior of various layers, we acquired the cross-sectional SEM images (Fig. 2e and 2f) of the full devices, which established the formation of   $  \sim  $  250-nm spiro-OMeTAD and   $  \sim  $  80-nm   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   layer sandwiched between   $  \sim  $  500-nm-thick perovskite and gold layers. Although a good inter-connection was established between other layers, small gaps associated from the large particle size can be obviously noticed between CFO and perovskite layers.

The scan-independent stabilized power output (SPO) is more reliable method to explore the performance of perovskite devices, rather than instantaneous J-V measurement. Therefore, we further carried out SPO curves of spiro-OMeTAD and   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   HTM-based cells, by probing at their maximum power point (MPP) under full-sun illumination over 150 s (Fig. 3c). Spiro-OMeTAD and   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   employing devices exhibited a steady-state efficiency of 18.6% and 15.4%, respectively, during the monitoring process. Remarkably, the stabilized PCEs measured with MPP-tracking are really close to the values obtained from J-V curves, suggesting low hysteretic behavior, which is further supported by the hysteresis measurements (Fig. 3d). The hysteresis index was estimated for spiro-OMeTAD and   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   employing PSCs as 0.07 and 0.02, respectively. It can be seen that   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   HTM-based cell exhibited less hysteresis, while the hysteresis for spiro-OMeTAD HTM-based cell was more pronounced. This result can be ascribed to the high hole mobility of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $ , referring an efficient extraction of charge carriers, as reported previously  $  ^{45}  $ .

The obtained curves are shown in Fig. S8. The high frequency arc seen in the Nyquist curve is attributed to the hole transport along the HTM and is expressed as   $  R_{HTM}  $  . On the other hand, the resistance values of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   and spiro-OMeTAD HTM-based cells at the low frequency arc, which is a measure of the resistance against recombination at perovskite/HTM/Au interfaces, called the charge recombination resistance (  $  R_{rec}  $  ), were calculated as 3.08 kΩ and 3.21 kΩ, respectively. In addition to the photovoltaic performance of PSCs, long-term stability is another indispensable criterion for large-scale commercial applications. Therefore, we comprehensively carried out the long-term stability tests in case of light soaking, thermal, and moisture stress. In this context, non-encapsulated cells based on spiro-OMeTAD and   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   HTMs were assessed at (MPP) tracking at full-sun illumination (100 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  ) under nitrogen atmosphere. After 1000 h,   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   HTM-based PSCs retained   $  >85\%  $   of their initial efficiency, while the PCE of spiro-OMeTAD HTM-based PSCs dropped to   $  \sim10\%  $   (Fig. 4a). A similar trend for spiro-OMeTAD can be found in the literature due to the penetration of very mobile lithium ions into perovskite layer by light soaking effect  $  ^{5,9}  $  .

Since the glass transition temperature (  $  T_{g}  $  ) of spiro-OMeTAD reduces significantly in the presence of additives, amorphous nature of spiro-OMeTAD can easily induce crystallization under the thermal stress  $  ^{7, 17, 49}  $  . Moreover, the transition of spiro-OMeTAD to the glassy-state upon heat-treatment resulting in a dramatic decrease in its hole mobility leads to the charge carrier transfer issues from perovskite to spiro-OMeTAD which is also responsible for thermal instability  $  ^{50}  $  . Additionally, thermal diffusion of ions (gold and lithium ions) and evaporation of volatile 4-tert-butylpyridine (tBP) additive, commonly used in spiro-OMeTAD, from the device during heat treatment at temperatures as low as 80 °C can be other degradation mechanisms upon thermal exposure  $  ^{51}  $  . In contrast, though a slight degradation of the cells was monitored after the thermal stress at 85 °C,   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   employing PSCs displayed much better thermal stability under the same conditions. Specifically, the performance of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   HTM-based PSCs maintained   $  \sim  $  80% of their initial value after 200 h. This behavior of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   layer can be attributed to the additive-free inorganic nature which behaves as a diffusion barrier on perovskite layer.

# CONCLUSIONS

In this study, we successfully synthesized   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   nanoparticles by low-temperature hydrothermal method and employed as HTM in PSCs with n-i-p structure. In the presence of   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   HTM, a comparable efficiency of 15.6% has been achieved with negligible hysteresis. More importantly,   $  CuFeO_{2}  $   employing cells exhibited an admissible photo-stability

Unless specified otherwise, all of the chemicals in these experiments were purchased from Alfa Aesar or Sigma-Aldrich with analytical grade, and used without further purification.
