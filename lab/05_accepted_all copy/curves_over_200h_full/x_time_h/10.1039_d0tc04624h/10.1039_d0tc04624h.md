# A dual promotion strategy of interface modification and ion doping for efficient and stable carbon-based planar   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite solar cells  $  ^{\dagger}  $

Chenyang Wang, $ ^{a} $  Yongjin Long, $ ^{a} $  Xiaohui Liu, $ ^{id*^{a}} $  Shiqiang Fu, $ ^{a} $  Jiahao Wang, $ ^{a} $  Jing Zhang, $ ^{a} $  Ziyang Hu $ ^{id^{a}} $  and Yuejin Zhu $ ^{*ab} $

Carbon-based all-inorganic cesium lead bromide (CsPbBr $ _3 $ ) halide perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted tremendous attention owing to their low cost, simplified preparation, and outstanding stability even under harsh conditions. However, the CsPbBr $ _3 $  perovskite always suffers from an undesirable crystallization and film morphology with incomplete coverage and numerous grain boundaries. Herein, a novel dual promotion strategy combining interface modification with ion doping is proposed to achieve highly efficient and stable carbon-based planar CsPbBr $ _3 $  PSCs. A thin CsBr modified layer was first inserted between the compact TiO $ _2 $  (c-TiO $ _2 $ ) and PbBr $ _2 $  layers, wherein the crystallization of PbBr $ _2 $  can be enhanced and then assist the growth of perovskite grains can be provided, so that CsPbBr $ _3 $  films can reduce pinhole generation and accelerate carrier extraction, and decrease the hysteresis effect. Then an appropriate cobalt (Co $ ^{2+} $ ) ion is doped into the CsPbBr $ _3 $  film, which not only increases the grain size with decreasing grain boundary, but also ameliorates the energy level and recombination. Consequently, the champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the CsPbBr $ _3 $  PSCs reaches up to 8.67% with an increment of about 30%. Moreover, the best-performing PSC without encapsulation also displays excellent humidity and thermal stability, maintaining above 98% of its initial PCE in an air atmosphere after 100 days, and over 95% under heat treatment at 85 °C after 600 hours, respectively. This study provides a promising route to boost the performance of all-inorganic perovskite solar cells.

# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have exhibited unprecedented development and have been widely recognized as promising next-generation photovoltaic technology due to their outstanding photoelectric properties, low-cost processing techniques, and abundant raw materials.  $  ^{1-3}  $   To date, their certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) has exceeded 25%.  $  ^{4}  $   However, high-efficiency PSCs are always fabricated with organic–inorganic hybrid perovskites as active absorber materials, and their long-term stability is seriously limited by the volatility of organic components (MA  $  ^{+}  $  , FA  $  ^{+}  $  , etc.), especially under the evaluated environment or illuminated conditions.  $  ^{5-7}  $   In comparison,

substituting organic cations with inorganic cesium ions (Cs $  ^{+}  $ ) to form all-inorganic cesium lead halide (CsPbX $  _{3}  $ ). PSCs is an effective approach to address the stability issues.  $  ^{8-10}  $  In particular, compared to the iodine-containing halides including CsPbI $  _{3}  $ , CsPbI $  _{2}  $ Br, and CsPbIBr $  _{2}  $ , the full-brominated CsPbBr $  _{3}  $  perovskite presents superior moisture and thermal tolerances even under harsh conditions, making it the ideal light-harvesting absorber for stable all-inorganic PSCs.  $  ^{11-14}  $  Also, planar all-inorganic CsPbBr $  _{3}  $  PSCs with a typical configuration of FTO/c-TiO $  _{2}  $ /CsPbBr $  _{3}  $ /carbon free of precious hole transporting materials (HTMs) and noble metal electrodes have attracted extensive attention due to their cost-effective and simplified fabrication process.  $  ^{15-17}  $  However, the PCE outputs of typical CsPbBr $  _{3}  $  PSCs are generally low, mainly originating from the intrinsic narrow light absorbance ranges, and inferior film quality with undesirable crystallization and morphology, which cause severe charge recombination at interfaces and within the perovskite film by the defect states.  $  ^{18}  $

# Experimental

# Materials

$ \mathrm{PbBr}_2 $  (99%), CsBr (99%) and  $ \mathrm{CoBr}_2 $  (99%) were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp.  $ N,N $ -dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.9%), isopropanol (IPA, 99.5%), and methanol (99.9%) were purchased from Aladdin. Unless stated otherwise, all materials and reagents were used without further purification. Commercial carbon paste was purchased from Guangzhou Saidi.

# Fabrication of compact  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  (c-TiO $ _2 $ )

In detail, 0.5 mol isopropyl titanate and 0.5 mol ethylene glycol amine were dissolved in 1 mL IPA. Then it will be annealed in air at 450 °C for 1 hour. After that, the c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  substrate was immersed in 40 mM  $  TiCl_{4}  $  aqueous solution at 70 °C for 30 min. Finally, the treated c-  $  TiO_{2}  $  substrate was cleaned with ethanol and annealed once in air at 500 °C for 1 hour.

# Fabrication of solar cells

Fluorine-doped Tin Oxide glass (FTO, Nippon, 14 Ω sq  $  ^{-1}  $  ) was etched with zinc powder and dilute hydrochloric acid before the preparation of solar cell devices. The PSCs adopted a plane heterojunction structure: FTO/c-TiO  $  _{2}  $  /perovskite/carbon. FTOs with a size of 2.5 × 2.5 cm  $  ^{2}  $   were successively cleaned using a detergent, ethanol, and UV-ozone treatment. After UV-ozone treatment for 15 min, the c-TiO  $  _{2}  $   layer with a thickness of 40 nm was deposited on the FTO substrate by spin-coating at 4000 rpm for 30 s, and sintered for 30 min at 450 °C. Then, the substrates were treated with 40 mM TiCl  $  _{4}  $   aqueous solution at 70 °C for 30 min. After washing with deionized water and drying out, the substrate is again sintered at 500 °C for 30 min.

The perovskite film was made by a multi-step solution-processed method. All operations were carried out in an air atmosphere without temperature and humidity control. Before spinning the solution of   $  PbBr_{2}  $  , a low concentration CsBr solution (1 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , 2.5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $   and 5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  ) was spin-coated onto the   $  TiO_{2}  $   substrate at 4000 rpm for 30 s, followed by heating at 90 °C on a hot plate for 5 min. Then, 1.0 M   $  PbBr_{2}  $   solution in DMF was spin-coated onto a substrate at 2000 rpm for 30 s. After drying at 90 °C for 1 h, the 0.07 M solution of CsBr methanol was spin-coated on the   $  PbBr_{2}  $   film at 2000 rpm for 30 s and annealed at 250 °C for 5 min. This process was repeated 4 times. The obtained perovskite film was rinsed with isopropanol and dried at 250 °C for 5 min. Finally, a carbon black electrode was deposited by a doctor blade method and

annealed at   $  90\ ^{\circ}C  $   for 30 min. A   $  Co^{2+}  $   doped   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite was fabricated by adding a certain proportion of   $  CoBr_{2}  $   to   $  PbBr_{2}  $   solution. The remaining processes were the same as the above-mentioned procedures.

# Characterization

The surface morphology of the membrane and the cross-sectional morphology of the device were detected by using a Hitachi SU-70 scanning electron microscope (SEM), which was combined with the energy dispersive X-ray spectrum (EDS) to detect the element content and distribution. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of FTO-based films were scanned using a Bruker D8 advance instrument using Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda = 6.162 \AA  $  ). Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) was obtained using a fluorescence spectrophotometer (Agilent, USA). The time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) spectra of perovskite films on glass were measured by using a PL spectrometer (Horiba, Japan) with an excitation wavelength of 510 nm. The absorption spectra of films on substrates were determined using a UV-vis spectrophotometer (Cary). The chemical states of the ions were tested by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Kratos Axis Ultra DLD) using Al Kα radiation and the C 1s line at 284.6 eV for spectral correction. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) patterns were measured using Axis Ultra DLD and He I (21.22 eV) emission lines. The surface flatness of the film was measured with a Kelvin Probe Force Microscope (KPFM, Asylum Research Cypher, UK). The characteristic J-V curves, maximum power output (MPP) tracking test and the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) were measured using a Keithley 4200 meter and a sunlight simulator (Newport, 91192A, AM 1.5, 1 sun). The solar cell is covered with a black hole to define a mobile area of 0.07 square centimeters. The transient photocurrent (TPC) decay and transient photo-voltage (TPV) decay were recorded using an electrochemical workstation (Zahner, Germany). The white LED supplied 80 mW cm  $  ^{-2}  $  light intensity to excite the PSCs. The incident-photo-conversion-efficiency (IPCE) of the device was tested using a Newport EQE system in the wavelength range of 300–800 nm.

# Results and discussion

In this study, a high quality   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite film was induced by adding low concentration CsBr by a modified multi-step solution spin coating method due to the large difference between CsBr and   $  PbBr_{2}  $   concentrations.  $  ^{26,36}  $   The specific details of device fabrication were described in the Experimental section. In the first place, we systematically investigated the effect of a thin CsBr layer on the crystallization and morphology of the   $  PbBr_{2}  $   layer, as well as the influence on the film quality of the final   $  CsPbBr_{3}  $   perovskite. $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  film surface has a large number of pinholes (Fig. 1a). The reason for this phenomenon is relative to the viscosity of  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  solution and the rapid evaporation of solvent. After being modified by  $ 1\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  CsBr, the  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  film is more uniform and its continuity is greatly improved (Fig. 1b). With a further increase in CsBr concentration to  $ 2.5\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $ , the  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  film has better morphology and fewer pinholes, which were conducive to the formation of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  perovskite. However, when excessive CsBr ( $ 5\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $ ) is introduced, the number of pinholes decreases, but the size of the pinholes increases. Several previous studies have shown that the disgusting pinholes and large grain boundaries degrade the performance of the device because these defects hinder carrier diffusion and lead to serious charge recombination.[39-41] Certainly, the underneath  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  layer may make a direct influence on the film quality of the upper perovskite layer. Thus, to verify the increase of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  perovskite grain size, the SEM images of pristine  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  and modified  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  films with different concentrations of CsBr are observed and presented in Fig. 1e-h. Although the pinholes on the surface of the pristine  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  are significantly lower than those on the surface of the original  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $ , there were still a large number of voids (Fig. 1e). With modification by CsBr, the coverage of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  films was significantly enhanced. When the CsBr concentration was  $ 1\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $ , the pinholes were distinctly eliminated. After being modified with  $ 2.5\mathrm{mgmL}^{-1} $  CsBr, the as-prepared  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  perovskite film exhibited a compact and homogenous morphology with greatly increased grain sizes, which helps to reduce the recombination of charge carriers.[42] After excessive CsBr modification, the morphology of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  films remained intact, but the grain size decreased obviously (Fig. 1h). This may be due to the large surface pores of the  $ \mathrm{PbBr_2} $  film modified by excessive CsBr, which limits the further growth of the  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  perovskite and thus limits the grain size.

In order to study the effect of CsBr on thin film crystallization, XRD measurement was carried out. XRD patterns of the as-prepared  $ \mathrm{PbBr}_2 $  films are depicted in Fig. 2a. When the  $ \mathrm{PbBr}_2 $  film is modified by CsBr with an appropriate concentration

of 2.5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , the diffraction peaks at 16.7°, 21.6°, 33.9° and 39.5° assigned to the (110), (020), (201) and (031) planes of PbBr  $  _{2}  $   (PDF#31-0679) are increased, indicating that the crystallinity of PbBr  $  _{2}  $   is improved after CsBr modification. To further explore the existence of the CsBr interlayer, XRD tests were carried out on PbBr  $  _{2}  $   modified by CsBr with higher concentrations, as shown in Fig. S1 (in the ESI†). When the concentration of CsBr increased to 10 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , a weak peak of CsPb  $  _{2}  $  Br  $  _{5}  $   was detected from the XRD image. This means that since the optimal modification concentration is 2.5 mg mL  $  ^{-1}  $  , even if the CsPb  $  _{2}  $  Br  $  _{5}  $   phase is generated, it is hard to be detected by an instrument due to low concentration. Therefore, we conclude that the CsBr modifier does not exist in the device as a single layer, but assists in the formation of the PbBr  $  _{2}  $   modifier to achieve better device performance.

It is well known that the trapstate density  $ (N_{\mathrm{t}}) $  can be determined via the SCLC model by utilizing single-charge carrier devices. Generally, the first linear stage of the curve is Ohm response under low bias and the next linear phase of the curve is a trap-filling limit (TFL) region. # Conclusions

In summary, a novel dual promotion strategy combining the interface modification with ion doping is developed for HTM-free carbon-based  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr_3} $  all-inorganic PSCs in an air atmosphere. Firstly, the interface modification was conducted by inserting a CsBr thin film between the  $ \mathrm{TiO_2} $  and perovskite layers. The CsBr modifier could reduce the pinholes of the

$ \mathrm{PbBr}_2 $  film, thus further benefiting the formation of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  with better coverage and uniform morphology, resulting in a relatively enhanced device performance, while the PSCs still yield a limited  $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $  value. Then, we further improved the photovoltaic performance of  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  PSCs via doping appropriate concentrations of  $ \mathrm{CoBr}_2 $  into the perovskite films. The high quality perovskite films with improved crystal morphology, favorable energy levels, reduced trap states and charge recombination were obtained. Consequently, the  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 - 0.2\% \mathrm{Co} $  PSC realizes the highest PCE of  $ 8.67\% $ , obtaining an enhancement by  $ 30\% $  in comparison with  $ 6.68\% $  for the  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  device. Moreover, the optimized  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 - 0.2\% \mathrm{Co} $  PSC without encapsulation also displays excellent humidity stability and thermal stability, by keeping above  $ 98\% $  of its initial PCE after 100 days in an ambient atmosphere, and over  $ 95\% $  after 600 hours under the continuous heat treatment at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , respectively. This study provides a promising route to boost the performance of all-inorganic  $ \mathrm{CsPbBr}_3 $  PSCs.
