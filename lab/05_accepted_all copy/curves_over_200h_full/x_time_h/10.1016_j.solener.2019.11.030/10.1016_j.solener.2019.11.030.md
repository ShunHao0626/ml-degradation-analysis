# 1. Introduction

The organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs), emerged as one of the most promising photovoltaic technology, have attracted enormous attention due to the excellent optoelectronic properties of the perovskite absorber, such as high absorption coefficient, long carrier diffusion length, high mobility and so on (Jeon et al., 2015; Green et al., 2014). The certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the resulting PSCs has rapidly boosted to over  $ 23\% $  from  $ 3.8\% $  in less than ten years (Heo et al., 2015a, 2015b; Kojima et al., 2009; Jiang et al., 2019). Planar perovskite device with the p-i-n architecture have advantage over traditional mesoporous architecture for its low-temperature fabrication processing, which exhibits particular superiority in future large-scale commercialization (Yang et al., 2018; Zhou et al., 2014).

# ABSTRACT

Organic-inorganic perovskite solar cells emerge as one of the most promising photovoltaic technology due to its high performances. Particularly, inverted perovskite device architecture, due to low temperature processing, have a great potential in commercialization. High-crystalline quality perovskite film and interfacial passivation are essential to yield high performance devices. In this work, we employ a simple strategy of using molybdenum disulfide (MoS $ _2 $ ) as both the interfacial layer and the additive to prepare efficient PSCs. MoS $ _2 $  as an additive in perovskite can form the CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ :MoS $ _2 $  heterostructure, resulting in the homogeneous perovskite film with larger crystal grains. In addition, MoS $ _2 $  as the buffer layer (BL) between poly (3,4-ethylene dioxythiophene)-poly(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) and perovskite can prevent the decomposition of perovskite film by avoiding the direct contact with the hydrophilic PEDOT:PSS films. On tedious optimization, the champion device based on active layer of CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ PbI $ _3 $ :MoS $ _2 $  (10 v%) as well as employing MoS $ _2 $  buffer layer shows a remarkable improvement in the power conversion efficiency (PCE) (from 15.29% to 18.31%) and a better stability, with 87% of the initial efficiency sustained after 20 days. Our finding herein provides a promising way to fabricate high efficiency and stable photovoltaic devices.

On the other hand, the optimization of precursor stoichiometric ratio or regulation of annealing conditions represent the most popular approaches to improve the film quality towards high performance PSCs (Luo et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2018a,b). Moreover, hindering the decomposition of perovskite film is also essential to guarantee the film quality (Akbulatov et al., 2017). Bearing this in mind, it is urgent to establish a method which not only improve the charge collection efficiency of HTLs/ETLs, but also promote the quality of the perovskite film. Two-dimensional MoS  $  _{2}  $  exhibits outstanding physical properties such as appropriate intrinsic band gap and remarkable optical properties, which is widely applied on the optoelectronic devices (Fan et al., 2015; Peng et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2018). The   $  MoS_{2}  $  /based hole extraction interlayer and   $  MoS_{2}  $  - based mixed HTLs in PSCs with the PCE of about 17% have been reported (Kakavelakis et al., 2018; Dai et al., 2017). In the inverted PSCs with PEDOT:PSS as the HTLs, the hydrophilic and acidic nature of PEDOT:PSS are the reasons hindering the high performances. Therefore, a buffer layer acting to the PEDOT:PSS is needed to modify the HTLs/perovskite interface. Furthermore, the introduction of additives into perovskite can effectively promote the quality of active layer (Jiang et al., 2018). Chiang et al. introduced the PCBM as additives into perovskite layer to form the perovskite-PCBM bulk heterojunction, promoting the PSCs performances with PCE of 12.1% (Chiang and Wu, 2016).   $  MoS_{2}  $  , for its similar lattice fringe with   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , indicates a perfect lattice-matching between   $  MoS_{2}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  , which demonstrates the potential of   $  MoS_{2}  $   as additives incorporated into perovskite (Ning et al., 2015; Lee et al., 2014; Almosni et al., 2016). Accordingly, since the limited report about the   $  MoS_{2}  $   as additives being introduced into perovskite, it motivates us to explore use of   $  MoS_{2}  $   as a buffer layer at the PEDOT:PSS/perovskite interface and as additives in perovskite layer to enhance the performances of resulting PSCs.

In this work, we report a simple method to improve crystalline and intrinsic quality of the perovskite film by introducing solution processed   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanoparticles that are less than 100 nm in PSCs as the   $  MoS_{2}  $   interfacial electron blocking layer and the additive. Taking into account the band alignment of   $  MoS_{2}  $   with   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  ,   $  MoS_{2}  $   BL is deposited on PEDOT:PSS films by spin-coating with the DMF dispersion of   $  MoS_{2}  $   (about 0.1 mg/mL).   $  MoS_{2}  $   BL effectively blocks the electrons reaching PEDOT:PSS layer and reduces the recombination of electrons and holes at the PEDOT:PSS/  $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   interface. At the same time,   $  MoS_{2}  $   nanoparticles is added into perovskite precursor solution with different volume ratios (volume of   $  MoS_{2}  $   dispersion liquid versus the volume of the DMF for dissolving perovskite precursors). It is observed that the incorporation of   $  MoS_{2}  $   into perovskite can lead to the improvement of film crystalline. On tedious optimization of the content of   $  MoS_{2}  $   inside perovskite and simultaneous addition of   $  MoS_{2}  $   as the BL, the champion device with a PCE of 18.31% is obtained. Furthermore, the   $  MoS_{2}  $   BL and the larger crystal grains of perovskite film can prevent water prolonging the lifetime of perovskite films. Under continuous 1 sun illumination, the device based on   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   (10 v%) film and   $  MoS_{2}  $   BL maintain 87% of the initial PCE after 20 days storage in air.

Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) of the pristine perovskite films deposited on PEDOT:PSS or PEDOT:PSS/MoS  $  _{2}  $  are shown in Fig. 2(c). The lower PL intensity of perovskite film with MoS  $  _{2}  $  BL, comparing with that of without MoS  $  _{2}  $  BL, indicates the effectively PL quenching caused by MoS  $  _{2}  $  BL. It could be due to the energy level matching between PEDOT:PSS and perovskite film improving the charge extraction rate at the interface of HTM/perovskite, especially for the slightly gradient energy level (inset in Fig. 2(a)) at the interface of MoS  $  _{2}  $ /perovskite (Zhao et al., 2015). The Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) in Fig. 2(d) also indicates the enhanced charge extraction caused by MoS  $  _{2}  $  BL. For the perovskite film with MoS  $  _{2}  $  BL, its PL is quickly quenched with an exponential fitted lifetime of 34.54 ns, while the fitted lifetime of that film without MoS  $  _{2}  $  BL is 46.53 ns. As described above, since MoS  $  _{2}  $  BL can block the electrons and contribute to the lower recombination rate, the HTLs is more efficient in charge extraction for the perovskite film deposited on MoS  $  _{2}  $  layer (Dong et al., 2017).

Although  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  can promote the charge extraction, it exhibits little improvement in the photoelectric performances. Consequently, we incorporated  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  into the perovskite layer based on the utilization of  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  BL. Results show that the PSCs having  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $  additives and  $ \mathrm{MoS}_2 $

BL give rise to a further improved performance. Interestingly, when the volume ratio of   $  MoS_{2}  $   increases from 0 to 10 v%, the PCE enhances from 16.43% to 18.31%, together with Jsc and FF from 21.14 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and 77.4% to 22.61 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $   and 78.8%, respectively (Fig. 3(a) and Table 2). When the volume ratio of   $  MoS_{2}  $   sequentially increased to 20 v %, the PCE declined but is still higher than the devices without   $  MoS_{2}  $  . The champion device with   $  MoS_{2}  $   additives yields a higher PCE of 17.40%, while the device without   $  MoS_{2}  $   additives only yields the PCE of 15.36%. These J-V characteristics based on the various devices indicate that the   $  MoS_{2}  $   as additives assuredly improve the device properties, especially for the PCE. Compared to the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film, the steady-state PL peak intensity of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   (10 v%) film is quenched by ~84% (Fig. S11 a). The reason could be resulted from its larger crystal grains with less crystal boundaries that reduce the in-situ defects to facilitate charge transfer (Dong et al., 2016). By comparison, these minor changes indicate that   $  MoS_{2}  $   additives have little effect on the electronic structure of the perovskite film due to the micro doping ratio. This phenomenon is similar to the previous reports by Li et al. (2018). Then the normalized PL without any shift (Fig. S12 b) reconfirmed that the electronic structure of the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   (10 v%) film does not change significantly. Fig. 5(f) shows the PL decay curves of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   (10 v%) film deposited on PEDOT:PSS/  $  MoS_{2}  $ -coated ITO substrate. For the decay curves, the exponential decay process are related to trap-mediated nonradiative recombination and radiative recombination (Son et al., 2016). By comparison,   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   (10 v%) film exhibit a shortened average lifetime of 24.38 ns while   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film exhibits an average lifetime of 31.96 ns. The decrease of lifetime suggests the decay channels increase and thus promote the charge transfer from perovskite film to HTL layer (Jiang et al., 2016). What's more, as shown in Fig. S12 c, the photocurrent of the device based on   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   (10 v%) film is 1.08 μs, which decayed faster than that of 1.39 μs for the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film. The faster decay could be attributed to the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   bulk heterojunction structure which accelerates the separation of photo-generated excitons and promotes the transportation of charge carriers (Huang et al., 2017). The   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   (10 v%) based device shows a longer photovoltage decay of 3.11 μs compared to the 1.95 μs for the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   based device (Fig. S12 d), indicating the lower recombination rate and longer carrier lifetime (Pockett et al., 2015). In other words, for the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   (10 v%) film, the decrease in traps associated with the increased film crystallinity and the increase in carriers transfer channels associated with the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   bulk heterojunction significantly enhanced the charge transport process in the resulting devices. Of course, whether the increased crystallinity or the bulk heterojunction structure plays the predominant role in improving the device performances requires further investigation.

It is known that perovskite film is sensitive to water which can cause the structure damage leading to poor stability of PSCs. In the Fig. S13, we measure the water contact angle of the corresponding perovskite film to investigate the hydrophobicity of perovskite films surface. For the perovskite film containing   $  MoS_{2}  $   additives deposited on   $  MoS_{2}  $   layer, the water contact angle changes from 59.2° to 64.6°, showing a small increase in hydrophobicity of the film. In order to further verify the perovskite film stability, FESEM images of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   (10 v%) film are recorded and shown in Fig. 6(a-d). After the   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   (10 v%) film stored in air for 20 days, it

still shows a better morphology with the clear crystal grains while   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film shows a cloudy morphology with the punch-drunk small crystal grains. The better stability of   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   (10 v%) film can be attributed to the dense film with large crystal grains which can prevent water infiltration (Wang et al., 2017a,b). Meanwhile, as to the hydrophilic PEDOT:PSS layer (Zheng et al., 2019),   $  MoS_{2}  $   BL can effectively decrease the direct contact of PEDOT:PSS and perovskite layer to reduce the decomposition of perovskite film induced by water. Thus, the pristine   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film with   $  MoS_{2}  $   BL shows less morphology changes than that without   $  MoS_{2}  $   BL (Fig. S14). Moreover, for evaluating the devices stability, the unencapsulated devices are stored in a drying box with the constant temperature (20°C) and humidity (20%) under the dark conditions. Fig. 6e shows that the champion device based on   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   (10 v%) film with   $  MoS_{2}  $   BL maintains 87% of its initial performance, while the device based on pristine   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   film and without   $  MoS_{2}  $   BL has the 36% of initial PCE retention. All in all, it can come to the conclusion that the device based on   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}  $   (10 v%) film and with   $  MoS_{2}  $   BL has a better stability, due to the positive roles of   $  MoS_{2}  $   BL and   $  MoS_{2}  $   additives.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we demonstrate a facile process of using   $  MoS_{2}  $   as an buffer layer between PEDOT:PSS and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   layer as well as an additive inside   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   absorption layer, fabricating the inverted PSCs with configuration of Glass/ITO/PEDOT:PSS/  $  MoS_{2}/CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}:MoS_{2}/PCBM/Bphen/Ag  $  . The champion device prepared by this novel approach shows a recorded efficiency of 18.31%. Due to the close energy matching with   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $  ,   $  MoS_{2}  $   acting as a buffer layer, can block electrons transmission efficiently, reduce the recombination of holes and electrons at the interface of HTL/Perovskite film. The doping of   $  MoS_{2}  $   inside the active layer improved the quality of perovskite films with the dense arrangement of large crystal grains, which could be attributed to the heterogeneous nucleation triggered by the addition of   $  MoS_{2}  $   in the absorber layer. Meanwhile, the lattice matching of   $  MoS_{2}  $   and   $  CH_{3}NH_{3}PbI_{3}  $   can facilitate the charge transfer process from perovskite layer to HTL. These leads to the improved performance of the resulting PSCs. In addition, it can be observed that both the   $  MoS_{2}  $   buffer layer and the high quality perovskite film with larger crystal grains, can effectively suppress the degradation of active layer, resulting a better stability than reference ones. This work, for the first time, shows the efficacy of the use of interface engineering of   $  MoS_{2}  $   buffer layer as well as the simultaneous incorporating   $  MoS_{2}  $   into perovskite layer as an effective way to enhance the efficiency and stability of the inverted planar PSCs.

# 4.3. Characterizations

The top-view morphology and section-view morphology of the perovskite film and corresponding devices were characterized by a field-emission SEM (Quanta 200 FEG, FEI Co.). The EDS was conducted by using an EDS device connected to FESEM. The XRD patterns of perovskite films were recorded by a Rigaku D/MAX-2400 diffractometer. The atomic force microscope (AFM) images were characterized by an Agilent 5500 SPM system (Agilent Technologies, USA). The transient photocurrent (TPC) were recorded using 532 nm pulse laser to excite the devices and the transient photovoltage (TPV) were recorded by a digital oscilloscope (HP54810A).
