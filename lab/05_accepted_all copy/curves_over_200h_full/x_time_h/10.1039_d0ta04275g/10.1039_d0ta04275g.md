# Intermeshing Electron Transporting Layer for Efficient and Stable   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   Perovskite Solar Cells with Open Circuit Voltage over 1.3 V

Shuo Liu, $ ^{a} $  Weijie Chen, $ ^{a} $  Yunxiu Shen, $ ^{a} $  Shuhui Wang, $ ^{a} $  Moyao Zhang, $ ^{a} $  Yaowen Li, $ ^{*a} $  Yongfang Li $ ^{a,b} $

Inorganic   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite has gained great attention due to its outstanding overall stabilities and great potential for application in semitransparent and tandem solar cells. However, the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) are being limited by their severe energy loss (  $  E_{loss}  $  ) due to the unfavorable device interface and defects. Here, an intermeshing   $  SnO_{2}  $   (Im-  $  SnO_{2}  $  ) electron transporting layer (ETL) is subtly constructed by combining two types of   $  SnO_{2}  $   with complementary electronic/physical properties for suppressing the notorious   $  E_{loss}  $  . With this strategy, the defects of conventional   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL can be greatly improved, which could facilitate charge extracting, increase the crystallinity and orientation of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, construct a cascade energy level in the device, simultaneously. Consequently, the   $  E_{loss}  $   of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SC can be remarkably reduced to below 0.6 eV, delivering an excellent PCE of 16.10% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   as high as 1.31 V. To the best of our knowledge, this result is one of the few   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs that enable high   $  V_{oc}  $   without sacrificing efficiency. In addition, the devices show high stability under both strong UV irradiation for 300 h and an ambient atmosphere for 1000 h.

Keywords: intermeshing  $ \mathrm{SnO}_2 $ ; antireflection; energy loss; cascade energy level; inorganic perovskite solar cells

# Introduction

Recently, the organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (pero-SCs) gained unprecedented fast-rising power conversion efficiency (PCE).  $  ^{[1-3]}  $   Unfortunately, the intrinsically volatile organic cation components (such as formamidinium and methylammonium), which lead to poor photo and thermal stabilities, have been considered the biggest hurdle for their commercialization.  $  ^{[4-6]}  $   Therefore, developing all-inorganic perovskite materials by replacing the organic with an inorganic component, such as cesium (Cs  $  ^{+}  $  ), may fundamentally address the instability issues.  $  ^{[7-9]}  $

Among the various all-inorganic perovskite materials,   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   with a high Goldschmidt tolerance factor (0.84) has garnered extensive attention in terms of stability,  $  ^{[10]}  $   and its reasonably wide bandgap of 1.90 eV also shows great potential for application in semitransparent and tandem solar cells.  $  ^{[11, 12]}  $   The active layer material in each sub-cell of tandem solar cells should not only prevent PCE deterioration during the multiple layer-by-layer processes and post-treatments, such as thermal annealing;  $  ^{[13, 14]}  $

the related sub-cells also need to have high open-circuit voltage   $  (V_{\mathrm{oc}})  $  . [15, 16] While   $  V_{oc}  $   of the devices is limited by the severe energy loss   $  (E_{\mathrm{loss}} = E_{\mathrm{g}} - eV_{\mathrm{oc}})  $  . [17] As seen from the   $  E_{loss}  $   statistics of high-efficient Cs-based all-inorganic pero-SCs with different ratios of I to Br (Figure 1a and Table S1), the severe   $  E_{loss}  $  , generally in the range of 0.65-0.85 eV, is a common issue in all types of all-inorganic pero-SCs, where only a few devices achieved the   $  E_{loss}  $   values close to 0.6 eV. There are two possible reasons for the high   $  E_{loss}  $  . [18] One is that the prevailing trap-state density and poor morphology of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite films may cause severe non-radiative recombination. [19] To address this issue, many studies have been conducted to prepare a high-quality   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film by optimizing deposition methods [20, 21] or using ion doping [22-24] or by optimizing the wettability and roughness of deposition substrates. [25]

Interface defects and a mismatched energy level between the perovskite layer and the interface layer were also considered to be another critical factor influencing charge carrier recombination.  $  ^{[26, 27]}  $   Yang et al. found that the poly (3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT) hole transporting layer (HTL) with low energy disorder could not only reduce its trap states but could also more efficiently passivate the surface defects of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  , which promote the charge extraction and result in a high   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.30 V for the pero-SCs.  $  ^{[28]}  $   Sang et al. synthesized a series of HTL materials to fine-tune their highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels. The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs based on poly-indenofluoren-8-triarylamine (PIF8-TAA) HTL with a deeper-lying HOMO energy level showed a   $  V_{oc}  $   as high as 1.31 V.  $  ^{[29]}  $   Unfortunately, although these optimized devices

Recently, Yip et al. employed a dual interfacial engineering approach with respect to the energy levels of both HTL and ETL. The well-matched energy level alignment of the resultant device effectively weakened the charge transport barrier, leading to a low non-radiative recombination. As a result, the trade-off behavior between   $  V_{oc}  $   and   $  FF/J_{sc}  $   was effectively avoided and the device simultaneously realized a high   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.30 V and high PCE of 16.2%.  $  ^{[30]}  $   Although the strategy is successful, it requires precise simultaneous control of the dual interfacial layer. Nonetheless, we also found that the optimized   $  SnO_{2}  $   with PN4N can significantly boost the   $  V_{oc}  $   from 1.08 V to 1.24 V, and PCE is improved from 12.3% to 15.1%, which emphasized the significant influence of bottom ETL on the final device performance.

Numerous studies also revealed that the pero-SCs performance is very sensitive to the bottom interface properties, such as wettability, roughness, carrier mobility, and defects. For example, Huang et al. proposed that a high hydrophobic interface layer could suppress heterogeneous nucleation, facilitating the growth of perovskite film with large grain size.  $  ^{[31]}  $   Zhang et al. claimed that the low-roughness substrates benefit a smooth-surface perovskite film.  $  ^{[32]}  $   Therefore, the underneath ETL in the n-i-p pero-SCs plays a critical role in the sequent perovskite film quality. Among the various ETL materials,   $  TiO_{2}  $   still dominated the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $  -based devices.  $  ^{[24]}  $   Unfortunately, the photonic instability and the high-temperature annealing process of   $  TiO_{2}  $   hamper its further application in pero-SCs.   $  SnO_{2}  $   with high conductivity and fewer oxygen vacancies attracted considerable attentions as ETL, and it has successfully realized several record efficiencies in the organic-inorganic hybrid pero-SCs.  $  ^{[19, 33-35]}  $   Colloid   $  SnO_{2}  $   (Col-  $  SnO_{2}  $  ) as one of the most widely used types of   $  SnO_{2}  $   possesses a high-lying conduction band maximum (  $  E_{c}  $  ), which can well match with that of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $ . However, its high wettability to precursor solution and enlarged energy level offset with the work function (WF) of indium tin oxide (ITO) would lead to a small grain size of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   crystal and unfavorable Ohmic contact, respectively.  $  ^{[30, 32]}  $   Although nanoparticle-type   $  SnO_{2}  $   (NP-  $  SnO_{2}  $  ) with confinement effect could bring down   $  E_{c}  $   and enhance hydrophobic surface, the mismatched   $  E_{c}  $   between the ETL and perovskite and the rough surface of NP-  $  SnO_{2}  $   are harmful to the charge extraction and perovskite growth.  $  ^{[36]}  $   Therefore, bilayer ETL with different   $  SnO_{2}  $   nanoparticles  $  ^{[37, 38]}  $   or partially ion-doping   $  SnO_{2}  $    $  ^{[39, 40]}  $   were proposed to improve surface morphology or optimize energy levels with the aim to facilitate perovskite growth and charge carrier extraction. Unfortunately, some new problems arose: 1) incompatible interface between the heterojunction bilayer   $  SnO_{2}  $   (with/without doping) can introduce an undesirable recombination; 2) uneven dispersion of dopants in   $  SnO_{2}  $   may give rise to impurity phase, leading to heterogeneous carrier mobility in the bilayer   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL, thus influencing the carrier transport. Therefore, it is urgent to develop   $  SnO_{2}  $ -based ETL, which can maintain low defects, and form well electronic contact and

favorable energy level alignment with   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and even ITO, for achieving low   $  E_{loss}  $   and high performance.  $  ^{[41, 42]}  $

In this work, an intermeshing   $  SnO_{2}  $   (Im-  $  SnO_{2}  $  ) ETL strategy was proposed to reduce   $  E_{loss}  $   for high PCE by subtly combining two types of   $  SnO_{2}  $   with complementary electronic/physical properties. Rational combination of the two types of   $  SnO_{2}  $   especially constructing an intermeshing contact can break the “bucket effect” limitation, simultaneously achieving a compact and smooth ETL surface with favorable wettability and high electron mobility, which are superior to the single   $  SnO_{2}  $   films. Importantly, this intermeshing ETL could not only facilitate   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film growth with a compact, high crystal orientation, and large-grain-size morphology but also further suppress the oxygen vacancies that exist in   $  SnO_{2}  $   to reduce the photocatalytic degradation property. The gradually decreased   $  E_{c}  $   of Im-  $  SnO_{2}  $   provide desirable cascade energy levels between perovskite and the ITO electrode for fluent electron transport, leading to the improved charge extraction efficiency and suppressed interfacial trap-assisted recombination. With this strategy, the   $  E_{loss}  $   of   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SC was greatly reduced below 0.6 eV, showing a PCE of 16.10% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.31 V. Both the device stabilities under strong UV irradiation and the ambient atmosphere were also simultaneously significantly improved.

# Results and Discussion

The  $ \mathrm{Im - SnO_2} $  ETL was simply constructed by successively coating two types of  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  precursor solution with optimized concentrations; one is NP- $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  obtained by dissolving  $ \mathrm{SnCl_2\cdot 2H_2O} $  in ethanol, and another is Col- $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  obtained by diluting colloid  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  in deionized water. Considering the diversity and complementarity of morphology and the electrical characteristics between these two-type  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  films, the synthesized NP- $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  with a deeper-lying  $ E_{c} $  close to the WF of ITO is more suitable to be as the bottom layer. And to exclude the effect of  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  on the prepared NP- $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  film, we compared the transmittance spectra of the NP- $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  film before and after washing with  $ \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} $  (Figure S1). The completely overlapped transmittance spectra suggest the subsequently processed Col- $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  would not influence the underlying NP- $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ . It should be noted that the Im- $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  fabrication process, including spin coating and thermal annealing, can be fully carried out in ambient atmosphere with a precisely controlled relative humidity (RH ~ 40%). Under the assistance of a moderate moisture, the Im- $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  growth can not only be simplified by avoiding the tedious atmosphere transfer between air and inert gas, but also ensure more sufficient oxidation of  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ . To facilitate the penetration/contact of the two-type  $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  with each other, additional UV-Ozone (UVO) was carried out before coating Col- $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  solution on NP- $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  film. After coating Col- $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $ , a thermal annealing treatment was conducted to assist their further growth. Therefore, Im- $ \mathrm{SnO_2} $  with good electronic contact at NP- $ \mathrm{SnO_2 / Col - SnO_2} $  interface was successfully fabricated.

of the colloidal   $  SnO_{2}  $   could bound with the   $  Br^{-}  $  of perovskite lattice through substitutional reaction,  $  ^{[56]}  $   thus the loss of the   $  Br^{-}  $  resulted in an I-rich perovskite film leading to the lattice expansion.

The highest ratio of the (100) and (200) peaks to the other peaks, such as (110) in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film grown on   $  Im-SnO_{2}  $   ETL, reveals that   $  Im-SnO_{2}  $   is more likely to induce perovskite to grow along the (100) and (200) planes (Figure 3c). The strong lattice orientation perpendicular to the substrate is beneficial for the charge transport.  $  ^{[57]}  $

In addition to the quality of the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   film, the matched energy levels between the ETL and   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and between the ETL and ITO also have an essential impact on the final device performance. Therefore, the exact electronic structures of these   $  SnO_{2}  $  -based ETLs as well as   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   were then measured using ultra-violet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) (Figure 3d, Figure S8). The WF, valence band (  $  E_{v}  $  ), and   $  E_{c}  $   of NP-  $  SnO_{2}  $   and Col-  $  SnO_{2}  $   films were estimated according to the cutoff edge of the binding energy and optical bandgaps; these results are illustrated in Figure 3e and summarized in Table S3. The Im-  $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL provides a cascade   $  E_{c}  $   between ITO and the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   layer,  $  ^{[26, 58]}  $   accompanied by a gradually deepening WF close to that of ITO. Meanwhile, we found that the WF and   $  E_{c}  $   of Im-  $  SnO_{2}  $   located between those of NP-  $  SnO_{2}  $   and Col-  $  SnO_{2}  $ , which indicated that there was an electronic coupling occurred between the two types of   $  SnO_{2}  $ . The NP-SnO  $  _{2}  $  -based device shows a PCE of 13.45% with a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.22 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 15.07 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $   and an FF of 73.18%. As shown in Figure 4b, these shunt pathways are likely to trigger serious carrier recombination. The pinhole-free and continuous Col-SnO  $  _{2}  $   ETL could effectively prevent CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br from directly contacting ITO and thus benefiting the charges separation and transport. Interestingly, when using Im-SnO  $  _{2}  $   as ETL, both the continuous structure and cascade energy levels of Im-SnO  $  _{2}  $   would contribute to a high charge extraction efficiency. Combining the high-quality CsPbI  $  _{2}  $  Br film grown on Im-SnO  $  _{2}  $ , the resultant device undoubtedly showed a reduced current leakage and recombination paths. As a result, the champion pero-SC showed a remarkable PCE of 16.10% with the overall improved photovoltaic parameters: a   $  V_{oc}  $   of 1.31 V, a   $  J_{sc}  $   of 15.53 mA/cm  $  ^{2}  $ , and an FF of 79.13%. # Conclusion

In conclusion, a subtle and effective intermeshing   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL was introduced for suppressing   $  E_{loss}  $   in the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs. By utilizing the complementary electronic/physical properties between NP-  $  SnO_{2}  $   and Col-  $  SnO_{2}  $ , the resultant Im-  $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL with an intermeshing contact has notable advantages over each single   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL, including a smooth surface, high electron mobility, favorable wettability and optimized band energy levels. As a result, this remarkable Im-  $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL can well assist the   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   perovskite growth in forming a compact and large-grain-size morphology with high crystallinity and favorable crystal orientation, and it also established cascade energy levels between   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   and cathode ITO beneficial to defects reduction and charge transport. The   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs with Im-  $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL showed a promising PCE of 16.10%, which is one of the few   $  CsPbI_{2}Br  $   pero-SCs with efficiencies over 16%. Importantly, this efficient device also exhibits a tremendous reduction in   $  E_{loss}  $   of 0.59 eV and enables the   $  V_{oc}  $   to surpass 1.3 V. The overall improved unencapsulated device stability under strong UV illumination and an ambient atmosphere can further confirm the great contribution of the well-designed intermeshing   $  SnO_{2}  $   ETL. Therefore, this method provides insights regarding interface design in all-inorganic pero-SCs that will be helpful to further boost the performance of this emergent field.
