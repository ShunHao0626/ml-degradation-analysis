# A ‘Smart-Bottle’ humidifier assisted air-processed CuSCN inorganic hole extraction layer towards highly-efficient, large-area and thermally-stable perovskite solar cells

Sawanta S. Mali,  $  ^{1}  $   Jyoti V. Patil  $  ^{1}  $   and Chang Kook Hong  $  ^{1*}  $

$  ^{1}  $  Polymer Energy Materials Laboratory, School of Advanced Chemical Engineering, Chonnam National University, Gwangju-S. Korea 61186

*Correspondence: sawantasolar@gmail.com, hongck@chonnam.ac.kr

# Abstract

Long-term thermally stable via low-cost inorganic-hole extraction layer (i-HEL) is the best choice towards the commercialization of air-thermo-stable, low-cost highly-efficient perovskite solar cells (PSCs). The low-cost, thermally-stable inorganic copper (I) thiocyanate (CuSCN) i-HEL is the best choice. However, due to its unique polar diethyl sulfide solvent solubility and conventional spin-coating method damage the perovskite layer and hampers the large-area fabrication. In the present investigation we have developed a simple novel smart-bottle-humidifier-assisted (SBHA) method for p-type CuSCN i-HEL deposition towards air-thermally stable low-cost PSCs to date. The developed method is most simplistic, ambient-air processed, cheapest and could be suitable for large area deposition. The best efficiency based on CuSCN i-HEL devices exhibited 18.78% with >95% and >85% thermal stability over 1000 hours respectively at 65 ±1 and 85 ±1 degree Celsius. Interestingly, CuSCN i-HEL based devices show >2000 hours' air-stability and much stable compared to conventional expensive additives doped spiro-MeOTAD based PSCs. We also explore this technique towards large area (~1.04 cm²) fabrication and it also exhibited 18.34% PCE with >85% thermal stability at 85 ±1 degree Celsius over 1000 hours revealed the developed method is also suitable for large area perovskite fabrication.

Keywords. Perovskite solar cells, low-cost hole transporting materials, novel technique for deposition, towards  $ >1000 $  hours air-thermal-stability at  $ 85^{\circ}\mathrm{C} $ , facile for large-area deposition

# INTRODUCTION

Now the perovskite solar cells (PSCs) crossing   $  >23.2\%^{(1-6)}  $   breakthrough power conversion efficiency (PCE) through different compositions of MAPbX/FAPbX (herein MAPbX stands for methylammonium lead halide and FA stands for formamidinium lead halide) and mixing of variety of cations such as RbI, CsI and using different hole extraction layers (herein HTMs, HTLs or HELs), but still suffered air-thermal stability. So far, the breakthrough certified efficiency has been demonstrated only for expensive and additives doped   $  (2,2',7,7'  $  -tetrakis(N,Npdimethoxyphenylamino)-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-MeOTAD) and poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA) HTLs. Recently Jeon et al. fluorene-terminated HTM and demonstrated 23.2% efficiency with 120 hours stability at 70 degree Celsius.  $  ^{(6)}  $   Although, the developed   $  (N2,N2,N7,N7  $  -tetrakis(9,9-dimethyl-9H-fluoren-2-yl)-N2,N2',N7,N7' -tetrakis(4-methoxyphenyl)-9,9' -spirobi[fluorene]-2,2',7,7' -

tetraamine) (known as DM) showed >23.2 % with 120 hours stability at 85 °C, but still, the precise doping of 4-tertbutylpyridine (TBP), bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide lithium salt (LiTFSI), and FK 209 Co(III) – TFSI salt (FK209) are needed in order to tune the efficiency. Although, the PTAA based perovskite devices demonstrated PSCs the several hours air-stability but cost of these HTLs (PTAA=3000 USD/gm and spiro-MeOTAD =500 USD/gm), thermal stability and preparation methods are still making problems. The uniform deposition of these HTMs, spin coating technique needed which is suffered from high waste of chemicals (>90%) which hampers the low-cost production technique (7). Although, carbon based PSCs (C-PSCs) is best choice towards air-stable PSCs but still it shows limited efficiency (up to 15%) via most controlled parameters (8,9). Also it shows major hysteresis issue. Therefore, the non-conventional, low-cost, inorganic (i)-HELs such as CuI, pristine and doped NiOx,   $  Cu_{2}O  $  , CuSCN,   $  CsSnI_{3}  $     $  CuGaO_{2}  $   etc. have been employed for regular (n-i-p) type PSCs.  $  ^{(10-15)}  $

Although, all-metal oxide based PSCs showed good efficiency with air-stability, but still thermal stability has not been tested.  $  ^{(16)}  $   Recently, Seo et al., used   $  Cs_{0.05}MA_{0.95}PbI_{3}  $   perovskite absorber which is sandwiched between inorganic metal oxide for both HTL and ETL in inverted p-i-n architecture and demonstrated 18.45 % PCE with 86.7% retention after 500 hours at 85 °C thermal stress.  $  ^{(17)}  $   The thermal stability was improved by using atomic layer deposited (ALD) deposited Al:ZnO (AZO) and NiOx. It is well known that, the stability of unstable methylammonium lead iodide (  $  MAPbI_{3}  $  ) (1.58 eV) can be improved by incorporating methylammonium lead bromide (  $  MAPbBr_{3}  $  ) (2.26 eV).  $  ^{(18)}  $   However, relatively low-band gap of black phase formamidinium (FA)-based perovskite   $  FAPbI_{3}  $   (1.48 eV) with unique composition of   $  (\mathrm{FAPbI}_{3})_{0.85}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})_{0.15}  $   (1.54 eV) stabilized at 100 °C and pushed PCE 20.8 % with circuit voltage (  $  V_{OC}  $  ) up to 1.16 V and current density (  $  J_{SC}  $  ) of 24.6 mA cm  $  ^{-2}  $  .  $  ^{(19)}  $   The stability of MA-cation is poor than FA-cation. Therefore, implementing a triple/quadrupole (CsI and/or RbI) cations in the above perovskite (  $  FAPbI_{3})_{0.85}(\mathrm{MAPbBr}_{3})_{0.15}  $   composition pushed   $  V_{OC}  $   up to 1.24 V having 1.63 eV band gap results in 21.6 % PCE with better stability via PTAA HTM.  $  ^{(4)}  $   Jung et al., used butylammonium (BA) and phenethylammonium (PEA) cations modified   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   perovskite and demonstrated 20.1 % PCE with >760 hours stability at 80 °C. However, PTAA HTM is most expensive and also LiTFSI and TBP doping needed.  $  ^{(20)}  $   Fu et al., reviewed the long-term stability of perovskite solar cells based on different aspects and suggested the current strategies for 2D/3D perovskite solar cells with depth-insight study is needed for further achievement.  $  ^{(21)}  $

Recently, MA-free perovskite absorber   $  Rb_{5}Cs_{10}FAPbI_{3}  $   demonstrated 18.16 % PCE after 1000 hours at 85 °C.  $  ^{(22)}  $   However, the preparation of Al:ZnO (AZO) and NiOx by atomic layer deposition (ALD) is expensive. Besides, the preparation of n-type metal oxides such as ZnO or heavily doped Ti(Nb)Ox, or additives doped spiro-MeOTAD or PTAA HTMs are complicated.  $  ^{(16,11)}  $   Therefore, to employ thermally stable inorganic HEL is the best choice towards low-cost highly efficient PSCs. The thermal stability of CuSCN HEL is much higher than any other conventional HEL. Due to its extremely low-cost, high thermal stability and simplistic additives free preparation method, CuSCN is promising i-HEL for stable PSCs with operational stability.  $  ^{(23)}  $   So far, the thermally stable CuSCN HEL has been deposited by various method such as doctor-blading, spin-coating, spray coating and electrodeposition with moderate PCE.  $  ^{(13,24-29)}  $   Recently, dynamic spin-coating method used for the CuSCN deposition with rGO or   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   interfacial layer in order to cross 20 % efficiency with good thermal stability.  $  ^{(30)}  $   Sepalage et al developed chlorobenzene interfacial layer and demonstrated 9.6 % PCE.  $  ^{(31)}  $   Wijeyasinghe et al., developed novel alternative to diethyl sulfide solvent by suing aqueous ammonia based precursor solutions processed CuSCN HTL and demonstrated 17.2 % PCE.  $  ^{(32)}  $   However, to our best knowledge, the polar diethyl-sulfide solvent is main cause for perovskite layer degradation. It is also noted that, the diethyl-sulfide solvent is not recommended solvent in glove-box for perovskite fabrication. Besides, deposition of   $  Al_{2}O_{3}  $   interfacial spacer layer via atomic layer deposition (ALD) is complicated and expensive one. Till date, the highly efficient PSCs based on CuSCN has been reported only for dynamic spin-coating technique. Thinking out-of-the box, more simplistic approach is needed in order to coat CuSCN i-HEL on to perovskite absorber without compromising perovskite degradation and efficiency during deposition. It is well known, the reproducibility of n-i-p is much higher than p-i-n type architecture. Therefore we focused on regular type n-i-p type PSCs. Based on the above discussion and previous literature, we strongly believe that, the present manuscript showed much higher stability. The limited literature survey has been provided in Table S4. Importantly, we have developed a very simple and cost-effective “Smart-Bottle Humidifier Assisted (SBHA)” method for the deposition of CuSCN HTM.

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

# Methodology, role of thickness and photovoltaic properties

We developed a simple and cost-effective technique for CuSCN i-HEL deposition for mesoscopic regular (n-i-p) architecture of PSCs, known as a smart-bottle-humidifier assisted (SBHA) technique towards highly efficient, thermally-stable PSCs (Fig. S1). Experimental arrangement and parameters are mentioned in Fig. S2. The perovskite-HEL interface degradation due to polar diethyl sulfide solvent could be terminated by using this newly developed air-processed processed method. This method facilitates highly uniform deposition of CuSCN layer without degradation of perovskite layer from polar diethyl solvent, since we used   $  \sim95\ ^{\circ}C  $   during deposition. The developed method is simplistic and different from previous method. The SBHA technique for highly uniform CuSCN coating has been shown in Fig. 1A. Initially, different concentration of CuSCN solutions were deposited onto glass substrate by SBHA technique in order to check the uniformity and thickness (Fig. 1B,C). Our SBHA revealed the sample prepared from 10 mg/ml concentration with 2 ml solution quantity showed highly uniform pin-hole free deposition with   $  \sim100  $   nm thickness. This is due to formation of ultrafine CuSCN droplets produced by Smart-bottle humidifier similar to ultrasonic spray.  $  ^{(33)}  $

# Topographical analysis and steady-state and time-resolved photoluminescence (PL) studies.

In the present investigation, we used the mesoscopic-planar bilayer device architecture for spiro-MeOTAD as well as CuSCN based i-HELs. High quality   $  \left(\mathrm{Cs}_{0.05}\mathrm{FA}_{0.81}\mathrm{MA}_{0.14}\mathrm{PbI}_{2.55}\mathrm{Br}_{0.45}\right)  $   perovskite layer was deposited by anti-solvent engineering process on to spin coated mp-  $  TiO_{2}  $   film followed annealing at 100 °C for 90 min. The highly crystalline nature of perovskite layer and   $  \beta  $  -CuSCN phase was determined by X-Ray diffraction analysis (Fig. S4, S5). The highly uniform perovskite layer coated film then transferred in fume hood containing hot-plate to deposit CuSCN layer. The film was kept on hot-plate for few seconds and 2 ml of CuSCN/diethyl sulfide (10 mg/ml) solution was poured in smart-bottle humidifier neck (Fig. S1) and coated on top of perovskite layer. The bottle-neck to substrate distance was kept ten centimeters. To remove diethyl sulfide residue solvent, the deposited substrates were kept on same hot-plate for next 5 min at 95 °C. # Reproducibility and thermal stability study

Reproducibility of developed method has been analyzed from J-V curves of 25 independent perovskite solar cell devices fabricated using CuSCN and spiro-MeOTAD HTMs. It is noted that, the reproducibility of CuSCN based HEL is very similar to conventional spiro-MeOTAD based HEL. For CuSCN devices we observed an average Jsc of  $ 22.5 \pm 0.50 \mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $  of  $ 1.05 \pm 0.03 \mathrm{~V} $ , and FF of  $ 72.55 \pm 2 \% $  yielded average  $ 18.2 \pm 0.50 \% $  efficiency. On the other hand, for conventional spiro-MeOTAD based devices revealed  $ \mathrm{J_{SC}} $  of  $ 22.6 \pm 0.55 \mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $ ,  $ \mathrm{V_{OC}} $  of  $ 1.110 \pm 0.01 \mathrm{~V} $ , and FF of  $ 73.65 \pm 2 \% $  yielded average  $ 18.85 \pm 0.50 \% $  efficiency (Fig. 4A). Furthermore, we have recorded the hysteresis analysis of champion cell based CuSCN HEL. The hysteresis behavior of fabricated devices was carried out with a  $ 50 \mathrm{~ms} $  scanning delay. The champion CuSCN device exhibits PCE values of 18.74 and  $ 17.79 \% $  for the reverse and forward scans, respectively indicating negligible hysteresis (Fig. 4B). In contrast, the champion spiro-MeOTAD based exhibited PCE values of 19.37 and  $ 18.52 \% $  for the reverse and forward scans, respectively also indicating negligible hysteresis (Fig. S7). The external quantum efficiency (EQE) revealed, CuSCN based device achieved very similar to that of spiro-MeOTAD based device. Integration of the EQE spectra of over the solar emission yields AM 1.5 photocurrents of 22.52 and  $ 22.72 \mathrm{mAcm}^{-2} $  respectively (Fig. 4C), in excellent agreement with the measured  $ \mathrm{J_{SC}} $  values, indicating that the spectral mismatch between our simulator and the true AM 1.5 solar emission is negligibly small. # Large area fabrication and its thermal stability

In order to demonstrate, the real commercialization application of our developed SBHA method towards large area, we have fabricated the large area device with an active area   $  \sim1.04~cm^{2}  $   and its hysteresis-thermal stability analysis (Fig. 5). The solar cell performance of the optimized large area is shown in Fig. 5A. The forward scan exhibited the   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.060 V,   $  J_{SC}  $   of 22.75 mAcm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and FF of 0.698 yielded of PCE 16.83 %, whereas the reverse scan exhibited   $  V_{OC}  $   of 1.070 V,   $  J_{SC}  $   of 23.01 mAcm  $  ^{-2}  $  , and FF of 0.745 yielded PCE of 18.34 %. To the authors best knowledge this efficiency is state-of-the-art for this active device area. These results demonstrated feasibility of our developed method towards large area device fabrication. This method may be expandable to other materials. The device stability and its reliable and accurate photocurrent measurements, we have recorded the steady-state photocurrent at the maximum power point (-0.90V). From, the steady-state photocurrent output (Fig. 5B) of our champion devices exhibits dramatic increment in the initial few seconds and reached up to   $  \sim23.0~mAcm^{-2}  $   corresponding to 18.30 % PCE for large area device. These results demonstrated the stable photocurrent output as long as 50s. The constant photocurrent measurements exhibited a faster stabilizing rate which is consistent with the fabricated CuSCN film contain a negligible density of trap sites.

Furthermore, we have monitored the thermal stability of large area fabricated devices at   $  65\pm1  $   °C and   $  85\pm1  $   °C (Fig. 5C). For stability we have fabricated 25 large devices (  $  \sim1.04  $   cm  $  ^{2}  $   active area) for each condition. Interestingly, both devices retain   $  >95\%  $   and   $  >85\%  $   stability respectively at   $  65^{\circ}  $  C and   $  85^{\circ}  $  C after 1000 hours respectively which is consistent with our small area devices stability data. These interesting results can be explaining as follows. The achieved higher efficiency at large area is mainly stem from uniform deposition of CuSCN layer through smart bottle humidifier technique. Therefore, this DES solvent can easily evaporate during deposition and that protect perovskite layer from polar solvent. In order to confirm the presence of unwanted diethylsulfide in perovskite device after CuSCN deposition, we have recorded FT-IR spectra of fabricated device (without gold metal contacts) and compared with a standard diethyl sulfide solution, Fig. S13. The commercial diethyl sulfide sample exhibited a strong peak at 2928 and   $  2982  $   cm  $  ^{-1}  $  , while the strong peak at 2172 cm  $  ^{-1}  $   for CuSCN/DES solution due to SCN vibration in   $  \beta  $  -CuSCN which is absent in bare diethyl sulfide sample.  $  ^{(42,44)}  $   However, our fresh and old perovskite device showed absence of diethyl solvent. This confirms that, the absence of diethyl sulfide in the device after CuSCN deposition. We also monitored the device stability of the large-area device of the encapsulated device under continuous illumination (Fig. 5D). Interestingly, it shows   $  >85\%  $   stability even after 200 hours under continuous illumination. This the good indication of our developed smart bottle humidifier assisted deposition technique for CuSCN coating will be beneficial for development of thermally stable large low-cost fabrication.

# CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, we have demonstrated a simplistic SBHA approach for CuSCN deposition with pin-hole free and controlled thickness could be a promising hole extraction layer with comparable efficiency (  $  >18.78\%  $   in reverse scan) to that of conventional, expensive, unstable, additive doped HTMs. The large area also exhibited 18.34 % PCE (in reverse scan) demonstrated the developed method is suitable for the large-area perovskite fabrication. The gold metal diffusion has been terminated by rGO interfacial layer which facilitates further extension for their thermal stability.
