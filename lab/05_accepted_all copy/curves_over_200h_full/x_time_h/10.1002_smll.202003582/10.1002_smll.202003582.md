# Morphology Evolution of a High-Efficiency PSC by Modulating the Vapor Process

Zhun Yao, Wangen Zhao,  $  ^{*}  $   Zhuo Xu, Jialun Wen, and Shengzhong (Frank) Liu  $  ^{*}  $

The morphological quality of the photoactive layer is the key component affecting the performance metrics of a photovoltaic device. Therefore, fine adjustment of the crystallization dynamics is urgently required. By manipulating the amount of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) remaining in the spin-coated perovskite films during the annealing treatment, an obvious morphological evolution arises. The crystallization kinetics is significantly altered due to the formation of intermediate phases and the variation of DMSO vapor pressure via producing the semienclosed space with a covering. On the one hand, the obviously formed intermediate phase  $ \mathrm{MA}_2\mathrm{Pb}_3\mathrm{I}_8(\mathrm{DMSO})_2 $  retards the crystallization process. On the other hand, the DMSO vapor in the semienclosed space intrigues the recrystallization process and results in Ostwald ripening to produce large-aspect-ratio grains with fewer defect states, decreased carrier doping, and longer carrier lifetimes. Thus, nonradiative processes are greatly suppressed. Besides, combined with X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy measurement and the surface energy of MAI- and PbI-terminated surface model calculated by density functional theory, the defect states are identified and the causes of  $ \mathrm{Pb}^0 $  defect states are explained. Using this strategy, a high power conversion efficiency of  $ 20.09\% $  is achieved based on  $ \mathrm{MAPbI}_3 $  photovoltaic solar cell, and the long-term ambient shelf and thermal stability are obviously improved.

In order to figure out the   $  Pb^{0}  $   was reduced, perovskite surface with respect to two possible surface terminations: A PbI-terminated surface, exposing lead and iodide atoms and an MAI-terminated surface, exposing methylammonium cations (MA) and iodide atoms were compared. We calculated the surface energies of these two surfaces. According to the definition of surface energy, it composes of two parts, one is the energy to cleave the surface from the bulk structure, and the other is the energy change during the surface relaxation. In   $  MAPbI_{3}  $   case, if we cleave the Pb-I bonds in the z direction, MAI-terminated and PbI-terminated surfaces can be formed simultaneously. Thus, the energy that need to cleave the two surfaces from the bulk structure is the same. While two relaxation energies of two surfaces are calculated to be 1.22 and 0.75 eV for   $  2 \times 2  $   supercell MAI-terminated and PbI-terminated surfaces, respectively. So, the MAI-terminated surface is more stable than the PbI-terminated surfaces. This is also consistent with the earlier result that MAI terminations are more prone to form in line with some facts by Quarti et al.  $  ^{[44]}  $   In addition,

# 3. Conclusions

In conclusion, we have successfully developed a strategy consisting of controlling the amount of DMSO remaining in the spin-coated perovskite film during the annealing treatment to alter the crystallization process via PSES. The crystallization dynamics was dramatically influenced due to the formation of intermediate   $  \mathrm{MA}_{2}\mathrm{Pb}_{3}\mathrm{I}_{8}(\mathrm{DMSO})_{2}  $   and variance of the DMSO vapor pressure. The intermediate adduct has retarded the crystallization process. The flow of DMSO gas molecular produced by DMSO left in the film and decomposition of   $  \mathrm{MA}_{2}\mathrm{Pb}_{3}\mathrm{I}_{8}(\mathrm{DMSO})_{2}  $   during the annealing treatment resulted in a secondary crystallization and growth process, producing high-quality photoactive film with much larger grains and higher carrier lifetimes, as well as reduced carrier doping and trap state densities. We further identified the defect states as   $  Pb^{0}  $   by XPS results and subsequently explained the cause of   $  Pb^{0}  $   by calculating relaxation energies based   $  PbI_{2}  $   and MAI-terminated structure model by density functional theory (DFT). Finally, the assembled photovoltaic device based PSES strategy displayed better metrics, and the power conversion efficiency was enhanced by 14.2% compared to the pristine one. Additionally, the long-term stability (humidity and light) was obviously improved due to its reduced defects and enhanced grain size, which can reduce the admission passages. In all, we provided

an effective and general protocol for fabricating efficient inorganic–organic hybrid perovskite solar cells in this paper.

# 4. Experimental Section

Chemicals:  $ \mathrm{PbI}_2 $ , lithium bis (trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Li-TFSI), 4-tert-butyl pyridine (tBP), and chlorobenzene were acquired from Sigma-Aldrich. Spiro-OMeTAD [2,2',7,7'-tetrakis-(N,N-di-p-methoxyphenylamine)-9,9'-spiro-bifluorene] was purchased from Shenzhen Feiming Technology Co.  $ \gamma $  -Butyrolactone (GBL), dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO), and  $ \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_3\mathrm{I} $  were purchased from Xi'an Polymer Light Technology Corp. All the chemicals were used without further purification.

Preparation of Dense   $  TiO_{2}  $   Layer:   $  TiO_{2}  $   was deposited on a glass (25 × 25 mm) substrate coated with fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO). The cleaning of the FTO glasses was conducted based on a standard process with washing in liquid/acetone/ethanol for 30 min each. Then, the clean FTO glasses were treated under ultraviolet light and ozone for ≈8 min. The treated FTO was flattened out in a petri dish filled with   $  40 \times 10^{-3}  $   M   $  TiCl_{4}  $   aqueous solution for 60 min at about 70 °C. Finally, the FTO substrates coated with   $  TiO_{2}  $   were washed with distilled water and ethanol, followed by drying with nitrogen.

Deposition of Absorbing Layer and Fabrication of Device: The precursor solution was prepared as follows: 1.4 mmol CH $ _3 $ NH $ _3 $ I and 1.4 mmol PbI $ _2 $  were dissolved in 1 mL mixture of  $ \gamma $ -butyrolactone and dimethylsulfoxide (with the volume ratio of 7:3), and then stirred at room temperature for several hours to obtain a clear, light-yellow solution. Then, the solutions were prepared by filtering using a 0.22  $ \mu $ m pore polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) syringe filter before spin-coating. The perovskite thin film was spin-coated onto the FTO glass substrates by a consecutive two-step spin-coating process at 1000 and 4000 rpm for 10 and 40 s, respectively. It was noted that 500  $ \mu $ L chlorobenzene was dripped as an antisolvent 15 s after the second stage began to obtain a light-brown smooth perovskite thin film. Then, the substrates underwent a preheating treatment at 60  $ ^{\circ} $ C for 1 or 3 min on a hotplate. Subsequently, the substrates were heated at 100  $ ^{\circ} $ C for 20 min upside down on soda-lime glass on a hotplate to acquire the final perovskite films. For comparison, the reference samples were heated immediately at 100  $ ^{\circ} $ C for 20 min without the preheating treatment. After cooling of the perovskite thin film, the spiro-OMeTAD solution with a concentration of 90 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  was spin-coated onto the perovskite films at 5000 rpm for 30 s. It was noted that the 22  $ \mu $ L Li-TFSI solution (520 mg mL $ ^{-1} $  in acetonitrile) and 36  $ \mu $ L tBP were added to the spiro-OMeTAD solution for the sake of improving carrier mobility

Computational Methods: In this work, the surface energy calculations were performed using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP). The projected augmented wave (PAW) method and the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) functional within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) were employed to describe the interaction between ion cores and valence electrons and the exchange-correlation effects, and an energy cutoff of 450 eV was set for the plane-wave function's expansion. The van der Waals (vdW) dispersion correction was found necessary to yield more accurate lattice constants, which are described by the DFT-D3 correction. A   $  2 \times 2  $   supercell tetragonal phase of MAPbI  $  _{3}  $   is used to build the slab model, and only   $  \Gamma  $   point was adopted for the k-point sampling. The lattice parameters and atomic positions of all the structures were relaxed until the total energy changes were less than   $  1.0 \times 10^{-5}  $   eV and the maximum force component acting on each atom was less than 0.05 eV Å  $  ^{-1}  $  .

Characterizations: The phase purities of the perovskite films were tested by X-ray Diffraction (XRD; DX-2700) using Cu Kα radiation (  $  \lambda = 1.5416  $   Å). The absorption spectra of the absorbing layers on TiO  $  _{2}  $  -coated FTO were recorded using a UV–vis spectrometer (Lambda 950, PerkinElmer). The morphologies of the perovskite films were investigated using field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM; SU-8020, Hitachi). An atomic force microscope (AFM; MULTIMODE 8, Bruker) was used to image the topography and to measure the surface roughness in peak force mode. The J–V characteristics of assembled

solar cell devices without encapsulation were investigated using a source meter (2400, Keithley) under simulated sunlight illumination generated by a solar simulator (XES-40S2-CE, San-Ei Electric, AM 1.5G filter at  $ 100\mathrm{mWcm}^{-2} $ ), which was calibrated using a certified silicon photodiode. The steady-state photoluminescence spectra were recorded on an F-7000 (Shimadzu, Japan) with of  $ 150\mathrm{W} $  halogen tungsten lamp as the light source. The TR-PL (time-resolved photoluminescence) spectroscopy data were acquired on an Edinburgh Instruments FLS920 fluorescence spectrometer with the time-correlated single-photon counting method. The excitation source was a picosecond pulsed diode laser at  $ 406.8\mathrm{nm} $  with the pulse width of  $ 64.2~\mathrm{ps} $ . The IPCE (monochromatic incident photon-to-electron conversion efficiency) was performed on a QTest Station 2000ADI system (Crowntech Inc., USA). The light source was a  $ 150\mathrm{W} $  halogen tungsten lamp, and the monochromatic light intensity for the IPCE was calibrated using a reference silicon photodiode.
